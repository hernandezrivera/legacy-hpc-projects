Project Summary

The convergence of multi-pronged shocks in South Sudan, continues to increase the level of vulnerabilities. In the year 2022, an estimated 2 million people will be at risk of acute malnutrition, including 302,078 children (148,018 boys and 154,060 girls) with SAM, 1,039,214 children (509,215 boys and 529,999 girls) with MAM, and 675,548 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW). In matters of underlying causes of malnutrition, a total of 8.4 million people (60% of the total population) will be at risk of severe food insecurity, while only 47.1 % of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The under-five mortality rate remains high due to preventable diseases, such as diarrhea, malaria and pneumonia; the situation is made worse when it is estimated that a child with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) or moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) is twelve or three times more likely to die than a well-nourished child, respectively (HNO, 2022).
The multi-sectoral analysis above provides abroad picture of the situation, this corroborate with underlying causes of AM in a number of States and Counties. The SMART Survey and FSNMS) conducted in the year 2021, Suggests that Pibor and Twic East County has high GAM of 21.6% and 16.8% respectively; the Level of SAM in the two counties and in addition to Twic and Bor South, are above W.H.O emergency threshold classified as very high and high in need of humanitarian assistance. The cyclic occurrence of aggravating factors and underlying causes for malnutrition still remains a challenge with the high level of food insecurity; lack of adequate health, water and sanitation services (WASH); age-sets fighting, barriers to accessing nutrition services that includes distance, poor road conditions, Poor hygiene with slow adaptation of good practices, disruption of humanitarian response due to youth agitations and frustration of staff from others regions. 158,600 individuals including persons leaving with disability (PWD) will be supported at a cost of USD 1.2Million and in line with Strategic objective 1, 2 and 3. JAM endeavors to contribute to decrease GAM among children under the age of 5 years and PLW in prioritized locations. This project is embedded to nutrition Sector Objective 1,2,3 and 4; JAM will support increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women through Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program (BSFP) to 7,742 under 5 of age children and 8,847 PLW in Bor South; Twic and Twic East. The project will promote behavior change communication, to 24,608 primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months through MIYCN counselling; while the plan is to reach 55,369 boys and girls aged 6-59 months to receive Vitamin A, and 51,267 boys and girl aged 12-59 months to be dewormed. SO2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of (AM), 10,127 (girls and boys 6-59 months of age and 2952 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will be enrolled in 37 static sites and 5 mobile units which will be established. Whereas, 10 static sites will support both (TSFP for MAM –Under 5 and PLW) and outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) in Eastern Pibor, the 27 static sites plus mobile units will be able to admit children (SAM) in Western Pibor. Engagement and participation of community members (men, women and girls, PLWD) will be essential. 110 CNV will be recruited; kitchen garden established at nutrition sites and train mother to mother members (MTMSG) and mothers on Family MUAC. Malaria testing to 7,356 SAM children and treatment of eligible cases; distribution of Long Lasting Treated Mosquito Nets (LLITNs), WASH and ECD kits to AM children; creation of child friendly spaces in nutrition sites; and lastly linking the caregivers to FSL, Health and Protection services

1 - Needs

Conflict, inter-ethnic violence and communicable disease has had a devastating impact on Jonglei and Warrap State; Widespread Prolonged flood-related crop loss and the impact of COVID-19 – including 40% hyperinflation – means that the year 2022 is still a starving year. Jonglei, State and some Counties like Twic in Warrap State are still facing high GAM rate; classifying the state and the Counties at Very High and High in terms of Priority need and level of Vulnerability. The SMART Survey and FSNMS (Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System) conducted in the year 2021, Suggests that Pibor County and Twic East County has high GAM of 21.6% and 16.8%; the Level of SAM in the two counties and in addition with Twic and Bor South, are above W.H.O emergency threshold. These situations seem to be similar when the same Counties had high GAM rates of even up to to 28.3% Bor - South and Catastrophic situations in Pibor in early the year 2020/ 2021. Upon secondary review of nutrition cluster (PIN) for the year 2021 and 2022 – Twic, Twic East and Pibor has demonstrated increase burden for SAM, MAM, and BSFP for both Children under the age of 5 years, Pregnant and Lactating women and this includes persons leaving with disability among the mentioned group. Twic has increase burden of 92,812 (2022) from 66,259 in 2021; Pibor has increased burden from 78,218 in 2021 to 89,339 in 2022; while Twic East Counties burden has increased from 40,927 in 2021 to 42,2644. Although Bor South had a burden of 111, 112 in the years 2021; there is still acute malnutrition need and burden for 92,812 in the year 2022.In, Pibor County the SAM burden has increased from 8,351 in 2021 to 9,9195 in the year 2022 and this similar to MAM burden for Under 5 and PLW that has increased to from 27,836 and 12,378 to 29,456 and 18, 105 for the year 2021 and 2022 respectively. Access to food remains to be a challenge in the County, as such, 99% of households reported using consumption-based coping strategies, with 72% reported begging or being generally exhausted to access food (REACH&FSLC, February 2021); and 86% adopting Emergency-level coping strategy, including selling land for food (IMC, SMART Survey March 2021).The under-five mortality rate remains high due to preventable diseases, such as diarrhea, malaria and pneumonia. The destruction of WASH facilities, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and lack of accessibility to clean drinking water all contribute to poor health conditions and increase Malnutrition and this is worsened by poor coverage in health services. To undress the unmet need, funds are required; also there is need to establish static and mobile nutrition sites is key in provision of both nutrition specific and sensitivity intervention. The need to recruit staff, optimize the use of health and the 37+ nutrition sites as platforms to provide and scale up of prevention and lifesaving Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN to address barriers, promote breastfeeding through counselling; Supplementation, deworming and treatment of Acute Malnutrition to both Under 5 and PLW through CMAM components, including BSFP (Blanket supplementary Feeding Program is paramount. The need to Strengthen inter-sectoral approach and collaboration is critical (HNO, 2022); this includes to enhance malaria screening and treatment; training and promote access to a backyard kitchen garden through integration with FSL; to promote and provide clean water and sanitation in the nutrition sites and care givers; need for sensitization and awareness creation on hygiene promotions; to promoted child stimulation as part of early child development (ECD). The need to implement on rolled out nutrition and gender-based violence (GBV) action plan to help in addressing the GBV related concerns raised during safety audits. The need for coordination, support nutrition surveillance and timely reporting remains paramount and essential.

2 - Project activities

In order to contribute to and increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations; JAM will contribute through (BSFP) to children between 6-23 months (14079 Bor South; Twic 2,336; Twic East 1050); Pregnant and Lactating Women (13599 Bor South; 1200 Twic East and 2,670 Twic). There will be engaging of the community members at all level and ensure participation of both men, women and girls, PWD (Person Leaving with Disability) to promote responsive nutrition service delivery. The project will promote behavior change communication, to 24,606 including elderly, PWD as primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months through MIYCN counselling, including promotion of Optimal breastfeeding; while the plan is to reach 55,369 boys and girls (51% girls) aged 6-59 months to receive Vitamin A, and a total of 51,267 boy and girls aged 12-59 months will be dewormed to prevent micronutrient deficiencies in Pibor County (both Western and Eastern- Jebel Boma) this includes PWD. In Early detection and treatment of 10,127 (girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition; 37 static sites and 8 mobile units will be established of which 10 sites will support both treatment of SAM & MAM; One In-patient (SC) including reaching out 143 HIV/Care takers in Eastern part of Pibor County (Jebel Boma); while 30 sites will be able to admit 7356, children between age 6 -59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in the outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) in Western of Pibor County. It is expected that 2770 Under 5 years; 2952 PLW and will be reached through Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) in Kaziongora, Boma, Maruwo, Labarab and Mewun Payam’s. At least quarterly monitoring & supportive supervision visits by nutrition manager/coordinator from County Office will be done; scale up capacity strengthening efforts through on job training (OJT)& mentoring. Through operationalization of CNV guideline; 110 CNV will be engaged, trained to conduct routine follow-up, screening for malnutrition and to undertake home visits, they will be supported to provide reports on monthly basis. JAM will establish kitchen garden to 37 nutrition sites and train mother to mothers supper (MTMSG) that comprises of 15 members each for cascading the same the HH level. Community staff including CNV and Nutrition assistants will be equally trained to inorder to support the care givers on the basic skills of establishing the kitchen garden at backyards and HH level. The Family MUAC approach, also known as MUAC for mothers or Mother-MUAC, will be conducted through training mothers and other caregivers to identify early signs of malnutrition in their children using a simple to use Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) tape. This intends to promote early case identifications, likewise, data will be collected at the 37 nutrition sites.To increase health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection and to enhanced coordination and integrated programming. 7,356 children will be tested for malaria and eligible cases treated, JAM will also roll out the treatment of diarrhea through Zinc and ORS to eligible children below the age of 5 years suffering with diarrhea in stand-alone nutrition sites. Long Lasting Treated Mosquito Nets (LLITNs), WASH basic supplies; ECD kits to AM children will be providing; creation of child friendly spaces in the 37 nutrition sites. Implementing (GBV) concerns raised through the safety audit. It is expected that the integrated response will address the underlying causes of malnutrition and reduce the level on Morbidity and Mortality related to malnutrition and the underlying cause; likewise contributing to reduction of GAM within the proposed project area. Lastly refer the caregivers to FSL, Health and Protection services. JAM will work with WFP, UNICEF, Nutrition cluster, line Ministry, W.H.O.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Having accountability framework translate in complying to some principles of Core Humanitarian standards including Principle number 5. In order to Promote transparency and accountability, JAM values; accountability framework/Programme quality; and safeguarding are some of the driving factor for complaint management. Apart from what has been discussed in the section of Accountability to the Affected Population JAM has standard Complaint flow chart and framework that guides the Country offices on Complaint management feedback from the time, complaint, allegation, feedback and other concerns are raise. To ensure no gaps, JAM has developed a CRM feedback tracker to ensure no cases goes un responded to. The project will carefully review feedback or complaints collected from beneficiaries for course correction. Depending on their nature, the feedback will be categorized into non-sensitive and sensitive feedback or complaint and sensitive complaints. The Non-sensitive feedbacks commonly concerned with implementation of activities/dissatisfaction with activities. Such Programmatic complaints/ feedbacks are commonly collected through the feedback helpdesks, project review meetings, stakeholder’s meeting and are analyzed by a team of M and E, Gender, Protection and Accountability team, technical officers, and project manager and immediate actions are taken in the consecutive activities for course correction and future programmatic consideration. Beneficiary feedbacks or complaints on fraud and corruption, abusive behavior and sexual exploitation and abuse are considered sensitive cases and handled at country office level and are reviewed through the higher management organ. Where required an investigation team is commissioned by the CD to investigate and recommend actions to management. The Country Director and focal point are always responsible to guide the team. JAM will use it complaint and feedback mechanism in the project sites to improve in service delivery as they expect community to raise concerns or provide feedback which will be taken as a means for lesson learnt, monitoring and follow-up, service delivery improvement/ scale up of assistance, advocacy to address unmet critical needs or to be used for future project designs including innovations and fundraising.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The above risks were not selected due to organization structures that have been put in place to comply with the humanitarian principles mainly at the accountability sections, however the following mitigations will be put in Place:-Operational: The primary risk is one of accessibility, compounded by topographical challenges, flooding, and lack of access roads. To mitigate, project teams operating in Pibor County are divided between Pibor and Boma Field Offices to ensure reach and timely delivery of services. Motor boats and canoes are kept to reach flooded communities, while teams travel on foot to reach villages which have limited to no road access. To ensure availability of critical supplies all-year, they are prepositioned in sufficient quantity, when possible, during the dry season. If faced with supply depletion during the rainy season, necessary materials are airlifted using WFP/UNHAS support. Security: Insecurity in project areas can threaten the lives of program staff, limit access to the population, disrupt services and cause delay in overall implementation. To mitigate against this, JAM will work closely with relevant security teams (including with the UNMISS, UNDSS and government officials) to obtain most up-to-date information regarding project locations prior to scheduling project activities. Faced with active conflict, all personnel (and equipment, where possible) are relocated to secure UN compounds, or relocated to Juba where possible, with temporary shift in implementation approach of in consultation with the relevant clusters. At the start of the project; pre-implementation meeting will be conducted and the beneficiaries will not be required to offer anything in exchange of the project’s assistance. All staff sign the Code of Conduct, are trained and expected to adhere to the Safeguarding Policy thus holding them accountable. Complaints desks and accountability mechanisms will be set up, beneficiaries informed of their rights and how to report any violations.

Establishment of nutrition sites will include, local authority engagement through discussion to enable the new structures constructed on higher ground. The annual review and operationalization of flood season contingency plan will be conducted; supplies and basic commodities will be facilitated in advance to ensure population are not displaced because of supplies and search for services. JAM also intend to work with other partners supporting CMDRR and support awareness raising; train caregivers, community members' local authority and nutrition staff; and support the community led initiative on development of CMDRR.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The Project targets care takers of children suffering from acute malnutrition. As hunger and food insecurity is an underlying cause of malnutrition. The plan is to link Mothers, Care givers to food security and livelihood component with its focus on provision of emergency livelihood inputs and skills training to revitalize local food production through vegetable farming and fishing. JAM intend to use integrated response like to establish demonstration gardens for vegetable growing at all identified nutrition facilities; train community nutrition volunteers on kitchen gardening and further make provision for training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, including reducing post-harvest losses. The nutrition staff will be encouraged to work alongside agricultural extension workers and FSL staff to achieve this part of capacity building, skills & knowledge development and community engagement. This will contribute to enhancing emergency food production through the complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support. In integration/ Linkage of Nutrition and FSL will support Production of short cycle vegetables and crops through Kitchen gardens, yard gardens establishment as mentioned above. There will be training and capacity building on vegetable production and food utilization to caretakers/caregivers of children admitted in CMAM Programme. The project intends to integrate fish harvesting activities that can increase protein intakes and diet diversity. Nutrition activities will promote agricultural crops like vegetables, carbohydrates to nutrition program beneficiaries; Provide cooking demonstration using locally produced crops in nutrition programs and involving women and men and referring nutrition beneficiaries (families) to agriculture (FSL) program activities in Pibor County. Through nutrition education and behavior change communication it will sensitize the community to ensure that food production is consumed at the household level and thus assisting the Agriculture FSL sector in meeting nutrition objectives. The project will also refer beneficiaries to access foods that are energy, protein or micronutrient-rich, including fortified complementary foods. It is expected that through the integrated effort there will be decrease proportion of populations facing IPC 4 and 5.
