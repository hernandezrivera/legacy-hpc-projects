Project Summary

UNICEF proposes to enhance access to the conflict-affected population in Eastern Ukraine to quality maternal and child health services by strengthening capacity of health workers, increasing access to basic and essential Maternal and Child Health services, increasing awareness of IYCF practices and immunisation, and provision of supportive services for PLHIV, children and adolescents. UNICEF also proposes to respond to the current COVID-19 crisis in affected areas in Eastern Ukraine by supporting health facilities essentially through the procurement of supplies and equipment needed for the ongoing outbreak, as well as to provide facilities and health workers with skills, knowledge and resources for optimal infection prevention and control measures.

1 - Needs

With the ongoing crisis in Ukraine and the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak, quality of care and safe access to health care remain areas of critical concern, particularly along the contact lines of non-government (NGCA) and government-controlled areas (GCA). Health networks continue to face disruptions in provision of services and women and children face challenges accessing specialised care due to lack of supplies, drugs, qualified staff, and equipment, limited preventative care and infectious disease control, and lack of referral pathways. The COVID-19 outbreak is compounding challenges and identifying weaknesses of the national health care system. COVID-19 has exacerbated issues which were already present around ensuring a protective environment in healthcare facilities (HCF) for families, women, and children. Furthermore, the environment in HCFs for health workers poses a high risk, with a high number of infected people being medical workers, according to MOH and PHC statistics. Low infection control awareness, underequipped medical staff and lack of experience in hazardous environments have led to the onset of infection inside HCFs and the spread of healthcare-associated infections. Routine immunisation continues to be a concern nationwide, including in conflict-affected areas. Low coverage pre-conflict resulted in outbreaks of preventable diseases, including the ongoing measles outbreak. Stockouts of certain vaccines as well as health worker and public perceptions of vaccination continue to pose challenges to increasing coverage and ensure population immunity, highlighting the need for strengthened education and communication campaigns for caregivers, health workers, and the general public. The COVID-19 outbreak has led to challenges nationwide regarding immunisation with concerns about access and safe environments, with Eastern Ukraine and NGCA particularly affected by supply issues and freedom of movement. Infant and young child feeding practices in Ukraine are suboptimal, with only 19.7% of children exclusively breastfed until 6 months. Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) affected by conflict remain at risk of incorrect IYCF practices due to various conflict-related factors and inadequate counselling support. During the ongoing COVID-19 outbreak, it is crucial that PLW receive accurate information regarding IYCF practices from health workers, so that they are aware of the implications of separating mothers from children due to misperceived safety concerns. The main needs of NGCA are similar to those of GCA but pose additional operational challenges due to limited economic opportunities and restricted access. The conflict has increased vulnerability of the population to HIV/AIDS, especially with the increase in substance abuse, termination of substitution therapy for injecting drug users, limited access of children and adolescents to HIV preventative and treatment services. Increased poverty of PLHIV in conflict-affected areas bring more vulnerability and food insecurity to HIV-affected households, especially for babies born to HIV-positive mothers who cannot afford to purchase milk formula. The situation was worsened by COVID-19 lockdown causing limitations to preventive and treatment services, increasing poverty and high risk of COVID-19 transmission in facilities. The Global Fund provides antiretroviral treatment for over 10,000 PLHIV in NGCA, however, care and support services for the majority of PLHIV are not available. Stigma and discrimination remain concerns for the majority of PLHIV in conflict-affected areas. Although new AIDS centers in GCA partially developed capacity to implement HIV control programmes, they still remain weak, understaffed and underfinanced. Essential support case management for HIV/AIDS positive children and adolescents on ART, testing and prevention services, and support prevention of Mother-to Child transmission and Early Infant Diagnosis, in both NGCA and GCA remains an unmet need.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Objective 1: Children, adolescents and women equitably access essential Maternal and Child Health (MCH) and HIV/AIDS services in conflict-affected areas. - Activity 1.1: Provision of essential services and medicines, including e-vouchers, for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 and for HIV affected children and their families.- Activity 1.2: Prevent and respond to vaccine preventable diseases through targeted vaccination interventions.- Activity 1.3: Scale up programmes for HIV treatment adherence, including care and support services, for children and adolescents in conflict-affected areas. - Activity 1.4: Support conflict-affected regions towards elimination of HIV Mother-to-Child Transmission, including promotion of Early Infant Diagnosis (EID). Objective 2: Caregivers and parents in conflict-affected areas access behaviour-change communication of key MCH and promotional messages to improve health, hygiene, and nutrition practices, (including Early Childhood Development (ECD), Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF), emergency first aid, and immunisation). - Activity 2.1: Information outreach through local media and dissemination of MCH education materials, including ECD, IYCF and nutrition, for families, women, and children living in conflict-affected areas. - Activity 2.2: Training programmes and information materials for parents and caregivers distributed on MCH and ECD practices. Objective 3: Disrupted essential care services for women and children in facilities in conflict-affected areas are re-established through capacity development and provision of essential drugs, medical supplies, and equipment. - Activity 3.1: Needs assessment on gaps in healthcare facilities in GCA and NGCA. - Activity 3.2: Capacity building of health workers on Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses, evidence-based nutrition, immunisation, infection control, MCH and HIV/AIDS practices. - Activity 3.3: Provision of medical supplies and equipment to health facilities in GCA and NGCA, including cold chain equipment and vaccines.- Activity 3.4: Advocacy for implementation of Integrated Management of Childhood Illness at local government and health provider level.- Activity 3.5: Promoting of out-facility health care services using telemedicine and remote consultation approach at local and regional level. Objective 4: Technical support to national, regional and local healthcare system to bridge critical gaps caused by COVID-2019 outbreak at all levels of mother and child health care services, in GCA and NGCA. - Activity 4.1. Provision of necessary medical diagnostic and treatment equipment and supplies for health care facilities.- Activity 4.2. Capacity building of health managers and regional/local authorities, medical workers, in GCA and NGCA, to minimize the impact of COVID-19 on access to essential healthcare service.- Activity 4.3. Improve quality of referrals through linkages within healthcare system and including social and other non-medical services for children and families.
