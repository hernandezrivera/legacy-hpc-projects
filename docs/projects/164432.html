Project Summary

This project will be reduced child protection concerns including violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation through improving the access of at-risk girls and boys to response and preventive services. Those services include the provision of focused and non-focused psychosocial activities, awareness-raising, skill-building through static child-friendly spaces. In the same way, this project aims to Enhancing local capacity to provide child protection services through building the capacity of child protection teams, service providers including CFS personnel, and the establishment of local child protection committees/ networks and building their capacity on Child protection Program staff will be trained on PFA, communication skills, child protection, PSS, GBV disclosures, child safeguarding, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), ethical data management, and case management
The project targets about 2500 (1200 male and 1300 females) affected girls and boys 5 child-friendly spaces in Idleb and Aleppo governorates by protection activities and in the child protection component aims to reach about 1500 (700 males and 800 females)beneficiaries through 5 static child-friendly spaces which will provide secure, supportive environment and will be built on existing structures and capacities within the local communities. And since AHS working in the area the children will benefit from other services like health, nutrition, and education and will conduct service mapping in the area to refer the children if they need any help.

4 - Needs

The prolonged conflict in Syria significantly influenced the smallest unit of community in Syria, leading to normalizing negative coping mechanisms affecting the lives of children. Child rights continued to be violated, where thousands of children were killed and injured due to the increasing level of violence. According to Syria HNO, 2020 draft; child labour, family separation, child marriage, child recruitment, and Psychosocial distress were the most reported CP concerns, jeopardising the lives of boys and girls. Besides, HNO 2020 highlighted the difficulties children living with disabilities and children who do not have birth certificates face in access to their rights and services.According to a rapid need assessment conducted by AHS in January 2020, there is a continuing need to provide child protection services, where there are thousands of boys and girls, living at risk of different types of protection concerns, including lack/loss of personal documents, child labor, early marriage domestic violence, harassment, and the lack of psychosocial support services.Increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to prevail in Syria, resulting from a variety of situations ranging from direct exposure to hostilities, displacement, conditions in sites/collective shelters and protracted displacement. Girls and boys of all ages continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis. Continuous displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty and the persistent lack of access to services and even the most basic necessities, including in newly accessible areas, are taking a huge toll on children. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. A rapid needs assessment conducted by AHS 2019 in project locations found that a constant feeling of fear for the well-being of their family members permeates all members of society, causing family members to be very irritable most of the time.

5 - Implementation of activities

AHS will provide support to safe spaces within schools/TLSs and static child-friendly spaces which will carry out structured, non-focused, interactive and supervised psycho-social activities for children from 6 to 14 years old.Those activities will be conducted directly by trained AHS PSS workers. AHS will rehabilitate those Safe Spaces and CFSs in the first implementation period. AHS will make sure those spaces are accessible by children living with disabilities. The activities will include the provision of “I Deal” curriculum for Psycho-social Support activities and life skills, play and recreational activities for children; awareness-raising on preventing violence and separation; how to recognize and avoid unexploded ordinance; and non-formal education for children.AHS will be utilizing the teams operating the child-friendly spaces, to provide community awareness-raising sessions for parents, community members, etc., on child protection related concerns including but not limited to child labor, early marriage, school dropout, lack of personal documentation, domestic violence, and risks of explosive remnants of war. During those awareness-raising sessions, AHS team will be discussing the reasons for these concerns, results and the prevention and response to these concerns. The activities will promote individual and collective support through art, music, drama, sports, and story-telling, and will employ culturally sensitive action methods, as well as games and activities aiming to develop a feeling of safety, trust, connection, communication, relationship, collaboration, play and imagination.Following a needs assessment with input and feedback from male and female adolescents in project locations, mixed-gender staff will facilitate awareness-raising sessions for adolescents including topics such as how to recognize and deal with stress, how to deal with everyday problems caused by living in prolonged conflict, in addition to meal activities will be in place.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. Protection Risk: Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Protection Risk: Protection risk faced by girls and boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: the security situation is stable and helps to deliver the project services. Risks: Security situation deteriorates (airstrikes, shelling, inter-fighting between NSAGs) and control over the area of operation changes which might cause a delay in implementing the activities: Mitigation: AHS may suspend some activities till the situation is stable. The staff is trained in and adhere to security protocols. AHS employs program and monitoring staff from the area so they can move more freely.Risk: Safety and security risks project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: The organization has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place.Risk: Staff and beneficiaries might be attacked or injured causing attrition or limits to the implementation of activities. Mitigation: All the activities that AHS implements are based on the skills and competencies of the field staff inside Syria along with strengthened support from Senior staff based in Turkey. Access: AHS maintains a good relationship with the communities it operates in allowing access and movement of staff from and to project location easily. Coordination: AHS coordinates its project activities with NGOs, through UN-led clusters as well as through direct coordination with NGOs and local authorities. Risk: risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of case management if service providers are not adequately trained.
