Project Summary

This project will reduce risks posed by explosive hazards, enabling the affected population to safely access productive land, natural resources and basic services. Additionally, the project aims to provide assistance to landmine/ERW survivors for socioeconomic reintegration. 30 known hazardous areas will be surveyed and cleared, securing access to 5,000,000 square meters of priority land for safe movement of refugees and IDPs and/or productive use by the local populations. 240,000 vulnerable populations will learn how to adopt safe behaviors in landmine contaminated areas. 190 landmine/ERW survivors and other persons with disabilities (PWD) will be empowered by gaining physical mobility and means for livelihood. Through this project, the United Nations Mine Action Service (UNMAS) will support the Government of Sudan to meet its commitment to the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC) by strengthening the institutional capacity of the National Mine Action Center (NMAC). In this endeavor, the project will continue to build the national capacity in mine action by providing training to national partners including NMAC and non-governmental organization (NGO) staff members.

Needs

The presence of landmines and ERW poses a great threat to the population to sustain livelihoods and gain access to basic human needs such as education, water and health services. 23,420,232 square meters of land in South Kordofan and 1,488,601 square meters in Blue Nile states are affected with landmines and ERW as of December 2018. This has worsened the already poor humanitarian situation of the two states, caused by ongoing conflict and displacement. Since 2017, a huge influx of IDPs was observed in the two states moving from the opposition-controlled areas to government-held regions in search of food and basic services, while refugees, escaping from war in South Sudan come to take refuge in the two states. That trend of displacement continued in 2018 without improvement. The danger that IDPs, returnees, host communities and refugees face is imminent as they walk through the contaminated land without knowing the risks of explosive hazards. With limited mobility due to landmines, people are faced with persistent barriers to secure livelihoods, food and other social services that satisfy their basic needs, such as education and health. Deploying mine action in the area will not only remove the existing barriers and increase safe access to basic livelihood for vulnerable population, but also enable timely humanitarian assistance and pave the route for development and also facilitate responses to the affected people's need regardless of their gender.In Darfur, five states were severely affected by civil war during 2003 -2011, including vast areas of land contaminated with UXO. UNAMID is present in Darfur since 2007 with a mandate on protection of civilians, facilitation of humanitarian assistance, and mediation among the parties to the armed conflict. In accordance with Security Council Resolution 2429, UNAMID is going to withdraw from sectors East, West, South and North by June 2019, handing over its tasks to the UNCT, including UNMAS Sudan. To fill in the operational gap during the transition, UNMAS Sudan is undergoing a process to extend its current mandate to cover the Darfur states. The highest number of UXO accidents in Sudan was observed in Darfur in the last few years, with increasing number of accidents in recent months, what is believed to be caused by the negative implication of the forced arms collection campaign. Men, boys, women and girls are affected differently by crisis and by ERW. Men and boys make around 88% of the killed and injured in Sudan based on IMSMA system, but, compared to men, injured women and girls are disadvantaged when it comes to assistance: less likely to have access to immediate healthcare, so more likely to die as result of injuries, less likely to have access to information about the kind of assistance and opportunities available for survivals, to find a partner and job opportunities, while more likely to face isolation and stigma.

Activities

-Identify and record areas with landmine and ERW contamination through NTS and TS-Conduct verification and/or clearance of routes suspected to be contaminated by landmines and ERW -Conduct survey and removal of ERWs through EOD spot tasks.-Release 5 million sqm of land previously contaminated by landmines/ERW through survey and clearance operation-Conduct mine risk education (MRE) through direct presentations and community-based MRE to 140,000 persons in mine/ERW affected communities-Print MRE material for distribution during MRE session-Broadcast MRE radio campaign to reach 100,000 persons in affected communities and IDPs in restricted and remote areas-Advocate with relevant authorities and institutions to support and endorse the Sudanese Strategic Framework for Victim Assistance and multi-year work plan to provide direct provision of services to PWD including victims of landmines and ERW-Provide necessary physical and psychological supports including prosthesis and assistive devices, rehabilitation and counseling to 190 PWD, including survivors of landmine/ERW accidents-Deploy survey team to collect and register victims data particularly including the identification of under-reported cases-Provide income generation support to address socioeconomic issues of the survivors that include vocational trainings, microeconomic workshops and provision of business start-up material-Promote positive perceptions and greater social awareness towards PWD through awareness campaign and advocacy with government entities-Coordinate with other relevant actors to establish/strengthen a referall system for victims of GBV, promoting the concept of victims as agents of change and involving them in the design of activities -Coordination and monitoring of MA operations in Sudan-Regular participation of the Sudan’s delegation in International Meetings and Events-EOD level 2 and level 3 training-Provide the required support to strengthen the capacity of NMAC in management

Impact on environment/natural resources

UNMAS will ensure that mine action activities are carried out in compliance with the National Technical Standards and Guidelines (NTSG) and International Mine Action Standards (IMAS) for what concerns environmental preservation. UNMAS and NMAC will identify and assess relevant environmental aspects and determine appropriate and effective measures to mitigate adverse impacts of explosive detonations on soil, air and immediate surrounding environment. This will include the inclusion of environmental requirements in the selection of implementing partners and their incorporation in SoP, proposals, and quality assurance monitoring plans.
