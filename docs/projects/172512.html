1 - Needs

Coordination and health information management are essential as the nature of humanitarian needs across the country has not changed. Reliance on lifesaving and life-sustaining health care services supported by the humanitarian response will continue across the country in 2021. While IDPs, refugees and migrants have the most severe needs, returnees and non-displaced Libyans in the worst-affected areas also need humanitarian assistance. Other vulnerable groups include children and adolescents, the elderly, patients with chronic health conditions and families facing economic hardship. Health sector Libya is comprised of 27 actors, including 11 INGOs, 6 UN agencies, 1 national authority, 7 donors and 2 observers. There is a national health sector coordination group, led and co-lead by MoH ICO (International Cooperation Office) in Tripoli and health sector coordinator, monthly meetings. There are 2 active sub-national health sector groups at: Sabha hub – led and co-lead by MoH/NCDC and WHO national staff, monthly meetings; organized in February 2019; Benghazi hub/Al Baida – led and co-lead by MoH Interim Government and WHO national staff, monthly meetings; organized in November 2018. There are five thematic sub-sector working groups: Gender-Based Violence –led by UNFPA; Mental Health/Psychosocial Service Support –led by IOM and HI; Tuberculosis – led by WHO; Sexual and Reproductive Health – led by UNFPA; Migration – led by IOM. 2020 (health assessments) - top challenges, included: Fragmented health sector institutions; Poor governance; Lack of accountability and transparency on the allocation of funds and distribution of medicines, equipment and supplies from national to municipal levels; Acute shortages of health care facilities, staff, medicines and supplies across the country; More than half of the health care facilities that were functioning in 2019 have since closed, especially in rural areas (security threats, lack of national and health sector funding). Over 1 million people in 72 municipalities have acute and immediate humanitarian needs. On the severity scale, 58 of these municipalities are classified as 3, 12 are classified as 4, and 2 are classified as 5 in severity scale. The health sector should target 16 municipalities in the east, 14 municipalities in the south and 42 municipalities in the west - classified at 3 and above on the severity scale. In 2020 health sector reached all 22 districts; 40-50% of 100 municipalities; 35-40% of reached municipalities are in areas of severity scale classified 3 or above. Health sector response involves an estimated of 28 different type of activities. 46 communities in 3 districts (Al Jufra, Misrata, Sirte) remained non-accessible due to a mix of reasons. The absence of an approved COVID-19 national preparedness and response plan has resulted in a fragmented, uncoordinated response in an already poor health system. Response capacities have been created around only Tripoli and Benghazi while neglecting vast geographical areas. As a result, there is no country-wide system to gather information and monitor and assess needs, response capacities and funding requirements. Moreover, national resources to establish COVID-19 treatment centres and isolation facilities have been allocated to private health facilities, which has diverted limited funds and led to increased public anger over the lack of transparency and accountability. In Libya, the weak capacity of the national health information system has hampered efforts to gather overall data on the burden of disease, the prevalence and main causes of morbidity and mortality, and the status (accessibility, availability) of health care services across the country. When a proper health information management system is fully up and running, it will allow the health sector to have a better understanding of the evolving health situation in the country. Overall monitoring and evaluation should be enhanced.

2 - Activities

Health information management for health sector Libya (including UN agencies and NGOs) will be strengthened through: Produce monthly 4W health sector snapshot (12) and interactive dashboard; Produce bi-weekly health sector operational updates; Distribute the minutes of national and sub-national health sector meetings; Lead technical working group of IM focal points of health sector; Develop and share monthly (12) health sector monitoring framework with OCHA and RO under 2021 HRP (including Periodic Monitoring Report); Maintain and update health sector assessment and capacity-building registries; Lead production of Flash Appeals on attacks on health care; Provide monthly inputs for COVID-19 response to inter-sector OCHA reporting tool on COVID-19 response; Develop standardized assessment templates for facility- and community-based assessments by health sector; Update health sector partners’ list on a quarterly basis; Provide IM support for production of monthly health sector bulletins; Maintain maximum number of IM deliverables under PHIS (Public Health Information Services) tool kit. Strengthened and supported health information management of national authorities: Provide technical guidance in the implementation of the routine health information system (at least 12 technical support meetings with HIS national team); Provide coaching to HIS national team in the implementation and use of relevant health information system (at least 6 health information system trainings will be conducted); Day-to-day technical support provided to the HIS center of the MoH and NCDC; Support assessments/surveys of health facilities to identify the main needs of health resources and services (e.g. HeRAMS, etc.); Conduct quarterly public health resources and services monitoring gap analysis; Oversee and design visualization of the results including mapping and infographics (COVID-19 updates; DHIS-2; etc.); Disseminate national guidelines on various subjects.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

The sector will ensure to have a balanced approach between different parties to the conflict. The emphasis of health sector coordination will be to empower health response on the ground, at a community level. The operational framework developed for health sector will enable tracking down humanitarian and development defined activities but with clear reference to “severity scale” approach. A quarterly review of severity scale prioritization will be produced with development of necessary recommendations. Rapid response mechanism should be more effectively continued via different modalities, including replenishment of health supplies, support to the referral hospitals, and deployment of mobile health teams. Health sector will emphasize inclusion of vulnerable and minority groups needs throughout the program cycle through gender, age, and disability disaggregated analysis of beneficiaries with identification of risk factors impacting service provision. Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicated health subgroups groups (trauma, physical and mental rehabilitation, sexual and reproductive health, etc.). Regular revision and updating of regular, contingency, and emergency plans with emphasis on Do No Harm principles. Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict. Affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP). Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

The health sector will promote considering accountability to affected populations to be a cornerstone of quality service provision to meet the affected populations evolving needs and to deliver more sustainable outcomes through community engagement. The COVID-19 response has allowed the health sector to strengthen community engagement across the country. Health operational plans include the public health measures that are required based on the situation in different parts of the country. The health sector carries out behavioral assessments to understand target audiences, perceptions, concerns, influencers and preferred communication channels. In 2020, the health sector tested health messages on trusted community groups including community and religious leaders, health workers, community volunteers, migrants and refugees, and youth, business and women’s groups. It has built an extensive network of media contacts to disseminate its message by means of TV, radio, the Internet, newspapers and other mechanisms. Health information messages and materials were adapted and communicated in both local languages and those of the main migrant and refugee populations. These activities will be continued in 2021. The health sector will also implement training workshops on community engagement through public health and community-based networks, media, schools and universities, national and local governments and other sectors. Established community information and feedback mechanisms including the Common Feedback Mechanism and social media monitoring (Facebook and Twitter) will be further strengthened. The health sector will report against 30 indicators, including 2 for inter-sectoral monitoring. All health sector updates are available in public domain: https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/libya/health
