Project Summary

Yo’ Care South Sudan’s life-saving essential health project for 2023 South Sudan HRP is aligned to South Sudan Health Cluster’s proposed strategic objectives SO1, SO2 and SO3 for the period 2022/2023 which are aligned with the HNO/ HRP South Sudan.
The project will address critical humanitarian needs in selected priority Counties of Cueibet (level 5) in Lakes State and Mayom (level 4) in Unity State by ensuring effective coordination, complementarity and integration of development and humanitarian nexus in all areas of collaboration with State Ministries of Health (SMoH) and their respective CHDs and with other partners in order to narrow the health care provision gaps in the most remote areas and for the most neglected groups.
This will be done by proposing a range of differentiated services provision modalities (static and mobile), expected to be able to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current health care coverage at all levels of the health system and/or resulting from emergency events and therefore to respond to the essential needs of the target population, in particular of those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) make them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging.
For this purpose, health related activities will be integrated with those pertaining to the protection sector, through the opening and sustained reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both clinical care and psychosocial support. In general, project impact will be maximized through a multisectoral approach, with the result of services being highly comprehensive, as addressing health conditions and the inter- related complications (i.e. malnutrition), and offering quality care, being informed by hygiene promotion and IPC principles, becoming the first setting for an effective prevention of the main infectious diseases.
In this regard, the project will guarantee timely and effective prevention, containment and management of outbreaks (COVID19, Cholera, EVD, etc.). Surveillance and early warning will be strengthened, with the engagement and coordination of various stakeholders.
In general, the project will promote the proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities and this will facilitate the selection and implementation of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable intervention options, focusing on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries.
The project is expected to reach 13,168 people in Cueibet County, and 3,499 people in Mayom County, with at least 4% of all beneficiaries being people with disability (658) in both counties.

1 - Needs

The needs identified and their proposed solutions in this 2023 HRP project proposal by Yo’ Care are based on HNO 2022 issued by OCHA and the 2023 HNO Narrative issued by the South Sudan Health Cluster. Cueibet County in Lakes State and Mayom County in Unity Stare are considered priority humanitarian setting in the 2022 health cluster severity classification at level 5 (Cueibet) and 4 (Mayom) respectively: In Cueibet County in Lakes State, people in need are the 53% (n=94461) of County population, whereas in Mayom County, people in needs are approximately 7% (n=6998) of the County population. Both Cueibet and Mayom Counties are being heavily affected by conflicts and flooding in 2022. The persisting high internal and cross-border conflicts in both Counties have hampered access to and utilization quality basic health services. The conflicts have resulted into internal displacement of people as more than 12,000 IDPs are in Cueibet and 687 IDPs in Mayom (Displacement Tracking Matrix and ISNA assessments, 2022). Matter of concern is the IDPs’ burden over host families whose resources are limited because of raids/lootings and environmental factors. This is even more concerning when considering that, due to the continuous fighting, the large majority of households are headed by women, more exposed to violence and likely to be food insecure. Despite the IDPs vulnerability, they are predisposed to diseases in the face of inadequate and inequitable health facilities distribution, and poor staffing conditions (staff number and skills, equipment, supplies), with insufficient funding to sustain routine services, and failure to offer due preventive and curative functions. Health indicators for both Cueibet and Mayom Counties are low (2021 Annual Health Sector Performance Review Report and ISNA 2022): In Cueibet, the health indicators are low:• ANC4+ (21.3%), stillbirth attendance (16.6%), OPD utilization (37.7%), and Penta3 (81.1%). In Mayom, the health indicators are also low as follows:• ANC4+ (41.7%), stillbirth attendance (11.9%), and OPD utilization (61.3%).

2 - Project activities

As indicated in the strategic approach above, Yo’ Care ‘s activities are aligned to Health Cluster strategic objectives to achieve its sectorial objectives and respond to the needs illustrated above. They will be implemented in selected PHC facilities/hospital and villages, agreed with Health Cluster, after needs assessment and discussion with beneficiaries in both Counties. Priority will be given to those areas where existing services are not sufficient to cope with care and demand, as neglected by common funding mechanisms or overloaded because of IDPs/outbreaks/flooding/violence. Activity 1: Provision of comprehensive, integrated and quality preventive and curative primary health care services at the static facility level (PHCC and PHCU). This activity is aligned with the Health Cluster Strategic Objective (SO1), and Yo’ Care focus will be on the availability of basic life-saving services – such as for prompt management common endemic infections like malaria, pneumonia and diarrheal diseases. All this will be realized by addressing the main gaps reported above as hindering services provision such as: • Hiring/rewarding/training/supervising health staff and BHWs/CHWs; • Procuring/distributing essential drugs and supplies buffer stock, complementing what provided in kind by other actors; • Replacing old/ looted equipment and repairing damaged infrastructures to restore enabling conditions for services provision; • Ensuring transport means availability/running. CHD will conduct regular supportive supervisions to targeted sites. Activity 2: Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including epidemic control and management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of healthcare workers. This activity aligned with Health Cluster Strategic Objective 2 (SO2), and Yo’ Care will focus on outbreak prevention, detection, investigation, and integrated response. UNICEF/WHO will be advocated for regular antigens provision and cold chain maintenance to ensure sustained outbreaks prevention of vaccine-preventable diseases. Surveillance continuity/consistency will be sustained while strengthening IDSR. Key strategic interventions under this activity will includes; • Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives/ICCM. • Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population.• Review and update the emergency preparedness and response plan for malaria/measles/ cholera/meningitis and hepatitis E outbreak in these counties. • Capacity building of health staff and CHWs to address various primary health care topics including but not limited to management of common illness, management of non-communicable diseases, mhGAP, surveillance, but also provide screening services for cancers (cervical and breast cancers). Activity 3: Data-driven decision making and stakeholders engagement in both Counties (Cueibet and Mayom). Under this interventional approach, closely aligned with the Health Cluster Strategic Objective 3 (SO3), Yo’ Care will participate in weekly/bi-weekly data and performance review meetings with both the health cluster team, health authorities and prospective donor (s) and any identified challenges/gaps will be addressed in collaboration with these stakeholders and the Health Cluster team. Yo’ Care will submit monthly/quarterly 5Ws data for selected facilities to HC and/or DHIS2 reporting. Yo’ Care will also write and submit monthly and quarterly reports to its donors. Yo’ Care will strengthen disease surveillance through completeness and timely reporting of IDSR and EWARs in identified health facilities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Yo’ Care South Sudan works in close collaboration and coordination with the County Health Departments (CHDs) in both Counties. Similarly, Review Meetings and community-based activities will be open to local associations representing women, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups. Boma Health Workers or other community-based health providers will bring services as much close as possible to beneficiaries, minimizing access constraints faced by who is not in condition to freely move because of physical, social and economic dependence, such as women and PWDs and offering these people the opportunity to provide their feedbacks and inputs. Yo' Care will use anonymous Clients Satisfaction Survey which has been designed and administered in a way that makes it accessible to all kind of people, independently on their education background (usually poor for the most vulnerable and neglected as women and PWDs) and without requiring time wasting and further movements. Use of suggestions box will be deployed in all health facilities run by Yo’ Care South Sudan.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project will be driven through existing community engagement mechanisms (community dialogues, quarterly partners meeting and engagement meetings, use of suggestion box at health facilities, publication of our reports in public domains, and client satisfaction survey). The same mechanisms will be used to get feedback and complaints and to make it easier for the beneficiaries to participate in decision making on how to improve health services provision to them. Yo’ Care is used to maintain a constant dialogue with community representatives; from health committees to community-based associations who are invited to take part in monthly micro-planning meetings at the facility levels and Quarterly Review Meetings held at County levels. This is to ensure wider perspectives on community needs and expectations and to strengthen service responsiveness and reinforce acceptability. The quarterly Client Satisfaction Survey offers the opportunity to get feedback on provided services to direct beneficiaries. It is administered at service delivery sites, meaning it is easily accessible. It is filled by literate volunteers so that it can be administered to any patient without discrimination based on education level (which would have left behind the most vulnerable) and it has been piloted (such as in the USAID/AHEC Activity) to confirm all questions being easy to understand and to be answered by people with different backgrounds. Finally, Yo’ Care will support local health authorities in managing Countywide networks of community-based health providers (Boma Health Workers) operating at grassroots level, directly in contact with beneficiaries and therefore in the position to inform them about the project and to get their feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Yo’ Care South Sudan has its own PSEA policy. The related communication strategy consists in integrating PSEA information dissemination into the ongoing activities aimed to sensitize people on Gender related matters, including Gender Equality, Gender Empowerment, SGBV as presently done with our USAID/AHEC Activity. These activities include mandatory health staff training on PSEAH, raising awareness about PSEAH in community meetings, PSEAH IEC materials production and dissemination during events and activities which are part of Yo’ Care in the ongoing interventions. Integrating PSEA information in this framework is expected to facilitate its understanding and to promote its acceptability.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be collected through community inception meetings and consultation mechanism, already existing and regularly used to get population perspective and perceptions on services responsiveness and acceptability. These consultation mechanisms have been designed to get continuous inputs on the situation on ground and to enable timely adaptations of the proposed activities/supported services. As already used to do, Yo’ Care field team will assist the local health authorities in collecting, consolidating and analyzing various feedbacks and to translate them into action points to be integrated into proposed project work plan and overarching strategy, making them more tailored and focused to respond to the needs and to address the issues beneficiaries perceived as priorities in that specific moment. This is also functional to ensure project constant update and alignment to the reality, narrowing the gap created by the time passed from project writing and project implementation.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Health services can be victims’ entry point to specific assistance (clinical, psychosocial, legal). Yo’ Care will sensitize and train health staff on how to identify and handle this kind of patients, offering first line care while encouraging their adherence to therapeutic remedies. Health authorities will be updated on protection actors and supported in linking with them; establishing effective referral pathways for victims’ comprehensive attendance. Yo’ Care will advocate for Health authorities and staff to promote countywide sensitization and education on these matters. Mobile modality for Clinical Services provision will enable marginalized and isolated groups to access health care close to their living places free of charge. Community representatives/BHWs will provide Health authorities with information on these groups' locations and status and for promoting their acceptance within local population. Rights-based approach will inform capacity building/training activities for health staff and authorities for all patients being attended. Yo’ Care and CHD will promote gender balanced composition of HF/Boma health committees through community-wide sensitization. Women inclusion will create a virtuous circle of increasing awareness on the importance of all groups contributing to health system running. Health services enhanced accessibility will make women healthier and released from their caretaker role, enabling them to engage in public functions and tasks. Proposed project complaint/feedback mechanism will coincide with that already in place and in which targeted communities are already familiar with. Further, health authorities and staff will be advised and assisted in taking advantage of each contact with beneficiaries to ask and get inputs on activities/support responsiveness and reinforce acceptability. For instance, client satisfaction survey will be done at the same place/during the same moment people are getting services, in a way educational background will not constitute an obstacle to a wide participation in it. Yo’ Care is working with targeted Counties' health authorities providing the needed technical assistance and mentorship for the development of evidence-based decision-making culture and approach. This constitutes a solid ground to prevent external interference in the identification of project beneficiaries which will take place through a wisely conducted field needs assessment, with the direct involvement of local health authorities and community representatives and the use of validated tools, to be confirmed with the competent Cluster.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will support essential preventive and curative health services, promoting them as entry point for protection ones, especially for women and children and people with disabilities. The project will enhance health services accessibility, by providing them as much close as possible to beneficiaries, overcoming those geographical/economical/social barriers which are more penalizing for vulnerable people and groups; in this way, health providers will be in the best position to capture these latter and to facilitate their entrance in the essential services net. Health staff will be trained to promptly identify patients with physical or intellectual impairment and/or who experienced GBV and provide them with the most suitable first aid, while arranging for their referral or for more structured treatment, according to facility/health provider capacities and competencies. The staff will also learn how and will be given the means to make health facilities as well as mobile health stations as much accessible and acceptable as possible, starting from infrastructural barriers reduction up to developing a right based approach in receiving and treating patients, creating an enabling and comfortable environment for those who are used or fear to be discriminated/stigmatized because of their conditions. Yo’ Care and health authorities will ensure the due coordination and cooperation with protection sector actors and stakeholders, creating structured and effective referral pathways for people reaching health facilities/being reached by health providers to have prompt access to all other services they are in need of for a comprehensive management of their conditions.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will contribute minimizing displacement by guaranteeing the provision of integrated GBV programming into health services in the areas affected by emergencies events and therefore giving affected population a reason to remain on ground or at least addressing and narrowing some of the gaps that would lead people migrating somewhere else. Similarly, it will complement existing services, making them more responsive to inhabitants' needs and therefore preventing them from looking for other places where to access basic healthcare services.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project does not directly address food security; however it will contribute to minimize its immediate consequences in terms of malnutrition and its complications. On one side, the project will support the provision of essential care as per Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services, therefore integrating, through staff training and mentorship and equipment/supplies provision, nutrition component main functions into the health offer, as growth monitoring and/or MUAC screening for children accessing OPD and EPI and for PLWH at ANC/PNC and consequent referral to SAM/MAM treatment program. In this way, health services will become the entry point to nutrition services for malnourished children, who will be timely identified and transferred to receive the due care. Further, health providers will include nutrition related topics into the health education sessions program, contributing to malnutrition prevention. Finally, health authorities will get a good amount of data on patients nutrition status to take informed decision on how to improve it. On the other side, the project will guarantee children overall health improvement, promoting their access and utilization to quality preventive and curative health services and therefore it will make them less vulnerable to malnutrition. The already mentioned health education session will transmit and spread good behaviors and practices as those related to wash and hygiene, which are functional to protect from both water borne diseases and malnutrition.
