Project Summary

Overall, the humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the most complex and longstanding emergencies in the world. The fragility of Somalia over the past 27 years has resulted in weakening of the public sector, its systems and personnel, with a subsequent focus on emergency response interventions to recurrent crisis. According to the FSNAU nutrition report, food security situation in Somalia is estimated to experience Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or (IPC Phase 4) for 2.2 million people outcomes through September and are urgently in need of humanitarian food assistance and interventions to prevent high levels of acute malnutrition. This number could increase as the dry season progresses. The average number of beneficiaries reached with food assistance from January to May 2019 declined 47 percent compared to those reached from August to December 2018.
Hence, this food crisis will contribute to increase the high acute malnutrition, high disease burden, severe food consumption gaps, and protracted displacement, loss of livelihood, and ongoing conflict and insecurity. The project will focus on considering responding the needs in which drought and conflict were caused including losses of lives and suffering the prolonged crisis. Responding these protracted crisis will eventually contribute to minimize and reduce the morbidity and mortality of the affected communities with special focus on children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating women through the provision of life saving emergency nutrition services by establishing mobile OTPs supported by a network of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs). The interventions are in line with the HRP of 2020 for Somalia that has components on responding to the severe malnutrition and drought affected population that has priorities set by the Humanitarian Technical Clusters and Somalia Humanitarian Fund (SHF) for emergency interventions. INTERSOS is proposing in considering to the country strategic plan of nutrition response to scale up its interventions through supporting 1 ﬁxed facility and 2 mobile OTPs in every district with a network of CNVs in Bay and Middle Shabelle which INTERSOS has been providing emergency response for more than two decades. INTERSOS were running integrated health and nutrition projects into all above regions, in the meantime, there are three ongoing projects targeting into Jowhar, Mahadaay of Middle Shabelle and Diinsoor of Bay region through provision of OPD consultations, vaccinations, Reproductive health, Screening of U5, PLWs, OTP admission and SC for all referred severe malnourished children. The Stabilization center and ﬁxed facilities will act as referral point for cases requiring observation and further referral for secondary health services. The referral is done from the outreach clinics to the fixed facilities for admission of SC in severe malnutrition with complication cases and for delivery or other inpatient healthcare for other severe cases who has been referred from other partners to diagnosis further and hospitalization. More so, INTERSOS will engage 24 CVs in every district of implementation. The Nutrition Package includes screening of under ﬁve children and PLWs immunization (for both routine and supplementary vaccines), deworming, vitamin A supplementation, treatment for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases, provision of iMAM messages on caregivers through health education, sanitation and hygiene promotion, and distribution of hygiene kits. INTERSOS will integrate components of Infant young Child Feeding (IYCF) in nutrition treatment and prevention. UNICEF and WFP would provide nutrition supplies for both SAM and MAM management. The project will serve to a total number of 206,720 beneficiaries in all the three different regions.
In addition, INTERSOS will ensure to respond the prevention of diarrhea and AWD outbreak in providing hygiene promotion and health education sessions to the community.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement Some of the major risks facing the targeted population includes incidents of Gender-based violence (GBV), especially in IDP sites. Abuses against civilians, including widespread sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), limitations on the freedom of movement, social exclusion of marginalized communities, forced evictions and secondary displacement remain a pervasive feature of the conﬂict in targeted locations. INTERSOS will ensure meaningful representation of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities) and play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination. Through community, engagement and provision of services at community level will actively ensure to include all target beneficiaries in an equal opportunity to serve for and provide the interventions. INTERSOS will collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender in a sense of understanding the served beneficiaries with their specific age groups and gender to mitigate in the upcoming projects to integrate the lessons learnt during the project implementation. INTERSOS will identify groups who face diﬃculties accessing services (e.g. women, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons, persons from minority groups, persons belonging to marginalized groups) and work with community leaders, religious leaders and service providers to support equal and safe access to services without discrimination. INTERSOS will identify the power dynamics in the area by focusing on who has access to service, who manages the facilities and who is vulnerable and marginalized. INTERSOS will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. Considering this when determining locations for interventions. Furthermore, INTERSOS ﬁeld staffs will undertake training's on protection mainstreaming with the support of INTERSOS Protection department. Mitigating the challenges to complaint and feedback mechanism by putting complain suggestion boxes, free hotlines to pass messages and calls for all beneficiaries in an equal manner to provide their concerns with highly confidential system. The project will Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to the needs speciﬁcally by consulting women, men, boys, girls, youth, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized groups to understand their needs, coping strategies and preferences for protection services. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect the targeted population, to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. INTERSOS will set up accessible, conﬁdential and locally understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promote beneﬁciary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct eg. Feedback box in centers. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of INTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address areas of the project weakness thus improving risks management in relation of the delivery of services.
