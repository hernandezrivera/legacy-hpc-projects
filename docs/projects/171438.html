Project Summary

The extended crises in the target areas, resulting from natural and man-made disasters have disproportionately affected women and girls including those with disability. Due to the displacements, social structures have been broken and women and girls’ support systems disrupted, exacerbating women and girls’ vulnerability to shocks and risk of violence both at home and in the community. Women have increasingly become heads of households, and main bread winners for their families, and gender roles have shifted putting women in a position where they have increased burden as they play double gender roles. This has put them at more risk as they often travel far from the shelters in search of work, food and water. For the male headed households, the risks of intimate partner violence (IPV) directed at women has been heightened as women are expected to do more than their traditional gender roles, including contribution to family income, the diminished incomes have resulted into high tensions at the household and women find themselves at the risk of IPV. Coping mechanisms that negatively affect girls like early forced marriages are employed to enable the family to get some income to cope with the economic shocks have increased, affecting girls’ education and development. This is coupled with congestion in the IDP settlements that increase GBV risks for women and girls and those with disabilities, left behind alone in unsafe surroundings women and girls with disability experience multiple forms of GBV referring to their physical or mental disability curtailing their free movement and confining them around the shelters, which in themselves are not secure enough to protect them from risks of sexual violence, or deny them access to vital services. This proposed project seeks to address these challenges and increase the protection of women and girls and those who with disability from exposure of risks.

Project Activities

1. Establish physical support centres (safe spaces for women and girls including those with disability): GBV experiences leads to psychological wounds that are harder to heal than bodily injuries Survivors of GBV may have intense negative feelings in the outcome or long after the violence, most of the survivors experience severe emotional disturbance during the violence struggling with anxiety, flashbacks, and trust issues they express instant behaviour change feeling, guilty, shame, isolation and loneliness that leads distress and negative thoughts which likely interferes with daily life functioning or even a bodily sensation such as pain or fatigue anxiety. Despite the provision of basic emotional support and physical care in the case management process, some of the survivors might not cope with the basic emotional and counselling sessions offered by the case workers and demand further psychosocial intervention. 2. Community awareness creation on protection issues: The project will increase community engagement on GBV prevention and will host community structures, including community based prevention networks (CBPNs), community leaders’ councils, gatekeepers, and psychological first aid volunteers (PFA , to develop action plans on community mobilization and community sensitization to reduce GBV risks for women and girls. CARE will also continue sensitize community and religious leaders through forums on GBV to encourage them to apply their power and influence in a positive way to promote the safety and wellbeing of women and girls, and support them to access services if they experience GBV. Community mobilization activities will be led by the GBV/FP volunteers under the supervision of the case workers.3. Strengthen access to complaint and feedback mechanisms: CARE will also ensure accountability of the interventions provided by strengthening the existing mechanisms through which survivors and effected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions. CARE will address any concerns or complaints for further action. Those mechanisms will include separate hotline for GBV service beneficiaries, installation of FCRM boxes, conducting client satisfaction survey at the end of case management which will assess the survivor’s satisfaction with the services provided, assess staff attitudes towards clients charged with provision of services, as well as glean information on how to improve the already existing services. CARE will conduct listening sessions separately with women and adolescent girls attending safe spaces to have their feedback on the program implementation and design. 4. Survivor centred approach to GBV Case Management along with Distribution of dignity kits: This is a multiple response activity which is in line with CARE’s priority (case management, including referral, action planning, and development of safety plans, etc.) and IASC minimum standards (Minimum Standard 6: GBV case management). CARE will provide GBV response services to the survivors of SGBV including case management, psychosocial first aid and psychosocial support, distribution of dignity kits and clinical management of rape among others. The response services will be offered from the women and girls’ safe spaces which are one stop spaces for all CARE GBV services in the community and where a service is not available; referrals are made to other agencies (service providers) in the referral continuum. Locally contextualized dignity kit with basic hygiene items will be distributed.5. Protection Mainstreaming: CARE will design and implement sector interventions while ensuring that protection mainstreaming is incorporated throughout. The proposed interventions will not discriminate between genders, giving priority to women, girls, men and boys equally with the general public Services will be provided free of charge, ensuring poor and marginalized members of the community and persons living with disability can access services at no extra cost.

Mitigation Measures

During international days, like the 16 Days of Activism campaign, CARE will collaborate with other actors, including the relevant government ministries and local organizations, to mobilize and sensitize the community on the consequences of GBV, referral pathways and available GBV services including case management, clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), psychosocial support), all offered by CARE, and legal aid and safe shelters offered by other protection actors. The project will engage youth networks for accountable practices to address gender, power and the role they can play as agents of change as allies in GBV prevention by providing guidance and mentorship. The project will employ community based approaches that build on existing structures like community based prevention networks (CBPNs) composed of men, women and youth, together with community and religious leaders, CARE will develop and implement survivor-centered practices when dealing with GBV cases, facilitate monthly meetings, establish community protection mechanisms based on the specific risks women and girls face and establish awareness raising action plans for increased coordination of GBV response and encouraging coping mechanisms that foster women’s resilience and address protection issues and concerns. Community and religious leadership will actively be involved in our programing to ensure safety for women and girls and those with disability in the communities.
