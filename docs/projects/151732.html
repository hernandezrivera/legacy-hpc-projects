Project Summary

The overall objectives of AMIIN Organization’s intervention are four-fold: 1. Provide emergency ambulance services for referral of cases to health facilities: With regard to the referral services, AAMIN Organization shall provide emergency ambulance services in the project locations (Mogadishu, Afgoye corridor and Galgaduud (Dhusamareeb). This is AAMIN Organization’s core area of focus. Both motor vehicle and motorcycle ambulance shall be provided. Motor vehicle ambulances will be deployed in Mogadishu and some sections in Afgoye corridor that are easily accessible by road. Motorcycle ambulances shall be deployed in Galgaduud (Dhusamareeb) and some sections of Afoye corridor due to poor road network. AAMIN Ambulance works closely with Health Service providers such as International Rescue Committee and plays an integral role in Mogadishu city’s referral management system. AAMIN Ambulance also responds to non-critical emergencies by the public especially the internally displaced populations in camps in various parts of Mogadishu. The refferal service will also closely coordinate with Maternity Waiting Homes (MWH) across Mogadishu and afgooye corridor. 2, Facilitate effective communication and coordination among various actors working on health, nutrition and WASH sectors: this will entail provision of communication equipment (handsets) to government agencies, NGOs, IDP settlements, refugees and communities to promote communication for ease of referrals. AAMIN Organization has state of the art communication equipment provided by UNDP and her staffs have been well trained on the use of the equipment. We shall provide all agencies the communication equipment and assign them specific channels for ease of access and communication. 3. Community-based bottom-line engagement: provide capacity building to service provider at the community levels including community educators, ambulance service providers and community volunteers in first aid. The trainings will take place at the district levels and the trained persons stationed in the districts to coordinate referral services with AAMIN ambulance and other service providers. The essence of this training is to put in place community level structures that will ensure sustainability and long-term use of the interventions. 4. Safe and affordable referral service for pregnant mothers from home to the clinic: The Eezer Initiatives entry point is to provide safe and affordable transportation of pregnant mothers from the home to the clinic. That’s why the Eezer motorcycle Ambulance was born! The three delays model illustrates crucial areas where the pregnant woman’s health is at risk. A) The decision to access care – factors at home like knowledge, attitudes and resources. B) The safe and affordable transport to a medical facility. C) The receipt of adequate and appropriate treatment at the facility. The entry point for the Eezer Initiative is the second delay. The challenge for marginalized people in the rural areas to get from the home to the clinic in a safe and cost efficient way. This will be mainly implemented in Dhusamareeb and Afgooye corridor (Hawa Abdi) as a pilot project.

Needs

Despite the fact that Somalia challenges are widespread, touching on almost all livelihood sectors, those touching on health, nutrition and WASH are particularly pronounced. According to UNICEF (November 2018), access to basic health services continues to worsen due to the ongoing conflict and drought-related displacement, increasing demand in areas receiving IDPs. The already weak health infrastructure and services are being further strained with displaced populations moving into catchment areas, bringing with them increased disease burden related to substandard conditions in settlements. The total number of recorded cases and deaths due to AWD/Cholera in 2018 stood at 78,435 and 1,118 respectively. By the end of November 2018, 20,078 suspected cases of Measles were reported with 84 percent of them being children under ten years of age. These figures mirror those by the Health Cluster Somalia (March 2017) bulletin which states that the health sector in Somalia is still in a critical condition with one of the worst health indicators in the world. With a population of 12.3 million, 1.1 million people are internally displaced. The under-five mortality rate is 137 per 1,000 live births while approximately 732 women per 100,000 live births die from pregnancy or childbirth-related complications. 3.3 million people are in need of health services. According to the Health Cluster Somalia (March 2017), the rapid movement of IDPs has overwhelmed health facilities, while the national supply chain has ruptured and is unable to rapidly redirect support. Delivery of life-saving medicines and medical equipment has been irregular due to insecurity, road inaccessibility, electricity and fuel shortages and rupture of the cold chain. Malnutrition levels have followed a deteriorating trend in recent years, with a steady increase in the number of malnourished children and number of internally displaced person (IDP) sites with malnutrition rates of 15 per cent global acute malnutrition

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The selected project locations will be accessible to AAMIN organization. We have integrated measures that will ensure buy-in and support by local communities throughout the process. Through the emergency task force, we shall establish an inter-agency coordination mechanism comprising local administration officials, IDP settlement management and local leaders with whom we’ll work throughout project implementation process. We will share and disseminate information through clusters informing the population of planned assistance and existing services - in a way that is culturally appropriate and is accessible to all, especially the most vulnerable. AAMIN organization will monitor the implementation of the project to identify any access barriers and use these structures to address them.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

AAMIN organization is a gender sensitive agency. Throughout the project cycle, we consider gender in relation to access to, and benefit from our interventions. We enlist, consult and engage both men and women, since they have different roles in the society. The combined information gives more accurate information on the total need of our support for a community. This project has potential to contribute significantly to gender equality.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

he security of Somalia, particularly Mogadishu, is still fragile. AAMIN organization will take precautionary measures while implementing the project. Largely, we shall be operating from a single location in Hodan district. Through our toll free number 999, we shall be accessible for 24hours a day to respond to emergencies as and when they occur. When contacted, the operations office dispatches a vehicle whose crew, on arrival at the scene, makes an assessment and, if required, transports the patient to hospital for treatment. Remaining located in one central, secure location will ensure our facilities and team are protected. On the other hand, we shall assess the risk levels when responding to emergencies. Where needed, our team shall be accompanied by Security Officers to provide security to the rescue team.
