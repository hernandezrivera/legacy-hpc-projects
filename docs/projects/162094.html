Project Summary

UNICEF will provide quality, equitable and gender-sensitive maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health services, sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services including counselling and referral for GBV, with attention to people with disabilities. The project targets a vulnerable population of 418,117 people across the Rohingya refugee and host communities. To respond to the basic health and nutrition needs of Rohingya and host community women and children, UNICEF will continue providing harmonized health services through the 18health facilities in camps (5 PHCs, 9 HPs and 4 DCTs – two of which are hibernated) as well as strengthening government health facilities in adjacent host communities (2 union sub-centres and 16 community clinics). A special focus will continue to support the functionalization of Newborn Stabilization Unit (NSU) and kangaroo mother care in the Ukhia and Teknaf Upazila Health Complexes and the special newborn care unit (SCANU) at the District Hospital. Health interventions focus on providing comprehensive primary health care services through the Essential Services Packages (ESP) as per Government’s guidance, include maternal, newborn care, immunization, integrated management of childhood illnesses, nutrition, acute watery diarrhoea prevention and management, prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV services and community engagement. UNICEF will also strengthen the Health Management Information System (HMIS/DHIS2) through technical support and capacity building of services providers of all health sector partners including government counterparts. Maternal Perinatal Deaths Surveillance and Response (MPDSR) will be extended to cover Rohingya communities in camp in addition to host population. These activities will be implemented through partnership with Partners in Health and Development (PHD), and Research, Training and Management (RTM) International.

Objectives

Objective #1 Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psycho social support; and non-communicable diseases. Objective #2 Ensure the prevention and timely response to outbreaks of diseases with epidemic potential and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone. Objective #3 Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behavior and utilization of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender considerations and vulnerable groups. Objective #4 Strengthen health sector coordination and information management with a focus on rational and accountable health service delivery systems, including monitoring of minimum standards and the quality of the services provided.

Needs

Rohingya Refugees has no record of immunization before the migration and remain vulnerable and prone to disease outbreak. Despite 10 massive vaccination campaigns since October 2017 to December 2018, routine immunization remains a challenge. UNICEF and WHO supported routine immunization through government to reach 30,960 children (male: 15,472; female: 15,488) under 1 year of age. 130 vaccinators (male: 89, female: 43) work in 816 outreach sites in the camps and in 58 fixed sites (through NGOs). Health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations but access to 24/7 services remain a challenge. Adherence to minimum standard of services and staffing is a concern and quality of services need to be improved. Scaling up Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission service in the selected health facilities in the camps, in two upazila health complexes and district hospital. Quality Newborn care is yet to be established, especially in community level. Referral linkages, especially at night required to be strengthen and more support needed for secondary care in field hospital and upazila health complex to reduce burden in District Hospital. Maternal and neonatal death surveillance need to be expanded to help identify and address avoidable mortality triggering factors. There are unmet needs to address vulnerable population particularly newborn and their mothers as over 70 percent of deliveries occur at home without trained assistance and antenatal care is infrequent and remains sub-standard. Children under 5 of age are at risk for common childhood illnesses such as pneumonia, diarrhea and malnutrition, some of which stem from difficulties ensuring exclusive breastfeeding for children under six months of age. There is inadequate access to information on services particularly for women and adolescent girls and boys, with men predominantly holding the decision-making power in Rohingya households. GBV and domestic violence affect women and girls disproportionately. UNICEF and its partners prioritize the recruitment of female (85 percent) community health workers (CHWs) who are better able to engage with mothers and families as well as on GBV issues. However, community engagement will involve not only the women and female caregivers but also men and male caregivers. This will continue to be vital for improving health seeking behavior and linking communities with the health care system. Community outreach activities need to be harmonized, cross linked with different working groups and monitored so it can be improved. Health information and management systems does exist, however, government platform DHIS2 needs to be strengthened adjusting the technicalities of interoperability with other existing systems. Acute watery diarrhoea and other infectious diseases are need to be prevented and treated, and consider the risk in the overcrowded makeshift settlement and host communities, it needs robust surveillance systems.

Activities or outputs

Activity 1.1: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners in implementing routine immunization for children under 1 year of age and pregnant women, and special Immunization Activities (SIAs) activities, when needed, for Rohingya and host population in Cox's Bazar. Activity 1.2: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners to provide quality and equitable maternal, newborn, child health, adolescent health services including GBV health services and integrated nutrition and C4D activities, strengthen support for community based new born care (includes acquisition of medicines, equipment, and supplies, capacity building) management and operational support in health facilities of the camps. Maternal Perinatal Deaths Surveillance and Response (MPDSR) will be extended to cover Rohingya communities in camp in addition to host population.Activity 1.3: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners establishment of PMTCT services for Rohingya and host communities (includes consensus building, awareness and education on HIV/STI/PMTCT, ANC HIV testing, initiate ART for identified positive pregnant women, ensure safe delivery, prophylaxis and ART for infants, ART adherence follow up mother and children through outreach workers, and referral for Early Diagnosis of infants ). Activity 1.4: Support Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners preventing and managing Acute Watery Diarhoea (AWD) outbreak including management of 4 Diarrhea Treatment Centers activities include capacity building, procurement of medicines and supplies, surveillance, hygiene promotion and Oral Cholera Vaccine effectiveness study). Strong collaboration with WASH and Communication for Development sectors will be maintained. Activity will be implemented by International Centre for Diarrheal Disease Research, Bangladesh (icddr,b) in collaboration with MoH&FW and health sector partners. Activity 2.1: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners to prepare monsoon and cyclone contingency plan (procurement and preposition of supplies, capacity building of community health workers, health mobile teams, rapid needs assessment, reinforcing health facilities and assisting vulnerable population).Activity 3.1: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners in strengthening community health mechanisms to enhance community based Maternal, Neonatal, Child, Adolescent health and Immunization including defaulter tracking, GBV counselling, including promotion of early childhood development initiatives and necessary referrals. Activity 4.1: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and health sector partners in strengthening Health Management Information System through District Health Information System 2 (DHIS 2) including Maternal, Neonatal, Child Health services, Immunization, Community Health and Non-Communicable Diseases data tracking, linking with national system will assist MoH&FW and partners to have evidence-based programming using real time data systems. Activity 4.2: Support Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW), WHO and health sector partners in strengthening coordination mechanism at district and field level. Improved coordination mechanism will assist MoHFW and health partners achieving results as promised in joint response plan.
