Project Summary

In line with its multi-year strategy to responsibly disengage from shelter activities in GCA, UNHCR will not conduct shelter repairs in GCA in 2021, but will continue to coordinate with local authorities and support their increased role in meeting humanitarian shelter needs.
In NGCA, UNHCR will continue conducting light, medium and heavy repairs of civilian homes damaged by the conflict, in line with Shelter Cluster guidelines. UNHCR will use multiple implementation modalities in order to maximise the reach and mitigate risks. For example, shelter assistance will be delivered through local partner NGOs, through construction companies and through direct distribution of construction materials combined with community mobilization to support families in conducting the repairs. Beneficiaries will participate in decision-making about the repairs.
Prior to selecting locations for shelter activities, UNHCR will conduct a security and protection assessment of the location . Beneficiaries will be selected through close coordination with local authorities and affected communities in accordance with vulnerability criteria. UNHCR will conduct post-repair monitoring that incorporates protection issues in order to measure the protection impact of the shelter intervention. UNHCR will integrate protection (including housing, land and property rights) into all shelter activities.
A small number of extremely vulnerable families will be assisted with heavy repairs.
Since the crises in Ukraine disproportionately affects women and elderly persons, UNHCR will ensure that 60% of beneficiaries of shelter project are women and at least 40% of all persons assisted are older persons. Based on past beneficiary selection for UNHCR’s shelter programs, it is estimated that 25% of beneficiary households will have a person with disabilities.
To respond to the immediate needs of those affected by new shelling, UNHCR will preposition emergency shelter kits including tarpaulin, complemented by other materials appropriate to the Ukrainian context. UNHCR will preposition emergency NFI kits for 150 households for such purposes in NGCA.
UNHCR will offer alternative housing to households with children living in particularly dangerous areas along the line of contact, ensuring that any relocation programs respect protection principles. UNHCR will advocate for IDPs’ access to housing and will provide protection expertise to programs implemented by the state and development actors to improve the access of IDPs and host communities to affordable and sustainable housing.
UNHCR ensures accountability to affected population through its field monitoring, which takes place during the phases of damage assessment, implementation and after the completion of the repairs. UNHCR operates a hotline for persons who receive services. Hotline operators make referrals to services and forward all complaints to UNHCR for follow-up. UNHCR assesses all partners’ capacity to prevent and respond to cases of SEA, and support partners to implement best practices. As part of Accountability to Affected People, UNHCR will conduct a participatory assessment with IDPs and conflict-affected persons in 2021.

1 - Needs

The armed conflict in Ukraine caused large-scale destruction of houses, buildings and communal infrastructure. Many communities along the contact line have suffered from regular shelling all over the conflict years. As in previous years, new damages caused by armed hostilities in 2020 were mostly concentrated in NGCA (86% of the 541 cases registered by the Shelter Cluster), in particular in Donetsk oblast (2/3 of the total cases). In a positive development, measures agreed by both parties to enforce a ceasefire end of July 2020 were, for the first time, effective, with no new damage registered in August and September 2020. According to Shelter Cluster estimates, some 1,000 households in GCA and 9,000 in NGCA will still require humanitarian assistance to repair their houses, as of the end of 2020. In GCA, the number of residual needs has shrunk. Since 2017, the State Emergency Services has repaired roofs in Donetsk GCA, using materials supplied by the oblast’ authorities and tools supplied by UNHCR. In 2020, 948 houses have been repaired. Also, in 2020, the government launched a program for compensation for housing destroyed by the conflict. In contrast, the number of households in NGCA with unmet humanitarian shelter needs remains high. Many homes were damaged in 2014-2015 and have not yet been repaired. These families live in extremely poor conditions, often under a shrapnel-scarred roof that leaks and with only plywood or plastic sheeting to cover shattered windows. In winter, conditions are particularly dire, since it is not possible to heat these spaces efficiently. Only few humanitarian agencies provide support with shelter repairs due to constraints on access. Therefore, UNHCR prioritizes response to humanitarian shelter needs in NGCA. Furthermore and building on its capacities for logistics and warehousing, UNHCR will also co-coordinate the logistics working group and facilitate the movement of convoys with humanitarian assistance to NGCA on behalf of UNHCR and other organizations. UN High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) Strategic Directions 2017-2021 underscore ‘putting people first’ and draw UNHCR staff to ‘be accountable for the people we serve, listening and responding to their needs, perspectives and priorities’. This requires that UNHCR applies age, gender and diversity (AGD) approach to all the aspects of its work, informed by the imperatives of promoting gender equality and empowering women and girls. Same approach will guide the implementation of the project in question. In particular, beneficiaries of the shelter project are yet to be fully defined, this process will be guided by the respective Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for Shelter programme, that inter alia contain vulnerability criteria. Respective colleagues apply these vulnerability criteria standardly for the identification of beneficiaries. Moreover, UNHCR protection staff will be regularly involved in the implementation of the shelter activities, also ensuring that that 60% of beneficiaries of shelter project are women and at least 40% of all persons assisted are older persons. Given the demographics, the majority of expected beneficiaries will be women without a family support. Further, according to AGD approach, women, whether or head of households or not, will be systematically consulted on identifying the particular shelter needs in their households. To the extent possible, women will also be associated to the shelter repairs and included in the labour forces required and receive the associated fees. Women will also be a distinct part of the groups involved in the monitoring of the project and will be interviewed specifically to ensure that their perspective is duly considered, and to understand the impact of the shelter intervention on their wellbeing and protection. The same will apply to key age groups in targeted communities, primarily elderly.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

1. Shelter and infrastructure established, improved and maintainedOutcomes: Long term/ permanent shelter provided and sustained; Shelter materials and tool kits providedUNHCR will provide sustainable long-term shelter assistance to families whose houses/apartments were damaged during the conflict. Priority will be given to conflict-affected persons living in the NGCA parts of Luhansk and Donetsk oblast close to the line of contact. In addition, those who are returning to the place of their previous residence and whose homes were damaged will be also supported through shelter programme. UNHCR will provide emergency shelter kits to persons whose damaged houses are in high-risk areas where durable shelter solutions are not advisable. UNHCR will offer alternative housing to households with children living in particularly dangerous areas along the line of contact. 2. Population has sufficient basic and domestic items. Outcome: Core relief items providedUNHCR will provide quick-impact material assistance to persons affected by displacement or by conflict-related damage to their homes, as well as in conflict-affected areas where conditions dramatically worsen due to disasters (e.g., wildfires). Along with the shelter emergency kits, UNHCR will preposition NFI kits close to the line of contact to be distributed to families at risk of displacement, newly displaced or returnees. UNHCR’s emergency response will be coordinated closely with local authorities and complementary to their efforts to respond. Acute emergency kits will have the same composition as in the past years. They will include tarpaulin and other materials appropriate in Ukrainian context. They will be used to respond to both conflict-related incidents such as shelling in conflict-affected communities. NFI emergency kits will be used to provide quick-impact material assistance to persons of concern affected by displacement or by conflict-related damage to their homes, as well as the most severe incidents of disasters (floods, wildfires) in conflict affected communities.3. Logistics and supply optimized to serve operational needsOutput: Warehousing provided, repaired and maintained. During the year UNHCR will maintain sufficient purchasing, warehousing and transportation capacities in order to support unhindered implementation of shelter project as well as distribution of core relief items. UNHCR’s operation in Ukraine requires complex supply arrangements involving large-scale and well-coordinated procurement, storage, delivery and distributions. Its humanitarian supply chain includes suppliers and logistics services providers, the government, de facto authorities, donors and beneficiaries. COVID-19 situation has had an impact on the implementation, and UNHCR adjusted its delivery of this activity accordingly. These arrangements will be maintained in 2021. UNHCR will co-coordinate the logistics working group and facilitate the movement of convoys with humanitarian assistance to NGCA on behalf of UNHCR and other organizations.
