Project Summary

Five years after the outbreak of internal conflict in South Sudan nearly 4.5 million people have been forced out of their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them internally. While the intensity of conflict may have reduced and clashes contained to certain regions, the humanitarian situation continues to be critical with impacts spilling through 2019. Gender-Based Violence (GBV), including sexual violence, is widespread in South Sudan. Even in displacement settings, threats and risks of GBV against women and girls, particularly rape and child marriages and forced marriage persist. During intercommunal fights and cattle raids, women are abducted and raped, just as during the armed conflict. Some of the key drivers of GBV are gender inequality, on-going conflict, inter-communal fighting, fragile community support systems and weak protection mechanisms, including limited availability and accessibility to basic social services, particularly for women and girls
Survivors of GBV need to access lifesaving psychosocial case management services to meet their complex and multiple needs. The reduced access to essential and lifesaving reproductive health services, and the lack of basic items which respond to the hygiene and reproductive health needs of women and girls, make living very difficult for displaced women and girls to maintain acceptable hygiene standards. Displaced women and girls need basic items in order to interact comfortably in public and maintain their personal hygiene, particularly menstrual hygiene. Without access to culturally appropriate clothing and hygiene items, the mobility of women and girls is restricted and their health might be compromised. Furthermore, without certain items women may be unable to seek basic social services, including humanitarian aid, which may increase their vulnerability to GBV
With the recent, signing of the peace agreement, there is a growing recognition to seize further recovery and development opportunities, where they exist, and to strengthen synergies between these efforts and the ongoing humanitarian response. UNFPA will work towards strengthening the current model of safe spaces into robust fully sustained multi purposes centers for women and girls with a strong focus on adolescent girls. The centers will provide both prevention and response services scaling up basic skills training, income generation activities, basic literacy classes. WGFS offers opportunities to other actors and services to coordinate and co-locate programming through WGFS to ensure a harmonized and integrated approach to GBV prevention and response and women’s empowerment more broadly. Opportunities for partnerships include with child friendly spaces (CFSs), youth centers, -school clubs, sports centers, health services, nutrition centers, livelihoods and economic empowerment projects. Strong referral networks and pathways are crucial in a WGFS. However, the principle of ‘integrated support systems’ could take coordination further, so that links are made between actors who can collaborate in terms of activities and resources (e.g. space).

Needs

UNFPA through its implementing partners aims at implementing GBV services through a static and mobile approach, which will include the establishment and strengthening of WGFSs and listening centers in Koch, Mayendit, Wau, Akobo, Leer, Rubkona, Panyijiar (Nyal & Ganyiel), Luakpiny/Nasir, Yei, Yambio, Aweil Centre The intervention foresees the establishment and strengthening of WGFSs and Listening Centers where women and girls can find a safe and supportive environment to gather as well as different types of services, including comprehensive case management, individual psycho-social support, awareness on GBV risks prevention and mitigation as well as income generating activities for those women and girl exposed to risk of GBV. UNFPA will regularly update risk assessments and service mapping of the area while advocating with the different Clusters, both at state and national level, for a prompt and more comprehensive response to the identified needs. The services provided within the WGFSs will focus on initial healing and the ability to identify and strengthen own coping mechanism. This service can be accessed by women and girls at particular risk of sexual violence, exploitation, abuse, denial of resources or forced early marriage. An integral part of the intervention will include establishing new or strengthening existing referral pathways to connect women and girls to multi-sector GBV response services. The GBV team will also prioritize the training of front-line agencies, community-based organization (CBOs) and key actors and community leaders in basic GBV concepts, provision of PFA and use of referral pathway for safe and dignified referrals. Moreover, with the aim of mitigating and preventing GBV risks Activities in the WGFS, will assist the economic empowerment of women through income generating activities that support their livelihood such as Fuel Efficiency Stoves, establish women Community groups for increased engagement and women’s voice and Capacity building for local women's groups/women's organizations and women leaders on mediation and peaceful resolution of conflicts
