Project Summary

Throughout 2021 Norwegian People’s Aid (NPA) established a humanitarian Mine Action and Disarmament (MAD) programme in Syria. NPA’s newly established programme will operate in territories currently under the control of the Government of Syria (GoS) with the primary objectives of protecting civilians from the impact of explosive ordnance (EO) and ensure safe access to livelihood opportunities and basic infrastructures for Syrian civilian population.
In 2022, NPA plans to start its operational phase which will focus primarily on recruitment and training national teams who will conduct survey and clearance of EO contaminated areas as identified under the Syrian Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and in coordination with the UN Mine Action Area of Responsibility (AoR) and UNMAS for the 2022 implementation period.
Currently 70% of all people in need of mine action and EO intervention, are living within Government controlled areas, according to the recent 2021 Mine Action analysis of need. More than a decade of civil conflict in Syria has left the country heavily contaminated. Improvised landmines, Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs), cluster munitions and wide variation of other EO affect all 14 governorates of the country. The contamination is particularly dense in areas previously held by the Islamic State, with both rural and urban areas impacted. Reports from Aleppo, Hama and Homs indicate a significant amount of contamination left by all sides of the conflict, as do reports from Daraa in the southern parts of the country, as well as from Rural Damascus. Moreover, these extremely high needs are not matched with the presence on the ground of qualified, professional humanitarian mine action actors that can address the problem in an efficient and timely manner. NPA's intervention aims at responding to these needs by ensuring the mapping, marking and safety removal of landmines, IEDs and other EO in full compliance with international standards and best practices.
Throughout the entire project period (2022-23) NPA will deploy, a total of twenty Multi-tasks Teams (MTTs) in conflict affected areas in order to protect the Syrian civilian population from the threats posed by landmines, cluster munitions (CM), improvised explosive devices (IEDs), and other explosive ordnances (EO). During the first year NPA will recruit, train and deploy ten MTTs teams to be deployed in Damascus and Rural Damascus governorates and according to the needs and priorities identified during the assessment phase (Jan-April). The second year of the project period NPA, in coordination with relevant UN agencies and local authorities, envisages of expanding its area of operations and recruit ten additional MTTs teams to be deployed in Homs governorate.
Each NPA MTTs teams will consists of eight team members and will be capable of performing non-technical survey, technical survey, mine clearance, battle area clearance, high risk search, Explosives Ordinance Disposal and Improvised Explosives Device Disposal. The work of the MTTs teams' will be guided by strict prioritizations criteria in order to target those people most in need of mine action and EOD/IEDD interventions. In line with these principles, NPA will primarily allocate its limited financial and operational resources in communities reporting explosive contamination in severity scales 3, 4
and 5.
Since the beginning of the programme NPA established a sound quality management system to ensure high quality and safety of clearance operations on the ground. In addition, NPA's work in Syria is guided by a robust conflict sensitive and “do no harm” approach which takes into account relevant risks and identifies corresponding mitigating measures. Finally, NPA Syria ensures a diverse work environment with staff of different gender, ethnic and religious backgrounds from a wide range of age groups.

4 - Needs

The conduct of widespread bombing campaigns by the Syrian Army and Russian forces together with extensive use of IEDs and other improvised weapon systems by ISIS in urban settings added further layers of complexity posing a particular challenge for future planning and clearance operations. This mix of conventional and improvised explosive weapons in highly populated urban centers, while creating an unprecedented technical and operational challenge, also hinder a comprehensive humanitarian response and reconstruction efforts. According to the UN, one in two people in Syria live at risk of explosive hazards and almost half of the Syrian population – approximately 11,5 million people – are estimated to live in areas affected by explosive ordnances. In the period from 2013 to February 2020 UNMAS has recorded more that 12,300 victims of explosive ordnance accidents in Syria. About 25% of the recorded victims are children. Men and boys are the most affected by mine/ERW/IED accidents representing more than 80 % of the total number of victims reported, while almost 30% of the total are IDPs. Women and girls are also heavily impacted by indirect threats and long-term consequences of contamination, which tend to exacerbate existing vulnerabilities. However, despite the ongoing data collection and information gathering efforts, it is believed that these figures significantly under represent the real extent of the EO impact.Sources: Syria MSNA, HNO and HRP.

5 - Implementation of activities

During the first year of the project period, the intervention will be implemented through the recruitment, training and deployment of a total of ten multi-tasks teams (MTTs) consisting of eight people each (six team members, one team leader, and one medic), all nationally recruited. Ten additional teams will be recruited and trained during the second year of the project, in 2023. Each team will be cross-trained in a series of humanitarian demining and clearance methodologies and, as such, will be able to conduct the following Mine Action (MA) activities: Risk awareness on Explosive Ordnance; Non-Technical Survey; Technical-survey, mine clearance, Battle Area Clearance (BAC), Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) and Improvised Explosive Device disposal (IEDD). The teams will be managed and supervised by international Technical Field Managers and by one Operations Manager who reports to NPA Programme Manager.During the first year of the project the teams will be deployed in the governorate of Rural Damascus in order to survey and clear areas as prioritized by NPA in close coordination with the UNMAS, the MA sub-cluster and local authorities. During the second year of implementation, the project aims at expanding its operations in Homs governorate. The project will pay particular attention at the teams composition in terms of gender and diversity, ensuring a balanced involvement of women and a fair representation of the country's ethnic and religious composition.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mine action actors need to avoid perceptions of discrimination in relation to choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries. As such NPA will be guided by sector severity analysis; needs assessments; analysis of accident/victim data; participatory and inclusive communication with communities on prioritization of tasks.A gender diverse staff will be critical as well as the engagement of women, and girls in project planning and implementation will be crucial to assess the communities needs and the impact of the contamination. Also important is the fact that the conduct of assessments or surveys may trigger grievances and mistrust if not followed by an adequate response. NPA will strive to communicate with communities and build trust with them and at the same time manage expectations. Equally important is the risk related to Housing, Land and Property in connection to mine action: the control of, and access to cleared land can be a critical element in early recovery and post-conflict settings. If not properly done, land release through survey or clearance can cause or exacerbate disputes and grievances. Thus it is essential for NPA to integrate HLP considerations into the land release process and into its conflict sensitive approach so to ensure that land release does not negatively affect the context.NPA maintains discussions with UN agencies and humanitarian actors working on HLP rights in order to ensure that a strict due diligence process is conducted on each tasks.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

NPA maintains and regularly revises its Ethical Standards and Framework. NPA’ Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct are essential components of the framework – these policies are complemented by a global Whistleblower system. The Safeguarding Policy especially focuses on preventing and responding to harm caused by sexual exploitation, abuse or harassment in and outside the workplace. When recruited each NPA staff member is required to read, understand and sign the policy while specific training is regularly provided. NPA Syria has made available both the Safeguarding Policy and the Code of Conduct in Arabic language. Specific SEA risks related to NPA’s Syria mine action programme mainly arise from NPA’s engagement in communities when conducting survey, community-liaison and clearance activities. In order to mitigate these risks, and as part of the accountability to affected populations, PSEA tools and processes are integrated at the design, implementation and reporting stage of the project. Adequate PSEA measures and mechanisms are incorporated into NPA’ assessments, response and monitoring activities and consist of participatory approaches during needs assessments and surveys; developing and establishing appropriate reporting/feedback mechanisms for SEA complaints; and work systematically to ensure that affected populations, especially women and youth, are informed and aware about their rights and how to access complaints/feedback mechanisms in a safe and anonymous manner.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Arguably, Syria remains one of the most difficult contexts in the world in which to deliver principled humanitarian assistance. The risks and challenges associated with operating in government-controlled areas remain extremely complex and multifaceted. In order to navigate this challenging context NPA has been involved with key personnel with different expertise including compliance, legal, finance, logistics, communication and security. Drawing on the experience of NPA’s existing programmes, NPA regularly monitors, maps and analyses all key risk categories that may have an impact on the set-up of the mine action and disarmament programme and the implementation of its operations in Syria. From the outset, NPA’s mine action work in Syria has been characterized by a robust risk management approach which allows the programme to navigate, respond and mitigate issues related to financial/banking de-risking measures, and compliance and legal issues related to sanctions and anti-terrorism regulations. A comprehensive, context-specific conflict sensitivity framework is also an integral component of ensuring that NPA’s programming causes no harm. Finally, the programme deploys a dedicated in-country Safety Manager in order to ensure that all NPA security needs, policies, and procedures are in place and observed.
