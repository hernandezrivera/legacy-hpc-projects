Project Summary

IOM will expand its MHPSS programme to a more systematic and comprehensive approach with engagement of community volunteers and health promoters. Access to quality reproductive health care services remains a challenge. IOM intends to enhance access to comprehensive services through the integration with primary health care. IOM will also continue working with the health partners in outbreak prevention and response efforts, capacity building of health professionals, health information management, and emergency referral and support system. As part of the health promotional and preventive activities, IOM will partner with local NGOs to implement health outreach through Health Promoters and community volunteers. Under this action, IOM will also strengthen its collaboration with the State Primary Health Care Development Agency and will continue in supporting the State Ministry of Health.

1 - Needs

Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities. The crisis in North-East Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world. Across three states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe, around 7.68 million people need humanitarian assistance including life-saving essential health care services. Wide areas across Borno State are completely inaccessible to humanitarians and are expanding, with hundreds of thousands of people cut off from life-saving assistance. While in 2018 only two Local Government Areas (LGAs) were considered completely inaccessible, these have increased to four in September 2019, compounded by increased displacements. The insurgency has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, the destruction of health facilities, security, dysfunctional legal and justice systems amidst increasing safety and security concerns. 42 percent out of 2367 health facilities in the BAY states are non-functional due to the conflict which is a clear indication of the catastrophic impact of the crisis on the health system. Additionally, the quality of health care is unclear owing to the unstable security situation and lack of standardized health care packages. The establishment of a robust monitoring mechanism and enhanced technical capacity of the health care providers was thus noted as a critical issue during 2019. The gaps in referral services have been strongly flagged by the Health Sector partners currently providing PHC services in camps and host communities as a key need specifically the inability of patients to receive critically needed health care nor timely access emergency care in hospitals. Health facilities were targeted during the conﬂict, and as a result many health facilities across BAY states are still non-functional or partially functional while those that are functioning with minimum capacity to deliver standard health care packages. In hard-to-reach and newly accessible areas, communities face serious challenges in accessing health services due to various reasons, including lack of medical staff, unavailability of drugs and supplies, security barriers, transportation issues and damaged/destroyed health facilities. Critical gaps also remain in functional primary and secondary health services. Other common challenges in accessing health services are related to high direct and indirect costs associated with healthcare, including the price of quality medicines. The disruption in supply chain of medicines and medical supplies has compounded the problem. The dire living conditions of these IDPs together with the often-poor water and sanitation conditions contribute to increased health risks and higher vulnerability to the spread of outbreak prone communicable diseases, such as cholera. IDP returnees in newly accessible areas or IDPs living in “Satellite camps” have reportedly limited or even no access to health care services. In Borno State, the vast majority of public servants have not yet returned to conﬂict-affected areas, in part because they remain inhospitable. There is an urgent need to expand the provision of integrated basic Primary Health Care (PHC) services, to prevent further deterioration of the health status of affected populations. Health services also need to be made available and accessible at the different levels of care, supported by a well-functioning referral mechanism, at and between the community, primary and secondary/higher care levels. While the Ministry of Health has made efforts in integrating the mental health through primary health care, there is a need to further this approach to ensure increased access to mental health services. GBV, including sexual violence, is widespread in the region due to the ongoing conflict, insecurity, living conditions in the IDP camps and informal settlements, also inadequate WASH facilities, lack of safe access to firewood, and restrictions on freedom of movement in and out of camps. 2 - Activities

Activity 1: Establish three Mobile Health Teams providing an integrated standard PHC service package, including referrals to secondary care. Activity 2: Identify and train local community volunteers on health promotion and community-based health interventions. Activity 3: Support the national disease surveillance system and contribute to control of disease outbreaks and other public health events. Activity 4: Refurbishment and equipping of 10 selected public health facilities in order to resume their function to provide integrated primary health services. Activity 5: Conduct training for PHC workers with the use of the WHO Mental Health Gap Action Programme Intervention Guide (mhGAP IG), Psychological First Aid (PFA), and staff self-care using already trained trainers. Activity 6: Strengthen referral and provision of specialized Mental Health services at tertiary and secondary health facilities (e.g. Federal Neuro-psychiatric Hospital, State Specialist Hospital). Activity 7. Ensure mainstreaming of protection, gender and MHPSS approach. Activity 8: Conduct an assessment on access of Mental Health services in PHC. Activity 9: Conduct awareness campaigns to increase concern, informing the targeted groups and attempts to change their behaviours promoting mental health and psychosocial well-being. Activity 10: Support the coordination of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Sub Working Group (MHPSS SWG) through capacity building activities and provisions of technical support in close coordination with State Ministry of Health.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons With Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process. IOM`s feedback (and complaints) mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit.
