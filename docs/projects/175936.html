Project Summary

This project is expected to provide integrated GBV mobile and static services through an integrated approach with Health in remote and hard to reach locations in Longechuk, Nasir and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile State in South Sudan where static and mobile services are not accessible. RI provides life-saving services in Upper Nile State since 2006 and key sectors are Health and Nutrition, FSL, WASH and Protection. Health services are delivered through Medical Mobile Units (MMUs). Since September 2020, RI piloted an integrated model comprising Medical Mobile Units and Mobile Protection Units to deliver life-saving services in Longechuk and Nasir in Health and Protection (GBV). In other Counties, RI continues to provide other services except Protection. So far, RI is running a total of 7 MPU teams out of which 2 are in static locations with support from BHA. Both MMUs and MPUs follow the same schedule and rotation, however, gaps still exist. Despite numerous constraints, lesson learned shows that mobile service delivery is the most appropriate approach to deliver services to communities that have been displaced or are unable to access the static services due to a myriad of barriers and risks that hinder safe access to humanitarian services.
RI proposes to form additional MPU teams to address GBV gaps in the 3 locations, alongside adding CP to its current mobile units. In Maiwut, although it’s one County nationally on the ground it is split by two administrative areas, Jekow is IO controlled while Maiwut Centre is controlled by the government. It’s important to ensure that both areas receive similar services to manage local political dynamics. A total of 12 MPUs will be formed-3 in each projection site comprising of 1 static team and 2 mobile teams. A target of 40,650 beneficiaries will be reached by this project. Key activities will include; static and mobile case management, PSS and referral services, outreach activities on GBV, risk mitigation activities (dignity kits distribution and training and supporting women and girls with fuel efficient stoves and establishment and of WGFSs. This proposed project is built on RI’s baseline protection survey conducted in all three proposed locations in December 2020.
It is against this background that RI proposes to implement mobile GBV services to improve community access to life-saving GBV response and prevention services in remote areas of Upper Nile State.

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence remains pervasive in South Sudan. Due to structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships, women and girls and especially those who are internally displaced or living near cantonment sites, or with a disability continue to suffer from GBV. Flooding, armed conflict in pockets of areas, sub-national violence and COVID-19 are escalating the risk of GBV including Conflict Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) and sexual exploitation and abuse. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 contributed to a rise in GBV incidents. In 2021, 2 million people are estimated to be affected by GBV coupled with gaps in response services which necessitates an urgent need for strengthened primary GBV prevention, integration of GBV risk mitigation in other sectors, and scaling up GBV response services, according to HNO. Upper Nile continues to experience multiple shocks in addition to protracted humanitarian crisis experienced in South Sudan, further deepening the levels of vulnerabilities. Relief International is among few humanitarian organizations operating in UNS-in IO Counties of Longechuk, Nasir, Maiwut, Ulang, Maiwut Jekow as well as in Maban since 2006. In 2020, RI piloted mobile and static GBV services in Longechuk and Nasir through an integrated approach with Health (Mobile Medical Units) and GBV (Mobile Protection Units (MPUs) to provide services in remote locations where centralized static services are not feasible. An MPU comprises of 4 core GBV staff namely: GBV/PSS Officer, PSS Caseworker and GBV Caseworker. The MPUs are further supported by local Protection Mobilizers/volunteers, and overseen by an MPU team leader. Each MPU covers between two and three locations a week and follows the same schedule as RI’s Mobile Medical Units (MMUs), enabling RI to provide tailored, comprehensive support to GBV survivors that addresses health, psychosocial, and protection needs. To better understand the needs and gaps, RI conducted a baseline assessment in Longechuk and Nasir, in September 2020, and a rapid assessment in Maiwut in February, 2021. Both assessments affirmed the prevalent of GBV in the community among other protection issues which exacerbates the situation for women and girls. Additionally huge gaps were identified in the provision of CP services for children residing in all three proposed counties. Some factors were noted to hinder survivor’s access to services-such as while travelling to access health services, collecting firewood or accessing market. Violence within domestic setting is also prevalent and most perpetrators are male family members questioning the safe of women and girls at home. Gaps in service provision were limited access to GBV specialized case management and PSS services including lack of safe spaces where women and girls can safely seek support, services and information. Generally, there are few partners operating in Sobat corridor and access to services is a major gap. Key recommendations include the need to strengthen GBV response and prevention programming across UNS to continue providing services to vulnerable populations in hard-to reach areas through mobile response. RI will replicate lessons learnt from piloting integrated approach in Longechuk and Nasir by forming additional 3 Mobile Protection Units (1 static and 2 mobile teams) in Longechuk, Nasir, Maiwut and Jekow) to work alongside MMUs to complement BHA by expanding the scope of MPU services to areas not covered by the MMUs, and integrate CP into this approach to ensure the the needs of children are also met.

2 - Project activities

Cluster Objective 1. Activity 1: Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery. RI will provide culturally appropriate individual and group PSS services to women and girls survivors of GBV and those at risk to foster their physical and mental well-being. The activities will be tailored for women and girls separately. PSS activities implemented will be in accordance with findings from the needs and interest survey conducted by RI in 2020 in Longechuk and Nasir. Suggestions include: handicraft, pottery, crocheting, traditional dances, beading, story-telling, football and volleyball, skipping ropes and coffee and tea corner discussions. Activity 2: Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support RI will take responsibility for making sure that survivors are informed of all the options available to them, and that issues and problems facing a survivor and her/his family are identified and followed up in a coordinated way. The survivor will be provided with emotional support throughout the process. Caseworkers will be responsible for direct service provision in case management. Case management services will be provided both through mobile services (temporary safe spaces) as well as in static WGFS supported by one static and two MPUs team in each location. Infrastructural mapping will be done to identify physical spaces from where case management services could be provided in collaboration with local communities and women and girls themselves. Activity 3: Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment RI proposes to establish 3 static WGFS in designated locations in Nasir, Longechuk and Maiwut Counties (Jekwo and Maiwut). Temporary safe spaces will be identified through a mapping exercise with the community and in consultation with women and girls. Two temporary safe spaces will be established in sites not supported by the static WGFS. Cluster Objective 2: Activity 1: Conduct awareness raising activities on GBV MPU teams will be responsible for creating awareness on GBV basic concepts, service available, and dissemination of key messages and referral pathways within MPU catchment areas. Protection Mobilizers will support the mobile team in disseminating GBV messages, mobilizing the community and will act as focal points in identifying and referring cases to the community as well as supporting survivors get re-integrated back to the community. Activity 2: Establish and/or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support RI will participate in service mapping exercise to review and update existing referral pathways and development of one where none is available. This will enhance complementarity and avoid duplication of efforts for maximum utilization of limited resources. Activity 3: Distribution of dignity kits to women and girls RI proposes to procure, assemble, transport and distribute 1200 dignity kits (300 per location-in Longechuk, Nasir, Maiwut and Jekow). The contents will be per UNFPA guidelines. Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted to get feedback on their usefulness and any propose changes. Activity 4: Train staff/frontline workers trained on GBV minimum Standards (Case management, PSS etc.) 120 (30/location) RI staff will be trained on the 16 GBV minimum standards and the guidelines distributed to ensure all GBV activities are aligned to the set standards. Activity 5: Train and support 30 women with Fuel Efficient Stoves (disaggregated by age) as part of GBV risk mitigation measureA consultant will be engaged in training 10 women and girls in each location (120 (80W, 20G) in making fuel efficient stove and those trained will receive fuel efficient stoves. They will cascade the training to other community members.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All RI staff and volunteers who are based in the four locations are trained on RI's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each RI field office has safeguarding champions who are given more intensive training on RI's safeguarding and PSEA approach. During project kick-off meetings with communities RI will inform communities of the PSEA policy, who the safeguarding champions are, what constitutes PSEA, emphasize that all project activities are delivered free of charge and that beneficiaries should not be asked to accept anything in exchange for any goods or services. RI will place posters and visuals outlining RI's policy and where community members can report at distribution sites. Additionally RI will have a help desk manned by RI staff at each distribution site, to collect complaints and feedback and to oversee distributions. Finally, RI's independent MEAL team and project management staff from Juba will conduct ad-hoc monitoring visits to project sites, meeting with community members and will reiterate the training and RI's position on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This project is designed to meet some of the most marginalized and vulnerable members of communities in Upper Nile - women and girls, and male and female survivors of GBV. RI's Protection Team and MEAL team will have equal representation of both sexes, to ensure that women/girls feel comfortable sharing their feedback. RI's feedback mechanisms for this cohort are based on an underlying assumption that most beneficiaries will be Illiterate. As such RI has embedded multiple layers of verbal feedback including monthly FGDs and KIIs, intake and exit interviews, and weekly monitoring by RI's Protection coordinator, Senior Management and MEAL team, to ensure that feedback is obtain throughout the lifecycle.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RI will conduct sensitization and mobilization prior to start of project, through consultations with community leaders, both formal and informal. Chiefs, women's leaders, and youth groups will be provided with full details of RI’s GBV program. RI has modeled this project on a pilot project conducted in two locations in Upper Nile. Community acceptance for GBV activities was obtain as RI spoke to key influencers in the community first, conducting a protection baseline to ascertain attitudes and feedback on how the activities should be delivered, and crucially established GBV activities alongside health – an already accepted service being provided to communities. The project introductory meetings in all project areas will include explanation and orientation on how to make feedback and complaints and how the feedback and complaints will be analyzed, and when feedback on complaints and requests received will be given back to the community. Moreover; RI staff and local authority will be trained on how to orient, collect, record beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received, as well as how and when to respond. all staff will be trained on core data protection and confidentiality principals also. RI has already conducted an AAP study in each location proposed which asked a statistically significant sample size what the preferred means of both receiving information and giving feedback were. Overall, individuals felt more comfortable giving feedback verbally through RI staff or community leaders. Interestingly, females preferred female community leaders or direct to RI staff. Further, all participants stated they preferred to have information shared verbally from RI staff, and then community leaders. A significantly fewer cohort stated they preferred giving feedback to local authorities. The verbal nature of feedback and information was emphasized throughout and is unsurprising given the literacy levels of the wider population in all counties, and the limited/no access to electricity and mobile phone connection for more advanced feedback mechanisms e.g SMS, hotlines or email. RI will ensure to have the following community engagement throughout the project lifecycle:- Project kickoff and close meetings with Community Leaders, and womens leaders, local authorities, - Monthly FGDs with disaggregated cohorts of the target population- baseline and endline surveys- in-depth interviews with sample beneficiaries- case studies. - PDM for dignity kit distributions - complaints hot-desk and AAP champions at trainings and distribution activities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RI will use the beneficiaries’ feedbacks to shape and adapt into project design and implementation. RI will conduct post distribution monitoring to get beneficiaries feedback on beneficiaries’ satisfaction on content, quantity and quality of the dignity kits and will change / adjust the kit content, quantity and quality based on the feedback. RI’s baseline interviews with community members, particularly women and girls – but also men and boys (for acceptance of the project and proposed activities as a whole) will be crucial to the success of the project during implementation. Ongoing monthly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on project activities will be conducted by RIs trained MEAL team. RI's response to feedback will be shared back with community leader. Action plans will be developed with RI's project team and where appropriate actions will be followed up to improve the implementation of the project.

Overall, the project will enhance support for prevention and response to the needs of GBV survivors and other vulnerable groups by timely provision of survivor-centered case management and PSS services, dissemination of information on GBV services available, referral services to enhance healing and recovery and in meeting their multiple needs for improved physical and psychological well-being.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

RI's WGFS and mobile activities will include elements of nutrition and health education aimed at mothers of children under 5. This will include the importance of dietary diversity, seeking medical treatment early, immunizations, importance of breastfeeding etc. Further, demo gardens by RI's FSL team will be established at WGFSs, alongside cooking demonstration with mothers. Finally RI's nutrition team will conduct mass MUAC screening within RI's mobile and static protection activities for PLW and children, and refer to RI/other actors nutrition program - in order to reduce the likelihood of children under 5 falling from MAM to SAM. The combination of these integrated efforts will support the decrease in children and PLW becoming malnourished.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The very nature of RI’s GBV program is aiming to provide support to the most vulnerable women, girls, men and boys who are at risk of or survivors of GBV. RI has already completed a comprehensive referral mapping exercise in each of the proposed locations, alongside service mapping. RI is aware of what partners are available and what services exist to support referrals. As part of its integrated approach, RI will refer GBV survivors and those at risk to other RI and partner services. This includes FSL – agricultural and livelihood distributions and training, health for clinical management of rape and other health services, nutrition team to take part in cooking demonstrations, health and nutrition education and training, and WASH for NFI distribution and hygiene promotion activities. RI’s protection and other sector staff have all been trained on the referral map, and RI has established a system of documenting and tracking referrals internal RI (inter-department) and outside RI (to other organizations). Through this RI will ensure that GBV survivors and those at risk are able to have equitable access to basic services provided by organisations through giving them a voice. Additionally, RI’s protection awareness raising and sensitization campaigns will target decision makers and influencers in the community (womens leaders, youth groups, community leaders, chiefs, local authorities). Messages will be shared and discussions held with these cohorts around ensuring equitable access to resources and services for all community members.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

RI's WGFS and mobile activities will include elements of nutrition and health education aimed at mothers of children under 5. This will include the importance of dietary diversity, seeking medical treatment early, immunizations, importance of breastfeeding etc. Further, demo gardens by RI's FSL team will be established at WGFSs, alongside cooking demonstration with mothers.
