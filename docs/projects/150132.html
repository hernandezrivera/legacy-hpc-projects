Project Summary

This project is aligned to HNO 2018-2020 Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 & 2. It also contributes to strategic objectives 1 and 2 of the Every Newborn Action Plan “ENAP” 2017-2020, developed and adopted as a joint collaboration among major UN agencies, WHO, UNCEF and UNFPA in addition to other active stakeholders i.e. MAP-UK.
The recent developments in Gaza and deteriorating situation following aid cuts, massive casualties due to "Great March of Return", have further exacerbated the humanitarian needs, and reduced access to essential life-saving maternal health services. UNFPA aims to save women’s lives and ensure that, despite the toll on the health system, women and girls have access to essential maternal and Reproductive Health (RH) services and information. This will be achieved through filling gaps in reproductive health including maternal health services, mitigating the impact of massive trauma casualties on essential maternal care and RH service provision and preventing excess mortality and morbidity through;
- Ensuring a continuum of care and provision of life sustaining RH services, counselling and information including strengthening community preparedness.
- Provision of essential life-saving maternal health equipment and supplies
- Implementation of MISP and training on emergency obstetric care, safe birth etc.
- Actively engaging communities, supporting coping mechanism, sensitization, awareness raising and outreach.
- Support linking and informing communities to available service with special focus to marginalized communities and groups including disabled, elderly etc.
While this project is meant to address immediate response to urgent and critical needs of Gazan’s women’s, and young girls, especially those in marginalized areas, it will also support men and other vulnerable adolescents and strengthen the preparedness and resilience of both the community and the health system for absorbing shocks, saving lives and recovering from crises.

Needs

The situation in Palestine, especially in Gaza, is well recognized as a protracted protection crisis with humanitarian consequences, driven, among others, by a lack of respect for international law by the occupying power. Palestinian women, men, girls and boys in Gaza face a range of serious protection and humanitarian threats including threats to life, liberty and security. Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to be a key protection and health concern in Gaza, further impeding women’s and girls’ access to resources including life sustaining health reproductive services, and information. Following more than 12 years of tightened blockade on Gaza, internal political divisions and public employees' salary cuts, with potential threat of complete political/administrative separation between West Bank and Gaza, the health system in Gaza has been eroded and is on the verge of collapse. Maternal health facilities and units are facing severe constraints such as regular and excessive electricity outages, shortages in essential drugs and supplies, and a decline in referral of patients. The political division between the West Bank and Gaza adds further complexities, straining coping mechanisms of the public health system. Recent health cluster sit-rep/Sept.10th 2018, indicates that 47% of the essential medical drugs and 30% of disposables in Gaza are currently at absolute zero stock level. The impact of the "March of Great Return" comes on top of an already fragile health situation where the massive influx of casualties is hindering the health system absorbing capacity. Scarce resources are diverted to deal with the massive injuries and trauma care needs, overlooking aggravated needs of other vulnerable groups including 45,000 pregnant women at any time. More than 7 % of these are high-risk pregnant women requiring special follow up care including obstetric care and safe birth. With a fertility rate of 4.5, it is estimated that 160 deliveries take place every day in Gaza. From experience during previous armed confrontations with mass casualties, we know that medical attention, resources and medical staff, including midwives, nurses and obstetric surgeons shift to responding to the massive traumatic injuries. As a consequence, pregnant women's access to life-saving maternal and reproductive health services including emergency obstetric care, safe birth and antenatal care decreases, exposing women to risk of maternal mortality and disability. This spillover effect of the diverted focus towards trauma care has been witnessed following the "Great March of Return" where MOH maternities have reported delays in scheduled elective surgeries and early discharge of cases. UNFPA aims at saving women’s lives through strengthening system and community access and preparedness to life saving reproductive health (RH) services and information on; safe childbirth, Emergency obstetric care, Preconception care, early detection of breast cancer, antenatal care with focus on high risk pregnancies etc. and enhancing knowledge of and utilization of essential RH services, during acute crises. Through this project, UNFPA will build on previous support, cooperation and coordination with major health providers in Gaza including UNRWA and Health NGOs to ensure access of vulnerable women’s, men and girls to RH services as well as strengthen system and community preparedness through supporting networks of community activists and implement community mobilization campaign including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspectives via educating families Men, boys and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs, risks “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of healthy outcomes and reproduction, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies.

Activities

This project complements a recent UNFPA humanitarian intervention, supported by Japan and targeted major maternities and PHC clinics, through provision of essential RH equipment, instruments and supplies, creating a network of 75 health staff, who were trained on safe delivery and referral as to support safe childbirth at the community and/or PHC facility, or appropriately refer cases to further assistance, and community awareness campaigns reaching thousands of people in vulnerable areas of Gaza strip. Through this project, and building on lessons learned, and project external evaluation, UNFPA will respond to urgent needs in RH services, by filling gaps and preventing system collapse through providing critically needed medications, equipment, instruments and supplies to the targeted facilities, and including others “mainly PHCs”. Focus shall also include facilities serving vulnerable and access restricted areas, including Shijayya, Johr Aldeek, eastern Salah Eldin, Khuzaa and southern Gaza.Practically, and in order to enrich the intervention and expand its coverage, UNFPA will work on the following domains;- Strengthen access to life saving RH services, through supporting major maternities and PHC facilities, with critically needed equipment/instruments, drugs, supplies as well as training on updated emergency obstetric protocols, - Support introduction of preconception care services & information at PHC level, training of staff and equipping clinics. - Support networks of community activists and implement community mobilization campaign including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspective via educating families and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs, risks “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of healthy outcomes and reproduction, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies.Activities:- Provision of life savings needs; drugs & disposables, equipment, instruments and medical furniture to 6 central maternities and 18 PHC clinics. - Improve the capacity/skills of 400 health providers, practitioners on EmOC, safe delivery, MISP, preconception care, breast clinical exam, and other RH issues.- Provision, distribution and deployment of individual safe delivery kits to major PHCs, trained staff, and ambulances.- Update and maintain the database of safe delivery trainees and disseminate to the public and media.- Improve community emergency preparedness in isolated communities through enhancing awareness and /or linkages with appropriate service delivery points and available members of the safe delivery and community networks.- Conduct community- based RH interventions; Campaigns supporting community networks, outreach sessions, and media campaigns advocating for universal health coverage and right to health and access to services.

Indicators

Monitoring and Evaluation: UNFPA shall integrate project components/activities into its annual work plans with implementing partners (IPs) and will develop a detailed monitoring and evaluation plan, in line with UNFPA’s M&E system and framework, which includes monitoring activities and appropriate tools that will be used to track project performance indicators, beneficiaries’ feedback and achievements of results. The monitoring framework shall track progress against achieving key deliverables and millstones both on partners and UNFPA directly implemented activities.UNFPA jointly with IPs will conduct focus groups discussions with beneficiaries and regular field monitoring visits to the implementation sites, to assess and track progress towards planned results, and address challenges that may affect implementation. UNFPA and IPs will be ensuring/advocating vulnerable beneficiaries i.e. marginalized people, GBV survivors and women & girls with disabilities are reached and mechanisms to reach them and facilitate their access to services are considered by providers. Also, field monitoring visits may be planned and done jointly with the donors upon their request. The following indicators/targets shall be monitored by UNFPA and partners: • Number of Gaza health facilities benefited from equipment and supplies provided: 24• Number of women/pregnant women benefited from enhanced maternity and PHC services, quality, coverage of obstetric care: 45,000• Number of beneficiaries reached through community campaign “sessions and media”: 85,000• Number of Health providers benefited from training on several RH issues: 400• Number of individual safe delivery kits procured: 250• Number of delivery kits/supplies pre -positioned at PHCs and Ambulances: 45
