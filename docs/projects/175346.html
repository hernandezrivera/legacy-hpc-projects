Project Summary

The 16 priority provinces for 2021 are targeted to strengthen the national capacity to improve access to treatment for 265,141 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition in the existing nutrition treatment units, both the outpatient (OTPs) and inpatient units (IPFs). The preventive nutrition services will be strengthen at community and health facility levels. This will include provision of much needed supplies and equipment, capacity building of frontline health staff and technical support within the health system and quarterly field monitoring visits. At the community level, the actions will integrate early detection of growth faltering and referral while building the capacity of mothers and caregivers on essential nutrition actions including adequate young child feeding practices to prevent all forms of malnutrition. The ministry of health will be supported to reinforce the coordination mechanism at both national and provincial level. This action will also contribute to the measles response through Vitamin A supplementation for 813,403 children and reduce micronutrient deficiency for 99,324 children.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Needs According to OCHA (Situation Report, January 2021), more than 6.4 million people, or one in three people, are in need of emergency humanitarian assistance in Chad. These needs are caused by structural development challenges and chronic poverty which exacerbate the vulnerability of the population. Chad has been experiencing three interconnected crises, food insecurity and malnutrition, population movements, and health emergencies, which were further exacerbated in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic and record rainfall during the rainy season. The SMART 2020 found a GAM rate of 10% and a SAM rate of 2.1% among children under 5 years of age. Despite that the data show a slight improvement compared to the previous year with a rate of 12.9% GAM and 2.9% SAM in 2019, the situation remains worrisome. Based on criteria of prevalence (GAM >= 10% and/or SAM >=2%) and/or mortality (overall mortality rate >=1/10,000/day and/or under-five mortality rate >=2/10,000/d), 16 provinces out of 23 are considered priority provinces for SAM treatment in 2021 (Barh El Gazel, Kanem, Lac, Borkou, Ennedi Est, Ennedi Ouest, N'Djamena, Batha, Hadjer Lamis, Salamat, Ouaddai, Wadi Fira, Sila, Tibesti, Guera and Mayo Kebbi Est). The highest GAM prevalence is observed in Borkou (19.4%). The situation has also deteriorated considerably in Lac (12.9% in 2019 vs 18% in 2020), Wadi Fira (12.6% in 2019 vs 14.8% in 2020) and Ouaddai (12.1% in 2019 vs 13.7% in 2020). In Chad, the key drivers of malnutrition include inadequate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and poor hygiene and access to water. These two factors, compounded with recurrent food and nutrition insecurity, leave households in a perennially precarious situation. As a consequence, in addition to the numbers of children already malnourished, many are near the borderline and risk falling into malnutrition. Based on all of the above data, it is estimated that 401,090 children under 5 years of age were identified to be in need of SAM treatment in 2021, including 292,278 in the 16 priority provinces.Management of malnutrition faces many obstacles, such as the lack of national investment and insufficient capacities at health facility and community level. Currently, less than half of functional health facilities-785 out of 1,577 (49%)- provide IMAM services. This unequal access prevents thousands of children from receiving life-saving treatment. The operational capacity of the nutritional units remains a major challenge, with low capacities in terms of human resources, materials and equipment, particularly for the management of malnourished children with medical complications. On the other hand, early detection is still low, but showing signs of progress, due to new approaches such as the “mother-led MUAC” approach. It should be extended to additional areas, while strengthening preventive nutrition intervention using the promotion of optimal IYCF practices as entry point. According to the SMART 2020 survey, the key infant and young child feeding practices indicators show positive trends compared to the 2019 figures. the rate of exclusive breastfeeding at national level is estimated at 16.4 per cent in 2020 against 8.8 per cent in 2019. The national coverage of children aged 6 – 23 months with minimum acceptable diet diversity is estimated at 12.4% in 2020 against 11.3 according to the 2019 SMART survey. There is therefore an urgent need to scale up life-saving nutrition interventions in order to address unmet needs and prevent associated mortality and morbidity." Activities 1.)
