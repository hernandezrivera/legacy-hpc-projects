Project Summary

This project seeks to Increase access to protection services to displaced population in hard to reach, newly accessible and underserved communities in North East Nigeria in line with minimum standards for Child Protection and GBV in humanitarian actions. This will be done through providing comprehensive case management services, building resilience by providing community based mental health and psychosocial support, reintegration of child survivors of GBV and other vulnerable children as well as strengthen community based mechanisms to prevent and respond to child protection incidences. The project directly targets 21,200 children including unaccompanied and separated children, child survivors of GBV, children with disabilities and excluded and marginalized children including those at risk. Particular focus will include adolescents especially girls and caregivers.
Plan International’s protection mobile programming is designed to respond to the urgent needs of populations in hard to reach communities, under-served communities, and areas where IDP influxes overwhelm the capacity of existing actors to respond to urgent protection needs. The mobile programming is designed to deal with two levels of response including a third level of transition from mobile to stable programming: First response, which ensures the deployment of the mobile team with 24 to 48 hours and will be deployed for a period of 6 and up to 12 weeks to provide urgent lifesaving assistance. The team will assist in the collection of rapid assessment data while providing direct services to include identification and documentation of UASC, immediate family tracing and reunification, emergency alternative care placement, identification of GBV cases, provision of dignity kits, and provision of psychological first aid. In this first line of response, the conditions for deployment will depend on large-scale displacement and influx of IDPs, population in newly accessible areas, and those affected by sudden natural disaster or disease outbreak.
The second level of response will focus on communities and populations with limited access to humanitarian assistance due to the lack of protection actors. The second level of response will focus on providing urgent services for a population considered more stable, such as individuals returning to their LGAs. The mobile team will provide psychosocial support for children, provide support to parents and caregivers, provide Child Protection and GBV case management, update referral pathways, conduct community safety audits and community safety action plans, advocate for stable and or static programming, and provide targeted response for high risk children including children with no suitable caregivers, children at risk of abuse in their current care arrangements, and unaccompanied and separated children under 5 years. The second line of response mobile team will deploy for up to a period of 6 months and leave communities when a static program is established. Additionally, Plan International will implement third level of activities that include reintegration and community based psychosocial support that strengthen resilience of children and caregivers. This will include targeted livelihood intervention through training and cash for start-up kits. The cash will target specific vulnerable groups including Children Formerly Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups who may have completed a skill training, those returning to school receiving cash support to pay for essential learning materials and those opting to start up small businesses.
Plan International had piloted protection mobile programming approaches in North East Nigeria for the past two years and this approach has proven successful in meeting the protection needs of affected population especially GBV survivors, unaccompanied and separated children, children with child protection needs and reactivating and strengthen community based mechanisms. Actors in North East Nigeria, especially the government, recognized the importance and need for mobile programming that gradually transitioned to a more stable program approach and address urgent needs as well as building social cohesion and resilience.

Needs

The violence and military operations have shrunk the protective space. Women and children, including adolescent girls and boys have been particularly affected by increased conflict-related GBV and child protection incidents, ranging from forced and early marriages to rape, physical, mental, and sexual assault of girls and recruitment of boys and girls by armed groups. Women and girls abducted by Boko Haram, survivors of rape, and children born out of sexual violence face stigmatization and often rejection from their communities upon their return. These girls, women and their children are often feared by communities and ostracized as they are often suspected to be Boko Haram sympathizers. Over two years of response in North East Nigeria, particularly Borno and Adamawa states, Plan International has identified the following gaps and needs especially in responding to the protection needs of population affected by the crisis in North East Nigeria. These gaps and needs are further validated by various assessments and humanitarian situation updates presented by UNOCHA. Gap/Need 1: Because of the nature of displacement and the mobility of the affected population, often many are not provided with initial and urgent protection services. For instance, in most transit and or reception centers, there are either very few or no protection actors present to identify and respond to urgent protection needs including the identification of unaccompanied and separated children and providing them with urgent support to address immediate needs. Compounding this, humanitarian organization have limited access to the affected population. If protection responses are not scaled up, it is likely that the situation will continue to deteriorate, threatening lasting damage to their safety and wellbeing of affected populations, particularly children and women. Gap/Need 2: UNOCHA has established a rapid response mechanism (RRM) to provide immediate lifesaving assistance to displaced population. However, the mechanism is focused in the sectors of nutrition, shelter, WASH, and NFI distribution. Protection, particularly Child Protection and GBV, are considered cross cutting issues within the mechanism, leaving the component unable to adequately respond to the identified Child Protection and or GBV needs of children and adolescents.Gap/Need 3: There is a very limited number of protection actors and services available to displaced populations in already accessible areas and as a result of overstretch of already existing protection services, often the needs of new arrivals and individuals living in hard to reach and underserved areas are not met. Most of the response projects are focused on specific Local Government Areas with very little or no flexibility to respond to emerging and urgent needs including influx, sudden natural disasters, and other changes. This type of programming poses significant delays to respond within 24 to 48 hours as approvals have to be sought from donors to respond. Therefore, there is a need for flexible mobile programming response approach that will not only ensure immediate deployment but sustainable follow up as the population moves.Gap/Need 4: Plan International assessment shows significant capacity and awareness gaps identified within communities in the 10 targeted LGAs in Borno, Adamawa. As the communities are IDPs and formerly not residents in these areas, there was a significant lack of awareness of what MHPSS and PSS services are on offer by the government or humanitarian agencies, however limited they may be. Even where there was some awareness, stigma and lack of trust towards children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) means that these children are effectively excluded from these services. There is a strong need to link CAAFAG reintegration programming and the current (MRM) response and develop guidelines and procedure for the identification and reintegration of CAAFAGs.

Activities

1.1 Identify unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and other vulnerable children. Protection desks will be set up in reception centers to identify and document children. Unaccompanied and separated children will be documented by caseworkers who will ensure proper documentation that will facilitate appropriate support actions. 1.2 Conduct rapid family tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children. Plan International after identification of UASCs will conduct immediate family tracing exercise and ensure immediate reunification of UACs. 1.3 Provide emergency alternative care for UACs. Immediate alternative care arrangements will be organized through the emergency identification training and support to community members to serve as foster parents for children in need of immediate care. 1.4 Conduct awareness on prevention of family separation as well as child protection. Both the mobile and stable program teams will engage both children and adults with key information and messages on prevention of family separation and where separation has occurred key information children and parents need to learn to make tracing and reunification easy and possible. 2.1 Provide dignity and safety kits. These will include sanitary pads, toothbrush and toothpaste, clothing, body lotion, feet wear, and flashlights, which will be provided to adolescent girls and children upon arrival at transit centers and or in communities of displacement. 2.2 Provide immediate case management for child survivors of GBV. GBV survivors will be linked to relevant services with health screening and treatment. The capacity of health care providers will be built to be able to effectively respond to the needs of survivors especially in provision of child and adolescent friendly services. 2.3 Conduct awareness on prevention and response to GBV. The mobile team will engage both children and adults with key information and messages on prevention and response to GBV. Additionally, key awareness and sensitization will be conducted disseminating information on referral pathway information. 3.1 Provide psychological first aid. Plan International mobile team will conduct regular and rapid psychological first aid to affected population to enable them to understand available services and how they can access those services. 3.2 Provide psychosocial support to children. Both the mobile and stable program teams will conduct non-specialized community based psychosocial support in line with the IASC guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support. 3.3 Conduct sessions for parents and caregivers on positive parenting and psychosocial support for children. 3.4. Train community leaders on Child Protection and GBV and support them to establish community based protection committees. 4.1 Conduct rapid assessment. Plan International protection team will conduct a rapid needs assessment through engaging with affected population to identify immediate needs and recommended actions. The outcome of the rapid assessment will be used to determine the line of response. 4.2 Identification and reintegration of children formerly associated with armed forces and armed groups. Plan International will provide ongoing psychosocial support to children rescued during military operations and those released from military screening facilities by liaising with the MWASD to provide short term rehabilitation services. 4.3. Conduct needs assessment and categorize children based on their specific reintegration needs. Children who are able and can return to school will be linked to existing education programs while provide learning materials through cash transfer and those unable to return to formal school, alternative life skills training, including vocational, apprenticeships and income generation activities will be provided with cash transfer for start-up kit or capital.
