Project Summary

To mitigate the threat of explosive ordnance in North-East Nigeria, DRC will continue to disseminate Explosive Ordnance Risk Education and conduct Non-Technical Survey of communities in local government areas like MMC, Jere , Bama, Banki, Dikwa, Ngala, Gwoza and Askira/Uba in Borno and Madagali, Michika in Adamawa. This intervention will allow people to live and work as safely as possible in the face of explosive weapons until contaminated lands are cleared. DRC targets 122,000 beneficiaries reached with EORE and 70 communities to survey. EORE and NTS activities will contribute to the freedom of movement, safe access to agricultural land and access to humanitarian assistance in the affected communities.

1 - Needs

The crisis that has evolved in North -East Nigeria for the past twelve years, has affected the population by leading to widespread displacement and devastation as well as acute food and nutrition insecurity and a desperate shortage of essential health care. An additional life-threatening situation for the population results from violent conflicts that left a wide range of explosive devices putting millions of people at risk. The presence of explosive ordnance in the communities is not only endangering the lives of the vulnerable population but hinders restoration and access to socioeconomic infrastructure and hampers delivery of critical humanitarian aid. To mitigate for this situation an estimated figure of 1.16 million people in Borno and 200,000 in Adamawa are in need of explosive ordnance risk education. The United Nation Mine Action Service reported 1,315 civilians injured and 755 killed by the explosive ordnance, with additional 274 explosive incidents recorded from January to September 2021. Data collected since January 2016 shows that 56% of all incidents in the BAY states are related to explosive ordnance. Three Local Government Areas ( Gwoza, Konduga and Bama) accounts for more than 35% of these incidents. The final report of the Office of U.S. Foreign Disaster Assistance (OFDA) project implemented by DRC showns an explosive ordnance contamination amounting to 15% . 52.17% contamination rate was also recorded for the current Bureau of Humanitarian affair award within the communities surveyed in Bama, Dikwa, Ngala, Gwoza, Konduga, Madagali and Michika local govermement areas(LGA).The explosive items reported includes high-explosive fragmentation submunitions and other types of explosive ordnance such as mortar, hand grenades, projectiles, rocket, bomb and improvised explosive ordnance. The extent of contamination could likely be higher when the technical specifications of the submunitions are considered as a dispenser can contains many submunition depending on the type. DRC recorded 27.61% of farmlands blocked by the presence of explosive ordnance during the surveys conducted from January to December 2021 in Borno and Adamawa States. Moreover, the recent fighting occurred in Askira Uba LGA in November 2021, could result in the contamination of the vital areas by explosive ordnance. In addition, movements of IDPs were recorded in Askira Uba LGA. 57% of the movements recorded in Askira Uba were triggered by conflicts/attacks and 22 % of the movements were provoked by fear of attacks. The resettlement of people in need could put IDPs and returnees in danger as a result of proximity to explosive devices during farming, collection of firewood and other daily activities in their villages of origin.Information concerning displacement and human mobility in north-east Nigeria shows the presence of 740,595 returnees, both IDPs and refugees from neighboring countries in Borno and 829,594 in Adamawa. furthermore, the recent announcement of the closure of IDP camps by the Borno state government could put the people in danger as a result of proximity to explosive devices during farming, collection of firewood and other daily activities in their village of origin. Cases of such accidents and death of adolescent boys were recorded in Wulugo community in Ngala LGA (Borno State) in June 2020. The continuation of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education and the Non-Techical Survey and the survey of new communities in local government areas like MMC, Jere , Bama, Banki, Dikwa, Ngala, Gwoza and Askira/Uba in Borno and Madagali, Michika in Adamawa will allow people to live and work as safely as possible in the face of explosive weapons until contaminated lands are cleared.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) for persons of concern in IDP camps and Host communities. Target: 122,000 beneficiaries (32,000 Girls, 35,000 boys, 29,000 women, 26,000 men) The EORE aims at reducing the risk related to explosive ordnance by ensuring that women, girls, boys, and men in affected communities are aware of the explosive hazard and the save behaviours to adopt in order to reduce the risk to people, property, and the environment and promote the economic and social development in the conflict affected communities. This activity will target the direct beneficiaries of the protection component in IDP camps and host communities in MMC. Jere, Bama, Banki, Dikwa, Ngala ,Gwoza and Askira Uba (Borno), Madagali, Michika in Adamawa. The EORE direct sessions will take into consideration the vulnerabilities and the needs that mostly put the affected communities at risk such as, collecting firewood (women and girls), farming (men, boys) collecting scrap metals (men, boys, and girls), and preparing sites for resettlement (boys) and returns to locations of origin. The EORE will be done with the use of material that is socially, culturally, and linguistically appropriate. This activity includes trainings to 12 (4F,8M) mixed gender and experience staff and 75 (30F,45M) community focal persons to enable the dissemination of safe behaviour messages in the IDP camps and host communities. The EORE sessions will be complemented with the distribution of leaflets for children and adults and the continuation of sensitization and information sessions on EO risks and safe behaviours in local languages through radio jingles funded by the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office which will end in December 2021. DRC will conduct EORE ToT for 70 ( 30F, 40 M) school teachers . DRC will train and equipped primary and secondary school teachers on EORE. The objective of this training is to build the local capacity to be able to further disseminate the explosive ordnance information with children in schools. The main five days training will be followed by a refresher after every six months. This activity will be done in collaboration with the local education authorities and education organizations. By creating a network of trainers, DRC can ensure the sustainability of EORE activities, which will contribute to the reduction of accident caused by EO. In addition, DRC will building the capacity of 25 (10F,15M) staff from NNGO . For this intervention, DRC proposes to identify and provide EORE refresher trainings to field staff and facilitators of National NGOs involve in mine action activities in Northeast Nigeria. The objective of this training is to build the local capacity of local actors and strengthen the EORE delivery capacity in Borno and Adamawa States. DRC will also train community leaders (Bulamas), scrap metal collectors and women led association members on how to deliver EORE messages. Activity 2: Non-technical Survey (NTS). Target: 70 communities surveyed to identify the potential contaminated areas. To further bolster the EORE and to ensure that the EO reported by community members are identified and removed through the collaboration of the Nigeria Police Force Explosive Ordnance Disposal teams, NTS will be conducted in the 70 new communities in Borno and Adamawa. The objective of the NTS activity is to collect information about the suspicion or the presence of EO to better identify the contaminated or suspected areas. The NTS will address immediate questions about the nature and extent of the hazardous areas and provide information to make all subsequent stages of the land release process more efficient and reliable. This activity will focus on the rural areas to guarantee safe access to farmlands and water. This activity will include coordination with local Explosive Ordnance Disposal operators for the removal of dangerous items.
