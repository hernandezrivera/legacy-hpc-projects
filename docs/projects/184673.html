Project Summary

The intervention seeks to ensure that the most vulnerable crisis affected children have access to safe, protective, inclusive and continuous learning opportunities. There are 3 million crisis-affected children (1,389,125 girls) in need of education assistance during 2022 (Somalia Joint Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment, 2021). Of the people displaced this year, an estimated 237,200 (40%) are school-aged children. In total, there is about 2.9 million displaced people in Somalia and an estimated 1 million of these are school-aged children.
The primary Gross Enrollment Ratio (GER) in primary learning institutions for IDPs is only 16.6%, compared to a national GER of 32%. Culture and traditional views on the role of women and girls have resulted in gendered discrimination of access to education for girls, as boys’ education is more favoured. Gendered discrimination on access to education for girls has also resulted in the feminization of poverty and a cyclical lack of female teachers in schools. Children in the targeted southern parts of Somalia are at risk of dropping out of school due to crisis, with limited changes of re-enrolling. In Banadir 62% households reported not to have been sending children to school. The same for 59% and 54% households reported in Middle Shabelle and Lower Shabelle respectively (Education Needs Analysis Note, 2020). The main reasons for the dropping out include, but are not limited to, the cost of school fees, drought, work to support the household (including domestic chores for girls), early marriage and conflict. IDP children are more at risk and the proportion of IDP and non IDP children in need of humanitarian assistance in southern Somalia is high.
Access to food affects school enrolment and retention in target areas. Children from vulnerable families in targeted southern locations are at risk of completely dropping out of school. Therefore, access to food appears to be a key element in retaining children in school, and NRC will incorporate school feeding to increase both school enrolment and retention rates.
According to the 2021 Rapid Needs Assessment conducted in Doolow (Gedo), the target area is characterized by overcrowded settlements, lack of learning spaces, absence of schools in some locations and lack of teachers. NRC response plan will therefore incorporate recommendations made from the Rapid Needs Assessment which include construction of temporary learning spaces, school feeding, provision of learning materials, Provision of teacher incentives and recruiting qualified teachers/training teachers.
Child protection, disability, psychosocial support and gender will be mainstreamed to ensure an inclusive education system in line with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 4 on ensuring inclusive and equitable education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all. NRC acknowledges that children with disability are disproportionately affected by crisis and experience additional challenges in accessing education and will there employ inclusive approaches to ensure enrolment and retention of children with disabilities.
The education response will support learners with scholastic material and dignity kits for girls. In order to promote safe learning and learner-friendly environments, the response plan will include establishing or rehabilitating learning spaces in line with the Comprehensive School Safety Framework. These facilities will include gender-friendly and disaggregated WASH facilities, safe drinking water, and COVID-19 prevention and control support. Capacity-building initiatives in school management and school safety will be provided to Community Education Committees (CECs). NRC will provide teacher incentives to maintain teachers in schools during crisis and lower the cost of education. To improve the quality of education during the crisis, NRC will also focus on building teachers on pedagogical and psychosocial skills to ensure the learning and wellbeing needs of children are met
