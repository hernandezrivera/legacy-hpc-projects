Project Summary

The project is aiming at responding to the health needs (PHC services, Reproductive Health Services and Cholera response) of the conflict affected persons in Bade and Jakusko LGAs of Yobe state. In the target location, CBI shall be building the capacity of primary healthcare workers on effective delivery of sexual reproductive health services and also facilitating referrals to secondary healthcare facilities as well as follow-up on services for which referrals were made. Support the provision and making available essential drugs and medicine to ten (10) health facilities within the LGAs. CBI will train 60 primary health care workers (30 each per target location) on the services aforementioned. CBI mode of response or operation will be mobile hard-to-reach team approach with regular visit to communities within the selected LGAs to render PHC services to the affected population. CBI will also be providing capacity building on sexual reproductive health services to 40 Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs) and 20 Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs) on practical observational skills around health service delivery in RH as well as responding to Cholera.
CBI shall engage the CHEWs in cholera risk mitigation awareness creation as well as house-to-house sensitization on malaria and other preventable diseases. The 20 CHEWs shall be engaged in the targeted LGAs for both communities and IDP camps within the location.
There will be support of referral services for conflict-affected individual by monitoring the referral cases and see to them been attended to at all level of care within the primary and secondary healthcare facilities, there will be support of drugs and essential medicines and strengthen the capacity of government health workers to be responsive to referrals of the conflict affected people in target LGAs. Children and women referrals will be of highest priority. CBI will target 1,500 affected persons (35% women and men & 65% girls and boys).
CBI will be reaching 400 adolescent girls with an intensive awareness programme on sexual reproductive health education in the selected LGAs. The scope of the awareness programme will be on know their body, pregnancy and how to maintain a healthy and hygienic reproductive life.
CBI, through a strategic partnership with WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA as well as Health Sector and Reproductive Health Sub-sector shall train the CHEWs on minimal initial service package on Reproductive Health (RH). Also, through zero cost strategic partnership, CBI shall work with WHO, UNFPA to support in the strengthening of the referral mechanism in the target LGAs.
For the hard-to-reach and underserved communities within the selected LGAs, CBI shall deploy mobile health outreach teams to provide appropriate health services and support to the people of concern. Part of the services to be provided by the mobile health outreach team include training and overseeing of CHEWs on the Oral Cholera Vaccination (OCV) exercise and distribution of the Reproductive Health (RH Kit) which will be provided by UNFPA through strategic partnership, medical treatments and referral where necessary. The trained TBAs will be engaged with stipends and Delivery kits for attending to births within communities and also do referral where necessary.
CBI will engage the community leaders, religious and traditional leaders in intensive advocacies so as to enable acceptance of the sexual reproductive health services and SRH kits.

1 - Needs

In northeast Nigeria, 5.8 million people including 0.5 million women and 3.9 million children are in need of life-saving and essential health services, amongst which 2 million are women and girls of reproductive age. Displacement and arrivals of people are being recorded regularly both from within the northeast as well as surrounding neighbouring countries of Lake Chad region. Between 4 and 10 October 2021, a total of 3,588 movements were recorded in the states of Adamawa and Borno; consisting of 2,791 arrivals and 681 departures. ETT assessments identified the following movement triggers: voluntary relocation (1,743 individuals or 49%), poor living conditions (815 individuals or 23%), improved security (703 individuals or 20%), conflict/attack (155 individuals or 4%), involuntary relocation (75 individuals or 2%), seasonal farming (51 individuals or 1%) and military operations (46 individuals or 1%). This has further exacerbated the already poor living conditions in which they live. The large numbers of displaced population and poor shelter conditions are affecting privacy with significant concerns especially for girls and women who are at GBV and compromise on menstrual hygiene management for women and girls. Women and children constitute 79% of the overall crises population and 65.7% of the displaced people. Critical needs for dignity, including in menstrual hygiene management remain largely unmet for women and girls of reproductive age, who constitute approximately 34% of the total response population in the total hotspot area. Affected people remain at significant risk of epidemic-prone diseases, like cholera, measles and viral hemorrhagic fevers due to low access to essential healthcare, seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and vulnerabilities as a result of continuous displacement. Women and children are left increasingly susceptible to disease outbreaks, especially cholera. In Yobe state as of epi-week 41, year 2021, the total number of suspected cholera cases in Yobe is 3,468 with 84 associated deaths (CFR 2.4 %). From the beginning of the outbreak in week 22 to the end of week 41 (17 October 2021), 850 cases were reported in Damaturu (6 affected wards), 314 cases in Potiskum (3 affected wards), 502 cases in Nguru (4 affected wards) and 359 cases in Jakusko (1 affected ward). 221 cases were reported in Nangere (4 affected wards), 182 cases in Bade (2 affected wards), 156 cases in Machina (1 affected ward), and 145 cases in Bursari (4 affected wards). Furthermore, 2,160 cases were reported in Fune (in 1 affected ward), 155 cases in Yunusari (1 affected ward), 117 cases in Gujba (2 affected wards), and 109 cases were reported in Karasuwa (1 affected ward). In Geidam LGA, 104 cases were reported (in 2 affected wards), 64 cases were reported in Fika (1 affected ward) and just 30 cases were reported in Gulani LGA. In Borno state; the total number of suspected cholera cases reported as at 17th October 2021 stands at 2,345 with 133 associated deaths, (CFR – 5.6%). Cases from Gwoza - 1,003; Damboa - 117; Hawul - 250; Magumeri - 7; Kaga - 72; MMC - 43; Biu - 93; Jere - 250; Askira Uba - 27; Kwaya Kusar - 13; Konduga - 293; Dikwa - 3; Bayo - 16; Shani - 37; Mafa - 59; and Bama - 62. Ongoing new conflict-related displacement, including the influx of IDPs, refugees and returnees, into overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements in some LGAs, mainly in Borno State, exacerbates the risk of disease outbreaks including cholera, hepatitis, meningitis and measles. It is also clear from assessment reporting and information obtained from sector team, AAH support the state government with about 10 health facilities in Bade and no mobile hard-to-reach services in communities that are far from the facilities. This is also same with JAKUSKO that is been supported by WHO.

2 - Activities

This project is design to save lives by providing needed complementary services in cholera response, integrated PHC services as well as RH services. Increasing access to health services for women and girls in the selected LGAs will be achieved through health education and promotion, outreaches, awareness creation, supporting safe deliveries and improved access to health services in prioritized areas with critical needs through a strengthened referral system.Specifically, activities will include:- Consultation with SPHCDA in the locations for the engagement of PHCWs, CHEWS and TBAs- Training of the aforemention HCWs and CHVs on clean and safe delivery, MISP & RH Kits, service rendering and procedure in an emergency, A/P Natal referrals and sensitizations- SRHE Training for 400 adolescent girls- Provision of free quality Primary Health Services via supported health facilities in the LGAs- Procurement and distribution of emergency drugs, delivery kits and essential supplies to the 10 supported health facilities, TBAs and Mobile health team- Distribution of RH Kits- Case finding/management and conduct of clean and safe deliveries for PLWs- Facilitate referrals of identified health cases to health facilities and supportive supervision of CHEWs and TBAs by skilled midwifes.- Provision of preventive consultations (ANC, PNC, HP and counselling)- Community sensitization on the prevention of communicable diseases and the importance of oral vaccination- Set-up a community based EWS involving community leaders and CHVs and participate in the EWARS

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

As a core value in planning and implementation of all programs and activities, CBI, advocate, employ “do no harm” approach which implies taking cognizance of protection risks or potential violations of rights of members of target communities or simply put, DOING HARM PRINCIPLE to intended solace to community members or target beneficiaries. CBI staff have been trained on protection issues by Protection actors and can identify and refer all cases of GBV, child abuse etc. Through the proposed projects in the areas, and so we will ensure that adequate Do No Harm analyses are being conducted and that participatory methods in all aspects of the project cycle are implemented. All services to be provided will be designed to ensure safe and easy access for all taking into account vulnerable person with specific needs (women, children, elderly, etc). Our monitoring tools will also include protection mainstreaming indicators as well as questions related to protection mainstreaming in all potential data to be collected so that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. CBI, also ensure that marginalized groups Women, Children and people living with disabilities) in the communities are carried along in process and outcomes of all interventions. Thus, in the course of executing this project, due care will be taken towards ensuring that fairness is being done to gender equity. This means due consideration is given to the inclusion of issues pertaining to the life and the life chances of men and women.On the side of disability, CBI has always allowed for representation of persons living with disability in the course of planning and implementation of its programs and activities. The design and delivery of activities should also factor in the special needs of people living with disability. Regarding conflict sensitivity, CBI is always conscious of the local norms and conflicts dynamics. We will therefore work with all the relevant stakeholders to identify any key concerns and factors that could lead to resistance/spoilage. In summary, CBI will employ the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) which does not only look the community as beneficiary but as partner: it recognizes the need to ensure people affected by crisis are not only informed and consulted about the core humanitarian but at the center of decision-making process.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CBI will set up Accountability and Quality Improvement Committee (AQIC) which comprises of Government health workers, community (IDP and host community) leaders and religious leaders who we directly and will continue to liaise and related with on the issues of accountability and feedback mechanism. This committee will be having monthly meetings and discuss several issues affecting the community. During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to health will be raised and aired and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners and sectors implementing in the location. Also, CBI shall use the existing compliant mechanisms to receive feedback from the beneficiaries of the project and community members that might have any concern on the implementation of the project, all the received complaints, concerns and other forms of feedback will be address accordingly and immediately. The religious and community leaders will take part in project monitoring, ensuring that the project benefits the target population. Monthly meeting will be done with the religious and community leaders and the team of the health facilities to discuss the service utilization, attitude of health worker toward the patients and any other relevant aspect that needs to be addressed. The performance of the community volunteer work and the messages sent will be discussed as well. At all stages, any complaints from the patients will be reported to the in-charge of the health facilities or the head of the community leader who will have daily contact with the health team. Beneficiaries to the intervention will be provided with feedback using Community-based approach/mechanism which is accessible at all time.
