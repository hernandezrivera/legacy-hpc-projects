Project Summary

In 2021 Shafak will continue working in the below mentioned locations under the UNICEF, UNFPA, GIZ, WFP, SCHF, and SCI support. The expected targets will be the following locations if the security situation will not be changed and the agreement with the donors will not be terminated:
Aleppo,Jebel Saman, Atareb (3 RRTs, 2 OTPs, TSFP)
Idleb, Idleb,Maaret Tamsrin (1 SC , 3 RRTs, TSFP)
In 2021, Shafak is going to establish an additional number of Rapid Response Teams and ensure the presence of emergency lifesaving integrated nutrition services in the operational areas where there is no service provided. With the new Rapid Response Teams, Shafak will fill the current service gap and provide emergency integrated health, nutrition, and child protection rapid services such as triage, emergency aid, and referral services.
Shafak proposes to scale up the nutrition intervention to cover the below-mentioned areas that are targeted under the WFP (GFA and BSFP) project, and after the coordination with nutrition cluster to avoid any duplication to cover the gaps:
Aleppo, A'zaz, Aghtrin: (GFA,BSFP,TSFP,1 RRT).
Aleppo, A'zaz, Mare: (GFA,BSFP,TSFP,1 RRT).
Aleppo,Afrin,Jandairis: (GFA,BSFP,TSFP, 1RRT).

4 - Needs

According to Shafak Organization's database and reports, Skafak 2020 had screened 65795 children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) for acute malnutrition, and more than 31738 of these were provided with micronutrient supplements. As a result of screening activities, over 788 children and PLWs were identified as severely malnourished and received lifesaving treatment. An overview regarding the situation in NWS and according to the last WoS Humanitarian Situation Report, a new movement of Internally Displaced People (IDPs) has been reported during September, and Displaced populations and host communities in northwest Syria (NWS), particularly children and women, continued to bear the brunt of the conflict, facing challenges to secure their daily basic needs. Based on 2021 HNO, chronic malnutrition cases among children of age 6-59 months continue to exist, as in August where the estimated prevalence was 33 percent. However, this means that one out of three children in NWS is losing her/his future potential, with developmental and cognitive growth hampered because of stunting. Additionally, the last development report shared by the Cluster shows a deteriorating nutrition situation in northwest Syria as indicated by monthly nutrition surveillance data with increasing levels of both acute and chronic malnutrition, particularly among IDPs. One-third of children aged 6-59 months are stunted and the proxy prevalence of acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women (PLW) is at 11 percent. In compliance with the adopted ToR of the RRTs, they will also responsible for identifying and referring specific cases, both women and children, to protection services as needed.

5 - Implementation of activities

In accordance with Shafak's cross-border strategy, we will be responsible for direct implementation in the target locations coordinating with and gaining approval from local authorities in Syria, building on their extensive network, and facilitating access. Shafak will be responsible for ensuring that quality and accountability standards are met, and will provide required training to staff in order to ensure that contractual requirements are adhered to and met. Further, Shafak will be responsible for all program and financial reporting to the donor and will also continue to coordinate with relevant clusters and other coordination bodies in Turkey. This joint program will be conducted through three main phases as the following: Phase I: - Recruitment of needed staff (Health/Nutrition) - Procurement of needed furniture, tools for training and screening. - Training sessions. Phase II: - IYCF activities. - Facility and community-based screening activities. - CMAM services. - Outpatient services. - Treatment. -Follow up/ Referral. Phase III: - Reporting, data analysis, monitoring and evaluation. Shafak Organization will run the project and its activity with a complementarity with the pre-existing nutrition teams, existing nutritionists, and community health workers who are already providing the nutrition service in Shafak health facilities. Service providers: The RRT service providers will be the team in each area; each team will consist of 1 nutritionist, 2 CHWs and 1 data worker. During the implementation of the proposed project activities, the project team will ensure community interests respected, and project objectives and activities are transparent and understood by the local population.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Shafak will apply the Nutrition sector’s Protection Risk Analysis for detailed guidance on the expected risks. The project overall objective aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual where they need it the most. Throughout the project life cycle the principles of protection mainstreaming are incorporated. All activities are designed to avoiding causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity of targeted populations, insuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation through several, action such as: • The project is designed to meet the needs of the community especially women, boys and girls considering the context, tradition, and norms in the community • Location of facilities will be accessible and easy to reach for all people., also safe and away from any military location. • Community will be clearly and widely informed about the date, location and timing of the service provision, and their opinion will be highly considered. • Team staff will be trained to be skilled in delivering PFA, identifying CP&GBV cases and refer them to adequate services. • Insure that the location is suitable for women, girls, people with disabilities, children headed household, old people, and other affected categories in the community--- outreach. • Distribution and providing services for specific categories mentioned above will be to the door. • Teams will be trained on PESA, Child safeguarding, and will sign the code of conduct related. • project team will set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need, provide feedback on the activities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Airstrikes and Shelling over NWS Shafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk: • security assessment of the operational areas to avoid sites that have been targeted before. • Prevent and decrease crowds in front of the center. • Equipping the staff with VHFs. • Training the staff on how to deal in cases of shelling and conduct practical training. • Equipping the center with fire extinguishers • An evacuation plan for center ready and communicated to all staff • An emergency contact list is available at the center. • Staff to be aware of communication protocols Risk 2: closing of Bab al-Hawa BCP that can resulted in a shortage of consumables, equipment, and kits. Shafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk: • Monitoring the general security situation in the border area. • provide each center with consumables and medical kits that are enough for each center to work for 3 months at least. • using a different route for securing the supply chain process instead of Bab al-Hawa BCP by using Bab al-Slama BCP near Azaz in northern-countryside of Aleppo. Risk 3: Deviation of aid. Shafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk: • Informing field team not to comply with any request from a person or armed faction to give them vouchers. • Stopping the work and withdrawal from the center, relocation also can be an option according to the situation, if there is any threat from the military factions, and then report the subject case to our donor. • An ATC Check to be done for all suppliers\ vendors before signing the contract with them.
