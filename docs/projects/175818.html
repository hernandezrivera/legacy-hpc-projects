Project Summary

Due to prolonged exposure to conflict and high levels of vulnerability, South Sudan remains in protection crisis. Human rights violations, abductions, conflict-related sexual violence, natural and manmade hazards e.g. flooding, forced military recruitment and negative social norms continues to risk lives of children and their families. The COVID 19 pandemic effects has negatively impacted the psychosocial wellbeing of children due to limited access to basic needs e.g. food, child labor, child marriage, mental health and psychosocial distress, domestic violence and family separations.
CMMB in partnership with UNICEF has been delivering comprehensive integrated child protection service in Western Equatoria, including case management, psychosocial support services, reunification of children, child safe spaces and placing children in temporary foster care. However limited funding and access issues has made it difficult to provide protection services to all vulnerable children in targeted areas. Using CMMB’s presence and experience in all the 3 targeted counties, the proposed project activities will be integrated and leveraged with other interventions e.g. health, nutrition, and protection services. Particularly Mvolo county will be accorded special attention given the enormous identified child protection concerns with minimal response integrating protection and nutrition services.
To strengthen psychosocial well-being of vulnerable children, both mobile and static structured, age and sex appropriate psychosocial support will be delivered through community based psychosocial support structures reaching 1750 boys and 1750 girls and strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to identify, refer, and support children with protection issues. Provision of life skills will enhance resilience of vulnerable boys and girls hence will be incorporated in PSS programs including adolescents and youths. Equally parents/caregivers will be equipped with basic parental skills through parenting sessions that will enhance protection of children at the family level. Focused on enhancing well-being of boys and girls in selected payams in Mvolo,Yambio and Nzara counties, the project will work with relevant stakeholders, to provide comprehensive child protection case management services including referrals to specialized services and cash based assistance for 200 unaccompanied and separated children as well as those exposed to violence and other protection concerns.
The project will seek to strengthen capacity of child protection services through training of staff and volunteers on case management, mentorship, and supervision to improve the quality of service delivery. 20 foster/kinship caregivers will be identiﬁed, trained and supported to provide alternative care for children in need. Family tracing and reuniﬁcation of 100 unaccompanied and separated children will be strengthened through working with volunteers in the local communities, Save the Children, UNICEF, and other partners to identify, follow up and refer UASC/released children who will also be linked with appropriate services and reunified.
To address the vacuum in child protection services, the project will empower communities to understand, identify, adequately support and/or refer children with protection concerns. 13199 community members including children will be sensitized on key child protection issues guided by current protection concerns e.g. early marriages, family separation and effects of COVID 19 pandemic, to prevent/reduce shocks trauma and mental health issues in children. 15 CP and non-CP members will be trained on CPIE. Child Protection referral pathways will be developed with other key stakeholders to link children and communities with various life-saving services. The aim is to ensure children are supported and protected, as they are the most important assets of a nation and its future.

1 - Needs

As per the 2021 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs overview(HNO), South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2020. Human rights violations, arbitrary killings, injuries, abductions, conflict-related sexual violence, forced military recruitment, looting and destruction of civilian property and extremely limited livelihood opportunities put already vulnerable people, including IDPs, at increased risk. COVID-19 related restrictions limited provision of protection services e.g. PSS and case management. Child protection concerns increased especially with school closure that exposed children to neglect, abuse and/or exploitation. The COVID-19 pandemic increased risks to children, families/communities; mental health and psychosocial distress, intimate partner violence, family separations, violence against children, and risk of exploitation e.g. child labor. Western Equatoria is no exception to the challenges above, exacerbated by deeply rooted negative social norms e.g. child marriages and negative coping mechanisms that lead to teenage pregnancies and recruitment into armed groups. Natural disaster incidents continue to increase, and in 2020, parts of Western Equatoria including Nzara county was affected by flooding that displaced families and destroyed homes and household assets, further plunging families into displacement and poverty(Flood Incidence Fact Finding Report, 14th November 2020).As per SS IPC results(2020-2021) food security situation is expected to deteriorate as the lean season progresses,food stock get depleted increasing market reliance. 13.5% of the population in WES likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse of which 29,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Mundri East, Mundri West, Mvolo and Nagero counties are expected to be in IPC 3. Physical access to food will be difficult,roads become impassable due rains. The effects of the economic crisis, currency devaluation and COVID-19 measures are likely to contribute to household food insecurity further accelerating protection needsIt is evident that the underlying causes of the issues affecting children described are multifaceted and demands multi-pronged approach addressing child protection in different settings including CAFAAG children, separated children, teenage single parents, incarcerated children, and children in IDP settlements. All these children struggle silently with trauma and mental health issues. Lack of adequate CP actors and resources,prolonged conﬂict has deepened suffering and increased vulnerability. This project aims at strengthening local capacity to provide protection, safe spaces,PSS to child survivors, parent coping strategies, community support and government protection services. Cluster forums will be important in sharing data and best practices. As the Covid-19 spread spikes in South Sudan, Covid-19 prevention will be mainstreamed in all activities, including targeted and robust education, awareness, and support with PPEs. Through the CMMB Covid-19 care and treatment response project, this project will seek to ensure referral pathways for vulnerable children are established and Covid-19 care and treatment staff are trained on their responsibility to protect children in their care and understand/uphold child safeguarding policies at all times.CMMB in Partnership with UNICEF has provided Child protection services in WES for over 6 years, working closely with the responsible State ministries and the relevant clusters. This project will build on the gains made and extend activities to all vulnerable children in the target area including the physically impaired and those in hard to reach areas. In seeking to address trauma and other mental health issues, the project will promote social skills of the targeted children through play, singing, dancing and sports, and story-telling, to create a safe space to reflect, forgive and heal.

2 - Project activities

CMMB will work with beneficiaries to offer comprehensive case management services, community based psychosocial support services; parenting skills sessions; family tracing and reunification services for unaccompanied and separated children(UASC); strengthening of community based child protection mechanisms to identify, refer, and support children with protection concerns. Awareness raising will address prevailing protection issues and prevent/reduce risk of recruitment of children by armed groups and family separation. Child protection in emergencies training will build capacity of staff and community structures including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse with special attention on children living with disabilities.Provision of both mobile and static structured community based psychosocial support services will be enhanced to improve mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of children and COVID-19 effects targeting 1750 boys and 1750 girls across the 3 counties. The child protection team will work with nutrition teams/community structures to provide PSS and parenting sessions for 420boys,720girls,2200women and 1400men to strengthen a safe environment for children.15 CFS animators/volunteers;5 men,10 women will be equipped with relevant PSS skills and GBV case management. Adolescent/youth peer to peer support groups will strengthen their coping skills towards life and build strong social networks using the UNICEF adolescent kits and life skills sessions. CP referral pathways will be developed/revived and disseminated to link children and communities with lifesaving services.A strengthened case management system will ensure vulnerable children receive specialized care based on individual protection needs and vulnerability including referrals. Based on the IASC case management guidelines, children’s individual care plans will be developed, implemented, and followed up to ensure identified needs are met.200 children(100 boys and 100 girls) will be targeted for comprehensive case management services including cash based assistance/nonfood items based on identified protection needs. To ensure that vulnerable girls and boys receive quality family tracing and reunification services, the project team will work with communities and other stakeholders to identify, document, trace and reunify 100 UASCs(50boys,50girls) with their families/caregivers providing other case management services. To enhance protection of vulnerable children,15 foster/kinship caregivers;10 women,5 men will be identiﬁed, trained and equipped to provide alternative care within communities of origin. To ensure quality service delivery,14 social workers and community volunteers(10 women and 4men) will be trained on case management, mentored, and supervised to support children.Community awareness raising on prevailing child protection issues, response and how to prevent/reduce risk of recruitment of children by armed groups/forces, family separation and COVID 19 prevention strategies. 4000women, 3199 men, 3000 girls and 3000 boys will be targeted through campaigns, door to door visits, community events etc. Establishment of child help desks in communities, nutrition sites and schools will disseminate key child protection messages, identify, and refer children with protection concerns. Community complaint and feedback mechanisms will be established across project sites to enhance accountability to affected populations by soliciting, receiving, processing, and responding to complaints received from beneficiaries including sexual exploitation and abuse. 15 child protection and non-CP staff(10women and 5men) will be trained on CPIE.CMMB, the current co-chair of the state child protection working group will continue to support strengthening coordination and reducing duplication of services.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

i. Functioning markets continue to be supplied as markets have not been locked down, and goods from Sudan are encouraged through easy access at the borders in the North.ii. Preferred and local food items are available although purchasing power continues to dip with the deepening economic crisis. Hence cash transfer will help access better nutrition and sustain market access.iii. Markets use local currency, and the same currency will be used in the cash transfer scope.iv. Markets are within the targeted Payams and so access, safety and security is currently unaffected.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash or vouchers provides individually tailored immediate support to victims of GBV and reduces the risk of negative coping strategies that exposes vulnerable women and girls to GBV. Cash and vouchers require functional markets and the target areas selected in this project have access to functional markets. Yambio also has cash transfer agents that will reduce the risk related with cash and voucher security risks. Cash and vouchers approaches respond better in Covid-19 restrictions and lockdowns, as families can stock dry and staple food for longer periods, and buy them time, while they resuscitate their livelihoods.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA Focal Points will be identified and trained across project sites. They will conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men from targeted communities on their rights and entitlements through posters,radio, videos,community meetings, focus group discussions and information pamphlets. Assessment and mapping of potential risk areas for children and recommending protective actions to combat SEA and having appropriate feedback mechanisms will be necessary. Community feedback mechanisms will be enhanced through volunteers, meetings with children and community, focus group discussions and displaying available complaint options/suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations. The project places emphasis on affected population’s ability to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Community child protection committees are empowered to enhance protection of affected and at-risk children working with caregivers. The project will endeavor to respond to their needs based on their level of vulnerability. CFS are within safe and central locations to ensure children do not walk long distances nor get exposed to unsafe locations. Approved information sharing protocols e.g. CPIMS tools will minimize exposure of sensitive information. Limited access to project sites by children living with disabilities will be addressed through engaging community volunteers who might identify and refer such children. Adolescents will be supported to organize activities with children.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project team will design appropriate tools for collecting information from each of the project beneficiaries tailored to their needs eg for individuals who are illiterate meetings where one can express their feelings would be convened. Pictures will also be used to express various feelings over a certain issue including levels of satisfaction. Separate focus group discussions

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will ensure beneficiaries are updated on the project activities through conducting a start up workshop to launch the project and explain what it entails,key activities,roles and responsilities of each stakeholder and where to report incase of concerns. Project meetings at the community level with beneficiaries will deliver key details of the project,conducting focus group discussions and making public announcements through radio and other community forums. The feedback mechanisms will be enhanced through broad engagement of representatives when identifying the most vulnerable members that would include community leaders who may be knowing these people within their communities, indigenous people, minority groups, women, children and people living with disabilities among others. Maximum representation at all stages of project management cycle would ensure that their concerns are taken care of and appropriate interventions designed including providing all relevant information. Open dialogues with the various beneficiary groups will keep track of any positive and/or negative impacts and ensure that corrective measures are taken early enough. This will also allow them freedom to engage with the project teams as appropriate as they have a neutral forum for interactions. Available feedback options will be shared including contacts where the beneﬁciaries can directly report their concerns without involving project teams. Regular monthly meetings at the CFS would encourage children to share their concerns and jointly ﬁnd appropriate solutions. The CFS sites would be centrally agreed with beneﬁciaries including children to reduce conﬂict and cater for their individual needs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project team will jointly discuss with the beneficiaries on areas that need improvement and get their suggestions. This would be implemented. Specific activities would also be reviewed to align with beneficiary concerns and in designing future projects lessons learnt would be used to improve design and incorporate relevant changes.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Provision of comprehensive child protection case management, child survivors of GBV will be assisted to access PSS and medical care. Based on need, consent, availability and relevance, referrals will be done for other services i.e. legal support, safety and medical care for service deliver points not linked to health facilities. Appropriate protocols and guidelines will be followed in providing both protection related and other services. These services will take into account age, gender, and priority.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Through the livelihood and cash assistance that will be provided after analyzing various shocks, identified beneficiaries will be provided with support that will help alleviate their level of suffering. Cash or vouchers provides individually tailored immediate support to children with protection concerns and survivors of GBV as per the vulnerability criteria to help reduce the risk of negative coping strategies that exposes children to various protection concerns eg child labour, early/forced marriage and survival sex.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The design of the project activities has taken into consideration the needs of various vulnerable groups hence delivery will be customized to suit identified needs. Where possible integration of service delivery will be emphasized to maximize use of resources and avoid beneficiaries covering long distances between various services. Community structures e.g. volunteers will be engaged to reach old people, sick, people with disabilities and young children with services to ensure they do not miss out on accessing GBV, health and Nutrition services.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

CMMB is implementing a 3 year Nutrition program in WES funded by UNICEF, in the targeted counties, including Mvolo. Through a CMMB Integrated program approach, this project will refer all identified incidence of acute malnutrition in the targeted communities of both Children under five, as well as pregnant and lactating women and the elderly. The program will work with the Nutrition program to ensure mothers for children under 5 are provided with education on nutrition related awareness, including food preparation, kitchen gardening and hygiene. This project will also collaborate with CMMB’s WASH program to ensure the HHs of children with severe acute malnutrition are provided with water purification sachets for clean drinking water. The project also aims to undertake a cash distribution activity to support identified vulnerable families, including children under 5 in severity levels 3 and 4.
