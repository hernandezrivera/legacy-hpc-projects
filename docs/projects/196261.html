Project Summary

SAADO's proposed project will provide comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response interventions to the floods and conflict affected communities in Ayod, Duk, Fangak and Malakal Counties in Jonglei and Upper Nile states respectively. The proposal will target 4,000 beneficiaries (1000 boys;1000 men;1000 women;1000 girls) in Malakal County and 10,672 beneficiaries (1974 boys, 2436 girls,2852 men and 3410 women) in Ayod, Duk and Fangak Counties of Jonglei state. SAADO proposes to implement comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors.
The proposed project seeks to implement GBV prevention, response and risk mitigation activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors. Further, the proposed project will provide cash assistance to the most vulnerable women and girls visiting WGFSs who are facing financial challenges to facilitate them in accessing multi-sectoral GBV services. Priority will be given to survivors who require clinical management of rape (CMR) and survivors of Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) so that they can continue to visit health facilities, access for legal aid and they can also access the nearest available centers and safe spaces that provide PSS.
This proposed project is designed to improve responses to cases of GBV and enhance the ability of survivors to recover from GBV incidences. SAADO will provide individual psychosocial support for women and girls in established women and girls' safe spaces in the targeted locations. The project's proposed activities will also include Strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group, GBV awareness and risk mitigation measures, Strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions, Strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of risk mitigation, GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support, through the provision of need based capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers and strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways. The activities in the established WGFS will be conducted using the guidelines from the ministry of health and World Health Organization (WHO) on COVID-19 prevention and will include maintaining social distance, wearing of masks and washing hands with soap. SAADO also intends to strengthen gender-based violence prevention through EMAP and community care programs to reduce GBV risks on men, women, boys and girls.
The targeted locations have been selected because of the prevalent needs stated in the HNO 2023 and appearing in category 5 and 4 in the severity mapping as well as SAADO's long standing presence and GBV programming in Duk, Ayod, Fangak and Malakal Counties through implementation of CERF funded GBV Prevention & Response in Ayod including management of two (2) WGFS in Jiech and Mogok payams; GBV violence risk mitigation in Schools in Duk including management of (2) WGFS in Duk Pajut and Duk Pagak and four (4) WGFS established in Duk Poktap and Padiet and implementation of the South Sudan Humanitarian Funded (SSHF) GBV Prevention and Response project in Fangak since August 2022.
The proposed project will build upon and complement SAADO's currently ongoing GBV prevention and response projects in the targeted locations. SAADO will coordinate this proposed response with the GBV sub cluster to ensure that the proposed activities are not duplicated and there is no overlap of activities and will work with health partners such as HRSS, CARE and John Dau Foundation to refer survivors for Post Rape services.

1 - Needs

In Jonglei state, the UNMISS report (2022) report cites violence, abuse and exploitation as the greatest protection risks to women, girls, boys and men including the disabled and the elderly reflecting continued gender inequalities exacerbated by the conflict and the prolonged crisis in South Sudan. The GBViMS statistics (2022) reports that there has been an increase in GBV cases reported in the second half of 2022 compared to the same period in 2021. During the same period 97 per cent of reported cases in South Sudan affected women and girls, and 21 per cent of survivors were children, of which 79 per cent were adolescent girls. Physical violence, commonly by an intimate partner or someone known by the survivor, continues to be the most common form of GBV, accounting for 42 per cent of reported cases. According to the IRNA assessment Report, Jonglei (August 22, 2022) the recent fighting between Agwelek, Kitgwang and SPLA-IO forces in Tonga, New Fangak and Canal/Pigi has exacerbated the already dire humanitarian situation in Jonglei and Upper Nile states leading to displacement of the entire population of New Fangak and Tonga who are currently seeking safety in Malakal and Old Fangak Counties. This is further corroborated by an Inter-agency rapid needs assessment conducted on August 22, 2022 in Old Fangak where an influx of IDPs has been witnessed. This desperate situation has increased the GBV needs in the proposed counties which remain severe as violence and violations persist as a reality of daily life for girls, women and children. The increased presence of mixed populations such as returnees and IDPs has exerted pressure on the few services available in these locations thereby exacerbating protection risks and increasing the need for GBV prevention and response interventions according to Fangak IRNA report September 2022. The GBViMS statistics (2022) further indicate that 97% of the reported GBV incidents survivors were females (women and girls) with intimate partners accounting for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Access to GBV services for the targeted locations remain a major challenge owing to the limited availability of GBV prevention and response services. A multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted by SAADO in October 2022 in Fangak County indicated that GBV is more prevalent within the households and mostly perpetrated by intimate partners. In addition, majority of the community members who participated in the key informant interviews and FGD acknowledged that the prevalence of GBV was high in their communities as they mentioned to have witnessed GBV related incidents perpetrated almost on a daily basis. This is largely attributed to the deep-rooted cultural beliefs, attitudes and perception about women and girls that contribute significantly to unequal power dynamic among men and women, hence resulting to normalizing violence against women within the agro-pastoral communities. In addition, fear of retaliation through revenge killings was mentioned to be a major barrier in accessing the available services by survivors. Across the four counties the GBV situation has further been exacerbated by the insecurity situation, flooding and COVID-19 pandemic.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through having tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions made by the project; In addition, consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard),as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A core component of SAADO’s beneficiary accountability and communication in this proposed intervention will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure that beneficiaries are provided with information about PSEA through tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; In addition, consultations with women and men(separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), will be undertaken as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and will include confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To adapt beneficiaries feedback into the project assistance, SAADO's proposed project will encourage communities to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will ensure there is adequate security using triangulated information from the UN,RRC and NGO forum at the distribution points. The project will also ensure that security protocols are in place when conducting activities bringing beneficiaries together. The project will ensure there is proper awareness creation with beneficiaries aware of their project entitlements and project staff trained on how to recognize PSEA and how to handle it using confidential means. The project will utilize technology to ensure that beneficiary data is protected and project staff will have limits of authorization with passwords provided. Risk of interference from external stakeholders: The project will utilize a highly transparent approach of beneficiary selection that will involve the local authorities, traditional structures and community structures to ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are selected as beneficiaries.
