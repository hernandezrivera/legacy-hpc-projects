Project Summary

Years of violent conflict have left millions of South Sudanese displaced as the country is facing one of the most devastating humanitarian crises in recent history. According to the 2019 South Sudan HNO, nearly 6 million people were facing WASH needs. Having sufficient access to water is a basic necessity of life, and along with health, are pre-conditions that need to be met for people to be able to not only participate and conduct their livelihoods, but also to thrive and develop.
Therefore, ACTED will aim to implement WASH interventions in 2020 in line with the following cluster objectives:
1. To provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, refugees, returnees and non-displaced affected populations. ACTED plans to provide potable water to a minimum of 40,000 beneficiaries (IDPs, host communities, returnees and refugees), through rehabilitation/drilling of 55 boreholes in Maridi, Ibba, Duk, and Maban.
2. To integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level. By implementing a WASH prevention and preparedness through water quality testing, targeting both Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) and bacteriological testing of water sources.
3. To mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence through ensuring gender appropriate 1500 latrines constructions and distribution of 500 menstrual hygiene materials.

1 - Needs

- Years of violent conflict have left millions of South Sudanese displaced as the country is facing one of the most devastating humanitarian crises in recent history. According to the 2019 South Sudan HNO, nearly 6 million people were facing WASH needs. Having sufficient access to water is a basic necessity of life, and along with health, are pre-conditions that need to be met for people to be able to not only participate and conduct their livelihoods, but also to thrive and develop. - Maridi and Ibba: Based on the assessment conducted by ACTED, lack of access to water and health issues have been highlighted as two of the main needs across all the rural communities in Maridi, with only 59% of respondents reporting a borehole or well is their main source of drinking water, and 64% reporting taking over 30 mins to access a water point (ACTED assessment June/July 2019). ACTED recently conducted a gendered needs-assessment in September 2019, which also showed that the main gaps were safe access to water for women, men, girls and boys, and a lack of incentives to dig latrines for all. Shallow wells had been constructed in the area, but had dried up during the dry seasons. The nearest access to a water point takes two hours, as the boreholes in the area have been broken and need to be rehabilitated. Therefore, the nearest access to the waterpoint identified in Maridi is a river stream, which is not safe for household consumptions and contamination has been raised as an immediate concern during the needs-assessment. ACTEDs assessment shows that this has put a time constraint on women, girls and boys as it is culturally expected for them to collect the water.- Maban: Due to the high refugee population in Maban (147,633), there is an extremely high need to maintain and improve the water supply to the refugee camps, which involves a large complex network of infrastructure such as boreholes, distribution pipelines, water storage tanks and tap stands. The water supply systems in all four Maban refugee camps rely on external inputs, particularly in terms of fuel and spare parts, and therefore require a significant amount of inputs and services to maintain. While progress has been made in 2018 and 2019 in improving the access to safe water and hygiene practices, the continued provision of water services and hygiene promotion is required. As a result of this population influx, Maban host community has witnessed significant changes in their land and environment since 2011, and competition for scarce resources have led to several episodes of tensions between these different communities. Most specifically, tensions have crystallized over land grazing, water, fuel sources, and rampant deforestation as a result of charcoal burning and timber harvesting. Recurrent and recent floods in Maban have showed high imminent need in WASH services both in-camps and host communities (Inter-Agency situation report, October 2019).and - Duk: According to the 2019 HNO, 72,100 people are in need of WASH services in Duk. ACTED’s CCCM mobile team conducted a joint assessment with DRC’s Mobile Protection Unit from May 21 to 28 May 2019 in Duk County, Jonglei State. With the influx of returnees since January 2019, the community in the locations assessed in Duk reported insufficient access to water. On average, each location has 5-7 operating boreholes, non-functional borehole requires functionality test but was reported to have stopped functioning in 2014. Latrine construction is extremely difficult due to the quality of the sandy soil. Initiatives by the community often face this obstacle especially during the rainy season. Open defecation is practiced by the majority of the community. The latest IPC update (September 2019) shows that IPC phase 4 (emergency) has persisted due to broadly seasonal flooding which destroyed crops, limited food and income sources among displaced households and disrupted delivery of assistance and market functioning.

2 - Activities

1. Increased timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe and sufficient water for a minimum of 40,000 IDPs, host communities, and returnees and 147,633 refugees:1.1 Drilling, rehabilitation and flushing of 55 boreholes and waterpoints in targeted areas. Borehole operators will ensure daily operation and maintenance of pumps and generators, refueling, as well as monitoring water production levels and fuel use to ensure efficiency. ACTED will target the supply of an average of a minimum of 15 litres of water per person per day (l/p/d) in each camp in line with the SPHERE Standards, to ensure sufficient potable water is provided for all refugees. 1.2 Maintaining and monitoring water quality: To guarantee the safety of water, water treatment (chlorination) in all targeted locations, will be carried out at the source (boreholes) or at storage facilities through the use of Dosatron dozers and batch chlorination. Water quality testing will also be conducted targeting both Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) and bacteriological testing. FRC monitoring will be conducted regularly at selected water points to check the residual chlorine levels, ensuring they remain at the required standard (between 0.3-0.5 mg/l). Bacteriological testing will be conducted monthly using H2S bacteriological strips and/or Delagua kits at hand pump and household (sampled) levels. When contamination has been detected, ACTED will treat the water and disinfect the water point. This activity is also part of the disease prevention and preparedness due to recurrent seasonal floods. 1.3 Water Management Committees (WMCs) - ACTED will support formation of WMCs representative of the target population (with women at least 55%) as appropriate. They will be trained in hand pump rehabilitation and given a toolkit and spare parts to ensure good maintenance. They will play a key role in community sensitization, support the identification of beneficiaries, and act as a formal feedback mechanism.2 Sanitation and hygiene:2.1 WASH in schools, teacher training and hygiene promotion will be carried out in targeted schools to encourage behavioral change through child-to-child exchange. WASH friendly schools will be promoted by conducting activities and campaigns on the benefits of hand washing, the Safe Water Chain and proper waste disposal.2.2 ACTED will conduct hygiene promotion activities in all four refugee camps in Maban. ACTED will aim to improve hygiene knowledge and practices of the refugee communities through a range of ongoing activities, including hygiene campaigns and community events, door-to-door household visits, and school hygiene clubs, in order to deliver key messages on hygiene-related topics. 2.3 ACTED will target the most vulnerable girls and young adolescence in targeted locations to receive menstrual hygiene materials (MHM). ACTED will distribute a total of 500 MHM kits in primary schools (girls-boarding schools), and will have a community sensitization and training on use of MHM kits. These beneficiaries will be identified through GBV risk analysis conducted in collaboration with GBV partners. 2.4 Facilitate construction of 1500 HH latrines (940 in camps), targeting high-density population areas with high risk of communicable diseases, prevalent open defecation and lowest ratios of HH/latrines using PHAST. Tools, some materials and technical support will be provided to the beneficiaries for construction. HH will be responsible for providing local materials to increase sense of ownership. Hygiene promotion teams will sensitize households on use and maintenance of latrines, including decommissioning. 2.5 When appropriate, ACTED will implement a CfW component for the construction of latrines to ensure immediate access to sanitation and hygiene, ACTED will target 80% men and 20% women as CfW beneficiaries to reduce the burden on women in male-headed households, and to ensure inclusivity for female headed households who currently dig latrines as unpaid work.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

ACTED will work with UNHCR and other operational HLP expert partners to seek solutions to high-priority cases in collaboration with existing initiatives. This will be supported by broader local authority engagement initiatives.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

ACTED will work closely with specialist partners such as UNHCR to identify where it would be appropriate for activities to support durable solutions. Where this is the case, ACTED will hold discussion fora and trainings to support individuals to identify, unwrap and take steps to resolve safety issues and risks faced by those in the communities, focusing specifically on children and women. Key target groups will be teachers, religious leaders, influential community figures, youth and new parents. Activities will involve risk mapping and risk mitigation planning and this represents a “soft” component of understanding better specific issues faced and specific solutions identified at a local level.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

As discussed above, the project will work closely with local stakeholders including community leadership, state leadership and the RRC.In Maban, ACTED will continue to work closely with community leaders, local authorities, UNHCR, and other humanitarian partners both inside the refugee camps and in the surroundings.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with the goals to ensure durable solutions for returning IDPs and refugees.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

This project will not directly target durable solutions activities. However, if the environment becomes conducive to returns in areas where the project may operate then it will contribute to fostering a secure environment for the safe, informed, voluntary and dignified return or relocation of IDPs and refugees.Implementation and success of the R-ARCSS remains a key assumption as it links directly with the perception of safety and security of IDPs and refugees. This move to a transitional government symbolizes cooperation and political will from higher levels of government to strive for the peace and development of South Sudan. ACTED will draw on the experience and expertise of UNHCR and UNMISS, as well as protection and other partners to identify whether conditions are met. Only then will any activities related to solutions comments, in close coordination with local leadership, RRC and other humanitarian partners.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

ACTED takes an approach in WASH interventions which aims to reduce the beneficiaries’ dependence on humanitarian aid by increasing the capacity of WASH committees to identify problems and implement minor repairs on the water supply system. This mitigates risk of disruption of the water supply chain, and increases the sustainability and self-sufficiency of the population in maintaining their own water supply and helps to reduce the dependence on humanitarian aid by building local capacity. The WASH Committees’ responsibilities include problem identification and reporting, as well as minor repairs. Trainings will be conducted on a quarterly basis for the WASH Committees on ownership, management and sustainability of WASH facilities.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ACTED will specifically deliver WASH assistance with the aim to mitigate against WASH-related GBV risks. In doing so, we will specifically ensure that the needs of especially vulnerable women and girls are prioritized and addressed during activities (including persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly) are prioritized and addressed during interventions. Distribution sites will also be chosen taking into consideration any specific protection concerns that might arise during the focus group discussions. At least one of the ACTED WASH team will be female in order to ensure women and girls feel more comfortable to come forwards with any concerns. GBV risk assessments, conducted through FGDs, will be conducted before all interventions. ACTED plans to collaborate closely with a GBV partner to ensure that their expertise is drawn upon to create GBV risk analysis of all activities. ACTED will refer to this partner any reports of cases of GBV, in order that they can provide support to survivors.ACTED will have a CRM focal point to ensure that beneficiaries can objectively and confidentially report complaints and feedbacks, this focal point will follow-up with the relevant ACTED staff or partner in order to provide an answer to the complainants for minor complaints while major concern complaints are directly forwarded to the AC/AME team in Juba. There will be CRM focal point for interventions based in ACTEDs Wau Office-base. Users of the CRM are provided with the option to speak to a female member of ACTED’s accountability team.ACTED has a strong code of conduct and policy on sexual exploitation and abuse which all staff are required to review and sign before working for ACTED.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba that will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in project areas, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report (verbally) complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. Feedback sessions will be arranged in coordination with community leadership groups including women and youth groups to ensure broad participation. The project will specifically gather feedback from women, girls and other vulnerable groups through FGDs about the risks, particularly GBV, that they face in accessing WASH facilities. ACTED will work in collaboration with other protection partners to ensure feedback mechanisms reach the most vulnerable.
