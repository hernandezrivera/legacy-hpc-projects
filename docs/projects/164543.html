Project Summary

Through this intervention ZOA will effectively and efficiently respond to urgent and identified WASH needs in Dar’a governorate, and integrate interventions wherever possible, between ZOA sectors (this project is part of a broader WASH/SHE/NFI programme) and also with health actors working in similar areas. ZOA will prioritise communities with high severity of needs and where there is a high concentration of IDPs and/or returnees. For household interventions ZOA will use vulnerability and selection criteria in line with Sector standards (to align with other actors), that are also discussed with and approved by local authorities and leaders. ZOA vulnerability criteria also incorporates analysis of protection concerns. Issues or concerns highlighted during assessments feed into project and implementation planning to ensure a mainstreaming approach and impactful support to households.
ZOA will implement a comprehensive WASH programme, involving rehabilitation of two community water networks, distribution of water tanks and essential WASH NFI, hygiene promotion, and solid waste management interventions. All activities will target beneficiaries based on vulnerability and need, in line with Sector-agreed criteria, and ZOA will integrate activities as much as possible in order to maximise the impact of the different activities and enhance resilience at individual, household and community level. For example, hygiene promotion will initially target areas where solid waste management activities are implemented. Water tank distributions will also initially target communities supported by the water network rehabilitation, in order to ensure that households are able to store water effectively and benefit as much as possible from improved water access. By rehabilitating water networks in two medium-sized communities ZOA will reach 25,000 people. ZOA will distribute 2,000 water tanks and different WASH NFI kits will be distributed based on identified need (dignity kits, hygiene kits, WASH kits) in areas ZOA is implementing other activities, targeting 32,000 individuals in total with NFIs. Through the establishment and training of 17 WASH committees ZOA will carry out hygiene promotion activities for 1,500 community members in order to increase collective capacity to mitigate against public-health related risks. ZOA will use varied engagement techniques and materials that connect with different groups (ages, genders, etc). ZOA will also include protection messaging in promotion sessions, and discuss more sensitive topics in smaller break-out groups. Solid waste management programming will target 20,400 people, working closely with municipalities to implement an impactful and sustainable intervention. ZOA plans on including a cash-for-work component within this activity, based on community engagement and acceptance, and a market assessment.
Overall the project activities targets 78.900 beneficiaries including double counting (23.419 Men, 22.501 Women, 16.820 Boys, 16.160 Girls)
Through assessment and community engagement, which is part of all interventions, ZOA aims to identify specific needs of different groups, adapting activities as required in order to best meet needs. ZOA also had mechanisms in place to ensure that accountability and learning is captured and acted upon throughout the project cycle. This includes detailed and direct project monitoring, varied feedback mechanisms and project impact evaluation.

4 - Needs

Assessments continue to highlight significant needs for HH in South Syria, especially for IDPs and returning HH. High rates of poverty is exacerbated by the deteriorating economic situation and one in seven HH include a member with specific protection needs (Medair,2019). Damage to water infrastructure in South Syria exists not only from conflict, but also from years of neglect and lack of maintenance. Although some water systems have been rehabilitated by GoS since regaining control, full water availability hasn’t been established and the majority of people in Dar’a are using a combination of the water network system and water trucking or closed wells to meet their needs. Dar’a City and Izra sub-district have low water system functuality and are under-served by public services (HNO,2020). Networked water is received every 10 days in some areas (MECC,2019). Poor water storage options affect water quality, increasing likelihood of contamination and disease. A combination of factors including a lack of water safety investment, the distribution of raw untreated water, and the lack of safe sewage management has contributed to increased public health risks (HNO,2019) and reinforcement of hygiene messages is important, especially in high IDP areas. Many HH do not have purchasing power to access items they need in order to implement healthy practices. Poor access to hygiene and dignity items creates health risks, particularly for the most vulnerable. NFI needs differ by gender and age. Dignity kits are a highlighted need for women and girls, and diapers for babies and adult diapers for elderly (MECC,2019). Local councils and communities struggle with solid waste management, which can contribute to increased vector breeding (HNO, 2019). 50% of HH in Dar’a reported no access to garbage collection and 50% reported disposing garbage in the streets, burning it or burying it (MECC,2019). Assessments also identified need for garbage bins used with garbage collection and trucks.

5 - Implementation of activities

ZOA targets communities with high severity of need, significant IDPs, returnees & vulnerable HH using defined vulnerability & protection criteria. Active participation & accountability within interventions, response to needs of different groups, promotion of social cohesion, direct M&E, feedback mechanisms & detailed technical review of contractor work. -Rehabilitate 2 community water systems: Restore adequate water supply through upgrade/installation of equipment by local contractor. Technical input and daily oversight from ZOA engineers & authorities. Stakeholder involvement & ZOA training for local ownership & sustainability.-2,000 HH water tanks: Assessment with municipalities to increase impact of water system rehabilitation and support HH reliant on other water sources. Distribution to HH unable to store water, in line with defined WASH & vulnerability criteria.-Hygiene promotion & protection messaging: Assessment of barriers, enabling factors & motivations. 17 trained WASH committees for 1500 community members. Integrated with WASH NFI & Solid Waste Management activities, with review of effectiveness to inform ongoing programming.-WASH NFI kits: 1600 family hygiene kits, 7000 dignity kits, & 1500 WASH kits: Selection criteria approved by local authorities & leaders. Focus on needs of specific groups, including sanitation & protection needs of women of reproductive age. Kits in line with sector standards. PDMs to confirm appropriateness of items, with adjustments made as needed.-Solid Waste Management in up to 5 communities: Practical activities that benefit whole community. Participatory waste removal overseen by municipalities & implemented by community members (men and women) through 2-month Cash for Work, informed by market assessment. Steel bins, protection equipment, clothing & ZOA training provided, including explosive hazard risk training. ZOA works alongside municipalities and advocates for them to continue services.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Market and exchange rate stability: While some products are still difficult to access in South Syria, markets across Dar’a governorate are largely functional. However, there is risk of volatility with the Syrian pound exchange rate which could not only affect prices of goods but also overall implementation as ZOA works through multiple currencies. As this project is heavily reliant on local supplies for rehabilitation and NFIs and on partner implementation within Syria, economic and currency markets will need to continue to be monitored and discussed with all suppliers as part of the procurement process.- Access and security: The proposed activities rely on access to and within South Syria districts. ZOA will adhere to GoS protocols and approval processes while observing core humanitarian principles and maintaining ongoing security and context mapping, security risk analysis and rigid security procedures. ZOA will ensure that all staff receive training on ZOA’s approach to safety and security in the field. ZOA will also maintain positive relationships with communities, local leaders and authorities as part of it’s acceptance strategy and access planning. - Availability of and ability to train staff: ZOA will hire Syrian staff and ensure gender equality wherever possible (in particular, aiming to ensure inclusion of female staff for WASH promotion work). Trained and qualified staff will also need to be willing and able to travel in South Syria.- Community members and local stakeholders accept and are allowed to participate in project activities: The success of ZOA’s project depends on active participation of community members and local stakeholders, facilitated by the key role of local authorities. ZOA will work within the parameters of GoS protocols in relation to beneficiary engagement through approved bodies such as Syria Trust. ZOA will define beneficiary engagement protocols based on its existing relationships with relevant authorities and it’s experiences.
