Project Summary

According to FSNAU Technical Release of September 30th 2020, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are projected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020, should humanitarian assistance not be available. An estimated 849 900 under the age of five are also likely face acute malnutrition through August 2021. The report further projects additional 3million people will to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 5.1 million. The report attributes this to combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks.
The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is projected to perform be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worse humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall delays or under performs.
The situation is exacerbated by continued displacement of persons in the country, with key drivers being cyclical climate shocks, including drought and flooding, which is challenging traditional rural livelihoods, and ongoing conflict. 2.6 persons are currently displaced out of whom 893,000 were displaced between January and August 2020 due to conflict, flooding and lack of livelihoods. Compounding this is high poverty rates among the urban poor which account for 64%. This is likely to go up by 10% should there be slightest trigger of economic and climatic shock (HNO, FSC page)
The 2020 Gu seasonal flooding (river flooding) contributed to loss and damage to property, housing, farmland and infrastructure. The riverine areas of Hiran, Middle Shabelle, Lower Shabelle, Gedo, Middle Juba and Lower Juba were some of the worst-affected areas. The COVID-19 pandemic and associated socio-economic impacts also contributed to the food insecurity situation in Somalia. Conflict/insecurity led to further population displacement and disruption to 2020 Gu season agriculture in some areas, including Lower Shabelle and Middle Juba.
Aid Vision proposed project is aimed at providing access to lifesaving and sustaining food security and livelihood interventions to 25,000 crisis affected population in Banadir and Middle Shabelle Regions, Aid Vision has presence in the two regions for over two decades and established networks of community structures and with local authorities. The project will provide Cash Based interventions, rehabilitation of farming infrastructure through canal rehabilitation, provision farm equipment and tools, supply of certified seeds, fuel subsidies, and training of farmers on contemporary farming techniques, provision of fertilizers and provision of agricultural extension services. It is envisioned that the interventions will go a long way in improving food security in the target regions and enhance community resilience to shocks in future.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1: Provide immediate food security and livelihoods assistance through Food vouchers (commodity voucher) valued at 56 USD to 1000 women, girls, boys and men including people with disabilities and minorities in crisis or stress for 3 months per selected households of IDPs and host community, with consideration to women headed households, HHs with malnourished children (boys and girls), elderly persons with no economically active persons taking care of them . This method of intervention is feasible in the target area as : a) food commodities readily available in the market b) credible vendors to provide the food items are locally available c) the intervention will not distort the market as no food items are brought from elsewhere d) local businesses will be supported as the target beneficiaries' purchasing power will be increased – redeem vouchers from selected vendors within beneficiaries’ locality e) beneficiaries will be spared of the indignity of queuing for long hours at distribution points as they will get their supplies anytime of their choice f) it reduces protection risks to women and girls g) it is easy to ensure exclusivity and targeting with gender and age lenses h) distribution points/vendors store are within the reach of the elderly, children, pregnant and lactating women and thus reducing protection risks of beneficiaries. Activity: 1.2: Rehabilitation/excavation of farm canals. Old and blocked canals will be rehabilitated and new ones excavated using CFW modality. This will increase the acreage farms under irrigation thereby reducing food insecurity. It will also have the multiplier effect of putting cash in the pockets of young men and women thus giving gainful engagement to youths would have otherwise been taken advantage of by armed groups or remained idle. Activity 2: Distribution of emergency agricultural inputs packages to targeted 100 farmers. The package will be comprised of: provision of tractor hours, assorted certified seeds and fertilizers to 100 farmers (40% women) in Balcad, district Middle Shabelle Regions. This will give impetus to recovery and build resilience benefiting around 4,500 women, girls, boys and men affected by crisis to protect their assets and generate income for their households. Provision of assorted fertilizers and pesticides will be done to improve soil nutrients and protect farmers from loss of yields to pests respectively. Activity 3: Training and provision of agricultural extension services to 100 farmers on good farming practices. This will increase farmer’s capacity and build their resilience to shocks. Further, this will have positive impact on both quality and quantity of yields as farmers will benefit from training on best farming practices from land preparation to post-harvest -storage and marketing. The farmers will also be trained on basic book-keeping. Farmers will also have opportunity to share knowledge and experiences. Provision of agricultural extension services to farmer will enable them receive guidance on best farming practices and enhance quality of their produce. Trained agronomist and agricultural extension officers will be available to targeted farmers throughout the project life ensure farmers get guidance and have their right skills/knowledge in real time.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The targeted beneficiaries are protracted Internally Displaced Persons and used to Cash or Voucher. Cash/Vouchers are more efficient and effective than in-kind aid. Cash/vouchers are more dignified and flexible modes of assistance. There are cost effective and efficient modes to transfer cash to beneficiaries. Risks of diversion/misappropriation is limited as cash is directed transferred to beneficiaries mobile phones.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The markets are integrated and can withstand the increase in demand (both in terms of quantity and quality of goods and services) the use of CTP will create. The food items and other goods/services beneficiaries need to meet their immediate needs are locally available.

Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups and socially excluded groups like women, children, people living with disabilities, elderly and people suffering from chronic diseases risk being excluded in consultation, decision-making and targeting as beneficiaries. This will be addressed by deliberately targeting them in the selection criteria of community representatives and target beneficiaries. All committees formed will be representative of all sectors of the community, with particular focus on the marginalized and the often socially excluded groups, decisions making process will involve women, girls and boys who are often excluded. The project will provide clear guidelines on who the beneficiaries are, and how they are expected to benefit/participate in the project. There is the risk of women and girls exposed sexual violence, sexual exploitation and abuse. To safeguard women and girls from being exploited/abused by organizational staff, project staff and vendors will be inducted on Aid Vision’s Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse policy and staff code of conduct that stipulate the consequences of engaging in SEA. Posters and messages on the same will be developed and placed at strategic places. The community will also be educated on their rights and how to report on incidences of sexual exploitation and abuse by organizational staff and contractors. Sexual violence may be meted on women and girls on the way to/from distribution sites. To forestall this, vendors selected will be from within the proximity of the communities served, thus girls and women will not have to travel for long distance to get their entitlements/ redeem their vouchers. The food voucher system used will be flexible to allow beneficiaries redeem them at any time of their convenience. This will go a long way in the protection of women and girls from sexual violence. There is also the potential risk of humanitarian assistance affecting peaceful coexistence and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. For instance, selection of Food Voucher and CFW beneficiaries may trigger hostilities between IDPs and Host communities or agrarian communities and pastoralists. Aid Vision will, from the outset, conduct sensitization and education of the project to make who should benefit from it clear and involve both IDPs and Host Communities in decision-making. The selection criteria developed will be inclusive and participatory. The project will also target the most vulnerable of IDPs and Host communities that meet the agreed upon selection criteria.
