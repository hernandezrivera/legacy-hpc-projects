Project Summary

According to HNO 2024,16.7 million individuals require humanitarian assistance, with 50% being women. 9.18 million are adults over 18 (4.96 million women) and 7.48 million are under 17 (3.44 million girls). Protection services are needed by 15.3 million individuals, with over 3.5 million in Aleppo, 0.9 million in Damascus, and 0.8 million in Hama. Also, 8.5 million individuals require protection services related to GBV. Adolescent boys and girls urgently need safe spaces for discussing issues such as sexual and reproductive health rights (SRHR), GBV, and mental health (MH) with well-trained facilitators in a confidential and supportive environment. The lack of knowledge and skills of local communities, especially in rural areas and displacement areas, resulted in a large decrease in the capacity of adolescent girls and female youth (AGFY) to make their own decisions regarding their lives, education,career path; consequently, increasing social, cultural and economic barriers to accessing safe, secure, equitable and quality education.Community members’ engagement with local CBOs, MH experts and educational leaders is needed to not only raise the community’s awareness on the importance of AGFY’s education, and their ability to make decisions, but also to ensure communities’ positive involvement in addressing the above issues.As HNO2024, there is a need to establish safe spaces for adolescents, mainly AGFYs, where they can freely express their opinions regarding their lives, thoughts, and future. AKF recognizes the importance of supporting affected AGFY, with a set of life skills to make decisions about their educational and vocational future. Hence, in line with the strategic objective 2, the project seeks to establish multiple safe spaces in areas of need, serving as a platform to cultivate the following:1)With 11 existing girl clubs in Hama, Damascus, and Aleppo serving as safe spaces for AGFY, AKF will continue to enhance life skills and address issues related to SRHR, GBV, and MH.This includes training a team of female facilitators to mobilize women and girls, facilitate sessions, monitor progress, and refer women and girls to health facilities. Each club will have 2 facilitators who will be trained on an AKF-designed life-skills curriculum.2) Establish 3 Community Management Groups in three areas consisting of LNGO members, CBOs, MH experts, and community leaders to present the perspectives of the community, support them with awareness-raising campaigns, and provide feedback. This includes developing information, education, and communication materials. Nine boys' clubs were alsoestablished in target areas, and these clubs will continue to sensitize boys and reducestigma about MH, SRHR, and GBV subjects, demonstrating that SRHR and girls’ safety are’nt the sole responsibility of girls, it is also for boys.3) Train facilitators to identify andprioritize approximately 916 AGFY requiring psychosocial support and refer them to MHservices. Processing and outcome monitoring activities under the project will includeend-line assessments of the target group, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries,and FGDs with stakeholders. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

AKF is committed to safeguarding including Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and has the Global Safeguarding Manual (GSM). It has processes and procedures in place to mainstream PSEA into all project cycle. There are Responding procedures in place to handle and address SEA complaints including speak-up channels, reporting mechanisms, case management investigation procedures, and disciplinary process that adopts the approach of ‘’Zero Tolerance’’. Also, AKF is accountable to affected-population and will consult with community through the non-formal committees’ members to make sure that the reporting mechanisms are based on their preference and reduce the barriers of reporting. AKF has developed specific processes and procedures to mainstream Safeguarding, including PSEA, into its all processes and operations including, Recruitment, Contracting, Procurement and Partnerships in order to decrease the risks of SEA incidents and enhance the culture of PSEA during dealing and contracting with the associated agencies, partners, individuals, community groups, and community members. A mandatory Safeguarding/PSEA training will be provided to staff, service providers, volunteers and associated members. All staff, volunteers, vendors, contractors and service providers will sign Code of Conduct. A specific Annex will be signed by stakeholders, to ensure mainstreaming Safeguarding/PSEA in all tasks of the third parties and sub-contractors.AKF adopts and prioritizes the Survivor-Focused Approach to consider the survivor’s wishes, safety, and well-being.AKF will handle SEA cases according to its policies and committed to raise the SEA reports to the donor.Risks.1: Resistance of local authority and community leaders due to the sensitivity of GBV and SRHR.
