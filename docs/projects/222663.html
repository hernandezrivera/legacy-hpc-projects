Project Summary

Low WASH standards impact living conditions amongst male and female groups of persons affected by conflict, displacement, natural disasters and other shocks. WASH directly affects health, nutrition, food security, protection and education in emergency settings. The counties of Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South in Northern Bahr El Ghazel; Jur River, Raja in Western Bahr El Ghazel; Malakal and Baliet in Upper Nile State, Fangak and Canal Pigi in Jonglei all exhibit low WASH standards per latest assessment reports. Provision of key WASH emergency services is key in alleviating suffering of populations mainly displacement, many multiply as a result of conflict and flooding.
Alliance for Community Development (ACD) programming focuses on provision of emergency yet sustainable locally driven durable solutions through a multi-sectoral approach. WASH interventions in the targeted areas will focus on responding to public health risks mainly driven by low WASH standards resulting in disease outbreaks such as Cholera, HEV, MPOX and other AWDs. Focus will be on HHs/Communities that exhibit high levels of food insecurity, (IPC 4/5), malnutrition, disease burdens especially amongst freshly displaced/returnee groups. Women, boys, girls, PLWs &<5s will be specially reached with WASH/Nut packages. Institutional WASH support will focus on mainly nutrition centres assisting SAM/MAM cases, whose low WASH standards could be a source of disease outbreaks at the centres as well as neighbouring communities. Collaboration with actors from other sectors will be key in identifying the most vulnerable, avoiding duplication of services and delivering a complete package to vulnerable groups. Safety audits will form the basis of the initial phases of programming, while upholding AAP standards. The project will provide clean and safe adequate water through setup/repairs of water sources, provision of HH water treatment solutions plus NFIs. Sanitation will focus on community driven solutions that are sustainable. Participatory hygiene promotion will foster behavioural change amongst communities. Provision of MHM/`dignity kits will be done collaboratively.
Alliance for Community Development (ACD) will reach a total of 25,000 people; 65% of these female; majority IDPs and returnees.

1. Needs

There’s a direct correlation between low WASH standards and high levels of malnutrition amongst U5s, PLW, PLHIV and the elderly. Poor WASH standards also affect school retention of adolescent girls, and contribute to GBV, SEA and food insecurity. Greater Upper Nile and Bahr El Ghazel have some of the poorest standards of WASH in South Sudan as a result of continuous depreciation in livelihoods for mainly women and girls, multiple displacements, insecurity and general lack of adequate service provision. Over 70% of populations in these regions are rural, agro-pastoralist in nature, and exhibit below average behaviors, knowledge and attitudes towards acceptable and recommended WASH standards. In Upper Nile and Jonglei, access to clean and safe water is below 40%, with over 70% of the water points requiring atleast 30 minutes of walking for an average family. This not only raises the likelihood of protection related issues for women and girls, but further encourages populations to rely on nearby open surface water points that are unsafe. Settlement patterns further affect water point access equity and equality, since they are mainly determined by flood proneness of the location rather than vicinity to basic services and security. A similar trend is observed in the states of Northern Bahr El Ghazel, Warrap and the Abyei Administrative Area. Open defecation is widely practiced in both semi-urban and rural populations in Jonglei, Upper Nile Northern Bahr El Ghazel; with less than 20% of the households having a standard latrine. This is mainly attributed to costs attributed to setting up flood resistant pit latrines, constant displacements as well as attitudes and knowledge pertaining to hygiene practices. KAP surveys conducted indicate low levels of knowledge related to handwashing, disposal of human and animal waste, personal hygiene, water treatment and management, vector control as well as disease prevention. GAM rates in the locations targeted exceed the global threshold of 15%, and are mainly attributed to low WASH standards and a high disease burden. Water related diseases are seasonally sound during the peak of the rainy season, due to contamination of water points with fecal materials improperly disposed. Locations in parts of Greater Upper Nile lie along the flood plains (Sobat, Nile and Eastern) witness a complete destruction of locally made pit latrines necessitating sustainable end environmentally friendly solutions to waste disposal. Women bear the brunt of ensuring availability of water for household consumption and use due to the social segregation of duties within the communal and household setting. This places a heavy burden on them, depriving them of time for livelihood generating chores and opportunities, reducing time to attend to breastfeeding babies and denying girls the focus and time required to attend school. Lack of adequate WASH facilities at learning centers affects adolescent girls consistency, compromises their dignity and affects learning. Recent public health alerts from disease outbreaks (Cholera, MPOX, Hep. E) in the counties targeted requires sustained and robust WASH responses. Alliance for Community Development (ACD) is a UNICEF, USAID, OCHA, WFP, FAO and World Bank partner, working and with a sustained local presence in the states of Jonglei, Upper Nile Warrap, Northern Bahr El Ghazel and the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA).

2. Project activities

Adequate and sustainable quantities (15l/p/d) and quality of water available for vulnerable groups of persons at communal, household and institutional level through;- Construction and rehabilitation of flood resistant water points suitable for all gender groups.- Provision of household water treatment solutions. Sufficient, accessible and dignified sanitation facilities available for vulnerable groups at household, communal and institutional level through;- Construction and rehabilitation of gender inclusive, dignified and flood resistant sanitation facilities- Provision of WASH NFIs to vulnerable groups- Communal mobilization for vector control and management Increased knowledge and practice in proper hygiene practices and norms through increased knowledge and sustainable community approaches to WASH challenges through;- Inclusive and gender sensitive hygiene promotion.- Distribution and provision of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), Hygiene and Dignity kits.- Enhanced coordination of WASH activities in the locations targeted. Enhanced AAP/AQA through safety audits, monitoring and evaluation as well as community/stakeholder feedback mechanisms.
