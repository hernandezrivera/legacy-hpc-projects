Project Summary

Community Aid for Relief and Development proposes to support most vulnerable 4,095 beneficiaries (1500 girls and 1500 boys, 547 men and 548 women) including those with disabilities, in line with the Education Cluster Sectoral Objective 1: Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth whereby key actions will entail supporting enrolment of children in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools/learning centers, establishment of temporary learning spaces and classrooms (established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy), rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces and classrooms, establishment of gender segregated latrines and water points and rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines and water points and provision of sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers. The action will further support the achievement of sectoral Objective 2: Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments, where CARD will provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials, teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education, train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children, train and update teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition. Third objective, which entails strengthening the education system’s response capacity of the school community to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, and communities; CARD will train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change, support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) and support conducting back to learning campaigns.
CARD has been operating in the three targeted counties since 2014 with ongoing FSL funding in addition to previous UNICEF supported education actions in Western Equatoria in 2016 and 2017. Through the coordination offices in addition to having competent staff, logistics and community goodwill, in addition to having good relations with the host communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county ministries of Education and General Instruction. These strong networks coupled with established local networks will be engaged and mobilized to rapidly implement the proposed activities. In addition, CARD will ensure involvement of beneficiaries in planning, implementation, and monitoring. CARD takes a “do no harm” approach, by employing community-based protection measures that aim at understanding humanitarian issues from the community’s perspective and contributing to the development of risk reduction strategies with and for the community. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations, inter-cluster synergies, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach.
CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP, GBV etc.

1 - Needs

Western Equatoria counties of Tambura, Nagero and Mvolo continue to grapple with renewed waves of localized clashes with consequent displacement of at least 83,904 people (including children) since active clashes started Tambura, with vulnerable boys, girls, men and women displaced to neighbouring counties of Nagero and Mvolo. Active armed hostilities have continue to impact the already dire situation of over five million children of school going age with 1,095 schools damaged due to flooding and conflict. The situation of these vulnerable children is further impacted by the COVID 19 pandemic where by 2.2 million children were already out of school. Recent activities hostilities have impacted at least 30 schools and learning spaces in the three counties with majority citing insecurity, lac of teachers incentives, lack of food among other reasons (IRNA Western Equatoria 2021/ OCHA January 2022). The situation of girls and boys has been further impacted by unprecedented food insecurity levels, diseases related to poor WASH access, forceful recruitments into armed forces and groups, protection concerns including gender based and sexual violence among others, which have been rampant. This is in addition to prolonged closure of schools due to COVID 19 in 2020 and part of 2021 that led to key protection concerns including adoption of negative coping strategies such as early/ forced marriages and GBV concerns, which have included abduction and use of children in armed hostilities (Save the children report on COVID 19 Impacts, 2020). This is in addition to lack of payment of teachers including conflict-related destruction of schools and learning spaces. Women and girls as well as children have particularly been vulnerable with reported abductions in Mvolo, Tambura and Nagero and its environs, including killings and psychosocial distress particularly among children (boys and girls). Internally displaced children and youth including women and men have particularity been vulnerable to likelihood of water borne disease outbreaks, which will be heightened with the current pandemic (UNICEFSituation Reports, 2021; Education Cluster Assessment 2021).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARD will ensure vulnerable members including people with disabilities are engaged and actively participate throughout the project cycle, including being part of implementation committees and have their feedback taken into account. Their targeting will also involve involvement of key community lead focal points in addition to ensuring disabled are not excluded from services provided, which include transport facilitation for those with mobility issues, or tailoredhousehold level service provision and engagement.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CARD will hold consultative sessions at the beginning of the project at village, Boma and school levels with the communities who will be engaged during inception to share views and inputs on the implementation. Throughout the action, key feedback and complaints desks will be established at the targeted schools in addition to various target Bomas, to support in collection of bothfeedback and complaints in addition to follow ups of raised concerns which will be communicated to the community. Key focal points for feedback and complaints will include women, men, girls, boys including people with special needs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARD staff will ensure target beneficiaries are sensitized and are aware of their rights including those relating to PSEA. PSEA focal points will be identified at Boma, village and school levels, with PSEA issues raised to them confidentially including providing confidential reporting channels through phone contacts and email contacts or through relevant local authorities such as chiefs, religious leaders among others. Issues raised will be investigated including ensuringbeneficiaries are informed on outcomes In addition, CARD staff and associated personnel have signed a code of conduct that includes PSEA including having in place communication to target beneficiaries on their rights and reporting mechanisms in case of violations, which include PSEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback including any complaints raised will be tackled CARD in consultation with implementation committees including making appropriate changes where necessary to ensure beneficiary views are taken into consideration. Presented views and action points will be discussed with benefciaries including additional dedicated clarifications in collaboration withkey AAP focal points at Boma, Village and school levels.
