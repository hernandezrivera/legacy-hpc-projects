Project Summary

The proposed project of NRC aims to ensure that the displacement affected adolescents and youths from the Rohingya and host communities can proclaim their rights to ongoing learning and education in a supportive environment. The sectoral operational plan will continue to guide the implementation process, with a coordinated process which will focus on utilizing Myanmar curriculum for learning. Specialised services will be designed, implemented, and monitored to ensure the access to inclusive education for female students, students with disabilities and youth at protection risks including LGBTI community. Process will be started with education need assessment to identify adolescent and youth (age group with 13 to 24 years) needs where special attention will be given to the learners with disabilities. Based on the findings on the need assessment NRC will seek to provide access to the targeted group for relevant and high-quality learning which will increasing their educational and market driven livelihood opportunities. A customized life skills program including PSS, professional skills training, pre-vocational and vocational training will also be developed and provided. Functional literacy, numeracy, and digital literacy related basic skills will be included with the linkage with skill development programme. Moroever, education in emergency also be imbedded as a part of DRR and risk mitigation which has been clarified in the cross-cutting section of this proposal. Rohingya students will have access to the use of current Myanmer curriculum resources through ALP. Services will include involvement of both rohingya and host in community activities and sports which will ensure social cohesion. Emphasis will be given on female learners with disabilities and LGBTI community for the inclusion of gender and diversity. NRC will work jointly with UNO and other stakeholders to support the host community students reducing engorged learning gap that will also improve the involvement of the government. Remedial classes, conditional monetary assistance, asset or repairing provisions, are few measures that can be taken to mitigate the gaps. A safe space, mostly with CBLF modality to ensuring cost effectiveness for learners to learn, engage, and experiences sharing opportunity will be given through inclusive programming. This space will be renovated or built with temporary community-based learning facilities, gender-specific WASH facilities and water sources, solar power for ventilation and lighting, boundaries etc. NRC will also provide appropriate services for learners with disabilities with structured and documented referral pathways. Besides physical modification offline learning for learners/ children with disabilities will also be incorporated in the programme. To reduce gender participation gap, teacher and facilitator training will be offered by using a framework for professional development of teachers.
Building capacity on inclusive and gender-responsive educational practices, crisis-sensitive planning, management, and coordination for quality provision will be ensured in collaboration with other actors as well as the government. Throughout the project, curricula and guidelines will be produced and provided to the partners and government. Community education groups will be formed including facilitators, community members, and guardians/ parents of the students. These stakeholders will serve as important discussion forums for the learning process, feedback, raising awareness of initiatives for inclusive education. Better Learning Programme (BLP), psychosocial support, supporting sports activities, and short-term learning programs will alos be included in the services, along with spreading life-saving information and engaging with the local community. The distribution of necessary resources and dignity kits will be included as well.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

By the fifth year of the protracted crisis, the response had dealt with the COVID 19 pandemic and was allowed to function with less constraints than required by COVID standards. During this time, various modalities were employed for basic help. Although there has been progress, the Rohingya refugees are still in a vulnurable situation since they are affected by climate change and natural disaster hazards. Engorged learning gaps, gender-based violence, subpar WASH facilities, and limited possibilities for education, and restrictions on participation in livelihood activities continue to be problems, keeping these populations heavily dependent on aid. Throuhg JRP PIN, agewise group 15 – 18, 47,518 (Female 22,601, Male 24,917) is targeted and age group 19 – 24 – 17,338 (Female 9,760, Male 7,578) is targeted to be provided services in 2023. There have been few opportunities for Rohingya and host community children and youth to access high-quality, all-inclusive, diversified, and age-appropriate education. A variety of factors, including a lack of space for facilities in already crowded camps, a shortage of qualified teachers, particularly females, and government restrictions for access to education, have hampered efforts to provide education services to refugees. According to an examination of the gaps in the education sector, the partners in the sector now offer services through a number of learning centers and home-based learning centers dispersed throughout the camps. With few educational possibilities currently available to teenagers and young adults aged 15 and beyond, services are currently largely available to children aged 3 to 14. Although the implementation of the Myanmar curriculum has begun, many adolescents and young people still lack access to education programs that are suited to their needs in terms of age based needs and wellbeing. Adolescent and youth-focused accelerated education has only recently begun to be implemented, and there are still significant implementation and technical capacity improvement with data-driven issues that need to be addressed in the near future. With the adoption of the Myanmar curriculum, there are still difficulties in providing steady implementation for ongoing support for adolescent and youth education. Increasing efforts must be made to ensure access, better quality services and catered pathways. For the host community, the most frequently mentioned problems while returning children to school were financial hardships, academically behind schedule students, child marriage, child labor, etc. The host community has received minimal aid. They identified the gap in response to the high drop-out rate among adolescents and young people due to the quality of education provided, income-generating activities, early marriage, and parents' disinterest in education, as well as the lack of alternative modalities which accommodates access to livelihood opportunities, during the consultation meetings with the key stakeholders. Extreme weather events and serious disasters are becoming increasingly frequent throughout Bangladesh as a result of the effects of climate change and environmental degradation. Monsoon season that brought torrential rain, strong winds, flooding, and landslides made the humanitarian crisis worse by causing forced migration and relocation for millions of people living along Bangladesh's coast and affected areas.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

NRC will continue to work closely with the CiC office, site management, and education sector and camp level focal points so that services can be tailored to reflect camp needs, sharing updates in order to avoid duplications and create synergies, including the continuous development of efficient and effective referral mechanisms. In addition, NRC`s presence and provision of services would enable NRC to understand the education landscapes at large, protection risks, needs and gaps in services` provision in areas where it works. This information will be collected, analysed and used for evidence-based advocacy and further programme development and will also be shared externally with other actors and stakeholders within relevant coordination mechanisms through Education sector, and Sub-Working Groups such as DRMWG, YWG and the Inter-Agency Advocacy Group as appropriate. Moreover, NRC will make available its global expertise in adoelscents and youth education and the experience gained in Bangladesh to technically support sectors and sector partners and relevant stakeholders on these thematic areas. In particular, NRC plans to conduct specialised technical assessments/researches on verified gaps and supports related to specific topics and technical areas, with special focus on adoelscents and youth education issues. These may include for example an assessment on youth education needs, as well as related practices and barriers, for both refugees and host community beneficiaries.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Mainstreaming principles will be promoted throughout the project implementation; CFM (hotline) will be constantly upgraded with NRC CFM Policy, procedures and NRC CFM Handbook. Programe targets adolescents and youth, with priority to PWD, female and gender diverse groups, special focus on girl child participation, with SAAD criteria. Protection, gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, meaningful participation, community engagement, and accountability by prioritizing the most vulnerable populations. Community engagement in implementation and monitoring will carry out by formal and informal community consultation session, door-to-door visit, observation, satisfaction survey etc. Training for staff, volunteers on protection sensitization, referral pathways and context specific emergency trainings for preparedness and response. Connect with PSEA Network to induct camp volunteers. NRC's existing PSEA focal point system will remain for needs assessment, risk analysis..

3.2 - Gender

NRC is part of DRMWG and will continue to be an active participant within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for disaster and emergency preparedness responses and in addressing the challenges brought on by COVID-19, seasonal and climate related affects. In coordination with PERU through NRC's overall operation teams during any emergency to support prevention, mitigation, response and overall phases. Staff, volunteers will be part of emergency response team, coordinated by NRC's emergency response team, where the participants will receive emergency capacity building training. Participants will share DRR and DRM messages at community level. Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, and Recovery measures as identified through overall emergency plan, and aligned with sector. Reduce, reuse, recycle concept will be included in project delivery to reduce environmental impact. Youth will be trained on the prepared/curated modules as per alignment with activities.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

To address the needs of the affected communities, targets are set as per sex and age disaggregation, gender and disability for ensuring participation for all age groups. These includes the individuals specific focus on adolescents and youth, with majority focus on increasing female participation and engagement, of Adult Females (18-59 years), Adult Males (18-59 years). Specific outreach forms will be use with screening process through using Washington Group Questionnaire. The project will target 18 years or above persons in the provision of volunteer activities. Through teachers and staff capacity building, further engagements will be provided with guardian for ensuring participation and engagement in activities. All staff and volunteers will be provided with child safeguarding and PSEA training, training contents will include referral pathways as per policy. The awareness sessions will include on prevention of child labor, child trafficking, child violence, and child marriage.

3.4 - Diversity

The contexts presents with specifics risks for women, girls, and persons with specific needs along persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations, continued to face heightened barriers in education services. Meaningful participation will be addressed the issue by inclusion of the diverse groups to increase participation of women, girls, and persons with specific needs along persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations through this project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In alignement with the Accountability to the Affected population (AAP) principles, targeted objectives will be achieved through the provision of services delivered by taking information sharing, communication and feedback mechanisms and transparency into place, while ensuring participation, egnagement and inclusion through gender diverse lences through out the humanitarian project cycle phases. Learning, feedback then wll be reflected through necesssary process and pathways cosnidering the contexts and needs in order to reflect accountability towards the beneficiaries.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

NRC Education is part of Education DRMWG and will continue to be an active participant within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for disaster and emergency preparedness responses. Education team will coordinate with PERU through NRC's overall operation teams in camps during any emergency to support prevention, mitigation, response and overall phases. NRC’s Emergency response team will provide capacity building training to staff and volunteers on preparedness and response. Also, environmental considerations will be made through reflecting reduce, reuse and recycle principles. Youth groups will have opportunity to address community level challenges through youth led initiatives and will aim to contribute through community led solutions for disaster risk reductions.
