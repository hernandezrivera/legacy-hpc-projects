Project Summary

This project aims to improve on the psychosocial wellbeing of conflict-affected teenage mothers and their children (2000 teenage mothers, aged 12 to 19, 3000 children aged 0 to 5) through life skills and vocational Education to build resilience and provides them with stability, structure, and hope for the future. TeenAlive Association seeks to achieve this through:
a) Creation of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanism; 10 community members per community in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision trained on Child Protection issues to enhance their understanding on child protection in emergency, deepen their understanding and increase skills on how to identify, refer and provide basic psychological first aid to vulnerable children in their communities and enlighten them on their roles and responsibilities in creating and promoting a protective environment for children by 2024.
b) Case Management for teenage mothers and their children; the provision of psychological first aid, mental health and psychosocial support to 250 conflict affected teenage mothers and their children in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision managed by 2 Trained mental health and psychosocial support experts (1 female and 1 male) and 8 volunteers who will be trained by 2024.
c) Production of civil documents to conflict-affected teenage mothers and children and adolescent boys; production of birth certificates to teenage mothers (200), birth certificate to children of teenage mothers (400); production of national identification card to teenage mothers (100), production of national identification cards for adolescent boys (100) in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision to ease movement and access certain benefits for teenage mothers and easy registration into school for children of teenage mothers by 2024.
d) Running of Safe Spaces for conflict-affected teenage mothers to enable them build resilience through discussion of their life challenges, coping mechanism, skills acquisition and to develop a good mental health and wellbeing in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision to be managed by case manager and mental health and psychosocial support expert by 2024.
e) Economic empowerment of teenage mothers and other girls/women through TeenAlive Vocational Training Center to empower 50 teenage mothers and other girls/women with embroidery, bead designing and production of fascinators and assistance with materials and start-up capitals in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision to be managed the coordinator of the TeenAlive vocational center by 2024.
f) Sensitization of communities on Gender Based Violence and Child Abuse in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision where emphasis will be made on the identification, elimination and reporting of GBV cases such as rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, as well as child abuse cases etc by 2024.
g) Provision of life skills to children in schools in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision where emphasis will be made on interpersonal skills and abilities that promote mental well-being, make it is easier to meet life’s challenges and crisis events in a realistic, positive and constructive way by 2024.
h) Provision of teachers with training on prevention of violence in school and MHPSS to teachers in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision by 2024.
i) Provision of positive parenting training to teen mothers and caregivers through community safe spaces in Muyuka Subdivision, Mamfe Subdivision and Njinikom Subdivision by 2024.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The crisis in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon has left many children and teenagers especially girls vulnerable. Psychosocial distress and mental disorders and family separation continue to be the main child protection concerns. Many children have been exposed to or been victims of extreme violence; they have witnessed their parents, siblings, or other relatives being killed. Other children have been separated from their families because of the death of their parents or the voluntary separation to attend school, insecurity, destruction of homes, poverty, etc. (HRP, 2023). Teenage girls face some common problems which includes; unwanted pregnancy, exposure to substance abuse, peer influence and issues related to child abuse, forced labor and career orientation, while boys, are reportedly being recruited and used by armed groups. This in turn exposes other children to the danger of arbitrary arrest on suspicion of being members or potential members of armed groups. There is a constant need for children and teenagers to be educated on these societal issues and how to mitigate the harms on them and be provided with skills and information that will affect their lives. In times of crisis, development is restricted and decisions made (particularly for teenage girls at risk of child marriage and teenage pregnancy) that will determine young lives. The lack of education, in terms of basic skills, qualifications and vocational opportunities missed, compounded by a lack of access to sexual and reproductive health education and resources will follow them into the future. Worthy of note, that the conflict has forced about 4,400 schools (80%) in NW/SW to close (PAID-WA 2018). An estimated 5% to 33% of girls aged 15 to 24 years who drop out of school in Cameroon do so because of early pregnancy or marriage. It was observed that the prevalence of adolescent pregnancy is high in rural communities as compared to urban cities (VAHA, 2018). Following the crises in NWSW, teenage girls in these regions and displaced teenage girls in other regions are facing high rate of rape, unwanted pregnancies especially in rural areas and high mortality rate as a result of abortion or complications in pregnancies. In these crisis regions, learners are traumatized as a result of violence such as killing, maiming and threat to stay away from school and abuses (PAID-WA 2019). Support systems particularly, Families and social structures have also been disrupted in these regions. Psychosocial support systems in schools and communities are inadequate. Many children and youth have limited opportunities to develop social and emotional life skills such as self-control, empathy, sympathy, negotiation, and joint problem solving, which are normally cultivated at the family level and further developed at schools. Teenage girls in Cameroon crisis affected regions of the North west and South west especially in the rural communities aged 12 to 19, lack life-skills in areas such as self-control, stress coping skills, problem solving challenges and reproductive health. They suffer from unwanted pregnancy, unsafe abortion and sexually transmitted infections STIs and HIV, smoking, drug and substance abuse (PAID-WA 2019). Teenage girls are at risk of SGBV, including early marriage that prevents them from accessing their right to education and livelihood opportunities. Furthermore, dropped out and internally displaced teenage girls in host communities especially in Buea Subdivision, South west region of Cameroon, have limited access to any form of vocational Education talk less of employability skills (PAID-WA, May, 2019). Therefore, there is the need to improve on the protection and psychosocial wellbeing of conflict-affected teenage mothers and pregnant teenagers through life skills and enhance the employability skills of teenage mothers and pregnant teenagers through vocational Education to build their resilience and provides them with hope for the future.
