Project Summary

The projects seeks to improve access to education for boys and girls of school going age while equally focusing to address enrolment gap of girls compared to boys in drought and conflict affected areas of Somalia. The response will target the regions where CARE is currently implementing emergency response program covering the sectors of WASH, Food security and Nutrition. This will contribute to fulfilling the multi-sectoral approach of providing humanitarian assistances in line with HRP 2018/19 as well as CARE strategic plan of addressing humanitarian emergencies. The project will target 5000 children (2900 boys and 2100 girls ) of school going age in the four regions of ;Sool, Mudug and Galgadud. Beside addressing access and enrolment gaps between girls between boys, the project will undertake advocacy campaigns to encourage stakeholders including parents to prioritize girl’s education in equal measures to boys, enhance security and safety for pupils in learning centers and strengthen coordination between actors to ensure quality learning, promote retention and avoid duplication of responses. The project will provide essential rehabilitation of learning spaces. teaching learning materials including school uniforms through voucher for girls, teacher incentives and training services rehabilitation of school based WASH facilities to improve privacy and dignity for girls and capacity building support for school managements bodies including Community Education Committees (CECs), Parents Teacher Association (PTAs) and officials from ministry of Education. Most importantly, the project is designed to mainstream protection principles and to promote access, safety and dignity for all beneficiaries. The project will mainstream key protection principles of promoting safety and dignity and avoiding to cause harm and ensure impartial and neutral education services provision for all the targeted learners based purely on needs. This includes; 1). Ensuring that location of learning facilities and sanitation facilities are close and safe 2). Ensuring WASH facilities are gender sensitive. 3). Identification and responding to obstacles to girls’ retention to access education facilities within the targeted schools especially for the vulnerable IDPs. Accountability to Beneficiaries and Participation will also remain an integral part in the response designing and implementation in line with education cluster requirements.

In line with Somalia Education cluster sector priorities of 2019, the project covers the following objectives.
Objective1: Ensure 5000 (2900 boys and 2100 girls) of emergencies and crises affected boys, girls and youth have access to safe and protective learning environments
Objective2: Ensure 5000 vulnerable children (2900 boys and 2100 girls) are engaged in life-saving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion.

Needs

In Somalia, over 3 Million of the country 4.9 Million boys and girls of school going age are unable to access basic education across the entire country. The recurring droughts compounded by perennial conflicts have greatly undermined enrollment rates in the country, which currently stands as low as 30% for primary and 26% for secondary. The FSNAU Post Gu 2018 report indicates 4.6 million people be food insecure and in need of humanitarian support. This includes 2.6 million people who are displaced by conflict and droughts in the last two years. approximately 1 million of the displaced population are school aged children. The Emergency education cluster states 1.83 million school aged children are in need of education emergency assistances with a focus on increased access, retention as well as of sustainability. Those suffering the greatest educational inequities are IDPs, females, nomadic/pastoralist and rural communities5; most of these groups are marginalized communities who face risks of exclusion CARE Rapid EiE assessment conducted in July 2018 in the regions of Sool, Sanaag, Galgadud equally established that access to basic education remains a critical priority in most drought affected areas. The 2017 drought had a significant setback impact in regular functioning of schools, eroding the little gains made in the sector before 2017. The major challenges hindering education access for IDPs and poor host communities include high poverty level, sudden decline of income level during the drought period, limited learning spaces, insufficient number of teachers, inability to afford teaching learning material, competition for child labor and traditional harmful practices such us early marriage for school going girls and FGM. In line with Education cluster sector 2019 priorities, CARE intends to provide emergency assistances to 5000 school aged children (2900 boys and 2100 girls) in 30 schools in three regions of Sool, Mudug and Galgadud .

Activities

The project will cover objective 1 and 2 of Education cluster 2019 priorities and targets 5000 children (2900 boys 2100 girls) in 30 schools in the regions of Sool, Galgadud and Mudug region. The proposed project activities will enable vulnerable schools aged children with immediate access of learning opportunities, support learners retention and improve earning environment. Provision of sanitary towels for 21000 female learners1.4 Conduct interactive hygiene and sanitation campaigns with messages on hand washing,drinking safe water and environmental hygiene and cholera /AWD control targeting 5000 children in 30 schools Objective2: Ensure 5000 vulnerable children and youth (4640 boys and 3360 girls ) in emergency crisis affected areas are engaged in life-saving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion 2.1 Provision of teaching learning materials including uniform vouchers to female learners for IDPs, returnees, Refugee and poor host community households targeting 3000 learners 2.3 Provide Emergency incentives to 80 teachers (50% females) in 20 schools1.8 Provide Emergency Water trucking targeting 5000 pupils (2900 boys and 2100 girls) in 30 schools worst hit by water crisis during big period of emergencies or dry spells.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The Somalia Recovery and Resilience Framework (RRF) developed in 2018 calls for a collective vision strategy of addressing recurring drought crisis and emphasizes on durable solution approach to tackle perennial problems of human displacements including IDPs and Refugee returnees in the country. The long-term need for continued learning and development of children in protracted crises is best delivered where there is a link between short and long-term planning and financing as well as better coordinated engagement between actors involved in delivering education interventions. Despite being critical need, education remains among the least prioritized and funded sectors in Somalia. Education sector is one of prioritized sectors earmarked for the new way of thinking of delivering humanitarian assistance. In recognition of this, CARE t will put a great consideration linking this response to various pillars of durable solution as outline in the Somalia durable solution framework. This will include strengthening government line ministry of education both at state and federal level to steer and provide sound education management leadership in the sector. The project will equally coordinate with the government led education actors in the country to sustain education services and ensure the most vulnerable and hard to reach children are not left behind in the education. The project will equally advocate for long term funding of education sector in the country alongside sustaining short term measures to address needs arising during the emergencies. CARE has already secured a number of multi-year education for its development program from various donors like EU, DFID, USAID and GPE. Several of the schools that will be targeted in this intervention will in the long run be absorbed in CARE in development sector program. Under multi-sector approaches, CARE will make use of education to provide access to holistic services for children, including nutrition, basic healthcare, WASH and protection. Already the schools targeted in this project are located in location where CARE is conducting emergency response programme such as WAS, Nutrition, Health, Food security and Protection. This linkage would certainly go along way addressing their diverse and complex needs in humanitarian and development responses. CARE will also use the project as launch pad to advocate with donors and governments to address existing gaps in planning and resourcing for the education sector by embracing greater levels of joint planning, coordination, and appropriate sector resourcing through multi-year funding. Other durable solution approaches to be taking into consideration include; development of Education management information system for schools, documentation of best practices, linking teachers recruited under this project to be included in the mainstream country workforce. Already CARE has done in this in Lower Juba and will roll out to other states.
