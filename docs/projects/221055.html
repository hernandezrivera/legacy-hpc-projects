Project Summary

In response to the immense needs resulting from the conflict in the Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) regions in Cameroon, CRS is implementing a 12-month project - Anglophone Crisis Emergency Response V (ACER V). ACER V builds on the success and learning of the previous phases of the response. Changes and adaptations are based on community consultations and input, needs assessments, coordination and harmonization with other humanitarian actors, and changes in the security context and accessibility.
The goal of the ACER V project is to improve food security and alleviate suffering of displaced and host households in the Northwest Region of Cameroon. The project seeks to achieve this goal through two purposes: (1) Vulnerable displaced and host households in the Northwest meet their basic food and NFI needs, and (2) Vulnerable displaced and host households in the Northwest establish successful businesses and have sustainable income generation. In partnership with the Archdiocese of Bamenda, CRS is delivering an integrated package of assistance, providing vulnerable displaced and host households with cash to start up businesses and business skills training, as well as emergency food and NFIs. The project is using market-based approaches to support 12,000 people in 2,000 households for over 12 months.
ACER V responds directly to the priority needs of the affected population. In the International Organization for Migration (IOM)’s multisector needs assessment (MSNA) conducted in September 2024, food, livelihood and NFIs continue to be cited as priority needs of the affected population in the Northwest and Southwest. IDPs, people with disabilities, older people, and female-headed households are disproportionately impacted by the crisis. ACER V will cover 3 Divisions in the Northwest; Bui, Donga Mantung and Mezam due to the high needs, presence of displaced persons and high percentage of the population in IPC phase 3+ based on recent Cadre Harmonisé data.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The conflict in the Northwest and Southwest Regions of Cameroon continues to upend livelihoods and severely limit access to food, markets, NFIs and farmland. Access remains low, particularly among IDPs, female-headed households, and persons with disabilities. The FSC projected 508,480 persons in need of food security in 2024 with 352,401 targeted. Response however remains insufficient. Access to food (22%) and livelihoods (14%) were identified as top priority needs by the affected population in the September 2024 MSNA by IOM. 63% of the assessed population reported having no food to eat in their household within 30 days prior to the assessment due to lack of resources to get food. The October 2023 CH further projected 10% of the population (272,491 individuals) in the NW to fall within IPC Phase 3-5, with divisions like Bui, Mezam, and Donga Mantung having the highest food insecure population. CRS’ Routine monitoring, consultations with community leaders, and findings from the ACER IV Real Time Evaluation (RTE) consistently demonstrate requests from communities for increased support in food assistance and livelihoods. In April and October 2021, CRS carried out livelihoods assessments with key informants. Interviewees identified productive assets destroyed in the conflict or lost to displacement as a barrier to their livelihoods and income generation. They also noted that the primary barrier to resilient and productive livelihoods was access to start-up capital. In addition, respondents to the ACER IV RTE in October 2023 and previous evaluation exercises over the past two years demonstrate a growing demand by affected populations to become self-sufficient and stop depending on humanitarian assistance through increased need for additional livelihood options that are more business-oriented, including cash to start small businesses. Furthermore, during displacement, households leave behind valuable household non-food items which are often needed to provide a basic standard of living in their new areas of settlement or upon return to their initial communities where household items have been stolen due to long term absence contributing to the deteriorating living conditions among displaced persons. 6% of September 2024 MSNA respondents identified NFIs as a priority need. The shelter cluster projected 428,591 IDPs and returnees in need of assistance in 2024. In the ACER IV baseline, only 12.97 % of respondents reported being able to meet all or most of their basic needs prior to receiving assistance. Barriers to accessing household items mentioned include items being too expensive and difficulty accessing markets due to insecurity or other reasons. The limited availability of funding further exacerbates the situation making it difficult to address the food, NFI and livelihood needs of vulnerable displaced persons. The number of assisted households continues to fall short of the clusters target. For example, in 2023, the shelter/NFI cluster targeted 47,860 households to be assisted with core and essential relief items. By November 2023, Shelter/NFI actors including CRS had jointly assisted 12,230 households (42,193 individuals) with NFI accounting for 25% of the cluster NFI target and a 75% gap for the NWSW. To address the above needs, ACER V will support 12,000 people in approximately 2000 households with monthly food assistance through vouchers and one-off voucher assistance to 950 HH to purchase core relief items. 465 HH will receive cash grants to establish a business alongside capacity building in business skills to support income generation. Activities will be implemented in Bamenda I, II, III, and Tubah subdivisions of Mezam Division, Oku and Kumbo subdivisions in Bui Division and Nkambe in Donga Mantung Division. CRS will explore new communities within the subdivisions with high numbers of people in need, limited humanitarian presence, and including those not covered in previous phases.
