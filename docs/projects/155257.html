Needs

As of November 2018, there are over 900,000 refugees in Cox’s Bazar district (source: https://data2.unhcr.org/en/documents/download/66823). Benefiting from the protection of the Government, the generosity of local authorities and host communities, new arrivals in 2017 joined an existing refugee population of some 166,466 people. Cox’s Bazar is one of Bangladesh’s poorest districts where one out of three people is a refugee. The influx has adversely affected nutrition and food security, put massive pressures on national services, pushed wages downwards and increased prices. Risks disproportionally affect the most vulnerable in particularly women, children at risk, persons with disabilities and older persons without support, who may have no choice but to resort to negative coping mechanisms. The 2017 influx required substantial and rapid needs assessments. Under the leadership of the Government, the humanitarian community engaged in 2018 in a multi-sectoral needs assessments, consultations and strategic planning, which culminated in the Joint Response Plan (JRP). A series of multi-functional community assessments as well as regular focus group discussions, and more specific assessments were carried out. An assessment of the baseline and end line of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) interventions’ efficiency over seven months provided key information by June 2018 which enabled better evidence based programming. Findings of the assessment found that SGBV response is now covering 57% of all refugees up from a baseline of 16% and the efficiency of interventions were at 84% up from 24% just seven months prior to June 2018. Key findings of all of the assessment found that:  There is a need to bolster SGBV mainstreaming. This includes the need to designate a Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Transgender and Intersex (LGBTI+) focal point and conduct trainings.  The over-crowded camps and the lack of adequate dwelling results in constraints to humanitarian actors’ ability to provide services.  Hundreds of incidents of SGBV are reported weekly.  Safe water, sanitation, and hygiene are an urgent priority.  Better access to information is needed. The assessments found that with the absence of an asylum law the protection environment is severely restrictive. Refugees do not have freedom of movement, lack a recognized status rendering them unable to access civil administration services, livelihoods and education opportunities, and justice. This leaves them vulnerable to exploitation, abuse and dependent on aid. Refugees cannot formally register births, deaths or marriages and they have no access to formal education which is coupled with gaps in law enforcement. With the monsoon and cyclone season, protection risks increase for all refugees, especially for persons with special needs (PSN), women and girls, who face mobility restrictions. The camp is situated on difficult terrain, highly prone to flooding and landslides. Dire living conditions exacerbate needs and the protection and safety risks they face and therefor disaster risk reduction (DRR) will be integrated into all programming. Consolidated and comprehensive identity management system is need to ensure reliable biometric data is available. Information on an individual level is a key need and priority which UNHCR and the Bangladesh authorities are working on. A comprehensive protection monitoring mechanism is required covering the entire refugee population and host communities and expanded outreach. Strengthening referrals, assistance delivery to meet needs, strengthen social cohesion are also key. SGBV prevention and response; child protection; legal counselling and legal assistance; support to authorities through system strengthening initiatives, protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming and protection advocacy are equally critical needs.

Activities or outputs

UNHCR has stepped up its protection monitoring, community outreach, referral pathways for protection services, mental health and psycho-social support, and engagement with all relevant governmental and law enforcement authorities. UNHCR’s community-based protection (CBP) approach is increasingly central to its emergency response which seeks to empower and support the resilience of refugees and their coping mechanisms. Interventions in the context of legal and physical protection, SGBV, child protection, and psycho-social support will be reinforced. While implementing a traditional multi-sectoral approach, UNHCR mainstreams protection throughout all its areas of interventions. Throughout its interventions, and in order to better inform the response, UNHCR has continuously consulted with the refugees through rapid needs assessments and regular community assessments focus groups. Needs assessments in combination with the 2017 family counting the on-going verification exercise have allowed for there to be population vulnerability analysis data. In line with the Joint Response Plan (JRP), in 2019 UNHCR and partners will step up analysis of protection trends and of the legal framework, protection monitoring capacity and its ability to provide protection by presence, advocacy in order to identify protection risks faced by refugees, inform effective responses, ensure refugees continue to have unimpeded access to asylum, and that any return is voluntary, in safety and with dignity. Key interventions will ensure that:o Access to territory is granted and reception conditions are improved; o Access to legal assistance and remedies are guaranteed and improved;o Returns are informed, voluntary and in safety and dignity;o Protection risks identified, trends analysed and addressed : o People with specific needs are identified and have access to services;o Communities are meaningfully engaged in the response;o Equitable access to information and accountability to refugees is ensured;o Refugees have access to civil documentation. There is an urgent need to galvanize solidarity for an approach to improve the situation for the people of Rakhine State. With the aim of garnering support from a broad range of stakeholders the "Solidarity Approach” provides a platform to ensure support is maintained for the refugees and host communities in Bangladesh and other hosting countries. In Bangladesh, the “Solidarity Approach” would ensure that that the response is sufficiently resourced and robust, and that the impact on the host community is mitigated including by remedying the environmental impact– and offering economic opportunities for refugees and host communities. In line with the Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF) approach, it will also encourage timely, adequate and needs-driven funding for the response, including through flexible, un-earmarked, and multi-year funding. UNHCR is ready to play a role in ensuring that returns take place on a voluntary and dignified way, once conditions are conducive in Myanmar. UNHCR will engage with refugees and government authorities to ensure that returns take place based on well-informed decision making and on a voluntary nature. UNHCR will play an important role ensuring voluntariness and dignified returns once conditions are conducive. Key messages shall be disseminated through UNHCR staff and the vast network of trained Community Outreach Members (COM) across camps. Access to information and accountability to refugees will be ensured via information points and referrals for persons with specific needs to other service providers. Recording and reporting complaints and feedback regarding services, with semi-private space for individual queries on sensitive topics will take place at the points. To respond to the needs of refugees who would not otherwise be able to reach conventional information sources, COMs also provide awareness raising and feedback on community concerns.
