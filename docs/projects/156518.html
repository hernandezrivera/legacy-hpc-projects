Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to reduce the specific vulnerability of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under five years (U5) through improved access to quality, safe and dignified nutrition and psychosocial support services in three governorates in Syria (Al-Hassakeh, Rural Damascus, and Aleppo). Access to primary healthcare services, including nutrition and psychosocial support, is a key priority for the nutrition sector where 4.7 million girls and boys U5 and PLWs are reported at risk of undernutrition and in need of preventive and curative nutrition services In addition, recent evidence of 137 low and middle-income countries across the world suggests that the cost of neglecting maternal psychosocial risk factors is huge and that addressing psychosocial factors among perinatal women can have a significant impact on child growth and well-being (Fawzi, et al., 2019). Given the distance and safety and displacement risks and the disrupted services, many PLW and U5 were unable to seek support for their nutrition needs. Of concern is the significant increase of acute malnutrition among PLW, which has more than doubled in one year. PLWs are in urgent need of nutrition services and should be prioritized for all basic services, according to the 2018 and 2019 HNOs. Furthermore, despite the acceptable levels for Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among under five in most of the assessed locations, there are pockets with poor levels of acute malnutrition, poor and/or serious levels of chronic malnutrition; such as, “Tel Abiyad (32%) and Eastern Ghouta (36%) that both exceed the ‘very high’ stunting classification according to WHO 2018 thresholds as evidenced by SMART surveys” (HNO, 2019). Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices in assessed locations remain suboptimal and are all below pre-crisis levels. Coverage of nutrition services continues to be insufficient due to limited resources. Another concern is the disturbing findings by AAH assessment in the clinic of Al-Loulaa in Hassakeh where growth monitoring was not perceived as a priority by the population despite population recognition of the medium to poor nutrition status of their children U5 and/or PLWs. Besides, a big proportion of infants are not exclusively breastfed due mainly to a perceived lack of milk production by the mothers. Thus, AAH aims to improve equitable access to health, well-being and living conditions of vulnerable women and children through accessible and quality healthcare, dignified living place and safe outreach. This will be achieved by strengthening the local institutional primary healthcare capacity in which at least one primary healthcare center will be rehabilitated, and one mobile medical service will be mobilized to reach 10,000 beneficiaries with nutrition services. Emphasis will be placed on malnutrition assessment and management, one-on-one individual IYCF counseling, and mental health and psychosocial support integrated. Additionally, a total of 30 healthcare staff (of which 15 are males and 15 are females) will be trained and mentored on-the-job to ensure implementation of national protocols and guidelines consistent with quality nutrition care delivery. Moreover, AAH will also target 10,000 PLW and children U5 with prevention and treatment of nutrition conditions in targeted health and mobile facilities where at least 1500 pregnant women will receive two comprehensive antenatal care visits, 1500 new-born babies will receive postnatal care within three days of delivery, and 1500 children will receive baby kits.

Needs

Population in need for nutrition interventions across all of Syria is estimated to be 4,734,890 PLW, and children U5. This includes 1,587,202 girls, 1,551,248 boys, and 1,596,413 PLW. Nutrition assessments show that acute and chronic malnutrition of children 6-59 months and women of child bearing age remain a problem in localized areas across Syria. Acute malnutrition among PLW has increased more than twofold in 2018. Additionally, 4.7 million girls and boys U5 and PLWs are reported at risk of undernutrition and in need of preventive and curative nutrition services. Around 91,811 girls and boys aged 6-59 months are acutely undernourished and, if malnutrition is not prevented, an additional 146,898 children U5 will become acutely malnourished during 2019. Moreover, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices in assessed locations remain suboptimal and are all below pre-crisis levels. For example, AAH assessment in the clinic of Al-Loulaa in Hassakeh shows that growth monitoring was not perceived as a priority by the population despite recognition of the medium to poor nutrition status of their children and/or PLWs. Besides, a big proportion of infants are not exclusively breastfed due mainly to a perceived lack of milk production by the mothers.

Implementation of activities

The project aims at improving equitable, safe and dignified access to nutrition services targeting PLWs and U5 children by strengthening the local institutional primary healthcare capacity in which at least one primary healthcare center will be rehabilitated, and one mobile medical service will be mobilized to reach 10,000 beneficiaries with primary healthcare and nutrition services. Emphasis will be placed on maternal, child and newborn health, including mental health and psychosocial support, and malnutrition assessment and management. Additionally, a total of 30 healthcare staff (of which 15 are males and 15 are females) will be trained and mentored on-the-job to ensure implementation of national protocols and guidelines consistent with quality nutrition care delivery. Moreover, AAH will also target 10,000 PLW and children under five with prevention and treatment of health and nutrition conditions in targeted health and mobile facilities where at least 1500 pregnant women will receive two comprehensive antenatal care visits, 1500 new-born babies will receive postnatal care within three days of delivery, and 1500 children will receive baby kits.

Protection Risk Analysis

Based on AAH’s internal Security Guidelines, Standard Operations Procedures and long experience in the field, possible risks have been identified based on context analysis, inputs from field staff and daily monitoring and observations. The possibility of any risk is constantly updated according to evolving situation and developments at the central and Governorate levels. The analysis takes into consideration several stakeholders that might influence the context as well as the planning and implementation of activities. AAH has a protection mainstreaming policy that all staff in the field sites have been trained in, and each field site has a protection focal point who is responsible for conducting continuous refresher trainings for the staff. AAH also mobilizes the expertise of a regional protection officer and a dedicated health and nutrition coordinator in Syria who are specifically trained in mainstreaming protection and safe programming in health to support continuous revision and proactive identification of risks involved in the implementation of this project. Key protection risks include: health workers moving between camps, potential looting of nutrition supplies, attacks on health and nutrition workers and beneficiaries in the facilities, discrimination against vulnerable groups like PLW and/or children U5, losing follow up of beneficiaries, and inequitable access to nutrition services. More specifically, when it comes to access, there are various risks involved, including: lack of official documentation or legal status for beneficiaries to receive nutrition care, beneficiaries reporting feeling unsafe during the journey to public healthcare centers, especially women and girls who are travelling alone, and women reporting lack of privacy, feeling unsafe, and waiting for long waiting hours and experiencing health concerns while waiting. There are a limited number of protection experts who are experienced in identifying and responding to GBV and SEA-related protection risks. This situation leads to serious challenges in the provision of information, ability to identify high-risk cases and provision of referrals and services. Mitigation measures include: • Apply the “Do No Harm” strategy while providing nutrition services to vulnerable groups throughout Syria.• Build health and nutrition staff capacity in using standardized approaches, services packages and tools when delivering services across Syria• Address the capacity gap by training health staff in static and mobile health facilities on providing first-line support, identification of GBV cases, safe referrals, and ensuring access of affected population to the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health, including clinical management rape.• Create and ensure safe, private and secure spaces for women and children that include spaces for confidential examination, availability of adequate female health staff to attend to the specific needs of women and girls, considerations for socio-cultural, age-specific, disability-specific and condition-specific needs (especially for psychosocial support interventions), differentiate prevention or treatment methods for boys and girls and women, to the extent possible, and ensure that both men (including boys) and women (including girls) will be involved in decision-making activities relevant to nutrition interventions. • Establish protection mechanisms in targeted healthcare facilities and mobile clinics, including case management, functional referral pathways, provision of psychosocial first aid and enough female health workforce.• Establish effective community-based complaints mechanisms.• Engage and sensitize community about targeting.• Adopt a flexible delivery modality with safe and accessible mobile outreach.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The security situation in targeted areas is unstable and AAH has no access to implement the activities; there are additional unforeseen administrative procedures imposed, and restrictions on access to beneficiaries. Likelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: AAH works and coordinates with authorities and Ministries to maintain access to target areas and beneficiaries, and the capacity to implement activities. AAH will reach out to local authorities and line ministries with trust building activities to further develop relationships and capacity to avoid or mitigate restrictions in AAH capacity to operate in Northern and Southern Syria. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community and humanitarian diplomacy activities on the local level are also considered to contribute to this mitigation measure. Risk 2: Substantive devaluation of the EURO or USD against the Syrian pound; International bank transfers to Syria are fluid and timely.Likelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, timely communicated to donors and options discussed. To overcome the lack of liquidity that affected several INGOs in 2018, AAH opened a new bank account, and resulted in successful completion of an international transfer. Risk 3: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruptionLikelihood: medium. Impact: highMitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Risk 4: Information gathering activities hampered by suspicion or misunderstandingLikelihood: low. Impact: mediumMitigation: Transparency, trust-building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers
