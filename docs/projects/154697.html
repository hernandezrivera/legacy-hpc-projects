Project Summary

The project (Integrated humanitarian response to the Provision of access to a quality education and Children Protection for IDPs children, OOSC and returnee children in Salahaldin) that Sorouh will implement aims at the provision of immediate access to inclusive safe and protected learning environment and accelerating the achievement of universal access to primary and secondary education; by considerably lowering the number of OOSC and re-integrate them into the formal education system in Salah Al-Din.
The project will register and enroll 10,000 OOSC children in the schools, ALP centres and community schools of OOSC, IDP and returnee school aged children (6-18) in Salah Al-Din governorate (Baiji, Tooz Khormatu ( Seleiman Beg ) Balad/ Dulueiya)
Also, the project will identify 40 schools, ALP centres and community schools in the 4 areas to register out of school age students through implementing back to school campaigns .
16 Radio programmes for promoting enrolment of school age children will be prepared and broadcasted .In addition, 1500 promotion materials such as brochures, leaflets, posters, will be printed and distrubuted to attract OOCS to enrol in schools , ALP centres, and community schools .
The project will also provide 8 counselling sessions in 4 locations to engage the leaders of the community to participate in the moblization of the OOSC students .
the project will distribute 2,000 back to school kits to the most venerable OOSC to reduce financial barriers at their enrolment in educational services.
The project will identify and select 100 teachers who lack the skills and tools classroom management, code of conduct, conflict sensitive education, pedagogy and psychosocial support.
Also, it is expected that many students may fail in the final exams due to the short period of classes, so the project will establish 4 remidial education centers ( hiring 20 volunteer lecturers for 3 months) in summer holdays to provide catch up classess.
The project will identify and train the existing PTAs in the 40 schools
The project will Provide psychosocial support to students who have lack of skills and tools to inclusive, safe and conflict sensitive education and psychosocial support (PSS).
100, children and adolescents will have referal pathways to case management services.
Also, 50 children will have support on Child Protection legal assistance .
the project will conduct hygiene promotion session in schools to raise awareness of good hygiene practices .
The project will provide Mine Risk Education sessions for the students about the risks of landmines and other explosive with support from ICRC trainers.
Sorouh will coordinating with the DoE in Salah Al-Din to ensure their support.
Total target groups of the project will be 10,770 students, teachers, volunteer lecturers and PTA members.

Needs

As per HRP it is estimated that in 2019 a total of ( 8.7) million people will require some form of humanitarian assistance. The Education Cluster total PiN stands at (2.6) and the Cluster target stands at (0.5) million. As the 2019 HRP focuses on a post-conflict transition towards durable solutions with a severity ranking of very high and high within Salah al-Din shows that - Baiji, Tooz Khormatu ( Seleiman Beg ) Balad/ Dulceiya /returnees required educational support .Salah Al-Din is the third governorate with highest number of OOSC children with 105,775 school-age children (6-12 years old) . Review of secondary data indicate that more than 302 in Salah Al-Din need rehabilitation (50% of schools in conflict-affected areas: Baiji, Tooz Khormatu ( Seleiman Beg ) Balad/ Dulceiya) . 25 % of children have limited or no access to formal learning opportunities. Adolescents and girls are particularly affected by economic, physical, psychological, cultural and policy barriers to accessing education. Schools are often in poor condition, unsafe and inadequately equipped to serve large numbers of students. Schools have been forced to host multiple shifts to accommodate students. Lack of educational opportunity is also a key source of social tension between host and displaced communities, as well as a barrier to the voluntary return of IDPs in these locations . DoE of Salah Al-Din faces many problems to meet the needs of the out of school children including the IDPs and returnees in Baiji, Tooz Khormatu ( Seleiman Beg ) Balad/ Dulceiya who are school age at the primary and secondary level due to the destruction of schools and low resources 302 schools in Salah Al-Din (out of 1800) are currently damaged /physically unsafe; little PSS offered in schools; weak school community for engagement /non-existent of PTAs; traditional learning techniques used (poor child participation /differing needs not met); no safe play areas for children. Many parents are unable to cover the cost of learning materials or school transport costs. The long distances to education facilities, coupled with the increasing use of informal child labour, further reduces access to education. More teachers are required as many schools are overcrowded and operate in two or three shifts per day, with many teachers and school staff also living in displacement. Moreover, greater teacher training is required, as few teachers are trained in psychosocial care and inclusive education. All recent assessments implemented in liberated areas highlighted similar needs: rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools; minor repairs of school infrastructure (windows, doors, electricity and heating systems); mine risk education; replacement of teaching and learning materials; and psychosocial support for students and teachers. The absence of education negatively affects the lives of Iraqi children in multiple ways. Unoccupied and disengaged children and adolescents (both boys and girls) are more likely to marry early, participate in child labour, be recruited by armed groups or be affected by other forms of abuse. Lack of educational opportunity is also a key source of social tension between host and displaced communities, as well as a barrier to the voluntary return of IDPs. The absence of education deprives children of the knowledge and skills necessary to live productive and responsible lives.

Protection Risk Analysis

Among the main protection threats and vulnerabilities that IDP and returnee communities in the target locations for enrolment in schools are likely to face in 2019: -The lack – or- confiscation of legal documents. -Acts of collective punishment (including both access denial to services, banned returns by tribal committees and attacks from communities). - Forced evictions leading to secondary displacement, premature/unsafe/forced and uninformed returns where IDPs also face high HLP concerns. -Separation of family members (part of the household staying in the area of displacement due to lack of services in the area of return for instance). -Lack of basic services and Social tensions. - Heavy procedures for return (including security clearance, claim for HLP compensation, etc). -Explosive hazards contamination and damaged/destroyed HLP. -Forced recruitment by local PMF (especially in Balad, Al Alam, and Baiji). -Secondary displacements due to lack of services, EH contamination and presence of ISIL sleeping cells.

Mitigation Measures

the mitigation actions against the protection risks1- Increase protection monitoring activities, especially in terms of evictions and related confiscation of IDs and/or use of force, and continue advocacy .2-Capacity-building of local actors (including local authorities and security actors) on protection principles, basic human rights, the principled returns framework and IDP/Returnee Protection under international and Iraqi law. 3-Strengthen the capacities of communities to protect themselves, including through setting-up and mentoring community-based committees (CBC). 4-Increase studies on the legal framework and lack in Salah-al-Din and pursue coordination efforts through the Legal Partner Meeting .5-Increase activities /research on social cohesion over the governorate of Salah-alDin. 6-Increase the provision of specialized protection services, including psychosocial support, case management.7- Increase Rapid Protection Assessments and multi-sectoral-assessments in areas of origin where returns are considered premature (for instance due to the presence of Explosive Hazards/ lack of services/ presence of armed groups- including extremist groups/ tribal conflicts) to strengthen advocacy on voluntary, safe, informed, dignified and sustainable returns. 8- Coordinate with governmental security actors (mainly ISF) and the PMF.9- Coordinate with the Health cluster to advocate on the severe lack of medical actors/specialized services in Salah-al-Din.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Sorouh has demonstrated a high commitment to accountability which aims to ensure that all sections of populations of concern have equitable and non-discriminatory access to education and protection assistance programmes, and a say in decisions that affect their lives. Sorouh is committed. to ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into programme proposal, monitoring and evaluations, staff inductions, training and performance management, and highlighted in reporting.communication and transparency; participation and inclusion; and learning and adaptation. The policy also standardizes the language used in reporting; informs priorities; supports assessments of the extent to which Sorouh achieves its commitments; and provides criteria when evaluating learning and improvements. Sorouh’s programmes integrate in : (1) A rights-based approach: that puts persons of concern at the centre of decisions that affect their lives(2) community-based approach: according to which planning, programming, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation should be guided by principles of equality and participation.(3) Community-based protection: this requires Sorouh to integrate community engagement in all aspects of its education and protection work, recognize the capacities as well as needs of communities in humanitarian crises, in order to avoid harm and ensure that humanitarian programmes do not inadvertently leave people and communities worse off. (4)Participatory assessment: this tool builds partnerships with IDP, returnee and host community women and men of all ages and backgrounds by promoting meaningful participation through structured dialogue. Separate discussions with women, girls, boys, and men, including adolescents, enable Sorouh to gather accurate information on the specific protection risks that different groups face, identify underlying causes, understand community capacities, and hear their proposed solutions. It helps communities to take collective action to enhance their own protection, and makes it possible to implement a rights- and community-based approach. Sorouh will continue to closely coordinate with IDP, returnee and host community women and men to ensure that Sorouh follows up on reported issues, and challenges are addressed and appropriate referrals made. Sorouh also will assess the protection outcomes of our legal assistance programmes, which uses a mixed methodology of household surveys and focus group discussions. Exit Strategy.Sorouh foresees a long-term plan which includes a viable exit strategy. We will include an adequate diversification of activities. Monitor closely the interventions to adjust them to the changing of funds based on the results and demonstrated potential. -Work on effectively with ED and CP clusters to provide support to the project .-Seek for partners who have approach to the same services and who can help the Sorouh to deliver sustainable services such as UNICEF, SCI, MC and DoE. -Liaise with the local government, community leaders, and other entities to provide support to the project.-Encourage teachers to work volnteerly until fund secured.
