Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the preparedness environment through increasing capacity of humanitarian shelter actors as well as communities, which includes the application of standard actions and positives approaches on gender, people with disabilities, protection, and environment while being accountable to the affected populations. The areas of intervention consider a combined analysis of risks, vulnerabilities, historical disaster profile, and average of people in need.

Implementing Partners

World Vision (WV): Registered with the Government as a trust and has been working in the country since 1983. WV aims to contribute to the wellbeing of 4 million girls and boys by 2025 and currently implements long-term development programs in Zambezia, Gaza, Nampula, and Tete. In coordination with local authorities, WV carries out mobilization and sensitization in targeted locations to ensure good beneficiary participation. Community volunteers are usually recruited and trained to assist in explaining the intervention objectives, selection criteria and raise awareness about the key activities. The selection criteria prioritize female, child and elderly-headed households, people with disability, and people with chronic illness and the identification process will involve community leaders.
UN-Habitat: The United Nations Human Settlements Programme has been implementing projects in the area of shelter, post-disaster interventions and post-conflict recovery areas for about two decades. The approach used by UN-Habitat, in addition to intervening in post-disaster recovery situations, has as its vision a more holistic approach to the shelter component. The range of actions includes technical assistance in the area of resilience of the built environment, buildings and improved reconstruction, for various actors without excluding the training of communities, more specifically local artisans.
IOM: Operating in Mozambique since 1994, with its Shelter and Housing Programme focusing since 2019 on "Humanitarian Preparedness and Response" and "Promotion of Resilience and Durable Solutions." Employing a multisectoral, settlements-based approach, the program prioritizes community-driven interventions that engage displaced and host communities to rebuild and recover sustainably. Aligned with Building Back Safer (BBS) principles, it supports not only the construction of stronger physical structures but also the strengthening of social networks and local economies. Its phased response plan encompasses emergency shelter distribution with essential items, early recovery support with materials and expertise, and long-term self-recovery and reconstruction, emphasizing resilient building techniques adapted to local contexts. By providing training and fostering empowerment, IOM equips vulnerable households to better withstand future crises and achieve lasting resilience.
AVSI: The main expertise is related to shelter intervention and capacity building, though have consistent capacity also in terms of distribution, particularly in 2024, in the provinces of Sofala (targeting over 1000 house – community new construction and retrofitting, training over 200 community members and giving opportunity to have hands on experience to about 50 students through a partnership with UniPiaget, construction of 6 climate resilient schools, training community members (50% of which women) on nature based techniques and finally distribution of ICSs (Improved Cooking Stoves) in the corridor Beira-Chimoio with target of 200,000 HHs in 2 years). AVSI works a lot also in DRR and preparedness, also in 2024 (and through 2025) is supporting the municipality of Beira and the district of Nhamatanda in revising the LAP (Local Adaptation Plan) through community consultations and working hand in hand with local authorities and INGD to pilot a revise a methodology that improves protection (and particularly child protection) considerations in the development of the LAP, with UNICEF support.
UNHCR: Plan to pre-position and distribute Non-Food Items (NFIs) aiming to meet the basic needs of forcibly displaced people due to climatic events. These people often face significant hardships and lack essential items, making the timely provision of NFIs critical. To ensure an effective response, the UNHCR's actions will be coordinated with relevant humanitarian actors, including Clusters, the HCT, and the ICCG. This coordination will help to align efforts and ensure that there is no duplication of assistance. The response will also involve close engagement with government bodies and the National Disaster Management Agency (INGD) to ensure that the intervention complements broader government and humanitarian efforts. The priority in distributing NFIs will be based on the most vulnerable populations and will take into account factors like the nature of displacement, access to affected areas, the interagency environment, and the operational capacity of UNHCR and its partners.
Peace Winds Japan: An International Organization that provides emergency humanitarian assistance, and its mission is to quickly arrive at the affected areas by natural disasters, conduct needs assessments, and respond according to those needs. Affected people need a safe place to stay, so when needs are identified, NFIs and shelter toolkits will be distributed. Also PWJ will train the beneficiaries and local communities to how to use the provided materials (shelter tool kits) correctly with techniques to build resilient houses (build back better method).
Although not detailed yet, the participation of other cluster partners in the implementation of the project is also expected, such as: AMACO, ADS, CARE, AJOPEM, Oikos, Peace Winds Japan, IFRC, CVM, Caritas and ADRA.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The Mozambican government approved this Tuesday (26), through the Council of Ministers, the Contingency Plan for the rainy and cyclonic season 2024-2025.This is an instrument with a deficit of nine billion meticais out of a total estimated at 11 billion meticais needed to meet all needs.The plan was approved through a resolution during the 35th ordinary session of the Council of Ministers, which took place yesterday in Maputo. According to a AIMThe plan includes cross-sectoral interventions aimed at rapid response and resilient post-disaster recovery and aims to reduce deaths and the destruction of vital infrastructure. According to forecasts by the National Institute for Disaster Risk Management (INGD), the 2024-2025 rainy season is expected to see 2.5 million more people affected.The rainy and cyclonic season of 2023-2024 was marked by tropical storms, heavy rains and urban floods, affecting approximately 242,611 people, about 48,522 families, between October 2023 and April 2024. Nearly 70% (168,585) of these people had their houses flooded, partially or completely destroyed. In the central and southern regions of the country, the most affected areas were the City of Maputo with 35% (83,756 people), followed by Maputo Province with 29% (70,074 people), and Sofala with a 7% (17,342 people). These three locations alone account for more than 70% of the total affected population nationwide. Damaged or destroyed houses together with flooding situations also impacts the life inside the dwellings compromising the domestic life in terms of available core items to sleep, cook, water and food storage, and sources of energy (fuel to cook, illumination). This project includes preparedness actions for natural disasters through the preposition of key supplies such as shelter and NFIs kits, the dissemination of education messages and the capacity building at community level on safer house reinforcement and reconstruction. The preposition of core supplies (Shelter & NFIs) aims to cover 60,000 people (12,000 households, 31,800 females. 28,200 males, from which 34,200are children, 22,800 adults, 3,000 elderly and estimated 9,000 with disabilities), the target number is based on the shelter partners capacity to respond during the rainy/cyclonic season 2022/2023 affected by a mid-scale event such as the Tropical Cyclone Freddy.The anticipatory actions aim to build capacity mainly through the positioning and awareness of key IEC materials in listed accommodation centers and training on safer reinforcement and rebuilding of shelters. These actions target 44,137 people (8,827 households, 23,393 females, 20,744 males, from which 25,158 are children, 16,772 adults, 2,207 elderly and estimated 6,621 with disabilities).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM is implementing the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) which enables the humanitarian community to identify priorities and activities through multi-sectoral assessmentsthat take into consideration gaps in humanitarian service coverage, including health, WASH, protection and shelterservices. The DTM displacement monitoring and emergencytracking tools will further inform humanitarian programming of protection risks and needs for better targeted and evidencebased interventions ultimately contributing to more secure settlements for affected populations. Regarding project design, the shelter cluster partners ensures that the shelter Kit content is tailormade to mitigate protection risks and takes into consideration the needs of vulnerable groups. Greater protection concerns are mainstreamed within the Shelter Cluster partners and into the multi-sectoral teams (in addition to having protection representation within IOM structure), who conduct referrals and services specifically targeted for those vulnerable persons. IOM is an active member of the Protection cluster and shelter-protection assessment visits inclusive of HH surveys that focus on vulnerability criteria and protection aspects are performed to ensure integration of these aspects into the design of the program activities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Shelter Cluster partners with support from other clusters, will conduct multisectoral community engagement exercises which include focal group discussions, visits to the affected communities, community and household assessments, inorder to understand their most immediate pressing shelter and NFI needs and for those displaced, the intentions of the communities to resettle into sites either set up by theGovernment (to be verified that have meet the minimumstandards), or plots provided by the Government within the host community. Communities will be engaged in on-the-job training exercises with the intention to give guidance on the use of the shelter materials and improved construction techniques. A right- and community-based participatory approach is essential to create meaningful involvement by displaced residents in protection and assistance activities.The community’s leadership, but also other representatives of men, women and youth, elderly and persons with disabilities will be included in the design of programming and assistance activities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities will be targeted and have a higher score while selecting the most vulnerable houses. These people, if with no labor capacity, will receive technical and labor support for the construction of their shelters. And estimated 20% of the population with disabilities will have been assisted under our programing.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The implementing partners will seek strict adherence to the Sphere Standards for Humanitarian Assistance which identify the environment as a cross-cutting issue to be incorporated to all humanitarian assistance. In that sense, partners will encourage the use of assessment tools to assess the environmental impact of the construction related program activities. This tool focus from the selection of the shelter sites, the construction of structures reviewing the possible negative environmental impacts, the management considerations while occupied and also considers the possible decommissioning or dismantling of the structures to reutilize the resources. The use of this tools will engage local actors and communities to understand the different conditions particular to the working areas
