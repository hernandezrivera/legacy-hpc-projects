Needs

Chad has been hosting more refugees than any other countries in the region. Refugees from Sudan, Central African Republic and Nigeria continue to put an additional strain on the already overstretched and scarce resources. The refugee crisis in Eastern Chad began in 2004, when populations fled the conflict in Darfur, Sudan, over the border to Chad. UNHCR’s operation in Eastern Chad currently covers thousands of refugees in 12 camps along the border with Darfur. The camps are in desert conditions characterized by scarcity of natural resources and lowland. Besides, the turmoil affecting the neighboring countries namely CAR and Nigeria has led to the opening new refugee camps in southern and western borders of Chad. Currently, over 18 camps are scattered across the country. Most of the refugees live in a difficult situation and relay on WFP and UNHCR food aid. However, due to budget constraints, the quality of nutrition interventions in the Sudanese camps decreased since 2014. The food aid rations have been reduced up to 881 kcal per person per day currently (standard = 2100 kcal/per/day). The nutrition situation of the refugees was made more challenging by the nutrition crisis in the Sahel regions of Chad. The nutrition SMART/SENS survey carried out in September 2018 revealed acute global malnutrition rate as high as 13.5% ,With the highest rate in Kanem region 25%, and the lowest in Mandoul region 4%. 13 out of the 23 regions have Acute severe malnutrition rate above 2% (the emergency threshold ,WHO). That nutrition situation had led the Ministry of Public Health with UNICEF support to implement a program of integrated management of acute malnutrition (IMAM), targeting under-5 children with severe acute malnutrition.

Activities or outputs

Procurement and timely distribution of ready to Use Therapeutic Foods and essential medicines ; Capacity strengthening of health centres (anthropometric equipment, training materials, stock management tools) Training of health workers on integrated management of acute malnutrition Technical support of frontline staff through on the job training Conduct biannual vitamin A and deworming campaigns Strengthening supervision of the health centres by the district
