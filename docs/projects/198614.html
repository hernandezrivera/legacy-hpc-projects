Project Summary

Jami Al Hakeem Foundation is a nonprofit, non-governmental organization, registered with the Corporate Affair Commission (CAC) in 2008 has been operating in North-east Nigeria since its inception, delivering humanitarian assistance in conflict-affected areas. J-HF works closely with its national partner organizations in GBV, employs a survivor centered approach. This project will work closely with Health and Protection partners to deliver comprehensive support for Women and Girl of reproductive age and GBV survivors while combining efforts on engaging the communities.
The proposed project seeks to initiate an intervention to build resilience through integrated SRHS and mainstreaming Protection among IDP's and returnees communities in selected LGA's of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States respectively, Which includes Bama, Fufure and Potiskum LGA's respectively; while mainstreaming protection as a sub component of the project to protect women, adolescent girls and children against Gender Based Violence in the target locations.
This project also seeks to strengthen the capacity of Public Health Workers /volunteers in the target communities in order to raise awareness on the need for SRHS. The provision of SRHS is essential in preventing and protecting reproductive health need for women and girls . Therefore, good SRH practices such as safe-access to family planning services and good mensural hygiene practices and general good health practices will be promoted. Therefore, the public health workers/ promoters will be saddled with the responsibility of raising awareness on maintaining good SRH practices. Consequently, sensitization to women and girls on menstrual hygiene management will be crucial to the project. Furthermore, sensitization on GBV prevention, community engagement and capacity building on GBV will be conducted. The purpose of the proposed intervention is to raise awareness on the need for maintaining good SRH practices and strengthening of GBV services.The proposed intervention will target 2000 beneficiaries (women and adolescent girls) in Bama, Fufore and Potiskum LGA respectively.
SGBV Prevention and Response: J-HF will maintain the established Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces (WGSS) where integrated case management and psychosocial support (PSS) counselling services are provided and will be strengthened to ensure women and girls receive the life-saving, confidential and holistic services they need to heal and recover and gain important life skills.Th WGSS will also serve as a space where ASRH programmes will be carried out through an established time and day with adolescents and women Referral pathways will be regularly updated in coordination with service providers in order to facilitate timely referrals. Confidential survivor information will be managed through the GBVIMS platforms. Clinical staff in the state-run health facilities will be trained on clinical management of rape (CMR) as well as providing responsive care for survivors at the facilities including GBV incident management skills to ensure appropriate and ethical standards are followed. A behavioral model that aims to engage community and religious?? leaders, men and boys as allies in gender equality and women’s empowerment will be strengthened through targeted trainings, of community committees on SGBV prevention through outreaches, Focus group discussions(FGD), community meetings. The model will also aim at sensitizing community members on the safe integration of women and girls formerly associated with armed groups back into the community.

1 - Needs

The conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. No early end to the conflict is foreseen. Some 8.4 million people(2.6 M girls, 2.3 M boys, 1.8 M women and 1.6 M men) in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022 (ocha_nga_humanitarian_needs_overview_feb2021 (1).pdf), only slightly fewer than a year ago. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. This figure also includes the majority (an estimated 733,000) of the 1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors of the following groups: (i) internally displaced people (IDPs), (ii) returnees (former IDPs and refugees alike), and (iii) host communities or vulnerable conflict affected populationAccording to Humanitarian Need Overview 2022, About 5 million people are still in dire need of humanitarian health assistance. Among the key risks are the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic (https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/nigeria/health). The BAY states have the highest maternal mortality rate in Nigeria, estimated at 1,549/100,000 live births (https://www.cdc.gov/reproductivehealth/index.html). Approximately 18% of IDPs (396,000 people), 17% of people in host communities (663,000) and 23% of returnees (345,000) do not have sufficient access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene.The high prevalence of GBV is a key challenge in the BAY states, especially for female IDPs, who are frequently exposed to sexual violence, or forced into survival sex and forced/early marriages. Mental health challenges are also rife in the BAY states, with statistics indicating that around 20% of affected people in humanitarian emergencies develop mental health conditions. Women, girls, and children face grave protection risks including abduction by NSAGs, impeding access to critical services including health facilities especially those cited outside camp/ living areas.
