Project Summary

An Estimated 2.2M people in south central Somalia are expected to experience crisis (IPC phase 3 and Emergency Phase 4) FSNAU/FEWSNET July 2019. droughts, resurgent conflict, lack of governance and rule of law coupled lack of long term development programs continue to negatively impact the lives of vulnerable Somali women, girls, boys, and men.
The sustained rain failures of three seasons has led to a severe drought in the country starting from deyr 2018 all through 2019. Crop production in the bread basket region as well as most Agro-pastoral areas that depend on seasonal planting have declined decimally beyond the level of the long-term average.This escalated increased food insecurity among the Agro-pastoral rural communities that mostly depend on own crop production for food source. The drought also led to widespread depletion of pasture and water in the predominant pastoral areas, a situation that led to substantial loss of livestock of between 50-70% in northern and around 50% in south central regions according to IRDO need assessments of 2019. Food security have severely declined among pastoral communities due to high loss of livestock’s, a situation that led to reduced income and low purchasing power for poor households. Other major drivers of food insecurity include, increased food prices in contrast to decline of livestock prices, poor livelihood diversifications and reduced access for areas under Al-shabab control. According to FSNAU July 2019 update, 2.2M people are categorized to be food insecure in Somalia. Out of this, 2.2M are classified to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). This means a total of 2.2 million people are in need of urgent humanitarian assistance. The other 3.1 million are classified as Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and will require livelihood protection to deter them from falling into increased destitution. Acute and widespread food insecurity and increased morbidity have contributed to deterioration of the overall nutrition situation in Somalia. According to July 2019 FSNAU-FEWSNET, an estimated 388,000 children under age 5 are acutely malnourished, including 87,000 who are severely malnourished. Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD)/cholera cases are also on the rise. The effect of the two could exacerbate acute malnutrition and mortality among children. According to the UNHCR-led Protection and Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), 893,000 persons were displaced by the drought since November 2018 while another 1.1 million people remain in protracted displacement countrywide. Increase in displacements due to drought continue to place additional strain on local communities’ scarce resources. Sustained humanitarian assistance during the first half of 2019 was a key factor in preventing further deterioration in food security and nutrition in Somalia. Similarly, with 2019 GU season being below average, the food security situation may worsen, and the risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) still exists through at least in late 2019. To address this problems, IRDO will undertake a sustained integrated food security and livelihood support intervention in 2019 and 2020. The geographical coverages of the response include regions of Lower Juba, Lower Shabelle, Galmudug, Middle Juba and South west state specific activities to be undertaken will be largely be cash and voucher-based interventions to improve purchasing power and food access for households in emergency situations. Recovery and Livelihood protection activities covering livestock and crop production support, economic recovery and diversification of livelihood opportunities will also be undertaken

Mitigation Measures

Security is a major concern in most parts of Somalia, although the areas that this project will be implemented is accessible IRDO will put measures to mitigate insecurity i.e. by ensuring that there is close monitoring of the security situation before deployment of project staff. Sharing information with all the stakeholders will help reduce incidences of corruption and fraud in instances of staff recruitment procurement of project materials. IRDO will ensure that there is adequate capacity and especially of monitoring to ensure that there is proper accountability of the project resources.in some instances the organization will accept the risks associated with natural disasters as the organization cannot influence nature, but will put a better contingency planning in place and use seasonality approach to mitigate the risk. IRDO will also put in place proper administrative procedures i.e. training and ensuring adequate staff cognizant of procurement guidelines; IRDO will ensure continuous engagement of all stakeholders encouraged

- The Somalia Food Security Cluster (SFSC) aims to be the primary source of information on the humanitarian response by addressing food insecurity, and by providing its members with a strategic vision and guidance in their response to the acute and underlying causes of crisis. In Somalia in particular, the FSC will aim to improve the timeliness of responses in order to provide a more effective and cost-efficient response.
