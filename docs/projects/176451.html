Project Name

Emergency response and improved access to essential primary health care to vulnerable populations affected by acute and chronic health emergencies, in Renk, Leer, Pibor, and any other location across South Sudan.

Start Date

01/01/2021

End Date

31/12/2021

Project Summary

To reduce morbidity and mortality by providing access to quality essential primary healthcare, including reproductive health, services to survivors of SGBV and mental health services, to girls, boys, men and women in emergency situations, with specific focus on accessibility for persons living with disability and the elderly. To build the resilience of communities and the MoH to prevent, detect and respond to disease outbreaks.

1 - Needs

The Health Cluster conducted severity and people in need mapping across South Sudan, identifying 5.2 million People in Need for health, out of which 52% in extreme need (HNO, 2021). The already weak health system was affected by several shocks including violence and flooding, causing damage or destroying existing facilities. It is estimated that only 7.5% of the 1,763 reporting health facilities provide basic health services and only 44% of the population in South Sudan lives within a 5 km radius of a health facility (HNO, 2021). The outbreak of COVID-19 further exacerbated the poor health situation by disrupting routine interventions such as vaccination campaigns and distribution of mosquito nets. This contributed to high malaria caseloads in 2020, with malaria being the highest cause of morbidity in South Sudan (HNO, 2021). Furthermore, South Sudan continues to face outbreaks of vaccine preventable disease such as measles which during 2020 affected eight locations in the country. Pibor in the HNO 2021 was described as having catastrophic humanitarian needs. The health needs are classified as extreme and the IPC report 2020 classified the area as phase 5 ‘Famine Likely’. Medair conducted a needs assessment in December 2020 including facility assessments and focus group discussions (FGD). Results showed that although some health facilities are functional, the available services do not meet the South Sudan Basic Package of Health Services. Key services such as reproductive health services do not meet the community needs, and services such as mental health case are not available despite the population having suffered numerous traumatic events in recent years. Besides this, the local health needs are beyond the capacity of the currently available services, demonstrated by frequent stock outs of essential medicine observed in health facilities and FGD participants describing the absence of available health services as a reason to not go to the health facility. MSF-B reported prior to their exit in December 2020 to have 1-2 under 5 deaths per week mostly due to acute respiratory infection and malnutrition with medical complications. The HNO 2021 identified Renk as having extreme health needs. Renk has faced an ongoing nutritional crisis with GAM rates above crisis levels since 2014 with the latest GAM at 26.9% in the host community and 22.8% among people living in the displacement sites in 2018. Renk was classified as IPC 4 in 2020. The nutrition emergency makes the population of Renk particularly vulnerable to poor health and communicable diseases. Although the MoH health system made improvements during 2020 with the support of the health strengthening partner, the functionality of the PHCUs and Renk Civil Hospital remain limited and insufficient to meet local needs. Focus group discussions highlighted that local health facilities often lack staff, drugs, or equipment. Insecurity since October 2020 resulted in disruption of all health and nutrition support in Renk. As a result, Medair was informed of several maternal and infant deaths due to a lack of access to reproductive health services. Leer has been classified as having severe health needs. There are no options for secondary healthcare referral and existing health resources are insufficient to meet the basic needs of the highly vulnerable groups (e.g. severely malnourished children). The absence of effective public services leaves the population at significant risk of communicable diseases and preventable deaths. Besides this, years of violence and multiple displacements have created a high burden of mental health needs, which, if unmet, increases the vulnerability of the affected persons and their ability to provide and care for their families.

2 - Project activities

This project will include acute emergency response capacity and longer-term emergency healthcare interventions, both through integrated multi-sectoral response. Medair’s Emergency response team assesses and responds to public health emergencies across South Sudan. The team has, depending on the size of ongoing interventions, the capacity to run 1-3 responses at a time. Triggers for deployment include large scale population displacement, emergency GAM rates, emergency maternal and child mortality rates, disease outbreaks and natural disasters. The emergency response team implements short term interventions aimed at managing the shock of a new emergency. Intervention duration is up to three months, after which activities are handed over to local health partners. Medair will maintain the flexibility to provide rapid responses using different modalities appropriate to the context, such as temporary or mobile clinics, emergency PHC services, iCCM (including SAM management where appropriate), and supporting longer-term health partners to restore PHC services at fixed sites in conflict-affected locations through technical and operational support. Interventions will be adapted to the COVID-19 context and will facilitate appropriate IPC and WASH measures to ensure safe delivery of services and protection of health workers. Medair is starting up a new longer-term emergency project in Pibor in 2021. The project is aiming to improve access to essential basic health services to a community heavily affected by different crises in recent years, including flooding and intercommunal violence. The project will aim to complement the already existent health systems and will include facility support (PHCU or PHCC level) and community-based services such as iCCM. Medair will aim to provide all basic services including reproductive health services such as support to SGBV survivors, according to international standards. Facilities supported by Medair will provide a full package of support including supportive supervision, technical quality assurance and operational support in line with the basic package of health services. Medair will continue to address the most critical primary health care gaps in Renk through at least two emergency primary health care facilities when access resumes. This includes the provision of 24-hour reproductive health care services as well as inpatient care for children with severe acute malnutrition and medical complications at the stabilization center in Abayok. All facilities in Renk provide integrated services with Nutrition and WASH and are accessible for persons with disabilities. The facilities receive weekly supervision and where possible the supervision is done jointly with the CHD. In Leer Medair will continue its community-based program providing treatment for common illness in children under 5 through iCCM, and family psychosocial support services through community cascade groups. Furthermore, Medair will continue to provide mental health services in three primary healthcare facilities utilizing mhGAP-HIG, as well as services for survivors of SGBV. Medair continues to work closely with other partners in Leer to ensure referral pathways are implemented where available.

3 - Project indicators

Medair will measure the quality of it program and success of the intervention through the following objectives: Sectoral Objective 1- Malaria incident rate - Percentage of children aged six months to 15 years who have received measles vaccination, on completion of a measles vaccination campaign Sectoral Objective 2- Percentage of deliveries conducted by skilled birth attendants- Number of consultations per person per year - Number of mentally ill persons receiving MHPSS services- Number of health facilities providing SGBV health and MHPSS services- Number of community health workers per 1,000 people- Number of primary healthcare centers with Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmONC)/500,000 population- Percentage of health facilities assessed using Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) scorecard- Percentage of facilities with community feedback mechanism- Percentage of functioning health facilities provided with supportive supervision Sectoral Objective 3- Number of SGBV survivors receiving CMR services Medair will collect project data on a weekly and monthly basis from all project locations, which is monitored by health managers and health project managers. The health project managers compile monthly sitreps in which progress against indicators is measured. Medair also submits monthly reports to all relevant partners, including MoH and the 5Ws report to the Health Cluster. Indicators such as percentage of skilled birth attendance and measles coverage are normally measured through KPC surveys, however in light of the current COVID-19 pandemic and new surge in cases all surveys are on hold and alternative ways to measure these indicators are considered. Medair has a separate monitoring, evaluation and learning sector which is responsible for measuring projects’ success using standard and custom monitoring tools. The M&E team ensures all the collected data are accurate and without double counting and evaluates the project against the indicators. If gaps are identified and the project is not on track, corrective measures will be taken and the project will be re-aligned. Technical oversight of data collection and progress measurement is provided by Medair’s Health Advisor.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Medair will be transparent with the affected population by providing them with accessible and timely information about PSEA and set up confidential feedback collection mechanism as mentioned above. Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities. Additionally, Medair will provide information about PSEA to beneficiaries through focus group discussions and in meetings with the community and community leaders. All Medair staff receive training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Medair ensures all members of the affected population’s voices are equally heard and their concern, comments, and perceptions will be taken into consideration during program design and implementation. The feedback collection mechanisms and focus group discussions particularly target the most vulnerable members of the community such as marginalized community members, people with disability, individuals who are not literate, women and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities. In additions, before project implementation, Medair will carry out assessments, focus group discussions with different gender and age groups, and key informant interviews that will inform the type of assistance the affected populations need, and the modalities through which they believe assistance is best delivered. Barrier analysis will be carried out to understand and identify barriers experienced by the community in health seeking behavior and access to essential healthcare services. During project implementation, Medair will undertake regular meetings with the affected communities and community leaders and feedback mechanisms will be put in place. Medair will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. A beneficiary telephone hotline, community focal point, information box, post distribution monitoring and exit surveys will be conducted to collect feedback on the affected population’s concerns, perceptions, and interests. Medair will respond to all complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and conducts and a community wide awareness raising session will be conducted. Medair tracks beneficiary feedback, the response to the feedback, and satisfaction of the community in an AAP databased monitored by the M&E team.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedbacks will be collected with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct by program staff. Medair analyses all feedback thoroughly to adjust the projects design according to the beneficiaries’ interest, context and resources. In situations where Medair is unable to make changes due to seen and unforeseen reasons, the affected beneficiaries will be informed and further dialogue will take place to find alternative approaches.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Medair estimates the risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence as a result of the distance to the location of humanitarian intervention as low, however the potential impact is high. In deciding on the location of the humanitarian interventions Medair will consult with the local community on the most suitable locations. Where possible Medair will aim to provide assistance as close as possible to the beneficiaries, limiting the travel distance. Medair will also consider the time windows in which assistance is delivered in order to prevent beneficiaries from having to travel in the dark. Medair mitigates the risk of PSEA through training of all staff and clear reporting mechanism for both beneficiaries and staff. Medair will also clearly communicate that any assistance is delivered free of any charge or compensation. During interventions Medair works in teams rather than individuals providing services General protection mainstreaming is done throughout Medair’s project to prevent beneficiaries from harm. Medair assesses available protection assistance in all project locations. In many areas of operation, no or very limited protection assistance is available. Medair is involved in national and sub national coordination, including the protection cluster, to advocate for addressing gaps in coverage. Medair further designs project interventions to be able to support survivors of protection incidents. All Medair staff is trained in psychological first aid and Medair supported clinics are equipped to provide medical support through clinical management of rape in a confidential matter. Where possible Medair will support the referral of the beneficiary for further protection assistance. Medair’s has learned through experience that marginalized groups have less engagement in general community activities. In order to address this Medair specifically targets these groups during community consultation. This is done by facilitating access for marginalized groups to participate in meetings or focus groups discussions or, if appropriate, having consultation with these specific groups. Medair further advocates with community leaders and other stakeholders for the inclusion of marginalized groups in all interactions with providers of humanitarian aid and in coordination mechanisms.

Referral is set up either to nutrition services provided by Medair or to other nutrition partners depending on the location of intervention.
