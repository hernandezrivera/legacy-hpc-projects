Project Summary

Through this project, NRC aims to provide immediate and reliable response to unmet need in the Northeast Nigeria including Dikwa, Munguno, Damasak and Mubi and other newly accessible areas.
In accordance with the NRC’s operational location and its program response strategy, NRC will implement shelter and NFI provision to enable targeted households to access timely the improved better living and protective living condition. The NRC shelter response will also include emergency shelter package, which will consist by providing the emergency shelter and NFI kits while the most and adapted robust shelter response will be designed. The NRC response strategy will also include technical training to enable local carpenter to build and maintain better the provided shelter and the provision of habitability measure training and awareness for the improvement and maintaining the healthier living environment.
Following the NRC community base approach, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the traditional leaders, the affected group representation and other stockholders (on ground humanitarian actors including the CCCM agency) to establish the Beneficiary Selection Committee (BSC) through which NRC will emphasize about the project objectives and beneficiary selection criteria. In order to strengthen the project impact, NRC will coordinate the response with other partners through the shelter sector in providing rapid and timely delivery and avoid gaps and duplication of efforts

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis across Northeast Nigeria, particularly Borno State, remains dire. The HRP estimates that people in the three most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe now depend on humanitarian assistance for their survival. The Shelter, NFI and CCCM sectors estimated that about 39 per cent of IDPs live in 252 IDP sites (camps, collective centres and camp-like settings) in northeast states, and the other in host community locations. According to the HRP 2018, 1.3 million returnees amongst 0.2 million are estimated to be in need of assistance including shelter support. An intention survey (http://bit.ly/2ipm4qR) indicated that a significant proportion of IDP households can be expected to remain at their current locations in the near future. Furthermore, depending on the upcoming election outcome, probable scenarios indicate that more transitional returns are to be expected next year, with most of the displaced persons returning mainly in LGA headquarters (HQ) due to ongoing insecurity in most of their areas of origin. In addition, several analyses show that the majority of IDPs are in need of food, shelters, and NFIs including kitchen sets. The 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) indicated that displacement and destruction of homes and infrastructure caused significant needs for shelter and NFIs. Nearly half a million homes and 700 public buildings have been destroyed by the conflict, along with 1,200 schools, nearly 800 health facilities, and 1,600 water supply sources. Communities have lost infrastructures and IDPs live among host communities while more than 1.07 million IDPs are residing out of camps, consequently overstretching host community resources to breaking point. According to the June 2018 DTM report round XVIII, Borno State continues to host the majority of IDPs followed by Adamawa. The increasing in number of IDPs is recorded in Dikwa due to ongoing-armed conflicts although Maiduguri remain the area with the large number of IDPs despite the significant number of individuals retained to their location. The already poor host communities have been sharing resources with displaced populations for more than five years with little support, and are now relying on negative coping strategies after savings and assets have been exhausted. If unaddressed, tensions between displaced and host communities could increase and exacerbate the conflict. Meanwhile, the continuous influx of displaced population and returnees and overcrowding in camps due to poor site planning, limited availability of safe shelters, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services continue to increase the risk of outbreaks (such as Cholera and fire). The overcrowding is a particular protection concern for women and girls, and negatively affects the elderly and people with disabilities. Due to lack of resources, IDPs who are living in low-income neighborhoods and informal settlements in urban areas/host communities continue to face a dire situation where dwellings are often made of non-permanent materials or ill-suited to protect people from harsh weather. The immense protection concerns particularly affect vulnerable people including children, boys and girls, pregnant and elderly as they are at risk to harm. Returnees are facing a wide range of difficulties upon return in addition to security-related issues. Challenges of how to meet basic needs such as shelter and re-establishing viable livelihoods strategies are daunting. Returnees are likely coming back to a rural landscape that has suffered through conflict, total destruction of infrastructure, and neglect.. The overall objective for the shelter programme in 2019 is to improve the living conditions, dignity, and protection of the most vulnerable people affected by displacement.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism in order to cater for the needs of the most vulnerable households. Inclusion of the less vulnerable will start from the beneficiary selection stage. The following process will follow; NRC will form a Beneficiary Selection Committee (BSC). The beneficiary selection committee will constitute both genders, person representing people living with disabilities, youth representatives and person representing minority groups. The BSC will have a clear terms of reference and will contribute to refining the selection criteria. NRC will moderate the discussions. Beneficiary selection shall ensure that the needs for the most vulnerable are met. Once the selection is done, the BSC will also be charged with the responsibility of ensuring that the target beneficiaries have their entitlements. In order to allow for beneficiaries to receive appropriate feedback to their complaints, NRC will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism. A hot line, through which the complaints will be channelled will be put in place and shared with staff. There will also be a Complaint desk during distributions. All the complaints shall be addressed on a timely manner. NRC will also ensure that the project meet the needs for the different groups within the target population. This will be done through holding separate focus group discussions with men, women, girls, boys, elderly, disabled and other minority groups, focusing on what their priority and needs are when it comes to the implementation of this project. NRC will hold periodic meetings with the BSC and will also ensure that the main meeting, giving feedback on the output of the project is done before handing over the facilities to the communities.
