Project Summary

This project will contribute to the Nutrition Cluster’s strategic objective two, strengthening lifesaving preventive nutrition services, by improving nutritious food acquisition, knowledge, practices, consumption and ultimately nutrition outcomes for rural and IDP populations within IPC 3 and 4.
In Somalia, the high cost of a nutritious diet does not support the creation of an enabling food environment for a healthy population. In addition to the issue of low affordability, poor and vulnerable people face physical access constraints related to the lack of availability of nutritious diets. These constraints, coupled with poor knowledge and skills in food choices and preparation, contribute significantly to sustained high numbers of malnourished individuals. Children Under-5 and lactating women are consistently the most vulnerable to malnutrition in Somalia.
FAO plans to support 2,000 rural and IDP households with children between 6 to 59 months with a blended approach prioritizing unconditional cash transfers (UCTs) valued at 80 percent of the Food Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) to improve immediate access to nutritious foods. To enhance the impact of cash, this intervention will be complemented by Behavior Change Communication (BCC) and nutrition education adapted to the local context. This activity will engage primary caregivers in cooking demonstrations, role-play and individual counselling to increase the chances of retention and adoption of new healthy habits. The aim is to improve complementary feeding for children 6-59 months to prevent recurrent cases of malnutrition.
FAO shall also provide 800 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) enrolled in nutrition treatment with conditional cash transfers (CCT), valued at 80 percent of the food MEB by region, on the condition that they attend maternal and child care support groups within their communities. Through these peer support groups, they will receive knowledge and peer support during pregnancy and lactation period. Through the cash assistance, they will have access to nutritious foods to meet their nutritional needs. Under this activity, FAO shall consider approximately 200 PLW to receive UCTs to accommodate participants that would be willing, but are not able to attend trainings. FAO shall work closely with partners to provide flexible ways of receiving the content of trainings for this smaller group.
FAO will also support the creation of mother-to-mother support groups reaching an additional 1,000 women between 15 to 49 years. Each group will be made up of 10-15 mothers from vulnerable households in rural areas. These groups will receive kitchen gardening kits and simple tools to promote the establishment of kitchen gardens that can provide a source of nutritious foods especially during dry seasons.
Nutrition education, awareness and training sessions will be conducted by locally recruited and trained members of the community called Nutrition Champions (one male and one female in every village). These champions will be trained to disseminate information and knowledge on nutrition and services available across their villages.
In total, FAO shall support 4,000 households/ 24,000 individuals with life-saving nutrition assistance. FAO will employ community- led beneficiary selection processes and ensure adequate targeting and representation of extremely vulnerable and minority groups in community committees including women, youth and people living with disabilities (PWD). Interventions proposed will employ a DoNoHarm, a gender lens, and conflict- sensitive approach to reduce protection risks for all individuals, and to reduce the risk of conflict or community tensions.

1. - Projects activities

1. Infant and young child nutrition (IYCN) assistance targeting 1000 households with children 6-59 months. This support will include monthly Unconditional Cash Transfers (UCTs) valued at 80 percent of the food MEB by region as advised by the Cash Working Group. These will be provided for 5 months to increase HHs access to nutritious foods. To increase the impact of desired results, the cash transfers will be complemented with voluntary participation in nutrition education to primary caregivers to enhance the knowledge, skills knowledge, attitudes and food consumption practices for children 6-59 months. There will be sessions on preparation of age-appropriate food using locally- available ingredients to increase sustainability of the actions. These local recipes will be consolidated and disseminated across project areas. Sessions will be designed to include the participation of men to increase their capacity in contributing to decisions related to food choice and consumption at the household level. Two Nutrition Champions (one male and one female) will be trained using a Training of Trainers (ToT) approach to deliver the trainings with the support of local community health workers. An IYCN media campaign will also be delivered through radio and text messaging highlighting the key messages for behavior changes with regard to feeding children 6-59 months in the local context. 2. 800 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) previously enrolled in nutrition treatment programmes (from rural IDP communities) will be provided with 5 months of conditional cash assistance valued at 80% of the food MEB per month. They will be required to attend trainings on maternal nutrition education. A small number of 200 PLW who face extreme levels of vulnerability and are willing but not able to attend trainings will benefit from 5 months of UCTs. Peer groups will be formed at the health facility level to share maternal nutrition related knowledge, encourage women to learn more on how to take care of themselves, share experiences and improve their resilience as a community. Social groups have been shown to increase the capacity of women to recover from shocks. This intervention is aimed at lowering the barriers to access to nutrition education and services, and raise incentives for any PLW to make healthy and sustainable food choices. It will also be an opportunity to strengthen their ability to practice healthy diets thereby reducing maternal malnutrition and related risks. 3. Approximately 100 mother- to- mother women groups, reaching 1,000 women, will be formed to include women of reproductive age. Through these groups, women will be provided with trainings based on a farm- to- fork approach and a kitchen gardening kit. These trainings are expected to spur improvement in household level practices on food production, choices and consumption. The trainings on kitchen gardening will also focus on nourishing all members of the households (particularly PLW, children Under-5 and adolescents). Local community members will be trained as Nutrition Champions (one male and one female per village) to promote positive nutrition messages in the target community.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash assistance provides households the agency to make decisions around cash expenditure, and selection of basic items in the quality and quantity and urgency of need. This helps to mitigate the exploitation of beneficiaries by vendors and also enables beneficiaries to acquire the best value for money by purchasing where, when and in the quantities that meet their immediate needs.In addition, the flexible nature of cash plays a significant role in enhancing inclusivity within interventions. For instance, women and girls are able to access services and goods tailored to their needs, thereby reducing protection concerns that stem from lack of such basic needs.Cash through mobile money addresses physical access barriers to offer more inclusivity by ensuring that people with disabilities, people with restricting security access can benefit from humanitarian interventions.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

This project utilizes a market- based approach to programming through the use of cash, a modality that has proven effective in supporting local markets. It also aims to promote household purchasing power and access to basic needs that are provided in local markets. By minimizing external procurement of assets, it provides opportunities for local market actors to interact and strengthen and stabilize the local demand and supply patterns. Markets across Somalia are responsive to increased demand. In designing this project, FAO considered the capacity of local markets to respond to increased demand. The geographic spread, timing and caseloads of the proposed cash interventions take into account pressures on local markets and their functionality. Further, FAO will continually monitor markets in Somalia through the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and the Cash Working Group for any significant issues related to market dynamics that would impact the efficacy of cash as a delivery modality.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

FAO mitigates the risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration and distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services by: (i) Carrying out beneficiary registration and service delivery at village level. (ii) Providing services to beneficiaries remotely through mobile phone, including cash transfers, information is disseminated through SMS and community focal points such as Nutrition Champions, and other community representatives in other complementary interventions. (iii) Using a consensus-based approach to beneficiary targeting and selection through a tripartite, that includes a committee of elders (representative of the local community), NGOs (ensuring compliance to FAO SOPs), and district authorities (ensuring the safety of the intervention and representing the government). (iv) Contracting NGOs and traders, authorized by local authorities to work in selected districts. FAO mitigates the risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, through: (i) Developing activities that do not require travel outside of the targeted villages. (ii) FAO’s Compliance, Complaints and Feedback (CoCO) system that enables two-way communication with communities, including confidential reporting on SEA. (iii) Toll-free hotline number (disseminated by bulk SMS before activities start, monitored through systematic surveys by FAO's Call Centre). (iv) Mandatory training to partners on PSEA and AAP. FAO mitigates the risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities through: (i) The above-described consensus-based approach to beneficiary selection and targeting. (ii) Systematic Call Centre surveys, which embed AAP questions, query the fairness of beneficiary selection and ensure at least 30% of beneficiaries are women. (iii) FAO’s Hotline, through which communities are able to report back. (iv) Mandatory training to partners on how to target and select beneficiaries against FAO criteria, AAP, Gender and PSEA, among other topics. (v) Provision of UCTs, to ensure other activities do not exclude people living with disability or illness, the elderly, among other minority groups. (vi) Mobile money and applications (e.g. voice recognition, voice messages) to provide more convenient, inclusive services to people who are less mobile, illiterate, etc. (vii) Field and Third Party Monitoring (TPM). FAO mitigates the risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, through: (i) FAO’s CoCO system (explained above). (ii) Call Centre systematic surveys, for a sample of beneficiaries, to receive feedback and complaints (iii) TPM and field monitors, who have direct interaction with beneficiaries and report feedback. (iv) Communication and sensitization sessions with communities, bulk text and voice SMS and radio campaigns to communicate FAO’s complaint and feedback mechanisms, and to update beneficiaries on their entitlements and other key information. FAO mitigates the risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries through: (i) Building consensus on the selection of villages and beneficiaries, through a tripartite approach (as above). (ii) District targeting by FAO, based on IPC data. (iii) Training partners on beneficiary selection criteria, AAP, PSEA and other mandatory topics. (iv) Mediation mechanisms, in place with district authorities, community representatives and government (federal and state). (v) TPM and FAO field monitors, who review and mediate cases of external interferences.
