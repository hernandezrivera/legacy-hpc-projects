Project Summary

The proposed project focus on identifying and addressing the needs of vulnerable groups of both HCs and IDPs in 10 communities in Idleb (Salqin, Dana, Qourqeena) through immediate and slightly longer-term interventions to respond to high vulnerability level
3 mobile teams will be established to conduct awareness-raising sessions around protection risks encountered by vulnerable
(CP, GBV, risks of ERWs, COVID-19, Services information, civil documentation/registration ) in addition to other topics, according to the development of the field context and observations of teams within societies
GAN will target 25000 individuals with awareness sessions activity.
GAN will provide a referral service and conduct protection monitoring to reach out to the most vulnerable populations and the most vulnerable areas
Each mobile team will consist of a child protection officer +Social worker.
While mobile teams are the safe entry point for the society they will identify vulnerable cases and record throughout the project, after verification of these cases GAN will provide monthly cash grants for 3 months to these cases to meet their needs with autonomy, dignity, and tackling economic poverty which will be reflected positively on the protection factors. This intervention is considered flexible and responsible programming to rapidly adapt to changes
Where poor households might resort to negative/harmful coping strategies, to reduce household expenditures, Covid-19 is further exacerbating economic vulnerability and associated risks.
The monthly value of cash for protection grant is $120 or equivalent TL (recommendations CWG) since this cash modality will give them flexibility in choosing they need and to give the project an ability to meet all changes in beneficiary needs, marginalized groups, minorities, and persons with specific needs will be better able to enjoy their rights and access basic goods and services with cash, 700 families(3500 individuals) out of 25000 the total number of beneficiaries, according to the following selection criteria:
Families:
-with disabled members
-with elderly members
-with children who joined armed groups to motivate them to leave
-that use child labor as an adaptation strategy
-with members affected by the remnants of war
-who cannot send their children to school due to economic poverty
-who resort to child marriage as a strategy to adapt to poverty
-economic debt, in order to thereby regain social credibility and trust in their communities
-headed by children and women
-Caregivers for unaccompanied or separated children
Mobile teams’ staff will be trained on the program’s objective eligibility criteria, distribution mechanism so they can assess the adequacy of the program to support each specific case.
All employees will attend an online training package developed by GBV SC in addition to all members of the project team will be trained on CSG, and PSEA, (Online)
GAN will identify the money transfer agents by conducting a competitive process, where the identified vendors will be responsible for delivering the cash amount to the beneficiaries in the distribution points and the payment for the agent will be conducted after finalizing the distribution of each few days which reduce the security risk in delivering the cash in such complex context,
The selected modality aims to use e-cards instead of paper, in order to reduce the number of papers that need to be signed by the financial department, the beneficiaries, and the suppliers.
Moreover, there is a risk of COVID-19 epidemic transmission during the exchange of papers if they were used.
Beneficiaries could access cash only through GAN’s e-voucher modality to facilitate and automate the distribution and strongly verify the beneficiaries’ IDs at the distribution points, GAN will also monitor the project and outputs on time through a web portal, fewer papers are used as the e-voucher system provides an electronic signature.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a protection crisis, Increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs have the potential to create long-lasting or irreversible consequences the longer they remain unmet.The vulnerable groups face a disproportionate impact of the crisis that continues to worsen due to economic deprivation, the COVID-19 pandemic, Women and girls continue to face discrimination and exclusion, economic insecurity, limitations of their freedoms, high levels of insecurity in almost all walks of life and are forced to rely on harmful coping mechanisms. Explosive ordnance contamination remains a major protection concern, severely affecting the lives and livelihoods of the population and further amplifying the social and economic crisis aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic.42 % of the communities reported the presence of at least one of the assessed eight protection issues as very commonly occurring 55% of communities indicated relying on community support, 50% on illegal activities, and 32 % on humanitarian assistance. Begging is prevalent in 24 % of communities.Early marriage is prevalent in 62 % of communities and child labour prevents school attendance in 67 % of communities.In communities where restrictions on freedom of movement were reported, the most frequently reported causes of restriction were: COVID-19 related measures (74 %).Financial and material incentives, as well as the need for protection, survival, status and lack of meaningful alternatives, remain the main drivers of recruitment.Explosive ordnance accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families. Economic support, as well as medical care, are key needs (HNO21)

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

GAN is a member of PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA, GBV), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available at distribution points. GAN recognizes the rights of all stakeholders, particularly the vulnerable, to access humanitarian programs without the risk of sexual exploitation or abuse. GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account. Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the distribution points. The distribution points will be inside the camps itself, during daylight hours, and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment
