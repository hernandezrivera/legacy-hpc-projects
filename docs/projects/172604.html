Project Summary

Operating in Libya remains challenging, with numerous security, logistical and access constraints. Thus, inclusive collaboration and coordination between national/international humanitarian, development and peace-building actors as well as Human Rights organisations are critical to ensure that assistance is provided timely, safely and in line with resilience, self-reliance and respect of people’s dignity. A large range of actors (humanitarian, development, peacebuilding, civil society, national and local authorities) are involved in the inter-agency and inter-sectoral approach and will beneficiate from the work of a dedicated Nexus Coordinator to assume strong and effective triple nexus coordination. Short-medium and long term interventions of all involved organizations will be coordinated towards collective outcomes in line with the HRP for more efficiency and effectiveness.
Inter sector coordination supports humanitarian actors with coordination, conflict sensitivity analysis and gap identification, information management, advocacy for access, safety and protection. It will benefit the entire humanitarian community to effectively and timely reach the most vulnerable people in Libya, as well as to sustain and extend their members’ operational reach and impact. The annual review of existing common coordination mechanisms and structures, includes the review of the sectors and the Inter-Sector Coordination Group, ensuring that the system is fit per purpose and able to deliver as expected.
Having a double-mandate for both humanitarian and resilience activities, WFP is ideally suited to work on the humanitarian-development-peace nexus (Triple Nexus), both within its own activities as well as in inter-agency cooperation. Nexus discussions for Libya started in 2019 and have been driven forward since then through WFP as the coordination agency for the newly created Nexus Working Group that brings together UN agencies, INGOs, donors, and local government to agree on a joint approach and seek for partnership opportunities to bridge the different pillars.
Systematic coordination with sister UN agencies such as FAO, IOM, UNDP, or UN Women, UN OCHA, RCO, International NGOs and other international humanitarian and livelihoods actors such as the world bank, as well as local and international peacebuilding actors (PCI and USIP), will be key for WFP to work with on a strong Nexus approach, to identify synergies, joint programming, share experience, best practices, and avoid any possible duplication of activities.

1 - Needs

Following the World Humanitarian Summit in 2016, the UN Agencies and the World Bank signed a Commitment to Action towards the adoption and implementation of the “New Way of Working” (NWoW). The objective is to meet the immediate needs of affected populations while reducing their exposure to risk and vulnerability as proposed in the Secretary General’s Report 'OneHumanity': 'shared responsibilities' and in the 'Agenda for Humanity' (Core Responsibility 4: ‘changing peoples’ life: from delivery aids to ending needs’). In line with the Commitment to Action, the UN Country Team in Libya has created a Programme Management Team (PMT), chaired by WFP, to initiate the work towards the NWoW and to develop a triple Nexus approach for Libya. In September, the UN Country Team agreed to create a PMT + Working Group (PMT+WG) to move forward with the design and the implementation of a Nexus approach in Libya. Sebha has been identified as a pilot location. As a part of key components for the over-arching Nexus strategy, WFP piloted conflict sensitivity analysis for inter-UN agency projects in Sebha and Sirte. Without a coordinator fully dedicated to ensuring the progress of the Nexus approach, there is a significant risk for all organisations and institutions operating in Libya not reaching their optimal level of coordination and efficiency, and their activities not leading to sustainable approaches. The risk for beneficiaries is to remain dependant of humanitarian relief without being part of a medium-term plan for resilience, self-reliance and social cohesion towards stabilisation and peace. The Nexus in Libya needs the motion to address the interconnected issues of the most in-need and at-risk groups, especially women and youth as well as migrants, on ground. Non-intervention at this stage could contribute to a recurrence of social conflicts, internal stability and communities’ dependency on external assistance. To be able to provide dedicated capacity to all actors (UN, INGOs, donor institutions, national/local authorities) and sectors, there is need to provide the organizations with enough human resources and support to avoid extra burden on the already tight structures.

2 - Activities

The proposal aims to provide adequate resources to all actors to enable them to implement their short, medium and longer term activities in a coordinated way and with a strong focus on conflict sensitivity to complement each other’s endeavours. Main activities include, among others: i) organize regular coordination meetings with the Nexus Working Group; ii) update a roadmap document of the Nexus process in Libya; iii) provide a mapping of all humanitarian, development and peace-building activities together in key location(s) identified by the Nexus Working Group, with a plan to operationalize a more coordinated approach; iv) work towards collective outcomes; v) organize a workshop in Tunis with all stakeholders to develop a joint-planning for key location(s) identified by the Nexus Working Group, as a response plan to the agreed collective outcomes with all stakeholders, and vi) facilitate Nexus dialogues with local authorities and national government and record key discussions and decisions.
