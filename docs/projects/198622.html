Project Summary

Across Nigeria, the advent of this year’s rainy season has led to heavy rainfall, rivers overflowing their banks, and the spillage of water from dams in neighbouring Cameroon, which have contributed to severe flooding in 31 out of the 36 Nigerian federal states. According to satellite analysis conducted by the United Nations Satellite Center (UNOSAT) between the 13-17th October, up to 6,600,000 people are potentially exposed to flooding and over 30,000 km2 of land has been flooded across the country. Adamawa State is among the most affected states with an estimated 260,000 people potentially exposed to floods and around a 1,000 km2 of land submerged across the state.
According to the State Emergency Management Agency (SEMA), flooding in the state is mainly a result of heavy rains starting around the 11th of July 2022 and still ongoing (as of 24th October 2022).
Additionally increased water flows from river sources in the Cameroonian highlands have contributed to the river Benue overflowing, leading to the flooding of several communities in Adamawa State. This was compounded by the spillage of water from the Lagdo dam in Cameroon which led to further displacement of people and the destruction of farmland, barns, and other property. According to SEMA, as of the 1st of September, 25 people were reportedly dead, 58 critically injured, 131,638 individuals were currently displaced, and 153 communities in 18 Local Government Areas (LGAs) were submerged. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, over 2.45 million People in Need (PiN) including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and host community populations live in Adamawa State.
Of that number, over 1.1 million are classified as being in either severe, extreme or catastrophic needs severity phases. With these flooding incidents occurring against a backdrop of a complex humanitarian crisis in parts of Adamawa State, this joint multisectoral Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) is intended to fill urgent information gaps on the effects of flooding on people in the affected areas, and to provide support to the humanitarian response, government and other key stakeholders.
The locations affected in Adamawa State are mostly Agricultural zone areas and are dependent upon for food productions. The flood crisis which has affected a lot large- and small-scale farmers and HHs will project increase in hunger, food insecurity and the inflation of food price. Hence many HHs will return to negative coping strategy if interventions are not provided.
The intervention proposes to provide farm inputs to vulnerable and displaced HHs from the recent flood crisis in Adamawa State. Hope and Rural Aid foundation will be targeting eight (8) affected LGAs in Adamawa State. These LGAs are Mayo-belwa, Michika, Demsa, Numan, Yola-South, Yola-North, Shellenge, and Madagali LGAs. HARAF is proposing to provide farm inputs and train 4,000 farmers (HHs), Five hundred HHs each per LGA. In the current period (March to May, 2022), food consumption across several states was under stress ; except in inaccessible LGAs and states with high incidences of insecurity namely Adamawa, Borno, Yobe, Kaduna, Sokoto and Niger where it is under crisis. During the projected period (June to August, 2022); food consumption is expected to deteriorate slightly due to reduced markets stocks and its resultant hike in staple food prices which often limits HHs capacity to acquire stocks and sustain access to quality and nutritious foods.

2 - Activities

1. Community Mapping and Community Based Participation: HARAF shall be conducting a community mapping to sample and identify all community structures, stakeholders, influential groups, women groups, Disability groups, men and youth groups etc. 2. Staff and expert recruitment/Training: HARAF shall recruit Staff with relevant experience to undertake the work, by advertising of positions, selection, interview and training of recruited staff on HARAF policies, PSEA, fraud, livelihood project orientation, communication etc. HARAF shall also Conduct training to all staff, volunteers and committees on mainstreaming protection and gender in all activities. HARAF shall be employing the following staff; Program manager, Livelihood coordinator, livelihood technical assistants-16 (seed production) for each LGA, Community mobilizer and engagement assistant, Protection officer, Compliant and feedback assistants (12), Admin officer, Procurement Officer, Human Resource Office, Finance officer, Storekeeper assistant, M&E Officer, M&E Assistant, Security officer and other supporting staff (Community Volunteers) and Experts. 3. Community and Stakeholders engagement and Inception meeting:Conduct inception meetings with the relevant Government ministries both at State and LGA level, the Community leadership, Community Members, and other actors to roll-out the project, its intended objectives, expected outcomes, activities and complementary support required from stakeholders.4. Setup and Training Community based committee (Targeting Committee and Project monitoring): HARAF will set-up beneficiary targeting committee amongst the community members (selected by them) to support in identifying the vulnerable HHs for the support. They will also be involved in spot check exercise. The Project Monitoring Committee (PMC) will be elected by community members and stakeholders, after which orientation and training will be conducted on humanitarian policies, beneficiaries’ selection process, and responsibilities of PMC etc. The PMC will facilitate monitoring of activities and ensure its localization. The PMC will participate in the monthly review meetings. 5. Beneficiary Targeting and grouping: Targeting and selection of 8,000 beneficiaries for the intervention. Spot check will be conducted with the Target Committee (TC). Beneficiary orientation will be conducted after the spot check and beneficiary registration disaggregated by age, gender etc. The orientation will center on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), Project orientation, PSEA, CFM etc.6. Beneficiary Training: Beneficiaries shall be trained on best seed practices, farming models, postharvest management etc. Additional value training would include financial literacy, business skills, book/ record keeping, equipment maintenance, nutrition and VSLA.7. Formation of Village Savings and Loan Association: Beneficiary shall be grouped into 25 per VSLA group. where thay shall be trained on the models of VSLA. The basic principle of the VSL system is that members of a self-selected group voluntarily form a Village Savings & Loan Association (VS&LA) and save money in the form of shares. The savings are invested in a Loan Fund from which members can borrow, repaying with a service charge added. The primary purpose of a VSLA is to provide simple savings and loan facilities, in a community that does not have access to formal financial services. Loans can also provide a form of self-insurance to members, supplemented by a Social Fund which provides small but important grants to members in distress.8. Distribution of Farm inputs (Seeds and Fertilizer) to beneficiaries. 9. Mentorship. After beneficiaries receives their inputs, HARAF shall provide beneficiaries with monitorship. This will provide an avenue for monitoring, strengthening of small businesses/skills, proper utilization of inputs, lessons learnt, and feedbacks. 10. Monitoring.11. Reporting.
