Project Summary

CAFOD and Caritas partners are targeting Gokwe South and Gokwe North, Mudzi, Uzumba-Maramba-Pfungwe, Mutoko, Binga, Hwange and Buhera. The project intends to drill and rehabilitate boreholes, construct and rehabilitate piped water schemes, provide participatory health and hygiene education incorporating COVID-19 messaging and provide essential hygiene kits. The aim is to increase access to safe water for 123,810 people, improving access to Hygiene kits and awareness of safe hygiene sanitation practices for 145,798 people.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The Caritas organisations have longstanding and permanent presence in the areas of operation and are therefore in continuous consultation, dialogue and assessment of the humanitarian and developmental needs of the targeted communities. Caritas ensures active community participation, working with relevant government stakeholders, local leadership & community committees such as Caritas committees, Ward Development Committees (WARDCOs) and with local volunteers with attention to participation of women and children. Specifically, the project will use three interrelated feedback mechanisms:Þ Community focal point: (third party person) to liaise and facilitate addressing all the types of complaints and feedback, the focal point person will be a community leader and forms part of the help desk team with project staff to receive, record and address complains or feedback from beneficiaries .Þ CAFOD and Caritas partner teams conduct regular meetings with beneficiaries and community leaders: to address the complaints and feedback regarding programming/ service deliveryÞ Complaint Office Desk or mobile hotline: specifically, for reporting sensitive complaints and feedback.The hotline number is shared to all stakeholders and communities.These mechanisms will be refined and adapted in consultation with communities and widely publicized through project structures and events. Communities will also monitor project works and activities and give feedback through their leadership and representatives.CAFOD will conduct refresher trainings for project staff on Safe, Accessible, Dignified and Inclusive (SADI) programming and Core Humanitarian Standards including accountability. PSEA policies extend to volunteers and contractors and are shared, including in local languages.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project may negatively impact on the environment through excavations and land clearing during piped water schemes construction and rehabilitation activities. This may be minimized through restricted excavation to infrastructure servitudes and will cause minimal impact on the environment. Damaged vegetation may be replenished with higher numbers of indigenous. Trees. The project will ensure that there is minimum harm to the environment. An Environmental Management Plan will be developed using the CAFOD’s Environmental Stewardship Tool. This tool assesses the potential environmental impact of different activities across the project and supports the development of mitigatory measures to both minimise environmental impact but also identify any areas where the project can enhance environmental health. These can be practises that curtail the degradation of surface water bodies and preserve water ecosystems, solid waste management systems, protection of catchment areas for water sources if need be. This will also point out social effects the project interventions may have on the surrounding people and mitigation measures be taken. The CAFOD’s Environmental Stewardship tool is comprehensive in that it not only scores the impact but also suggests mitigation measures accordingly. For pumping water, the project will not use fossil fuels and will use either hand pumps or renewable energy e.g. solar-powered piped water schemes. The project will ensure direct and active participation of relevant Government Departments including Environmental Management agency (EMA) within the project.
