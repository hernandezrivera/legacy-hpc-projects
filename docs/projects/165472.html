Project Summary

REACH, a joint initiative of IMPACT, ACTED and the United Nations Operational Satellite Applications Programme (UNOSAT), aims to facilitate evidence-based planning, technical advisory and coordination support for humanitarian actors in Syria by providing information management, technical advisory and coordination support to at least 225 humanitarian organizations. REACH will be leading on the implementation of project activities. More specifically, this will be done through the following activities:
• Producing Monthly Humanitarian Situation Overviews in Syria (HSOS)
• Emergency assessments on the humanitarian situation situation in Syria
• Cash and markets assessments in collaboration with CBR-TWG
• Camps and sites profiling and mapping
• Remote sensing to support IDP site identification and shelter counts
• Technical advisory to the humanitarian coordination structure, among other towards the successful implementation of the 2020 Syria MSNA
• Thematic assessments/specialised assessments to meet information gaps
REACH has designed this intervention based on feedback from humanitarian actors and seven years of experience in implementation in Syria. REACH maintains regular discussions with Whole of Syria coordination structures including the deputy regional humanitarian coordinator, OCHA, clusters/working groups, and the NES forum, and sits as well at the ICCG in Turkey and the ISWG in NES/Iraq. Additionally, REACH is part of the NGO Forum and the SIRF and also joins the SIRF Policy and Advocacy working group. These engagements enable a timely and efficient identification of information needs and gaps as well as opportunities for partnerships and collaboration. Activities included in this proposal have thus been designed based on these interactions. For example, conversations with the NES Forum, OCHA, SSWG and CCCM cluster have confirmed a general lack of information on camps and sites in northern Syria, which was flagged as of particular concern given the worsened emergency situation in both NWS and NES. REACH Camps and Sites sweeps conducted in 2019 in NWS also revealed a significant number of sites that were not yet registered by the CCCM cluster, which outlined an information gap and a need to support CCCM processes.
This project is already fully funded, but may require additional top-ups later in the year.
IMPACT is a leading Geneva based think-and-do-tank, created in Switzerland in 2010 on the initiative of ACTED and firstly operationalised in 2012. While autonomous organizations, IMPACT and ACTED operate together thanks to a formalised global partnership. Together, IMPACT, ACTED and UNOSAT set up the REACH program in order to promote and facilitate the development of information products that enhance the humanitarian community’s decision making and planning capacity. IMPACT is the lead-organization in REACH and hosts the REACH global team in its Geneva HQ. As sister organizations, IMPACT and ACTED coordinate on field implementation. In all field operations of the REACH program, ACTED provides operational support (security management, office space, cars, national HR recruitment, etc.), while IMPACT provides strategic and programmatic oversight through its global team and is in charge of the validation and dissemination of reports, and external relations

4 - Needs

The Whole of Syria humanitarian architecture has evolved since 2014, to foster and promote a better understanding of humanitarian needs and gaps, through an improved collective response throughout Syria. The REACH program has been engaging with this process extensively, working closely with OCHA, with hub and with Whole of Syria (WoS) cluster and working group leads, NGO fora, as well as with partners more directly, creating a strong understanding of information needs and priorities across all hubs responding to the Syrian crisis. Being present and engaged with actors across Lebanon, Turkey, Jordan, Iraq, and northeast and northwest Syria has been a critical component of understanding regional dynamics, as well as the capacity of gathering information among multiple stakeholders. Through this engagement, REACH has observed a greater climate for data sharing and improved understanding of the need for evidence-based programming, opening the way for collaboration and harmonisation of approaches. Nevertheless, a number of broad challenges remain across the Whole of Syria: (1.) the need for agile and quick data collection, cleaning and analysis systems to support emergency responses with timely and reliable information– as was seen in the recent Northeast Syria emergency response. Not only must such a system be context specific and tailored to the needs of emergency responders, it must also be coordinated at the different levels of the response – from the coordination level to the operational level to ensure greater coordination and avoid duplication. (2.) Data collection capacity and technical expertise to conduct large scale multisector assessments, paired with strong verification methods such as the use of satellite imagery. In both regards, REACH brings a flexible and reactive approach to both data collection and analytical processes to ensure that timely, useful and reliable data is provided to humanitarian actors.

5 - Implementation of activities

REACH has worked in Syria for six years, operates out of Jordan, Turkey, Iraq and Syria, with an enumerator network of over 140, based in opposition and Kurdish areas of Syria. REACH has strong relationships with different parts of the humanitarian coordination system, with all relevant NGO fora, and with other IM actors. Activities:• Monthly Humanitarian Situation Overviews (HSOS): multi-sectoral information on needs and vulnerabilities. Indicators were designed with OCHA, clusters/working groups, and other data collection partners.• Emergency assessments on the humanitarian situation in Syria including Rapid Needs Assessments designed with the coordination structure and an Emergency Needs Tracking system, tracking on 1-3-day basis, most urgent priority needs. • Cash and markets assessments in collaboration with CBR-TWG to inform humanitarian cash-based and livelihoods programming in Syria through in-depth assessments, thematic zoom-ins, and market-community network analyses.• Camps and sites profiling and mapping of both formal camps and informal IDP settlements/sites in partnership with CCCM and SSWG.• Remote sensing to support IDP site identification and shelter counts: through tasking of satellites to capture imagery and provide analysis. • Technical advisory to the humanitarian coordination structure and NGO fora, among other towards the successful implementation of the 2020 Syria MSNA, as well as emergency response support, gaps analysis, REACH dedicated staff to clusters/NGO fora, capacity building, etc.• Thematic assessments to meet information gaps: to be identified in coordination with OCHA, clusters and sector working groups, and other relevant coordination bodies such as the NES Forum.REACH aims for assessments to be used for humanitarian decision-making by a wide-ranging audience, from humanitarian leadership, clusters, operational NGOs as well as advocacy actors.Where access is limited, REACH collects information remotely or through partners

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In order to collect information that reflects different sub-groups within society, REACH enumerators are selected from a wide range of backgrounds and origins in Syria and are trained and monitored by experienced field staff. REACH aims to identify an equal number of male and female respondents, as well as male and female enumerators who are then subsequently trained on the methodology to understand the importance of reaching individuals from various demographic groups. Teams are trained to review questions for cultural and gender sensitivities, with specific questions administered to women by women only and to men by men only. Nevertheless, it remains highly challenging to assure an equal number of male and female respondents from within Syria due to cultural norms dictating that heads of households. Furthermore, indicators and questionnaires are developed to allow for disaggregation of all data per gender and vulnerable sub-sets in order to inform programming that addresses the needs of unaccompanied women and households with particularly vulnerable individuals such as young girls, pregnant or lactating women, and elderly and disabled persons. All new REACH tools are reviewed by the Protection Cluster (focusing in particular on GBV) and the GenderCap with the aim of ensuring the inclusion of indicators that allow for identification of vulnerabilities of specific groups. This also considers other vulnerable groups, such as the elderly and the differently-abled. For REACH enumerators and staff, a risk, apart from safety, is psychological, including the challenge in simply reporting needs without being able to help directly, the pressure from communities to have aid delivered to them. Counselling is therefore available in Arabic to all ACTED and REACH staff. In addition, female enumerators are at particular risk when moving between communities and their movements are therefore particularly closely monitored by team leaders and ACTED security.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Deterioration of security situation or encroachment by other actors - The security situation inside Syria is extremely volatile. Security incidents perpetrated by disparate actors active in these areas have occurred periodically and are likely to pose a threat to field staff. This could cause delay and may require redesign of some project activities. Staff safety is a number one priority for REACH who will switch to remote or partner data collection. Partners will be notified immediately in such case, proposing alternatives. Limitations imposed by authorities in Syria - The project depends on the willingness of local bodies to cooperate. REACH will seek close cooperation with the relevant bodies to gain access to target areas and acceptability of proposed interventions. While REACH will target areas where relevant bodies are willing to cooperate, unexpected changes in local dynamics may lead to delays and/or relocation of project activities. Should there be a need to halt operations, temporarily or permanently, areas of coverage can be redefined. REACH will also see to work in partnerships to ensure comprehensive coverage. Furthermore, assessments do not include sensitive, political or conflict-related questions. Armed groups demand involvement in the program and in operations - REACH abides strongly to the 2015 Joint Operating Principles and will not compromise the ability to operate according to humanitarian principles. Long term presence in targeted areas, local staff from the communities as well as provision of essential services will support ACTED to operate in an enabling environment. If pressure intensifies, ACTED and partners will seek collective action with other NGOs and OCHA, and potentially suspend activities in the area. Opposition from extremist groups towards humanitarian aid – this might lead to delays in implementation and/or relocation of activities. Minimized visibility and branding will reduce both the likelihood of this issue.
