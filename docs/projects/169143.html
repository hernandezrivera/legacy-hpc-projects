Project Summary

Overall objective: Provide boys and girls and caregivers at risk and survivors of violence, exploitation and abuse with access to improved and equitable prevention and response services.
The project aims to further build on on-going initial child protection and GBV responses following the explosions in Beirut and implemented by Tdh since the beginning of August. This project contains three main components: (1) MHPSS and PFA for children and caregivers, (2) Child Protection and GBV case management for children, girls and women and heightened risk and (3) awareness raising on child protection, GBV, PSEA and related topics.
This project will support children and adolescents and their caregivers, who experience shock and psychosocial distress following the Beirut Port explosions, to gain a sense of normalcy and to improve their well-being, through the provision of individual and small scale group MHPSS sessions (for children and adolescents from 12 years and above). Through the set-up and running of Child Friendly Spaces, girls and boys of different age groups (3-5/ 6-12 and 13-17 years) and caregivers benefit from community based recreational and psychosocial activities, as well as information sessions, while Women Friendly Spaces will support girls and women at risk or subject to gender-based violence with information and sensitization sessions, dignity/hygiene kits and community engagement in a safe environment. Furthermore, the project entails the immediate identification of children at risk of or subject to abuse, violence and exploitation, as well as girls and women at risk or exposed to GBV. Identified children and women at risk will be provided with case management support and referral to relevant services, including shelter, basic assistance, legal support and health, in order to mitigate and address identified child protection and GBV concerns. As part of child protection case management, Focused PSS sessions, providing emotional support to children and adolescents at medium and high risk based on inter-agency standardized curricula will be conducted tailored for children from 6-12 years and 13-18 years. In addition, the project aims to increase awareness on child protection, PSEA, and child safeguarding through messaging, information sessions, leaflets and other means of communication. Throughout the project monitoring and reporting of child protection concerns will be ongoing. Tdh will continue to contribute to inter-agency child protection assessments.
Tdh will work with mobile teams of trained, specialized staff, including child protection and GBV case workers, psychologists, and community mobilizers. The project will build on existing capacities and promote community engagement through the involvement of Child Protection/CFS volunteers and GBV outreach workers. The project focuses on the most marginalized girls and boys, including children facing disabilities. All proposed activities are interlinked and are implemented using an integrated approach. Tdh will closely work together with relevant government counterparts and other sectors and actively engages in inter-agency platforms to enhance coordination, following coordination tools in place. Tdh will target locations identified based on mapping of the most affected, priority areas: Marfaa, Remeil, Medawar and Bachoura in Beirut; Bourj Hammoud and Sinn El-Fil in Mount Lebanon governorate. Locations of the establishment of Child and Women Friendly Spaces will be determined in coordination with UNICEF and the respective municipalities. Throughout all its interventions, Tdh uses inter-agency agreed approaches to prevent the spread of COVID-19. The project is carried out in line with Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action and Inter-Agency GBV Guidelines and other core inter-agency standards.

1 - Needs

Multiple, recent rapid assessments and surveys show high numbers of children and their families have been disproportionally affected by the blast in Beirut on 4th August 2020. Many families in areas affected by the explosion were already facing extreme socio-economic and other vulnerabilities, which have been exacerbated following the blast, leading to high risks of violence, abuse and exploitation of girls and boys, including children facing child labor, street connected children, children facing disabilities and/ or gender-based violence (GBV). Also, children have been injured or have been separated from their caregivers (UASC). Children at heightened risk are in immediate need of identification, case management support and GBV interventions, to meet their most pressing needs and to mitigate and address abuse, violence and exploitation and other child protection concerns. Furthermore, high levels of psychosocial distress among children, as well as their caregivers are widely reported, affecting children’s well-being, safety and development, which can result in increased vulnerability and long-term harm. Children and their caregivers affected by the explosion are in need of Psychological First Aid (PFA) and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) to help them overcome the psychosocial impact of the blast, to build on their resiliency and to gain a sense of normalcy and safety. In addition, in order to prevent risks of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), increased awareness is needed on the risks and impact of SEA and on child safeguarding, through messaging, mass communication and information leaflets. Furthermore, Child Protection needs to be mainstreamed across all sectors, in order to ensure inclusion of children, including the most marginalized girls and boys, to enhance children’s protection and to avoid creating harm.

2 - Activities

1. Girls and boys, and caregivers (175 cases: 88 girls and 87 boys), affected by the explosion, who are at risk of abuse, violence and exploitation, are identified through on-going monitoring and are provided with adequate and immediate case management support and referrals, including for shelter, legal support, basic assistance, health and other relevant services through 8 trained case workers (mobile team). Small emergency case management funds are in place to respond to identified immediate needs on a case by case basis, complementary to case management interventions. 2. As part of case management, Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) are provided with support of alternative care, family tracing and reunification. 3. Girls and women (88 cases) who are subject to or at risks of GBV receive case management support through trained case workers (2). 4. Girls and women subject to or at risk of GBV receive dignity kits (500). 5. Coverage of Tdh hotline will be expanded to affected areas to enhance identification of children at risk and to monitor and track child protection concerns. 6. Continued MHPSS through individual and small scale group sessions provided by psychologists (3) for the most affected girls and boys and their siblings (1200 children: 200 girls 6-11 y.o/ 200 girls 12-17 y.o / 200 boys 6-11 y.o / 200 boys 12-17 y.o) and caregivers (400 caregivers: 200 women/ 200 men) as well to help them overcome shock and psychosocial distress. Children and their siblings who are engaged in MHPSS sessions are provided with PSS kits. 7. Focused PSS sessions will be provided for children at medium and high risk receiving case management and their siblings/ other relatives/ close neighbors (240 cases: 50 girls 6-11y.o/ 70 girls 12-17y.o / 50 boys 6-11 y.o / 70 boys 12-17 y.o) aiming to provide psychosocial and emotional support, using e.g. inter-agency standardized tools, including the “Emotional Wellbeing” PSS tool. Sessions are provided by trained FPSS Facilitators (2) 8. Structured, community based PSS activities for different age groups of children and adolescents (3-5/ 6-11 and 12-17 years) and information sessions for caregivers is provided to girls and boys (700 children; 350 girls/ 350 boys) and their caregivers at designated, established and equipped Child Friendly Spaces (4) and mobile services, through the engagement of community volunteers (16) and outreach workers (8), managed and supported by dedicated a CP Community Mobilizer (1). 9. Awareness raising, information sessions and community engagement is facilitated targeting 1000 girls/ women at a designated, established and equipped Women Friendly Space, run through community outreach workers (8) and supported by a dedicated GBV Community Mobilizer (1). 10. Awareness is increased on the prevention of SEA and child safeguarding through dissemination of leaflets and information sessions, including online sessions (total target: 1825: 500 girls/ 500 boys/ 425 women/ 400 men). 11. Throughout the emergency interventions, staff, volunteers and outreach workers are provided with staff care support. Providing ECA to 400 families, and link it with case management. In total, the project will reach 4355 direct beneficiaries. The number of indirect beneficiaries will be 13065
