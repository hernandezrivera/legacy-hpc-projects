Project Summary

Currently there is a serious humanitarian crisis in Afar region. Peoples are forced to flee their homes and are sheltering in deteriorated and unpredictable conditions. The main cause for the emergency displacement is the conflict that begun in Tigray on 4November 2020 and recently expanded into Afar and Amhara regional states. The violent conflict reached Afar on 16th July 2021 and since 20/01/2022 the conflect expanded to Megale, Abala, Erebti, Dalol, koneba and Berehale districts causing crisis for Afar pastoralist households who fled their homes and are living without basic emergency shelter and NFI items. There are over 300,000 individuals) displaced from these areas and thousands of people had to move to relatively safe areas in Afambo , Awra, Gulina, Yallo, Dubti, Kori, Afdera, Logia and Semera in need of protection.
Lack of Shelter and non-food Items are the main and critical problems of the displaced community. Relevant government structures are few and are lacking the capacity to emergency Shelter and NFI intervention assistance. The Shelter/NFI Cluster has prioritized , Awra, Gulina, Yallo, Dubti, Kori, Afdera, Logia IDP sites as one of the top priority sites for rapid response. Women and children are highly vulnerable and the most affected due to the current situation. The nature of the pastoralist community where their livelihoods is attached to rearing of animals, mobility and living in tense security situation currently aggravates the crisis. For instance, some HHs has moved to their generous relatives to get shelter and food assistance which might also negatively affect hosting communities on the contrary. The existing shelter conditions do not meet the minimum standards and are far away from being dignified and IDPs and host communities are in need of access to basic, safe and dignified shelters solutions. There is significant lack of shelter, with people suffering from harsh weather as it is very hot and windy, lack of privacy, increased protection and security concerns, decreased family cohesion, etc.
Furthermore, IDPs reported not having access to NFIs including cooking materials, clothing, tools, water storage containers, and hygiene items. IDPs also complained about having to sleep on the ground without any protection, which can lead to respiratory infections and other diseases, as well as risks for animal bites. IDPs urgently need NFIs, including shelter materials, blankets, dignity kits and cooking sets.
Welthungerhilfe and the implementing partner AISDA has been actively responding to emerging crises through implementing effective and timely emergency humanitarian response programs in various districts in Afar region which has brought its experience and reputation to an excellent level at this time. Welthungerhilfe and the implementing partner AISDA highly values the quality, effectiveness, and timeliness of all emergency humanitarian responses anywhere.
This strong presence, experience, knowledge about the areas and the already established collaboration with local community and authorities makes us in the right position to respond for this proposed ESNFI assistance at these targeted IDP sites.
The project will focus on procurement and delivery ES and NFI kits for 13738 IDP households to IDP sites and out of which 4000 IDP HHs and their 15% (600 IDP HHs) receive ESNF and ESNFI kit for disable (ESNFI V1) respectively. Similarly, out of the total IDP HHs, 7768 HHS and their 15% (1371 IDP HHs)receive NFI and NFI kit (NFI V2) for disable respectively at in target Areas/districts (Afambo, Awra, Berhale, Gulina, Dubti, Kori and yallo ) of Afar Region. ESNFIs and NFI kits disable are for persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness. In Total , this action will be reaching more than 75,000 HHs .

Implementing Partners

Action for Integrated Sustainable Development Association (AISDA ):Action for Integrated Sustainable Development Association (AISDA ): AISDA is an international award-winning local NGO (The 2012 Stars International Health Impact Award Winner of Africa, Asia and the Middle East) with deep roots in Afar regional state since 2006. It has 13 years of experience in cooperation with international NGOs and donors like WHH, GIZ and IRC and has experience in addressing humanitarian emergency needs, maternal and child health problems, implementing integrated development and humanitarian programs. The organization is one of the four 2014's Medallion Award Winners of Afar Region at the Pastoralist Day celebrated in early 2015 as a best NGO operating in Afar Region, and similarly received an achievement certificate from the Local Capacity Development program of the USAID on program and financial management, organizational transformation, sustainability and leadership with a score that enables the organization to access a direct fund from the USAID.
AISDA, since 2014, has a strong presence in all zones and 16 districts of Afar region including Abala, Erepti, Kuneba, Yalo , Gulina , Awash Fentale, Gelalo, Elidar, Mille, Afdera, , Awra, Dawe, Dalefagi and Telalak implementing both humanitarian and long term development projects which still exist at Elidar, Mille, Yalo and Gulina , Awra, Ewa, Chifra, Gelalo, Abala, Dalafagi districts.
AISDA has long experience working on emergency response at the centre of the communities to assess and respond timely to disasters before causing severe damage on the livelihoods of the people. AISDA responded to the emergency situation very fast in Afar Region since 2011 both in the areas of WaSH and Food security and livelihood. AISDA’s Emergency WaSH capacity was developed through the SIDA local partners WaSH capacity building program invested on it for three years. Recently , AISDA with a fund from WHH also has been implementing a Life-saving multi-sectoral Emergency Response project to the Conflict Affected IDPs in Megalle, Semurobi and Hadelela Districts of Afar Region that started on March 01, 2022 and will be completed on July 31/2022.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1: Conduct introductory meetings to share clear communication about the selection criteria and ensure that all stakeholders understand the process. The meetings will involve Woreda and Kebele representatives, the implementing partner and representatives of the affected communities (both IDPs and host communities). Groups with specific need that will be prioritized for assistance include girls and boys at risk, including unaccompanied and separated children, persons with serious health conditions, persons with special legal or physical protection needs, single women, women - headed households, older persons, persons with disabilities. This meeting will take place initially before listing any potential beneficiaries, transporting of items and providing assistant to the specific kebele. Activity 1.2: Beneficiary identification and registration will take place once a committee is established for registration of beneficiaries. The relevant parties from woreda DRMO, kebele officials and committee will work as a team, WHH project technical person and with the implementing partner -AISDA, to do the registration. This will reduce challenges during verification; however, independent verification will be conducted by WHH/AISDA. Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be incorporated into the selection process.Activity 1.3: The final verification will be done independently by the implementing partner. The verification will be carried out at the household. Groups with specific needs that will be prioritized for assistance, in accordance with the Shelter/NFI Cluster endorsed vulnerability criteria, include girls and boys at risk, including unaccompanied and separated children, persons with serious health conditions, persons with special legal or physical protection needs, single women, women-headed households, older persons, persons with disabilities. The verification process will also provide an opportunity to identify vulnerable households among the beneficiaries that require special attention to access and use project inputs, so that the team can appropriately plan for additional support accessing the distribution sites, safely transporting in-kind materials, and distribution to specific households by implementing partner-AISDA staff as needed.Activity 1.4: Procurement and Delivery ES and NFI kits for 13738 IDP households to IDP sites and out of which 4000 IDP HHs and their 15% (600 IDP HHs) receive ESNF and ESNFI kit for disable (ESNFI V1) respectively. Similarly, out of the total IDP HHs, 7768 HHS and their 15% (1371 IDP HHs)receive NFI and NFI kit (NFI V2) for disable respectively at in target Areas/districts (Afambo, Awra, Berhale, Gulina, Dubti, Kori and yallo ) of Afar Region. ESNFIs and NFI kits disable are for persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness. Activity 1.5: Distribution of ESNFI and NFI kits Kit for 13738 IDP households. 4600 ESNFI V1- i.e 4000 ESNFI and 600 ESNFI for Disable, and 9139 NFI V2 i.er 7768 NFI and 1371 NFI for disable will be distributed to a total of 13738 IDP households in Afambo, Awra, Berhale, Gulina , Dubti, Kori and yallo districts. During the distribution of ES and NFI , the implementing partner, AISDA, will provide COVID-19 awareness to all beneficiaries and will put measures in place to prevent the spread of COVID-19 including hand washing station for beneficiaries, social distancing in the waiting area and lines, and information. The distribution of emergency shelter and NFI kits will be at the same time and for the same households. Kits are in-line with ES/NFI Cluster standards.Activity 1.6 Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM). PDM on the ES/ NFI distribution and will be conducted two weeks after the distribution. Enumerators will be trained on how to collect data and conduct assessments using tools adapted to this project, and they will survey a sample of households in the area that received assistance.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

WHH and its partner will establish a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) together with the affected communities. The CRM includes various channels depending on the preferences of the affected communities, at least one of which is anonymous. Possibilities are for example 24/7 hotline, community help desks and suggestion boxes. As a pre-requisite for a functioning CRM WHH and its partners ensure that the affected communities will receive timely and clear information about the overall project and its activities, targeting and process as well as the behavior that they can expect from the project staff (e.g. Code of conduct). Moreover, WHH and its partners repeatedly and clearly explains the purpose of the CRM and how to access or use the system to the affected community, beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike. The various channels of the CRM remain available throughout the project. When a complaint is received, the CRM focal person processes the information and refers it according to set standards. Especial care for confidentiality and professional handling of complaints is given in case of sensitive cases, here WHH has a professional system in place at head office level. Depending on the matter of the complaint response is given in a set timeframe and feedback is given to the complainant or the community in total. In general, complaints or feedback are welcome by the WHH and its partners and seen as an opportunity to learn and improve the project and communication with the community, this culture is supported by the leadership and trough capacity development for the project team
