Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates that the number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 14.2 million people (48.7% male and 51.3% female), of which 6 million people are in acute need. This figure above on people of need includes 6.7 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); an estimated 320,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 438,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. It is estimated that more than 70% of the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria.
Overall, 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure (including 1.27 million severely food insecure and 9.5 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 42,378 crisis-affected households through support to vegetable and poultry production (equivalent to 254,268 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to enhance the resilience of the food insecure rural population in areas with high concentrations of returnees and IDPs. Project outputs include:
Output 1: Income generating capacities and access to alternative sources of revenue for crisis affected farmers households are increased.
It is expected that the project support will improve the food security and nutrition status of crisis-affected, small-scale farmers. Specifically, the project will aim to diversify diets through increased income and creation of employment for the most vulnerable household members, especially for women and youth, which will lead to improvement of livelihoods, increase local availability of food, increase self-reliance and improve food and nutrition security of crisis-affected households, especially the most vulnerable

4 - Needs

Rural livelihoods continue to be severely affected by the crisis. Widespread displacement, the massive destruction of productive assets, the severe disruption of input and output markets and the continuing impact of climate related shocks and crisis have severely tested the resilience and affected the livelihoods of rural households. Livelihood loss, unemployment, deepening poverty, inflation and steep depreciation of the Syrian pound have further eroded the capacities of families to meet basic needs and cope with the crisis. Household food security and the economic situation of the families remains dismal, with an estimated 9.8 million people in need of livelihoods or emergency employment, sustainable income generating activities, business revival and adequate financial transfer mechanisms. Agricultural production in Syria has also been hampered by a number of factors. Farmers (men and women) are facing a combination of decreasing availability and increased prices of key agricultural inputs. Furthermore, continuing population displacement and insecurity have created a shortage of labour, leading to an increase in daily wages beyond the financial means of many farmers. With so many adverse factors now stacked against them, an increasing number of farmers are forced to abandon their lands. Support to agriculture based livelihoods and cash support to the most vulnerable population (men and women) through support to income-generating activities, establishment and implementation of conditional cash transfers and labour-intensive schemes for the rehabilitation of damaged critical agricultural infrastructure in severely affected areas of the country will be key in strengthening the resilience of both IDPs and host community

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for examples IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees).2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place a clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation measures: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements in line with the do not harm principles. To the maximum possible extent, all measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted as a result of the being selected for the project. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well to limit possible frictions between the two groups, and special attention will be provided to the returnees to help them in their re-settlement and resuming their production activities through protecting and rehabilitating their assets. Moreover, the project will target those breeders who receive food support (about 15% of the total targeted breeders) in order to help them shift from receiving food support into producing their own food or able to afford it.
