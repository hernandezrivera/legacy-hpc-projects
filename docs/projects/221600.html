Project Summary

The project under WASH is targeting at total of 21,000 returnees and non-displaced people from Mueda and Palma districts affected by conflict. Under FSL the project is targeting a total of 124,997 people of being 46,512 for livelihoods and 78,485 for food assistance. These will be returnees and non displaced persons from Montepuez, Mueda, Muidumbe, Mocimboa da Praia, Nangade and Palma. The support will enhance emergency access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene promotion services and life saving through direct distribution of food and agriculture inputs. WASH activities will include drilling boreholes, rehabilitation of water points, water committee trainings, latrines construction and hygiene promotion.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project targets supporting returnees, displaced people and non-displaced people from Montepuez, Mueda, Muidumbe, Mocimboa da Praia, Nangade and Palma districts of Cabo Delgado in Mozambique affected by conflict. The assistance will be through emergency response for life saving, focusing on providing safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene promotion services, distribution of food and agriculture inputs. The project activities will include drilling boreholes, rehabilitation of water points, creation and training of water committees, latrines construction, and hygiene promotion, food kits distribution, distribution of agriculture tools and seeds and onsite training of farmers though farm field schools.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project pretends to implement emergency activities on water, sanitation and hygiene:a) Drilling of boreholes at communities with high deficits of water for emergency supply of safe and drinkable water: Establishment of water supply systems through boreholes with handpumps and solar-powered systems will be prioritized to ensure that vulnerable populations, both returnees and non-displaced populations population have access to sufficient quantity and quality water.b) Borehole/water supply systems maintenance/rehabilitation to support responding to emergency water deficits to returnees and non-displaced persons and families in Mueda and Palma districts: Rehabilitation of boreholes will also be undertaken. ForAfrika will support conflict-affected and vulnerable families with point-of-use water treatment and purification materials/products.c) Water committee establishment/training/re-training to support in responding to emergency water deficits to returnees and non-displaced persons and families in Mueda and Palma districts: Water committees will also be established for new water points and trained for the maintenance and repair of newly established boreholes. Existing water committees will also be targeted based on assessed training needs. d) Emergency Sanitation: Access to appropriate, safe, clean and reliable sanitation facilities, especially for conflict-affected populations, will be prioritized. Support needy households and communities to ensure access to emergency sanitation services, including providing concrete slabs to construct latrines. People with special needs and female-headed households will also be assisted in constructing latrines. Emergency communal latrines in transit centers and new resettlement sites will also be prioritized.e) Critical Hygiene promotion: Emergency hygiene promotion supporting information dissemination to influence change and actions and reduce the risk of preventable disease will be undertaken. The targeted population will be reached with sanitation messages delivered by ForAfrika technical personnel on a one-to-one approach and at the community level through megaphones and community leaders. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to vulnerable households – with women and girls prioritized with products that support their dignity and wellbeing.Emergency distribution of food: food kits will distributed to families at risk of starving and with no other means of survival priority will be given to women, children, elderly people with disabilities amongst other needy groups.Emergency distribution of agriculture inputs: seeds and tools will be distributed to families that have access to land and willing to engage into agriculture activities. Priority will be give to families led by women, families with people with special needs, families led by elderly people amongst other needy groups.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

ForAfrika will apply and promote protection mainstreaming principles in the design and implementation of this project. Given that this project is targeting vulnerable populations affected by conflict in Cabo Delgado, special care will be taken to ensure that the vulnerability of girls, boys, and women are not exacerbated in the provision of Emergency WASH services and emergency food security and livelihoods and this will include: i. Prioritizing safety for all beneficiaries and avoiding causing further harm to the IDPs, returnees and non-displaced populations and host communities, especially in the provision of WASH services, and food security and livelihoods e.g., gender segregation, access, lighting and privacy for sanitation facilities. ii. Encouraging meaningful access to interventions for all IDPs, returnees and non-displaced people in proportion to their need and without discrimination. ForAfrika will provide special attention to more vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities, elderly people or neglected and who usually have difficulty accessing the services.iii. Enhancing accountability by setting up an appropriate mechanism for the IDPs, returnees, and host communities to measure the adequacy of intervention and address the concerns and complaints. iv. Promoting the participation and empowerment of the IDPs, returnees and host communities by developing self-protection mechanisms and self-awareness within the beneficiaries to claim and defend their rights.v. ForAfrika will highlight PSEA to the target beneficiaries and take advantage of the existing community structures to provide more information on the project. i.e., Linha Fala Criança 116 or Linha Verde 1458 that are hotlines that are already under use within the humanitarian assistance in the country. Moreover, the project will conduct security risk assessments to identify potential risks that may harm personnel. The Social and Environmental Responsibility assessment will be conducted to assess any possible negative impact of the project. Through community consultations, possible sources of conflict and risk will be identified.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will strive to guarantee that project implementation is centered and anchored on addressing gender equality and empowerment of women and girls as well as people with disabilities and the elderly. This will be done by ensuring that IASC principles are enforced during the project planning and implementation. The project will meaningfully include crisis-affected women and girls, persons with diverse SOGIESC, and the organizations representing them in the coordination of decision-making processes for assessing, planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating humanitarian programming, as well as promote their inclusion in conflict prevention, resilience and peacebuilding efforts. Besides this, the project will strive to involve gender in humanitarian action working groups, particularly in the context of Cabo Delgado, which includes representation from UN entities, INGOs, LNGOs, clusters/sectors, and local government. The project will also safeguard that the existing working groups have clearly defined linkages with and reporting lines to the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) or Humanitarian CountryTeam (HCT) to guarantee a coherent integration of Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women and Girls throughout the crisis response plan action and funding; and to steer contextual analyses of the specific impact on the crisis-affected women, girls, men and boys in all their diversity caused by power imbalances, structural inequalities, and gender-based discrimination.Besides this, identifying potential beneficiaries and selecting vulnerable people will be conducted in consultation with community leadership. This will be conducted to ensure that everyone in need is appropriately identified and included. The project will use the Households Vulnerability Assessment Tool (HVAT) to assess vulnerability. The tool will enable the project to identify different household vulnerability situations and determine those that need to be targeted. ForAfrika will ensure that at least 50% of women are prioritized during beneficiary targeting for the project. Particular attention will be given to the community sensitization process, beneficiary verification, and community feedback mechanisms to influence the outcome. ForAfrika will consider five key issues on gender mainstreaming as follows: i. All project data will be disaggregated by sex, disability, and age to the minimum.ii. Project analysis during the project implementation will determine where women and men have had a fair share in the project.iii. Meaningful participation, engagement, and accountability of both women and men in the project will be promoted.iv. Project decision-makers and staff will have the right skills, knowledge, and commitment to make a lasting change for the targeted beneficiaries. They will consider the diverse vulnerability of the beneficiaries.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

ForAfrika will be guided by the IASC Guidelines on the inclusion of Persons with Disability in Humanitarian Action (2019) and UNICEF’s “WASH Disability Inclusion Practices,” which encourages organizations to promote meaningful participation, remove barriers, empower persons with disabilities, and disaggregate data for monitoring inclusion. Specifically, ForAfrika will:1. Implement organizational policies that promote non-discrimination and the rights of people with special needs to access WASH initiatives and their inclusion.2. Ensure feedback and complaints mechanisms are designed to guarantee accessibility for persons with disabilities.3. Train staff to include people with special needs. ForAfrika has so far identified 50 people living with disabilities (PLWD) in various resettlement sites in Montepuez, Muidumbe, Mueda, Mcimboa da Praia, Nangade and Palma districts. The community will be involved in planning and activities implementation and ensure that they are part of the main target for our WASH initiative. 4. Coordinate with other humanitarian actors to advocate and share experiences on disability inclusion.5. Safeguard that all data collected is disaggregated at a minimum by gender, age, and disability.6. Ensure that persons with a range of disabilities participate in key informant interviews, FGD, needs assessment and the entire project implementation cycle. 7. ForAfrika will understand the needs, capacities, and difficulties of persons with disabilities and their caregivers. Disability inclusion allows people with disabilities to take advantage of the benefits of the same health promotion and prevention activities experienced by people who do not have a disability. Inclusion should increase participation in socially expected life roles and activities, such as student, worker, friend, community member, patient, spouse, partner, or parent. There will be disability measurement in household surveys using Model Disability Survey to collect functioning and disability data at the population level. The tool will help to identify people with disabilities in consultation with the affected population and community leadership.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Environmental considerations will also be considered during the project's planning and implementation. Specific activities will include sensitization of community leaders and households on proper waste disposal and management; proper rehabilitation and decommissioning of wells, conducting hydrological surveys and assessments before drilling, carrying out advocacy on proper utilization of water resource management to avoid pollution and over extraction of water resources, proper disposal of damaged WASH infrastructure and materials that can cause environmental pollution and promotion of appropriate water conservation approaches. ForAfrika will also engage with beneficiaries regarding digging unplanned hand dugs in the resettlement sites to ensure the process is well managed. Environmental impact assessment (EIA) is a process that evaluates the potential environmental impacts of a proposed project and identifies mitigation measures to reduce those impacts. Mitigation measures are to minimize or avoid the effects identified.
