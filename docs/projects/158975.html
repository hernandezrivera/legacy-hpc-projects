Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the 2018-2020 HRP speciﬁc objective of WASH Sector: “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and suﬃcient WASH services with dignity for the underserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank.” Based on recent ACTED assessments and extensive consultations with key stakeholders (WASH Cluster, PWA, CMWU, MoDS), the project will target 200 vulnerable households (1,140 individuals) in Middle Area governorate. ACTED and CESVI have successfully covered the needs emerged in 2019 for Wadi al Salqa community through an ongoing project funded by AICS (Italian Agency for Development and Cooperation). Based on new assessed needs, ACTED, CESVI and Natouf are now proposing a similar intervention targeting vulnerable households in other communities of Middle Area governorate. The aim of this project is to restore access to domestic and drinking water for most vulnerable households of Middle Area (Gaza Strip), by ensuring their access to drinking water supply, increasing water storage capacity and improving their sanitation facilities (bathrooms and kitchens). This project will also raise awareness and build capacity on good hygiene practices. By improving access to drinking water, basic sanitation and hygiene awareness, this project will also prevent further deterioration of the households’ conditions of living, reduce the protection risks related to bad and inadequate WASH facilities such as exposing to public health and nutrition risks, adopting coping risking mechanisms (purchase of water from unreliable vendors or using open or semi-open spaces as toilets). The project will be implemented by ACTED in partnership with CESVI and Natouf. CESVI has been working in Palestine since 1994 and has developed a strong expertise in WASH (South Hebron Hills and ongoing project in Gaza in partnership with ACTED – adequate access to water in terms of both quantity and quality, introduction and testing of innovative technologies for water, sanitation and hygiene). Natouf has proven track experience in implementing WASH program as installing water desalination plants, distributing hygiene kits for households in rural areas, building capacities and raising awareness in WASH issues to achieve a positive impact on public health issues. The project design has been coordinated with relevant stakeholders, such as the WASH Cluster, WASH Focal Point for Middle Area (GVC), Palestinian Water Authority, Coastal Municipal Water Utility (CMWU), and the Ministry of Social Development. The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA) during the targeting selection (individual vulnerability-based assessment), the implementation methodology (support to reparations to HH’s WASH facilities unable to overcome the workload), the monitoring (respect of shelter and WaSH protection principles and minimum standards) and the accountability in the action (identification of arising difficulties through the establishment of a Complaint Response Mechanism). Gender principles will be mainstreamed during the action and Committees will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases with a particular focus given to women and children issues such as related to GBV. ACTED Protection Oﬃcer will be focal point of the Committees to receive and follow-up on cases. Through this intervention, ACTED, CESVI and Natouf have indeed proposed a project design that will simultaneously address the urgent WASH needs of vulnerable populations, while concurrently empowering members of the community to become knowledgeable in WASH infrastructure and good hygiene practices.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip faces a dire situation in terms of access to quality water, sanitation and hygiene services and 1.5 million of people are in need of humanitarian assistance in the WASH sector (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020). Due to decades of blockade, Israeli restrictions, succession of wars, existing WASH facilities have been severely affected and are non or partially operational. Due to shortage and inﬂation of fuel, households are reliant on private water trucking to access domestic and drinkable water; desalination and wastewater treatment plants cannot complete treatment cycles, thus respectively resulting in the delivery of undrinkable water and release of partially-treated wastes in the sea. As a consequence, over 96% of water extracted from Gaza aquiferis is unﬁt for human consumption (according to the World Health Organization standards), and only 10,5% of Palestinians in Gaza have access to safe drinking water through the public water network (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2019). In addition to WASH issues linked to access to safe water for domestic use, this crisis might exacerbate the dire economic situation in Gaza. Indeed, according to this same report, the residents in Gaza are well aware of the tap water unﬁtness. 90% of people rely on desalinated water, that mainly comes from private sector (81%). The containers and tanks bought by those households in Gaza are 10 to 30 times more expensive than piped water, stressing on already heavily indebted families. In order to identify and select vulnerable households eligible for the proposed intervention, ACTED and its partners followed different processes and relied on several sources. First, ACTED conducted a multisectorial assessment, published in May 2019, to address WASH, Shelter and Protection needs in the Gaza Strip. Through this assessment, ACTED assessed 143 HHs in Middle Area (only 6 in Wadi al Salqa community to avoid overlapping with the ongoing project). The outcomes of the assessment confirmed the global trends in terms of WASH needs (90% of the households assessed do not have a minimum of 3 water sources in the housing unit, 95% of the households have shown not to have a minimum of 1 toilet and shower/bathing space per housing unit, trapped and vented to the outside, and connected to public sewage system, or to private sewage disposal system and 69% do have a ﬂushing system working, with no mark of leakage with openable window, 63% of the households do not have ﬂoors tiled with non-slip tiles and shower walls tiled to minimum 160cm height. Half of the HHs assessed do not have access to a 1000L water tank with safe drinkable water.) In addition to this list, in September 2019, the Ministry of Social Development provided ACTED with an updated list of households living in hardship conditions. Moreover, in November 2019, ACTED will start implementing a shelter project in Gaza Strip funded under OCHA 2nd allocation of the Humanitarian Fund. Under this project, ACTED will conduct a new multisectorial assessment targeting vulnerable households in Middle Area. Through these assessment, and in coordination with the Ministry of Social Development, ACTED will update the available data and verify the needs in order to select the most vulnerable households in need to be targeted by this intervention. Finally, a CESVI WASH specialist carried out a ﬁeld technical assessment to identify the needs in Middle Area , at community level in term of water quality and sanitation hygiene. The majority of people rely on poor and unsafe cesspit tanks that cause untreated wastewater over ﬂoods which highly impact the health of children and other vulnerable people such as pregnant and lactating women, or elders. The poor water quality delivered to HHs members is highly at risk of waterborne diseases.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 - Establishment and capacity-building of WASH committees. 5 Community- Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPC) will be established. They will be involved throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee a better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups. CBWPCs will provide support in the distribution of hygiene kits, upgrade of basic water and sanitation facilities. ACTED will provide capacity-building to the committee members on WASH, SPHERE and protection standards. . Activity 2 - Provision of drinking water and installation of Household Water Treatment Storage. ACTED and CESVI will provide targeted households with Household Water Treatment and safe Storage (HWTS) unit with Gravity Driven Membrane (GDM) technology to minimize the risk of cross contamination before consumption. The design and selection on the HWTS is based on previous experience of CESVI in the West Bank. The technical performance and acceptance by users was assessed during 2018 in two projects, one funded by HIF and the second by UNICEF. Dedicated practical trainings will be carried out in the 200 identiﬁed HHs on the operation, correct use and maintenance of the module. The project team along with the CBWPCs' members will test the quality of water at the supplying point, treated water and at the point of use (at the cup). ACTED and Natouf will distribute 200L of potable water tanks to 200 HHs in order to ensure that every HH has enough storage capacity. ACTED will also distribute a water storage maintenance (incl. cleaning) and repair kit to the WASH committees. The kit will include a saw, glue, and spare piping and connectors, boots, gloves, chlorine tablets or granules, bleach, and scrubs. The CBWPCs will be trained on how to use the repair and maintenance kit and will be responsible for ensuring that the items are properly used and maintained in the long term. Activity 3- Rehabilitation and upgrade of sanitation facilities at households and neighborhoods’ level 200 HHs will receive conditional cash assistance to upgrade sanitation facilities (upgrade of kitchen and bathroom/toilets, connection of drinking water tanks to a sink equipped with water saving supply, and connection of their domestic water tank to the water network inside the house). Work implementation will be monitored through visits conducted by ACTED to each household to review progress made by beneﬁciaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel installments. Monitoring visits will also be used to identify any technical issues beneﬁciaries could be facing and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance. ACTED will provide drainage to 150 HHs in critical need of sewage drainage; pits the drainage will be carried twice a month during 9 months. At the project onset, ACTED team will start directly contracting sewage drainage service providers in order to provide immediate intervention. It is expected that 2000 6000 m3 of sewage will be drained/pumped to closed sewage network in coordination with authorities Activity 4 – Promotion of hygiene practices and protection principles. At the beginning, of the project, a KAP survey will be implemented by CESVI to assess WASH knowledge and practices within the 200 targeted households. The KAP’s outcomes will be used to adjust WASH promotion materials and topics of the awareness-raising sessions. CESVI will then implement awareness-raising sessions. In total, 4 members for each of the 200 HHs targeted by Activities 2 and 3 will beneﬁt from this campaign. At the end of the training, CESVI will implement a post-training test to measure progresses in terms of WASH knowledge. Speciﬁc sessions onhygiene promotion will target mothers, children and supported by the distribution of 200 hygienic kits, incl. both long-term and consumable items.

3 - Indicators

Indicators are selected based on clusters' indicators. In addition to those, ACTED and its partners will measure the following additional indicators: # of Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPCs) reinforced through a capacity-building training to scale-up their ability to provide WaSH services to their communities (Target: 5; Means of Verification: Terms of Reference (ToRs), Training report); % of female members of Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (Target: 40%; Means of Verification: CBWPCs ToR and list of members, Training attendance sheet); % of CBWPCs members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the capacity-building training (Target: 80: Means of Verification: Pre-Training Assessment (PTA), Post Training Evaluation (PTE)); # of potable water tanks (HWTS) distributed (Target: 200; Means of Verification: Activity report, PDM); # m3 of sewage drained/pumped to the closed sewage network (Target: (2000 6000); Means of Verification: (technical tool to be developed). Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. AME activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneﬁciaries and to measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups. Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneﬁciaries and non beneﬁciaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneﬁciaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP standards and ACTED’s SoPs. The mechanism will feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBWPC members, ACTED Protection Oﬃcer and independent monitoring staff from Natuf, to ensure that feedback is received from beneﬁciaries, and, when preferred, conﬁdentiality is ensured and respected. During the project, the Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPC) will be responsible for monitoring the works and ensuring that protection standards are upheld and concerns are addressed or referred to the ACTED Protection Oﬃcer. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring, identifying and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to ACTED as well as the relevant local service and maintenance providers, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, ACTED Protection Pﬃcer, with the support of the CBWPC, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process. In particular, ACTED Protection Oﬃcer and the committees will regularly visit all beneﬁciaries to ensure that protection concerns are being respected and addressed through the works. Were any lack to be identiﬁed, the Protection oﬃcer will offer guidance and complementary training to beneﬁciaries.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through ACTED’s Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including oPT. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. All beneficiaries of the action will be explained their right to provide complaints in confidentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance, and will provided with small pamphlets summarzing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, field paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms features several channels, including a dedicate hotline and frequent visits by ACTED Protection Oﬃcer to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. Engagement and acceptance of community members is key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of ACTED projects. Beneficiaries' satisfaction is continuously monitored by ACTED’s AME Unit, that regularly visits and interviews beneficiaries regarding ongoing activities. The AMEU will report any issues brought to its attention. ACTED has been implementing projects in the targeted governorates since 2010 and benefit thus from a high level of acceptance in local Gazan communities. The project will be implemented in compliance with ACTED security rules to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented if needed.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

A community-based protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level. 5 Community-based Protection Committees will be strengthened through a training in Protection and WASH. Their role throughout the whole project will that that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female-headed households, households with people with disabilities). Do No Harm: members of CBWPCs will receive refreshing training on protection and all beneﬁciaries will be informed on the correct usage of the hygiene kit received, in particular items which could expose beneﬁciaries to risks if misused. All team members involved in the project implementation will undergo prior extensive training in Protection and WASH Sphere standards to ensure they engage with the beneficiaries and take into account the specific needs of all household members. The Protection Officer, together with CBWPCs’ members, will conduct additional visits to ensure that the responses to specific needs, regarding gender, age and disability, are integrated and the needs of female members are prioritized in the rehabilitation plan. Inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanism such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Beneﬁciaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance (for example items to be included in the kits), and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. A self-help approach will be adopted to implement rehabilitation and improvement of sanitation facilities at HH level has recognized to have several added values as it assigns an active role to the beneficiary in carrying on rehabilitation works of their own house, thus increasing ownership and personal satisfaction, and tends to support economic recovery in Gaza Strip by involving the local market contractors which is finally more efficient in terms of costs.. Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneﬁciaries will be informed and sensitized by CBPWC members on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practices. CBWPCs will be responsible to refer to the project team any acutely vulnerable households that could have been neglected, or who would present significant vulnerabilities although not well fitting the criteria mentioned before for further investigations.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have different needs. CBWPCs will receive training on the speciﬁc needs of various groups, in order to ensure they factor them in throughout project implementation and that assistance can be adapted to beneﬁciaries’ needs. As women and girls in sanitation bear most of the burden of carrying, using and maintaining WASH facilities at the household level, their involvement is crucial to effective WASH and sanitation projects. Women also have the most responsibility for sanitation, hygiene, health of the home and the children. Moreover, inadequate facilities for sanitation and hygiene are harmful to the dignity of women and girls as they can put in place risky coping mechanism such as using semi-opened places as toilets. A strong protection and gender mainstreaming will be followed through the implementation of activities on different levels. Community-based WASH Protection Committees and ACTED Protection Oﬃcer will ensure that the rehabilitation work of the WASH facilities will include adequate lighting, internal lock and privacy fencing in order to ensure women safety, security and privacy inside their house. Women will be highly encouraged to be part of the Committees, follow all the implementation and join ACTED team during the ﬁeld visits to the households. During the awareness sessions ACTED, CESVI and Natouf team will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as a “women issues” In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actually engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. Speciﬁc sessions of hygiene promotion will target mothers and children. In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected population, ACTED is engaging households in the intervention design. Prior to the procurement of assistance, each household will be informed on the planned assistance in order to determine if needs have signiﬁcantly changed since the original needs assessment. If outstanding needs are identiﬁed through this process, the material assistance will be adapted to meet those needs, as long as it ﬁts within the budgeted amount for the location. Community-based WASH Protection Committees will represent communities’ interests throughout the implementation process. Indeed, they will ensure that the repair to be undertaken by the household is decided in collaboration with the household members (women and male) and is in line with their priorities and needs. They will also ensure that all protection and privacy concerns faced by the household due to the substandard WASH facilities (toilets and kitchens) are addressed in priority.
