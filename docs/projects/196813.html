Project Summary

Multipurpose cash transfers provide flexibility for beneficiaries to choose priorities among the needs. The project aims to provide cash support for 3,600 of the most vulnerable HHs "out-off camps" in NWS with a value of 100 USD per household per round for 6 rounds, based on the SMEB agreed by the Food Security, Cash Working Group for Syria. The number of rounds and transfer value aligns with the North Syria CWG guidelines on MPCA 2023. Furthermore, the selection of locations process for regular MPCA intervention will be based on coordination with the FSL cluster and gap analysis.
Also, the project aims to provide MPCA to 2,350 displaced HHs within camps NWS with a transfer value of 150 USD per HH as one-off distribution under emergency response to cover their basic needs based on the coverage of SMEB as determined by the CWG guidelines 2023. Also, IhsanRD is planning to reach 316 displaced HHs living in different locations/camps who may be affected by any incident such as (fire, flood, storms, etc) by the distribution of MPCA under rapid emergency response as one-off distribution with a transfer value of 120 USD for each affected HH where those 316 families will receive other services of education, protection, and WASH from the same fund which consider as comprehensice response. Using the SMEB as the basis for a transfer value and conducting market assessments to ensure these items are available in the targeted markets means that households are likely to spend the funds on the intended basic needs items, something which is monitored by PDMs.

3 - Needs

Syria faces a multitude of challenges that make it one of the world’s most complex humanitarian and protection emergencies. After eleven years of crisis during which the country has lost 42 places on the Human Development Index, most Syrians face a context of continuing humanitarian decline. The country still has the largest number of internally displaced people in the world: 6.8 million people, and the highest number of people in need since the beginning of the crisis. Humanitarian and economic indicators continue to deteriorate, basic services are collapsing, and an ongoing cholera outbreak and climatic shocks compound an already dire situation. As a result of these challenges, Today, over 1.8 million IDPs living in 1,421 last resort IDP sites in north-west Syria who need assistance in 2023, an increase from 1.7 million in 2022. Also, as the Turkish lira, which was adopted as an alternative to the Syrian pound in 2020 in north-west Syria, has devalued to its lowest level against the dollar at 18.574 TRY in November 2022. The devaluation is felt by communities who have witnessed additional increases in the prices of basic commodities, including food, water and fuel. In addition, a cholera outbreak was declared in September 2022. The outbreak is mainly attributed to the deteriorating socio-economic conditions and the water crisis in northern Syria, which triggered a significant increase in waterborne diseases across the country.

4 - Implementation of activities

 Needs and market assessment:IhsanRD will conduct a rapid Needs and Market assessment in the proposed locations to determine immediate unmet needs and the feasibility and appropriateness of using cash-based modalities, as well as ascertain the ability of markets to restock if demand increases. Contracting with the financial service provider and arranging beneficiary payments:IhsanRD uses a financial service provider (FSP) to distribute cash in NWS. This process of contracting with the FSP to select the service provider per project is carried out according to the total value of the fund’s transfer and the value of the transfer fee. Procurement and contracting of the voucher printing company:Beneficiaries will receive a ‘voucher’ to present at the money transfer agent to be redeemed with the allocated cash. Therefore, these vouchers should have a minimum level of security features, and the relevant procurement process should be followed based on the value of the voucher printing. Voucher distribution and redemptionBeneficiaries will receive their cards and then the BNFs will redeem the voucher at the contracted FSP under supervision of IhsanRD’s team. Arranging beneficiaries’ payments:Confirming the paid amount of exchanged vouchersand transferring money to the FSP.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA is at the core of IhsanRD responses, and all IhsanRD staff must comply with it. Effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to SEA acts by integrating PSEA in IhsanRD policies and ensuring that all personnel read and sign the code of conduct and that all contracts with local council/hawala agents include clauses on PSEA. IhsanRD also provides awareness training to all staff in the organization on PSEA principles.The proposed program is built taking fully into account: Do No Harm, Age, Gender, Diversity mainstreaming (AGDM), and commitment to providing dignity and equal access to assistance. The project has been designed based on the needs of all groups affected by the ongoing crisis in the target location through different channels: direct field observations of IhsanRD field team, local councils, and community feedback. Before starting the project, IhsanRD will carefully analyze and map all the stakeholders present in the targeted areas, their roles, influences, and potential impact on the intervention. This will allow the project team to set the necessary participatory mechanisms and decide which stakeholders need to be involved in the different implementation stages.
