Project Summary

According to FSNAU/ FEWSNET September 2020, acute food insecurity and malnutrition conditions exist in Somalia at alarming levels due to multiple shocks that negatively affected livelihood assets of the most vulnerable households. GU seasonal flooding and Desert locust upsurge contributed to loss and damage to property, housing, farmland and infrastructure. Also, social economic impacts occasioned by COVID 19 pandemic significantly contributed to food insecurity in Somalia. Social unrest in some parts of the country led to unprecedented levels of population displacement and disruption of livelihoods forcing hundreds of thousands to adopt negative coping mechanisms. Although livestock holding and milk availability is improving generally in Somalia, however, protracted droughts over the years combined with desert locust manifestation resulted drastic decline of livestock herd size holding among pastoral and agro pastoral households in Somalia leading to increased destitution and displacement in rural setups.
401, 136 people are in emergency (IPC 4) of which 31% are rural population, 8% urban, 61 IDPs and urban population, while 3,048,080 people are in crisis (IPC 3) of which 24% are non-displaced rural population, 51% urban population while 26% are IDPs/ urban/ rural population (FSNAU/FEWSNET December 2020). Projection estimates shows that over 3.5 million Somalis will be deprived to access essential food and non-food needs in 2021 if current food indicators persist. According to FSNAU post GU report released on 30th of September 2020, Galgadud and Lower Juba regions of Somalia has combined population of 6,000 people in in emergency (IPC4) from July to September 2020. Additional 7000 People are projected to be in (IPC 4) from October to December 2020. The report further revealed that an estimated 30,000 people in the same regions are in crisis (IPC 3), while putting projection figures at 39,000 from October 2020 to December 2020. Without sustained large scale humanitarian assistance worse food security outcomes
The proposed intervention seeks to respond to the food needs of the affected households in Galgaduud and Lower Juba regions of Somalia with essential livelihood packages such as cash, voucher, livestock treatment and feed in order to strengthen and protect the livelihood assets of the poor and most vulnerable households.HOD currently implementing livestock activities with FAO Galgaduud. HOD seeks to use integrated food security packages which will respond to both immediate and the long term needs of the affected population. The intervention will support 2,100 households with total population of 12,600 persons (Women 3110, men 1285, boys 3765, and girls 4340). The transfer value will be based on the current regional MEB. The proposed project is cost effective because 80% of the project budget will go to the project beneﬁciaries making the project cost effective. Accountability on cash tracking will be achieved through the use of vouchers which will contain the name and unique identification of each beneficiary. A competitively selected Money vendor will also be engaged to process payments for beneﬁciaries in order to reduce risk associated with the cash and to enhance accountability in the cash transfer process. The use of cash is informed by the availability of well-functioning market system. It also responds well to local needs during crisis. A post distribution assessment for the beneﬁciaries will be done to verify how much cash each received and the use they have put the cash to. A feedback mechanism/grievance mechanism will also be put in place for beneﬁciaries to reach out for any support. Frequent spot checks will be done to verify whether beneﬁciaries are receiving the right amount. Market data will be collected on regular basis to monitor cash impact on the market

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of cash instead of vouchers is justified by the existence of well functioning markets in project target areas. Cash has the potential to provide immediate response during crisis so it is effective during emergency. Use of cash is also justified by its cost effectiveness in terms of less logistical cost involved in its use. In kind is prone to misuse and fraud and therefore cash is safe and always transfer traceable. Use cash provides wider choices to its recipient unlike in kind which limits the choices and options available for the recipient.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The program prioritizes frequent market assessments and analysis to ascertain its functionality. key issues that will be constantly monitored include demand vs supply factors, commodity price trend, market inflation rates, cash circulation in the market. This help in not over pumping cash that can jeopardize the market and has the potential of causing unnecessary market inflation.

Mitigation Measures

1. Protection of civilians: i) Distribution points and routes shall be located in central areas that are known to be safe and have been cleared of any landmines, not near military installations or near conflict areas or in areas known to be infested with robbers. ii) The distribution will be done door to door as much as reasonably possible iii) All distribution will be done during daylight between 10.00 AM to 3.00 PM. iv) Route guides shall be stationed at various locations to safely guide the beneficiaries to the distribution points. v) The shortest route to the distribution point shall be used. 2. Exclusion and marginalization risks: i) Data about beneficiaries shall be segregated by age, gender, physical ability/disability and group identity (minority or dominant) ii) During invitation to community sensitization meetings, messages will be sent through door to door, social media and posters to youth groups, parents, community elders to invite boys and girls to the meetings. ii) During meetings an opinion of women, boys and girls will be sought before conclusions are made on a matter. Iv) Beneficiary registration will be done according to categories such as women, people beyond 60 years old, youth, physically disabled, pregnant and lactating women and minority clan. v) Those who are most likely to be marginalized or excluded in distributions such as women and those who do not have documentation will be made part of the project implementation team to champion the rights of such groups. vi) All stakeholders will be involved in the selection of beneficiaries so that no vulnerable and deserving beneficiary is left out and all who can benefit are informed of the details of the project. 3. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: i) All project workers will sign a code of conduct which prohibits interference in the beneficiary selection process, involvement in gender-based violence and sexual exploitation. ii) A stakeholder engagement plan will be prepared and all stakeholders will be invited for community sensitization meetings for the project. Selection of beneficiaries will be conducted after an agreed-on criteria by all stakeholders iii) At distribution points, female staff shall be in charge of women and girls’ queues iv) A feedback and grievance mechanism shall be set up to pick out issues arising from stakeholders v) Distribution teams shall be rotating among the various groups of stakeholders. vi). 4. Protection mainstreaming risks: i) Beneficiary lists and personal data shall be kept confidential and safe from unintended use. ii) The location of distribution points and access routes shall have enough space for orderly distribution and will be guarded by security staff to ensure the safety of beneficiaries. iii) Distribution will be done twice in a month and during the day. To minimize safety risk and to ensure beneficiaries do not face theft, intimidation, sexual assault, and extortion whilst taking their rations home, undercover escorts shall be deployed during distribution days. Avoiding community tensions between displaced and host communities by providing the local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of food, assets, and inputs.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed proposal is designed to respond to the most immediate needs of the poor and destitute food insecure households by stabilizing their livelihoods through conditional and unconditional transfer. The proposed project will strategize in the protection of livelihood assets of the targeted vulnerable households through livestock input and treatment to contribute to livestock protection resilience among the poor vulnerable and drought affected households in order to enhance access to food and nutrition. The project will target 18,000 goats and shots for treatment and provision of feeds in order to ensure their resilience against diseases and the effect of droughts.
