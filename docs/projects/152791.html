Needs

Assessments carried out in Jur River and Baggari Corridors between June - October 2018 indicate high levels of needs for WASH support services. In Baggari Corridor, communities live far away from the main centers and are cut off from safe and sufficient WASH services. There are very few water points from the main centers and where they exist, most have been destroyed during the conflict. The prevalence of water related diseases was reported to be high from testaments from respondents during focused group discussions held with respondents and key informants. Children are particularly affects with an increased number of children suffering from diarrhea diseases increasing their vulnerability to compounding effect of under-nutrition. There is need to increase and expand access to safe water and also help improve safe sanitation and hygiene awareness due to increase in water borne and water related diseases. Many families are currently using dirty water in pools thereby exposing themselves to diseases In Jur River, Many communities fleeing conflict in Wardalelo have sought refuge in a number of locations in Jur River where local host communities have welcomed and sheltered them, Access to safe water in Barwol , Kuajieno , RocRoch Dong is poor. The increased pressure for water as a result of new influx has placed a strain on existing water points. There is need to improve access to safe, accessible and equitable water supply by increasing number of water points but also supporting with rehabilitation and repair of existing ones. The community systems for maintenance of water systems will needs to be strengthened through formation of water management committees. The ability of the communities to contribute for repair and maintenance of water facilities has been hampered as a result of hard economic situation and compromised their abilities to pay and maintain them. Water committees where existent will be established. NCA will support with repair of the water points and also help to construct new ones to ensure that access to safe and sustainable water facilities in the most affected locations in both Jur River and Baggari. Additionally, hygiene promotion will be undertaken to help raise people knowledge to safe and dignified sanitation through analysis of risk and adopting protection sensitive measure to reduce SGBV related incidences associated with water and sanitation service provision.

Activities

NCA will provide timely and equitable and access to safe water through drilling of new water points and repair and rehabilitation of existing ones in areas where IDPs are settled in order to increase the number of water points and thus improve ease of access to safe water and also increase water quantity the at the household level. This will include the training of water pump mechanics, training water committees and supplying of spare parts will be undertaken in all areas. Additionally , NCA will respond effectively and timely response o newly displaced populations helping to provide quick emergency Water and Sanitation facilities to newly displaced populations in Baggari and Jur River and surrounding areas We will help to improvement of access to safe sanitation by helping to increase access to safe sanitation facilities at the household level by distributing latrines digging tools to household in affected communities . In doing so , we will ensure that the protection of women's gender rights are respected. NCA together with our partners will increase knowledge and awareness of appropriate Hygiene practices by carrying out hygiene promoters training and running hygiene promotion campaigns in Schools, households, in all the location targeted under this response. This will be undertaken by disseminating hygiene awareness messages to raise community’s awareness to the importance of safe water, sanitation. Hygiene promotion volunteers will be identified within the community and trained on basic messaging techniques.NCA will enable vulnerable, displaced and affected populations to practice safe and dignified gender appropriate sanitation by facilitating the construction of household latrines

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Before projects are started or implemented, communities are initially consulted to understand what their biggest problems and challenges are. We use participatory approaches to engage with community members ensuring all different groups and age groups are consulted. When we develop proposals and programmes and when funding is available, duration, source of funding and any conditions and expectations from donors and we go back to the communities be provide information about funding secured and what components are to be included. We encourage feedback from community. The way the project will be implemented will be discussed with the communities and the communities will be informed about their rights to complain if any aspects of the project does not meet their expectations. The manner about how these complains, and feedback can be provided to NCA will be discussed and agreed on and the preferred mechanisms put in place.NCA has already developed a Complaints Response mechanism at the country level which details how we provide information to with communities and receive feedback and complaints at the country and project level. Within the NCA CRM, we have established contact details of the focal points at the project level as well as at the Country and HQ level. Project level staff provide information to communities and beneficiaries where they explain the purpose of our programmes. This information is provided in a variety of ways. We carry out community dialogue where we exchange ideas and information and we also print brochures for the literate members. We are also considering to start using mass media in future. Most engagements are though social interactions through members in public meetings but also through key opinion leaders who also then pass e information to community members formally and informally.

The messages we provide are context specific and tailored to different age groups. During focused group discussions in Barwol in Jur River, when the community was asked what their biggest problem was, women, men, girls and boys had different needs. When we gathered then separately to discuss detailed and specific needs we were able to capture the unique needs of different interest groups. Assistance proposed and planned will be tailored to address the specific needs of the group. Disaggregated data based on age and gender will collected from the needs assessment and context specific response will be provided. During monitoring, data collected will be disagreed by Age, Gender and vulnerability. Our monitoring tools will be adapted to ensure that disaggregated data is collected ad also analyses during our monitoring. We will ensure that we document the lessons learnt in a way that distinguished the different groups as unique entities. The messages that we share with each group are there contextual to their specific needs and priorities.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Since NCA works with and through partners, right holders will either file a complaint with partner or directly to NCA. Partners shall be required to inform NCA country or field offices immediately a complaint is received from a rights holder. All complaints shall be received by CRM focal point in NCA Country Office in Juba, NCA field offices in Torit and Kuajok. Non-Sensitive complaints shall be filed and handled in Juba, Torit and Kuajok field offices while sensitive complaints shall be registered, filed and forwarded to the NCA Head Office in Juba. Sensitive complaints shall be handled by Country Director and/or an Investigation Team constituted by the CRM Committee. Suggestion boxes and or registers shall be placed at strategic points for use by rights holders and stakeholders in lodging complaints. E-mail addresses and telephone numbers shall be included in partners’ contracts and shall be clearly visible in community and within partner office premises for easy access by rights holders and stakeholders. Other methods identified or outlined by communities can also be established in cases where email and telephone communication systems are not available. Once complaints are received, they will be registered and handled in a timely and appropriate manner that protects dignity and safety of the complainant. NCA shall acknowledge receipt of the complaint within 24 hours. CRM focal point shall convene a CRM Committee meeting in each office to review the complaint and take appropriate action including recommendation to NCA Country office in Juba to carry out investigations. In dealing with sensitive complaints such as allegations of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and fraud and corruption, NCA CRM Committee shall refer to relevant sections of our Code of Conduct and institute an investigation in the matter. Findings of the investigations shall be forwarded to the CRM Committee who will then forward recommendations to Senior Management Team for further action and decision

NCA has a very deliberate policy of community engagement at every stage of our programme cycle ad this includes at the monitoring and evaluation stage. We try to ensure that in all these stages all members of the community are consulted to provide input and feedback on how the project components has affected them positively or negatively. Our monitoring processes are undertaken regularly with monthly and quarterly field monitoring. While our evaluation programmes are undertaken annually. We also make our complaints response and feedback mechanism feedback part and parcel of our programme monitoring frame work. We use participatory approaches and processes to ensure that the data collected has input from all sectors of the communities we serve and also ensure that every individual opinion counts. We use a variety of tools to ensure that we have effective and structured community engagement so that we can obtain information that will help us to get an accurate picture from women, girls, boys, displaced, hosts, returnees, elderly, disabled and other special interest groups but also representative. These include focused group discussions, Key informant interviews, and household surveys to engage community to provide us information on what is important to them and what they feel they need addressed or to provide feedback on how they feel our programme are going. Normal community meetings are also undertaken to engage community members during normal course of programme implementation. This helps to improve our engagement with community. Data collected during monitoring and evaluation sessions is also shared with community members, leaders and stakeholders as part of feedback and to also see how their input was reflected. Information we collect from our monitoring evaluation is analyzed to inform us on whether we are on track and if needed to carry out adjustments and for learning. We also use evaluation feedback for learning

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

When we develop our programme implementation plans we consider the time it takes to complete the most critical aspects of the project assuming that we will have reasonable access to sites and communities without hindrance. Sometimes , our visits need travel authorizations to allow us to visit those locations . We have developed benchmarks for turnaround times for obtaining Approval's from the various Government authorities. Any time an approval is not granted , this is recorded as an incident within our security management system. When our teams travel in the field and are refused to pass roadblocks to access our intended project areas, we also record this as an incident on the Simpson website . These tools help us to monitor access for beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

We carry out Gender audits as part of our ongoing programme planning and implementation. The gender audits helps to understand what risks our programmes are likely to bring about and how these affected Women, Children , elderly , disabled . For instance we interview women to finds out if NFIs makes them more vulnerable. We find out if choice location and time of distribution of NFIs makes them more vulnerable. When risks are identified , the project team working closely with community representatives sit down to identify how these issues of risk and safety can be mitigated by adapting the programme so that the implementation of the project or any part of the project does not contribute to more harm to he beneficiaries.
