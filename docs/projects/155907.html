Project Summary

AL Resala Foundation (RF), aim through the SCHF, to provide WASH services in 9 camps North west of Idleb in sub district of (Darkosh , Janudiyeh, Harim, Slaqin and Ma'arrat An Nu'man).
In the last week of April 2019, southern Idleb and northern Hama governorates saw a sharp escalation in hostilities, shelling, and airstrikes, preventing access to essential services and endangering the safety of those living in the area. Following these developments, 334,2941 people were forced to flee their homes between 1 May and 13 June 2019.
In august 2019, RF provides water trucking the targets IDPs in the 9 camps.
The project will target 747 families in the 9 camps, this project consists of 2 main components by continuity of providing water trucking, and provide WASH services package in the selected camps. The project will run for a six months’ period, the first month for preparation (staff recruitment, tendering and contracting). The project will focus on new IDP families
The project will target the IDP’s new arrived in the locations, (IDP’s 4,482) (1073; Women 717; Men 1,5705; Girls 1,122 ; Boys) in order to improve the safe, adequate and sustainable access for these service along with the improvement for the public health and environment to avoid any outbreaks caused by contamination of the drinking water and lack in the SWM which might cause an outbreak in leishmaniasis..
To respond to the most acute WASH needs identified during the assessment, RF considers relevant to continue water trucking activities in the localities where it already operates and where no alternatives exist yet In accordance with RF principles, water trucking must be envisaged as a last resort. The action has been designed in line with this principle and defines a clear exit strategy limiting water trucking to an emergency response phase. The provision of safe water via trucks will be implemented in support to longer term alternatives such as boreholes and water network rehabilitations – the latter falling within local stakeholders’ responsibility.
- Providing and distribution747 Hygiene kits for the new arrival IDPs families, Providing Hygiene promotion activities.
- WASH service for the IDPs (Dislodging of the sewage from septic tanks and pits for 4 months, Solid waste management for 4 months, Daily water trucking 108 cubic meter for five months and running, cleaning latrines units for 4 months.
- Construction 18 Bathrooms and toilets units (each units contains 6 toilets and 3 showers). Construction WASH infrastructure (pipe, manholes and septic tanks)
- Providing and installing 16 water storage tanks (3 m3), Providing water treatment services. Providing and installing 18 solar power for lighting units, Providing and installing 18 galvanized water tanks (2 m3) for washing Bathrooms and toilets units
- Focus group discussion (FGD) eight
- Monitoring and Evaluation by RF staff
This project aims to support the coordination with the WASH actors and local councils and camp managers through monthly and weekly meetings to review the WASH needs and to develop an integrated WASH response. However, monthly WASH cluster meetings will be attended by RF

Needs

Displaced persons in last resort sites continue to be among the most vulnerable people in Syria and, having exhausted all other options, are dependent to a high degree upon humanitarian assistance and services to meet their daily needs.23 Children and women make up 73 per cent of camp populations in north-west Syria. Idleb governorate as well as surrounding contested areas have become extremely crowded amidst the significant displacement to and within those areas. Increased pressure on already overstretched WASH services has resulted in increased dependency on alternative and often unsafe water sources that significantly increase the risk of waterborne diseases. In addition, the continuous drilling of wells in search for water has negatively affected groundwater resources, with a significant depletion of water aquifers observed especially around IDP sites near the Turkish border. The extremely high levels of displacement combined with increase in the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites has presented significant challenges to WASH sector partners, in view of the increased cost and complexity to deliver in IDP site settings than in host communities. Cross-border and cross-line access are of particular importance for the WASH sector, especially for people with acute WASH needs as this type of programming constitutes the bulk of the WASH emergency response. Between 1 May and 13 June, an estimated 258,0771 people moved to Dana sub-district and 13 other neighbouring sub-districts in northern Idleb governorate, a trend that continues from a previous inter-cluster rapid needs assessment (RNA) conducted by REACH in May 2019. Following this large wave of people arriving to northern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates within the past several months, this factsheet provides an overview of current needs, gaps, and priorities in the area to inform a rapid humanitarian response.As the potential locations for displacement are becoming increasingly limited, the high number of people arriving into already densely-populated areas of north-west Syria has further compounded the already precarious humanitarian situation in the region. This has led to a growing strain on resources, further exacerbating people's vulnerabilities and likely eroding resilience. Many humanitarian organisations have recently increased their activities and support to people in the region through the provision of life-saving assistance, but have voiced concern that financially this is not a sustainable solution.2 Without urgently needed funding, the already challenging living conditions of IDPs and residents may significantly worsen in the near future, according to KIs, people became sick after drinking potable water (reported in 8% of assessed communities), while others perceived the water to taste bad. Certain healthcare concerns are likely to be affected by poor sanitation practices, especially in cases whereby a large amount of garbage in the streets is likely to encourage a larger number of rats and pests contaminating food or people.Based on the cluster rapid needs assessment up to 55 per cent of surveyed households are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. Protection Risk Analysis

Risk Solid Waste Management (SWM)- Movements of garbage trucks in congested, camp like settings; Reliability of services- Potential exposure to explosive hazard (UXO/ERW) in case of support to municipal solid waste removal Mitigating Measure- Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding SWM services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Provide household level solid waste containers/plastic bags to ease disposal of waste from H/H to the public SW collection points. Establish clear routes and schedule for garbage trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; - Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators; Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area; Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators; Add Risk Emergency water supplyQuality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards Mitigating Measure - Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils.- Ensure and comply with minimum humanitarian standard (SPHERE) at 15 litre/person/day and establish water quantity tracking & monitoring system involving beneficiaries;

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

uring the project implementation all the targeted beneficiaries will be ensured equal access to the provided assistance. They will be duly informed about the dates and locations where the inputs of the WASH services will be distributed and the technical support provided. At the distribution site the necessary arrangements for each specific targeted group will be ensured (elderly, women etc.). In case some of the beneficiaries cannot access the services due to their extreme vulnerability alternative ways of providing the services. Throughout the project implementation Do No Harm will be a guiding principle for the project teams. For this purpose, they will be trained on humanitarian principle, human rights and other approaches that will ensure the successful implementation of the planned activities. In case any protection incidents occur during the implementation process, they will be duly reported to stakeholders.A focus on building partnerships with communities, beyond the direct provision of aid inputs, is a guiding principle in all work RF do. RF implements a framework for Accountability to Affected Populations that prioritizes leadership/governance; transparency; feedback and complaints; participation of communities and beneficiaries; and a design, monitoring and evaluation approach that engages affected populations and feeds learning back into the organization. Do No Harm framework that IRF adopt holds the safety of staff and beneficiaries, as well as accountability to beneficiaries, as key operating principles. RF will promote the dignity, agency, and safety of beneficiaries at all times. Protection considerations – mitigating the risk of harm, abuse, discrimination, exploitation, and neglect of beneficiaries – will be overriding components of all activities as well as a specific objective of some activities, which will be implemented in line with SPHERE guidelines and other international standards.Beneficiaries selection is based on a community mobilization mechanism where the criteria of selection shared with all stakeholders and the project advertisements will be published in the community. After conducting introductory workshops for community to explain the project outputs. Also, the project will complement WASH services input activities that are targeting the same intervention areas. RF has a community feedback mechanism to receive all complains from local community. Giving the vulnerable households the ability to resilience will increase the opportunities of children to learn. RF through the field team will advocate to this among the targeted beneficiaries and further data will be collected in the endline to have indicators. Also, through the coordination with protection department the project will advocate and implement awareness raising on child protection, including child recruitment.
