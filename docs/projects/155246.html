Needs

Since 2014, Haiti has been regularly hit by the "El Niño" phenomenon characterized by repeated episodes of drought accompanied by a poor distribution of rain. Currently, the situation does not seem to be improving because according to the latest IPC analysis (CNSA, October 2018), a prolonged drought hit again several communes of Haiti and strongly affected crops of the spring 2018 agricultural season, a season that contributes over 60% of national agricultural production. Therefore, 98,251 people in the departments of Grande Anse (197,358 people), South (170,705), West (14,415), North (319,256) and Northeast (150,415) are in acute food insecurity. The weather outlook indicates a strong likelihood of strengthening this "El Niño" phenomenon for the next year. The objective of this project is to restore the food security of 60,000 agricultural households (300,000 people) affected by drought in the departments of Grande Anse, South, West, North and Northeast through the revival of their agricultural production and the improvement of their resilience to climate change. Four strategies will be used by FAO to recapitalizes family farmer households: (1) Assistance in agricultural inputs to affected farmers to revive their production (seeds, planting material and tools). (2) Improvement of the availability and accessibility of quality seeds and planting material of climate-resilient crops/varieties in local markets. (3) Improvement of food diversification and nutrition by promoting fruit and vegetable crops and training communities in nutrition education. (4) Improvement of agricultural practices resilient to climatic change as well as community savings and credits for targeted vulnerable households. For assistance in agricultural inputs, two strategies will be used: (1) in the communes where inputs are available, agricultural inputs fairs will be organized and vulnerable household will be allocated with subsidized vouchers and (2) in communes where agricultural inputs are not available; FAO will purchase and distribute tools, quality seeds and planting material to vulnerable households. To improve the availability and accessibility of quality seeds in the local markets of affected communes, FAO will strengthen the operational capacity of Groups of Seed Producers (6 Groups/Department) on the production and conservation of Quality Declared Seeds (QDS) and planting material of climate-resilient crops/varieties through trainings and improvement of their packaging and storage system for seed security.To diversify the family diet, groups composed largely of vulnerable women (more than 60%) will receive vegetable seeds and fruit seedlings and will be technically supported in vegetable and fruit production; especially for the feeding of their children under 5 years old. Nutrition education trainings will accompany this activity to enable vulnerable households to prepare nutritionally balanced foods using local foods.In addition to agricultural inputs, the project will provide training sessions for leaders of Community Based Organizations (CBO) good agricultural practices to improve the resilience rural communities to climate change and related extreme weather events that more and more affect Haiti on a regular basis.Finally, to diversify the sources of income, heads of households (more than 60% of women) grouped into associations will receive technical support for the initiation and effective management of village savings and loan associations.

Activities or outputs

Activities or outputs  120,000 vulnerable households have access to seeds and planting material of climate-resilient species and varieties; At least 60 Groups of Seeds Producers are trained in production, packaging and conservation of quality seeds. At least 4,000 members of these Groups receive quality seeds and planting materiel to multiply;  20,000 vulnerable households (60% of women), grouped in associations, receive vegetable seeds and fruit seedlings and each of them produce at least 480 kg of vegetable during 3 agricultural seasons; 1200 leaders of CBOs trained on nutritional education, food and water hygiene and food preparation. They share the knowledge gained with at least 3,000 neighboring families; 800 leaders of CBOs are trained on good agricultural practices resilient to climate change. They share the knowledge gained with at least 2,000 neighboring families; 100 village savings and loan associations are initiated and technically supported.
