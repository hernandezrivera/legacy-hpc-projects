Project Summary

The emergency nutrition response is aimed at reducing the mortality and morbidity linked to life-threatening forms of malnutrition in Konye, Mbonge and ekondo titi Health districts in the South West Regions. This will support the nutrition cluster HRP 2023 strategy and response framework hence creating a significant impact on the nutrition response plan for 2023.
Caritas Kumba will undertake both passive and exhaustive screening of children under-5 for acute malnutrition, appropriate treatment of uncomplicated SAM cases and referral of complicated SAM cases. Also the provision of cash to cover for the transportation expenses of caregivers seeking “inpatient” treatment of SAM treatment will be provided. Family MUAC approach will be scaled up to enhance early identification of SAM and MAM cases. Preventive actions that will include promotion of infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E), deworming prophylaxis and micronutrient supplementation - with the aim of addressing micronutrient deficiencies shall be incorporated. For 2023, Caritas shall improve on the equitable access of iron/folic acid supplementation and deworming prophylaxis by pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers - with special attention to pregnant adolescent girls.
To reinforce partners and the community’s capacity to deliver basic nutrition services to affected communities, the need for refresher courses to revisit the IMAM protocol by Trainers of Trainees (ToT) is necessary. Caritas Kumba will continuously liaise with the Regional delegation of Public health in implementing appropriate nutrition interventions without affecting health system. To enhance collaboration, endeavors to participate and support nutrition cluster activities and enhance multisectoral collaboration with other nutrition sensitive clusters (Health, Food Security, WASH and protection) to realize optimal nutritional outcomes shall be emphasized .
The “do no harm” principle shall be implemented through the training of all staff on core humanitarian principles mainstream aspects related to gender, protection centrality, age, gender, disability and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP). Interventions will also be adapted in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic.
The outstanding performance in collaboration with UNICEF over the years has given Caritas Kumba a high level of access and acceptance within Mbonge, Konye and Ekondo titi due to her over 3 years experience in the implementation of General Food distribution as well as WASH and Child Protection Interventions, within these communities. Intervention will use static health structures for inpatient care, rapid response mechanism (RRM) and comprehensive child response (CCR) approaches to access the affected population both in accessible and those in hard to reach remote areas.
The target groups will include boys and girls (irrespective of their disabilities/abilities) under-five for screening, referral, children with SAM, Vitamin A supplementation and deworming prophylaxis. Adolescent, pregnant and breastfeeding will be targeted for the iron and folic acid and deworming prophylaxis. Health workers (Male and Female) aged over 18 years will be targeted for training on SAM management (mobilize, screen and refer acutely malnourished) and IYCF-E.
Konye, Mbonge and Ekondo Titi have recorded very low access to health care services with most caregivers and mothers resorting to the use of traditional methods in managing malnutrition. It is extremely important to manage this diseases at the community level since the hospital of these vulnerable communities are faraway from their homes and the highly impoverish communities lacks the financial capacity to pay hospital bills.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The armed conflict in the North-West and South-West regions (NWSW) remains worrisome, with women and children most vulnerably exposed inter-alia to violence, abuse and exploitation leading to continuous displacement. According to OCHA, in April 2022, several roadblocks and ghost towns obstructed humanitarian access, preventing the delivery of aid to affected people. Populations living in remote areas were more vulnerable to malnutrition since they are exposed to aggravating factors of food insecurity, WASH and health. According to OCHA on the humanitarian needs overview (April, 2022), it is estimated that more than 263,000 people were in need of nutrition services in the North West and South-West regions. The situation is estimated to may have worsen due to suspension of activities and low coverage as a result of limited funds recorded in 2022. Despite the prevalence of food insecurity, only 387 children including 207 girls were identified with SAM and referred for treatment. This represents just 5.0 per cent of 8,000 estimated SAM cases targeted for 2021. This is largely due to the low coverage with less than 20% funds made available for nutrition activities and poor access to communities in need of nutritional assistance. In 2022, the nutrition situation is unclear due to the absence of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey using SMART methodology. The coverage has tremendously reduced with very limited funding and subsequent suspension of nutrition activities in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon. With the foreseen on the deterioration of nutrition situation, as anticipated by the nutrition cluster, there is an urgent need to an emergency nutrition intervention in Konye, Mbonge and Ekondo titi health districts. considering that the major drivers of malnutrition are likely to remain in the same trend in 2023 due to the prevailing crises, the nutrition situation will deteriorate faster than predicted. In 2022, Up to 18,800 boys and girls under 5 are likely to develop acute malnutrition in the two regions including 3,800 with wasting. Konye, Mbonge and Ekondo titi Health Districts in Meme and Ndian Divisions in the South west region of Cameroon is one of the main areas highly affected by the crises with numerous displacement of inhabitants. The communities however, live under precarious conditions with severe low level of food security and poor access to healthcare facilities. The cholera outbreak reported and the overarching pressure of COVID 19 as well as the prevailing crises has contributed a great deal to increased the likelihood of acute malnutrition among children. Base on the strength of the forgoing, there is a high risk of acute malnutrition in these communities and hence a probable surge in the rate of morbidity and mortality for infants under 5 years if the situation is not arrested in good time. The emergency nutrition intervention in Mbonge, Konye and Ekondo titi health district is aimed at reducing the mortality and morbidity linked to life-threatening forms of malnutrition.The following needs shall be addressed through the activities listed below; - Active Screening to Identify Malnourished Children: 35000 beneficiaries to be reached. These will be done with the use of 75 Trained Community health Workers and Caritas Nutrition team and 1500 caregivers within the Family MUAC program hence widen the scope of the screening. - Sensitisation of IYCF-E: 20000 caregivers shall be sensitized on the importance of Infant and Young Child feeding Practice at emergencies with the use of counseling cards - Treatment and referral of 100 SAM cases in the Areas of intervention with the use of RUTF at a 75% recovery rate. Malnourished children without complication shall be treated at the OTP while complicated Children shall be referred to the IPF; St. John Hospital Fiango Kumba as well as the district health centers.
