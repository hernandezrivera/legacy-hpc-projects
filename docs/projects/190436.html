Project Summary

In the first three months of 2021 the number of people in need of urgent humanitarian assistance in Ethiopia increased from 19 to 23.5 million, compared to the end of 2020 (HNO).
Conflicts, desert locust invasion, cyclical climatic shocks including floods and droughts, as well as the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 are the key triggers of increasing humanitarian needs. Ethiopia ranked second in the International Crisis Group’s list of conflicts to monitor in 2021and 18th out of 191 countries for its exposure to hazards, vulnerability, and lack of coping capacity (2021 INFORM).
The conflict that erupted on 04 November 2020 in the Tigray region in Ethiopia has led to immediate and large-scale internal displacement. Joint humanitarian needs assessments conducted by government and humanitarian actors on 20 December 2020 in the region reporteda dire humanitarian context with high humanitarian needs, poor access to services and limited livelihoods. Meanwhile, the Tigray Emergency Coordination Center (ECC) on their 1st meeting on 1st January 2021 highlighted that more than 4.5 million people in the region are in need of humanitarian assistance, including 2.2 million IDPs (Ethiopia Humanitarian Bulletin, 10 January 2021). According to the information from Tigray region BOLSA, there are 85,510 IDPs in Mekelle and Shire alone. Reports also indicate that children are currently out of school due to disruption of school services and insecurity, and protection concerns abound affecting children, women and girls, including alleged reports of gender-based violence. Imagine1day, World Vision and ICRC have distributed core relief items including mattresses and blanket to IDPs sheltered in different schools in Mekelle, but were only able to address 1 per cent of the need (OCHA, 14 Jan 2021).
