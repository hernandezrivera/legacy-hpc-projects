Project Summary

Conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. Children in Somalia continue to face acute humanitarian needs due to conflict and environmental hazards such as drought, conflict and flooding. COVID-19 pandemic has compounded the challenges exacerbating vulnerability of the crisis-affected populations across the country. The crisis in Somalia has negatively impacted the health system and most affected are the vulnerable pregnant and lactating women, girls and boys, including children with disabilities with no access to health and nutrition services. Fear of conflict, insecurity and heightened tensions have triggered massive displacement in Guri Ceel, Galgaduud region over the last few days. The country is forecast to experience its third consecutive season of below-average rainfall, which has already resulted in a sharp increase in food insecurity, especially in rural areas. Without humanitarian assistance, nearly 3.5 million are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021.
The project aims at contributing to improving food access of food insecure vulnerable communities living in Galgudud, Lower Shabelle and Banadir regions. The target population include IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities living in urban areas and rural areas. The target population have been facing shocks resulting from natural and man-made disasters that affected their livelihood, outbreak of COVID-19 and infestation of desert locust, worsened the situation for the already vulnerable communities. To intervene the situation the project is designed to reach 16860 beneficiaries in the three regions of Galgudud, Banadir and Lower Shabelle. The project aims reaching 500 households (3000 beneficiaries) with malnourished children living in the three target regions. IDPs, returnees and poor host communities are targeted. Households will be receiving monthly unconditional cash transfer of US$60 per household per month for three months.
The project also plans to provide farm inputs to vulnerable 1200 (7200 beneficiaries) farming households with small holdings living in Lower Shabelle Region. The project is also designed to have 5 secondary canals in Lower Shabelle region rehabilitated in order to improve food production. 610 food insecure households (3660 beneficiaries) will rehabilitate their community secondary canals to improve irrigation. Participants will be provided with conditional monthly cash of US$60 via mobile transfer for the three-month exercise.
The project will also provide skill training to 500 unskilled youth (250 male and 250 female) from food insecure IDP HHs, returnees and vulnerable host communities living in Mogadishu. The trainees will be provided with conditional cash of US$60 per month for six months.
Beneficiaries will be selected based on vulnerability of households. Where cash transfer is involved, mothers will be registered as recipient. Fathers will be considered in the absence of the mother. In the case of child headed household the oldest son/daughter will be registered. Vulnerable households with persons living with disability, especially those headed with disabled persons, will also be given consideration in the programme.
During registration, the organization will ensure the beneficiary has phone cell registered under her/his name. Where the beneficiary hasn’t one the programme will liaise with providers to register one for the beneficiary. One of the firms will be engaged to make the transfers and the organization will confirm money has been transferred to the right numbers.
The project design considered market functionality by adhering to the cash working group's recommendation of quarter three of 2021. The project used MEB recommended the respective regions by the cash working group as of August 2021.

1. - Projects activities

1. 500 HHs (3000 beneficiaries) of IDPs, returnees and poor host communities with malnourished children in Marka, Dhusamareb and Banadir will be provided with family ration for three months via cash based transfer. Each household will be receiving US$60 per month 2. 1200 riverine and agro-pastoral household farmers in Qoryoley, Barawe, Kuntuwarey and Marka districts will be provided with viable seeds and farm hand tools for the two planting seasons of 2021. 3. 5 secondary canals in Marka and Kuntuwarey districts will be rehabilitated via conditional cash transfer; 610 (3660 beneficiaries) will be getting cash transfer of US$60 via mobile transfer for three months. Recipients will rehabilitate their community irrigation canals. The exercise will take three months. 4. 500 (250 female youth and 250 male youth) unskilled youths living in Banadir and IDPs and poor host communities affected by COVID-19 pandemic will be provided with skill training via voucher for training (VFT) of US$60 per month for six months. Trainees will be provided with employable skills. 5. 6 community led Early Warning Early Action systems will be Established in nine districts 6. Provide COVID-19 related PPE’s to all training centers

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project involves CTP where 500 households with malnourished children will receive cash transfer of US$60 per month for three months via mobile cash. The project opt for this because of the following reasons: 1. The target beneficiaries are vulnerable IDPs, returnees and poor host communities who might encounter security threat if given assistance in kind 2. Those beneficiaries who live far away from distribution centres might not be able to access distribution points 3. With cash money in their phones they can buy variety of food staff unlike in kind programming where they can only get limited food items. 4. distribution of food (in kind) at distribution points may pose health risks amid COVID-19 pandemic.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: The project will mitigate this risk by making sure that registration and distribution will be done venues close to beneficiaries (less than 2 km distance). Registration and distribution exercises will be done during day time. 2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: Food/voucher distribution venues will be selected areas far from armed groups/military camps. Project staff will be trained on humanitarian code of conduct and consequences of sexual exploitation. Beneficiaries awareness on sexual exploitation will be raised and complaint mechanism will be provided 3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.): The project will closely work with organizations providing protection services in the project area and refer people with protection issues to them. 4. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: To mitigate this risk, the project will develop criteria that will consider people living with disabilities, members from marginalized groups, IDPs, women, boys and girls to be included in the community project committees supporting agency in the project implementation. Women and people with disabilities will also be considered in the staffing.Children and people with disabilities are among the most vulnerable, marginalized groups within Somali society because of the attitudinal, environmental, and institutional barriers they face. This practice means they are unable to interact freely and receive appropriate service they deserve. NWO has given priority to PLWD in its all programs, in term of accessibility and inclusion. Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment are unacceptable breaches of fundamental human rights. 46 staff trained including senior management officers on PSEA and its procedures, NWO has zero tolerance on PSEA. Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted are in place. NWO adapts the strategy of AAP by working transparently and effectively sharing information with communities, giving communities influence over decision making in a way that accounts for their diversity, and allows the views of the most at-risk to be equally considered. Giving communities the opportunity to assess and if appropriate sanction our actions.
