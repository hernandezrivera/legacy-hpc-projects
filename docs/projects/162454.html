Project Summary

Sudan hosts the largest population of South Sudanese refugees in the region, with more than 810,155 individuals reported across the country as of 31 oct 2019. Among these, nearly 385,000 fled to Sudan since the outbreak of conflict in South Sudan in 2013, and have been registered or recorded at reception areas by UNHCR and the Government’s Commissioner of Refugees (COR). White Nile state hosts the largest population of South Sudanese refugees in the country, with more than 247,555 as of 31 oct 2019 during last six years since the start of the conflict in South Sudan, and continues to receive a regular flow of South Sudanese refugee arrivals each month. The majority of refugees disseminated in 9 camps are women and children (85%), who are in urgent need of protection, nutrition, shelter and health support.
For more than 5 years and according to the open border policy which has maintained by Government of Sudan, White Nile state one affected areas by generously hosted South Sudanese refugees, the largest recent influx originates from South Sudan. state communities host refugees on their land and de-facto integrate urban refugees within their towns and villages mainly in Three localities Aljabalain 25,919, Alaslam 140,098 and Kosti 81,538 as of 31 oct 2019 .White Nile and Eljabalain locality in three village Alagaa ,Dabet bocien and Alwarad (About 15,000 person) has been faced with economic hardships and high costs of living , which already suffering availability of adequate basic services particularly in areas adjacent to the refugees camps that not fairly reached by the humanitarian actors.
. Against this challenging context, Host community members struggle to meet their basic needs and together South Sudanese refugees and Sudanese returnees have further stretched the limited socio-economic capital such as community socio-economic infrastructure and limited livelihood opportunities in the state. This situation has created a disproportionate effect on the lives and livelihoods of the host communities in general.
Overall objective:
Contributing towards building resilience among host communities, and sustainability of interventions across the response.
Specific objective:
Improve the self-employment of and entrepreneurship skills of host community members through access to applicable vocational, technical, and skills training's.
This project will contributing towards building resilience for vulnerable host communities,as well as promotion of social cohesion and peaceful co-existence between refugees and host communities by contributing to the stability and resilience of communities through improve the self-employment and entrepreneurship skills through access to applicable vocational, technical, and skills training's for men and women .

2 - Activities / Outputs

Activity 1 : conduct a rapid needs and market assessment:The assessment will be comprised of three-tiers and will explore the access to supporting functions, including their access to information, training and financial services. The next aspect will be a target group assessment to gain a profile of the host community members and their skills, , education and work experience. Finally, a market analysis to gain an overview of the relevant sectors and subsectors and potential for sustainable growth and employment creation will be done. This assessment will inform the future design and development of a needs-led and market-driven livelihood program for refugees.Activity 2 :Beneficiary Selection:The project team will carry out several meetings with different stakeholders including community leaders, relevant local officials, and host community leaders. These meetings will elaborate and clarify the roles and responsibilities of partners. The project's objectives, activities and outputs will be discussed in close collaboration with COR and modified if neededActivity 3: Implement vocational training livelihoods strengthening program will be implemented for host community members. will focus on improving the employability, self-employment and entrepreneurship skills of beneficiaries through access to applicable training's, learning, and skills building.• If confirmed by the needs/market assessments conducted at the start of the program, syllabi could include the following: food processing, traditional perfume making and soap making, cell phone maintenance, fishing, bread making, vegetable trading, and agricultural extension. If livelihoods are identified which differ from the established syllabi, vocational trainers will be found to develop new curricula;Activity 4 : Business Start-UpAfter completing vocational training, each participant will be equipped with a full essential toolkit to enable her/him to commence livelihood activities based on the knowledge acquired in training. If determined feasible through the early assessments, participants may be provided with either a cash transfer or voucher to purchase kits in local markets, the exact modality will be determined pending the outcome of the assessment. If not feasible, business start-up kits will be purchased in Khartoum and provided through in-kind distributions to participants. If feasible (political and community acceptance exists) a cash grant will be provided to each person successfully graduating from the vocational training courses to cover business startup costs. The exact amount of the startup grant will vary depending on the training area and the location of the beneficiaries.
