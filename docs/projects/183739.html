Project Summary

The last Israeli attacks on the Gaza Strip in May 2021 & the COVID19 pandemic have given rise to new types of violence & GBV especially those who are displaced from their homes or who lost their husbands, brothers or children and who are subjected to sexual harassment and abuse, as well as those who have lost their inheritance rights or personal belongings as a result of the demolition of their homes & the loss of their official papers.
Moreover, the results of the meetings with the local committees and different groups of the local community, confirmed that there is an urgent need to continue the provision of protection services for GBV cases in the targeted areas.
Therefore, the idea of the project is based on the provision of multisectoral protection services for women & girls survivors or at risk of GBV in the Eastern area of Khanyons governorate & the Northwest of Gaza Governorate through the Al- Awda Health & Community Centers.
The project will provide psychosocial support (face to face & remote) and legal counselling, as well as emergency health services, this is in addition to providing case management & referral service to more specialized institutions, particularly in the area of mental health, and representation in the courts.
On the other hand, women & girls including PWD with difficult economic conditions, will be provided with dignity kits according to specific criteria and in coordination with the Ministry of social development.
The project will focus on increasing the sensitivity of the community to issues of protection, women rights and PSEA through conducting face-to-face & remote awareness sessions targeting different groups of society (men-women-boys-girls, including PWD).
In order to enable the staff of the project to provide services with high quality while preserving the protection & dignity of the beneficiaries, they will participate in a training course about remote service delivery, contingency planning - GBV Minimum Standards - PSEA - Feedback, Complaint & Response Mechanism (FCRM).
And to improve the performance of the project staff they will work under the supervision of technical supervisor.
The project staff will participate in self-care training in addition to 3 self-care recreational days that will be organized to rid them of the stress they suffer & increase their resilience to life pressures to enable them to continue providing services.
while other 2 courses will be implemented for Al-Awda workers who are protection specialized & non-specialized, where they will be trained on topics related to protection mainstreaming & disability inclusion.
Moreover, 4 training courses will be conducted for non-GBV humanitarian workers about risk mitigation, psychological first aid, detection and referral of GBV cases.
In order to highlight the suffering of women & girls and protection issues, a fact sheet & position paper will be issued by Al-Awda & will be disseminated by the protection GBV sub-working group.
In order for the beneficiaries to access the project information, Radio Spot & a sponsored Facebook ads will be published to inform the targeted group about the project services & locations.
In order to achieve the principle of AAP, the beneficiaries will participate in all the project cycle phases & will be informed about Al-Awda FCRM (which they have been consulted about) through publishing motion graphic video that will be interpreted to sign language.
Adding to printing & disseminating 2000 leaflets about PSEA including information on rights and safe complaint channels available to beneficiaries.
Within the framework of coordination & complementary work & to avoid duplication in the provision of services, coordination was made with the GBV Sub-working group Coordinator on the project activities and the targeted areas.

1 - Needs

HNO 2022 reported that 1.8 million Palestinians are experience or are at risk of conflict, violence and displacement, including forcible transfer, 64% of them in the Gaza Strip. As a result of the recent Israeli aggression on the Gaza Strip in May 2021, the martyrs reached 260 citizens, including 40 women, and 23 girls under 18 years of age, which increased the suffering of women where the number of widows increased to 101 due to the loss of their husbands (GBV sub-cluster NA May 2021). More than 2,200 citizens were injured, including 480 women, of whom it is expected that 10% will suffer from long-term disabilities after six months of injury, which requires rehabilitation and social integration. (GBV sub-cluster NA May 2021). As the HNO 2022 indicated that more than 113,000 people who were displaced from their homes as a result of the aggression on the Gaza Strip, most of them have recourse to UNRWA schools and host families, where the number of internally displaced women and girls reached 17,700 women and girls, while thousands of women and girls suffered from enormous psychological conditions as a result of losing their homes or one of their relatives. Hundreds of them have been subjected to cases of harassment, whether in shelters or with host families, exposing them to increased risks of gender-based violence. In Gaza, around 8,250 persons remained displaced as of end-August, primarily those whose houses were destroyed during the May escalation, or so damaged as to be uninhabitable. According to local authorities, over 2,000 housing units were totally destroyed or severely damaged, and an estimated 15,000 housing units sustained some degree of damage. This has led to a significant increase in demand for legal aid support for affected households, including for GBV survivors and widowed women relating to issues of denial of resources, custody of children and the loss of identity documents due to destruction of houses or while taking refuge. (HNO 2022) The report of the needs assessment issued by the protection sector in May 2021 confirmed that there is a need for women in general, especially widows, divorced women, and survivors of violence, to legal services, which include individual and group legal advice, legal awareness, adding to representation for them in the courts and to facilitate their access to justice. As well as, there is a need to find an appropriate mechanism to follow up on women's rights regarding custody issues and viewing in addition to conflict resolution, especially during displacement. The HNO2022 reports, based on the violence survey, showed that there is a clear increase in women and girls in terms of gender-based violence, while the percentage of psychological violence reached 56.6%, physical violence reached 17.8%, sexual violence reached 8.8%, social violence reached 32.5%, and economic violence reached 41.1%, and the percentage of cyber violence reached 8%. Moreover, early marriage and forced marriage continued, especially in rural areas, Bedouin gatherings and refugee camps. Based on the mentioned percentages, we note that cyber violence issues have surfaced as a result of the expansion of the horizons of dealing with social networking sites and the adoption of large segments of society on its use, as women and girls are exposed to electronic crimes, either by harassment or extortion. On the other side, regarding the COVID19 pandemic crisis, a fact sheet issued by Aisha Association in 2021 stated that 40% of the women surveyed were subjected to economic violence as a result of the domestic quarantine related to the COVID19 pandemic, which restricted the movement of the population and negatively affected the general economy in the region. Despite the increase in the number of women and girls who need protection services, including psychological, social, legal and economic support programmes, in addition to educating & raising the awareness of the community about issues of gender-based violence and sexual violence, including women, men, girls and boys; the services provided in protection centers have clearly declined as a result of the recent aggression and the damage to service centers on the one hand or the crisis of the COVID19 pandemic and its negative impact on the movement of people and their freedom of movement and their ability to access these services, especially those with disabilities who have become difficult to access these services either because of poverty and the lack of money for transportation fees or because of the restriction of movement or fear of movement linked to COVID19 on the other hand. Women and girls with disabilities face additional barriers that limits their access to services that puts them in additional risk. In Gaza, 58% of persons with disabilities lack of Information about GBV prevention and response, including how and where to access relevant assistance and services during emergency (HNO 2022) Within the community framework of the Al-Awda and through its 6 community centers located in all the governorates of the Gaza Strip and its implementation of a series of community activities for the protection of women and girls, including focus groups meetings that the community centers’ staff organizes from time to time to study the most important problems, needs and priorities which concerns the community & the local committees; A wide segment of women and girls indicated that there is an urgent need for them for protection services, especially those related to multi-sectoral services, including psychological, social, legal, economic and health support. In addition to the importance of raising awareness of women, men, girls and boys including PWD about issues of gender-based violence and PSEA in order to increase the sensitivity of community members towards protection issues in general and PSEA in particular.

2 - Activities

The project activities are designed to respond to the needs of the targeted groups, with ensuring the implementation of these activities in line with protecting the dignity of the beneficiaries and responding to their needs according to the nature of the problems they face, provided that it contribute to covering part of the gap resulting from the lack of protection services. Through this project, the following activities will be implemented: 1- 1- Building the capabilities of the project staff through: - Implementing a 36-hour training course, (6 days, 6hours/day) for the project staff (12 persons) (10 females - 2 males) that includes the following topics: - remote service delivery, contingency planning - GBV Minimum Standards - PSEA - Al-Awda Feedback, Complain and Response Mechanism (FCEM). - Conducting a self-care & stress management training course to the project staff (12 persons) (2 men – 10 women) for 18 hours (3 days – 6 hours/day). - Organizing 3 self-care days creational days to the project staff (2 men – women). - Organizing 2 training courses, 12 participants/course with a total of 24 participants (18 females - 6 males) specialists in the field of GBV (including the project staff) and non-specialists working in the Al-Awda for 6 days, 6 hours per day, including topics related to mainstreaming protection and disability inclusion. 2- Implementing 4 training courses (3 days, 6hours/day/course) in the field of risk mitigation, psychological first aid, detection and referral of GBV cases. Each course includes 12 non-GBV participants (12 participants * 2 courses = 24 persons, 18 women – 6 men) from humanitarian staff, provided that the participants are of both sexes, including PWD. 3- Providing multi-sectoral services, including psychosocial and legal support, in addition to emergency health services, case management and referral services, as follows: -Providing face-to-face psychological support services for 200 beneficiaries (120 women - 40 girls - 20 boys - 20 men), including 4 PWD. -Providing remote psychological support services for 400 beneficiaries (300 women - 100 girls), including 8 PWD. -Providing Face-to-face social support services for 200 beneficiaries (120 women - 40 girls - 20 boys - 20 men), including 4 PWD. - Providing remote social support for 400 beneficiaries (300 women - 100 girls), 8 of whom have disabilities. -Providing face-to-face legal advice services for 100 beneficiaries (80 women - 20 girls), including 2 PWD. - Providing emergency health care services for 300 beneficiaries (250 women - 50 girls), including 6 PWD -Providing case management services for 100 beneficiaries (70 women - 30 girls), including 2 PWD. - Referring 10 cases (8 women - 2 girls) to specialized organizations to present them in the court, and referring 10 cases (8 women – 2 girls) to specialized organizations in mental health in addition to providing transportation for the referred cases to enhance their access to the services. 5- Raising the awareness of different groups of the community on issues related to the protection and women rights including the PSEA, through the implementation of 352 face-to-face awareness sessions for 3520 beneficiaries (1,760 women - 880 girls - 440 boys - 440 men) including 35 PWD, where 4 sessions will be allocated to include PWD of both sexes, provided that a sign language interpreter service will be provided during the implementation of these sessions to ensure that persons with hearing disabilities benefit from the sessions information. 6 – Implementing 176 remote awareness sessions on the protection and women rights, including PSEA, in which 1,760 beneficiaries will participate (880 women - 440 girls - 220 boys - 220 men) including 15 PWD, where 2 sessions will be allocated to include PWD of both sexes, provided that a sign language interpreter service will be provided in order for persons with hearing disabilities benefit from the session information. 7. In order to involve different groups of society, including PWD, in information about complaints & feedback mechanisms by beneficiaries, a motion graphic video with a sign language interpretation will be prepared and published on Facebook with a sponsored advertisement & will be displayed on screens in the waiting areas for beneficiaries in all Al-Awda facilities. 8. 2000 copies of the leaflets about PSEA including information on rights and safe complaint channels available to beneficiaries will be printed & distributed to the targeted group. 9- Distributing dignity kits to 300 women and girls (260 women - 40 girls), including 6 PWD, based on specific criteria and in coordination with the Ministry of social development. 10- In the context of shedding light on the suffering of women and girls from GBVS, a fact sheet and a position paper will be issued and sent to the protection cluster with the aim of disseminating them to partners to benefit from them when drawing strategies for future interventions. 11- To have access to information about the project, including the services provided, in addition to informing the local community about Al-Awda FCRM, advertisements will be issued through radio spot and sponsored Facebook advertisement. 12- Implementing 90 technical supervision meetings with the case managers & 90 meetings with the psychologists during the project duration at a rate of 10 hours each month for each of them.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The results of HNO 2022 pointed the need of multisectoral services for women and girls exposed to gender-based violence. The Project activities were designed to respond to these needs, as the Al-Awda owns permanent centers in all Gaza Strip governorates, including Gaza and Khnyounis Governorates (where the project will be implemented), where multisectoral protection services are provided in these centers for women and girls, including PWD, and this proposal will contribute effectively to expanding the range of services provided, on the one hand, and increasing the number of beneficiaries of the services on the other hand. Noting that the psychosocial, legal and health services are provided in the same center, and this make it easier for women to access multisectoral services in an accessible manner while ensuring that they are protected from social stigma while receiving the service, as the health center is considered as entry point for the community services. The experience of the Al-Awda in the field of providing multi-sectoral services has accumulated over the years, as it has implemented dozens of projects in the field of GBV and protection, and the protection program is a vital program in Al-Awda strategic plan 2020-2024. The services planned by the project are therefore an urgent and necessarily need to improve the lives of women in the target areas.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The Al-Awda gives great importance to obtaining feedback & complaints from the beneficiaries, as they are considered a key partner, where they are involved in the planning, implementing & evaluating Al-Awda activities. Several mechanisms have been allocated to receive feedback & complaints while ensuring that they can be submitted by all age & gender groups, including PWD (whether hearing, visual or physical disabilities) as the means of submitting the complaint varied in terms of written complaints directly to the complaint box (note that there is a special protocol for opening the box) or voice complaints through the use of the Toll-free No. which is a free No. specified for complaints, this in addition to electronic complaints via a generic e-mail, besides receiving complaints face to face or by filling out questionnaires. The Al-Awda is currently developing a Feedback, Complain and Response Mechanism (FCRM) through the development of an electronic model that includes the following classifications:• Sensitive complaint.• Complaint & dissatisfaction.• Inquire about existing services.• Request for individual support & assistance.• Thanks & appreciation.Where the call center employee, through the electronic form, fills in all the details of the complaint and forwarded it to the MEAL manager, except for sensitive complaints that are forwarded to the Executive Director directly. As for the complaints on the Executive Director himself, they are forwarded to the Chairman. In order to response to gender needs, there will be call center workers of both sexes. It is worth to mention that, the beneficiaries of the center have been consulted about Al-Awda’s FCRM and will be informed about it through publishing 3D motion graphic video interpreted to sign language to reach to PWD.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The idea of the project, its services and implementation mechanisms stem from respecting the protection principles stipulated in the Humanitarian Charter, including the right to live in dignity, the right to assistance and the right to protection. Moreover, these mechanisms are part of the values of the AL-AWDA within its strategic plan 2020-2024. Through this project and to avoid exposing the beneficiaries to harm the following pillars will be taken into consideration: 1- Safety:- The Al-Awda will provide the project services through its 2 health and community centers in Gaza and Khanyounis. The geographical locations of these facilities are distinguished by their presence in populated places, making it safety and easy to reach and are known to the public since their establishment.- In order to prevent SEA while providing project services, project staff will attend PSEA training. In addition, their contracts will include a special clause on PSEA in accordance with the IASC Principles and Guidelines.- In order not to expose the beneficiaries to further harm, they will be educated about PSEA and the Al-Awda complaints mechanism.- To ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed while receiving services on the basis of dignity and protection, the project staff will participate in training course related to protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. - Insurance of work accidents for the project staff. - Providing health insurance for the project staff in the AL AWDA health facilities. 2- Confidentiality and privacy.- Cases consultations and documentation are private and confidential. - Beneficiaries will be provided with all the necessary information about the intervention plan. 3- Adaptability and acceptability.- Gender needs will be considered by having female staff among the work teams to meet the gender needs of women and girls. 4- COVID-19.- To achieve protection from COVID19 infection while providing services, prevention and safety procedures are followed according to the directions of the MoH and the WHO, that includes providing work teams and beneficiaries with PPE.- As part of building the capacities of the Al-Awda’s staff to confront the COVID19 pandemic, a group of them was trained on ways to prevent the transmission of COVID19 infection.- Avoid stigmatization in the case of dealing with cases infected with COVID19.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Al-Awda adopts the principle of equality and impartiality in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups who need humanitarian interventions to improve their protection and humanitarian situation. Al-Awda provides its services without any discrimination based on age, gender, religion, race, or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project and to achieve the principle of impartiality in providing services, Al-Awda will provide services to the targeted people on the basis that each case has her own individual characteristics and special needs, especially PWD as the most vulnerable in society and who need special care and protection while maintaining dignity, confidentiality and nondiscrimination. The project services will be easily accessible to the target groups, as they will be provided through 2 health and community centers affiliated to Al-Awda in Gaza and Khanyounis governorates that are well known to the beneficiaries. To ensure that the targeted women have access to the services provided under the project, especially women who do not have the money needed for transportation, they will be provided with transportation fees. It is worth noting that the training courses that will be organized through this project, that will target the project staff and other staff from Al-Awda, will take into consideration training them on the mechanisms and methods of dealing with PWD to ensure that the services are provided without compromising their dignity.
