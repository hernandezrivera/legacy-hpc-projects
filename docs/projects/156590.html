Project Summary

The project aims at supporting basic and emergency food needs of the vulnerable IDPs, returnees and HCs. In addition, the project will target the newly displaced people in the latest. The project comprises the following components:
a) distribution of regular Food Baskets to 8000 HHs over 9 rounds for host communities and IDPs residing in the community for more than 3 months (actual number of beneficiaries as of mid-year: 4000 HHs).
b) distribution of Ready to Eat Rations (RTERs) to 35,700 newly IDP/returnee individuals (for three rounds); for IDPs and returnees who displaced to the area for less than 3 months. (actual number of beneficiaries as of mid-year: 22350 individuals)
c) distribution of cooked meals to 1000 IDP HHs as a response of spike within 72 hours. (planned)
d) distribution of 300 tonnes of flour to bakeries. (planned)

Needs

Drawn from a country-wide household assessment of 10,080 households and technical consultation, 6.5 million people are estimated to be food insecure in Syria and a further 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity, according to the draft report of HNO 2019. The assessment did not cover five districts120, where consultative process and secondary data analysis were used instead to establish needs. Those who are categorized to be food insecure are reporting large food consumption gaps, depleted coping strategies and a large food expenditure share. Those who are considered at risk of food insecurity are those “worst off” in the moderately food secure category and may be falling into food insecurity if their livelihoods are not supported. The main drivers of food insecurity in Syria are a set of interrelated factors related to the deteriorating economic environment as well as food insecurity stemming from massive displacements. Almost 40 per cent of the surveyed households reported to be an IDP or returnee, 74.5 per cent of which reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Given the above circumstances and the disruption generated by the crisis, particularly with regard to large-scale displacement and family separation, traditional gender roles have changed. access to resources and participation in the humanitarian response also varies across different sex and age groups. Particularly in the most traditional segments of society, the traditional division of labor in the private sphere is replicated in the public sphere, often restricting both the mobility and ability of women to engage in roles related to distribution and management of humanitarian services and may also affect their ability to access assistance.

Implementation of activities

Shafak will be delivering the food assistance to the targeted beneficiaries through the direct in-kind distribution. Shafak has a vast experience in distribution activities and will be implementing them under this program through the following steps: - community mobilization and orientation to the community members the project activities and approach. - signing MoUs with the concerned local authorities. - beneficiaries selection and verification based on agreed set of criteria.- procurement of the NFI items. - distribution of assistance through the different modalities (in-kind or through voucher system). - Monitoring and Evaluation Shafak will make sure to include 10% of the targeted individuals to be from adolescents and youth. The design of the food baskets, and RTERs will consider the guidance of the FSL Cluster as well as will take into account the different gender and age groups (men, women, boys, girls especially children below 5, and elderlies) of the households. Shafak studies the targeted communities and ensures that assistance is tailored to all gender and age groups. Furthermore, as part of the usual practice, Shafak starts its programs with community mobilization activities, where they meet with the community representatives and the local authorities, and conduct orientation sessions about the project activities, project approaches and assistance modalities. Shafak accountability team conduct information sharing sessions, and orient the community on the feedback mechanisms. The received feedback from the different gender and age groups in such sessions and in the similar previous projects are taken into consideration while designing the current program with specific focus on the vulnerable groups of women, youth, adolescents and elderlies.

Protection Risk Analysis

- limitations on movement and physical security risk due to conflict activity.- Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girl’s mobility. - Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. Shafak will mitigate the above risks through the community orientation sessions, in which the Shafak staff ensure that equality concepts are conveyed to the community.

Forgery of vouchers; The security features included in the vouchers will prevent these attempts.4. Deterioration of security (military, civil: riots, bombing, air strikes) in operational area; ending the process and evacuate the area from staff and beneficiaries and reschedule the distribution dates.5.
