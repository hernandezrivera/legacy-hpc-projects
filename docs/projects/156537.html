Project Summary

According to the HNO of 2018-2019 there is 1 million children in Syria are out of school, yet provision of education services is limited. 1 in 3 schools have been damaged, and more are occupied by IDPs. Schools in IDP/returnee receiving communities have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students, causing overcrowding. Shortage of qualified teachers also limits the quality and provision of education.
As a result of the chronological and geographical extension of the war in Syrian since 8 years, many children and adolescents have not been able to reintegrate into the schooling system or reintegrate it successfully, and will need to have educational support and alternative education sessions. In addition, schools need to a small and quickly rehabilitation to be a safe space to receive students.
SSSD, through the educational program, provide effective educational programming for “at-risk” children who are in the age of school (5-17 years).
Under this program SSSD applies:
1. Remedial classes: This collective activity targets the regular students who are in school (5-17 years) by providing an assistant in their school lessons.
2. Tutoring: This individual activity targets the most vulnerable regular student who are in school
3. InFormal (alternative) education (IFE) to drop outs and at-risk children
4. Registration support to drop outs to reintegrate the schooling system
5. distribution of school and learning support supplies
6. The development of IFE as an alternative educational model with MOE
7. school rehabilitation (small repair).
SSSD alternative education model combats the factors hindering the progress of these school-aged, gets struggling students back on track toward high school graduation, and creates a positive school culture proven to transform at-risk students and schools.
SSSD works to restoring the drop-outs children to the education and to reintegration to the regular classes through the Informal education program (IFE). SSSD uses the Participatory Learning Methodology (PLM) which allows the drop-out to develop their learning capabilities and critical thinking that will enable them to go back to learning and eventually be mainstreamed in the schooling system.
This IFE model implemented by SSSD is being taken one step further with MOE to use it as a model for alternative education, developing legal frameworks, criteria, guidelines and measurement tools.

Needs

According to HNO education severity scale 2018, a dangerous situation is observed in sum of governorates, ranging between major and catastrophic conditions. And where SSSD working. The MoE statistics showed that there are about 1 million children out of school inside Syria need to be reintegrated into education and the schooling system. Moreover, 5.7 million children and adolescents (in and out of school) and education personnel are in need of education assistance. As of 2014 the GER of basic education was 78.1%. This is lower than any Syrian Education statistics available (91.7% in 1996). The crisis has pushed Syrian basic education back by two decades at minimum. In Addition, functioning schools accommodate more students while small schools are closed. Congested classrooms give no ability to teachers to follow up each students.SSSD conducted assessments with the parents, especially in the hard to reach areas, focusing on three needs; lack of educational supplies, overcrowded classrooms, incapacity of paying the registration fees, and an emergency need of educational support for at-risk children to save them from dropping-out.The absence of the educational interventions in the locations above in addition to the other social factors (mental problems, child labor… etc.) will increase the numbers of dropouts, which will lead to many social, educational, …. problems .SSSD intends to respond to these emergency needs in addition to the problems hamper the education process

Implementation of activities

1. Remedial classes: collective sessions to provide an educational assistant to the enrolled students in their school lessons2. Tutoring sessions: Individual session to empower the enrolled student in the basics material; Mathematic, Arabic, Science, English.3. Literacy sessions: Collective session for the drop-outs using the Informal Education program through the Participatory Learning methodology (PLM).4. Back-to-school and awareness-raising campaigns for the dropout and at-risk children by the Informal Education program5. Self-learning and life skills sessions for the dropouts and at-risk children by the Informal Education program through the Participatory Learning methodology (PLM) to improve their educational level.6. Provide basic teaching and learning materials.7. Train volunteers, tutors, facilitators on the PLM (participatory learning methodology).8. Implementation of school works: repair the classes room with repair the WASH facility for students and teachers, safety measures, water insulation and electricity wiring (to the possible extent).
