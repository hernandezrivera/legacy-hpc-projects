Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen case management through direct service provision to children at risk (including child survivors), issues which increase risks to children such as when they are unaccompanied or separated children (UASC), children without proper care who are children at risk of being recruited by armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG).
The interventions aim at direct services like family tracing of the primary duty-bearers if in the best interest of each individual child, reintegration into the community for children formerly associated with armed groups (including psychosocial first aid- PFA) and Psychosocial support to both children, caregivers in addition to victim support and referral services for where a holistic approach is necessary.
The project will include capacity building by providing training and mentoring to CP, Other protection staff from INTERSOS as well as implementing partners (IPs) or members of Cluster. The approach CPiE aims to provide immediate lifesaving assistance to vulnerable populations, prevention, and response risks faced by children.
Project beneficiaries will receive comprehensive case management and psycho-social support (PSS) – including emergency care, referral to other actors for services not offered by INTERSOS, access tailored material assistance, then follow up on all services (both directly provided and those received during/after referrals).
Capacity building of duty bearers including formal and informal authorities to strengthen and improve identification. Considering current approaches, the emphasis is on scaling up prevention and response services to protection-related risk for children (including vulnerable caregivers to improve the protective environment and the quality of care).
INTERSOS also commits to participate in strengthening service mapping to facilitate appropriate referrals and through coordination and maintaining links necessary in service delivery. Comprehensive services to children at risk (UASC, CAAFAG, and/or experiencing any form of abuse; including those neglected), to ensure the protection of children from recruitment or use by armed forces or armed groups and even use of girls (child-survivors of gender-based violence (GBV)).
The project will link to mainstreaming of protection monitoring in areas of operation where the settlement of returnees and IDPs through community-based protection advocates linked to project ensure that quality evidence-based reports about protection issues and human rights violations are collected in a systematic protection-related manner for the data to inform better response and advocacy (including information feeding to the monitoring of grave violations against children (MRM) when applicable. Secretary General’s Report on Children in Armed Conflict released in June 2020 show, the United Nations verified 3,709 grave violations against 2,959 children in Somalia. The highest number of these violations were related to recruitment and use of children as soldiers (1,442 boys and 53 girls) followed by abductions (1,065 boys, 93 girls), killing and maiming (518 boys and 118 girls), rape and other forms of sexual violence (227 girls). Other reported violations also include denial of humanitarian access (50 incidents) and attacks on schools and hospitals (74 reported attacks).
In safeguarding children’s best interest process, INTERSOS will consider the child’s family, culture and social situation and conduct actions, procedures and programmes in a manner that does not put the child at risk of harm. The participation of boys and girls in decisions that affect their lives will be planned and facilitated in a responsible and ethical manner with due regard to conﬁdentiality. Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) approach ensures that all persons of concern, including boys and girls enjoy equal rights. It recognizing that children may be affected differently by protection risks as a result of their age, gender, disability and background.

Project Activities

(1). Improving the quality of CP services for boys and girls at risk most (including child-survivors of violence, exploitation, and abuse). Children of concern for protection from child labor (including hazardous forms). High-risk groups such as unaccompanied and separated children, children with disabilities, those exhibiting severe signs of distress, and children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG) prioritizing protection enhanced by direct support to children at risk, child survivors including unaccompanied and separated through providing case management services that meet their unique needs, which include family tracing and reunification services, alternative care, of inclusive community reintegration (including victim assistance where applicable). (2). Provision of structured MHPSS activities including individual and group counseling focus on children in need, caregivers, and families (including positive parenting and in-kind of support) in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their own children. Supporting tailored material support to at-risk children (including but not limited to UASC or CAAFAG) and child-survivors GBV as well as provision of dignity kits (to at-risk women/caregivers and girls) including solar lanterns to enhance safety and protection. (3). Strengthening community-based protection mechanisms to prevent and mitigate risks faced by boys, and girls (as well as caregivers or those at high risk of abuse/exploitation) through multi-layered, community-based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks by building a protective environment at family and community levels. And additional consideration to encourage peer to peer awareness-raising led by youth and community volunteers’ networks to prevent/mitigate protection risk; identify vulnerable children and caregivers (including informal fosters) in need of support such as case management and emergency care (i.e. alternative/interim care, material support or basic PSS activities). (4). Systematizing efforts to develop sustainable local capacities through facilitating workshops/seminars/training with civilsociety members and authorities on protection principles and best interests’ practices for children (including meaningful participation of children). Equip traditional duty bearers as well as medical personnel, and humanitarian staff with relevant information and approaches on child protection in emergencies including assessing and management of child-friendly environment or space; as well as nutrition to at-risk children under the care of vulnerable women. (5). Facilitate awareness-raising initiatives, direct advocacy, and public outreach campaigns on Child risks and harmful traditional practices (such as FGM) prevention (and mitigation) targeting community-wide engagement approach on awareness sessions. Advocating against recruitment or use / CAAFAG, all forms of violence, harmful practice, voluntary family separation while defending MRE, MHPSS, and Children’s rights (6). Strengthen child safeguarding measures including PSEA and code of conduct to ensure that all interventions are safe for children including support to staff trained as a PSEA and child safeguarding focal point linkages with community focal point on implementation of safe reporting and action relating to PSEA issues. (8). Coordinate with CP AoRs and clusters (including relevant taskforces on MHPSS, MRM, Case management among others) on safety audits/walks/mapping exercise to identify safety and protection issues for children in camps and host communities that allow children’s participation in identifying risks they are exposed to, and then participate again on coming up with mitigation strategies owned by them (children), the community members, service providers, and humanitarian actors.

Mitigation Measures

Considering Communities are forced to leave their place of residence in order to access humanitarian assistance (humanitarian assistance as pull factor) in addition to being displaced as a result of conflict or natural disasters. The key mitigation measures to reduce movement include; (1). Strengthening coordination with non-protection service providers (present in less accessible areas). (2). Deploy mobile teams to respond to in less accessible areas (3). Conduct needs assessment and advocate for resources to those in need (where they are). (4). Build the capacity of the local community members on the rights approach and link to community-based mechanisms to foster resilience With the risk that members of marginalized community/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. INTERSOS mitigation measure include; (1). Working with community-based groups and focal point, INTERSOS links to multiple sites ensuring equal opportunities for all clan and sub-clans as per family tracing networks (2). Special focus to support vulnerable caregivers (such as the elderly), beneficiaries with disabilities (including those with mobility issues) INTERSOS will ensure non-discrimination principle and remain neutral per humanitarian principle. (3). Needs assessments conducted will also take consideration to ensure the participation of different demographic from, age, gender, clan, location, or social status in the community focusing on risks for all and protective environment for all (4). Where applicable jointly with all stakeholders consider special arrangements for persons whohave difficulty/need For the risk in terms of barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms, the mitigation measures would include;(1). Creating awareness to beneficiaries on the existing contact numbers; sites for suggestion boxes and staff trained to handle and document all forms of feedback. (2). Ensuring that the community is aware of the responses to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures put in place per feedback/complaint received. (3). Promoting a confidential process (for submitting feedback that does not require the beneficiary to expose themselves). (4). Where possible integrate feedback mechanism with other actors (a joint mechanism which is less confusing).
