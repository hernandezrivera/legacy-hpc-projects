Project Summary

Sector wide, agencies are supporting the scale up of the Myanmar Curriculum program, and IRC will be continuing its focus in 2025 on supporting learners, facilitators and community members through ECD, MC and adolescent programming, language skill development using language labs, Anticipatory action (AA) plan to reduce learning loss, teachers’ capacity building, evidence generation and knowledge sharing with Sector-members to support the scale up. Under JRP 2025, thus the activities will follow the Education Sector's 3 Objectives: Access, Quality and Community Engagement while delivering the project, working in camps and host communities, with 6010 children (Girl - 4119, Boy - 1891), 13273 caregivers (Female - 6750, Male - 6523), 203 teachers/facilitators (Female - 175, Male - 28) and sector members.
The project will reach its goal through existing learning facilities (153) in four camps and two unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazila, ensuring continuation of ECD, MC and Adolescent programs (mainly for girls) under the ECW MYRP consortia, generating evidence using two Burmese language labs and ERICC Education Research project. IRC will have a special focus on the older adolescents, especially girls below 18 years old who are not eligible for livelihood activities and are not being able to mainstream in MC due to age and competency issues, will be supported with Alternative learning opportunities.
Thus, the overall project will include center based ECD intervention in host (20 CBLF, 600 learners) and camp (26 CBLFs, 1208 learners), MC in camps (12 LCs, 44 CBLFs, 1996 learners) and ALP for adolescents in camps (48 CBLFs, 2206 learners). In addition to that, teachers’ capacity building and parents/caregiver sensitization activities will be included for the parents of all targeted learners in both camp and host community.
Apart from the regular activities, through two Burmese language labs, IRC will ensure opportunities for the facilitators and learners to practice Burmese language to develop language skills as well as generate evidence for further scaling up the initiatives. IRC will also continue supporting children with disabilities, through screening using WG questionnaires, technical support from HI to identify specific cases and will ensure assistive devices. Considering the increased frequency of natural disasters, IRC in 2025 will focus on Anticipatory Action (AA) plan development to reduce learning loss as well as orient relevant stakeholders on DRR and emergency preparedness plan by Emergency Preparedness Team of IRC and safety materials will be provided at facilities. Based on the Facility risk assessment and GVB risk assessment conducted in 2024, IRC will take measures in 2025 to reduce the facility and GBV risks to ensure that children especially girls have safe learning opportunities.
To focus on the community members, there will be capacity building and sensitization activity/messaging which will focus on gender issues like girls’ education and drop out, early marriage prevention messages, parenting, and social cohesion between two communities. Even while designing projects, IRC will focus on the exact need of the two communities and will divide the activities accordingly to reduce tension between the two communities. IRC in 2024 is also planning to support teachers from the host community particularly in the areas of emergency responsive teaching learning for the host community, teacher and student wellbeing and sensitization.
The project will follow ISCG and education sector’s standard and guidelines during implementation and will share all required data/information agreed with sector as sector’s partner as well as ensure compliance with government law/policy and will work closely CiC, site management and education focal at camp level. To further support the education sector, IRC’s ERICC research project will generate more evidence in 2025 related to adolescent programming.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Since August 2017, an estimated 745,000 Rohingya refugees fled to Cox’s Bazar, increasing the total number of Rohingya refugees to 1,003,394 among which 52% are children (Gob UNHCR Population Factsheet, September 2024). IRC's 2022 report, "The State of Learning for Rohingya Refugee Children Affected by Displacement and Crisis in Bangladesh" notes the following barriers hampering quality education provision: 1) a lack of qualified teachers, with specific challenges in identifying qualified female Rohingya educators; 2) complex language issues, with divergent language of instruction and home (oral) language; 3) curriculum restrictions and changes, with the shift from the LCFA to the MC; and 4) COVID-19, with extended disruptions to education and a lack of learning continuity. IRC's assessments showed that 78% of children are non-readers, with only 22% showing emergent English literacy skills; over 32% of children have not developed any math skills, 67% are gaining foundational math skills, and only 20% of children being able to conduct simple subtraction problems correctly. Overall, the achievement gender gap was stark, with boys outperforming girls in all learning measures. For example, in literacy, the differences were statistically significant across all EGRA subtasks, with boys scoring, for example, 20 more correct letter sounds per minute than girls. In addition, children with disabilities are behind in literacy tasks, as well as in assessed social emotional learning dimensions. The roll out of Myanmar curriculum entered its second year in 2023 with all the grades from KG – Grade 10. It is also identified that due to the roll out of Myanmar curriculum many adolescents particularly girls are being out of the mainstream education a part of them have been supported by Accelerated Learning Program or Adolescents program. The year end assessment in June 2024 showed that boys achieved higher scores than girls across all subjects and mathematics had the lowest average score among the four subjects. In addition to that, comparing the two language, Burmese and English, learners performed better in English that Burmese. According to the Inter-Sector Needs Analysis (ISNA) – 2024, children from 22% households are completely not accessing education, referring in numbers - 53% (female) and 56% (Male) for age group 3 – 4, 25% (female) and 25% (Male) for age group 5 – 14, 92% (female) and 70% (Male) for age group 15 – 18. This shows that only 8% girls aged 15 – 18 are enrolled in education facilities resulting a huge gap to focus. The analysis also shows that the enrollment rate starts gradually decreasing from the age of 9 and above while the number of boys and girls not enrolled increases significantly from age 12 onwards. the Inter-Sector Needs Analysis (ISNA) – 2024, also identified that there are safety concerns among 7% of the learners and the concerns include fear of abduction (32%) and fear of armed group (21%) and communication issues (19%). While exploring the reason for not enrolling in educational facilities, it was found that menstrual age (9%), too old (15%) for education and child marriage (12%). According to one of the IRC’s studies in 2024 titled “Implementing the Myanmar Curriculum in Rohingya camps: overcoming challenges and barriers”, Teacher recruitment and retention are problematic, with high turnover rates among host community teachers (over 26% annually), while Rohingya teachers face language and subject knowledge gaps.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IRC is a global leader in Protection and Child Protection programming, and currently implements Protection Rule of Law, Women's Protection and Child Protection programming in Bangladesh. IRC's Education department draws on this expertise to ensure rigorous approaches to child safeguarding, codes of conduct, and improved safety and referrals for children in education programs. In 2024, IRC conducted facility risk and GvB risk assessment to identify issues to prioritize safety and dignity as well as ensure meaningful access. IRC will build on this assessment findings and take necessary actions in 2025. In addition to that, IRC has client satisfaction survey and client feedback and response mechanism (CFRM) which regularly generates data to guide the program to foster accountability as well as promote participation and empowerment. IRC will continue to strengthen approaches to ensuring safety and reducing gender-based violence in education programs. This will be accomplished through ongoing research efforts, which include gap analysis on safety and protection in education programs, with a potential focus to fund research into protection-based risks and barriers impacting school aged children. IRC will work with parents and caregivers to raise awareness on codes of conduct, violence at home, and reporting mechanisms for violence in schools and on the way to school which impacts children's learning and development. Particularly for the reporting mechanism for protection issues, IRC has multiple ways which includes protection focal person, hot line numbers and email address from the Ethics and Compliance Unit (ECU) where any kind of concerns and cases can be reported, and all the reports were addressed with high confidentiality.

2.3 - Age & Disability

IRC will cover all the age range from 3-18 through its programming in 2025 with ECD, MC and ALP programs. Only in the host community IRC will focus on age 4-6 to prepare the children for formal schooling. IRC always uses the WG questionnaire to assess the disability level of children (if any) and ensure that the children are getting adequate support to continue education including assistive devices as necessary. In 2024, IRC conducted facility risk assessments which included access issues to support accessibility of learning facilities. In addition to that, IRC is planning to procure 32 assistive devices for children with disabilities to ensure continuation of their learning. All the training modules are inclusive of disability so that facilitators can support the learners who needed. In addition to that, IRC has a very strong focus on parents and community members to raise awareness and enhance sensitization towards the access and quality education for the children, especially for adolescents’ girls. IRC seeks to provide equitable services based on need to all client populations. IRC's staff is 99% Bangladeshi, and facilitators are recruited from the refugee and hosting community. IRC ensures that all program targeting is needs based, and this begins with understanding the challenges and barriers facing learners and communities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to client populations is the cornerstone of all IRC's programming. IRC seeks to ensure all programs are based on participatory needs assessments, so that project design reflects the inputs of communities, including children. Ongoing monitoring seeks to improve engagement with children, whose voices are often overlooked in improving accountability to clients. Active mechanisms for engaging children, based on IRC's new Child Participation Guidelines (2022), will be employed so that child-friendly mechanisms are used to obtain diverse perspectives on design and implementation, and that safety risks facing children are understood and handled in an active and not passive manner. Not only children, but also parents also/caregivers and community members will be engaged in multiple ways throughout the project design and implementation through community consultations, testing of contents developed and agreeing on a delivery mechanism which best suits the needs of the community. In addition to that capacity building for the education committees, sensitization and parenting session with the parents/community members will be ongoing. IRC has its own Client feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) which receives weekly feedback from clients as well as share response to clients to meet their queries and it is done by IRCs MEAL team to ensure accountability. While making any project related decision or plan, IRC prioritize consulting with the clients, follow any needs assessment or data shared by education sector as well as IRCs internal research and evidence so that clients were selected according to the needs and gaps identified.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The IRC is committed to integrating PSEA into the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh by following a comprehensive framework, including the IRC’s safeguarding policy, the Core Humanitarian Standards, and the Minimum Operating Standards for PSEA (MOS-PSEA). The IRC will ensure that all staff and partners are fully informed of, and comply with, the organization’s codes of conduct and safeguarding policies. A rigorous hiring process will include screening for any history of SEA, including self-declarations by candidates. The Senior Management Team (SMT) will lead policy implementation, fostering a zero-tolerance culture with ongoing training on PSEA. Dedicated Safeguarding staff and PSEA Focal Points will work with the project team and PSEA Network to align PSEA. IRC will offer tailored, context-specific PSEA training for staff and partners, especially frontline workers, to build capacity in a “do no harm” approach and confidentiality. Community engagement is key: IRC will develop complaint and feedback mechanisms that are accessible, safe, and clear to beneficiaries at service points. A survivor-centered approach ensures survivors' safety, confidentiality, and access to support services without the obligation to formally report. Feedback mechanisms, including in-person reports, hotlines, and complaint boxes, will allow for reporting across genders and ages. IRC maintains a mandatory reporting policy for SEA involving minors under 18. As the reporting of SEA is a mandatory requirement, IRC is committed to notifying the donor and relevant stakeholders of any SEA concerns. High-quality investigations will be conducted with the intent to support survivors, hold perpetrators accountable, and strengthen prevention. Regular field visits, interviews, and focus groups will monitor implementation, share best practices, and improve program quality.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. IRC is actively gathering data on the impact of climate change events to better understand and address learning losses among students. Based on primary and secondary sources, this data analysis will provide a detailed view of how climate-related disruptions affect teachers and learners. By identifying these impacts, IRC aims to design a targeted Anticipatory Action (AA) framework in 2025, outlining proactive steps to safeguard education during climate events.The planned AA framework will include early interventions to protect education continuity, with specific support strategies for teachers and learners. These early actions will minimize learning loss and ensure that students can maintain their educational progress despite natural disasters. By developing these forward-looking measures, IRC is strengthening resilience in education systems, ensuring that learning remains uninterrupted and accessible despite the challenges posed by climate change.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. Under the ECW-funded program, IRC has designed comprehensive Disaster Risk Management (DRM) training for teachers and community members to enhance resilience within vulnerable communities. This DRM training will equip participants with a clear understanding of risks, local capacities, and practical mitigation measures to reduce the impact of disasters. Tailored to address the region's specific natural hazards and historical disaster impacts, the program incorporates DRM strategies to protect lives and the environment from adverse outcomes.A robust information management system has been developed to facilitate early warning dissemination and provide multi-hazard preparedness messages. This initiative aims to increase community awareness and equip frontline staff and volunteers with the knowledge and skills needed to implement effective disaster risk reduction (DRR) measures. Periodic community risk assessments and facility risk evaluations will further strengthen learning facilities, ensuring they remain operational and resilient during emergencies.In the event of a crisis, remote learning services will be reactivated to minimize learning disruption and prevent dropouts, helping maintain continuity in education. Additionally, community facilitators will receive orientation on referral mechanisms, enabling them to guide affected individuals to essential multi-sectoral services during emergencies.To ensure the project remains responsive to the needs of high-risk areas and effectively addresses the needs of affected populations, the IRC Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) team will work closely with the Education team to ensure project-specific preparedness work. This collaboration will guide project-specific actions and support efforts to secure additional emergency funding, ensuring a well-rounded and sustainable approach to disaster preparedness and response.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

As self-reliance refers to the ability to meet essential needs and enjoy their rights in a sustainable and dignified way, this education project does not directly support strengthening livelihood but rather prepare learners through educational activities to be able to participate in livelihood activities. It also contributes to understanding their rights to reduce vulnerabilities. The inclusion of protection activities in education supports awareness building about rights, specific awareness session targeting parent and caregivers helps them understand the importance of education for future development. The project also has a specific focus on resilience building mainly to recover from shock. This project has MHPSS volunteers to support facilitators and learners with their wellbeing. In addition to that, the project has emergency preparedness and response plans as well as working on Anticipatory Action (AA) plan to reduce the impact of climate change for recovering learning loss.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project activities are not directly related to the development initiatives for Cox's Bazar district. But it does contributing to the government policy of starting the early childhood development (ECD) initiatives from the age of 4. The project only includes ECD activities at host community level for the children of age 4- 6 from the remote areas and the location and children were selected with the support education officials considering the needs and gaps. While operating, all the activities are done in accordance with government policy and guidelines. Though it is not directly related to development initiatives but in the long run, it does contribute to the development initiatives.
