Project Summary

Lebanon has been struggling with multifaceted crises since the end of 2019, all of which had an enormous impact on the education sector. Prominently, an increasing number of students are dropping out of school. Although alarming, this is not surprising, as the majority of the population is not able to secure their basic needs as 82% of them are living in multidimensional poverty (ESCWA). Families are unable to secure their children’s basic educational necessities like transportation and stationery. Hence, caregivers are resorting to sending their “children to work in often dangerous and hazardous conditions, marrying off their young daughters or selling their belongings” (UNICEF, 2021). Even if vulnerable families can afford their children’s tuition fees, the lack of support at schools increases the risk of low-performing students dropping out. The shift to remote learning has only pushed already academically-struggling children further behind their peers.
Teachers in Lebanon faced unprecedented challenges over the past year as well. In 2020, many teachers lost their jobs or were asked to work part-time, due to limited funding and the deteriorating economic situation. The economic crisis has resulted in acute losses in the value of public teachers’ salaries as they are paid in Lebanese Lira, which has lost most of its value making a teacher’s salary now worth between 1/2USD/hour. This, coupled with delays in salary payments, has resulted in frequent teacher strikes and consequently lost learning time.
Moreover, Lebanon’s deepening fuel crisis has led to chronic shortages of fuel across the country and is threatening children’s access to quality education. The cost of transportation has been identified as the greatest challenge for both students and teachers to access schools due to difficulty to secure fuel, with the cost of transportation rising by more than LBP40,000/ child, and the average cost of transportation/ child/ month, rising to LBP175,192.To put things into perspective, the minimum wage still stands at LBP 675,000 per month.
Hence, ISWA is proposing to implement a holistic back-to-school campaign in Saida city to address the increase in school drop-out rates.The city is known for its poverty hotspots that coincide with the largest Palestinian refugee camp as well as old Sidon neighborhood. The campaign, which will be launched at the beginning of the upcoming academic year, will include the provision of age- and gender-appropriate school kits to 3500 students enrolled in public schools along with transportation and daily snacks (4 days/week). In addition to that, 250 teachers in public schools will be provided with transportation costs (paid once) to help decrease some of the financial burdens they are facing. Moreover, 372 students in Saida city will be selected to be enrolled in a remedial and PSS program based on needs evaluated by the outreach program that will be conducted at the beginning of the project. The aim of this component is to provide students with catch-up sessions to decrease their learning gaps, minimizing the risk of dropping out due to underperformance. The remedial sessions covering the core subjects will be provided over the course of 3 months, 4 times/week.Targeted students will benefit from age-appropriate stationery kits, along with daily transportation, and daily snacks. The PSS sessions, which will be offered once/week for each group, will introduce healthy behavioral changes by providing the 372 students with support to face their day-to-day challenges, hence enhancing their resilience and improving their mental health.
ISWA(A.D. No 574)has track record for implementing such programs through its educational subsidiary, Vision Academy. Partnering with numerous organization like UNESCO, Human Appeal, Islamic Relief and Save the Children and others, ISWA has been conducting remedial and PSS programs since 2018. For this project, ISWA will utilize 4 of its centers containing 43 classrooms.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Under the education sector, this project is in line with the 1st objective as it aims to procure daily snacks to 3872 vulnerable Syrian, Lebanese and Palestinian students in South Lebanon, 372 of whom will be enrolled in a remedial and PSS program (5 days/ week over the course of 3 months). Also, it is in line with 2nd strategic objective, as the 3872 students will be provided with basic learning supplies to access education as well as daily snacks, and transportation support. Moreover, the transportation support provided to the teachers will facilitate their commute to their duty, allowing a smooth progression of the academic year. Thus, by implementing this project, we are supporting them by ensuring their access to quality education, ultimately combating poverty by equipping them with the skills and knowledge they require to move on to stable employment as adults. In this way, we can effectively build the capacity of the next generation to weather the current crisis and undertake positive development in the future.Moreover, this project is in line with the second objective under the protection sector, as the students will be provided with PSS sessions once a week over the course of three months aiming to decrease the rate of violence, aggression, exploitation, and abuse among children, a rate that has been on rising since the beginning of the crisis. Therefore, allowing the students to learn positive coping mechanisms, hence improving their mental health and wellbeing.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ISWA has its own gender, protection, and safeguarding policies that abide by the core humanitarian standards. Moreover, ISWA staff are always trained on these policies and are entitled of spreading awareness among the beneficiaries which in return will encourage them to later talk about it. A complaint/Feeback mechanism is set in place where any complaint, especially those with sensitive content will be tackled immediately.Among ISWA’s child protection policies, that we take to address gender, the inclusion of children with disability or critical medical conditions are the following:- Equal opportunities for accessing our services- Zero tolerance for any kind of bullying and discrimination, whether coming from staff or from students towards each other.- Creating an enabling environment by trying to eliminate the barriers (the centers are student-friendly)- Awareness and integration of inclusion principles within the policies and implemented activities

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ISWA adheres to the 9 commitments of the core humanitarian standards, ensures beneficiaries’ access to information, and allows their involvement in decisions that affect them. Hence, to ensure APP, ISWA will:- Establish two channels for complaint/feedback (out of them one is anonymous). Along with a complaint box, a hotline is established for collecting the student’s caregiver’s feedback throughout the project- Organize focus groups with the students to collect their feedback.- Conduct staff surveys to elicit their feedback on the program.- Organize teacher-parent conference meetings to inquire regarding their feedback on the project and following their children’s progress.- Take into consideration feedback's outputs to identify areas of improvement- Ensure beneficiaries access to information by providing them with adequate information regarding the program's outcomes- Monitor attendance so that any issues related to the drop in attendance are addressed in a timely manner
