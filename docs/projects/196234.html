Project Summary


This project seeks to provide critical and Lifesaving Basic Services to Vulnerable Populations in hard to reach areas especially in Akobo, Pibor, Tambura, and Tonj East where CIDO has been implementing other emergencies interventions. CIDO seeks to provide timely and tailored Shelter and NFIs support to vulnerable targeted beneficiaries through static response and presence. This is in response to early conflict displacement that forced many of the target population to either flee to neighboring settlement areas,
POCs or neighboring countries and are now returning to settle back in their original homes and are in need of immediate ES/NFI support and or resilience support. The host communities in these areas are getting it hard to cope with the number of returnees who are in need of their support and care with the little resources that they have. CIDO will provide lifesaving shelter and NFIs in-kind support in response to specific needs of 40,752 IDPs, returnees and conflict affected populations. Deep field locations with recent
displacements having populations in dire need will be the priority of CIDO in the distribution of life-saving S/NFI. CIDO will consider the specific needs, concerns and priorities of women and girls, men and boys of different age and with specific needs such as people with disability, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerabilities with the aim of incorporating protection principles during the project implementation. As a short-term intervention, the impact on the population and on the contextual dynamics is sensitive and delicate balances are at play, for this reason the team will ensure that all accountability processes to the affected population are carefully followed. The intervention must be clearly defined and protection concerns taken into consideration so that distributions respect community dynamics, do no harm and are conducted in a dignified and meaningful way. Contextual feedback mechanisms will be established in
line with the distribution method and the location dynamics. Beneficiaries will be identified using approved South Sudan SNFIs cluster criteria of selection. In addition, CIDO will endeavor to give priority to child-headed households, women, persons with disability, the elderly and other vulnerable people in the community who will benefit from Emergency Shelter and NFI distribution. CIDO will aspire to conduct joint missions including joint assessment sand responses when needed in order to better respond to the complex needs of the beneficiaries, pull resources together and minimize overlap. This project will focus
on cluster objective 1, 2, 3 and 4 and will include the following activities:
• Needs analysis of Shelter and NFIs needs
• Beneficiary selection based on the most critical needs
• Distribution of emergency shelter/NFI to population in need
• Rapid monitoring and Post distribution monitoring surveys
In all the activities, Ebola mitigation, COVID-19 Protocols and SOPs will be observed.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview, shows that people’s humanitarian needs in South Sudan continue to be mainly driven by years of conflict, increasing underlying vulnerability and which has been further compounded by the impacts of climate change and pandemics like Ebola and COVID 19. This compounded by severe flooding in 2022, with 900,000 people reported as affected between April and November 2022. Floodwaters have destroyed critical infrastructure and have limited humanitarian access to heavily affected populations. Barriers to movement, imposed by flooding and insecurity, have also limited the ability of vulnerable groups in affected areas, particularly women, to implement traditional coping strategies already weakened coping mechanisms and exacerbating vulnerability. In 2022, ongoing sub-national conflict had major impact on people, resulting in mass displacement, overcrowding and loss of access to markets andbasic services . There are reports of negative coping strategies as a result of homelessness. According to an Inter-Agency Rapid Need Assessment (IRNA) conducted on October 3rd, 2022 by humanitarian agencies in Akobo East including CIDO, in all the four payams (Bilkey, Dengjok, Gakdong and Nyandit) were displaced away from their homes. Part of the findings are that 4,131 HHs of the population are affected. The situation requires immediate humanitarian response to provide lifesaving SNFI assistance. According to REACH Humanitarian Situation Monitoring in Warrap (March 2022 ), in Tonj East, KIs in 56% of assessed settlements where the presence of IDPs was reported, reported that more than half of IDPs were living in the open without shelter (40% in December). Conflict dynamics appeared more pronounced in Greater Tonj than in the rest of the state in 2022. COVID-19, fear of Ebola virus , sub-national violence, and flooding continue to strain access to the very limited basic services and disrupted local markets in Pibor, Tonj East. Sub-national violence in Pibor, Tonj East resulted in relocation of humanitarian partners, looting of lifesaving supplies and targeting of humanitarian workers based on ethnic identities. The humanitarian country team in South Sudan has since recommended immediate humanitarian response scale in the counties of Tonj Eastand Pibor. Findings from International Organization for Migration (IOM) suggest that episodes of localized conflict in Pibor County resulted in widespread displacement across the county in 2022. Pibor has been marred by protracted shocks since 2012; marginalization; conflict and floods 2021/ 2022; FRC ‘famine likely’ classification in 2022; drought in 2022/ 2023; desperation cattle-raiding. Despite improvements in shelterconditions for internally displaced persons (IDPs) across the state, IDPs in flood affected counties in August reportedly continued to mainly live in improvised shelter types. According to OCHA South Sudan, Flash Updates October 2022, previously displaced people by fighting in Tambura County are still struggling to access services. Many of the people displaced who had fled to neighboring Western Bahr el Ghazal State are beginning to return back to their original homes. An estimated 45,000 people fled to Ezo County,partners are reporting limited resources with key ES/NFI gaps highlighted.

This project proposes to intervene in multiple locations and will use CVA in locations where the intervention is feasible and in-kind assistance where CVA is not feasible. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CIDO will establish a Complaint and Feedback mechanism and establish Helpdesk during distributions. There will be Community communication andengagement (CCE) before the project begins to harmonise the expectations ofthe community in regards to the project activities vs the available services.During CCE , consultations will be made with communities to establishcommittees which will oversee the distribution, GBV issues, and AAP issues.The committee on AAP will ensure that voices of the most vulnerable are heardincluding people with disability who may be faced with access challenges. Thecommittee will ensure that location of static services like legal clinics does notbar access to PWDs .The team will be careful to provide opportunity to peopleliving with disability, women and girls to air their views through separate FGDsand ensure that they are included in various leadership roles like being part ofthe committees. Feedback from People with Disability will be used to tailorservices to meet their most critical needs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) is committed toensuring that frontline staff engage in timely and proper CommunityCommunication and Engagement (CCE) while being guided by theAccountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through theproject implementation. CIDO will conduct entry and exit meetings prior to theend of the project implementation to have discussions with not only communityleaders but also directly with all targeted beneficiaries who are women, mengirls and boys to capture their interests and recommendations and asses thevulnerability. In addition, CIDO will conduct awareness sessions on AAP rightsand how to use the established Complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs).The AAP committee composed of community members representing men,women, elderly, youth , people with disability will be established to ensurecommunities rights and dignity is respected by all stakeholders. There will be anopen-door policy, complaints, response, and feedback mechanism (CRFM)boxes, hotline and CRFM desks set up at each distribution site wherebeneficiaries will be given opportunity to air their feedback to the project teamthrough using both leaders and open-door policy. The hotline will be linkeddirectly to the management. This will facilitate Communication, transparency,and Community full engagement.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During AAP sessions , CIDO team and committee on AAP and GBV will provideinformation on PSEA to all beneficiaries including how to identify and reportPSEA cases. The beneficiaries will be made aware that vendors of CVI, CIDOcontractors, CIDO staff and volunteers are all required to sign codes of conductand commit to protect beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse. IECmaterials on PSEA will be sourced from the Lead (UNFPA) and translated anddistributed in project locations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CIDO team will compile feedback from the commitees and beneficiaries andpresent to the management in Juba who will confirm the grievances andcompliments . Feedback on how the complaints have been addressed on adaily basis on ground will also be shared with the management and will beused to define further actions to improve service delivery. There will be projectreview meetings where project staff and management will analyse theusefulness of the CRFM , information gathered and how the system can bemade better to encourage all beneficiaries to use it freely. Feedback frombeneficiaries will be used to improve programming and adjust weaker areasaccordingly.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CIDO will ensure all staff are trained on data management and security. Themanagement will provide lockable safe boxes in all field locations for keepingconfidential files and encourage the use of passwords to protect soft copies of data.CIDO will conduct rapid HLP assessments with focus on the immediate shelter andprotection needs of affected people and the potential risks they face, including theiraccess to different shelter types, security of tenure, including risk of forced eviction.Assessments at this stage may also be able to get an overview of the level ofdamaged/destroyed HLP and any (secondary) occupation trends. This will defineways of engaging the local authority and raising awareness on HLP rights amongcrisis-affected populations, including the services available to support claiming suchrights (e.g., legal assistance, dispute resolution and shelter assistance) and local rulesand regulations on HLP matters (e.g., rental/lease agreements). CIDO will also consultwith all groups within affected populations to identify most effective and appropriatemethods of awareness-raising. CIDO will be guided by the cluster safety audit whilemainstreaming protection, conflict sensitivity and AAP. CIDO in close collaborationwith Chiefs, AAP committees and Protection partners will conduct HLP messagingwhile at the same time addressing existing and emerging HLP issues throughoutproject implementation in line with the cluster humanitarian shelter and land rights duediligence guidelines for S/NFI actors in South Sudan to ensure smooth intervention.Community engagement and communication including with the local leadership in allaspects of the project will mitigate external interference likely to spark conflict. CIDOwill adhere to principals of protection and the Do no Harm policy while engaging withall stakeholders .CIDO will work closely with county security organs and local authorityto prioritize safety of staff, lives and property of community members through localdialogues and peaceful negotiations. To promote beneficiary access to CRFMmechanisms, CIDO Project team will use Community Communication andEngagement (CCE) strategies while being guided by the Accountability to the AffectedPopulation (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will put inplace AAP committees in all project locations to monitor and promote rights ofbeneficiaries as far as access to quality services is concerned. To mitigate the risk ofexplosive hazards in the project locations, CIDO will coordinate with Mine Actionpartners through sharing data in subnational and national cluster meetings and usetheir expert recommendations to advise on safety of both beneficiaries and staff.
