Project Summary

The project will target 41,546 Rohingya individuals to ensure access to and use of safe water and sanitation facilities as well as adopting improved hygiene practices. Save the Children (SC)'s WASH programming is basically of institutional focused, such as, SC integrates WASH services into learning centers, health posts/centers, child/girls friendly spaces, and several facility points. At community level intervention, SC only targets those blocks/areas of the camps, from which people usually used to visit our health posts and get services.

Needs

Surveys by REACH-UNICEF and Needs and Population Monitoring (NPM) reveal the following:- 27,600 refugees are in very high need of water (3.2% of the overall Rohingya population), and 299,000 refugees are in high need of water (34.5% of the overall Rohingya population).- 6,300 refugees are in very high need of sanitation (0.7% of the overall Rohingya population), and 37,200 refugees are in high need of sanitation (4.3% of the overall Rohingya population). - 1,400 refugees are in very high need of hygiene (0.2% of the overall Rohingya population), and 17,400 refugees are in high need of hygiene (2% of the overall Rohingya population).Key priorities for the WASH response are to ensure that a sufficient number of functioning water points, latrines, and bathing facilities are available in the camps. Beyond these, the WASH analysis (ACAPS, March 2019) further identifies the following priorities: - Women report being harassed by men during water collection. The use of latrines and bathing spaces is associated with safety concerns as well, particularly for women and children. Factors influencing a sense of insecurity include lack of gender separation, lack of security at night, and facilities being in unsafe locations.- Rohingya refugees face a variety of problems when accessing water points, latrines, and bathing facilities. This shows that even if Sphere standards are met in terms of the number of WASH facilities, access to them is not guaranteed. For water points, access issues include waiting times, distance to water points, and water points not functioning. For latrines and bathing facilities, they include lack of lighting, lack of gender separation, and facilities not functioning. Access is a concern particularly for people with disabilities. Based on the need analysis and considering sector priorities, following are key strategies for WASH programming: covering all institutional facilities (Health, education, CP, Nutrition), hygiene promotion, behavior change intervention and monitoring at community (for refugees and host communities), considering equitable access for vulnerable people (children, people with disabilities, PLW, old aged) in existing facilities at communities and institutions, durable solutions like water supply system/network using renewable energy, fecal sludge management unit, female friendly sanitation spaces, child participation in planning and monitoring programme, and provision for menstrual hygiene management. Access pathway, lighting system at WASH facilities including the male: female latrine ration require improvement to increase access including the appropriate space for the special need of children, women, PLW, people leaving with disability. Further to ensure the long term regular operation of the latrines; there is a need of decentralized Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) unit and continuing desludging activities throughout the year. Engagement of the beneficiaries/community particularly children, women, girls and old age person from planning and operation is utmost important for sustainable operation. Furthermore, community based solid waste management is one of the needs observed at camps. Hygiene promotion activities need to intensify with better access to hygiene product to improve the hygiene practice contributing towards the behavior change and to increase health assistance seeking behavior. MHM campaign is required among the women and girls with reproductive age girls. Skill of women and adolescent girls need to enhance on production of the reusable menstrual pad for sustainable access to those requirements. Further there is need of water supply system with distribution network to provide adequate and safe water to the population, closer to household and other facilities in camps. Special consideration need to provide to GBV, protection and equity during design and implementation of the WASH programme.

Activities or outputs

a) Upgradation of existing water supply systemsb) Operation and maintenance of existing water supply systemsc) Construction, improvement and rehabilitation to durable, gender segregated latrinesd) Construction, improvement and rehabilitation to bathing units with female friendly sanitation facilities at community levele) Desludging of latrine pit/septic tankf) Distribution of hygiene and dignity kits, top-up and latrine cleaning kits and water purification tabletsg) Installation and repairs of solar lights to ensure safe access to latrines and water pointsh) Construction, operation and maintenance of feceal sludge treatment plant (FSTP), DEWATs unit, solid waste managementi) Hygiene promotion activities including menstrual hygiene management, children’s session, campaigns and day observations

Key project/performance indicators

# of beneficiaries have access to adequate, safe and functional access of water and sanitation services: Target: 41,546 (48% female, 52% male); # of beneficiaries benefitted from safe fecal sludge and solid waste management facilities established and operationalized: Target: 11,313 (48% male, 52% female); # of WASH Committees established and formed to manage and maintain WASH facilities: Target: 170 committees (members: 510 male, 300 female); # of beneficiaries adopted at least 3 improved hygiene practices out of key 5 critical practices: Target: 41,546 (48% female, 52% male); # of beneficiaries satisfied with distributed materials: Target: 13,991 (48% female, 52% male)
