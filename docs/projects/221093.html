Project Summary

The Integrated Emergency Nutrition Response Project aims to address critical levels of malnutrition among vulnerable populations in South Sudan through a coordinated, multi-sectoral approach. With escalating food insecurity, conflict, and limited access to healthcare, many communities are at heightened risk of acute malnutrition and related health complications. This project will deploy an emergency response to provide lifesaving nutrition support while simultaneously addressing underlying factors through water, sanitation, health, agriculture, and education interventions. The initiative will mobilize key sectors to strengthen community resilience, improve food security, and ensure sustainable access to essential services, targeting the immediate and long-term health needs of children under five, pregnant and lactating women, and other at-risk groups.

1. Needs

Malnutrition continues to be a significant public health concern in South Sudan. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition (IPC-AMN) projects that between July 2024 and June 2025, approximately 1.65 million children aged 6 to 59 months will suffer from acute malnutrition, necessitating immediate nutritional interventions. This includes an estimated 480,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.17 million cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). The analysis, derived from Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) surveys, the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS), program admission trends, and routine morbidity data, indicates that about 72% of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity, and Warrap.c.Recent findings from the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Round 30 reveal critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in several South Sudanese counties. Aweil South reports a GAM rate of 26.0%, and Panyijar stands at 20.1%, both indicating severe acute malnutrition. Other counties, including Rumbek East (9.9%), Rumbek North (8.2%), and Yirol West (6.9%), also face significant GAM rates. Notably, Yirol West has shown substantial improvement from the previous year's GAM rate of 15.1%, as reported in the 2023 SMART survey.The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) reflects these disparities. Aweil Southand Pnayijar are classified in Phase 4 (Emergency) for both the first and second projections of 2024. While Rumbek East, Rumbek North, and Yirol West are in Phase 2 (Stressed) in the first projection, Rumbek North and Yirol West are expected to deteriorate to Phase 3 (Crisis) by the second projection, indicating worsening conditions in these areas.In Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBEG) Lakes and Unity State, malnutrition among children under five and pregnant and lactating women has reached critical levels, signaling a worsening crisis. These counties face ongoing challenges from intercommunal conflicts and recurring floods, which disrupt stability and food production, leaving families struggling to secure essential nutrition. The rainy season compounds these difficulties, as severe floods further delay the delivery of emergency nutrition services, exacerbating malnutrition rates among young children and mothers who already have limited access to adequate food and healthcare in these vulnerable communities.Given the escalating food insecurity across South Sudan, an urgent and comprehensive increase in multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance is crucial to halt further deterioration. This response should encompass both nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions, essential for preserving lives and protecting livelihoods in areas experiencing critical food insecurity ( priority 1,2&3 ). Addressing malnutrition-related illness and mortality is imperative in regions facing high malnutrition rates, as poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices exacerbate these conditions. Data from FSNMS Round 30 highlights the low coverage of minimum acceptable diets, with Lakes and NBEG states reaching only 4.4% and 11% respectively, reflecting a severe lack of adequate nutrition for children and raising their vulnerability to malnutrition.Aggravating factors such as poor dietary diversity and limited feeding practices further intensify the crisis. Only 14.4% of children in Lakes and 23.1% in NBEG consume a minimally varied diet, depriving them of essential nutrients needed for growth and development. Severe food insecurity, high morbidity from diseases like malaria, fever, diarrhea, and acute respiratory infections (ARI), combined with inadequate Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) conditions, elevate the risk of disease outbreaks. 2. Project activities

To combat malnutrition effectively, the project will implement OTP and TSFP across designated counties, adhering to revised WHO guidelines. The community services component aims to empower local populations by raising awareness about malnutrition and available CMAM services. Participatory problem-solving discussions are used to identify barriers to accessing care, strengthen early case detection, and improve referral systems. Health education and counseling are central to fostering positive health behaviors, supported by locally adapted educational materials. Engaging key stakeholders in planning and implementation reinforces community ownership. Follow-up visits for at-risk cases are conducted through Boma Health Committees (BHCs), enhancing accountability and sustainability. MUAC is extensively utilized as a reliable tool for assessing nutritional risk in children aged six weeks to 59 months, with specific thresholds guiding interventions SAM and MAM. Family MUAC programs empower caregivers to detect malnutrition early, while community-based and passive screenings at distribution sites ensure comprehensive coverage. Project activities prioritize managing acute malnutrition and addressing its root causes through nutrition-sensitive strategies. Active community-based screenings, home visits by CNVs, and passive screenings using MUAC and Weight-for-Height (WFH) measurements ensure effective case identification. Health facilities provide in-depth assessments by multidisciplinary teams, covering clinical conditions, breastfeeding adequacy, and maternal well-being. The program aims to support 121,918 primary caregivers with breastfeeding assessments, psychosocial support, and health education, while providing high-quality nutritional treatment to 28,592 children under five with SAM, 14,593 children under five with MAM, and 101,795 pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW).Capacity building through on-the-job training equips nutrition assistants for efficient service delivery, while enhanced tools and reporting systems across 61 nutrition sites bolster operational efficiency. Regular joint supervision and collaboration with donors and health departments ensure accountability. Partnerships with organizations such as WFP and UNICEF support complementary initiatives, including Vitamin A supplementation, deworming campaigns, and the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) survey. These efforts strengthen the referral system, health education, and distribution of critical supplies, ensuring a seamless and integrated response to malnutrition.Beyond immediate interventions, the project tackles the root causes of malnutrition through nutrition-sensitive activities that promote sustainable development and resilience. Community-based agricultural programs enhance food availability, dietary diversity, and local food production. Social safety nets, including food aid and cash assistance, safeguard vulnerable families from economic shocks. Early Childhood Development (ECD) initiatives and maternal mental health services foster holistic well-being and nurturing caregiving environments.Women’s empowerment programs address socio-economic disparities, promoting informed decision-making on health and nutrition while preventing Gender-Based Violence (GBV). Child protection services ensure safe environments for children’s growth and development, while Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) initiatives reduce disease risks through improved access to clean water and sanitation. Comprehensive health and family planning services further improve maternal and child health outcomes. School-based nutrition education integrates healthy lifestyle promotion among children and adolescents, reinforcing the multi-faceted approach of the project.This integrated approach creates a robust environment that addresses both and underline causes of malnutrition. By combining immediate nutritional interventions with long-term strategies for resilience.
