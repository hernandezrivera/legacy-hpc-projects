Project Summary


Burundi is estimated to have an annual cholera incidence rate of 4 per 1000, experiencing 19,943 cases with 758 deaths (Case Fatality Rate 3.8%) annually and around 4.9 million people living in areas at risk of cholera. Outbreaks have occurred every year since 1978, with the worst outbreaks reported in 2011 and 2013. The outbreaks recorded in 2017/2018/2019 have been primarily reported in the provinces of Cibitoke, Bubanza, Rumonge, Bujumbura and Makamba.
Burundi recorded the first case of cholera in 1978. Since then and every year, it has been reported resurgences of cases in the provinces along the Lake Tanganyika (also called the Imbo plain). As of July 2019, Burundi has already declared two cholera outbreaks with the latest still on-going and occurring during the dry season. This outbreak is a critical concern since it is not contained yet and new hotspots are still being identified. In the end last year and beginning of 2022, Burundi has recorded a few cases of cholera and several suspected cases, the WASH sector has provided assistance to stop the spread and currently the situation remains under surveillance
The main objective of the project is to prevent and respond to outbreaks of infectious diseases through the provision of health and WASH services complemented with activities related to risk communication and community engagement through hygiene promotion. It proposes to build community resilience and address these issues through a coordinated and holistic response to affected communities including health facilities and schools. The proposed project aims to significantly reduce the risks associated with the spread of waterborne disease outbreaks by 1) providing coordinated and tailored WASH assistance that takes into account the different vulnerabilities of the affected people and 2) providing access to WASH services in selected areas that take into account the geographic realities and needs of both the displaced and the host population. Through this approach, the risks of waterborne diseases and poor hygiene (e.g. diarrhea and cholera) as well as malnutrition will be minimized.
