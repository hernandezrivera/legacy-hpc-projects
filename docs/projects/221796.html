Project Summary

The proposed project aims to strengthen the effectiveness of CUAMM's integrated humanitarian nutrition intervention in Cabo Delgado by
- Supporting availability and access to the comprehensive treatment of acute malnutrition, both MAM and SAM, for under 5 children and PLWs;
- Timely identification and referral of malnutrition cases through existing programmes (i.e. Integrated Mobile Brigades);
- Supporting the integration of the acute malnutrition treatment and any related medical complications in existing health system.
CUAMM will adopt a human rights-based and people-centred approach, implementing tailor-made and flexible activities to respond to the most acute needs of the population.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The proposed project aligns with recent developments in Cabo Delgado's humanitarian situation, where conflict and insecurity continue to impact millions. As of mid-2024, an estimated 2.8 million people in northern Mozambique are affected by the crisis, with 2.3 million in need of urgent humanitarian support. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) remain a significant concern, numbering around 1.3 million across the region. While returns to origin districts have increased, many returnees face inadequate living conditions, exacerbated by lingering insecurity and lack of access to basic services.Food insecurity remains critical, as aid covers only a fraction of the population's needs. Over 570,000 children under five face moderate acute malnutrition, and 256,000 suffer from severe acute malnutrition, compounded by high stunting rates among displaced and host children. Additionally, cholera outbreaks and limited health services worsen vulnerabilities. Violations of international humanitarian law (IHL), including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and forced child recruitment, persist as significant threats.The intervention proposes an integrated approach towards nutrition aiming at addressing the most urgent needs of the most vulnerable categories, i.e. children U5 and PLWs. Thanks to this proposal, CUAMM will reach more than 12.800 beneficiaries, disaggregated asfollows: SADD Age: - children (<18 years): almost 5.000 - Adult: around 7.800 of which almost 2.700 PLWsGender: - Male: around 4.800- Female: almost 8.000. Considering the status of the beneficiaries, the present intervention targets: - IDPs: around 5.400; - Host communities: around 7.300. Due to the complexity of the humanitarian crisis in Cabo Delgado, CUAMM considers it fundamental to direct its activities towards all people in need, regardless of their status, paying particular attention to their level of vulnerability.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will be in line with the cluster's objectives and will implement the following main activities : - support the improvement of mobile integrated nutrition screening brigades; - Support the improvement of integrated community case management; - Support the strengthening of the provision of micronutrient supplementation; - Support for screening, referral and treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). Service delivery will be improved through technical guidance and supportive supervision, while monitoring will be through an improved data collection and analysis system to facilitate informed decision-making in order to improve the current situation.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CUAMM designs its projects in close cooperation with local authorities to ensure buy-in and active participation of local actors. Project activities are implemented through participatory approaches, ensuring that community members and beneficiaries are actively involved in the development and implementation of every project components, thus ensuring that their needs are effectively met. Prior to theimplementation of all activities and regularly throughout the project, information meetings will be promoted at community level and with government services to ensure support and accountability. CUAMM, in coordination with the Cluster, will promote the establishment of effective project community feedbacks and complaints mechanisms, enabling beneficiaries and community members to provide feedbacks on CUAMM activities in a safe, non-threatening and accessible manner. Community feedbacks and complaints could be collected at service delivery points through focus group discussions with beneficiaries and community members. In addition, CUAMMwill continue to promote access to 'Linha Verde' as an effective inter-agency complaint, feedback and information hotline, including for sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA).

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project's activities are designed to ensure equitable and effective access to assistance by implementing integrated interventions that address both the demand and supply sides of service delivery. On the demand side, CUAMM will assess and address the specific barriers faced by different population groups in accessing humanitarian assistance. Through consultations with beneficiaries and local authorities, CUAMM will develop and implement practical, locally accepted solutions. Community awareness initiatives, carried out in collaboration with local civil society organizations (CSOs) and activists, will focus on fostering inclusive attitudes, particularly toward gender equality and disability rights. These initiatives aim to ensure that women and girls are informed about the services available at operational centers, enhancing their ability to access essential care and support. On the supply side, CUAMM will work with local authorities and relevant humanitarian clusters to strengthen referral and follow-up systems, ensuring critical and complicated cases receive appropriate specialized care. In collaboration with CSOs and community activists, CUAMM will develop accessible information, education, and communication (IEC) materials and lead inclusive awareness campaigns. These campaigns will promote active participation by disadvantaged groups, including women, girls, individuals with disabilities, and the elderly. Recognizing the systemic inequalities faced by women and elderly persons, CUAMM will implement targeted measures to facilitate their engagement in project activities. This includes creating enabling environments and providing specific support to empower these groups to actively participate in and benefit from the interventions.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

CUAMM conducts comprehensive analyses to identify barriers, challenges, and constraints hindering equitable access to humanitarian services across various demographic groups. Particular emphasis is placed on understanding the specific obstacles faced by individuals with disabilities (PwDs). To guide its efforts, CUAMM will develop a protection map, which incorporates community feedback and assessments from other partners and institutions. This tool will help pinpoint vulnerable groups and high-risk situations that may impede intervention outcomes, enabling a strategic allocation of resources and focus on priority areas. The protection map is instrumental in avoiding resource mismanagement and maintaining a results-oriented approach. It highlights areas requiring immediate attention while aligning with CUAMM’s broader strategy of delivering targeted assistance. In implementing these initiatives, CUAMM adopts a human rights-based and people-centered approach. This framework ensures that the specific needs of individuals with disabilities—across all demographic categories including women, men, girls, boys, and the elderly—are addressed effectively. Such a strategy fosters inclusivity, dignity, and equity in delivering humanitarian services while reinforcing the broader goals of protection and empowerment for marginalized groups.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed program incorporates environmentally responsible practices to minimize its ecological footprint while delivering essential services. This includes the following key measures: Safe Disposal of Consumables: Nutrition workers and community nutrition workers will receive training on the proper disposal of consumables, with a particular focus on personal protective equipment (PPE). This ensures that waste does not pose an environmental hazard or health risk. Community Awareness Campaigns: The program emphasizes education on environmental and personal hygiene, sanitation, and the prevention and control of disease outbreaks. These campaigns will target both host and displaced communities, fostering community-wide practices that contribute to a cleaner, safer environment. Reduced Fuel Consumption: CUAMM will optimize transportation logistics during project implementation. By coordinating vehicle movements and reducing redundant trips, the program aims to decrease fuel usage, thus lowering greenhouse gas emissions. Fuel Management and Monitoring: Stringent monitoring protocols will be established for fuel storage and handling to prevent leaks, protecting the surrounding environment and communities from contamination risks. Safe Handling of Medical Waste: Coordination with local health authorities will ensure that hazardous waste, including materials from drug store cleanouts, is safely disposed of. This prevents harmful residues from contaminating water sources, agricultural land, or livestock feeding areas. Field Cleanup Exercises: Regular cleanups will be conducted to ensure that no waste is left behind in operational areas, reducing potential environmental impacts and promoting sustainability. By integrating these practices, CUAMM not only addresses immediate humanitarian needs but also contributes to longer-term environmental stewardship, enhancing the resilience of both natural ecosystems and the affected communities.
