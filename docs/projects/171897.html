Project Summary

The project refers to the following strategic and cluster objectives:
SO1. Provide emergency and time-critical assistance and ensure access to basic essential services, to XX million people affected by the conflict, exacerbated by COVID-19.
PCO1. Ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to people with specific needs.
and
SO3. Ensure an implementation of humanitarian exit strategy in GCA from 2021-2023.
PCO3. Improve resilience of conflict-affected people and system of service provision.
PIN will work with partners to provide free psychosocial support to the most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in the most underserved areas. Such support will focus on the most vulnerable based on analysis of individual compounded (through age, gender and diversity-sensitive assessments engaging women, girls, boys, men, people with disability…) with contextual vulnerabilities (most underserved areas with unmet needs). PIN will in particular, and with partners, review needs which might traditionally be overlooked and need to be addressed still with emergency – including in particular from gender and age lenses (e.g. engaging men and boys and addressing toxic masculinities). Access to services will be ensured through the activation of mobile teams of psychologists and social workers to the conflict zone, PSS hotline, group activities with special outreach and individual support.
PIN will also work to build capacity of PSS practitioners and prepare sustainability of access to essential PSS services in underserved through community-based approaches and enhanced coordination and referral systems. PIN will coordinate with communities, local social and psychological services and partners to prioritise gaps in service provision in the most underserved (isolated) areas. PIN tested a sound cooperation on PSS provision with a local NGO partners, a localised approach that PIN aims to expand and compliment with other community-based solutions. To keep offering support to the affected population and work towards exit strategy solutions, PIN together with selected NGO partners and community structures will provide PSS along LoC. PIN will also support the realisation by community interest groups of inclusive initiatives for community’s social cohesion and resilience.
Throughout its action, PIN will pay special attention to Accountability to the affected population. PIN will do so by actively engaging women, girls, boys and men, individuals in their diversity without discrimination (with due regard to underlying inequalities) in the identification and prioritization of their needs through extensive consultations and facilitation of community actions. PIN will also strengthen its established Community Feedback and Response Mechanism, including with strengthened response to complaints as well as referrals, as well as internal learning cycles to ensure that affected populations’ feedback is integrated into project proposals, strategies, monitoring and evaluations, trainings and performance tracking management, partnership agreements etc. PIN will continue to provide accessible and timely information to beneficiaries on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organisation and its beneficiaries over information provision.

1 - Needs

Against the backdrop of protracted conflict crisis, with uncertainty and isolation exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic, communities in the conflict zone have been facing various issues that often translate into protection concerns. The conflict and its consequences in particular continues to influence individuals' behaviour and mental health and psychosocial wellbeing, affecting trust and sense of belonging and creating daily stressors for the most vulnerable. Almost 40 % of the residents of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts have experienced trauma resulting in depression, anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). They are often unable to identify signs of psychological disorders and apply negative coping strategies like alcohol abuse, self-medication, and overloading with work. There is some evidence that the conflict may have led to an increase in the prevalence of domestic and GBV, however, it remains largely a hidden problem and incidents frequently go unreported due to stigma, shame or fear of retaliation. (HNO, 2020). A generation of over half a million children (more than 16 % of people in need) is growing up surrounded by violence and fear, which will have a long-lasting effect on their future, and on the social fabric. Women, who account for over half of people in need (56 %) and many of them as single heads of households, face their own share of psychosocial stress due to gendered, disproportionate responsibility for care in the family and community. Nearly all the elderly people have at least one chronic disease and report conflict-related mental health issues due to loneliness, isolation and immobility. More than one in four children in Donetska and Luhanska oblasts are estimated to be in need of psychosocial support.According to MHPSS TWG pre-existing mental health and psychosocial support needs of the affected by the conflict population in eastern Ukraine and across the whole country are also intensifying as a result of the significant distress and indirect socio-economic effects caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. This is compound by the lack of awareness on coping mechanisms in maintaining mental health and psychosocial well-being during COVID-19 (54%) as well as coping mechanisms on reducing stress and anxiety are unknown (35%). Furthermore, population is fearful of their community members having COVID-19, which increases psychological distress among communities (17%) (for HNO 2021, draft version).However, there is an enduring lack of mental health and PSS is a key gap in protection and health in conflict-affected areas, particularly near the ‘contact line’. Access to essential services to improve wellbeing remains compromised for various reasons. The available services (including social case management) are either difficult to access, over-stretched or under-funded. Adolescent girls and boys remain often invisible due to insufficient prioritisation and outreach. Gender norms on masculinities and insufficient outreach efforts prevent some men and boys from seeking psychosocial support, which can expose their families to abusive behaviour. Isolation remains a key factor, especially for the most vulnerable, like people with disabilities (represent 12 % of people in need), the elderly (32 %) of people in need). Lastly, some 350,000 internally displaced persons (10 % of people in need) living in GCA continue to face innumerable challenges in accessing durable solutions. The majority of people (83 %) is unaware of where to seek help – a fact particularly true for population groups with lesser access to information and literacy on existing protection systems. Overall there is also a lack of attention to the social dimension of psychosocial support, especially with regard to youth engagement, to encourage and nurture life skills, sense of belonging to society through participation in dialogue and decision-making.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

PCO1. Ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to people with specific needs.Activities:- Providing Psychosocial Support through Child Friendly Spaces, Group Sessions, or Outreach;- Individual Psychosocial Support; Such support will focus on the most vulnerable based on analysis of cumulative vulnerabilities (age, gender, diversity-sensitive) in isolated/underserved communities, those most at risk living in the areas along the ‘contact line’, where fighting has severed access to basic services (individual compounded with contextual vulnerabilities). PIN will in particular, and with partners, review needs which might traditionally be overlooked: due for instance to gendered dimensions, exploring outreach interventions to engage PSS to address toxic masculinities and unmet needs of men, but also needs of adolescent boys and girls; but also to improve specialisation in PSS for the elderly etc.Focusing on such unmet needs in hard-to-reach and isolated areas and where services are lacking, PIN with partners will continue to provide psychosocial support for children and parents, adults in vulnerable communities in the form of mobile teams of psychologists and social workers, which will bring such PSS services (awareness raising sessions, individual/ group consultations/events) to the field primarily across the LoC. The cognitive and psychosocial programs will be directed to stabilize and improve the psycho-emotional state of children and adults, reduce the risks of adopting any kind of self-inflicting coping strategies and reduce the negative consequences of the military conflict (anxiety, aggressiveness, neuroses, imbalance, fear, apathy and etc.), form anti-stress coping strategies, skills of coping behaviour in crisis situations, skills of self-regulation of psycho-emotional state, formation and development of resilience. All proposed PSS intervention will adhere to best practices as identified by IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings. PCO3. Improve resilience of conflict-affected people and system of service provision.Activity:- Capacity building for PSS practitioners (social workers, psychologists, educators, local activists/volunteers, representative of international organizations, etc.) Focusing on underserved communities, PIN will assist while other services are not in place and capacitates community-based and local structures to become sustainable. PIN will maintain the process of coordination with PSS services in most isolated communities along LoC to strengthen and find ways to expand their services to the most vulnerable groups on the community level. Cooperation with local actors (including community structures) and improved referral pathways and practice will be key in paving the way for more sustainable efforts to improve access to essential services for wellbeing.In view of preparing exit and sustainability of access to services, PIN will scale-up community-based mechanisms. PIN will strengthen the capacity of other PSS local providers (local NGO’s/CBOs) through small-scale financial support. Funds will be granted to the most perspective and well-developed local organizations after a comprehensive selection and evaluation. PIN’s PSS hotline will be handed over to local organisation.Capacity building of PSS practitioners through trainings will also be implemented. Capacity building will target identified community specialists in the Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts especially in isolated areas and along the line of contact in GCA. An assessment will identify their needs to improve their technical competencies concerning PSS services, including through workshops on Protection/PSS.
