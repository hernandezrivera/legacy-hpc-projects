Project Summary

According to the 2024 HNO for Syria, the food security situation in Syria continues to deteriorate. An estimated 15.5M people (65% of the population) need food or livelihood support, Or assistance from national safety nets. At least 12.9M people need food assistance, 2.1M who live in camps are all food insecure, and a further 2.6M are at risk of food insecurity. The driving factors of food insecurity are likely to remain in place in 2024 and if the required support is not provided, there is a high probability that vulnerable people will fall into extreme poverty and that the number of people in need will increase further during the year.
During 2023, a reduction of the food assistance levels due to limited funding and resources has had an impact on the food security situation of the most vulnerable households in Syria.
The March Remote Monitoring report showed that around 63% of HHs in earthquake-affected locations were experiencing inadequate food consumption, greatly affecting their food security.
This year, the FLSA also collected data from camps in 33 subdistricts in NWS and NES. Data shows not all 2.1M people in camps need humanitarian food assistance, the results showed that 79.3% of the camp population in NWS are food insecure, with 18.0% of the total camp population being severely food insecure.
White Hands (WH) proposes a 12-month action that uses a flexible integrated programming approach that includes emergency and regular food distribution using pre-positioned in-kind & CBT modalities and bakery support.
This program contributes to the first sector objective of the FSL cluster, which is "To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance," and aims to address the immediate food security needs of vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host communities with acute needs, living in the districts of Afrin, A'zaz, Bulbul, Sharan, Jandairis, Mare', Atareb, and Daret Azza (Aleppo), and the districts of Dana, Salqin, Armanaz, Maaret Tamsrin, Harim, Idleb, Ariha, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur (Idleb) in NW Syria, in both the short to medium-term.
In the short term, WH is planning to reach 5000 HHs/25000 INDIVs with 10,000 in-kind RTEs based on their needs and reported situations. This is designed to be a one-off distribution for newly displaced or crisis-affected households as part of a rapid response mechanism for exceptional cases in which beneficiaries do not have access to cooking facilities.
Equally, to meet the food security needs in the medium term, WH aims to assist 8000 vulnerable IDP, returnees, and HC HHs (40000 INDIVs) that meet the Food Security Program beneficiary eligibility criteria with monthly food assistance through the most appropriate modalities. This will include providing 3500 HHs/17500 INDIVs living in areas where markets are inaccessible or food is unavailable or unaffordable with in-kind food baskets for 8 months and restricted food vouchers/Cash for Food (value $65) to a further 4500 HHs/22500 INDIVs with access to functioning markets in 8 distribution rounds, as recommended by the FSL Cluster.
The Bakery Support component provides approximately 15000 individuals living in supported bakeries' catchment areas (mainly IDP camps) with free bread. This is achieved through subsiding up to 100% input (flour, yeast, and operational costs) of bread production, thus supporting five local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditioned upon free bread production and distribution to IDPs daily and to be monitored by WH cash & markets and monitoring teams.
Throughout this program, WH will identify the unique needs of men, women, girls, and boys and seek to maximize equality of treatment and access to services for all of these groups.
The proposed targeted districts are currently facing a funding gap in the Food Security Program, leaving many people in need without assistance.
