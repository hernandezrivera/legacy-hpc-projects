Project Summary

The proposed project aims at providing better livelihood opportunities and economic security for people with disabilities, awareness-raising of services and rights, and cultural barriers. The project will provide an intensive entrepreneurship training package to 100 PWD in north Syria followed by coaching and mentoring to produce business plans and a small cash grant to enable them to engage and start-up their income-generating activities.
an online registration form for the anticipated candidate, after publishing the application form on SENED website and social media pages, SENED will receive and evaluate the applications and conducted interviews with the applicants to determine their ability to join the training program. Applicants with innovative business ideas will be prioritized for selection. Additional selection criteria will include education background, vulnerability level, and commitment to attend the program training.
The proposed project will commence by raising the awareness of the PWD community and generate interest in the program, this will be conducted through informing the local stakeholders by information dissemination of social media and meeting with relevant community representatives, and local stakeholders
SENED will be hiring technical experts in the field of entrepreneurship and business development and prepare the training program curricula which will be tailored to be realistic in the local settings and meets the beneficiaries needs and expectations.
and the training hours will be assigned in consultations with the beneficiaries to ensure their commitment to the training.
SENED will ensure that training methodologies are aligned with the different needs of beneficiaries. The provided training will focus on elevating the knowledge of the participants regarding the local market needs, and best practices for successful business management methodologies. The proposed training program will be conducted through online platforms to avoid any accessibility challenges for the beneficiaries. SENED will prepare a well-equipped room within its officer for trainers to provide interactive and practical training. All participants will be provided with internet service packages to ensure effective attendance and performance as well as incentives to commit to the training program.
SENED Will also provide face to face training in PWD safe spaces and assure the accessibility of the beneficiaries. as well as SENED will work to provide sessions in SENED Centers taking into consideration the capacity of the center.
The physical training sessions will be interactive sessions to assure that all beneficiaries will exchange also their experience and share the ideas.
By the end of the training program, SENED will provide coaching and mentoring service to the participants to enable them to develop their innovative ideas and develop their businesses plans.
Finally, SENED will provide small cash grants to the participants to enable them to put their ideas to practice and initiate their businesses. Commitment to attending training and producing business plans will be a crucial factor in receiving the cash grants for participants as per Cash-for-Work Recommended Minimum Actions.

SENED will circulate the project idea in the ER cluster to make sure that there is no duplication of services in the area , in addition to receiving recommendations from implementing aid providers in the area
such cooperation will foster social cohesion among beneficiaries (host community and IDPS ) as mentioned in the project activities. as well as the beneficiaries will be able to create network through their business plan and this network can be active in the market and affect positively the communities market.
SENED will follow Do No Harm approach: through consulting community/local stakeholders at area of implementation on project design, the daily wage rate, number of work hours per day, and project duration will be taking into consideration.

4 - Needs

the pandemic has been affecting the entire socio-economic system and has led to a dramatic loss of livelihoods across the country. At the end of 2019, around 86 percent of Syrians lived below the poverty line, and estimated just under 90 percent as of mid-August 2020 (HNO 2021). A distinct category has emerged, the working poor, households unable to meet the cost of basic needs despite one or more member of the family are working. As per the UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment, before the pandemic, 41.9 percent of households could cover their expenses, while currently, only 9.7 percent can do so. This is exacerbated by a severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital. About two-thirds of households lost one or more income sources, and about half lost between 75 percent to 100 percent of their previous income.Moreover, the impact on livelihoods and the gloomy economic prospects poses an even higher risk of individuals joining extremist armed groups and committing crimes, avoiding health-related treatments, such as for COVID-19 and due to the fear of livelihoods loss, and parents sending children to work, and/or encouraging early marriages.An assessment of the state of disability conducted at the national level presents the most alarming situation for people with disabilities (PWD). 3.7 million people – or 27 percent of the total population (aged 12+) – in Syria have a disability

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sened protects the privacy of beneficiaries and vulnerable groups by adapting and securing their data, adhering to the "Do-No-Harm" concept, and maintaining their dignity. Beneficiaries are informed on how to use effective feedback and complaint method. This mechanism comprises established reporting procedures and channels for sexual exploitation and abuse. The PSEA idea is also considered in the awareness-raising themes provided for children and adults in order to raise their awareness of their rights and safety, as well as the safety measures to report such misconduct. Risk: Affected populations and Sened staff, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to Sened staff PSEA Policy and Code of Conduct. which would be provided by our specialized staff in protection to other staff in Sened 1. SEA by humanitarian workers male-dominated, gatekeepers, and potential other beneficiaries. 2. Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurring (crowd control) Likelihood: High. Impact: High in terms of reputation, trust. mitigation measures: 1. Establish a feedback mechanism 2. Recruit female staff and train them. 3. Train staff on code of conduct 4. All staff contracts to include PSEA 5. Community committee with equitable representation of women 6. Train staff on the Inter-Agency Coordination in Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk #1: The utility of the program - Acquiring the skills and competencies within the project will not improve the financial independence of beneficiaries.Response Strategy: SENED planned complex intervention capacity building training and the small cash grants which will allow the targeted beneficiaries to be able to start their businesses and thus increase their financial independence.Risk #2: Beneficiaries are not committed to complete the training program.Response Strategy: SENED will consult with the beneficiaries regarding training hours. It will, in addition, cover the communications fees (internet packages) given the training will be provided via online platform. Risk #4: Protection concerns dealing with female beneficiaries.Response Strategy: SENED will make sure to hire female trainers to avoid any protection concerns that might arise from the female beneficiariesRisk #5: COVID-19 spreading and restriction measures. Response Strategy: SENED will ensure that all prevention and safety measures are in place during the project activities’ implementation including disinfection, usage of masks, and social distancing. However, the risk will be minimized given that the training will be provided via online platform.
