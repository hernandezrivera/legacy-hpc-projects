Project Summary

Due to socio-economic inequalities exacerbated by multiple conflicts, famine, and widespread insecurity in Somalia, constant displacement and instability forces more than three million vulnerable boys and girls are out of school due to lack of an enabling learning environment. With one of the lowest primary school enrolment rates in the world, girls are at a greater risk of being deprived of their right to education due to lack of a positive environment for women, cultural biases towards boys, and the prevalence of GBV in form of psychological, economic and physical abuse for adolescent girls who have a higher school dropout rate. According to HNO 2022, Education services are inadequate across Somalia, with only 52% of schools classified as permanent structures and most schools at least 30 minutes walking distance for 1 in 3 households. The Mercy-USA assessment conducted between 11th – 15th July 2021 in Hiraan, showed that to ensure sustainability and cover some of the school expenditures and provision of meals, school fees are required; however, these can only be afforded by 66.7% of parents. Some of the reasons for the school dropout rates include nomadism, displacement due to floods, and famine. 41.7% of the students are not supported and there is an urgent need for intervention to sustain school attendance. The availability of school facilities suitable for children with special needs, hand washing stations, and girls’ access to hygiene/dignity kits is at 25.0%, 34.6%, and 16.7%, respectively. This calls for increased interventions towards children with special needs, girls' support, and child protection. Additionally, there is a need to install/rehabilitate improved water sources to improve hygiene practices at schools, especially in light of the COVID-19 pandemic. Only about 32.5% of the schools treat water before use, and only 35.4% of schools mentioned have water storage facilities. Only 25.8% of schools have an active WASH club. The barriers to girl child education include cultural practices, inadequate finances, early marriage, and insufficient sanitary support. Only 8.3% of schools have established girl child support structures, like clubs.
This proposed intervention is designed based on critical education needs in Somalia. The program seeks to build on existing knowledge of the targeted communities and the capacity of ministry officials with the aim of achieving increased access and retention of out of school and children from vulnerable households. The proposed activities will focus on strengthening education in Hiraan and Mudug regions in an attempt to help out of school children and those who are economically disadvantaged. The project aims to ensure a safe and protective learning environment with good quality teaching and increase access and retention of crisis-affected and IDP children through an integrated package of school-based emergency assistance. The project sought to increase access and retention to education and will include establishment or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces with gender-friendly and inclusive WASH facilities ((gender-sensitive latrines, water points, girl-friendly spaces), provision of school furniture, teacher incentives, and teacher trainings in pedagogical skills, handling special education needs children and psychosocial support, inclusive, protective and socio-emotional teaching and learning, as well as training of Community Education Committees in school management and school safety and preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement and disability through advocating for the inclusion of IDPs and people living with disability in decision-making processes and removing barriers associated with accessing education services and facilities for children with disabilities.

1. - Projects activities

The project aims to ensure emergency and crisis-affected children and youth have access to safe and protecting learning environment. Mercy USA will conduct community mobilization to increase community participation in schools and employ these forums to mobilize community leadership and local authorities on their roles and responsibilities to protect and support all children in ensuring safe access to school. The project activities will address the needs of targeted 32 schools which includes support for infrastructural and learning materials and teacher’s incentives. Through the collaboration of all stakeholders including the ministry of education and other NGOs working/supporting education in the target regions. Project activities include:To ensure emergency and crisis affected children and youth have access to a safe and protective learning environment.Activity 1.1. Conduct social mobilization drives to improve enrolment, retention, and attendance;Activity 1.2. Conduct back to school campaigns to enroll out of school children;Activity 1.3. Support for adolescent girls with menstrual hygiene kits; Activity 1.4. Rehabilitation of classrooms with ramp and handrails;Activity 1.5. Creation of 32 child-friendly spaces;Activity 1.6. Establishment/Engagement of Child to Child clubs (CtCs) to promote peer to peer support on practical life skills, child rights, improvements in the school-learning environment in schools;Activity 1.7. Conduct school-based GBV activities, including awareness-raising on child protection referral systems;Activity 1.8. Provision of psychosocial support in schools and referral pathways/systems to children in schools.To ensure vulnerable children and youth are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion.Activity 2.1. Provision of school supplies (teaching, learning and recreational materials) for children in schools;Activity 2.2. Provision of monthly teacher incentives to teachers (50% male/50%female);Activity 2.3. Improve school WASH facilities such as construction/rehabilitation of gender-friendly latrines, handwashing facilities, water sources and improved hygiene promotion;Activity 2.4. Facilitate WASH club activities in 32 schools – quarterly.Strengthen capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies, preparedness and response within education system.Activity 3.1. Train teachers (50% male/50%female) on pedagogical skills, classroom management and assessment of learners, psycho-social skills;Activity 3.2. Train CECs members on improving good governance, management of school, resources, social mobilization, child protection issues;Activity 3.3. Train teachers and CECs members on skills and knowledge on GBV relevant to education programming.Additionally, to ensure disability inclusion, Mercy-USA will:• Consult all stakeholders, teachers, school leaders, parents and families, as well as students with and without disabilities outlines a plan to collect information on the perspectives of stakeholders both while preparing the project and during the implementation of the project;• Ensure the constructed infrastructure will be suitable for children with disabilities;• During implementation, the project team collects and reports feedback on both process and outcomes for project beneficiaries with disabilities.

• Mercy-USA, guided by internal PSEA and Child Safeguarding Policy and international best practices, will safeguard gender justice and equity and promote gender equality.• Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities. • Measures are in place to ensure that beneficiaries understand that it is their right to receive equitable and safe assistance, including people with specific needs or vulnerabilities;• All project staff will be trained on GBV prevention and response in emergencies and PSEA, in order to ensure confidentiality and full adherence with the Do No Harm principle with strict dealing with Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and GBV issues.Additionally, to ensure protection and inclusion and mitigate the risks, Mercy-USA will provide schooling close to settlements or in the central areas of camps, provide classes for different age and skill levels, provide appropriate clothing and sanitary supplies to girls so that they can attend school and ensure other obstacles to increasing the enrolment and attendance of girls are addressed.
