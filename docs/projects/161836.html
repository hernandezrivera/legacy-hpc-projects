Project Summary

The protracted insecurity in Borno state has great shelter and NFIs impacts on the displaced population.
Following the recent and alarming surge in insecurity in northern and eastern Borno State and parts of Northeast, many persons have been displaced and push to find refugee in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) and other LGAs in the state. Many have since arrived in MMC and other part of Borno have been staying without any form of NFIs.
This project aims to meet the immediate life-saving humanitarian needs of an estimated newly
displaced people from other part of Borno into MMC and camps in other LGAs, most of whom are living in deplorable conditions and urgently require humanitarian assistance especially NFIs and Shelters
The proposed project is targeting new arrivals who have fled inaccessible areas and are in dire need of aid and emergency shelters.
This project will be focusing only on the provision of Emergency Shelters and improved NFI kits to the most vulnerable in MMC and other LGAs in Borno state and the activities will include, community social mobilization, need assessment, beneficiary registration, procurement, warehousing, transportation, emergency land mapping and layout for emergency shelters, distribution of NFIs and post distribution monitoring every two weeks after distribution as the distribution will be done on a rolling-on-going bases according the the needs and sector directives.
In implementing this project GREENCODE will work closely with the Sector and the nationally approved guidelines and standard as uploaded in the Humanitarian Response Info will be used.
Given the emergency shelter and NFI needs for the new arrivals in the MMC and other LGAs and considering the important of providing lifesaving assistance, the project will procure, transport and distribute improved NFIs kits to the most vulnerable 700 households such as female and child headed HHs among IDPs (new arrival) in MMC based on needs of the gaps jointly identified/agreed by GREENCODE and Shelter/NFIs sector in Borno state.
The distribution will be on a rolling/on-going basis as GREENCODE team will continue to assess, verify, and deliver assistance according to identified needs to affected populations wherever they are in MMC, and most likely in some in locations with gaps as directed and agreed with the Shelter/NFIs sector.
In all stages of the project protection issues such as SGBV and child protection will be closely monitored and referred to appropriate partners for counselled and refer, also during distribution of NFIs.
GREENCODE will work with IOM, SEMA, UNHRC and Protection sector to ensure gender and age awareness including in the dis-aggregation of data and the targeting of female¬ headed households, incorporating gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs assessments and response recommendations. Further, GREENCODE will pay special attention recognising the different needs of women and children as well as men and boys of different ages, we will ensure targeting criteria are taking into consideration specific vulnerabilities and needs. Also, in procuring NFIs and ensure that kit contents procured for this project are gender sensitive as well as easy to use by women in performing daily tasks.

1 - Needs

Over the last 5 months Borno and other BAY states has witnessed large displacement and movement to some safe perceived zones and it is estimated that 7.1 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. The conflict has resulted in widespread displacement, with1.8 million people currently estimated to be internally displaced. Heightened tensions in recent months have triggered further displacement, with close to 72,000 new arrivals registered between November 2018 and January 2019. 87 per cent of newly displaced people are women and children. The trend in new displacements/new arrivals from November 2018 through January 2019 continually increased from 7,842 people in November 2018, to 19,854 people in December 2018 and a high of 44,111 people in January 2019.Borno State remains the epi-centre of the crisis, with 14 LGAs that have received the highest number of new arrivals over the last three months. OCHA Humanitarian Situation Report, March 2019 and 90 Days Emergency Response Plan March 2019.MMC is so far has received the largest number (over 27,000 out of 71,000) of the recently displaced persons and the camps are already over congested. Humanitarian needs across all sectors are increasing as a result of the latest waves of displacement and the ongoing crisis, specifically in the Shelter and NFIs, WASH, Protection, Food Security, Health and Nutrition sectors. The fresh population movements and the high influx of IDPs are leading to camp overcrowding and congestion in some of the hosting sites such as Teachers Village in Maiduguri, whose capacity has been stretched from 5,000 to 28,000 people within one month. As a result, a significant number of new arrivals are sleeping outside in the open, without NFIs. With the new movements from northern Borno State into MMC, the needs in terms of NFIs are drastically increasing. However, there is a major challenge in procuring additional NFIs for supply to the influx of IDPs. Currently, more than 26,000 households are living outside or in rudimentary shelters. The current level of need is overwhelming sector partners and additional funding support is urgently required. The living conditions of the existing caseload in terms of NFIs aredeplorable. In addition to the high level of congestion, IDPs already residing in the camps are forced to share their living space with new arrivals in extremely confined spaces. According to DTM 25 more than 40 per cent of the IDPs are living in makeshift shelters. Also the Shelter/NFIs sector is targeting 279,207 displaced persons in its 90 days planAmong the displaced in the last few months, 87 per cent of newly displaced people are women and children. Considering the important role played by emergency Shelter and NFIs in the saving lives and restoring human dignity, GREENCODE recently conducted on-the-spot assessment of some locations of new arrivals in MMC and discovered the lack of emergency and NFIs and there is an urgent to provide the most vulnerable with NFIs and emergency shelter

GREENCODE has places high value and premium on humanitarian accountability. The proposed project is highly gender and age¬ sensitive, considering gender equality for effective programming with assurance that all affected members of the target population have equal access to services and the targeted actions is based on gender and age analysisBeneficiary selection criteria will be jointly agreed by the target groups, while distribution standards through a Banner or bill board will be made accessible to the community.
