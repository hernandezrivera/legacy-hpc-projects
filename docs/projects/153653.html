Project Summary

Magwi county in South Sudan has been in political crisis since 2016, fighting between the warring parties in juba rapidly spread to other parts of the country which were relatively calm. Magwi county saw protracted conflict which left most of the essential infrastructure destroyed by the fighting.
WASH infrastructure was not left out in this crisis and most of the essential wash facilities have been destroyed. This therefore calls for urgent humanitarian assistance to help alleviate the suffering of the most vulnerable in Magwi county.
Open defecation is a major challenge in this county and this practice has led to massive contamination of water points which has therefore led to outbreaks of waterborne diseases with the most recent outbreak of cholera being reported in April 2017. Women and girls are exposed to sexual harassment and abuse when they go to the bushes to answer call of nature. This therefore calls for argent humanitarian assistance.
Ebola virus outbreak which has affected neighboring CONGO posses a major threat to the residents of Magwi county. This can be spread by unhygienic condition and lack of hand washing practices.
Health facilities and nutrition centers lack water and the malnourished children are exposed to more infections which might increase child mortality rates in South Sudan.
Health workers have little knowledge on minimum WASH package activities. This has led to low knowledge and passage of key WASH messages to the communities.
Women and girls of school going age lack sanitary pads. This has been caused by the economic meltdown in the country which has left many lacking even the most basic essential needs. This has led to women and school going girls using unhygienic methods of menstrual management which has predisposed them to infections. School going children have missed school and are unable to do co curriculum activities due to fear of embarrassment.
This project will address this issues by ensuring that there is distribution of menstrual hygiene kits to the vulnerable women and school going girls. This will ensure that they don’t miss classes and women can carry on with their daily activities even during menses.
Construction and rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines will be done to ensure that cases of GBV among women who are exposed to this risk as they go to the bushes are reduce. This will also reduce water contamination as it will greatly reduce open defecation thus reducing cases of disease outbreaks.
Hygiene kits will be distributed to ensure that households have clean containers for water storage and treatment. The treatment tablets supplied with the hygiene kits will ensure that beneficiaries have clean and safe water for drinking and domestic use.
Water management committees will be formed to ensure that they take over management of water points and ensure safety of the water points.
Hand pump mechanics will be trained to ensure that all the rehabilitated boreholes are well maintained and to ensure sustainability of the project.

Needs

South Sudan descended in to political crisis in December 2013. A peace deal signed in 2015 did not hold and in June 2016 fighting erupted in Juba and soon spread to other parts of the country, this political crisis in affected most of the WASH facilities in Magwi county. There are 7145 IDPs still in the IDP camps in county according to UNOCHA and more than 20000 returnees according to RRC Magwi county. With the signing of the peace agreement, it is expected that there will be influx of more returnees in to the county which will further strain the available water points and WASH facilities.Open defecation is a serious concern in the county and its aggravated by lack of latrines in the communities and in schools. This exposes women and school going children to danger of sexual abuse as they go to the bushes to relieve themselves. It also increases the chances of contaminating the water points thus increasing the risk of water borne disease outbreaks. The already existing latrines have been destroyed and need urgent rehabilitation in order to avert disease outbreaks and reduce cases of GBV.Lack of menstrual hygiene kits has exposed the women and school going children to infections by using unhygienic ways of managing menstruation. They also miss school during menses period and are unable to participate in co curriculum activities. Health facilities and nutrition treatment centers lack clean and safe water for drinking and therefore water points in the health facilities need to be rehabilitated to provide clean water to this vulnerable populations. Health facility staff lack the information on minimum WASH package activities. This exposes the patients and malnourished children to danger of contracting waterborne infections.Hygiene kits are lacking in Magwi county and residents don’t have hygienic ways of water storage which exposes them to using contaminated water.Water points and boreholes are broken down and need construction of new boreholes and rehabilitation of the existing ones in order to ensure that the residents get clean water for drinking to the minimum required standards of 7-15 liters per day per personWater treatment tablets will also need to be distributed to the community in order to ensure that they take clean and safe water for drinking and for domestic use. This will greatly reduce cases of water borne disease outbreaks and cholera.

Activities

1. Construction and rehabilitation of water pointsWater points like boreholes and the other water sources will be constructed and those in bad condition will be rehabilitated in order to ensure that the communities get clean and safe water to the required standards of 15 liters per day per person. Water points in health facilities and nutrition centers will also be rehabilitated to ensure that all the pregnant and lactating mothers and children with acute malnutrition have access to safe water for drinking and domestic use.2. Construction and rehabilitation of latrinesThe project will construct and segregated latrines which will help in reducing cases of open defecation and reduce disease burdens caused by water contamination. The latrines will also help reduce cases of gender based violence and sexual abuse by ensuring that women and girls have a safe place to relieve themselves.3. Distribution of menstrual hygiene kitsMenstrual hygiene kits will be distributed to mothers of childbearing age and school going girls. This will preserve the dignity of the mothers and the girls and will also ensure that the school going girls don’t miss classes during the menses period.4. Distribution of hygiene kitsThe distribution of hygiene kits will ensure that the communities have commodities for safe water storage and also water treatment tablets which will ensure that there is clean and safe water for drinking and for domestic use.5. Training health and nutrition workers on minimum WASH packagesHealth and nutrition workers will be trained on the minimum WASH package activities. They will be encouraged to disseminate the messages to the community and to the patients thus improving the knowledge on key WASH messages6. Formation and training of water management committeesThe water management committees will be formed and trained on how to ensure water safety and how to manage and rehabilitate water points. They will also be trained on how to disseminate key WASH messages to the communities in order to reduce water contamination. 7. Training of hand pump mechanicsHand pump mechanics will be trained in order to ensure that the beneficiaries are able to maintain the boreholes and to ensure the sustainability of the project.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The project has put in place and will sustain a beneficiary project team made up of opinion leaders and representatives of different beneficiary categories. This group of people are actively involved in project conceptualization, implementation planning including monitoring and evaluation. The project team regularly interacts with this beneficiary group to plan, reflect and share information including brainstorming on overcoming existing and emerging challenges. Members of the community actively participate in giving feedback on the implementation of the project. Since they live in the community, they act as valuable "eyes in the field" and "ears on the ground" for the project team.

There will be suggestion boxes put in place and SHAO social media platforms will be used to ensure that feedback is got about the progress of the project. focus group discussions and key informant interviews will be taken. all feedback whether positive or negative will be treated with respect.

The project is aware that certain cultural practices and customs may pose a barrier to access and participation of beneficiaries.
