Project Summary

The proposed project swiftly addresses food insecurity of 40,000 households (240,000 individuals), where 25,000 households will be supported via three rounds of food distribution, in addition to distribute a bread on daily basis to 15,000 HHs (75000 individuals) whom living in the camps in North of Syria for 10 months, where each family will receive 1 K.G of bread for 6 days a week for 10 months.
According to the multiple reports from the Clusters and multi-sector needs assessment at the end of 2019, about 7.9 million individuals are food insecure and 1.9 millions are at risk of food insecure.
After informing details of food distribution 3-7 days prior to the event, 10,000 households will receive food basket for 3 months through small and multiple distribution points at each village to avoid a high number of beneficiaries at the same place.
Ensuring the beneficiaries feedbacks and recommendations to inform the project implementation, a leaflet on feedback mechanism will be circulated during distribution in addition to PSEA leaflets.
Through setting a selection criteria based on vulnerability analysis, the most vulnerable groups among the IDP will be supported and they can reduce their likeliness of engaging negative coping mechanism, and their life-risks related to food shortage to be mitigated which will be verified through post distribution monitoring activities.

4 - Needs

Nine years into the crisis, the food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 million people in need; of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity.This year the number of food insecure people has also increased from 6.5M (2019) to 7.9M (2020), approximately 20 percent increase from last year, with a decrease of almost 0.7M in people at risk compared to last year – those at risk have likely become people in need this year. Food insecurity in Syria remains to be defined by continued conflict causing mass and prolonged displacement, loss and lack of livelihood opportunities contributing to high poverty levels and reduced production capacity hampering financial and physical access to food, high inflation and soaring currency depreciation have reduced purchasing power and continue to deplete the livelihoods of the most vulnerable populations.Approximately 2.5 million people displaced across North West and North East Syria. Within this group, some 760,889 are residing in last resort camps. During recent assessments 82 percent of IDPs surveyed reported being displaced for more than 12 months. In addition, there are 438,000 Palestinian refugees who are also particularly vulnerable to food insecurity given their status. In North East the impact of the conflict could affect at least 1.2 million people as a result of the creation of the 32km buffer zone. Currently there are 496,590 displaced people across three governorates with fears of further unrest if Syrian refugees were forced to move into the territory from Turkey.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented directly by HIHFAD staff and will provide 10,000 HHs from the IDPs and Vulnerable Returnees with food baskets for 3 months, HIHFAD will conduct the distribution through many small distribution points at each village. also HIHFAD team will distribute wheat flour bread to 15000 HHs from the IDPs whom living in the camps on daily basis for 6 days a week along of 10 months of distribution. the project implementation will be through 5 main activities:1. HIHFAD field team will start to collect the updated new IDPs beneficiaries list.2. HIHFAD logistic team will announce for 3 public tenders for procurement of Food baskets and the wheat flour, and the 3rd one will be to bake the bread inside Syria.3. while the head office in Turkey is working on the procurement of the food baskets and wheat flour the HIHFAD field team will conduct home visits for verification of the Beneficiaries.4. HIHFAD field team will conduct kick-off workshops with all of the local councils and local authorities and camps managements in all of the targeted villages to sign a MOUs.5. HIHFAD distribution team will conduct the distribution of the food baskets for the selected beneficiaries for three rounds, and will conduct the daily bread distribution in the selected camps.6. HIHFAD filed MEAL team will conduct the PDM after each round of food baskets distribution and on monthly basis for the bread's one to measure the beneficiaries satisfaction regarding the quality of the distributed food baskets and the quality of the bread, the field team behaviors and the timing of the response.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk 1: There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination for some groups from the beneficiaries lists: Likelihood 1: Medium. Impact 1: High Mitigating measures 1: 1. Ensure consultation with communities related to locations of distribution to understand relevant community perspectives; 2. Conduct verification visits on the household level to ensure that the provided lists inline with the agreed criteria; 3. open many channels for feedback and complains from the beginning of the project. Protection risk 2: risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood 2: High. Impact 1: High Mitigating measures 2: 1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; 2. Train staff on code of conduct; 3. Code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any SEA complaints; 4. ensure existence of Female staff in each distribution point "from the distribution teams or MEAL team".

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to specific Sub-districts remains open. The Food baskets arrived to our warehouses on time. No drastic changes on the Bab-Alhawa border regulations. Risks: Risk 1: escalation the airstrikes in targeted areas. Mitigation 1: Conducting the distribution via many micro distribution points meaning don't have any high number of collected people in the same place in addition to cancel the day of distribution when mentioning any fighter jet hovering in the sky. Risk 2: Turkish Government decided to close the Bab-Alhawa border for urgent issue. Mitigation 2: 2.1. put this issue on the contract with the supplier and put a condition that after signing the contract if Bab-Alhawa closed for any issue, the supplier will provide HIHFAD with the same quantities of agreed items in Side Syria with the same specification from local resources. 2.2. Immediate contracting with multi suppliers inside Syria to provide the same food items with the same quantities. Risk 3: Delay from the supplier on delivering the Food Baskets to HIHFAD warehouse. Mitigation 3: Put a high penalty for the delay in delivering on the supplier in the signed contract, so the supplier will be more focus on his commitment with the delivery date.
