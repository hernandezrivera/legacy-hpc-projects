Project Summary

In addition to the ongoing war, the country remains prone to various risks and hazards such as floods, inter communal violence, displacement, climatic shocks, disease outbreaks, food insecurity, malnutrition, protection risks and deteriorating economic status, leading to an increased risk of unintended pregnancies, sexual violence, STIs including HIV, and complications which often imply mortality and/or long-term serious health problems from pregnancy and childbirth for millions of women and girls. In Sudan, girls and women face substantial gender inequalities that increase their vulnerability. The presence of armed actors, economic hardship, resource shortages continue to exacerbate the risk of GBV, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and negative coping mechanisms, including survival sex. Poor women feel compelled to become sex workers increasing their vulnerability to violence, abuse and poor health outcomes including unsafe abortion and HIV risk. People living with disabilities face additional concerns and challenges in this unstable environment. Maternal Mortality remains a key issue affecting women of reproductive age across Sudan with a high maternal mortality rate (270 per 100,000 live births as per 2020 UN estimates). In reference to Sudan Maternal death surveillance and Response (MDSR) reports, the most prevalent cause was Obstetric hemorrhage, followed by hypertensive disorders then sepsis. The functionality of MDSR was significantly affected after 15 April clashes resulting in staff forced displacement with poor communication network combined with loss of reports and assets. EMONC coverage was already low prior to the conflict, with only 43% of needs covered. This figure is expected to be much lower due to the ongoing conflict and its consequences. Capacities of the community midwives were identified as low and further deteriorated as midwives are operating with limited access to commodities and supplies; while some of them were forced into displacement due to insecurity further impacting the service coverage in conflict affected locations. Responding to the health needs of survivors of SGBV remains challenging due to limited access to (CMR) services with only 23% of the previously identified facilities are still able to deliver, and even where services are available, it can be life-threatening to physically access these services due to ongoing hostilities. Sudan HNO 2024 indicates total of 24.8M will need humanitarian assistance. Health cluster indicated 14.7M in need of health intervention, it is estimated that out of them 3.5M are women at reproductive age in need of humanitarian assistant, including 351,312 pregnant women
UNFPA will target 980K using the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for SRH aiming at reducing SRH mortalities and morbidities through: functioning coordination mechanisms and MDSR system, timely provision of Emergency Obstetric care and save delivery services, timely referral to lifesaving services, support the availability of Family Planning services, improving Clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV, availing HIV and STIs prevention and response, dissemination of awareness-raising material on positive health seeking behavior including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and reporting mechanisms, improving Antenatal and postnatal care, Post abortion care, and prevention and response of obstetric fistula. The stock of IARH kits currently available in port Sudan and in the pipeline will boost the continuation of the provision of SRH services covering over 10 M population
The geographical coverage follows the HNO identified locations with severity ranking Four and Five. Intervention will be implemented through government partners and NGOs (CAFA, SRCS, CDF, NIDO, SFPA, NEF, ALIGHT, NCA, HAI, PHF) in close coordination with the relevant partners in the National and state levels SRH working groups to avoid duplication in addition to effectiveness and efficiency.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all project steps, starting from planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation, the following principles will be adhered to 1. Avoid causing harm: UNFPA and actors will ensure that service and assistance delivery preserves the physical integrity of individuals and communities, their dignity. These packages have already been adapted to the context of Sudan, and are culturally appropriate and minimizes any harmful and unintended negative consequences. 2. Equality: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that assistance and services will be provided according to needs and not any other determinants such as ethnicity or nationality, in good quantity, within safe and easy to reach locations, are known by the affected individuals and area accessible by all various groups including disabled individuals. 3. Accountability to beneficiaries: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that the affected individuals and communities are engaged in the measurement of the quality of interventions serving them, including mechanisms for suggestions and complaints from the population. 4.Participation and Empowerment: Foster inclusive participation to decision-making processes, support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empower themselves

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

UNFPA and its partners are striving to systematically and meaningfully engage beneficiaries across all project phases starting from consultations on needs identification, prioritization, program implementation and feedback. The project implementation calls on transparency and functioning feedback mechanisms during regular monitoring visits, field visits, and events. Individual discussions along with focus group discussions allow for meaningful feedback and responsive corrective measures as relevant.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

UNFPA has zero tolerance for all forms of sexual wrongdoing, whether perpetrated against a recipient of assistance or coworker. UNFPA conducts rigorous pre-employment checks of staff and personnel to prevent the rehiring of known offenders. All UNFPA personnel are required to undergo mandatory online training on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment. UNFPA does not partner with entities that fail to appropriately address sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment. UNFPA investigates all allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse, imposes disciplinary and/or administrative sanctions when allegations are proven, and may, through the UN Office of Legal Affairs, refer matters to national authorities for criminal prosecution. UNFPA Sudan CO is a member in PSEA Network which includes UN agencies, Donors, and International NGOs with possibility of expansion. All UNFPA partners both governmental and none-governmental, including vendors were updated on requirement of compliance to PSEA and agreements were updated accordingly.
