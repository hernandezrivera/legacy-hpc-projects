Project Summary

ARC is proposing an emergency WASH project with DRR component interventions in the regions of Banadir, Lower Juba, Middle Shebelle, Sool, Sanag and Togdhere, targeting over 120,000 IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host community members across these regions. The proposed intervention will provide access to safe Water, Sanitation and Hygiene for women, men, boys, girls and the disabled in emergency need while developing sustainable, gender sensitive and disaster-responsive WASH infrastructures including building capacities and resilience of the target group. ARC targets 82,000 women, 58,000 men, 19,000 girls, and 13,000 boys through WASH infrastructure and services. This emergency WASH project will be integrated with Health, Nutrition, Protection and Livelihood sectors to have greater impact on the lives of the affected communities. The linkage to health component will ensure a comprehensive approach to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with diseases resulting from deficient sanitation conditions, lack of access to safe water, and low level of good hygiene practices.
Most importantly, ARC will focus on increasing access to safe water by improving the water infrastructure. This includes protection and rehabilitation of shallow wells in Kismayo, Afmadow and Sool, rehabilitation of strategic boreholes through hybrid solar water pumping system, construction of water storage facilities and water distribution networks/extensions to health facilities, construction of berkads, provision of spare parts, water treatment, quality inspections, and by providing subsidies for water charges for the most vulnerable individuals. Activities proposed by ARC also include construction of desludgeable and disaster-resistant communal latrines, construction of institutional latrines with hand-washing facilities for schools and health facilities, and desludging of existing latrines. ARC will also promote improved hygiene in the target communities through trainings, sensitization of volunteers and community members, and provision of hygiene kits to AWD patients and families with malnourished children. For sustainability and ownership, ARC will create, train and equip community mechanisms and committees to improve and maintain sanitation and solid waste management in the targeted IDP settlements to reduce the of spread of diseases. ARC will use methods like "Super Mothers" and camp cleanliness competitions, which have proven effective to ensure community engagement and behavioral change.
ARC’s WASH technical designs adhere to SPHERE standards, mainstreaming of gender considerations, IASC guidelines for GBV Interventions in Humanitarian settings, WASH Chapter, securing access to the elderly and those with special needs as per Handicap International Guidelines, and to the environmental considerations. All programs are informed by recent assessments in proposed areas of intervention, and based on the ARC’s experience and operational capacity in South Central Somalia, including its role as WASH cluster lead in Lower Juba and deputy focal point for Banadir region.
All ARC WASH programs are community led, and leverage stakeholder participation to ensure safe, equitable, and dignified access to WASH services for all. The integration of Health in this proposal will ensure a holistic approach to the AWD/Cholera outbreak with a view towards building the capacity of both the affected community and the health workers in order to better prevent and manage outbreaks in the future. ARC will work with other responding partners like IOM, NRC, DRC, SAVE THE CHILDREN, CONCERN WORLDWIDE, HINA, MUSLIM HANDS, UNICEF, PAH, IMC, MURDO, WHO, and other partners in the region to ensure a coordinated, non-duplicated response.

Needs

The overall humanitarian situation in Somalia remains fragile due to ongoing conflict, displacement, and extreme weather conditions like floods, drought, and cyclones. Extreme water and pasture shortages have been reported in areas of Bari, Nugaal, Sool and Sanaag regions. Lack of access to safe water is a striking feature in almost all parts of Somalia (OCHA). Only 53% of Somalis have access to improved water sources (UNICEF 2017). Recent reports show Sanaag as being severely drought-affected with populations in need of immediate assistance to improve safe water access (WASH Cluster Vulnerability Analysis, March 2018).Unreliable access to a temporary water source and/or unprotected water is still an important Moreover, REACH's 2017 JMCNA shows that for 56% of people in Sanaag region, the main source of drinking water is water trucking, highlighting the need for more sustainable solutions. Access to dignified latrines is challenge across Somalia, as per the JMCNA assessment. The recent assessment on access to latrines, conducted by ARC and JRIA, shows that 664 communal latrines for Kismayo IDPs are filled up, while 40 camps lack latrines. Assessment conducted by ARC on 8-10th October 2018 reveals that 68.41% of Banadir IDPs have no access to sufficient water supply and 95.72% (403 out of 421) of the respondents said and 11% of the respondents reported cases of diarrhea in their households. according to humanitarian overview more than half of the population lack of access to adequate sanitation. The situation is particularly critical in Lower Shabelle, Lower Juba, Gedo and Bakool (below 25% each) and in IDP settlements. Heavy rainfalls affected more than 700,000 people in 2018. WASH infrastructures suffered from important damages in more than 25 districts. Most of these infrastructures have not been repaired yet. This is mostly in south-central Somalia where lot of sanitation and water facilities were destroyed or damaged and required rehabilitation/construction.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

ARC will continue to undertake WASH activities that encourage families to return to their homes, continue rebuilding their livelihoods, and resume normal life. The solar power system will allow cheap and affordable access to safe water to serve both the community and all health facilities in the target areas. The proposed project will address protection concerns in all stages, from project identification, design and planning, to implementation and monitoring by involving direct beneficiaries and ensuring equal representation of both women and men. Latrine construction will be done in a way that it addresses gendered needs, has disability features, is lockable, has lighting, and is not more than 50 m away from homestead. Women will be consulted and included in the planning of water infrastructure sites, and involved in decision making throughout the construction process. Regarding durable solutions for pumping, ARC proposes installing hybrid solar water pumping systems in strategic boreholes, deep wells and shallow wells in order to promote a system that does not solely rely on a single source energy source. Expanding use of solar cell driven techniques also reduces reliance on diesel and reduce C02 emissions. There are challenges associated with poor and unscheduled service regimes caused by lack of local technical skills, hard-to-find service kits and lack of financial capacity among users to afford the service and repair kits. The installation of solar power, despite its limitation of running hours, provides considerable relief to affected communities, as it requires minimum operation and maintenance costs. Solar power can also be harnessed and stored for use for lighting the neighbouring settlements at night, improving the safety of women and girls. This project is aimed to empower the affected people, water management committees, local authorities, line ministries and other stakeholders through technical capacity building. After successful completion of WASH infrastructure, it will be handed over to a legal authority of the locality, and WASH committees formed, equipped and trained by the project, to instil community ownership and project sustainability. To increase the project impact, ARC will ensure coordination between existing and planned WASH, Nutrition, Health, Protection and Livelihood projects in the same regions. ARC will ensure all WASH facilities are sited and designed considering environmental requirements and disaster risk reduction measures. To enhance transparency and accountability, project beneficiaries and line ministries will be involved in all stages of project planning an implementation.ARC uses real time Kuja Kuja beneficiary feed back system (www.kujakuja.com). The system includes a mechanism to incorporate findings in project design.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

ARC will prevent risks of exclusion based on displacement by ensuring that internally displaced persons benefit from the interventions just like the host communities. The project will target women, men, girls and boys in both IDP camps and host communities so that equity and equality is maintained at all levels. In actualizing this, there are risks that ARC will need to mitigate so that service provision to the most deserving communities is not hindered. All distributions will be done in safe, convenient places considering all aspects of protection and security of the beneficiaries as per the principle of "Do No Harm" ARC will also put in place community consultation and feedback mechanism Kuja Kuja to collect real time community satisfaction feedback (www.kujakuja.com).

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Long distance to the nearest water source is a protection risk for most of the IDPs in Kismayo, Afmadow, Dhobley and Middle Shebelle (Balcad and Warsheikh), putting women and adult girls who normally fetch water under threat of rape and other sexual violence. In order to mitigate this risk, ARC will ensure water points are located at a central location that are easily accessible to all and less than 500 m to the targeted beneficiaries. Secondly, communal latrines in IDPs set up are currently not gender-separated, lack lighting and are not always lockable, which will increase likelihood of GBV cases occurring at the communal latrines. In order to reduce this risk, ARC proposes to construct gender separated latrines where possible, ensuring latrines are gender separate with lockable doors, lightening at night by providing solar lamps, and installation of disability features. The other protection risk that need to be addressed by the project is gender discrimination in family chores, which puts women and girls at risk. This risk can be avoided by ensuring men and boys take part in fetching water from long distance or getting water from a closer source. The risk mentioned above can be addressed by adequate community WASH facilities. It is also essential to ensure equal representation of both genders and that the voices of women are heard and considered in project planning and implementation. ARC's WASH program will ensure reduction of overcrowding at the water points, reduction of queuing time to the acceptable level (30 min. maximum per SPHERE standards), and that locations are accessible, convenient and don't pose a threat to the beneficiaries. ARC will also put in place community consultation and feedback mechanism Kuja Kuja to collect real-time community satisfaction feedback (www.kujakuja.com). All distributions will be done in safe, convenient places while considering all aspects of protection and security of the beneficiaries as per the principle of "Do No Harm."

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Through the adoption of "Do No Harm" approaches, efforts are made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs are implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of Somalia conflict situation. ARC believes that understanding the situation and the context in which we operate is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and to avoid negative consequences in the operation area. In order to mitigate risks facing the targeted communities ARC proposes to ensure all issues that can cause conflict of interest are restricted, training and community gathering will be conducted in safe locations within the targeted communities to avoid risk associated with long travel distance and road blocks. Community water points and sanitation facilities are constructed in safe and accessible locations. As a part of protection activities, community capacity building will be conducted to promote peaceful co-existence and conflict resolution.
