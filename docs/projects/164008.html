Project Summary

Using its rapid response mechanism, SCSD proposes a 12-month action uses an integrated programming approach that includes NFI distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind and cash/voucher assistance modalities and basic shelter repairs.
SCSD will contribute to the two objectives of the Shelter/NFI sector which are:
SO1) Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support.
SO2) Reinforce an enabling protection environment and cohesion of communities by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
The proposed project aims to address emergency and more sustainable shelter and NFI needs of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and affected host communities with acute needs, living in Afrin, Atareb, Jebel Saman, Daret Azza, Azaz, Aghtrin and Mare sub districts (Aleppo Governorate) in addition to, Bennsh, Idleb, Ariha, Mhambal, Armanaz, Dana and Harim sub districts (Idleb Governorate) in NW Syria.
SCSD’s shelter and NFI program recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group across the range of shelter and NFIs interventions.
Considering their vulnerability, IDPs living in hard to reach locations, female headed HHs, persons with disabilities, and elderly persons at risk will form the priority groups of SCSD’s shelter & NFI program, particularly those among the high numbers of newly IDPs & returnees requiring core Shelter & NFIs assistance.
SCSD considers that the above-mentioned groups are the most vulnerable as they do not have the same access to livelihood opportunities as males (reasons related to security concerns & skills). Therefore, their capacity to access Shelter & NFI assistance is lower, forcing females to resort to negative coping mechanisms, therefore this program will target at least 25% female/child headed HHs & 30% HHs with disabled/elderly members from the total number of the targeted HHs.
SCSD’s NFI response aims to assist 2,000 households/12,000 individuals with one-off distribution of emergency NFIs kits including cash/voucher for NFI and 2,500 households/15,000 individuals with the provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuel including cash/voucher for NFI, to heat their shelter during the winter season 2020, prioritizing female or child headed households and elderly people and considering people with disabilities. Using a restricted value voucher modality, SCSD will organize three shopping periods during the project implementation as part of which identified beneficiaries will be able to access roughly 360 liters of Diesel fuel in total (120 liter of fuel per month upon to the SNFI cluster’s recommendation).
SCSD shelter program is designed to ensure the affected populations and returnees shelter needs are met and to continue the support of IDPs and returnees returning to their houses. Therefore, the shelter response will focus on durable and long-term shelter solutions.
Equally, the shelter program will enhance sustainable, recovery-oriented shelter solutions through support to IDPs returning and returnees to their original homes, contribute to rehabilitation of buildings in newly accessible areas, SCSD’s shelter program aims to address shelter needs of 800 vulnerable households/4,800 individuals through basic shelter repairs. This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensuring that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel with field coordination with the relevant local councils.
The program will seek feedback from both male & female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access & equal opportunities.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 31,800 individuals (27,000 individuals provided with NFIs & 4,800 individuals supported with Shelter solutions). More specifically, 8285 women, 6725 men, 7990 girls and 8800 boys will benefit from this program.

4 - Needs

According to SNFI HNO 2020, overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20% to 5.65M people. Of this, 3.8M (69%) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. The NFI PiN has declined by 22% from 2019 to 3.42M people in 2020. This reflects both a continuing but slow improvement in market functionality and the successful efforts of the sector to distribute assistance across the country. However, this progress is fragile as many communities suffer from high prices, unpredictable inflation, and low wages, while populations routinely report need for items outside the core NFI packages.In NW Syria, there are critical levels of overcrowding putting significant pressure on overburdened communities, with few if any remaining shelter options available. This lack of access to basic services including safe and adequate shelter increases the vulnerability of households and forces the adoption of harmful coping mechanisms such as selling assets or keeping children out of education, especially among female-headed HHs, HHs including youth, and those with functional difficulties. The largest number of PiN are in Aleppo (1,282,805) and Idleb (932,434) governorates. Moreover, according to CCCM updates in NW Syria 2/2/2020, since the 1st of December 2019, more than 586 K (20% Men, 21% Women, 28% Boys and 31% Girls) have been displaced mainly from the southern Idleb governorate. The displaced HHs were basically from Ma’arrat An Nu’man and Saraqeb Sub Distrıcts moving further north away from the hostilities to Dana, Afrin, A'zaz, Atreb, Idleb, and Maaret Tamisrin Sub Districts. This is raising the total displaced people in NW Syria to 2.5M (887,887 in Aleppo & 1,618,965 in Idleb). NFI assistance is a main need for 100% of the newly IDPs, and shelter support is a main need for 79% of them.The program is considering targeting areas of severity scale number 4-5 in both Aleppo and Idleb and aims at addressing urgent shelter & NFI needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs including cash/voucher for NFIs: This will include the provision of one-off emergency NFI kits through the most appropriate modality to 2,000 newly displaced, returnees and affected populations HHs. (Target items: 2,000 NFI Kits –Target individuals: 12,000 –Cost/item: 160 USD –Total Funds/Activity: 320,000 USD).1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance including cash/voucher for NFI: 2,500 HHs are able access diesel fuel for cooking and heating purposes during the winter season, using a restricted value voucher modality through 3 rounds of distribution. (Target items: 7,500 Fuel vouchers –Target individuals: 15,000 –Cost/item: 72 USD (120 liters per voucher with 0.6 USD per liter) –Total Funds/Activity: 540,000 USD).2.1 Repair and rehabilitation of damaged houses: This program will support the shelter and living condition for at least 800 most vulnerable internally displaced, returnees and host HHs and currently living in houses/buildings were moderately (non-structurally) damaged during the crisis in return of hosting beneficiaries - in case of IDPs beneficiaries - for one year free of rent. Shelter repair works will include repair of existing/installing of new internal walls, doors, windows, WASH Facilities, cooking space, solar panels, etc. Shelter repairs/rehab will include selecting locations and shelters, assessing their damage level through house-by-house technical assessments and HHs vulnerability, developing the BOQs and Scope of Work, signing agreements with Local Councils and Shelter owners, and completing the repair and rehabilitation works via qualified contractors since resorting to contractor is preferable as it allows professional rehabilitation works. These will be paralleled with Quality Assurance by SCSD’s qualified engineers and program M&E activities. (Target items: 800 Private shelter units –Target individuals: 4,800 –Cost/item: 750 USD –Total Funds/Activity: 600,000 USD).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts & Address any SEA complaints. Risk2: The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. In multi-household dwellings, lack of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Tensions linked to overcrowding may lead to an escalation of intimate partner violence and other forms of domestic violence. Where situational and risk analyses are not systematically conducted, these risks might not be identified and rectified. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girls and other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy; and/or if shelter materials and shelter-related NFIs are being distributed in areas that are safe, Consultations with women, girls, boy and men should be organized & community safety audits. Risk3: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines, Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed & Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: SCSD has full access to the targeted communities and the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the program. Risk: the escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in targeted area affecting the SCSD’s ability to operate and access it. Mitigation: SCSD will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities, Hiring of field staff from the local community of the targeted areas, Purchase of materials from the local market with guarantee the Do No Harm principles, Communicate, coordinate and consultation intensively with LCs, stakeholders and other NGOs operating in the areas, Re-direction of programming into other areas. Assumption2: Aid delivery continues to be accepted and possible in Syria despite of the presence of various armed groups. Risk: Implementation of the durable activities is prevented/stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping. Mitigation: SCSD will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/violence is noted, alternative routes will be used and additional warehouses will be rented to mitigate against the possibility of an armed group seizing control of all of SCSD stock inside Syria, or consider alternative areas/activities under the planned intervention. Assumption3: Markets are functioning properly in the areas identified. Risk: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/or supplies. Mitigation: SCSD will conduct a Rapid Assessment for Markets in the initial stage of the program and situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. In case the situation would change, SCSD would consider relocating the area of intervention where the required conditions are met.
