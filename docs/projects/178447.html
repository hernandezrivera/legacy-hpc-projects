Project Summary

Bonyan designed its Food Security interventions to meets the actual food needs in Syria and comply with the strategic objectives of both the HRP and FSA Cluster guided by an analysis of the severity of needs in accordance with assessment findings and key indicators. Bonyan 2021 Food Security program will focus on time-critical interventions which lay the foundations for sustainable recovery and a speedy return to longer-term development are also imperative integrating cluster objectives in order to provide inclusive respond to the specific needs in each targeted locations by improving farming livelihoods, crops production and productivity, food security and food availability targeting at both geographical and household level and then providing needs-based assistance to meet the needs that vulnerable households (prioritizes vulnerable children including adolescents/youth, girls and boys living with disabilities) who cannot meet for themselves ~ 42,000 INDV (21,000 F/ 21,000 M), of which 21,000 are IDPs, 15,000 are Host Community, and 6,000 are Returnees.
Obj.1: To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance targeting 6,500 HHs ~ 39,000 INDV (19,500 F/ 19,500 M, including 26,000 children <18)
====================
Bonyan will provide 5,000 Food basket response for IDP influxes for 5,000 HHs ~ 30,000 INDV (15,000 F/ 15,000 M) in order to improve food security score. On the other hand, Bread in the middle east’s culture, Syria particularly, is an essential part of food the daily meals and because of high ratio of carbohydrates which provides body with as energy and give them the feeling of satiety, Bonyan will participate in Bread supply chain through supporting local wheat farmers, wheat millers, and bakeries to distribute bread with less prices enabling access to 1,500 HHs to bread rations ~ 9,000 INDV (4,500 F/ 4,500 M) The distribution will last for 60 days and will be subject to vulnerability criteria.
Obj.2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives targeting 500 HHs ~ 3,000 INDV (1,500 F/ 1,500 M)
====================
The project will provide critically needed life-saving farming inputs, including seeds, fertilizers, farming tools and introduce farmers to modern agriculture methods to protect their crops from various natural hazards. Targeting 250 HHs of small farmers ~ 1500 INDV (750 F/ 750 M)
Another 250 of vulnerable HHs ~ 1,500 INDV (750 F/ 750 M) will receive livestock along with feed for 4 months.
Bonyan will provide training, supervision, and consultations for the beneficiary’s households by expert agronomists and veterinarians aiming to prevent crop, animal losses and increase the production.
Registration of beneficiaries in all activities will be through outreach campaign by announcing at accessible points for all groups. The selection and prioritization criteria will also be published clearly. Transparency of shortlisting of beneficiaries will be achieved by establishing a committee in each community consist of one member from LC, one community leader and the team of Bonyan. This mechanism ensures accountability and promotes trust between Bonyan and beneficiaries.

4 - Needs

HNO2021 indicated up to 14.2M PiN of form of food and agricultural assistance equivalent to ~69% of the total population, and up to 12.4M estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure and 1.8M are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity. Aleppo and Idelb governorates contain the highest number of PiN for food. Of the total PiN, the ratio of total male to female is (Female 51% 7.2M and Male 49% 7M), with a substantial number of children (at 45% 6.4M under 18 years old) while adults, elders, and PwDs are 50%, 5%, and 25% of the PiN respectively This is sharp increase from the last year compared with 2020: 9.8M PiN and in 2019 29% of population was food insecure.The evidence highlights an alarming deterioration, caused by a combination of factors including currency depreciation and associated loss of purchasing power, considerable hikes in food prices, large population displacements, lack of livelihood and income generating options, losses in agricultural production and assets, all compounded by ongoing conflicts and the COVID-19 pandemicThe agricultural production witnessed a slight improvement over the past few years, especially for wheat, with an estimated total production of 2.83 million tons, though this is almost 30 per cent less than the pre-crisis average of 4.1 million tons.
