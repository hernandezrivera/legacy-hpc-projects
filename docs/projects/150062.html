Project Summary

The proposed intervention will target the food security and livelihoods needs of the most vulnerable farming and herding communities affected by protection threats in Area C of the West Bank. The activities will contribute to their enhanced protection from forcible transfer by improving the main factors involved in food production (roads, water, livestock shelter) and agricultural inputs. By doing so, the proposed intervention will contribute to improving the food security of marginalized families living under the poverty line.
The project activities will benefit all members of the household, including women and children, and will be carried out on two levels: household level through the rehabilitation of animal shelters and the supply of agricultural inputs; and community level by improving the access to water and the communities’ accessibility (road rehabilitation).
The proposed intervention will deal with the protection threats to the livelihoods of the targeted families. Fomenting a community participation approach in the assessment, design and implementation of the activities, all the community’s sub-groups will participate in different focus group discussions, technical assessments and in the implementation process. The communication with communities (CWCs) will mitigate potential security and protection risks and, ultimately, empower the beneficiaries at community, family and individual level.
As outlined, the proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as participation and empowerment.

Needs

The 2018 HNO characterized conditions in the West Bank as a protracted crisis, with repeated shocks and continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and lack of economic opportunities resulting in high unemployment and low household incomes. This, together with the high cost of living - particularly relating to the cost of food - has left 1.6 million Palestinians (50,000 of them located in the West Bank’s Area C) food insecure and in urgent need of assistance. Families who are already food insecure and have limited resilience are unlikely to be able to retain (or rebuild) their livelihoods with the expected loss of the last of their remaining productive assets. The Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) has identified the most affected during emergency weather crisis in the agricultural sector as livestock, greenhouses, field crops and agricultural infrastructure. Additionally, women have limited space in the agricultural sector, despite their active involvement in agricultural activities. In oPt, at least 35% of formal agricultural labour requirements are provided by women, but they own only 7.2% of agricultural holdings (HRP 2017). Food insecure families are spending 50% of their incomes on food, other basic needs such as health and education are deprioritized, the families are forced to sell part of their assists to cover their basic needs, causing a deterioration of the agricultural sector and the national food security rates. According to the 2017 OCHA Vulnerability Profile Plus assessment, 163 communities reported a decrease in their access to land; almost 80% of them are located in Area C or East Jerusalem. In May 2017, OCHA conducted a vulnerability profiling exercise for 46 communities in the central West Bank, which indicated that these communities are under a real threat of forcible transfer. Structures owned by Palestinians in Area C are also subject to demolition; between January and August 2018, a total of 308 Palestinian structures were destroyed resulting in the displacement of 431 people including 250 children. Palestinians are affected by adverse weather conditions with the associated high winds and flash floods destroying farms and vital agricultural infrastructure, damaging crops, killing livestock and depleting savings when limited household funds are allocated to damage repair. It is likely that potential extreme cold weather in the 2018/2019 winter could occur with high winds, storms, flash flooding and frosts, exacerbating the already poor humanitarian status for large segments of the targeted population, namely women, girls and boys. In July 2018, AAH carried out an assessment of 80 communities in Area C through stakeholders’ interviews, focus groups with families in the communities and questionnaires. This enabled AAH to identify communities requiring priority emergency support due to their vulnerability (unemployment rate, income, demolition/eviction orders, and sensitivity of their location). These families lose their assets due to demolishing incidents or natural hazards and are unable to recover due to their limited financial capacities. An animal shelter assessment was conducted by Action Against Hunger: Date - July 2017. Geographical coverage - 6 governorates in Areas A, B and C Areas. Data gathering method: Interviews with MOA directorate in each governorate and completed questionnaires. Confirmed that herding is the main source of income for most herding families (90% of the targeted families) - especially in Area C. The study highlighted that most of the animal shelters in the targeted areas are not adequate for the flock size resulting in overcrowded shelters. In addition, a proportion of the flock spent extensive periods of time in the open thus resulting in high abortion and mortality rates (25% in most of the targeted communities).

Activities

The proposed intervention will facilitate an appropriate community-led emergency response to reduce vulnerability, enhance resilience and protect livelihoods during emergencies. The high priority needs identified by AAH, PARC and UWAC, prior to any activity implementation, the partners will coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with MOA directorates, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector. To ensure diversity and a balanced gender and age representation, communities’ residents, mainly women children and elderly family members, will participate in all stages of the project cycle. PARC and UWAC will be the local partners responsible for the collection and analysis of baseline and end line data each according to its area of experience with AAH’s support for the latter. PARC and UWAC have been fully involved in the project design, identification of target area and communities, and development of technical components of the activities. The targeted areas and activities will be divided between the two partners, who be responsible for monitoring the implementation of activities, together with AAH. Cooperating with line ministries and working the partners will ensure sustainability, participation, empowerment and supporting the development of self-protection. A.1 Improve the access to agricultural water for vulnerable farming communities (Hebron, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Jericho and Tubas): the intervention will reduce the input costs for their farming activities. The proposed activities will target the families who are under the threat of forced displacement and their lands under the threat of confiscation. The interventions will cover construction and rehabilitation of water pipes (20 Km), 80 agricultural cisterns and bonds, and rehabilitate 3 springs. A.2 Livestock accommodation for 350 vulnerable herders, damaged emergency and poor-quality shelters will be rehabilitated with resistant plastic covers (Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem and Tubas): this emergency intervention will help to ensure hygienic conditions and maintain production levels during milking and milk processing. A.3 Supply of agricultural inputs to improve the total productivity of the farming activities ( Jericho and Tubas): the intervention will target vulnerable farmers with low incomes and under the poverty line and who aren’t ensuring the maximum benefits of their agricultural plots due to the limited financial capacities and Israeli restrictions against construction in (Area C) by supplying them with agricultural tools and materials (steel pipes, plastic sheets, HDPE pipes…etc) for rehabilitating 260 green houses and 50 irrigation systems. A.4 Improve the most vulnerable herding and farming families’ safe access to livelihood assets and services through roads rehabilitation (Hebron, Bethlehem and Ramallah): this will allow them to reach their agricultural lands and grazing areas and, ultimately, protect them from forcible transfer by enabling them to satisfy their basic needs through the rehabilitation of 20 km dirt roads. The proposed activity will also protect their livelihoods assets (land and grazing areas) from confiscation for settler expansion or road construction. A.5 Support vulnerable farmers and herders to rehabilitate their lands under threat of confiscation (close to bypass roads, settlements, military areas, etc.) (Hebron and Bethlehem): this activity HH land and improve their livelihoods by improving land productivity and reducing their inputs’ costs (rehabilitation of 200 Dunums). A.6 Improve access to locally produced pastures (20 dunums per herder, for 500 herders) by providing drought tolerant seeds. The proposed humanitarian intervention will improve target female and male farmers’ food security status by increasing their production levels, improving their own nutrition status and improve their access to grazing areas.

Indicators

A baseline survey will be conducted at the start of the project targeting a representation of all gender and age groups, which will be updated and compared to an end line survey after the activities’ completion. Information will be collected through semi structured interviews, household visits and field observations. Selection criteria will be identified by the partners in coordination with MOA and the related local councils, based on MOA classification for governorates priorities AAH and the partners will coordinate with the related MOA directorate in each governorate to benefit from their assessments and data, the project main target groups are the vulnerable herding and farming families in Area C. Ongoing monitoring will allow AAH and PARC to evaluate methodologies and the impact of the activities, to identify challenges and best practices. Particular attention will be paid to beneficiary selection based on diversity including their economical and displacement vulnerability, methods of working with village councils, mechanism of asset distribution and gender sensitive approach. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation; the results communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through the establishment of AAH’s standard beneficiary feedback mechanism. Output 4: 350 herders (1680 women and men) receive technical and material assistance to rehabilitate their animal shelters.Output 5: 500 herding HH (2400 individuals) received climate adapted (drought tolerant) seeds for locally produced pastures (20 dunums per herder);Output 6: 70 farmers (336 individuals) benefited from the rehabilitation of 200 dunums The expected objective verifiable indicators and targets from the project are as follows: - Outcome 1: The livestock mortality and abortion rates for the households targeted by livestock accommodation decreased by 30%;- Outcome 2: The agricultural water cost for the communities targeted by water interventions will decreased by 35%;- Outcome 3: The targeted farmers targeted by agricultural inputs will improve their productivity by more than 25%.
