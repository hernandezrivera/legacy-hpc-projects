Project Summary

The “Provision of life saving interventions among vulnerable children under five years of age, pregnant and lactating women and cash for nutrition trainings and referrals of complicated SAM cases to Stabilization Centers in kapoeta East County” ” is aimed at; strengthening the referral system especially of cases with complicated Sever Acute Malnutrition (SAM) to Stabilization centers. improving MIYCN, WASH & health care seeking practices, the utilization of therapeutic foods, adherence to treatment, immunization and routine medication coverage in Kapoeta East County. This will be achieved through, capacity strengthening of health workers, training and mentorship of CNVs and the MtMSGs, promoting positive social behavior change among the targeted households and through cash transfer to caregivers of children referred to SC and to members the MtMSGs who have completed all the MIYCN and WASH training packages. The project will target a total of 23 health/nutrition centers (PHCCs/PHCUs) in Kapoeta East County. AFSS will use the already existing Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs) and as well form new groups where needed. The MtMSGs will be trained on the optimal Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) and Maternal and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices, proper use of therapeutic foods, gender and protection. These trainings will be conducted during SC and OTP clinic days. Cash will be transferred to the targeted beneficiaries on condition that they have completed all the training packages. Additionally, cash will be given to mothers/caregivers of referred/transferred children 6-59 months with complicated SAM on condition that they have arrived and have been enrolled at SC. Prior to the project implementation, AFSS staff, health workers, Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and MtMSGs will be oriented on the project implementation modalities. The Cash transfer component of the project will constitute 30% (cash for referrals and cash for trainings) and the remaining 70% will cater for the nutrition sensitive interventions such as; Community Mobilization and sensitization on programme activities, Community dialogues on Nutrition, mentorship and coaching of CNVs and screening for acute malnutrition, trainings the MtMSGs on the optimal WASH and MIYCN practices and follow up of the project beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

Based on the June 2019 Integrated Food Phase Classification-Acute Malnutrition Analysis (IPC-AMN) report which shows that one county of the 44 counties are in Extreme Critical phase (IPC-AMN Phase 5) while the remaining 43 counties are in IPC Acute Malnutrition Nutrition phase 4 including Kapoeta East County. Additionally, the FSNMS 2019 reported that the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) for children 0-59 months was 11.6% (Nationwide) which is classified as serious (WHO emergency threshold). According to the same assessment, the complementary feeding practices which have a bearing on the GAM were very sub-optimal, where less than half of all children (47.7%) 6-8 months of age fed on solid/semisolid foods, only 20% of children 6-23 months received diversified foods, nearly a quarter of children 6-23 months (24.3%) met their minimum desired meal frequency and only 7.1% met the minimum acceptable diet. Furthermore, in the Greater Equatorial, access to and use of latrines was at 45%. It was attributed to insufficient and damaged infrastructure, and in some places, cultural norms around sanitation practices. Which may have influenced the high prevalence of water or vector borne diseases. Where up to 74% of the households self-reported diseases such as malaria, fever, and acute watery diarrhea (AWD). Several development partners including UNICEF, WFP, SSHF, AFSS together with the government of South Sudan have been providing nutrition and health support to the vulnerable communities in Kapoeta East County. However, the support provided is inadequate and cannot fully address the health and malnutrition related challenges as seen in the recent health and nutrition surveys. Most of the nutrition interventions have largely been nutrition specific aimed at treating acute malnutrition as stipulated in the CMAM guidelines. According to the Interim Guidelines on the CMAM of South Sudan (2017), children with complicated SAM are to be referred to SC for inpatient care. Yet, due to factors like; long distances, inadequate financial resources to care for children left at home, poor attitude towards SC, and limited male involvement in key family care practices, there is a relatively low enrollment of referred complicated SAM cases into SC. This results into late case management, poor adherence to treatment and subsequently malnutrition related deaths. However, the CMAM guidelines does not clearly stipulate nutrition sensitive components for sustainability which should be able to prevent occurrence and re-occurrence of acute malnutrition as it is mostly based on the “identify and treat” approach. Therefore, to address malnutrition sustainably, scientific evidence supports a need for combined approach of nutrition specific and sensitive programmes, and integration of available livelihood interventions. It is against this back ground, AFSS proposes to implement the “Provision of life saving interventions among vulnerable children under five years of age, pregnant and lactating women as well as the cash for nutrition trainings and referrals of complicated SAM cases to Stabilization Centers in kapoeta East County” with the objective of improving MIYCN practices, immunization coverage, the use of therapeutic foods and WASH practices among households with children (6-59 months)and to strengthen the referral system and improve adherence to treatment.

2 - Activities

Training of AFSS staff;(30, 20 male and 10 female) A five-day training will be conducted for the AFSS project staff on the project implementation modality, gender and protection and on the MIYCN and WASH. Baseline assessment; this will be conducted at the beginning of the project with focus on the project’s thematic areas. Findings from this assessment will be used to inform programme design and to monitor the project progress. Orientation of stakeholder/key project players (30 ,2 per Payam and the other two from the county ); orientation meetings with the county Health Department, health workers of the targeted health facilities and CNVs will be conducted with the aimed af familiarizing the key project players with the proposed project implementation modalities. Community mobilization and sensitization; this activity will be conducted by the CNVs at the community level and health workers at the health facility level so as to create awareness and increase programme coverage. Training of MtMSGs on MIYCN and WASH; adopting and contextualizing the existing ministry of health training manuals for optimal WASH and MIYCN practices, AFSS will train at least two MtMSGs per health facility. Food and cooking demonstrations; to strengthen MIYCN practices AFSS will conduct food and cooking demonstration using all the locally available food in each of the targeted health facilities. Establishing vegetable gardens; AFSS will establish at least one vegetable garden at each nutrition center. The purpose will be to promote the production and consumption of micronutrient rich vegetables Community dialogues;(28 , 1per Payam per quarter) for community involvement and complaints and feedback, AFSS will hold community dialogues in each of the targeted health facility catchment areas on a quarterly basis. This will ensure that the views of the targeted populations are taken into consideration. The dialogues meeting will also be used as platforms to promote male involvement in key family care practices. Knowledge Attitude and Practice assessments; to strengthen documentation and evidence generation AFSS will integrated KAP assessments into routine programme activities. This will improve the measurement of knowledge and behaviors changes among the programme beneficiaries. Mentorship of health workers, CNVs and MtMSGs on cash for referral; the CNVS, health workers and MtMSGs will be oriented on the modality of the cash for referral and training as well. Training on the formulation of Sorghum-Peanut-Blend (SPB) (METU-1); caregivers of SAM patients will be trained on the formulation of METU-1 using locally available materials (sorghum flour, peanut powder, ghee and honey). This is a highly nutritious therapeutic blend that can be used to manage and prevent acute malnutrition at the household level. This formulation was developed and tested in karamoja region in Uganda. AFSS will therefore empower the local communities in kapoeta East County with this skill as a sustainability strategy to prevent relapsing of already cured SAM patients (1 training session per site per month). The training sessions will be integrated with the MIYCN and WASH training sessions. Nutrition sensitive loverhood activities will include: Smallholder poultry farming; to be able to transfer knowledge and skill in poultry farming and ultimately contribute to increased diet diversify as well generate income from the sale of poultry and eggs, AFSS will form 46 MTMSGs constituting of 15 mothers (their husbands will be encouraged to join) who will be trained and provided with 322 local birds (5 hens and 2 cocks) per group. A boma learning centre of the poultry farming will be established in the home of a lead mothers. Cash transfer to caregivers of referred patients.Caregivers of children 6-59month with complicated SAM referred to the SC for appropriate care will be given cash worth $35 upon arrival at SC This will enable them to purchase diversified food for the rest other children

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM); helpdesks will be established at all the nutrition sites (24 sites) in which beneficiaries or community can report any complaint in relation to Nutrition services. These help desks will be headed by one member from the community. The complaints will then be shared with AFSS on a weekly basis. Reports on these complaints/feedback will be given to the government and other relevant stakeholders on a weekly and monthly basis. Community sensitization on PSEA and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse toll free numbers; sensitization and awareness creation of the beneficiaries on the toll free numbers will be integrated into the onsite nutrition and health education sessions and will also be printed on the IEC materials including AFSS visibility T-shirts. AFSS will integrate knowledge assessment of the numbers into routine data collection tools.AFSS will ensure that people with special needs are taken care of despite of their disabilities during the implementation period.For those unable to read or write, posters that carry pictures on PSEA, where and how to report will be displayed at each site.All beneficiaries will be enlightened on the modalities of the project, where to report cases of abuse and how they will get their feedback.The beneficiaries will be encouraged to select one member from their community who will be responsible for reporting any form of PSEA imposed to them by AFSS staff to the reverent authorities such that measures are taken against such staff.
