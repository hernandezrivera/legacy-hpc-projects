Project Summary

The HNO -2023 report indicated that 65% of participants required Support from MHPSS services,72% of KIs were recently displaced, 27% were grappling with housing, land, and property HLP issues, and 12% were facing restrictions on freedom of movement.
“Voices from Syria 2023" confirmed GBV as a pressing issue affecting women and community organizations. GBVIMS+ reported 99.6% of incidents involving female survivors, with 55.8% occurring at home. GBV forms included physical assault 41.5%, psychological abuse 27.4%, and resource denial 22.4%.
According to a rapid need assessment conducted by WSA in 2023, in the locations (Hazano, Kafer Lucin, and Kafer Takhreem ) with groups of women, girls, men, and boys, including people with disabilities and the elderly.
Most beneficiaries believe that WGSSs must continue providing various activities for women and girls to achieve a more significant impact in the long term and create positive change in society by a more significant percentage towards women's issues and GBV.
The primary objective of this project is to support the operation of three WGSS to continue addressing the heightened risks of GBV resulting from the EQs disaster and those affected by the ongoing crisis in NW Syria during the years 2024 and 2025
In 2024 the project will focus on:
-GBV Specialized Services for women and girl survivors.
- Prevention Activities GBV: PSS, life skills, recreational initiatives, Awareness sessions, and advocacy campaigns.
-Income-generating empowerment activities for women and girls.
- Legal assistance is provided through consultations for internally displaced women and men affected by the conflict.
This involves addressing various legal issues, rights, available legal options, supporting documentation, and assisting in obtaining essential documents.
- Capacity Building: WSA will provide GBV basic concepts training for non-GBV-specialized humanitarians on how to deal with GBV survivors and how to refer them.
- Also, WSA will focus on supporting 14 WLOs In Idlib and its countryside, and empowering them to play an active role in addressing GBV within their communities.
- (IPA) will be provided to all groups, emphasizing the most vulnerable individuals within and outside WGSS and addressing their immediate needs
-Provide prevention activities, Parenting skills program for care givers and awareness on CP.
The three WGSSs are equipped to receive women and girls with disabilities; after assessing the type of disability and the needs and desires of women and girls with disabilities, they are directed to activities appropriate to their situation
and regarding the elderly women, we will target them during the implementation of the activities in proportion to
PSEA Strategy:
WSA has a clear policy statement outlining zero tolerance for PSEA within the Safe Spaces project.
WSA implements robust recruitment and screening processes for staff and volunteers, including background checks and training on PSEAH policies and procedures.
Establishing and communicating clear codes of conduct for all individuals involved in the project, emphasizing respectful behavior and zero tolerance for abuse.
Provide comprehensive training on gender equality, women's rights, consent, and respectful behavior to staff, volunteers, and beneficiaries.
WSA established confidential and accessible reporting mechanisms for incidents or concerns related to sexual exploitation, abuse, or harassment.
Train staff and volunteers on how to respond promptly, sensitively, and effectively to reports of incidents, including referral pathways for support services.
By designing interventions that correspond to HRP, WSA ensures a strategic approach in its work, promoting comprehensive and targeted support for neglected and vulnerable women and girls, people with disabilities (PwDs), the elderly, and those affected in NWS
During the project, (18090) services will be targeted.
