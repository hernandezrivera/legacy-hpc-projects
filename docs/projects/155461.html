Project Summary

The Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) assessment report of June 2018 reported a deterioration in the food security situation during the peak hunger period (January to March 2019). According to the report, over 1.1 million people are at risk of being food insecure during the peak hunger period. Adding to that, the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) which was conducted in May 2018 projected that over 2.4 million people will be food insecure during the peak hunger period and at least 1.7 million people are projected to be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). The country is likely to experience an increase in numbers of children with acute malnutrition resulting from food insecurity, compromised child care and feeding practices due to negative coping mechanisms. The drought situation could be further exacerbated by the recent cholera outbreak and on-going poor macro-economic situation affecting access to basic food commodities.
It is anticipated that 72,228 children could be affected by acute malnutrition (25,280 SAM and 46,948 MAM) in all 63 districts in the country between January and June 2019. In addition, 360,000 young children (0 to 23 months), 905,933 children aged 6 to 59 months and 575,384 pregnant and lactating women with elevated nutrition needs in the selected 24 districts are likely to be immediately affected by the prevailing and up-coming food insecurity including through the looming El-Nino drought (90% likelihood according to the most recent SARCOF analysis).
The UNICEF response in the affected districts will aim at: 1. Supporting active screening for early identification and referral of children with acute malnutrition through community based health workers and community volunteers. 2. Capacity building for facility based health workers on treatment, management and reporting of children with acute malnutrition. 3. Procurement, pre-positioning and distribution of life-saving nutrition commodities (F75, F100, RUTF and anthropometric materials to all affected districts. 4. Provision of community level support for IYCF-e to parents and caregivers of children below the age of two years through the establishment of mothers support/care groups for Maternal, Infant and Young Child Feeding. 5. Provision of micro-nutrients (vitamin A for children aged 6 to 59 months; multiple micro-nutrient powders for children aged 6 to 23 months; iron and folate for pregnant women; and provision of zinc in diarrhoea management. 6. Provide effective coordination for the Nutrition Sector response and strengthen government coordination structures at provincial, district and sub-district levels.
From July 2019 to April 2020:
"1. To provide leadership for a coordinated nutrition response to women and children affected by the drought.
2. To protect the nutrition status of vulnerable children under the age of five years (includes children with disabilities and those affected by HIV; pregnant and lactating women from deterioration due to the impact of the drought.
3. To provide life-saving nutrition treatment to all children affected by acute malnutrition.
4. To provide community level Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-e) support to parents and caregivers of children below the age of two years in collaboration with the HIV programme.
5. To improve decision making and effective nutrition response through strengthened data collection, reporting and information systems using the rapid-pro platform.

Activities

Activities that have been selected to ensure that children affected by acute malnutrition are identified early, referred to the nearest health facility for treatment; effectively treated for for SAM; followed up and supported in the community and that the program is smoothly implemented and coordinated are given below:• Facilitate coordination of government and development partners for nutrition emergency preparedness and response through monthly coordination meetings including through the Provincial, District and sub-district Food and Nutrition Security Committee structures • Increase access to supplies and efficient stock management of life-saving therapeutic and supplementary foods at Health facility and community levels through an integrated system under the Ministry of Health and Child Care.• Conduct seasonal nutrition assessment in partnership with WFP, FAO, MoH and FNC• Implement active screening for early identification and referral of children with acute malnutrition through community based health workers and community volunteers • Capacity building of facility based health workers on treatment, management and reporting of acute malnutrition• Provide Community IYCF support and counselling specifically where cash and/or food assistance intervention are provided in coordination with WASH and Food Security Clusters• Provide child and maternal micronutrient supplementation (VAS, MNPs, Iron and folate for pregnant women and increased consumption of fortified staple foods) and reach specified areas through village health days• Continue to support implementation of community nutrition resilience program through the multi-sectoral community based model (MCBM) in all at risk districts to prevent an increase in acute malnutrition. From July 2019 to April 2020:1. Increase access to and efficient stock management of life-saving therapeutic and supplementary foods at Health facility and community levels2.
