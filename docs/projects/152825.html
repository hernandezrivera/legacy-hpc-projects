Project Summary

The goal of this intervention is to provide quality lifesaving CMAM interventions to children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women in 14 Bomas of Greater Bagari (Baggari, Bazia and Besselia).

Through this project, Johanniter will screen over 90% of children under five years 9,564 (4,878 girls and boys 4,686) and also over 90% of pregnant and lactating women 1,910 for malnutrition in the 14 Bomas of Bagari and Besselia areas. Additionally a total of 4,027 women and 425 men will directly be reached through MIYCN messaging and counselling at community level. The project will reach at least 80% of SAM and MAM in girls and boys 6-59 months; 80% of PLW. The estimated number of SAM cases is 628, MAM in children under five years 1,913 and MAM in PLW 523. Indirect beneficiaries of this project will include 790 males who are caretakers of children under five, County health department staff, health workers and Community nutrition volunteers.
The project will be a continuation of the current emergency nutrition response in Bagari, Bazia and Besselia by Johanniter under the SSHF 2018. The project will be complemented by on-going UNICEF and WFP projects in Wau and Jur River, and a GBV project privately funded under Johanniter which will support with protection services.
In an effort to address the prevailing humanitarian situation in the Baggari, Bazia and Besselia area, Johanniter is requesting SSHF funds to continue the current emergency nutrition intervention which has already seen nutrition centers established, staff trained, increased outreach activities, formation of mother to mother groups, reporting and referral system set up. This will also complement the on-going UNICEF and WFP projects to provide integrated nutrition services to the affected and vulnerable populations. At the moment, Johanniter is final stages of development of the PCA with UNICEF and FLA with WFP to cover the OTPs and TSFP activities in Baggari for the period of 2019.
The 250,000 USD that is being requested for will ensure that there are no gaps or interruption in services to the population that has been greatly affected by prolonged insecurity, limited access to basic services and displaced. Under this funding, Johanniter will scale up outreach activities, train health workers on CMAM and MIYCN, recruit more nutrition staff to meet the minimum standards in the nutrition sites, integrate WASH interventions particularly messaging and WASH supplies at facilities so as to reduce on WASH related diseases, setup feedback mechanisms at the facilities, referral system for both complicated medical cases and GBV cases.

Needs

Greater Bagari (Baggari, Bazia and Besselia) continues to face multiple humanitarian challenges. Continued insecurity as a result of fighting has led to population displacements, access constraints for both civilians and humanitarian actors, inability to carry out agricultural activities which coupled with hyperinflation and deteriorating economy has resulted in a fragile food security situation. The recent fighting that started in June to August 2018 saw some 2,000 IDPs displaced to Agok and Hai Masna near Wau town and many more within Bagari. WFP led an Interagency Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism (IRRM) mission to Greater Bagari areas ( Farajallah, Ngodakalla, Ngisa, Govendeko and Ngoku) from 5th to 9th September 2018 to carry out an urgent needs assessment and deliver response on food assistance, health and nutrition, protection, WASH and NFIs. The findings evidently pointed to the fact that the conflict had destroyed the existing structures and systems including schools and health facilities, homes burnt, food stocks looted, thousands of civil population displaced to the bushes and eating wild roots and leaves, reports of high rates of sexual violence including rape and killing of civilians, no basic services, no functioning market hence increasing their vulnerability to malnutrition. About 1,649 children from 6-59 months were screened using MUAC and height measuring stick of which 76 had SAM (4.6%) and 321 MAM (19.46%). MAM among 795 PLW screened was 66 (8%), SAM 45 (5%). The most urgent needs identified by the population were food and agricultural inputs, shelter and non-food items, WASH and health services, and nutrition supplies. A similar assessment was done in 5 Bomas of Bazia (Gedi, Maju, Bazia center, Nakpataguru and Tirgka) from the 20th to 23rd September 2018. About 864 children from 6-59 months were screened using MUAC and height measuring stick of which 47 had SAM (5.3%) and 92 MAM (10.6%). MAM among 995 PLW screened was 150 (15.1%). According to the September 2018 IPC analysis, it is expected that Greater Baggari population is in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). The reasons cited for the counties including Baggari in IPC phase 5 included; harvests likely to be poor, access to humanitarian assistance limited for a section of the population, insecurity, lack of freedom of movement and extreme depletion of livelihoods. The report also mentions extreme levels of acute food insecurity expected to persist and recommended immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring. While there have been limited improvements in access to the Great Baggari area, at the time of writing it remains a challenge and on-going negotiations will be required to ensure immediate, free, safe and unhindered access to the affected communities. Johanniter is currently providing nutrition services to the displaced communities in 8 of the 14 OTP/TSFP sites under SSHF with supplies from UNICEF and WFP. The other 6 sites which were initially functional were destroyed during the recent fighting and in need of rehabilitation. However outreach activities are on-going in these areas.

Activities

1) Identification (active and passive case findings) and treatment of acutely malnourished children 6-59 months and PLWs 2) Integration of nutrition activities in all existing health facilities. 3) Support CHD/SMOH to conduct regular supportive supervision to nutrition sites 4) Integrated nutrition outreach activities and referrals. 4) Training for health workers and community nutrition volunteers and MIYCN counselors 5) Provision of Vitamin A supplementation and deworming to children under 5 years disaggregated by gender. 6) Promotion of MIYCN and mother to mother support groups 7) Participate and support National, State and County Nutrition Cluster forums 8) Monitor and analyse the nutrition situation on a quarterly basis 9) Monthly sharing of NIS and sitrep reports to the nutrition cluster 10) integrate health and WASH in the nutrition activities provision of safe drinking water equipment like buckets and jerrycans, hand washing facility and soap for daily operation of OTP/TSFP and emphasis on awareness raising on hygiene and sanitation, EPI activities, malaria and HIV awareness campaigns.11) Participate in Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Systems (FSNMS)12) Test of malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months 13) Conduct promotion of nutrition education through schools

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The beneficiaries are involved in the project cycle from the beginning. They will be involved in selection of the Community Nutrition Workers/Volunteers (CNW/V) and also in the feedback mechanism. Together with the CNVs and mother to mother support groups messaging will be discussed that will be tailored to the local context, gender and age. The groups involved will have representation from the different age groups and gender including adolescents and males to ensure comprehensive information and key areas addressed.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Johanniter will engage the CHD, target beneficiaries, community leaders and other stakeholders in decision making with regards to; safety considerations for women (including lactating and pregnant mothers), boys, girls and special categories e.g. persons with disabilities, distributions being inclusive and having no biases whether IDP or host community for purposes of neutrality, and addressing safe access and distances to be traveled. Community input will guide us in identifying locations for outreaches and also distribution days. The nutrition sites will have adequate facilities such as toilets, water and shades available to protect people’s especially women’s well-being and dignity. Johanniter will ensure that it’s’ program staff are well inducted/trained on the job in order for them to roll out this ‘do harm approach’ to the other volunteers in the field. Safe programming will also include accountability through complaint feedback mechanisms. Johanniter will commit to Interagency Standing Committee (IASC) Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian particularly through risk mitigation for example familiarizing staff with the guidelines, trainings, community awareness and advocacy, regular reporting on GBV in cluster meetings, working with partners and early referrals of victims. Johanniter is a recipient of UN funds and therefore complies with minimum standards included in the agreements and also Johanniter own code of conduct.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Johanniter has been working closely with the county health department (CHD), community leaders, health facility staff and the mother to mother groups that also includes some men. These consultations are through community sensitization during outreaches, in mother to mother support groups and through the feedback mechanisms set at the health facilities.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Johanniter will set up a feedback mechanism in all the operation sites (static and mobile) as it has done in other project locations. The beneficiaries will be made aware of the reporting channels at each of the operation sites. Any complaint raised by the beneficiaries will be discussed at community level, at field office level or at Juba level and solutions provided. In order for our beneficiaries/clients to receive the quality care that they deserve, any form of harassment or abuse or negligence by our staff reported will be investigated and once confirmed will face necessary actions taken by Johanniter/CHD and RRC.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will work with the community nutrition volunteers who are identified by the community and share concerns and complaints from the community for example about days of nutrition activities, location of services and use of nutrition supplies. This information will guide Johanniter in reassessing the implementation of activities so as to ensure beneficiaries get quality services as is expected.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This response strategy will ensure more equitable and better-quality nutrition services to Girls and boys under age five, adolescent girls and women and in a timely manner. Mainstreaming and/ or integrating protection will be central to nutrition programming through appropriate nutrition response mechanisms that protects beneficiaries from increased insecurity risks, training protection staff on MUAC screening and referral of children with protection concerns to nutrition sites. In turn, nutrition staff will be trained on non-specialized protection package to identify and refer children with protection concerns for specialized care. Special attention will be given to prevention of neglect among vulnerable groups to accessing nutrition services among care givers and nutrition staff. Further, nutrition services will be considered as potential entry point for GBV victims looking for assistance, with nutrition staff to be trained on GBV cases management and referral pathway.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Most of the locations in Bagari and Besselia are accessible by road throughout the year. However, the road condition is more favorable during the dry session. During the joint assessments and response, Johanniter was able to access most of the areas where we intend to implement activities. There is no airstrip in Baggari and Besselia area. Johanniter has a strong working relationship with the Local leaders and RRC who can advise on security matters and accessibility and will be consulted on all movement within or outside of Bagari and Besselia prior to departure. Maintaining a good relationship with local government administrators and community leadership enhances Johanniter’s acceptance in the area. Following the fighting between June and August 2018, access to Greater Baggari has been a challenge since we need to get written permission from security office in Wau through UNOCHA before we are able to go to sites. Since this written permission is given on a two weekly basis at the moment, Johanniter ensures that we work closely with UNOCHA office in Wau so that they facilitate communication with the government in timely manner to prevent gaps in service delivery.
