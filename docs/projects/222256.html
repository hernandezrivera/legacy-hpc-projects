Project Summary

Malnutrition in South Sudan is driven by compounding factors like conflict, increased food insecurity and other factors associated with climate change. Flooding in the country has also ravaged farm lands affecting the food production and therefore poor food diversity.
According to the IPC analysis released in November 2024, the acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation is deteriorating in the country and that can be directly attributed the factors related to climate change, conflict and displacements and also adverse flooding that has been ongoing in the country and has affected over 1.4 million people in 22 counties.
The IPC analysis further shows that over 6.3 million people that over 47% of the total population are facing acute food insecurity of IPC3+. The situation is expected to continue to deteriorate as contributing factors are not improving ultimately.
The nutrition situation is further in deterioration with over 2.1 million children under five suffering from acute malnutrition out of which over 650000 will be having severe acute malnutrition. Also over 1.1 million pregnant and lactating mothers are also having malnutrition and this is a worrying trend since it exposes women and children to low immunity and the risk of opportunistic infections thus increasing child and maternal mortality rates.
This project will address the nutrition needs of the children in Ayod, Bor South and Duk counties by working closely with the nutrition cluster and ministry of health to support nutrition prevention activities. This will include family MUAC that will ensure that children with malnutrition are identified in the communities on early stages and referred to the nutrition centers for support and treatment.
Women Agency for Resilience and Transformation (WART) will also Ensure that mother to mother support groups and father to father support groups are formed to support families and children with malnutrition with key malnutrition messages. Cooking demonstrations for mothers to understand food diversity and ways of hygienic and quality food preparation using available food items to ensure that the food is well balanced will be conducted. This will ensure that mothers can prepare well balanced diets for their families and thus greatly reducing malnutrition in the country.
WART will support management of acute malnutrition through supplementary feeding programme that will be done in collaboration with UNICEF and ministry of health through mobile interventions and thus ensuring that children with malnutrition are reached with key treatment interventions according to the Ministry of health guidelines on treatment of acute malnutrition.
WART will ensure that key IYCF messages are passed to the communities through the mother to mother support groups that will promote early initiation of breast feeding and exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months of live. This will ensure that children acquire the right nutrition and antibodies necessary improved immunity.
WART will work very closely with other sectors like WASH, food Security and Livelihood, health and protection to ensure that there is integration of Nutrition services in these key intervention that will include key nutrition messages during hygiene promotion and health promotion talks, other nutrition services like distribution of WASH kits in nutrition centers and IEC materials with key nutrition messages that will ensure that communities are informed on how to prevent malnutrition in the community.
WART will also work closely with the ministry to support government led initiatives like vitamin A supplementation, world breastfeeding week and deworming that are key at improving the malnutrition status of the communities in the target counties.
This nutrition interventions will be done in Ayod, Duk and Bor South Counties and will be targeting 10000 children and pregnant mothers (3000 female, 3500 boys and 3500 girls).

1. Needs

Acute malnutrition in South Sudan remains a major issue of concern as the malnutrition status of children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers continue to deteriorate year in year out.According to the IPC analysis Nov 2024, the food security situation in the country looks precarious with over 47% (6.3 million people) of the total population experiencing acute food insecurity. Out of this over 1.7 million are experiencing critical levels of food insecurity classified as IPC 4 emergency situation.The war in Sudan has led to influx of returnees and this according to the report over 35000 returnees are experiencing catastrophic levels of food insecurity. The situation is not expected to improve with the country experiencing sporadic flooding that has already affected over 1.4 million people and displaced over 379000 people in 22 counties across the country.(Flood Snapchat Nov 2024). This has destroyed farm lands and thus increases the risk of malnutrition in the country.Ayod County is experiencing severe flooding with over 26700 people already affected by the flooding and According to FSNMS R-29 report shows Ayod GAM rate of 23.7% ( FSNMS R 29 Nov 2024) that’s way above the 15% WHO emergency threshold for acute malnutrition. Over 30000 people are also in emergency phase of acute food insecurity and thus increasing the risk of malnutrition in the country.Over 2.1 million children in the country are experiencing acute malnutrition or are expected to experience malnutrition up to June 2025 and this is an increase of 20.3% from the numbers that were projected last year meaning the food security and nutrition status in the country continues to deteriorate due to factors associated with insecurity, influx of returnees from the Sudan conflict, poor food production associated with climate change and other compounding factors.According to a coordinated IRNA conducted in Ayod county in September 2024, over 25000 people have been displaced by flooding . There were clear signs of acute malnutrition in the county. During the assesment, there increased prevalence of malnutrition in the assessed locations evidenced by proxy GAM rate 46% obtained through MUAC screening where a total of 232 screened, 124 had normal MUAC, 96 had moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and 12 had severe acute malnutrition (SAM). This clearly shows the need for nutrition interventions in the county to avert deterioration of the situation thus contributing to increased mortality and morbidity. (AYOD IRNA Sep 2024).A similar situation is also witnessed in Duk County with also over 15000 people also affected by flooding and having severe food insecurity. Poor WASH practices, food diversity and increased prevalence of malaria and acute watery diarrhoea associated with flooding and increased food insecurity are likely to increase cases of malnutrition in the target counties. This therefore calls for concerted efforts to reduce cases of malnutrition through multifaced approach and integration of services geared towards prevention and management of children, pregnant and lactating mothers with malnutrition in Ayod, Duk and Bor South Counties.

2. Project activities

• WART will conduct 3 KAP surveys to understand the nutrition situation in the target counties geared towards improving the nutrition status of the children under five, pregnant and brestfeeding mothers.• Vit A supplementation and Deworming campaigns will also be conducted to children under five in collaboration with the ministry of health and the nutrition cluster.• There will be distribution of family MUACs that will ensure that children with malnutrition are identified on time and are able to get early treatment in the nutrition centres and thus improving treatment outcomes.• Formation of mother to mother support groups and father to father support groups will be done to ensure that they are emotionally supported with counselling good nutrition practices.• There will be kitchen garden establishments and cooking demonstrations that will ensure that mothers have knowledge on food diversity and thus improving the nutrition status of their children.• There will be training of nutrition workers in CMAM guidelines that will equip them with the knowledge on how to manage malnutrition both at the OTP, TSFP and Stabilization centres. They will also be trained on how to test for malaria and the nutrition centres and thus reducing cases of malnutrition.• Early childhood development activities will be integrated in the nutrition centres to ensure that children cognitive values are improved through play and toys that will support development of the children.• WART will also support iron folic acid distribution to adolescent girls targeting 3000 girls in the three target counties.• WART will also support 15 nutrition centres that will ensure that the 15000 target population get nutrition services through OTP and TSFP and referral of the children with SAM with medical complications to stabilization centres for support.
