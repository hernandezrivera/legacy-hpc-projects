Project Summary

The proposed project aims to enhance the living conditions of displaced communities in targeted sites by strengthening the predictability and effectiveness of service delivery and protection mechanisms, with a long-term goal of fostering durable solutions. The project focuses on empowering displaced communities through inclusive governance structures, promoting meaningful participation in site management, and advancing coordination and information-sharing among all stakeholders.
Through community-led initiatives, the project will encourage displaced individuals to take active roles in care and maintenance activities that improve safety and hygiene, thereby mitigating protection and health risks. Site maintenance activities will be supported through cash-for-work programs, providing temporary income opportunities for displaced individuals while addressing critical infrastructure and site management needs.
DRC will establish or strengthen inclusive governance structures and thematic committees at each site, ensuring balanced representation of women, youth, and vulnerable groups. These structures will actively engage in CCCM coordination mechanisms alongside humanitarian partners and local authorities. Capacity-building efforts will include targeted training sessions for governance members, committee participants, and local authorities to enhance their ability to collaboratively address site needs and promote community self-reliance.
The project will also prioritize accountability through robust complaint feedback mechanisms, enabling displaced communities to voice their concerns and influence decision-making. By integrating community-driven approaches, this initiative will support safer, more dignified living conditions while fostering ownership and sustainability within displacement sites.

1. Needs

South Sudan remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian landscapes, grappling with significant displacement, acute protection risks, and pervasive resource shortages. Protracted conflict, environmental hazards, and spillover crises—such as the ongoing conflict in Sudan—exacerbate these challenges. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024, over 9 million people require humanitarian assistance, with displacement and basic service shortages reaching critical levels. As of October 2024, over 880,000 people remain in 124 formal and informal displacement sites across South Sudan, many of which face limited access to coordinated services, community support, and vital information. In 2024, more than 320,000 individuals in these sites lacked Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) support due to resource constraints, leaving significant gaps in assistance. Additionally, the CCCM Cluster anticipates that 15% of returnees from Sudan will settle in existing IDP sites, further straining resources and necessitating a coordinated response to ensure safe access to assistance and protection. Unity and Upper Nile States remain among the hardest-hit areas, hosting an estimated 135,284 IDPs in Unity and 127,694 IDPs in Upper Nile, alongside over 120,000 refugees in camps such as Pamir, Ajuong Thok, and Yida. In Abyei, over 50,000 displaced individuals and host community members face severe service gaps across 41 locations. The Sudan conflict has further compounded vulnerabilities, with 175,493 families (732,197 individuals) arriving in South Sudan through Upper Nile State since its onset. Of the 857,625 individuals crossing various border entry points, 528,923 intend to stay in Upper Nile. This surge highlights the urgent need for strengthened coordination, as outlined in the 2024 HNO and cluster evaluations. DRC’s CCCM response directly addresses these critical needs across IDP camps, PoCs, informal settlements, and host communities in key locations such as Malakal, Fashoda, and Unity State’s Rubkona, Guit, and Koch Counties. In Unity State, Rubkona and Rotriak remain some of the most severely affected areas due to the 2024 flooding. Combined with the pressures of returnees from Sudan, these regions require continued and adaptive multisectoral interventions to manage protracted crises while addressing new emergencies. DRC will prioritize community-driven approaches, empowering displaced populations to engage in planning and decision-making, thereby enhancing ownership and self-reliance.In Malakal and other flood-affected areas of Upper Nile, DRC is intensifying its efforts to improve service delivery, strengthen coordination mechanisms, and advocate for essential needs such as shelter and Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights. This comprehensive approach aims to safeguard displaced populations navigating resource scarcity and prolonged displacement.In Abyei Administrative Area, where nearly 85% of the population requires humanitarian assistance, DRC’s interventions focus on facilitating collaboration among local actors, humanitarian agencies, and international partners. This contested area, affected by cross-border tensions, faces acute needs in protection, WaSH, and shelter. DRC will lead coordination efforts to optimize the humanitarian response, build durable support infrastructure, and strengthen access to life-saving services while promoting resilient, community-based solutions. By addressing immediate and long-term needs, DRC’s CCCM strategy will ensure that displaced populations and returnees can safely access assistance, protection, and coordinated services. Through targeted activities, DRC will contribute to durable solutions and foster resilience among South Sudan’s most vulnerable communities.

2. Project activities

Coordination of Humanitarian Services: DRC’s CCCM activities ensure equitable and efficient service delivery through strong stakeholder coordination and accountability. Key actions include:• Site Coordination: Regular meetings with humanitarian actors and community leaders to address gaps and align services.• Cluster Engagement: Active participation in national and state CCCM Clusters, including co-leading in Upper Nile.• Monitoring and Advocacy: Using service mapping, population data, and 5Ws for evidence-based decision-making.Support to Community Leadership and Governance: DRC supports inclusive governance structures to enhance community representation and leadership. Activities include:• Leadership Development: Training community leaders in CCCM principles, conflict resolution, and protection mainstreaming to address site challenges effectively.• Resource Provision: Equipping leadership structures with essential tools to fulfill their roles.• Conflict Resolution Support: Organizing workshops to promote mediation and peaceful community coexistence.Engagement with Local Authorities: DRC collaborates with government counterparts, particularly the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission, to strengthen displacement response capacity. Activities include:• Capacity Building: Providing training and mentorship to government officials on site management and durable solutions.• Strategic Planning Support: Assisting authorities in developing localized displacement response strategies.Community Engagement and Community-Led Initiatives (CLI): Through the “RISE: Resilient, Inclusive, Strong, Empowered” initiative, DRC encourages active community participation. Key actions include:• Community Projects: Supporting construction and maintenance of communal facilities via CLI.• Training and Resources: Providing technical training and resources to ensure sustainability and self-management.• Inclusion and Protection: Mainstreaming gender, age, and diversity considerations through Community-Based Protection Networks (CBPNs).Mobile Response in Hard-to-Reach Areas: The Mobile Response Team (MRT) addresses needs in inaccessible locations through:• Coordination: Facilitating site-level meetings with humanitarian actors to ensure integrated assistance.• Data Collection: Conducting headcounts, service mapping, and CFMs for program planning.• Empowerment: Strengthening local leadership and resilience through CLIs and Cash-for-Work programs.Displacement Monitoring and Information Management: Improved information management supports targeted responses through:• Data Analysis: Conducting population headcounts, needs assessments, and intention surveys for durable solutions.• Information Sharing: Publishing site profiles, 5Ws, and thematic reports to inform stakeholders.• Advocacy: Utilizing data-driven insights to align solutions with community needs.Digital Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM): DRC’s paperless CFM enhances accountability and responsiveness by:• Real-Time Feedback: Collecting and tracking community complaints through digital platforms.• Data Integration: Ensuring alignment between CFM and sectoral programs for coordinated service delivery.• Evidence-Based Advocacy: Using CFM data for service improvement and community communication.Site Maintenance: DRC ensures site safety and hygiene through:• Cash-for-Work Programs: Engaging residents in drainage maintenance, bush clearing, and dyke repairs.• Infrastructure Upgrades: Building new drainages, repairing retention dams, and improving access roads.• Community Prioritization: Empowering leaders to identify and prioritize maintenance needs.
