Project Summary

FSNAU put the 2020 national median GAM prevalence in children at around 13.8% translating to 0.96 million boys and girls. Out of this 162,000 are affected by life threatening severely acute malnutrition while around 800,000 are moderately acute malnourished. Further, despite the positive 2020 post GU outcomes, FSNAU estimated that in 2021 around 2.3 million people (1.14 million for treatment services and 1.16 million for preventive services) in Somalia will require both lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition support and this number includes children (boys and girls) under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women.
The proposed intervention aimed to provide enhanced access to integrated promotives, preventive and curative nutrition services including but not limited to: Regular screening and identification of acutely malnourished child and PLW's, provision of therapeutic and supplementary support, establishing strong referral pathways for those requiring in-patient stabilization care, promotion of optimal child feeding and care practices, provision of essential Vitamins and Micro-nutrient supplements to both children-boys and girls and PLWS's, promotion of appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices, uptake of immunization services in both the static and the mobile centers to reduce the high morbidity and mortality trends emanated from vaccine-preventable diseases as well as poor hygiene and sanitation practices.
Children < 5 years, pregnant and lactating women are the primary target group, as they are considered to be the most vulnerable and are the first to show signs of malnutrition when the slightest form of shock hits. SFH will provide treatment services for acute malnutrition, focusing on quality and coverage and using the existing protocols enshrined in Somali Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition guidelines. In addition, SFH will ensure that the underlying causes of acute and chronic malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies are addressed through the provision of a High impact intervention/BNSP as outlined in the national guidelines.
SFP and OTP activities will be carried out both fixed centers (Bulagaduud-HC, Yontoy, Wirkoy, Barsaanguni and Gobweyn-All newly liberated area in Kismayo and Jamame) in Lower juba being supported by SFH as well as in selected outreach sites to ensure maximum coverage. There are a total of 5 fixed centers and 3 mobile teams in the 2 districts. All SFP/OTP activities at the facility and outreach sites will use an integrated approach and will ensure that all the Basic Nutrition Services Package components are incorporated in the intervention. SFH will engage Community Health Workers and volunteers in follow-ups as well as active case finding and referrals.
Plumpy Nut distribution will be carried out for the severely malnourished children who have been registered in the OTP programme on a daily basis. Bi- Monthly Health and nutrition promotion sessions will be provided mostly for mothers and caretakers of infants and young children to compliment the daily promotion sessions.
The severely malnourished children with medical complications who cannot be managed at the mobile sites will be referred and transported to the in-patient facilities/stabilization center within the district.
Vitamin A supplementation targeting all children 6-59 months will be carried out at both the fixed centers and outreach sites and indicated into their child health cards. Children will be supplemented with Vitamin A twice yearly and other mass campaign strategies such as Child Health Days will be used to increase Vitamin A supplementation. Postpartum women will also receive vitamin A supplementation within six 6 weeks after delivery.
Multiple Micro Nutrients will be administered to pregnant women during the antenatal care clinics to boost their immunity and also to ensure proper growth and development of their unborn babies.

Project Activities

Through 4 fixed nutrition centers and two (2) mobile teams, the proposed intervention will provide enhanced access to integrated curative and preventive nutrition services through the following activities:1. Establish 6 functional fixed and mobile Nutrition sites (OTP) in Kismayo/Badhadhe/jamame.2. Conduct daily screening and identification of acute malnutrition for at least 24000 <5 Children annually at the sites3. 6000 <5 Children screened, identified and treated for moderate acute malnutrition.4. 2000 <5 Children screened, identified and treated for severe acute malnutrition.5. 6000 PLW’s received individual counselling for IYCF6. 1500<5 children are given VAS and Deworming tables annually7. Provide IYCF individual counselling for 6000 pregnant and lactating women annually.8. Conduct family -led MUAC training for 30 care givers and FCHW’s.9. Provide essential micro-nutrient tablets to 2000 PLW’s10. De-worm 2000 pregnant women in their second and third trimesters11. Train of 24 Technical Nutrition staff on CMAM, IYCF and IMCI12. Conduct routine education/promotion sessions including COVID-19 RCCE in both fixed and mobile centers

Mitigation Measures

A) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services . Mitigations:1) Mobile teams will be used to reach the beneficiaries far from the fixed facilities2) Ambulance or project vehicle will be used t ferry the beneficiaries with medical complications and are able to reach the facilities.3) Community health workers will support in the identification/screening and referral of cases at the household levels and by extension accompany the beneficiary where applicable to minimize the risk of violence.B) Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA.Mitigation:1) Establish separate shades and waiting area for men and women2) The staff will be gender dis-aggregated and female nurses will do examinations with men colleagues supporting other culturally appropriate services3) There will be no 1 to 1 engagement with children at all times. Staff will sign PSEA and child protection statements and violating will tantamount to disciplinary action including termination of employment.C) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms Mitigations: 1) AAP Committees will conduct awareness raising prior to project inception on the available complaint/feedback mechanisms available for the beneficiaries to channel their feedback2) The project visibility banners will contain hotline/M&E contact lines where beneficairies will make free calls.3) There will be feedback desk/complaint boxes at the fixed facilities or the distribution centers.

A healthy society free of diseases and malnutrition will be able to withstand sudden shock and build resilience through engaging productive livelihood activities and prevent negative coping strategies that contribute to high burdens of malnutrition and diseases. Female CHW's and care givers trained will be able to detect and refer any signs of malnutrition at the community level that increase health seeking behaviors. Improve child care practices and optimal breastfeeding mean less child mortality and increase growth and development of children both physical and cognitive. Cascading and transferring capacity at the lowest level will strengthen sustainability of services while transitioning to durable solutions.
