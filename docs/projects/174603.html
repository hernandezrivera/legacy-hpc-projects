Project Summary

The resurgence of the Taliban movement and its affiliate organizations in Malakand division since 2004, combined with military operations in 2009 and the displacement of residents created a humanitarian crisis in the area. The devastating floods of July 2010 again created opportunities for militant organizations to regain their lost position by filling the vacuum in relief efforts left by the government and international organizations. This trend reached its climax in 2009 when the Taliban inspired and allied, Tehrik-e-Nifaz-eShariat-e-Mohammadi (TNSM) took over the Swat Valley and banished all Government functionaries operating there. The point of the movement was to impose Sharia Law in Swat and its adjoining areas. Since the end of the State of Swat, judicial processes were going haywire; people in their search for quick justice were relying on Jirgas, Qazis and Political Agents. Ineffective local government was not only the reasons for the conflict but it was further eroded after the violence which created social cleavages. Women’s participation in education and work was not initially a central conflict issue; it became a focal point at a later stage with the destruction of girls’ schools and attacks on working women. These conditions led to great marginalisation and inequity especially the already marginalised including women, children, ethnic, sectarian, and gender minorities. Children including female faced the brunt in shape of education and emotional wellbeing.
In KP there are issues regarding family, properties, inheritance, honour, and women mainly in the remote rural backward areas of KP, particularly Malakand, Swat, Kohistan, Shangla, Dir Lower and Upper, Battagram, and Tor Ghar. Violence against women in various forms is high in KP. There is also issue of land titles requiring reforms. Early/Child and forced marriages exist but there is a hope after the Child Marriage Restraint Act of KP assembly. Cases of Ghag, Swara, exchange marriages, and bride price also prevail. Small children work as domestic helpers while people are availing of cheap labour. Child abuse is also increasing day by day. There are mafias of traffickers of women and children. These practices have acceptance in the society. Misinterpretation of religion, culture, and patriarchy is used to support this.
Religious conflicts exist between Ahle Hadis and the Deobandies, Barelvies and Deobandies, There are shia sunni problems and sectarian violence everywhere in KP but particularly in Kurram district, Hangu, Dera Ismail khan. The Qadianees are persecuted all over Pakistan. While sectarianism was already there Talibanization and various other militant groups further fuelled the conflict between these sectarian groups.
There have been attacks on Christians and their Churches, places of worships of minorities have been attacked such as attacks on churches on Christmas, Easter. Religious freedom is also a problem. Minorities lack the right to practice their religious beliefs, worship, and rituals. There are no places of worships. Hindu family laws are not made till today. Hindu Marriage Act was made in 2017 but since 18 months it does not have rules of business. There is no Sikh marriage law in KP, Christian family laws are as old as 1869. People are forced to resolve divorce and family disputes through Muslims laws. Occasions such as Christmas, Easter, Diwali and holi is celebrated with fear by the Hindus. There are no burials places for minorities.
Transgenders are especially targeted in KP. Since 2015 there has been no justice in the 68 murders of Transgenders in KP and more than 1400 cases of violence in which there has been no punishment . There is traditional bias against the Transgenders and are looked down upon, even the doctors and police consider transgender exceptional and do not understand the dignity of humanity. Activity # 1: 30 Child Protection Centers/day care centers will be established for promoting child rights and other child centered activitiesActivity # 2: 60 Child Protection Committees (30 male and 30 female) are regularly meeting and working on the identification, prevention and referral of CP cases, 20 Action Plans developed to respond effectively to child protection issuesActivity # 3: Life Skills Based Education provided to adolescents (600 girls and 600 boys) in 10 UCsActivity # 4: Religious leaders mobilized to talk about child right to protection in Islam and its application to daily life during Friday SermonsActivity # 5: Trained staff /psychologists providing Psychosocial Support/counseling services to girls and boysActivity # 6: 50 media representatives of Swat mobilized through orientation sessions to cover child protection issues Activity # 7: 500 Orphaned, separated and unaccompanied girls and boys identified and registered with the Child Protection Unit for services provisionActivity # 8: 3,000 cases of child labour, child marriages, trafficking, child sexual abuse, corporal punishment, children with psychosocial problems, out of school children, children with disabilities, children needing legal support identified and linked with existing recovery, rehabilitation and reintegration servicesActivity # 9: 200 children (100 boys and 100 girls) registered in 10 UCs

2 - Project Indicators

• 3,000 of separated, unaccompanied, disabled and orphaned children are identified and registered in a database.• 1,500 of separated and unaccompanied children are reunited with their families or family based care ensured and monitored• 120 trained CP Monitors, govt. officials and local NGO partners are monitoring, reporting and responding to the CP issues in 10 UCs of District Swat.• CPC Facilitators trained in and providing Life Skills Based Education to 1,800 adolescents (900 boys and 900 girls)• 3,000 cases (1,500 boys and 1,500 girls) including child labour, child marriages, trafficking, child sexual abuse, corporal punishment, children with psychosocial problems, out of school children, children with disabilities, children needing legal support registered and linked with existing recovery, rehabilitation and re-integration service;• 30 child protection centers for most vulnerable children and women established, 9,000 children (4,500 girls and 4,500 boys) and 1,500 women are regularly attending different activities and psychosocial support provided.• 60 child protection committees identify children at risk and make timely interventions to prevent violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation of conflict affected girls and boys
