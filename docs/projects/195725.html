Project Summary

According to UNICEF, the education system has been devastated by the conflict. Over 7,000 schools have been damaged or destroyed, limiting the ability of children to access accredited educational services, and leaving over two million children out of school and another 1.6 million children at risk of dropping out. Self-reported attendance (MSNA 2022) indicated a slight increase of two percentage points from last year at 88 percent for 6-17 years of age. Schools and learning spaces continue to be unconducive to learning due to overcrowding, dilapidated school buildings and classrooms, teacher shortages (especially in rural schools), poor school furniture/school supplies, insufficient lighting and heating within schools and classrooms, and inadequate availability and quality of WASH facilities (N1).
It is estimated that one in three schools cannot be used as schools because they are damaged, destroyed, sheltering displaced families, or used for military purposes (N2).
Thus, UNOPS proposes the project to support the increased access to education for the communities with high vulnerability in Homs and Hama governorates, by equipping public schools with renewable energy solutions, and delivering related training.
The locations in Homs and Hama governorates are selected based on the accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2023 Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) with all the localities being rated 3 and above on the severity scale, with possible extension to other governorates based on the needs.
To fill the gaps in the shortage of electricity in schools, UNOPS will provide solar energy solutions through the procurement, installation and commissioning of photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems (including controller, solar panels, inverters, monitoring systems, mounting system etc.) and training to around 60 technical staff (30 male and 30 female) on operations and maintenance to the technical staff of target schools, UNOPS aims to support school-aged children prioritizing the primary schools via improved access to safe and protective spaces and provide equitable access to qualitative education services in the targeted areas. This project will be planned and implemented in compliance with Inter-agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE), Syria Education Sector, and MoE standards.
As part of UNOPS intervention in the schools under this project, UNOPS will ensure that the students with disabilities are attending the schools via the established Parent Teacher Associations (PTA).
UNOPS estimated the number of direct beneficiaries as school-aged children per year based on the discussion with the Education sector: it is estimated 35 students per class*10 classes per floor*3 floors*2 shifts per school*4 schools = 8400 students on a yearly basis.
It is expected that this project will directly contribute to the Strategic Objectives (SO 1: Available & Access: Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth and SO 2: Quality: Enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth) of the Education Sector in the Syria HRP 2022-2023.

3 - Needs

The PiN in Education Sector is 6.9million children, teachers and education personnel (severity3-5), which has increased by 4%compared to 2022(N1)The protracted crisis continues to impact overstretched education services. According to the report on access to electricity and humanitarian needs March2022, widespread damage to education infrastructure during the crisis has led to a majority of public schools functioning in double shifts to maximise the number of children they can serve. A lack of electricity in schools implies that students attending afternoon/ evening shifts do not have sufficient lighting to study in their classes. Lack of electricity also negatively affects the skill development of children and youths, as computers and other learning equipment do not function. Thus, it is urgent to extend the support to public schools through the provision of sustainable energy solutions for improved learning environments.Consequently, numerous schools rehabilitated by humanitarian partners are now unable to operate due to a lack of electricity. This undermines the impact and sustainability of invested humanitarian resources. Without access to reliable sources of electricity, education facilities rehabilitated by humanitarian partners cease to operate at partial capacity, impacting their ability to meet the needs of the sector. Therefore, access to electricity and the distribution of solar systems to educational facilities is a critical humanitarian need. N1: HNO2023

4 - Implementation of activities

The overall objective of this project is to "enhance the equal access to quality education services to the most vulnerable school-aged girls and boys in Syria".Key activities are summarized as follows:Coordination with MoE/DoE for identifying priority needs, selecting project locations and obtaining the necessary approvals.Field visits to assess technical needs to define the exact scope of works and prepare BOQs.Tendering and contracting process in compliance with UNOPS procurement policy.Providing schools with solar energy solutions through the procurement, installation and commissioning of photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems (controller, solar panels, inverters, monitoring systems, mounting system etc.) and the provision of operations and maintenance training to technical staff in targeted schools. Official handing over to MoE 12 months Defect Notification Period (DNP) (N1) These activities will be monitored and evaluated through school data, a list of distributions, reception works forms, engineer's reports, periodic project reports, pictures of pre/post-implementation, completion certificate, and handover certificate. Also UNOPS will report to the coordination bodies (Education Sector/OCHA) through the 4Ws. N 1: DNP will be applied for 12 months between handover and final completion of the works. Final completion occurs when the DNP has come to an end, and all defects notified to the contractor have been addressed.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1) Students exposed to protection concerns during the installation works in the project site. Likelihood: Poor; Impact: High. Mitigation Measures:1) UNOPS will select schools in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the students when attending school. 2) The project implementation work will be carried out after/before the regular school time to the extent possible to ensure the safety of students and teachers. Risk 2) Communities/authorities prevent access to schools (e.g. checkpoints/barriers) which delays the delivery of services. Likelihood: Poor. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: community engagement and outreach activities through the established PTAs.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (SEAH) risks are present in the direct contact between UNOPS and it’s contractors’ staff and local community members (beneficiaries) during the rehabilitation works and installation of equipment. An on-site SEAH risk assessment will be conducted before initiating rehabilitation works and installations at schools and mitigation measures will be included in the project Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP). All UNOPS personnel will take a mandatory online PSEA training upon joining the project while contractor personnel working on the project will receive a PSEA training after having signed their contract with UNOPS. The contractor in turn shall train all workers engaged in the project from their side, as well as have them sign a Code of Conduct. Works contractors will also be responsible for conducting regular refresher sessions for their workers as well as for putting up PSEA awareness raising posters adapted to the local context on all work sites to facilitate reporting of cases. For this purpose, the posters shall include the referral pathway for reporting cases, i.e. the contact information and uptake channels of the project Grievance redress mechanisms (GRMs). In addition, SEAH clauses will be included in all UNOPS personnel and contractor/supplier contracts.
