Project Summary

QRCS under this project will undertake activities such as: Selection of drought affected households of vulnerable community of peri-urban and IDPs chosen through registering families/HHs with malnourished children who have been discharged from the nutrition sites to also benefit through provision of unconditional cash transfer to 1000 drought affected HHs with malnourished children through provision of food for work/asset to 2000 drought affected families; provision of farming inputs to vulnerable riverine and agro-pastoralist farmers to 1000 HHs giving consideration to women headed HHs; provision of restocking to pastoralists who lost their animals during the drought; provision of technical Assistance to riverine, pastoralists, fishermen and agro-pastoralist; Rehabilitating primary and secondary irrigation canals and mitigate flooding including food/cash for work and Establishment of community led Early Warning Early Action systems; in addition provide supplements blocks 10 KG per HH, vaccinate against PPR/SGP and deworm and also Provide cash-plus to target families who benefited from the re-stocking; Strengthen the monitoring and evaluation activities in order to optimize the project implementation. The overall goal of this project will be to provide lifesaving assistance to most affected households in Peri-urban and IDPs integrated into host communities in food crisis in the identified regions of Somalia in order to prevent death due to starvation.

Needs

According to FSNAU-FEWSNET post GU 2018 release, there are still pressing humanitarian needs. The 2018 Dyer rainfall are expected to be average and above average in most places in Somalia, however, acute food security will still be prevalent among populations that lost their livelihoods during 2016-17 drought and the 2018 April-May floods as well as those displaced by protracted conflict displacement and natural hazards. These population groups are expected to face large food consumption gaps and need humanitarian assistance to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation. With Deyr 2018 expected to be above average, this might cause flooding in the low lying areas further worsen food and livelihood situations. There are no improvement in the overall current acute malnutrition prevalence compared to 2017/18 Deyr. GAM prevalence is Critical (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed. According to FEWSNET, although Deyr cultivation will begin normally, forecast average to above-average Deyr rainfall is expected to cause significant flooding disrupting activities and sustaining Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes. Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes persist across most IDP settlements, particularly among pastoralists who became destitute after extreme livestock losses in the 2016/2017 drought and are continuing to have difficulty meeting their minimum food needs. Food security and livelihood coping outcomes suggest some IDP households may be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5)[1] in the absence of humanitarian food assistance. The forecasted El Nino phenomena and associated abnormal rainfall in last quarter of 2018 is expected contribute to an increase in production while at the same time risks of shocks, including loss of production are real. Indeed, the expected above normal rains may cause the deterioration of the situation of IDPs who are already in a precarious situation.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

QRCS will work the Government in order to build a resilient and responsive health system infrastructure. QRCS will focus both on the everyday routine challenges that front line staff face on a regular basis as well as focus on sudden shocks such as disease outbreaks. QRCS will work to build the leadership capacity of district and regional state health systems and in this regard will target Government key health managers, health facility staff, community members as well as patients accessing services so as to empower them to solve problems at their level and build relationships and social networks. QRCS will focus on strengthening existing social networks to provide access to multiple resources available in the community and forms of value such as community knowledge and reputation that can build and restore resilience to health systems. QRCS will use the model of everyday resilience building in the health system of Government through a combination of absorptive, adaptive and transformative strategies that health authorities, staff and community members can use to adopt the health system in responding to strains and shocks. This will help in producing a strengthened and more resourceful resilient health system infrastructure. QRCS will train the Health Authorities, health facility staffs, patients and community members on how to deal with sudden shocks to the health system such as outbreak of diseases like Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), Cholera, and Measles and so on. All the different strata of the health system will be taught how to manage such disease outbreaks at different levels starting from the community level to the primary health unit to the health center to the referral health facility level so as not to overwhelm only one level of the health system but to build a resilient and responsive health infrastructure into every level. QRCS will use the funding from donors as well as its own funds to ensure adequate resources and strong health system infrastructure is built to contribute to generating a resilient and responsive health system in all the states of Somalia.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

QRCS is an independent, neutral organization ensuring humanitarian protection and assistance for victims of armed conflict and other situations of violence, natural disasters such as drought and famine. It takes action in response to emergencies and at the same time promotes respect for international humanitarian law and its implementation in national law. QRCS as part of the movement of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Society it is guided by a code of conduct to ensure that displaced people are treated with the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. As such the highest regard for the protection of human life and health will be provided to all people needing and seeking help with clear policies and procedures in place for QRCS staff and partners to endure that not protection risk is incurred. In the event of any exposure to protection risk, QRCS will follow its laid down procedures to mitigate those risks to ensure protection is provided at all times to all in need. Displaced populations, where by natural or human-made disasters are at a high risk to be excluded from accessing the humanitarian support they need the most due to multifactorial issues some of which include segregation of minority groups, stigma, exclusion due to lack of knowledge or information sharing. Taking cognizance of these risks of exclusion that displaced populations face, QRCS will actively seek an all-inclusive policy in order to reach all those in need especially in the areas QRCS is working. QRCS will recruit some of its project staff from displaced populations especially for positions such as community health workers in order to ensure IDPs get inclusion in accessing and benefiting from all our projects and will also ask for their participation in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

QRCS is an equal opportunity employer and does not discriminate against anyone on the basis of sex, religion, race, or any other factor. QRCS as a member of the International Convention of the Red Cross and Red Crescent Society follow its code of conduct at all levels of its operation whether at HQ, regional office, or facility staffs, to ensure that displaced people are treated with the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. In this regard, QRCS will ensure no discrimination of whatever form is allowed on any basis including on gender and age. QRCS believes all members of the society of all gender and age have a role to play in alleviating human need and suffering. In that vein, QRCS will engage men, women, boys and girls, young and old alike, in the delivery of services either as staff or as beneficiaries.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Populations displaced by conflict and violence face a lot of problems especially humanitarian need and lack of life-saving health services. QRCS will work to ensure the provision of access to basic life-saving health to all populations in need in our areas of interventions without discrimination. In this regard, QRCS will also work within the humanitarian context and coordinate with other actors to ensure the severe impacts of armed conflict and violence is reduced. QRCS works very closely with communities and local authorities, ensuring that local laws, customs and culture are adhered to, and avoiding action and advocacy that may be perceived as offensive.
