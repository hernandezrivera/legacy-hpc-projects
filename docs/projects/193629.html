Project Summary

The proposed project aims to support the most vulnerable communities through solid waste management services. The project will target four marginalized communities in Deir Al Balah Governorate, amongst the top vulnerable communities according to WASH solid waste vulnerability index 2022, namely Al Salah, Al-Rashid Al-Sahaba, Al Mahata, and Block 3-Block 4. Moreover, the selected localities are characterized by poor socio-economic conditions; amongst 12,550 residents, more than 6,000 are under the poverty line, and 350 people live with disabilities. In response to these dire circumstances, this project aims to provide most vulnerable communities with equitable, affordable and safe access to solid waste management services to improve their public health and to advance their human rights.
Around 2,481 HHs (including 257 FHHs) have no access to public solid waste collection services, leading to the accumulation of more than 10 tons of solid waste per day in public areas. The insufficient capacities of municipalities and designated Joint Services Council (JSC) to cover the targeted localities have led to the formulation of 9 unofficial dumpsites and to negative coping mechanisms including dumping waste in the streets. According to WASH Vulnerability mapping, the negative environmental and social impact has been exacerbated by the communities' vulnerability to flooding i.e. severe floods that cover streets and open markets and directly affecting 455 HHs. These floods results in the scattering of solid waste amongst streets, public spaces, and HHs, which increases the contamination of water, and affects quality of living environment. Furthermore, initial assessment in targeted localities indicates that women often are at a great disadvantage as they are the primary caregivers for both their families and their communities. Hence, the lack of waste collection increases the burdens on women in terms of navigating means to dispose of waste as well as to care for their families amidst an environment plagued by the health hazards imposed by the accumulation of wastes.
The project approach focuses on enhancing the capacities of service providers to remobilize their resources for better of provision of quality solid waste management services, and on improving community members capacities and practices related to WASH services; mainly through implementing 4 interlocking activities: (i) conducting awareness campaign on solid waste management including proper waste disposal, at-home waste composting and reuse; (ii) providing of cleaning materials, PPEs, and equipment (while ensuring the utilization of most durable materials and equipment that would help the service providers and communities to sustain solid waste collection process beyond the project); (iii) providing 1 cubic meter of solid waste bins and containers across the community (3 bins for every 500 people, assuming there will be 2 collections per week); and (iv) supporting municipalities and designated JSC to properly manage solid waste collection in targeted localities through provision of materials, spare parts, and tools that are required for maintenance and operation.
The municipalities will utilize the provided tools, materials, and equipment to increase number of labors – solid waste collectors, which will help them to sustain better quality of public services and to increase community satisfaction.
The project is designed based on recent needs assessment conducted, jointly by UN-Habitat and MA’AN in October 2022, through discussions with service providers and community representatives. The project recognizes the importance of community participation in design and implementation process, hence continuous consultations with community representative committees will be maintained to ensure that marginalized groups i.e. women, elderly people, and PwD have equal access to project benefits.

1 - Needs

The project is designed based on recent needs assessment conducted, jointly by UN-Habitat and MA’AN in October 2022 and using the 2022 mapping conducted by WASH. The assessment explored SWM operations quality at the household, and community levels, and identified the level of solid waste coverage regarding waste collection, transporting, and disposal. Several discussions were held with municipal staff at the targeted municipalities and president of the JSC for the Southern Governorate to fully understand the main gaps in primary and secondary collection processes as they are considered the most crucial stages of the SWM cycle. MA’AN, through its active forums in Deir Balah governorate, conducted focus group discussion with community representatives to identify complaints and recommendations.Research indicates that more than 2,000 tons of waste are produced per day across the Strip, and is managed by the LGUs along with two Joint Service Councils for Solid Waste Management (SWM). Yet, service providers face critical challenges in the provision of collection, transportation, and disposal of waste. The long-lasting blockade, internal Palestinian divide and recurrent conflicts has undermined the quality of these services. The lack of resources, outdated equipment and tools, and limitation of collected fees have added a further toll on the ability of service providers to properly manage solid waste in a healthy and timely manner, which by default has yielded a number of bad practices.Many health risks related to inadequate SWM could be induced. Flies, rats, dogs, snakes and other scavengers are attracted to garbage, particularly in hot climates. If food is scarce, people may be forced to scavenge as well, which will lead to increased cases of disease. Pools of rainwater associated with waste collection will propagate the breeding of mosquitoes that transmit malaria, dengue, and yellow fever; and garbage washed by rain can contaminate water supplies. Heaps of garbage present a fire risk and smoke can also be a health and environmental hazard if the burning waste contains items such as plastics or chemicals. Breathing difficulties can arise from the fungi that develop on garbage tips; and sharp items such as needles and broken glass present a further hazard to walkers and passersby. Finally, a study carried out by WHO revealed that inadequately disposed or untreated waste may cause serious health problems for populations surrounding the area of disposal. Considering that SWM is a crucial intervention within the HRP 2023, UN-Habitat in partnership with MA’AN is proposing a humanitarian response intervention to bridge the gap in SWM services in most vulnerable communities identified by WASH cluster. The project is focusing on four communities namely: Al-Salah and Alrashid Alshahaba in Alzawayda city, Block 3 Block 4 in Alburaij , Almahata in Deir Balah city. The targeted communities are economically and socially vulnerable; amongst 12,550 residents, more than 6,000 are under the poverty line, and 350 people live with disabilities. Around 2,481 HHs (including 257 FHHs) are largely vulnerable and lack access to effective SWM where around 12.5 tons of daily generated solid waste are not covered by public collection services. 9 un-official dumpsites exist in targeted localities, and negative coping mechanism are vast such as throwing garbage in the streets. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, almost 90,000 households suffer from an acute lack of solid waste collection services or are located in the vicinity of informal and unregulated dumping sites. As a result, the most vulnerable groups including women and disabled persons are exposed to a range of health and environmental hazards.The negative environmental and social impact has been exacerbated by the communities' vulnerability to flooding i.e. severe floods that cover streets and open markets and directly affecting 455 HHs. These floods results in the scattering of solid waste amongst streets, public spaces, and HHs, which increases the contamination of water, and affects quality of living environment. Furthermore, initial assessment in targeted localities indicates that women often are at a great disadvantage as they are the primary caregivers for both their families and their communities. Hence, the lack of waste collection increases the burdens on women in terms of navigating means to dispose of waste as well as to care for their families amidst an environment plagued by the health hazards imposed by the accumulation of wastes. At the primary collection level, the main identified gaps are a) lack of municipal capacity for the provision of adequate number of workers (for example 8 workers available in Deir Balah municipality while 35 workers is the optimum capacity); b) lack of cleaning materials and tools; c) most of the vehicles, loaders are out of date and need daily maintenance; and d) the need for physical survey to identify hot spots, scattered un-official dumpsites, and bad practices as this is crucial to better provide SWM services during emergencies and to be the base for developing community awareness campaigns that will support a positive behavior change in the residents’ coping mechanisms and practices. At the secondary collection level, the service provide, i.e. the JSC, stressed the need for a) the daily maintenance of 7 loaders that are designated to marginalized areas specifying that the daily maintenance cost ranges from 500 to 1000 ILS; b) enhancing the coordination mechanism between the municipalities and JSC for more efficient use of resources; and c) insufficient resources for the provision of fuel.The proposed activities are in line with interventions set forth by the HRP, are approved by Ministry of Local Governments, and in line with the National Adaptation Plan (NAP) to Climate Change. The project fall under proposed priorities in the SWM sector to Nationally Determined Contribution

2 - Activities

According the recently conducted needs assessment, the project will contribute to enhance solid waste management services for the most vulnerable communities who severely suffer from the lack of access to public solid waste collection as per WASH vulnerability mapping, through implementing the following activities:Activity 1: Solid waste management awareness campaign:A physical survey will be conducted to map existing unofficial dumpsites, areas not covered by solid waste services, hot spots that should be given priorities during emergencies, and bad practices or negative coping mechanisms, disaggregated by the different practices undertaken by the different population groups including men, women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities. The physical survey will support the municipalities to have an updated GIS SWM map which will enhance their capacity to better manage and respond to SWM services during emergencies, i.e winter, conflicts, and pandemics. The project team will build on the findings of the survey to design and conduct an awareness campaign to address bad practices and change community behavior to better handle domestic solid waste. Furthermore, the campaign will also address issues related to more equal division of domestic responsibilities, hoping that the behavioral change and reduction of negative coping practices will reduce the work burden on women and primary caregivers and will support other members within the family to engage in household activities related to SWM, such as composting and reuse. The campaign will initially address waste reduction, proper waste disposal, potential at-home composting tools, potential waste reuse, and will include community activities such as a cleaning days, youth activities to promote their engagement and their leadership in promoting for a cleaner environment, which will contribute to the sustainability of the interventions. Several communications tools and materials will be utilized to maximize reach including flyers, posters, radio spots, and awareness events. It is envisaged that the awareness campaign will mainly target the 12,550 residents of the four selected localities and will indirectly target 124,327 residents living in Deir al Balah, Al Zawayda, and Bureij cities. Activity 2: The provision of community cleaning materials and tools:Upon project initiation, a detailed technical needs assessment and discussions will be held with the service providers and the local community representative committees to identify type and quantity of needed cleaning materials, PPEs, and new equipment. The project team will ensure to provide the most durable materials and equipment that would help the service providers and communities to sustain solid waste collection process beyond the project. All equipment will be in line with WASH technical manual which provides the technical specifications for the various cleaning materials, equipment and PPEs that might be needed by the service providers and the communities. Activity 3: The provision of domestic solid waste containers/bins:The communities will be supported with the provision of solid waste containers/bins with a capacity of 1 cubic meter. These bins will support the municipalities and Joint Services Council to expand their area of service to include the four targeted communities who currently do not have access to public solid waste management services. The physical survey will help to distribute appropriate new containers to fully cover all HHs within the targeted communities. Initially, a total of 76 containers will be provided and distributed among the four communities, assuming that each container could serve 500 people, and assuming the solid waste collection will take place twice a week by the service providers. Activity 4: The provision of sanitation facilities critical operation and maintenance materials and tools:Upon project initiation, a detailed technical needs assessment and discussions with service providers (municipality and Joint Services Council) to identify the needed equipment, spare parts, and materials needed for maintenance and operation that are critical to sustain the provision of service. Identified items will be provided to service providers according to an agreed schedule. All equipment will be in line with WASH technical manual which provides the technical specifications for the various cleaning materials, equipment and PPEs that might be needed by the service providers and the communities. Project activities will be technically designed and implemented based on the findings of FGDs that will be held with community representatives committees, women and people with disabilities in particular, through which the project team will identify women and PwD daily struggles, challenges, needs and recommendations related SWM. The project will adopt positive deviant approach where local pioneers (men and women) on environmental rights will be identified and used as a model in the project’s roll-out.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The service delivery modality is the main response modality to ensure both local ownership and commitment to the activities and interventions, as well as to ensure an element of sustainability after the end of the project. The service delivery modality will support building partnerships with service providers and the designated Join Services Council for better provision of Solid Waste Management Services in marginalized communities. The provision of services will help the service providers to remobilize their resources to increase the area of service coverage and ensure that solid waste collection services are provided on a regular basis for targeted communities. This modality ensures that the technical and operational capacities of these service providers are built and enhanced to sustain solid waste collection activities beyond the project. The service delivery modality will also be used for the awareness campaign, where the implementing agencies will directly deliver these awareness services (e.g. print materials, cleaning days, and other awareness tools) within the targeted communities, but with effective participation from the communities themselves in order to ensure local ownership. By building partnerships between the communities and the service providers, and by ensuring that the benefits of this intervention are tangible, the project aims to support sustainability where the municipalities and the JSC will continue to prioritize resources to ensure that these communities continue to benefit from SWM services.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Community feedback and complaints are critical measures that will be carefully addressed by the project team. Since the concept of this project is to alleviate the daily suffering of marginalized people, the project activities, outputs, and impact shall aim at delivering the service in a safe, inclusive, and transparent manner, while ensuring the full satisfaction of targeted communities. The project will be introduced to the community via participatory announcement workshops and the targeted communities will continue to have access to the project implementing team (including all stakeholders) throughout the project to submit their potential complaints about the quality of the services provided or if any changes are suggested. The received feedback and complaints will be discussed internally by the project team to understand the raised issues, afterwards the project team will call for a discussion with community members to collectively discuss the cause of the problems, if any, and to identify suggested tools and mechanisms that would enhance the quality of work and that are adapted to meet community needs and priorities as well. This way, the project ensures that all complaints and suggested solutions are handled in a participatory manner and based on a locally owned vision of improvement. Based on the final recommendations and in coordination with service providers, the project team will modify the approaches and tools to ensure community satisfaction throughout the implementation process.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :
9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The project will utilize MA’AN’s existing accountable, accessible, and gender-sensitive complaint system in the Gaza Strip. The system offers different means to address complaints or feedback in writing, verbally, via phone or SMS, directly to the responsible person/field monitors from MA'AN, and verbally and directly to MA’AN offices. The system includes particular procedures to address gender-sensitive complaints or other critical issues. The system has been managed online involving different layers of MA’AN’s governance structure. MA’AN has been working on adapting the system to include special procedures during emergencies to get it more accessible for IDPs and host communities. This project will include introducing the system to the partners, volunteers, and targeted communities ensuring to provide orientation about the system and how to access it in the event of emergencies. The communities’ feedback on the system will be considered for further adaptation to ensure accountability and accessibility. During the project implementation, a feedback mechanism will be applied to ensure the involvement of the targeted communities in the project management and decision making. Orientation workshops will be conducted at the beginning of the project to discuss the project activities and get further feedback from the targeted communities about the implementation methodology. A set of focus group discussions will be conducted during implementation to get regular feedback from the targeted communities about the implementation process and the applied measures of accountability. If women do not actively participate in the community workshops and events, then specific field visits to the households, including female-headed households, will be conducted to directly interact with the women and learn more about their needs, feedback on implementation mechanisms, and feedback on satisfaction with the delivery and results of the interventions.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Do No HarmUN-Habitat and MA’AN have extensive experience in designing and implementing responsive interventions that promote protection mainstreaming through the do-no harm approach. Throughout managing a number of extensive community-led projects in the Gaza Strip, the project team fully understands the social, political, and economic situation in targeted localities. Through the conducted initial needs assessment, and through a more in-depth assessment that will be launched at the start of the project, the designed project ensures full adherence to the context and culture of the targeted population. Furthermore, the complaints and feedback mechanism will ensure that if any constraints arise that may impose harm on communities, then the project team will drastically make the required changes to ensure protection. Several measures will be adopted to ensure that project activities do not harm to any community segment, and to vulnerable groups in particular:a) Project team will continuously coordinate with service providers and community representative committees to identify any specific cultural and social concerns before starting the implementation phase. Initial discussions have already been conducted during the design phase with community representative members in Deir Balah Governorate, and the activities of the project have not been highlighted to cause harm to any segment of the community. On the contrary, the project will benefit community members by supporting a healthier environment, both inside the house (through more positive behavioral change) and outside the household (through a cleaner environment). b) In case of documentation, the project team will be allowed to take photos/videos only if the permission is granted by the beneficiaries. Specific attention will be given to the needs/cautions of women when documenting cases/success stories and even when conducting awareness activities at the household and community levels. c) The location of solid waste containers will be carefully selected by specialists to make sure that the collection process will not harm human health or the environment, including the built environment, and by ensuring that the waste bins are accessible to all vulnerable groups. d) Specific attention will be given to the working environment for all hired labor and/or community volunteers, in terms of safety measures, tools (such as personal protective equipment), and working hours. This is to protect the project staff, volunteers, and community leaders from any environmental risks related to solid waste collections and/or other related activities within the project. e) Project team will strictly prohibit any use of child or forced labor.f) HHs headed by elderly people and/or PWD who may have trouble accessing/reaching SWM services will be given highest priorities throughout the implementation, and the project team will coordinate with service providers and laborers to facilitate the collection of solid waste at household level.g) The project team will ensure a continuous open communication channel between community representative committees, project team, and service providers, so they will be able to communicate any concerns, needs, and complaints.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed to provide equitable access to solid waste management services, with the aim of offering marginalized community members, in particular vulnerable groups such as women, the elderly and PWD, a safe and healthy living environment. The project will not discriminate on the basis of gender, age nor disability status.Women groups will be targeted, both separately and within community-level activities, by raising awareness and advance discussions about the right to healthy and safe environment, solid waste management, women’s roles in enhancing solid waste management through green and environmental practices. Furthermore, the provision of solid waste management services will be open to women to participate, so it can provide employment opportunities for women, also challenging the dominant stereotype about women’s roles in society. In line with international frameworks that require the involvement of women in humanitarian actions and UN Resolution 1325 on women, peace, and security, women-only focus groups will be conducted to allow women a space to voice their concerns and aspirations about their needs in general and the project in specific. Young women and men are of key focus of this project. The role of youth as social change agents and leaders in sustainable spatial development is well recognized. Furthermore, the project recognizes that children, especially girls, female-headed households, PWD and the elderly should be integrated during the project design, implementation, and evaluation to ensure equitable access to solid waste management services, and the overall benefits of safe and healthy environment. In June 2019, the UN Secretary-General launched the UN Disability Inclusion Strategy (UNDIS) as part of the commitment to make the UN an inclusive organization for all. The UNDIS includes an accountability framework with two aligned components: one for individual UN entities and one for the UN Country Teams (UNCT Scorecard). The accountability framework contains practical indicators covering both programmes and operations, with clear benchmarks to accelerate and track progress on disability inclusion. The project will witness progress in the efforts of the UNCT in Palestine on the roll out of the designated scorecard and accordingly will include the relevant activities. Important to note that at present there is no available information on disability in the targeted communities, except the percentage of 2.8%. The project will document cases and needs of this group during the implementation process as outlined in the mechanisms for ensuring a do-no-harm approach. All interventions will be open and accessible to persons with disabilities ensuring their participation at all stages of the design and implementation of the awareness campaign. More specifically, the technical meetings and action plans will follow national and international benchmarks to safeguard the needs and aspirations of persons with disability in enjoying their built environment.
