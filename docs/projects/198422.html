Project Summary

The overall aim is to reduce mortality, morbidity and undernutrition by providing life-saving nutrition and health services in health facilities and communities.
1. Affected populations have improved access to quality primary healthcare services in the targeted areas for reduction of morbidity and mortality
2. Affected populations have access to quality nutrition treatment and preventive services through strengthening the capacity of health facilities
The program will be focusing on;
- Provision of quality primary health care services that includes basic management and prevention of communicable diseases for all age groups from children to adults,
- Providing medical equipment, supplies and pharmaceuticals and nutritional supplies to the health facilities through the award and in coordination with SMOH and LGA authorities.
- Sexual and Reproductive health services; antenatal and postnatal care services, support to uncomplicated vaginal delivery services and minor perinatal complications,
- Support to referral services for specialized care.
- Support to Routine Childhood Immunization in collaboration with SMOH and WHO.
- Support to provision of clinical management of minor physical injury
- Prepositioning of treatment kits and supplies for Cholera and Measles outbreak in the targeted LGAs
- Provision of nutrition services through CMAM approach in targeted communities.
- Supplementary feeding program through Cash based intervention for PLW (Porridge MOM)
- Reinforcement of technical capacity through sector/sub-sector specific technical trainings for quality service delivery.
- Building institutional and community capacity for promotion and provision of appropriate IYCF-E integrated with priority health and hygiene practices.
Target:
1. Children 0-59 months of age for malnutrition treatment and other children for provision of primary health services.
2. Women: key target of the project, both in its direct activities (e.g. Care Groups) and preventive and curative health services, as well as with the women empowerment agenda applied across the project.
3. Men: men in the targeted communities will be provided with primary health care services for the management of non-communicable diseases (NCD) and communicable diseases.
4. Health workers and community-based structures e.g CHIPS, CV etc

1 - Needs

Nigeria’s Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states in the Northeast have been facing one of the most severe humanitarian crises in the world for over a decade, characterized by insecurity, large-scale population displacement, chronic food shortages and the collapse of basic social services (Humanitarian Response Plan-HRP, March 2021). The limited capacity to effectively respond to sudden onset emergencies due to escalating insecurity has resulted in disruptions of basic services. Based on the most recent NFSS report (Dec 2022), the nutrition situation continues to deteriorate with some increase in the GAM rate for Borno state 12.3% (NFSS round 12) as compared to NFSS round 11 (GAM 11.8) leading to a spike of malnutrition. Although, the NFSS report for Yobe shows some improvement, certain domains still record high level of malnutrition. Furthermore, in Yobe state, the malnutrition situation is unstable and remained serious, with two of its LGA Gujba and Damaturu in phase 4 IPC (21.4 and 17.1, CH Analysis 2022), others such as Yunusari, Yusufari, Nguru Gaidam and Bursari were classified to be in IPC phase 3 (IPC Oct 2022). According to the Nutrition sector data– 5W, in 2022, the malnutrition admission caseload in the BAY states started on a higher trend compared to the previous years and has continued to deteriorate through the fourth quarter 2022 even before the start of the lean season. Worst hit is the MMC/Jere domain which has seen a geometric increase even in severe acute malnutrition cases with complications thereby overwhelming the capacity of the existing stabilization centers. Although the total number of OTP sites increased in 2022 by approximately 4%, in some LGAs, OTP sites drastically reduced, for example: in Borno, MMC reduced by 15%, and in Nganzi LGA the reduction is by 50%. Furthermore, the nutrition sector admitted 290,238 children with severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) between January and October 2022, a 14% increase in comparism with the previous year. Among these, ACF contribution to the SAM admissions was 36,234 (12.5%) of the sector admissionsThe major contributing factors to acute malnutrition include the lack of access to adequate humanitarian aid following the camp closure in MMC/Jere, poor access and low coverage of CMAM/health services, the high prevalence of child morbidities (fever/malaria, diarrhea) and very poor food consumption patterns (both in terms of diversity and in terms of frequency) among the population. The exclusive breastfeeding rates and Minimum diversity diet rate for Yobe are 40.7% (33.2 - 48.7) and 19.0 [15.1- 23.7] which is below optimum. For Borno as well Maternal infant and young child feeding (MIYCF) Practices are also still below averages as reported by nutrition and food security surveillance (NFSS, 2022). Exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) is 59% while 58% breastfed into the second year. Of children 6-23 months, 19.9% met the criteria for minimum diet diversity (MDD), 11.9% for minimum meal frequency (MMF) and 1.5% for minimum acceptable diet (MAD), confirming that appropriate complementary feeding (ACF) practices are dangerously low in the State.The health system faces dilapidated infrastructure and equipment, lack of qualified staff, gaps in the supply of medicines and medical supplies, and under-funding (OCHA HRP 2022). The July 2022 Health sector bulletin shows 58.1% of HFs are fully functioning, 10.2% non-functioning, 11.4% partially functioning while 12.4% are fully damaged across the BAY states. Same reports points that suspected and confirmed Malaria was the leading cause of morbidity and mortality reported through EWARS each accounting for 39% and 19% of the reported cases and deaths respectively while acute respiratory infection (ARI) and acute watery diarrhea disease caused 16.7% and 2.3% of all reported cases in the BAY state. Moreover, there is a high risk of epidemic outbreaks, the northeast region is highly endemic for malaria and cholera.

2 - Activities

The interventions proposed by Action Against Hunger will provide lifesaving health and nutrition activities including the provision of treatment and prevention of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) services in health facilities (outpatient therapeutic programs/OTPs and stabilization centers/SC) and communities, building the capacity of health workers to provide quality physical and mental health services, health facility rehabilitations and provision of medical supplies and equipment. AAH interventions will contribute to the achievement of the objectives of the Health and Nutrition Sectors for 2023. The response will also directly contribute to specific objectives of the Health and Nutrition Sectors’ response plans as outlined in the HRP, and the Nigeria Nutrition in Emergencies Sector Strategy and Response Plan (2023). AAH will continue to support the State Ministry of Health and SPHCDA to expand the provision of primary health care beyond urban areas to newly accessible areas for adequate health assistance where there are gaps not covered by any other partners cover all population age and demographic groups, while providing standard packages for groups such as children under five, pregnant and lactating women (PLW).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Across all components of the programme, data collection and reporting will be done in a gender and socially inclusive manner. Thus, data will be disaggregated by age, gender, disability and other vulnerability markers such as head or member of single-parent household and unaccompanied/orphaned child. Due to the social dynamics characterised by the lower participation of women and girls in most communities, emphasis shall be given to the constitution of project teams with up to 70% female staff to ensure service access to the target beneficiaries. Action Against Hunger teams will conduct a baseline gender analysis/assessment in the first quarter of program implementation, an interim progress assessment at the end of the first year and an end of project assessment at project close out to measure the gender and inclusion gaps of the project and how they are met in the course of implementation

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Participatory monitoring will ensure to get feedback from beneficiaries. This will be done through a feedback and complaint response mechanism established for all projects as a way of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints. These are embedded in the monitoring system and beneficiaries and stakeholders are sensitized on this feedback mechanism and toll-free number so that they understand how to express concerns relating to the project. Feedback on ACF activities will also be solicited during post distribution monitoring and meetings with communities. Discussions with beneficiaries/ community leaders will be done regularly to address emerging issues and to ensure community obligations and participation in project implementation. This approach has helped to establish a relationship of trust between staff and communities in various projects and to improve the impact of the response.
