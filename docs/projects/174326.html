Project Summary

The proposed 7-month intervention aims to directly support the CCCM Sector’s decongestion strategy in Dikwa by providing vital CCCM Coordination to enable the resettlement of IDP HHs from over-congested camp sites, developing humanitarian site plans and setting up standard CCCM response modalities in the new camp extension zones, through site facilitation, participatory camp governance, infrastructure improvements and RCCE.

1 - Needs

Identified needs and activities proposed by ACTED have been informed by monthly CCCM site profiling assessments conducted by ACTED as leading CCCM agency in all 8 ACTED-managed camps in Dikwa throughout 2020; ACTED’s CCCM progress monitoring assessments carried out in target camps in November 2019 and April 2020 and REACH’s 2019 MSNA. Common themes highlighted by assessments findings, as well as field observations and monthly beneficiary complaints and response reports included: high over-congestion rates and prevalent gaps in Shelter/NFI service provision, increasing protection risks and overall vulnerability of IDP populations to COVID-19 and other disease outbreaks. In Dikwa, ACTED’s CCCM assessments recorded deplorable, severely over-congested shelter conditions, with high makeshift prevalence rates, including 60% in Shuwari, 70% in Kamchejin, 90% in Modu Kasa and 100% in Mohamed Kyarimi, rendering the practice of social distancing and other COVID-19 prevention measures practically unfeasible to avoid epidemiological expansion. ACTED’s CCCM site profiling estimated a mere 3.5m2 average of individual living space and assessed only 61% of overall shelters meeting SPHERE standards. The recent outbreak of COVID-19 in Borno State has further exposed the extreme vulnerability of IDP populations, with unpredictable population movements compounding transmission risks and camp-level outbreaks threatening to further deteriorate pre-existing humanitarian drivers. Overcrowded conditions, WASH and S/NFI gaps underscore poor social determinants of health, increasing IDP vulnerability to not just COVID-19, but also malaria, Lassa fever and cholera, the latter posing elevated risks of flooding expected during the 2020 rainy season. Accordingly, the presence of CCCM agencies serves as a first-line defense against these compounding critical camp-site risks, including fire hazards or flooding, while operationalizing effective camp management mechanisms for an emergency COVID-19 response capacity and coordinating the humanitarian response to ensure compliance with COVID-19 mitigation and prevention measures. This is particularly relevant for over-congested and underserved IDP camps in Dikwa, leaving ACTED as CCCM agency to fill critical gaps in service provision and lead coordination of a comprehensive COVID-19 prevention and response strategy to avert excess mortality and transmission risks.

2 - Activities

1. Camp Coordination, Information Management and Site facilitation in support of the CCCM Sector’s decongestion in DikwaTo ensure informed, evidence-based planning and implementation capacity as part of the Dikwa decongestions strategy, ACTED will continue the facilitation of robust coordination and information management mechanism between active humanitarian partners, relevant Sectors, NEMA/SEMA, and beneficiary and host communities in Dikwa. Through information gathering, analysis, and reporting mechanisms, ACTED CCCM Team will ensure humanitarian stakeholders have access to up-to-date, reliable, and continuous flows of information on needs, on requisite interventions to meet objectives set out in humanitarian site development plans, and to protect equitable access to life-saving services in newly created camp zones. ACTED will conduct a safety audit, assessing available and equitable access of services, and camp development needs for the anticipated new camp zones. This will inform the development of the Humanitarian Site Development Plan (HSDP). Additionally, ACTED’s dedicated CCCM Team will conduct regular demographic data collection and monthly site-profiling and to continue the facilitation of consistent analysis of needs, gaps, and trends. 2. Establishment and Capacity building of Participatory Camp Governance Structures in new camp zonesACTED will conduct surveys and focus group discussions to ascertain community members’ perceptions and level of trust in these structures. All activities will be conducted in strict adherence to social distancing and IPC measures. Building on the findings of the mapping exercise, ACTED will support fair, representative and transparent governance structures through the development of ToRs with area-based site-specific community action plans, collaboratively created for each target location, taking context-specific dynamics into account. 3. Camp Site infrastructure improvements to ensure dignified living conditions and reduce COVID-19 transmission risks ACTED will facilitate site improvement and maintenance works. Information generated through participatory hazard mapping, the development of multi-sectoral contingency plans, and continuous site monitoring and profiling activities, ACTED CCCM will work to proactively identify priority site care, maintenance, and improvement needs in cooperation with the CCCM Sector. In addition, ACTED will conduct consultations with local stakeholders, including SEMA, local leaders, camp committees, and site residents, and other humanitarian actors, in order to jointly prioritize critical matters, and to develop an agenda for site development plans for integration into HSDPs. Site improvement and maintenance works will focus on mitigating environmental, critical protection and COVID-19 transmission risks.4. Risk Communication and Community Engagement for coordinated COVID-19 preparedness and response ACTED CCCM Team will conduct outreach programs, including socially distant door-to-door campaigns and megaphone announcements and will maintain information boards within the camp to ensure reliable access to information on COVID-19 risks and mitigation measures, available services, camp developments and key messages. Further, ACTED will establish and maintain a Complaints Feedback Mechanism (CFM) desk three days a week, accessible to all camp residents. ACTED will refer feedback to operational partners as relevant, strongly encourage timely closure of complaints and assistance requests for partners, and compile monthly beneficiary complaints and feedback reports. ACTED further proposes to continue effective RCCE to further support the Nigerian Government’s COVID-19 readiness and response strategy and improve access to reliable information on COVID-19 amongst camp residents. Building on findings from a previously conducted RCCE assessment, ACTED will provide effective, timely and transparent information updates to camp populations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project, and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. ACTED will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the wellbeing, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to CCCM assistance, and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. All ACTED staff will uphold protection principles at all times, as signatories to ACTED’s Code of Conduct, including ACTED’s Policy on PSEA. Moreover, as a leading CCCM agency in all proposed target sites, ACTED will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer protection cases identified through the CRM. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: 1) Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: ACTED will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. ACTED’s CCCM team will ensure that community meetings and sensitization campaigns are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. Gender-segregated queues with mandatory COVID-19 social distancing measures and available disinfectant materials will be mandated to all camp-level communal and distribution sites. Under the CCCM component, ACTED will integrate gender and protection mainstreaming, ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups in decision-making processes. Strong referral mechanisms will be enacted, enabling direct referral of any vulnerable IDPs to relevant service providers, including victims of assault or GBV. 2) Ensure meaningful and equal access: Beneficiary selection will be conducted based on need and vulnerability, and will be conducted in liaison with site CCCM Teams, protection partners. When possible, the AMEU will conduct household level assessments to inquire on target individuals’ living conditions and needs against selection criteria. Moreover, the CCCM Team will strengthen participatory camp governance structures, facilitating direct inclusion of beneficiary groups into decision-making processes, facilitating greater equitable and meaningful access to assistance programs. ACTED will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy. 3) Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: Beneficiaries will be members of camp committees, ensuring strong participation in camp governance, channeling ideas and influencing the wider humanitarian coordination and response planning, and play a vital role in the implementation of site maintenance activities, providing foundation for community-led camp management and interventions. Daily laborers, hired to support of distribution to vulnerable beneficiary members, including PWDs, will be recruited from the IDP community to provide employment opportunities, with hiring process reliant on a strict equal opportunities basis and considerate of protection and gender aspects. Accountability will also stem strongly from the CRM, ensuring timely and appropriate response to complaints. Moreover, PDM exercises will assess beneficiary satisfaction and inform further programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

- Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU): ACTED will ensure regular and systematic monitoring of project activities through its AMEU to assess risks, progress towards targets, identification and targeting of most vulnerable individuals, impact measurement, and implementation quality assurance in line with the Core Humanitarian Standards. To facilitate monitoring of project activities, the AMEU will: conduct a CCCM progress surveys to assess key camp risks, specific needs and displacement dynamics, and manage and raise awareness of ACTED’s beneficiary Complaints & Response Mechanism (CRM) in line with ACTED’s accountability towards affected population policy to ensure that beneficiary feedback and needs are channeled into the response. Finally, where relevant and feasible, the AMEU will monitor distributions and perform spot-check assessments of project activities, conducting assessment on the specific needs faced by women, children, the elderly, people with disabilities and with increasing vulnerability to COVID-19.- ACTED’s communication and community engagement (CCE) approach adopts a 360-degree engagement strategy with affected communities, realizing wide community engagement beyond camp committees, enabling informed decision making and advocacy at camp-level. Closing the feedback loop between beneficiaries and partners remains at the core of ACTED’s CCCM strategy, enabling comprehensive feedback capable of informing the greater humanitarian system of response at camp-level, while simultaneously underpinning accountability to affected populations. In Dikwa, this approach will be composed of multiple streams of CCE and COVID-19 specific Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE), building on and expanding activities undertaken in prior CCCM Actions.
