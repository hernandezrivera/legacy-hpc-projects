Project Summary

Education sector remains critically underfunded despite its centrality to protection of women and girls, and providing the bridge between emergency, recovery, and long-term sustainable development of communities. Access to education is largely constraint due to myriad of challenges including shortage of quality teachers, learning and teaching materials and lack of infrastructure. According to research by UNESCO and MOGEI, there are estimated more than 2.8 million children out of school with majority being girls and displaced children. Several factors have contributed to this including lack of adequate school infrastructure to provide safe and protective learning environment, cultural barriers, conflict, COVID19 pandemic, economic hardships, and natural disasters such as floods. According to recent GEAR 2022 statistical analysis, South Sudan has 33% of permeant classrooms, 21% of Semi-permanent, 6% roof only, 3% tent and 39% open air. Most communities affected with subnational violence and floods continue to struggle to get children in school. Meanwhile, pastoralist communities have limited access to education as they from one location to another. Over the years, humanitarian and development partners have increased access to educational facilities in the country. The HRP 2022 estimates that 3.6 million people with 50% female, 97% children and 15% persons with disability need education services. This project aims to increase access to safe learning environment for children out of school especially the girls and improve quality of teaching and learning in the targeted communities. This project is in line with MOGEI General Education Strategic Plan (GESP) 2017-2022) and Education Cluster sector priority which aim to address the immediate access to education services by alleviating some of the structural barriers to education such as inaccessibility to safe and conducive learning space and improve quality of education to children affected by crises. The project aligns with WTI strategy of expanding access to education in emergency context.
Windle Trust International has decades of experience in delivering similar projects in the country. Through this project, WTI aims to increase access to safe, protective, and conducive learning spaces by increasing availability of classrooms drawing support from the community, provide learning and teaching materials and improve the capacity of teachers, education managers and community leaders to scale up quality of learning in the affected communities. WTI will ensure community participation during the implementation to ensure that children are send to school. Back to Learning Campaigns will be organized to ensure the community is aware of the importance of sending children to School. The project will target 6,350 beneficiaries in Cuiebet (1,689), Rumbek North (2,668) and Leer (1,993). All these locations are among the priority one areas of the education cluster. These locations are some of the hard-to-reach areas with complicated shocks that makes them unique context for interventions. They fall under the education cluster priority locations for response.
To increase availability and functionality of learning spaces WTI will construct TLS classrooms in schools, renovate destroyed school infrastructure, construct of WASH facilities, provide teaching and learning materials, provide dignity kits for girls in schools. To increase access and enrolment of children in school, WTI will conduct these activities including, conduct one BTL campaign in each county targeting the locations with low enrolment and where the school construction and renovation has been done. Conduct orientation of teachers, facilitators and PTAs on enrolment drive and mobilization of OOSC. Conduct community engagement sessions in the primary school areas and provide cash assistance to young school mothers.

1 - Needs

Education sector remains critically underfunded despite its centrality to protection of women and girls, and providing the bridge between emergency, recovery, and long-term sustainable development of communities. Access to education is largely constraint due to myriad of challenges including shortage of quality teachers, learning and teaching materials and lack of infrastructure. According to research by UNESCO and MOGEI, there are estimated more than 2.8 million children out of school with majority being girls and displaced children. Several factors have contributed to this including lack of adequate school infrastructure to provide safe and protective learning environment, cultural barriers, conflict, COVID19 pandemic, economic hardships, and natural disasters such as floods. According to recent GEAR 2022 statistical analysis, South Sudan has 33% of permeant classrooms, 21% of Semi-permanent, 6% roof only, 3% tent and 39% open air. Most communities affected with subnational violence and floods continue to struggle to get children in school. Meanwhile, pastoralist communities have limited access to education as they from one location to another. Over the years, humanitarian and development partners have increased access to educational facilities in the country. The HRP 2022 estimates that 3.6 million people with 50% female, 97% children and 15% persons with disability need education services. Most schools and learners are still recovering from the spillover effect of COVID 19 pandemic as schools were closed, persistent subnational violence and unprecedent level of floods that have destroyed school infrastructure and displaced millions. Before the COVID school closures, at least 2.2 million children attended school but with the myriad of contextual pressures, many children have either dropped out of school or remains without access to learning. The education sector analysis shows key gaps in the response including access to education which is constraint by inadequate qualified teachers in classroom and lack of safe and protected infrastructure have affected children ability to go to school. In addition, protection, and wellbeing of children especially girl child remains critical due to increased abuse, cultural pressure, and food insecurity. The season migration of cattle herders has also affected the ability of children in cattle camps to have access to learning. This further creates potential social problems as many school-aged children end up in violent practices that deepens insecurity in the communities. Lastly, quality of teaching and learning remains a big gap in the education response. This is largely because of the lack of teaching and learning materials and high level of teachers' absenteeism resulting from poor and untimely renumeration. Similarly, the situation of children with disability is dire as most schools do not have mobility or assistive devices to enable CwD to attend and participate in class. The capacity of teachers to deliver inclusive education and lack of community awareness continue to hamper creating conducive learning environment for CwD.

2 - Project activities

Windle Trust will implement the following activities to address the above. To increase availability and functionality of learning spaces; WTI will (i) construct TLS classrooms in schools, (ii) renovate destroyed school infrastructure, (iii) construct of WASH facilities, (iv) provide teaching and learning materials, (v) provide dignity kits for girls in schools. To increase access and enrolment of children in school, WTI will conduct these activities including, (i) conduct one BTL campaign in each county targeting the locations with low enrolment and where the school construction and renovation has been done. (ii) Conduct orientation of teachers, facilitators and PTAs on enrolment drive and mobilization of OOSC. (iii) Conduct community engagement sessions in the primary school areas, (iv) train school mothers who will be champions for girl child education and (v) select and provide cash assistance to young mothers.

3 - Project indicators

WTI has a robust monitoring and evaluation system that will be used to track project activities and measure performance. During project inception, the MEAL Manager and the M&E Officer assigned to the project will develop a monitoring framework. The monitoring framework will have detailed indicators to be tracked, detailed implementation plan, frequency of data collection, community groups involved, how and where the data will be collected from. Description of indicators will be made user-friendly to ensure that the project beneficiaries, especially women and girls, are able to participate effectively in both the targeting and monitoring process. WTI will adopt education cluster and IINEE mandated tools and standards to monitor and measure the performance of the project. Post distribution monitoring questionnaire will be used to interview project beneficiaries who received dignity and learning kits to gather information on satisfaction of beneficiaries in terms of timeliness of input distribution, relevance of the inputs distributed, quality and quantity distributed, overall organization and modalities of distribution including security and protection issues in collaboration with protection cluster. The PDM will be conducted within a period of 2 weeks after delivery of assistance to follow up on beneficiary satisfaction with the quality of assistance provided, security and distance distribution sites, and time for distribution and collection particularly to female headed households and people with disabilities. At the end of every school term, post-term surveys will be conducted to get more information on enrolment, teaching and learning performance of the supported schools. Data from the post-term surveys will be used analyze access and quality of learning for the children. Findings from the surveys will be used to during the BTL and community events to improve enrolment, attendance, and teaching standards. Findings from the assessments will be shared and presented in the education cluster meetings and other stakeholders. The planned monitoring and evaluation activities will be coordinated with the cluster, specific donors, and other stakeholders. WTI will embed gender sensitive monitoring in all the different stages of intervention through conducting gender analysis, that aims to identify and analyze gender relations, identify gender specific needs, and suggest specific measures to strengthen women and girls’ rights in relation to access to education during the different stages of intervention. WTI will continue to use the existing feedback and complaint mechanisms based on community consultations, to avoid marginalizing the vulnerable community members, more than one channel will be available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Analysis and results of the feedback and complaints provided will be shared monthly and this will contribute to learning and provide information for evidence-based decision making to improve to program performance.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

WTI will utilize available market information and analyses reports provided by both WFP and the joint market monitoring initiative (JMMI). This is mainly based on trend analysis of the market prices and availability of essential goods and services, based on seasonal changes.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Due to COVID 19 school closure, several girls have dropped out of school due to early, forced and teenage pregnancies. This has caused them to stop going to school as they struggle with the responsibility of taking care of the infants. WTI implements an initiative called Young Mothers. These are underage girls who are forced out of school because of child bearing. Our approach involves talking to school managers and community leaders to allow such girls to return to school and continue with their studies. In addition, WTI provides monthly cash assistance to the young mothers to support them with meeting some of their household needs that also contribute to their absenteeism from classes. Thus, this project will embed the same approach to ensure that young mothers are given opportunity to go back to school in their community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Project beneficiaries will be sensitized about Protection against SexualExploitation and Abuse (PSEA), reporting mechanisms, posters and bannersabout PSEA will be displayed at project sites. Community Project ManagementCommittees will be trained on PSEA and channels for reporting. All PSEA messages will be embedded and mainstreamed into program activities in the field.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To avoid marginalizing the vulnerable community members, different channels of communication and feedback mechanism will be made available focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. These include public community meetings, regular consultations, focused group discussions, community committees, micro feedback surveys.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WTI has in place an active and established Feedback, Complaints, and Response Mechanism that all staff have been trained in and are using regularly. Our PSEA response is embedded into program activities where PSEA messages are mentioned in all events. To avoid marginalizing vulnerable community members, WTI has more than one feedback channels available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Focal points are identified and trained in all the selected project areas to lead on Community Complaints and Response Mechanisms including listening and reporting to feedback and response. During project community sensitization and community mobilization phase, WTI establishes and leverage on existing structures as entry points to better understand community priorities and levels of organization and management of projects intended to address their community problems, concerns and gaps. Consultations will be established with all the women representatives, youth leaders, representative of people with special needs (people with disabilities), elderly people, civil society organization present, community leaders, private sector actors to ensure all their views and concerns are captured for a smooth implementation of interventions.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Routine synthesis and analysis will be conducted as part of regular projectperformance review meetings. The analysis will be shared with senior management; sector leads or other stakeholders to inform future decision making on new policies or projects or increase inclusiveness.
