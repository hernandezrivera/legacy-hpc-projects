Project Summary

UNFPA and its partners will work to ensure nutritional support to Pregnant Lactating Women (PLW) & Adolescent Girls ( AG) affected by the economic situation Lebanon. It is estimated that 160,000 AG including pregnant adolescent girls and 35,000 PLW will be reached with nutritional support & counseling including provision of nutritional supplementation. These will be coupled with health interventions that focus on SRH and GBV. UNFPA implementing partners will reach out to PLW & AG in communities across Lebanon increasing their knowledge on healthy practices, promoting health seeking behavior & referring them to health care, nutritional counseling & GBV services.
UNFPA will focus on the following interventions:
Ensuring nutritional support for Adolescent Girls by
-Availing Iron Folic Acid Supplementation to 160,000 AG across Lebanon which will complement UNICEF support in MNT to PLW. The IFA will be provided to MOPH to be dispensed trough 245 PHCs and at least 100 dispensaries across Lebanon.
-Capacity development of 1200 health and social care providers and front line workers on nutritional needs for AG and PLW including SRH, GBV ,PSEA to ensure protection of vulnerable women and girls. The training will also tackle disability as per International guidelines.
-Development of training manuals on nutrition for AG and for PLW alongside IEC material. UNFPA has developed specific training packages for adolescent girls that will be used in the training of care providers and front line workers. A plan for dissemination of awareness messages on nutrition, SRH and GBV will be developed in coordination with local communities and municipalities.
-Provision of nutritional counseling and support to 100,000 AG through a network of 100 skilled care providers on nutrition, hygiene, SRH and GBV. Adolescent girls in need for diagnostic services, health care and IFA will be referred for further care in the nearest PHC; they will be informed about the possibility of medical consultation through tele-health either with a family medicine doctor or with a gynecologist. AG identified at risk of GBV will also receive support and be referred to further care as per GBV referral pathways.
-Community mobilization and awareness raising on nutritional needs, balanced diet, hygiene, menstrual management, SRH and GBV to adolescent girls that will be reached at the community level and then referred for further support including for nutritional counseling. IEC material and information leaflets about available Nutrition, SRH and GBV services will be also provided to adolescent girls alongside the awareness raising activities.
Promoting balanced diet, maternal care and healthy practices for PLW
-Development of education/ training material and IEC on nutritioally balanced diets for PLWs. IEC material will be translated and culturally adapted to the migrant population. A dissemination plan for the IEC material to be coordinated with local communities and municipalities.
-Capacity development for 750 women/ mother on balanced diet, food recipes, proper hygiene, SRH,GBV. These women/ mother will be identified by UNFPA implementing partners based on a set of criteria. They will be gathered into small groups that engage in awareness raising and support at the community level. The groups will also be informed about nutrition, SRH and GBV services available in their community for referral of cases as needed. They will be linked to midwives from LOM and Social workers from SWSL in same area for technical support if needed.
-Community mobilization and awareness raising on nutrition, SRH and GBV for 35000 PLW across Lebanon. Provided by the mother support groups, these will revolve around 1) nutritional needs and balanced diet for PLW including for preparation of healthy food and ensuring proper hygiene, 2) promoting health seeking behavior among PLW namely for antenatal and postnatal care as well for breastfeeding practices, 3) increasing awareness on GBV and referrals.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Activities will be tailored to the specific needs of women and girls of different age, nationalities, cultural background, sexual and gender orientation. Needs of women and girls with disabilities will factored into the project to extend the access to services to all the segments of the vulnerable target, marginalized and discriminated. Safety audits and community consultations will be regularly held to identify risks within targeted communities and to engage women, girls, men and boys in identifying plans to respond to the identified risks. Key messages about what constitutes PSEA and how to report allegations will be mainstreamed in all the outreach initiatives and community-based activities. Monitoring activities will include a section on protection risks and PSEA that will aim at maintaining a close relation with communities supported and regular collect their feedback

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNFPA operates according to the Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (Leadership and governance, Transparency, Feedback and complaints, Participation, Design, monitoring and evaluation). Women and girls are key actors in their own protection, and it is critical that they are active partners in identifying protection risks and solutions. UNFPA will facilitate their active involvement in the design, implementation and monitoring of this Action, by consulting with women and girls on GBV risks and constraints to their participation in and access to aid delivery, GBV and SRH services. UNFPA will also work with women and girls to identify those who are most marginalized and at high risk, and design approaches to ensure their participation. UNFPA will engage with women, girls, boys, and men from the affected communities to explain protection activities, facilitate community acceptance and address barriers to women's and girls' attendance.
