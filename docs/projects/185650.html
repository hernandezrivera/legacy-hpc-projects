Project Summary

BRAC’s Food Security & Livelihoods Sector sub- sectors: Agriculture & Environment, Ultra-Poor Graduation, Skill Development and Ayesha Abed Foundation, in Cox’s Bazar will undertake a holistic humanitarian approach with partners to enhance livelihoods and resilience of vulnerable host community (HC), promote self-reliance of Rohingya refugees and strengthen natural resource management for both communities. BRAC will implement interventions under the FSS objectives 2 and 3 in sync with BRAC’s objectives of localization, human development nexus and resilience building.
BRAC’s response will include technical capacity building and ensuring sustainable livelihoods, e.g homestead gardening, livestock services, inputs/service support, other off-farm skill development and job placement (in tailoring, gas burner repair, furniture making , light engineering etc.) to build self-reliance. Agriculture/environment activities will be implemented with respect to climate resilient practices i.e. crops that can withstand the environment and salinity that prevails in Cox’s Bazar. BRAC will prioritize localization by developing capacity of local NGOs, CBOs, youth clubs, government stakeholders and engaging them in implementation.
BRAC, a PSEA network member, has different policies prioritizing protection and safeguarding with an emphasis on ‘Do No Harm’ to ensure meaningful access, accountability, participation and empowerment of its beneficiaries. BRAC ensures protection and harassment policies are practically implemented with training for both staff and beneficiaries, so that the latter can understand their rights and engage in the feedback process. BRAC consults continually with its beneficiaries, taking feedback in design, implementation and completion of project. BRAC will periodically conduct community based inclusive approaches (e.g. PRA) and gender analysis, using sex, age and disability disaggregated data to analyze and tailor to food security needs, understand capacities, barriers and the differential food and nutrition requirements for different groups, especially vulnerable groups of women, children, elderly, persons with disabilities and female-headed households. Patriarchal attitudes and security concerns are the main barriers to participation in livelihoods, especially for women which the project will strive to address through sensitizing the male through community male leaders and volunteers.
Gender equality is core focus of BRAC's strategy and BRAC will emphasize empowerment and inclusivity of the vulnerable groups, mainly women and girls, elderly people, third gender and persons with disabilities supporting them to be linked to critical services in protection and health, ensure decent work environment to bring about a longer-term well-being. Since the reported rise in GBV as result of the lockdown in 2020-21, identified GBV victims will be referred to focal agency services.
BRAC emphasizes building resilience to disasters and emergencies and will have in place a contingency plan to replenish agriculture input for households and individuals most adversely affected. Two percent of the project resources will be allocated for cash transfer interventions including unconditional cash transfer and cash-for-work (e.g. plantation, nursery preparation) in the HC. The refugees will be trained in portable skills (agriculture, tailoring, mobile repair etc) so that when they are repatriated they can continue to apply these and ensure their food security and self-reliance.
The project with 236 direct staff will work in 11 unions of the HC of Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts with 17,600 households and in 21 refugee camps with 16,250 households. Under JRP 2022, total beneficiaries of BRAC’s food security and livelihoods project will be 33,850 with an estimated budget of USD 5,034,383.

1 - Identified Needs

"The food and nutrition security of both refugee and host community is a growing concern. The hard-hit host community is struggling to ensure basic needs of their family due to the increase of the commodity price and COVID-19 impact, making clear that the response actors will need to increase their focus on host community food security. Sustaining the food assistance needs is threatened by the funding gap and is limiting expansion of livelihood activities that could foster self-reliance. Breakage in the food supply pipeline could deepen the existing emergency and threatens the survival of the vulnerable refugees. The multifaceted efficient and need-based initiatives led by the response actors will ensure food security both for the refugee and the hard-hit host community. The Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 4, WFP April 2021) findings reveals that overall vulnerability has increased since 2019 to 2020 across both Rohingya and Host community and 24% and 30% of refugees and host community respectively have moved into higher vulnerability group.Both refugee and host communities have strong patriarchal culture, limiting women’s participation in income generation, leadership and decision-making. Food allocation for refugees is based on family size instead of being based on differential needs of women and girls. In both communities, the major food share goes preferably to male family members. Inadequate food and low diet diversity has caused malnutrition, especially among children, pregnant and lactating mothers, and despite the efforts to positively target women and girls, they are still lagging in terms of food security and nutrition indicators (Gender& intersectionality report October, 2020, UNHCR). The. In sync with sector findings (JMSNA 2020), BRAC’s need assessments have also found that COVID-19 has exacerbated these inequalities for the women, children and also for the other vulnerable groups - elderly and persons with disabilities which has continued into 2021.Needs of Rohingya Refugees: 902,947 Rohingyas reside across 34 heavily congested refugee camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar (Population factsheet, UNHCR, Sept, 2021). With limited access to self-reliance activities and extended impact of COVID-19, the refugees remain highly reliant on humanitarian assistance, and almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs, most commonly related to food security and livelihoods (JMSNA, Oct2021). JMSNA (Oct 2021) reports that 72% of households cited food security/livelihood is one of the top needs out of 3 priority needs for 2022. Sustaining regular and timely food assistance to the Rohingya population will continue to be of paramount importance in 2022 and disruption in the food supply pipeline could deepen the emergency and threaten their survival. Multiple factors continue to drive high vulnerability including limited livelihood opportunities, restrictions on movement to search for income opportunities, depleted savings and asset stripping. Allowing the refugee population to engage in small-scale agricultural and skill development activities will foster self-reliance, food security, dietary diversity, and access to fresh foods.Needs of Host Community: Since the Rohingya influx in Cox’s Bazar, the intense pressure and competition are increasing in the host communities for local resources like food, labor market. The sustained impacts of COVID-19 on livelihoods contraction and absence of universal food aid coverage affected consumption (REVA 4, WFP April 2021). Access to alternative income besides assistance is vital to reducing their vulnerability. Scaling up economic and livelihoods support initiatives will assist in mitigating the potential impacts of the influx. JMSNA (Oct 2021) finds that adoption of livelihoods-based coping strategies remained similar to 2020 J-MSNA findings, indicating households having not recovered from the effect of lockdown, with a risk of further deterioration.
