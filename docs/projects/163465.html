Project Summary

Cameroon’s South West (SW) and North West (NW) regions began to experience social instability and unrest following protests against the government’s treatment of the Anglophone population and the government’s response to this unrest in 2016. The resulting socio-political crisis gradually turned into insecurity and armed violence as early as October 2017, which has only escalated. Since 2018, civilians have had to endure heightened tensions and erupting conflict between the area’s separatist militias and the country’s national security forces. Since the SW/NW conflict began, over 500,000 people have been internally displaced, and more than 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance . The continuous offensive by Government forces against non-state armed groups (NSAGs) has also pushed almost 40,000 Cameroonians to seek refuge in Nigeria. By the end of 2018, UN agencies had declared a Level 2 emergency owing to the worsening humanitarian situation in the SW and NW.
In a context of insecurity, the protection of civilians is particularly at risk. Between January and July 2019, protection actors identified and reported over 3,700 protection incidents through protection monitoring and assessments conducted in two thirds (seven) of the NW/SW divisions. Among these incidents 2,300 were recorded in the SW and 1,400 in the NW. In both regions, Mezam, Fako, Meme, Lebialem and Manyu were the most affected divisions, with 84% of incidents; 55% of the victims were females and 13% were children. The most frequent incidents were violations of the right to property through house destruction, which constitutes about 50% of the incidents.
To respect the Do-No-Harm principles and to achieve the objective stated above the proposed project will partner with and work within the framework of the WFP’s Multi-Purpose Cash assistance activities to provide a complementary protection component, which will be carried out through the following activities:
Activity 1. Conduct Do No Harm risk assessments and support Protection Mainstreaming Action Plan implementation
The IRC will undertake an initial protection risk assessment and do-no-harm assessment at the beginning of the project to identify main protection risks faced by people in their daily life and issues related to the cash assistance transfer cycle. After identifying risks faced by populations, IRC and WFP will develop an Action Plan proposing mitigation measures, to address risks and the barriers as well as ensure meaningful access to assistance. This assessment will also include the validation and/or review of WFP vulnerability targeting criteria with affected communities.
Activity 2: Conduct protection mainstreaming training and ToTs for beneficiary sensitisation
To ensure implementation of a ‘Do-No-Harm’ (DNH) approach and protection mainstreaming plan, the IRC will develop training modules and conduct a joint training with WFP at the beginning of the project for relevant staff and local partner, on their own code of conduct, protection principles, and specific measures to ensure implementation of a DNH approach and specific measures for protection mainstreaming during project implementation (including protection-sensitive management of complaints and feedback mechanisms). The pre-test and post-tests are part of the training component. All participants to IRC training organized with partners will undergo these tests before and at the end of the training in order to verify that they have achieved the learning objectives.
Activity 3: Supporting and reinforcing feedback mechanisms for clients and accountability mechanism
The IRC's protection staff and accountability and feedback mechanism staff will support the strengthening of WFP’s existing complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM).
The multipurpose cash component of the project is implemented by WFP.

To respect the Do-No-Harm principles and to achieve the objective stated above the proposed project will partner with and work within the framework of the WFP’s Multi-Purpose Cash assistance activities to provide a complementary protection component, which will be carried out through the following activities: Activity 1. Conduct Do No Harm risk assessments and support Protection Mainstreaming Action Plan implementation The IRC will undertake an initial protection risk assessment and do-no-harm assessment at the beginning of the project to identify main protection risks faced by people in their daily life and issues related to the cash assistance transfer cycle. After identifying risks faced by populations, IRC and WFP will develop an Action Plan proposing mitigation measures, to address risks and the barriers as well as ensure meaningful access to assistance. This assessment will also include the validation and/or review of WFP vulnerability targeting criteria with affected communities. At the same tim e and In the first instancof the risk assessment, IRC will conduct a protection baseline of the project which will help identify negative coping mechanisms used by the most vulnerable and which put them at risk. These findings will then IRC articulates recommendations on how cash interventions can reduce identified protection risks through adapted targeting and mitigation measures and ensure that the assistance reduces direct safety concerns. The final protection endline will help measure if the cash grant provided following recommendations has helped reduce vulnerability and increase capacity of the most vulnerable to cope. In addition, a comprehensive Protection risk analysis which includes a do-no-harm analysis will include systematic identification of risks that people may face as a result of accessing cash assistance as a modality in this context. Upon review and approval from WFP, the findings of these assessments will be shared with local partners in order to establish a Protection Mainstreaming and a Risk Mitigation Action Plan. Those can be used by all partners and volunteers during their activities in order to minimize protection risks for beneficiaries. In addition, based on the identified risks, materials and awareness messages will be developed and disseminated. Based on the findings of the risks assessments, the IRC will propose IEC materials for beneficiaries which are adapted to the findings of the riskassessments, in order to ensure they have access to information and assistance in a manner which prioritizes their safety, dignity and avoids causing harm, allowing for information that contributes towards meangingful access to cash assistance by ensuring knowledge on their rights to assistance and existing accountability mechanisms. IRC will consider WFP’s existing IEC materials and messages and help contextualise them to take into account the various special needs of the people we are targeting through this project (literacy, disability, etc). IRC will then produce these materials and give them to WFP’s partners to disseminate. IRC will develop a protection mainstreaming Action Plan and WFP will be in charge of implementing it. IRC will support in training local partners for its implementation. This whole process will enable an improved gender, age and ability-inclusive process. The IRC will also carry out a second protection risk and do-no-harm assessment at the mid-term point of the project to see if risks identified or mitigation strategies initially developed need to be adapted or updated. This will include assessing the delivery of assistance against the initial protection and DNH assessment and the protection mainstreaming action plan. This will also include collecting information on existing protection risks related to the delivery of the cash assistance.
