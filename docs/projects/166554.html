Project Summary

The lock down coming in into effect on Monday 30th March has had a significant impact on access and quality of child protection services. Even though child protection services have been designated by Government as essential services with approval to deliver services despite the lockdown, most child protection service providers had to temporarily halt their services at the beginning of the lock down to revise strategies of delivery of services through online platforms and for sourcing PPE. Most CSO partners of UNICEF are willing to attend to the most critical cases through face to face interaction. This is especially important for reaching children with disability and survivors of sexual violence.
Economic stress, further increased by the impact of the lockdown on those dependent on the informal economy for their survival, anxiety surrounding the COVID-19 outbreak and living in lockdown without access to basic services, has increased the level of stress in households, psychosocial distress and depression in children and caregivers, resulting in increased risk of sexual and other forms of violence and abuse, and separation from caregivers for survival. The Child helpline has seen an up surge of calls during the lockdown, receiving 27,943 calls more than those received last year during the same period. 41% of the calls received are directly related to violence against children and SGBV, with 75% of the perpetrators being people within the child’s home environment.
Residential care centers and border quarantine facilities and other places of safety where children who were previously living on the streets and children returning from Botswana and South Africa have been placed, lack the bare minimum of basic services to maintain adequate personal hygiene, recreation and services to care for them.
This project is aimed at helping to mitigate the negative short and long-term effects on children as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak, preventing and responding to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children , promoting safety, mental and psychosocial well-being of children especially the most vulnerable including children living with disabilities, children on the move, those living in the streets and in residential care.

1 - Activities

The Project activities aim at 1) Equipping child protection service provision facilities to ensure COVID-19 infection prevention and control measures are adhered to 2) Scaling up of child protection service provision to respond to the impact of COVID-19 on most vulnerable children in affected areas and 3) enhancing the Child Protection Subsector coordination through the Ministry of Social Welfare, with technical support from UNICEF.The key activities include; 1.Integration of MHPSS, child protection, PSEA messages in IEC materials and other information and awareness tools/channels targeting front line workers, children, caregivers, women and men2.Through local radio stations (in local, Shona and Ndebele languages), TV, social media platforms dissemination of child friendly COVID-19 prevention messages as well as messages on prevention of children from Violence, Abuse and Exploitation.3.Development of activity toolkit for children and caregivers in isolation to facilitate parenting and child protection learning.4.Development and dissemination of MHPSS toolkits for frontline workers as well as children and caregivers 5.Facilitation of rescue, access to health services, psychosocial support and referral for children survivors of sexual and gender-based violence.6.Training of social workers and CCWs on COVID-19-sensitive CPiE response, referral pathways and after care7.Re-enforcement of community interventions, referral systems and outreach to manage child protection cases and prevent spread of disease as a result of service delivery8.Improving quality assurance and working environment for child protection actors
