Project Summary

Rationale:
9 years of conflict in Syria has not only led to destruction and reduced functionality of infrastructure, but also affected socio-economic patterns, and diminished Syria’s population’s ability to sustain living standards, to benefit from decent and gainful livelihood opportunities and to access essential services. Consequently, protraction of this situation has led to a further strain on social cohesion and community security, stressing the Social Capital of Syrian communities.
Needs assessments through various sources indicate that currently more than 8.6 million Syrians are in acute need of Early Recovery and Livelihood support.
Based on the on the ERL sector’s objective framework, needs have been categorized under the three interrelated components: : infrastructure, improving access to basic and social services, and promoting the social cohesion through working for and with communities.
Through the area-based approach, UN-Habitat aims to boost sustainable access to basic and social services to displaced, host and returnee populations in selected neighborhoods in Rural Damascus, Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Hama and Aleppo.
In practice, the approach consists in rehabilitation of public spaces, roads, sidewalks, and debris removal whenever needed in damage-affected neighborhoods, which are prioritized and developed through ‘Neighbourhood Action Plans”, a municipality-led program supported by UN-HABITAT. The holistic notion to serve entire conﬂict-affected, vulnerable communities through improvement of essential infrastructure and related services, under the objective to revive neighborhoods, is at the core of this project.
Objectives:
This area-based early recovery project, derived from experience gained in previous pilot projects and evaluation of similar interventions, aims to:
• Restore essential infrastructure and services, promote the rehabilitation of living space, key factors in reviving affected neighborhoods and contributing to improve stability in areas,
• Enhance security in communities through provision of street lighting,
• Involve the community members and main stakeholders in identifying community-based needs, planning of suitable response options and implementation of activities, thus, to promote neighborhood ownership.
• Enhance municipalities capacity to assess, plan and implement adequate programs to respond to communities’ most urgent needs, thus to reinforce local coping capacity.
Project components:
The project targets 140,00 vulnerable returnees, IDPs and Host families in Rural Damascus, Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Hama and Aleppo. Planned and implemented through neighbourhood action plans developed through MTOS (Municipal Technical Office Support) programs, its main components comprise:
• Procurement and installation of solar street lighting systems,
• Public – space-, sidewalks- and road- rehabilitation, implemented to the best possible extent through local workforce, especially unemployed youth,
• Rehabilitation of market streets
• Capacitating municipal staff to improve service delivery at the municipality- and neighbourhood- level through training workshops and provision of technical equipment to support the technical services, improve the data collection, analysis, planning and monitoring.
All activities will be carried out in consideration of environmental- cultural heritage and social aspects.

4 - Needs

The ERL Sector analysis of humanitarian needs is categorized in 3 inter-related themes:Infrastructure and basic services: Estimated 3.4 million people need humanitarian assistance to make their houses habitable, to restore accessibility and safety in areas, especially newly accessible, e.g. through debris clearance, and to restore functionality and performance of basic WASH-, electricity- infrastructure and related services. In addition, health- and education infrastructure and public space, is in critical need of attention to provide essential services and to facilitate socio-economic recovery not only for 6.5 million internally displaced persons, but also for host communities and spontaneous returnees.Livelihood: According to sector estimates, more than half of 4.9 million potential members of the workforce (between 18 and 65 years of age) lack access to sustained employment, a fact exacerbated by severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital. One of the consequences is high likelihood of youth to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as joining extremist organizations, crime, trafficking etc. Social cohesion and community security: Prolonged conflict impacts the social fabric and equally affects host families and IDPs. This situation is likely to aggravate due to returnees adding strain to already overburdened neighbourhoods and communities. Besides the criteria in HRP,the selection of targeted locations is based on *Being in line with the ERL Sector andHRP 2020 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs *Being prioritylocations identified through detailed field assessments and consultants with local expertsand municipalities (MTOS) *Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UNHABITATresources and donor commitments *High numbers of people in need,vulnerable and expected returnees in target locations *High complementarity with areabasedinterventions in WASH, Protection, and shelter

5 - Implementation of activities

In the governorates and sub-districts mentioned above, the project will implement the following activity lines: 1. Restoration of and access to basic infrastructure: • Cleaning the neighbourhood from debris and waste and sterilizing the roads and streets;• Rehabilitation of roads and footbaths;• Rehabilitation of public and open spaces• Provide streetlighting • Creating a gender friendly learning open spaces for women, youth and children to conduct awareness campaigns and sessions. 2. Restoration of markets streets: • Market rehabilitation; cleaning the Market street, providing street lighting 3. Improve effective local public services delivery: • Building the capacity of municipality staff through the implementing the MTOS (Municipality Technical Office Support) programme to strengthen service delivery at municipality and neighbourhood level. • Provision of technical equipment to for data collection, analysis planning and monitoring in the municipalities; • Conducting training workshops for community representatives; and • Refurbishment of municipal services premises and workspace. Following the respective ministries’ procedural requirements, projects will be implemented through local resources – communities, municipal technical departments, private sector entities, and workforce engaged from affected populations, in particular unemployed youth, as well as NGOs, in areas and thematic sectors identified through municipality-led MTOS processes. As much as possible, implementation of this project will be linked to other sectorial projects in the same target areas.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Discrimination or exclusion of possible beneficiaries / beneficiary groups from Early recovery assistance on grounds of provenance, ethnicity, social adherence, religion, age, gender or other criteria. Likelihood: H Impact: H Mitigation Measures: *Adopt interactive, stakeholders’ participatory transparent process for criteria definition and selection. *Strengthening municipalities’ and relevant authorities’ capacity to restore and update relevant records and to develop area-and situation specific action plans for all residents regardless of documentary status; *Constant advocacy on all levels to adopt suitable mechanisms and procedures for inclusion of all possible beneficiaries in temporary assistance and durable solutions. Protection Risk/Threat: Discrimination or exclusion of female or young beneficiaries / female or children/youth headed households from Early recovery assistance due to absence of registered male family leaders Likelihood: H Impact: H Mitigation Measures: *Transparent process for criteria detention and beneficiary selection; *Project design to prioritize vulnerable beneficiaries and at-risk groups *Collaborative, stakeholder inclusive process to identify priority areas and to design specific programmatic interventions. Risk: Youth's risk of exploitation through child labor in ERL projects due to absence of working age male family members, family's lack of other revenues and/to lack of financial resources Likelihood: M Impact: M *Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ labourers; *Add zero tolerance disclosure on child labour in the contracts; *Obligate the contractor to not engage under-age labor for project implementation Coordination supervision of contractor’s requirements with to ensure labor regulations are respected. Random check of personnel’s civil documentation on project sites

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk/Assumption: Non-consideration of livelihood, Early Recovery support projects on grounds of national/local spatial development plans Likelihood: L Impact: H Mitigation: *Municipality-led, stakeholder inclusive, transparent processes for project identification; *Capacitate municipalities and technical departments to lead assistance and recovery projects in their constituencies, through project- management-oriented capacity building programs, provision of adequate workspace, IT and technical equipment *Align assistance activities on the basis of requirements, situational circumstances and municipalities’ spatial development plans through a collaborative, consultative process *Advocate for intermediate solutions for affected communities in areas unlikely to be developed on short term. Risk/Assumption: Delayed assistance to vulnerable and people in need due to cumbersome processes to approve programs, projects, locations Likelihood: H Impact: H Mitigation: *Close coordination with the specific ministries and other related public departments to expedite processes *Diligence and due consideration of time aspects in project planning processes, incl. anticipation of possible refutation *Selection of project locations with high concentration of vulnerable / at-risk groups but with little likelihood of being contested Risk/Assumption: Incomplete, resp. partial assistance to vulnerable and people in need due to authorities' curtailing of comprehensive programmatic approaches Likelihood: M Impact: H Mitigation: *Close coordination with the resp. bodies in the ministries and other related public departments to identify and design projects based on municipal priorities and action plans *Capacitate municipalities and technical departments to lead assistance and recovery projects in their constituencies, through project- management-oriented capacity building programs
