Project Summary

"UNDP proposes to extend the sanitary landfill at Camp 20 Extension, construct a sanitary landfill beside Camp 27, and another that services Teknaf municipality. Alongside these, the project seeks to establish 4 Material Recovery Facilities (MRF), support waste segregation at source and waste transport, and strengthen the overall management of solid waste through raising awareness of 3R's, development of SOPs for MRF management and landfill, training on SWM systems and developing emergency waste management plans. Since the arrival of the Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar District in 2017, Teknaf and
Ukhiya Upazilas have suffered the challenges of massive amounts of waste generated in
an area where had no functional systems of waste collection. While in the refugee camps,
Solid Waste Management (SWM) systems have immediately been set up by WASH
partners of the international humanitarian response, host communities have suffered the
major consequences of waste threatening the health of people and the environment. In
2019 UNDP initiated a project to establish basic systems of waste collection in the most
affected Unions of Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas (Raja Palong and Palong Khali, Whykhong,
Nhilla and Baharchara and Teknaf Municipality) and 27 camps in Ukhiya. However there
are still major gaps and needs that the new project proposes to address/reinforce (see
section needs). Based on the lessons learned of that ongoing initiative, the current
project aims at supporting the local government to lead quality and community-driven
service, with particular attention to gender, age and people with disabilities barriers to
participate in the SWM systems in place, and continuing the waste management
operations for final waste disposal and collection of recyclables in the refugee camps.
Based on the lessons learned of that ongoing initiative, this project aims at approaching
with the same solution the remaining 7 camps in Teknaf Upazila and operational
maintenance of camp 20 ext.
PROJECT GOALS:
1) Technical Support to the local government and wash sector to establish basic systems
of waste collection and safe disposal and build their administrative, financial and
governance capacity to take over the overall management of waste collection, recovery
and generation of green job opportunities.
2) Supporting the District level authorities (DPHE, WASH Sector) to operate and maintain
the large SWM facility camps for waste recovery and final waste disposal.
3) Supporting the camps transportation of waste to the sanitary landfill to ensure proper
and safe disposal of waste, and recyclables to the recycling sector in host communities.
4) Improve and consolidate the business modality and marketplace of the Recycling
sector, as well as the camps access of the association members. Zero tolerance will be
applied to child labour within the recycling waste collection.
5)Proper collection and management of solid waste significantly contributes to reduce
the impact that solid waste causes to the environment. SWM also contributes to mitigate
climate change"

1 - Identified Needs

UNDP successfully implemented a Sustainable Solution to SWM project in Cox’s Bazar (CXB) from 2018-2022. This project has contributed significantly to improving the health of the people who live there and to cleaning up the environment. Due to the protracted nature of the Rohingya crisis, the SWM project in CXB needs continue support. 2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The Project will work in close collaboration with WASH Sectors, local government, andother relevant stakeholders to support DPHE overall solid waste managementstrategy. The project will contribute to improve camp-based coordination for landfillaccess at the camp level. So, it will contribute to improving waste managementservice delivery for Ukhiya and Teknaf camps. The project closely works with theRRRC office to get access for the camps and other government departments. UNDP isaiming to work closely with the WASH sector coordination platform to improve theSWM systems and Sanitary landfill activity in the camps, including the construction ofa new Sanitary Landfill in Teknaf camps. In host communities, UNDP will work withthe WASH sector in improving the coordination, communication, and prioritizing of theSWM issues in the local government agenda. UNDP will also monitor the SWMsystems established in host communities including the financial modality to identifythe feasibility of future funding options.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"UNDP has a comprehensive commitment to ending child labour and forced labour, aspart of broader United Nations protection strategy. In recruiting local partners toimplement specific activities (cash for work, recycling, rehabilitation of areas alreadycontaminated by solid waste) UNDP shall ensure that all contracts contain a zerotoleranceclause in reference to child labour or forced labour. In addition to the above,the SWM project, with its strong livelihood content, has also to be informed by theconcept of ‘decent work’ as articulated by ILO. The project expects to ensure equalparticipation of women and men (inclusive of girls and boys of 18 years and above) inthe SWM systems and decision-making processes. As gender matters in the SWMsystems because members of the families have different perceptions and views onroles and responsibilities of work both domestic and public, the needs andpreferences differ between men and women."

3.3 - Age & Disability

"Age, Disability and Gender are essential and core components of all UNDP projects, and this is especiallytrue when working with populations which are already particularly poor anddisadvantaged. Solid waste management techniques and practices, when donecorrectly, have the potential to improve the lives and opportunities available to womenand girls across the affected project area’s. "This project will Implement a community-level PSEA plan that will collaborate with local leaders, engage the community through tailored awareness sessions focusing on local context, provide training to members to recognize and report abuse, establish accessible and confidential reporting mechanisms, integrate PSEA into existing practices, mobilize resources, coordinate with stakeholders, and regularly review and adapt strategies based on community feedback. This will involve the enforcement of clear PSEA policies, accessible, and culturally appropriate PSEA awareness materials regular training sessions for staff, partners, volunteers, and project participants, the enforcement of codes of conduct, the implementation of confidential complaints and feedback mechanisms, transparent investigation and accountability measures, coordination with other organizations, continuous monitoring and evaluation, engagement with local communities, and a strong commitment from organizational leadership. UNDP is a member of the PSEA network and regularly participates in meetings. UNDP has a strong PSEA policy, confidential and accessible reporting mechanisms, and focal points in place. The project will utilize the existing PSEA system to ensure that it is mainstreamed across project activities and approaches. These efforts will collectively aim to prevent and respond effectively to incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse within humanitarian and development assistance. "

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"The proposed solid waste management project will help to rehabilitate areas of thelocal environment which have been damaged by improvised and unplanned wastedisposal, improving the local water quality and restoring key natural areas. Wastecontributes to the damming of waterways, contributing to the formation of stagnantpools which hinder movement, increase flood risk, and contribute to the erosion offragile grounds. Clearing waste and providing basic waste management systems willhave a critical impact on the daily lives of the vulnerable people who must navigatethese areas daily. Improved waste management systems are critical investmentstowards protecting the few remaining forests and biodiverse areas in the district."
