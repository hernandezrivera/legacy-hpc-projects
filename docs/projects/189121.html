Project Summary

The chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructures, conflicts and repeated flooding with massive destruction of facilities in South Sudan have aggravated access to basic WASH services among the affected community members
Due to this, there is a need to reduce the burden of WASH-related diseases causing malnutrition in targeted locations. This Integrated WASH project to be implemented by NSDO will be aiming at breaking the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhoea and cholera), thus improving food intake for malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition objectives.
The project will begin by jointly reviewing the needs with beneficiary members of each activity site. This will involve identifying most vulnerable households within the community, i.e households with pregnant and lactating woman, female-headed households, households with disabled and/or elderly persons, etc. In addition, with other partners specialized in nutrition, identification of the payams/villages/communities with the highest prevalence of malnutrition and the most catastrophic WASH conditions will take place. The assistance/response package will include nutrition facility WASH package, community WASH package and cross cutting package.
Throughout the response, NSDO will mainstream protection and mitigate WASH Gender Based Violence to guarantee that beneficiaries of WASH services, in particular women and children can access facilities in a safe manner. In addition, sensitive practices will be considered at all stages in the programme cycle; from preparation and design, through to the evaluation and re-design of intervention.
The project will be implemented in Kapoeta East, North and Lafon. These locations were selected as Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in Eastern Equatoria State. These areas are characterised with not only difficulty in securing enough water but also having extremely low coverage of water infrastructure, which explains why 49 per cent of households rely on surface or unimproved water as their main water source.
The project will target 20,682 beneficiaries, 10,547 females and 10,134 males. 34% of the population and children, boys and girls whereas 15% will be PWD.
During the entire project cycle the affected population will be enabled to give feedback, have access to adequate and timely information and participate in the project design, implementation and evaluation.

1 - Needs

In the WASH Cluster severity mapping, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North and Lafon Counties in Eastern Equatoria State are mapped at severity level 4, whereby the area is marked as Cholera hot spot in addition of presence of other diseases such as diarrhoea. In addition, Kapoeta East is classified as priority 1 for WASH response, whereas Kapoeta North and Lafon counties are priority 2 counties for WASH response. These areas are characterised with not only difficult in securing enough water but extremely low coverage of water infrastructure, which explains why 49 per cent of households rely on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. Surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods, when latrine pits seep into waterways. Limited access to water is also compounded by only 14 per cent of households reporting having at least one jerrycan and/or bucket to safely store water. The current people in need of WASH services in the country are 6.1 Million. With more returnees arriving from IDP and refugee camps, there is an increase of the population dependent on existing facilities. This might result in an increased WASH-related disease morbidity among children, risk of disease outbreak, limited sustainability of Nutrition and Health outcomes and higher prevalence of GBV at WASH facilities. Children, women and people living with a disability are likely to be those suffering the most as a result of these conditions.. Therefore, there is a need to reduce the burden of WASH-related diseases as aggravating factors for malnutrition in the targeted locations exhibiting extreme WASH vulnerability and high prevalence of malnutrition. This Integrated WASH project to be implemented by NSDO will be aiming at breaking the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhoea), thus improving food intake by malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition objectives. According to HNO 2022, limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, for instance, water points and latrines, has carry-on impacts when utilizing a multi-sectoral view. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health. The project will target 20,682 beneficiaries, 10,547 females and 10,134 males. 34% of the population and children, boys and girls whereas 15% will be PWD. NSDO is well placed to implement this project since it has operations in Central Equatoria State- Juba-Rajaf, Kajo Keji and Mangaten with a clear understanding of the dynamics and context of these locations, given the past experience in implementing SSHF funded projects, in the areas of WASH. In addition, NSDO’s project staff are on the ground to respond to the need and have established good relationship with the community authorities and have received written testimonies from authorities appreciating the team and donors for the current WASH services provision during previous WASH projects in the county.

2 - Project activities

NSDO will undertake the following actives as per the SO. In order to reduced morbidity and mortality of vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs (SO1), the organization will integrate WASH in nutrition response through: • Provision of a WASH kit to each unit (SAM child / caretaker/PLW) • Provision of WASH services in nutrition facilities • Provision of WASH services especially water supply and hygiene in communities with high GAM and WASH vulnerability. This will include repair/rehabilitation of water points, extension/upgrading of existing water supply systems; upgrading water points with flood-resilient approaches for those located in flooded areas. Sanitation and hygiene promotion in locations facing a high risk of outbreak and congested sites. In order to ensure that vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action SO2. In order to achieve the above SO the organization will undertake the following:• Provision of WASH NFIs, hygiene promotion, emergency sanitation and water supply in emergencies. Response scenarios include floods, water scarcity and dryness, diseases outbreak, displacement or response to violence.• Conduct safety audit conduct localized barrier analysis to identify WASH needs related to disability• Protection assessment conducted by female staff with women, girls and children through FGD for safe and accessible WASH facilities with community input • Training WASH staff and community WASH groups on the safe design and construction of WASH facilities that mitigate GBV risk. In addition , NSDO will also ensure that water points are not triggers of conflict.• Training of women on operation and maintenance of water supply and sanitation systems In order to ensure that vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions that respect their rights, SO3, NSDO will ensure that community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure. In this regard, the following activities will be undertaken:• Flood-resilient water points will be rehabilitated/ constructed.• Flood-resilient Latrines stances rehabilitated/ constructed

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be incorporated during the implement ion period and changes made accordingly . In addition, a committee representing the beneficiaries will be set up at the start which ensures that the project staff incorporate the feedback.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

To pass info to the vulnerable, the Organization will engage mostly local who understands the language (local) as well as use visuals

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Through PSEA Coordinator who will disseminate info about SEA to the beneficiaries and establish a reporting mechanism. NSDO will take into account protection issues in its project implementation to ensure that women’s dignity, safety and security during the WASH service provision is put into account. This will reduce and curb possible risks of GBV which deter women from using the latrines. The facilities will therefore be renovated to ensure safety by paying special attention to door locks, privacy/curtain walls, clear labelling to disintegrate the facilities by gender and provision of separate rooms for MHM. This will in turn reduce protection challenges of females against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse during access to WASH services. Increasing the number of water point will also help to ensure protection of women and to reduce GBV as women will not have to travel long distances in search of water and thereby not or exposing themselves less to sexual harassment.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Complaint Response\Feedback (CRFM) desk will be standby right from the initial Phase of the analysis and at the distribution points and the overall feedback and complaint mechanisms to be established will play a key role in resolving beneficiaries’ queries and complains and compliments in a proactive manner. Beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or raise issues during the provision of services, whereby the hygiene promoters, latrine attendants, and water point attendants will gather information daily and report to the Field Coordinator. Additionally, NSDO will gather complaints and feedback through the weekly FGDs. The NSDO gender focal person will be dedicated to conduct this activity once in a week and work with protection colleagues and the Protection Cluster to provide further support that improves dignity of women and girls. Therefore Beneficiaries will be involved in the formulation of the project and the the use of communication tools and strategy will be an essential to ensure two-way information sharing.
