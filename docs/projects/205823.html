Project Summary

The proposed NRC’s WASH (Water, Sanitation and Hygiene) interventions for 2024 is continued to support the people affected by conflict, natural disaster and displacement in emergency context and static as long-term support for the IDPs, host community and people to return, resettle and reintegrate with host community. The interventions are designed to address the emergency WASH needs through rapid response mechanism (RRT) and emergency response and contribute to improve health, nutrition, education, Livelihood and food security, shelter and protection outcomes and durable solutions through NRC static WASH response. The proposed interventions are to reduce prevalence of malnutrition, contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality, provide access to basic WASH services in underserved and hard to reach locations affected by disaster, disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility and durable access to WASH amongst 250, 000 IDPs, returnees, refuges and vulnerable host communities in in South Sudan. NRC’s WASH interventions will be integrated and implemented in close coordination with NRC’s other core competencies of Shelter, Education, Livelihood and Food security, ICLA and protection programming and teams, applying an integrated approach to uplift people’s lives and contributing to South Sudan HRP 2024 and WASH Cluster objectives and the 2024 Humanitarian Response.
Recognizing WASH services to be key drivers of health and malnutrition outcomes and women and girls to be the primary users of WASH services, the project aims to reduce GBV risk by ensuring adequate and needs based provision of WASH services that address key protection concerns. NRC’s integrated approach will further address high levels of malnutrition in targeted locations. NRC will consult and engage communities in preliminary assessments and design process to ensure all NRC’s interventions are tailored to local context and to establish appropriated water and sanitation facilities at community, institutions and household level. To enhance and ensure sustainability and proper usage of the facilities comprehensive training will be provided to female and male members of Water User Committees (WUCs) , Schools and other institutional staff. Solid waste management training will also be provided to solid waste/hygiene committees to implement community-driven solid waste collection and disposal campaigns.
To ensure durable solution and continues access to safe water; NRC will establish / rehabilitate small scale solar powered water supply schemes , new boreholes through cost effective manual Borehole drilling and machine drilling . The proposed water supply interventions will increase the safe water access to schools, health, nutrition and protection centers, disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WAH facilities and route to WASH facilities.
NRC will construct owner driven household / family shared latrines to ensure that immediate and long-term sanitation needs are adequately met and facilities are gender and culturally appropriate. The approach will enhance the ownership and sustainability of the facilities. NRC will also promote good hygiene practices and awareness through training of community hygiene promoters who then will be conducting hygiene promotion at their own communities, community hygiene campaigns, hygiene promotion campaigns and sessions at intuitional levels. NRC will also provide hygiene kits, WASH-NFIs and soaps to the vulnerable communities specially in hard-to-reach areas.

1. Needs

People of South Sudan has been experiencing and continue to face deteriorating humanitarian situation. The situation further attributed and worsened due to multiple shocks and unaddressed root causes of need of endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges, and economic and climatic shocks. All these challenges impacted affected and vulnerable people’s physical and mental well-being, living standards, and coping mechanisms, exacerbated by marginalization of women, youth, and persons with disabilities. Access to essential services, including health care, education, water, and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, continues to be limited. The WASH cluster 2023 Humanitarian Need Overview sectoral analysis revealed that; total of 6.15 million people needs access to safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene facilities. The conditions are worse in Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. The main WASH cluster objectives and strategies for 2023-2024 is to Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WASH severity Counties (WSC) 4 and 5 counties; Thus, NRC will deliver WASH services through static response and mobile emergency response in all NRC existing counties across all ten states in South Sudan. Access to water and sanitation is projected to remain a major issue in 2024, further aggravating the risk of malnutrition. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera, AWD, etc. Hence to significantly to contribute to achieve WASH Cluster objective 1:- Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition; NRC will implement integrated WASH , Health and Nutrition, Shelter, Education and LFS response in targeted counties in all ten states. Moreover, the HNO (Humanitarian Needs overview) 2023, further reveled that ,42 percent of the population reported do not have access improved water sources to meet their household needs countrywide and 45 percent of households travelling for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities. The HNO found that 49% of the populations / households are relying on surface and unimproved water such as river, canal, stagnant water as their main water sources for drinking and secondary usages,. Water storage and water collection containers are limited and only 16% of households reporting having at least one jerrycan and /or bucket to safely store water. Protection to access the water points is also another concern.HNO 2023 revealed that, the rampant open defecation is further creating public health risks especially in areas where people rely and using surface water. The HNO assessment revealed that; Sanitation facilities across south Sudan are uncommon access to improved sanitation is below 10% in 56 counties with most extreme percentage in Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei states; there has been some cultural barriers to use the constructed latrines is also another challenge to improve people sanitation practices. Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Overall, only 16 per cent of the households reported owning soap and this figure was as low as 4 per cent and 11 per cent in Jonglei and Upper Nile States respectively, (HNO,2023). Through proposed WASH interventions in water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion. NRC further aims to improve and strengthen ongoing WASH initiatives and expand into further IPC 3,4 and 5 locations in all targeted counties.

2. Project activities

Conduct Assessments and Baselines: • in-depth situation analysis, baseline and KAP survey to assess WASH needs and key trends in target communities.• Feasibility, Geophysical survey, technical and sanitary surveys of targeted new water points drilling / sources for upgradation / construction / rehabilitations; • Conduct Safety audits for WASH facility establishments and rehabilitation.• Conduct NEAT+ environmental screening assessment to identify the potential environmental impacts through NRC WASH projects and implement the appropriated mitigation measures. • Post Distribution monitoring of WASH-NFIs and Hygiene kits distribution Water Provision:• Establishment and operation of water treatment plants for first line emergency response. • Provision of safe water through water tracking for first line emergency response. • Rehabilitation of water haffirs / water collection pond / dam. • Construction of new boreholes through manual / machine drilling – 4 BHs.• Establishment and rehabilitation of solar motorized small-scale water supply distribution systems - 8 WSSs.• Rehabilitation and repair damaged and abandoned boreholes – 50 BHs.• Establishment of safe Water Storage and distribution facility at Health Centres –5 Facilities.• Drill / Construct and rehabilitate water sources at schools.• Form and train Water Users Committees with more than 50% being women (at least one woman in position of responsibility/influence)- • Introduce households water treatment methods. • conduct water quality testing and surveillance for all NRC’s water sources, water points, distribution networks and random household’s level.• conduct basic and refresher training for pump mechanics and caretakers with key attention placed on the engagement of women. Sanitation: • Construction of emergency communal latrines for Sudan crisis effected population including returnees and refugees and displacement affected IDPs and host communities.• Construction of family shared household pit latrines for host, returnees and IDP communities through owner driven process. • Construct and rehabilitate institutional latrines – (schools and Protection centres) Environmental Health: • Conduct safe Solid Waste Management disposal, drainage and debris clean up campaigns- • Form and train solid waste management communities at village / Boma level. • Construction / rehabilitation of drainage system. Hygiene Promotion: • ToT training of Community hygiene promoters (40% male and 60% female) in targeted payams/bomas.• Conduct hygiene promotion sessions / awareness creation at house to house,. Community and institutional levels.• Distribute hygiene kits • Distribute WASH –NFIs kits – • conduct special “Women and Girl’s menstrual hygiene education and promotion” sessions on good menstrual hygiene practices• Form and train School Hygiene Clubs.• Establish handwashing facilities in institutions and common places.• Conduct and celebrate WASH global events in schools / Community (GHD , World water day, etc)• Sanitation and Hygiene promotion campaigns per project site, - • Prepare and implement cholera prevention measures through hygiene promotion campaigns, • Broadcast Covid-19 awareness, prevention and control messages using Jingles in local languages using Mega phones.
