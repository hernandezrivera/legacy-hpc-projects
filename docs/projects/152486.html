Project Summary

The main purpose of the project is to increase agricultural production and build community resilience for the vulnerable food insecure households through a multi sector integrated response to prevent catastrophic consequences like famine which has been caused by the continued insecurity, disrupted markets, animal diseases, low harvests due to pests and worms like fall army worm, natural drought conditions.
These populations that consists of mainly IDPS and host communities have been experiencing a confluence of consistent clashes between warring parties in the country especially the most recent which was between September and October 2018 in yei and Lainya which had spill overs into kajokeji leading to displacements into the korijo idp settlements in liwolo payam in western kajokeji. the recent idps who fled the crises from mukaya,Lainya and some parts of yei and sokare in liwolo payam were forced to settle in korijo which is the newest idp settlement in kajokeji. This report was verified through an IRNA conducted on the 18/10/2018 by Titi foundation teams and partners operating in kajokeji. Given that this areas where titi foundation operates as a static partner in, are also close to the border of Uganda, Kenya and DR.Congo we have experienced an inflow of mainly returnees and some refugees into areas like kajokeji through the moyo and mijale points, magwi and Lafon through lobone from Uganda and yei where the returnees are hailing from Uganda and DR.Congo escaping the harsh conditions in the various refugee camps in search for better living conditions in south sudan. These locations have not just been experiencing the effects of war alone but also in the 2018 september –october IPC released results we have seen that areas like kajokeji have been classified in phase 4 and the other locations between phase 3-4 which depicts emergency situation and depicting the high need for an intervention in various sectors including food security and livelihoods.These areas have also been unique in that politically some are controlled by both IG and IO parties especially locations like kajokeji, Lainya and partially yei in central equatoria which has also to a greater extent escalated the suffering of the populations in this areas.
Its in this regard and background therefore that Titi foundation has identified through information gathered from sources like REACH SURVEYS and the recently just concluded IRNA as of 18/10/2018 that the population in some locations like Korijo in kajokeji is hosting a total population of 40294 newly displaced idps and hence there is a high need for FSL intervention to the vulnerable populations in these locations (according to the October/18/2018 IRNA report).
The other objective is to improve food and storage systems plus the animal health in the pastoral communities. This will be done through direct distribution of quick maturing seeds and tools to the farmers in these locations, training of farmer groups on new agricultural technologies, supporting livelihood projects like the( FES ie fuel efficient stoves and briquettes aimed at mitigating GBV risks) of women,men and adolescent girls and boys. They will also be provided with food assistance especially to the idps, returnees, refugees and host community.
This project is targeting 3000 households who are most vulnerable in host communities, IDPs, returnees and refugees in Kajokeji, Lainya, Yei in Central Equatoria ,Magwi, Lafon and Kapoeta East in Eastern.
Equatoria. During the project implementation, Titi Foundation Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and learning (MEAL) teams will conduct field visits and reports will be developed on the progress of the projects in the selected locations of Kajokeji, Yei, Lainya, Magwi, Lafon and Kapoeta East. This will involve key informants interviews in the various communities and focus group discussions to determine the efficiency and effectiveness of the intervention.
Titi foundation which already has office spaces and implementing ongoing projects like S/NFI, GBV and protection activities in all this locations will mainstream its activities like the ongoing fuel efficient stoves and briquettes program which is aimed at mitigating gender based violence will be mainstreamed to our planned FSL interventions to ensure that the beneficiaries will also be trained along the (FES) project to reduce on the possibility of gender based violence that they could have been exposed to. Titi will also blend ongoing projects like child protection especially to our FSL beneficiaries to assist targeted beneficiary category such as child headed households and people living with disabilities to acquire psychosocial support (pss) by involving our specialized staffs from the various sectors to assist our FSL beneficiaries acquire our extra services.

Needs

Since the signing of Khartoum Peace Accord (12/09/2018) between SPLA – IG and SPLA – IO, armed clashes in Southern parts of Central Equatoria State between (September and October 2018) (Kajo keji, Lainya & Yei) and magwi, kapoeta East and lafon in Eastern Equatoria continues to displace people into new locations within the states. Previous humanitarian systems established and support given to these people in the last two to three years were highly disrupted in this areas raising same priority needs responded to in the past. We have to acknowledge that frequent armed clashes in this areas poses high risk to civilians and humanitarians operating in the area alike given the complexities of the operating environment at hand, yet the high needs of emergency lifesaving requirements like FSL, NFI/ES for hundreds and thousands of IDPs, host communities, refugees and now for the returnees from Uganda cannot be just neglected. The overall picture is that almost 1.9m IDPs in South Sudan are in need of emergency food support which information was obtained from the recently concluded ipc analysis (September-october) indicating areas like kajokeji at phase 4 and the rest between phase 3-4. This number is expected to rise due to the hectic transitional activities of migration, cattle raiding and land disputes causing immeasurable property and food loss to the survivors. This includes spontaneous settlements of around 250,000 people due to the conflict. People are forced to flee their homes with nothing. Most displaced are women, children, elderly men and also people with disabilities who remain vulnerable with exposure to protection risks for-instance Gender Based Violence (GBV) . The host community members are also in need of food and livelihood assistance as they use their own limited resources to provide for the displaced. Although this project focuses on FSL needs of these counties with an estimated population of over 110,083 (HNO, 2018) we have to put in mind that this intervention is not holistic in itself to fully meet the needs of all the new arrivals and displaced civilians in these areas given the complexities of access to all vulnerable people living in isolated areas. This is why this project focuses on the needs of the most vulnerable groups within the IDP , refugees, host and returning communities. Joint IRNA assessments conducted on 18/10/2018) and also based on Titi Foundations knowledge of the areas as a static partner in previous and ongoing projects have identified emergency food security and livelihood assistance as a priority need for the most vulnerable populations who are recently displaced and settled in various IDP camps and spontaneous settlers among host communities. The project aims to provide emergency FSL response to vulnerable people with focus on the newly displaced and those who are being affected by a confluence of crises like returnees and migrants who find themselves fleeing into new settlements. Titi Foundation will target most affected vulnerable households through acquiring seeds and tools(inputs) from core pipeline, which will be distributed to the targeted beneficiaries.Due to diverse needs of various groups, community mobilization and participation with hosts communities and IDPs’ full involvement will be included in the process. Titi foundation will also mainstream its ongoing projects such as the ongoing fuel efficient stoves and briquettes (under our GBV program) which targets at mitigating possible exposure of beneficiaries to gender based violence like rapes and assaults especially for women and girls to benefit the FSL beneficiaries. Also psychosocial support (PSS) services under our protection programs will be offered to our FSL beneficiaries.

Activities

-Conduct Rapid Assessments for Kajokeji, Yei, Lainya, Lafon, Magwi and Kapoeta East counties. - mobilization and sensitization of the community about the project.- identifying, registration and verification of the most vulnerable beneficiaries in consultation with other stakeholders like the community leaders and the local authorities.- Distribution of crop, vegetable and fruit tree seedlings and farm tools to 3000 households for inclusive food productivity in the areas of Central Equatoria (Kajokeji, Lainya and Yei) and in Eastern Equatoria (Magwi, Kapoeta East and Lafon Counties). - 150 self-selected producer groups formed as a platform for transferring improved agricultural technologies and making stronger market linkages. Agricultural training modules include social cohesion, gender, and conflict and nutrition sensitivity.- Recruitment of 20 Field Extension Agents (FEAs) equipped with modern agricultural education and skills. - Training and support 150 agricultural producer groups to evaluate and adopt improved crop technologies in outreach demonstration plots established by the groups. These will serve as Farmers Field Schools (FFSs). Train 150 seed producers on basic seed preparation methods. - Monitoring and Evaluation. This activity will be undertaken throughout the project. The MEAL team will conduct field visits throughout the project to evaluate the progress of the project

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Titi Foundation has several methods of passing information to communities and beneficiaries that are targeted by our interventions before project implementation. These approaches include: • information passed to target groups like local authorities and various groups representatives by conducting focus group discussions during initial rapid assessments • information dissemination through community leaders and sensitization meetings • information through beneficiary identification, registration and verification exercises • information through beneficiary training and workshops relevant to the beneficiaries of the project and through visibility materials like banners and flyers which informs on the scope of the project.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Titi Foundation messages on FSL are context specific and tailored to the design of the project. Sometimes there are generalized messages which seek to inform all highly vulnerable groups of IDP , host community, refugees and returnees. This kind of information will target all the women and men involved in a project. While on the other hand, context-specific information may be prepared for vulnerable beneficiaries like women and people with disabilities. These messages may be delivered by the Titi foundation staff to the targeted beneficiaries.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Titi Foundation is committed to the principles of quality of service delivery and responsibilities of the beneficiaries on our project . This means beneficiaries are fully consulted and informed about the benefits of such projects. They are also allowed room to discuss, speak and analyse what short and long term benefits such a project may offer them this is encouraged to allow the targeted beneficiaries and communities to be able to own the project throughout the project cycle. Therefore, all Titi Foundation humanitarian funded projects are guided by the humanitarian principles of ‘Do No-Harm’ and This allows Titi foundation to use its Human Resource policies guiding staff behavior, PSEA code of conduct which has been signed by all staff to abide by. This therefore will also guide the employees in their daily interaction with beneficiaries in the field.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Titi Foundation projects have broad consultative base which goes beyond the local government leadership. For example, in FSL Project Cycle, before implementation, we have to carry out wide consultations with RRC, Local County, Payam and Boma Authorities (Chiefs, Elders), and Women, Men, Girls’ and Boys’ Groups (18 – 59) and complimentary NGO partners in the area. The FSL project has an in-built feedback or complaint mechanisms put in place. For example, Titi Foundation has help desks and appoints FSL Group Leaders who form the complaint committees from the beneficiaries who link with the staff implementing the project directly to pass forward any complaints or feedbacks. Also we avail beneficiaries with hotlines to communicate complaints and feedbacks to Titi Foundations senior management.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on protection risk analysis which takes into account the different and wider protection needs of the community into consideration. Titi Foundation uses, Protection Cluster Flash Assessment Reports, Joint IRNA protection reports and also we carry out our own access, Safety Audit Reports based on tools prepared by the cluster by conducting interviews, and focused groups discussions with women, authorities, men, boys and girls. All these take into account internal, and external risks that may cause protection risks during the project management.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Kajokeji right after the December, 2013 internal fighting, became a very difficult place to access for most humanitarian actors. In 2016, Titi Foundation on its own made Local Access arrangements with local authorities in SPLA-IG and SPLA-IO controlled areas by involving key figures from Kajokeji, Diocese of Kajokeji, and Uganda District Local Authorities bordering Kajokeji and Uganda revenue authority(URA) to come to a common understanding on the use of uganda routes to take in humanitarian assistance to locations like kajokeji which until now cant be accessed from south sudan. This was based on a catastrophe which was looming over Kajokeji for the civilians trapped there. This method worked for us and until date this has attracted more NGOs to work in the area and till present titi has access into this locations as a result of such arrangements.
