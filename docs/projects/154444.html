Project Summary

The project directly addresses the revitalization of livelihood options for smallholder farmers through rehabilitation and replacement of rural agriculture assets and equipment. This rehabilitation of infrastructure and provision of machinery will provide the means to rebuild livelihoods of the rural families improving access to food and income for their families as well as for rural young/ poor through cash-interventions. Altogether, this would help the families involved in accessing better diets while also increasing their cash income.

Needs

The most serious effect of ISIS occupation in Iraq on the agricultural sector has been the looting and destruction of facilities and infrastructure as well as the collapse of government support in providing inputs. Damage to physical structures such as shops, markets and transport and storage facilities in rural areas in addition to financial difficulties to rebuild damaged property and restore infrastructure is affecting rural livelihoods income and food production. Consequently, farmers are in a desperate need of assistance to recover their livelihoods. Agricultural infrastructure has suffered extensive damage, theft and neglect since 2014. The persistent drought has further worsened agricultural conditions. The result has been the disruption of large sections of the agriculture system with consequent substantial weakening of its components including farmers who rely on irrigation systems. Farmers’ livelihoods and stability are weakened with so many challenges that continue to place more pressure on their lives due to high costs of agricultural inputs, less availability of water for irrigation and disrupted economy. Given the high cost of irrigation, the damage to pumping stations, irrigation structures and equipment and the unreliability of electricity, many farmers have abandoned irrigation and turned to predominantly rain-fed production. Saving farmers’ life through rehabilitation of critical infrastructures to support crop production especially wheat the major staple has become an urgent concern that calls for immediate actions to be taken. The top priority actions are to support farmers to get access to water for irrigation through rehabilitation of small scale irrigation infrastructure and replacement of necessary equipment and storage facilities to support household food production for food and nutrition security. Governments in Iraq are over-stretched, particularly in Nineveh and KRI, which together host more than 57 per cent of Iraqi IDPs as well as more than 240,000 Syrian refugees. Host communities, infrastructure and services are strained and unable to meet the needs of such large numbers of IDPs. As part of the need to restore agricultural livelihoods, this project will seek to rehabilitate key agricultural and irrigation infrastructures through contracting of skilled labor and unskilled labor (where applicable under cash for work schemes e.g. cleaning of irrigation canals, farms, fruit orchards, etc.). Rehabilitation of infrastructure will be complement by supplying necessary on farm equipment in the target districts which include: Pumps and drip irrigation systems Agriculture machinery (tractors, ploughs, planters, harvesters etc.) Beehives, greenhouses and fruit tree nurseries Poultry houses and egg incubators Returnees and remainders will receive direct cash benefit from Cash for work, this intervention provides the ability of men and women to earn income will reduce their household food insecurity and malnutrition and increase their income generating opportunities. The flexibility of unrestricted cash transfers in Iraq's emergency allows beneficiaries to choose what to spend the assistance on based on their individual needs. In the same time, establish agriculture and irrigation assets for the local community. FAO is trying to address this by investing in propositioning of cash transfer and working with the government to rehabilitate essential infrastructure.Sensitivity to gender dynamics will be a central component of CfW while targeting women headed households who have increased drastically over past years. Helping these women gain access to financial resources not only helped them to improve their livelihoods, but also promoted positive attitudes in the community toward women in terms of their capacity and their contribution to economic and social development. The approach would also engage communities, through village committees, and participation of both women and men in all the project cycle to ensure effectiveness.

Activities

Key Outcomes Outcome 1: Small scale agriculture and irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are rehabilitated and/or replacedOutcome 2: Improved household food security and livelihoods status of rural returnee and remainder households Key ActivitiesActivity 1.1: Detailed baseline survey of irrigation infrastructure damage and needs in coordination with MoA and MoWR;Activity 1.2: Identification of priority list for urgent interventions to rehabilitate primary, secondary and necessary infrastructures; Activity 1.3: Technical specifications, procurement and distribution of machinery/equipment;Activity 1.4: Selection of Cash for Work beneficiaries of the most vulnerable households according to a set of criteria;Activity 1.5: Rehabilitation of damaged agricultural and irrigation infrastructure;Activity 1.6: Conduct regular monitoring to evaluate the impact of the interventions, including an end line rapid agriculture and livelihoods assessment.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The Project incorporates important principles such as Right to Food, Decent Work, Accountability to Affected Populations, Do No Harm and Leave no one behind. Strong engagement with beneficiaries as “Rights holders” and government officials as “Duty bearers” help to prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on the affected populations. The Project will use community-based approaches, which includes efforts to engage and empower persons of concern in decisions that affect their lives is a core principle of the 2030 Agenda.
