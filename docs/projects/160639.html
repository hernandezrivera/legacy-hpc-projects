Project Summary

according to HNO 2020, the PIN for SNFI stands at at 2,442,716. This cuts across to IDPs( left all they had-destroyed), Host Community( stretched beyond due to sharing the little they have) and returnees(returning to absolutely nothing but land).This project will ensure that RuCAPD responds to arising needs and continue filling gaps in conjunction with other cluster partners at all points of the project cycle: assessment, verification, registration, distribution, and post¬ distribution monitoring as well as handing over for sustainability. Gender mainstreaming as well age and disability will be key in our intervention. South Sudan has undergone frequent challenges due to conflict and the people remain vulnerable as they continue to face psychological and other vulnerabilities due to the of protracted conflict with most of the population lacking basic shelter and non-food items which are essential for their survival. According to 2020 HNO, climate change has taken a toll on communities whose coping capacities are already weakened by years of protracted conflict. Dry spells and floods have recently racked havoc in Greater Upper Nile, a shortage of water in an area where it has traditionally been available is resulting in the local community, especially women and children, engaging in negative coping mechanisms, such as having to travel longer distances and putting their security and safety at risk in order to access water for the family even with the little SNFI available. RuCAPD is part of the solution working group in Malakal and therefore understands the dynamics of upper Nile so much so Panyikang county. On the same note, the proposed New Fangak Humanitarian hub will be helpful as we coordinate our activities in Panyikang and Canal Pigi. With proper HLP in place, RuCAPD as an SNFI partner contributes to longer term investments by the sectors thus leading to community development. This can be through ensuring that the places where the communities, returnees, IDPs or even host communities choose, the land belongs to the community and there won’t be any problems after they have settled and are running their lives. RuCAPD will include training and sensitisation on HLP in our intervention. This is important so as to ensure certainty of land ownership, security of land tenure as well as keeping in mind the do no harm humanitarian principle. We better not intervene if it is going to cause more problems to the community. On the same note, with HLP in consideration, the right to adequate housing which is actually a human right will be achieved. All our activities are carried out with gender lens on. This is because the challenges hit men, women, boys and girls differently thus calling for gender specific response. On the same note, our intervention will keep into consideration the needs of the elderly, disabled, pregnant and lactating women as well as those with chronic illnesses like HIV and AIDS.
On the same note, we will improve our community feedback mechanism. This is important whether it is positive or negative as it helps as improve our interventions. Where as an organization we know it is not our area, we refer. This is important as it helps close the feedback loop.
RuCAPD will work hand in hand with the cc and our SFPs for upper Nile and Jonglei to ensure smooth running of our intervention. We inform the sfp of any need arising, together we decide for analysis, who then get in touch with LC in case of response. The pipeline request is raised to the cluster. once approved, cc and sfps will notify us. RPDM and M&E are invaluable aspects of the project. Community participation through out the project cycle is our strength e.g through community committees. we work hand i hand with the sfps. all these will be done with AAP, Risk mitigation, conflict sensitivity and gender mainstreamed.

1 - Needs

In line with Cluster policy, RuCAPD will respond based on needs. Due to returns necessitated by the current relative calm we anticipate more and m0re people to return to their homes.- which were totally destroyed during the crisis. The needs will however be confirmed through IRNAs and sector¬ specific assessments in where focus group discussions, key informant interviews, individual household interviews and direct observation are used to assess the extent and types of need the community has. RuCAPD cannot really tell on location but we are confident that a highly significant population will be in SNFI need in 2020. We base our numbers on the cluster guidelines. Some of our areas of intervention like Panyikang county are experiencing an influx of returns with the opening of the Liri boarder point. RUCAPD will conduct desk research using the cluster and IRNA analysis tool the give recommendation on the way forward. All through, gender, protection, risk mitigation and conflict sensitivity will be mainstreamed. if yes, the modality will be selected and submission will be made fro pipeline approval. The community engagement is key in all these as we verify the number, do registration and then the response- distribution. All these is done by our gender balanced staff in collaboration with the community and other partners.

2 - Activities

both in kind and CBI will be undertaken. this is in line with the four cluster objectives. RuCAPD aims at intervening through integrated activities like cash based intervention targeting me, women, and the youth segregated by age and also considering disability. we are also looking at doing activities that are geared toward resilience building as well as long term solutions with the community we serve. These include construction of semi permanent shelters. RuCAPD will engage in integrated approaches as guided y the cluster including AAP, protection, HLP, settlement, humanitarian hubs as well as joint missions and messaging. Being a strong FSL partner as well other sectors like education, RuCAPD will ensure integration with SNFI. All these will e done in conjunction with the community committees ensuring participation of the community. Training of community committees which are segregated by gender and age as well other vulnerabilities like disability.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The importance of inclusion of all gender and age representatives through out the project is key as they each face the different challenges differently and their specific input into the project is invaluable. RuCAPD ensures that we bring SNFI services as close to the community as to boma level. this will cut down on distance and location risk of looting, abduction and rape among others. We also distribute between 9am and 4pm to allow the women and girls to return home on time in daylight.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback, (positive and negative) from the community we serve is invaluable. RuCAPD, has mechanisms in place to help with this. We have community communication agents at all the project sites. Their main function is to collect all feedback from the community and channel it to the concerned personnel for further communication and action as well as receiving feedback on the community concerns and reporting back to the community on the way forward. At the current stage, all the communities in our areas of operation have asked for early pre positioning of inputs in respect with seasonality- we forward this to donor and core pipeline managers. Our good rapport with the community is a plus as we use local language in the field to effectively communicate about services and the support available. Arabic and English are also used but not as much as mother tongue. We also have suggestion boxes at our distribution sites to help people communicate their concerns.
