Project Summary

The IRC developed the project in line with its global vision, the Strategic action plan for Bangladesh 2022-2024, and JRP 2023 objectives. The prioritization of activities and targeted beneficiaries is based on the priorities expressed by the community, protection monitoring data, and client feedback initiatives, insights from IRC field teams and a gap analysis. IRC coordinates with the GoB, PWG on protection mainstreaming, PSTT, ATWG, ADWG, co-chairs the GDHWG, monthly 5W and Sitrep, quarterly PM updates, and participates in camp-level coordination meetings with the CICs, and other actors. The IRC's Protection project will be implemented in the following camps: 1E,1W,6,15,16,21, and 25 where IRC has implemented integrated protection programming since 2017. In close coordination with UNHCR, IRC has recently extended its legal services to additional 5 camps (3,4,6,7, and 9), and plan to expand the legal services (awareness, counselling, and mediation) at camp 25.
IRC will use AGD approach, and the project will focus on systematic protection monitoring to unearth protection risks, incidents, and trends, relevant to protection and assistance to inform prioritization, advocacy, program re-design, and decision-making. IRC staff and volunteers will collect the AGD segregated data and information through KIIs, FGDs, and HHs interviews, DOs, verified, and analyze the data to share with the PWG at Cox's and at field levels, donors, and other stakeholders. IRC will provide IPA support to the identified vulnerable individuals, a process informed by UNHCR profiling and the regular identification of detailed protection risks, including PWD.
The IRC will establish and strengthen inclusive and representative community-based protection structures (CBPs) (50% M and 50% F, including persons with disability) to identify protection issues, handle non-criminal protection cases, and conduct timely referrals to foster access to services for those in need. The project will promote peaceful coexistence within the camps, and between refugees and host community, taking into account the sources of tensions identified within protection monitoring, through meetings and recreational activities; CBPs will conduct regular protection risk analysis, to identify risks, incidents, and trends, which will inform community specific action plans, ensuring referral and CBP mechanisms in place. IRC as a key player in the PWG, will conduct a multi-sector gender sensitive assessment on climate change and associated protection risks, continue with disaster preparedness efforts for monsoon and cyclone responses, and identify the negative coping mechanism; provide legal assistance in camps and host communities and strengthen alternative dispute resolution mechanisms specifically village courts and gender-sensitive mediation at IRC hubs/appropriate community structures in ways consistent with the principles of fairness, equality, and accessible to women and adolescent girls; conduct protection mainstreaming training, support IRC, partners, PWG, and other sectors to mainstream protection through prioritizing safety, dignity, and do no harm, ensuring meaningful access by all to services, promote accountability, and participation & empowerment.
IRC has well-established CFRM at office and field level, is co-chairing the PSEA network and has focal point staff to create awareness on SEA, child safeguarding, and reporting mechanisms with the support of IRC’s Gender Equality Diversity and Inclusion team. The organization will continue to conduct capacity-building initiatives to IRC and partners' staff, volunteers, PWG, Health, FSL, and WASH sectors, and initiative advocacy efforts at the national and local levels including sector, RRRC, and CICs to foster accountability by duty bearers. IRC will establish 2-way communication system to provide life-saving information both in the host community and camps using a tested method to provide and respond to information needs.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Now in its fifth year, the protracted crisis is a protection crisis. There are over 945,953 Rohingya refugees are resided in Cox’sBazar and Bhasan Char where 52% female, 52.61% children, 4% orderly people. The 2021 Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment among Rohingya Refugees in camps found that 12% of individuals have a disability. The stateless Rohingya are currently residing in extremely overcrowded camps, with no freedom of movement outside the camps, restricted access to rights including education, livelihood, and specific protection needs contributes to the cyclical denial of identity, persecution, and displacement. IRC joint protection monitoring shines a light on the on-going threats they face on a daily basis: violence, physical hazards, and exploitation, in particularly affecting women, adolescent girls, older persons, pregnant women, children, persons with disabilities, and other diverse populations. On-going monitoring and analysis of such concerns is required to strengthen the understanding of the complexities of the current crisis and in real-time identify changes in the situation that require an adaptation to the response. The IRC’s July-Sep’22 protection monitoring report revealed that limited access to services (29.65%), safety and security/ incidents of violent nature (25.18%), and HLP violations (24.09%) are most commonly reported protection concerns.Protection Monitoring identified the on-going tensions between host and refugee communities, and among refugees, as a main protection concern. 16% of households assessed during the J-MSNA, reported having witnessed tensions between both communities. According to IRC PM report (Jul-Sep’22), the most common reasons are tensions over access to land 69%. The conflict is attributed to rental issues, landslide, property ownership dispute, conflict over WASH facilities; followed by disputes over shelter. Tensions between host and refugee communities are also related to the illicit drug trade (24%).In accordance to people with disability, one-on-one legal counseling(female-50%:male-48%), legal information awareness sessions (female-54%:male-48%), legal case management (female-25%:male-74%), legal representative for court hearing (female-14%:male-33%), and informal dispute resolution mechanisms like mediation (female-82%:male-17%). The key factors preventing access to legal services are safety and security and lack of financial resources, particularly affecting women. Rohingya refugees cope by using informal community systems, therebyexposes them to a greater risk of exploitation. According to the IRC’s GBVIMS data for November 2021 - May 2022, Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) against Rohingya women and girls is prevalent across the camps, with one in four women identified as survivors of SGBV (10% higher compared to data from 2020) and 94% of the reported SGBV cases are of Intimate partner Violence (IPV). SGBV survivors lack documents registering their marriages and face difficulties taking legal steps. They also face social stigma to report SGBV, and fear reprisals and threats from perpetrators and powerful members of with communities. Child marriage rates in Bangladesh continue to be the highest in South Asia: 51.4% of Bangladeshi women were married before the age of 18, which puts them at risk ofunplanned pregnancies, unsafe abortion and associated complications. Additionally, according to the IRCs Safety audit in 2022, the refugees are at high risk of protection concerns that are heightened following natural disasters such as flooding and fire that camps frequently experience. Women and girls’ vulnerability is exacerbated during emergencies particularly those with disabilities, multiple intersectional identities, such as elderly women and pregnant and lactating mothers.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IRC will follow the sector strategy to cover the gap and reduce duplication. Currently, IRC implements general protection activities in 5 camps (1E, 1W, 15, 16, 21,) and two host community locations. In coordination with UNHCR, IRC has extended its legal services to 5 camps (3, 4, 6, 7, and 9) to cover the identified gap. Based on the protection sector GAP, and IRCs’ country Strategic Action Plan (SAP) for 2022-2024, IRC plans to undertake general protection interventions in 8 camps (1E, 1W, 6, 15, 16, 18, 21, and 25) in 2023-25, for stronger integration with ( CP, GBV) along with other Health, SRH, DRR, and Education sectors . The IRC’s rationalization is driven by five objectives: ensure services are integrated, reduction of existing service gaps, cost efficiency of the intervention and quality of programming. IRC already has a strong presence in the targeted sites, and has been closely coordinating with the government, and area of responsibilities (AORs) and UN, INGO, NNGO, CBOs since 2017. IRC also has a robust resource mobilization plan, capacity, and relation with donors to cover the proposed interventions. IRC has a separate government liaison team to coordinate with the relevant government entities. The IRC Bangladesh, regional and HQ teams work closely together to provide high quality protection services following technical standards and the organization has an integrated protection program (CP, GP, and GBV). IRC is a co-chair of PSEA network. Similarly, IRC has specific focal points for PSEA, Child Safeguarding, and a strong complaints and feedback mechanisms, in addition to general AAP activities at the organization level.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will build on IRCs extensive PM experience in Bangladesh, by designing and implementing all activities while considering protection risks and potential violations and ensuring that the protective impact of aid programming is maximized. The IRC will prioritize the safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment throughout the project management cycle. The activities will enhance rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming that considers age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness throughout the project. The community structures aim to strengthen existing community capacities, increase access and inclusion of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. Regular community consultations, risk assessments men, women, boys, and girls, including PWD, diverse persons to identify and mitigate risks, and ensure safety, dignity and equity.

3.2 - Gender

The patriarchal representation of masculinity is strong in Rohingya and host communities. Men and boys dominate decision making, men are bread winners, and protectors for any emergency. The participation, mobility, and decision-making of women and girls is limited and controlled by men. The deeply entrenched social, and cultural norms disadvantage women and girls. Gender analysis through an intersectional lens and location specific considerations will be used to empower marginalized people to mobilize and claim their rights. IRC will reach the age, gender, and diverse population throughout the project and disaggregate the analysis inline with AGD approach. IRC will follow the gender ratio in staff and volunteers’ recruitment and management representation, train on gender mainstreaming and PSEA, and design and implement a gender-sensitive intervention to transform gender norms and cultural practices , where diverse women and girls can participate as equals in society.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The IRC has a child safeguarding strategy in place to ensure that IRC projects, workers, or others working with or on behalf of the IRC do no harm to children, and to ensure that safety risks and cases of misconduct are identified, reported, and addressed in an appropriate and timely manner. IRC has a separate child safeguarding focal point at the project location, country, region and HQ to handle the cases related to child safeguarding. The policy covers all staff, volunteers, visitors, sub-grantees, suppliers/sub-contractors, and implementing partners who have direct contact with children through IRC-implemented or financially supported projects. All are oriented and signed before engaging with any task. Through the project, IRC will follow and practice the same procedure through the project.

3.4 - Diversity

Age, gender, diversity (AGD) and inclusivity approaches will be mainstreamed throughout the project. People with specific diversity characteristics, including PWD, will be specifically engaged throughout the project. IRC has a strong presence at PWG and ADWG, and strong relations with other specialized organizations. Through the project, timely referral will be conducted, either to the working group, through partnerships and at the camp level. All the tools and standards will be reviewed through a genderlens, and ensure that considerations relating to diversity and inclusion are appropriately included. All staff are trained and on gender, diversity, and PSEA considerations. PM will help to identify the risk for the diverse population, and inform the modality of service delivery tailored to the needs The integrated programming approach will contribute to expediting cross-referrals and will allow for assistance to be tailored to the individual needs of beneficiaries.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

As a Client Responsiveness organizational, IRC committed for meaningful participation of people receiving aid and services more effective, relevant, and appropriate, and our decisions and actions more accountable to clients, communities, and other stakeholders. The IRC’s CFRM is already in place to solicit, process, and respond to feedback and complaint regarding services and workers’ behavior. The trained staff and procedures are in place, to collect the complaint and appropriate feedback, for instance through satisfaction surveys and tailored feedback and response mechanisms. The CBP component of the project allows for meaningful participation on communities in decision making processes. Throughout the project, the IRC will implement the activities in line with the ISCG AAP mechanisms. Community resilience to increase throughout the project. IRC also co-chair of the PSEA Network that also continues to rely to reduce the SEA reporting system for staff and partners.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will focus peaceful co-existence within Rohingyas and between host and camps. IRC will scale up the emergency programming given its unique capacity to implement integrated protection programs. IRC also works with PWG,and PERU to ensure effective representation for EPR. As part of disaster preparedness, the protection monitoring system will collect information on protection concerns and trends following a disaster, to be shared with stakeholders to inform a tailored emergency response. Instead of building structures, IRC to use the existing centres to reduce the environmental hazard, use the online secure system for documentation, all while following the internal rationalization to use for cost effective programs. CBP and community volunteers will be engaged in preparation for an emergency, through community led action plans and EPR programs. IRC also contributes to joint data collecting and response, by collecting5W and monsoon incident data and sharing this with the PWG
