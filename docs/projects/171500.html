Project Summary

American Refugee Committee's (ARC'S) health project will be implemented in close coordination with the MOH/D/RMOs in each region, as well as WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA, other health cluster partners and stakeholders in each area. ARC plans to respond in Banadir, Hirshabelle and Jubaland regions of South- Central Somalia and Sool, Sanag and Togdeer of Somaliland, where ARC established presence and with critical humanitarian need and excess morbidity and mortality due disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks, displacements and conflict.
The intervention will support 14 existing government health centers or MCHs with two clinicians(under five and over 5 years) in each site providing safe and quality primary and reproductive healthcare services to 161,280 individuals as direct beneficiaries in an equitable and dignified manner while reaching to estimated 520,000 individuals as indirect beneficiaries. Access to essential health services remains a priority for the conflict affected vulnerable populations in proposed project target locations largely affected by the COVID-19 outbreak and displacements due to flash flood. ARC will undertake WASH interventions across all facilities like latrines, drinking water, handwash installation to improve patient safety and prevent COVID-19 transmission and poor health outcomes.
ARC health project will provide emergency and basic primary and reproductive health care services that include treatment of common illnesses, maternal and child health care, health education, provision of essential medicines, control of communicable diseases within the community, identification and management of gender based violence, mental health services that includes psychosocial counselling to the distressed individual and referral of severe cases for secondary healthcare.
Protection will be mainstreamed into all project activities, through a comprehensive package of training of Health Facility staff on identification and referral of severe cases of S/GBV. The intervention will reduce morbidity and mortality among the displaced vulnerable population by primarily focusing on ensuring that target facilities provide an integrated package of primary health care services with emergency referrals, disease surveillance and the inclusion of reproductive and mental healthcare services. The project will Improve access to quality and essential lifesaving health services in an equitable, safe and dignified manner to contribute to improvement in physical and mental wellbeing of the displaced and vulnerable population including disabled and sexual assault survivors to reduce excess morbidity and mortality due to disease outbreak, climatic shocks and conflicts.
ARC will closely coordinate with the MOH and respond in locations where significant gaps in health service delivery have been identified. ARC will ensure the participation of the local communities in decision making throughout the life cycle of the project to further strengthening community acceptance and ownership. ARC will invest in local health capacity, strengthening the existing physical structures and human resource, ensuring that staff incentives are in line with MOH scale paving the way for transition to locally and nationally owned and managed facilities. Through the lifetime of the project, ARC will work with the MOH to pave the way for a proper handover, and discuss transfer of responsibility slowly or in phases to D/RMOs where and when possible.

Project Activities

Provision of lifesaving primary and reproductive healthcare services through fixed and mobile outreach services that will undertake essential services in line with Somalia EPHs standards including; Identification, treatment and detection or case management for epidemic prone diseases for crisis affected vulnerable populations aimed at reducing morbidity and mortality by providing emergency health care services including emergency or trauma case, NCD treatment, STD/HIV treatment, mental health for the inaccessible communities targeting number of outpatient consultations per person per year (Target > = 1 new visit/person /year). ARC will coordinate closely with the MOH and UNICEF in providing child health and ensure all children below 5 years have been immunized to ensure a great proportion of children under 1 year received Penta3 and measle vaccines at ARC supported facilities further screening all beneficiaries for COVID-19 and providing specialized reproductive health care for pregnant and lactating women that includes skilled deliveries to increase proportion of skilled birth attendance. ARC will continue upgrading national facilities that need rehabilitation, ARC will support MOH facilities and will facilitate training on EWARN, disseminate weekly EWARN reports, and send alert for notable and outbreak-prone diseases. Communicable diseases outbreaks investigated and responded to in the first 96 hrs. (target for ARC is 80%), procure, distribute trauma kits, emergency health kits, common disease medication, reproductive and diarrheal kits to supported facilities for optimal and continuous supply of critical lifesaving essential medicines and medical supplies-Number of equipped trauma management centers / 250,000 population (target > = 1). ARC will provide Clinical management of rape services (CMR) and GBV / IPV services and referral, psychosocial counselling to the stressed and establish referral pathways for different conditions and ensure immediate movement of emergency and critical patient to secondary level of care. Building the capacity of the health work force, ARC will ensure all the project staff have the necessary skills and will be trained on standard treatment guidelines, iCCM, CMR, PMTCT, BemNOC/CemNOC, IDSR, EPI and Emergency preparedness and response and any other training need identified during the project implementation. Capacity building will be provided for the health facility staff on Infection Prevention and Control (IPC): Provider and patient safety (e.g. PPE, triage, handwashing); Environmental health, monitoring as well maintain Medical logistics and contingency supplies. The teams will provide Health awareness, social mobilization on COVID-19 or other outbreak prone diseases, education and advocacy activities in disease control, health, and nutrition. ARC will ensure improved access to people with disability and enhanced accountability to the beneficiaries and donor through robust and effective monitoring, evaluation and learning component utilizing lessons learnt from current programming.

Mitigation Measures

ARC is committed to ensure that beneficiaries safely access services, devoid of harm, to promote meaningful access, accountability, and participation in the provision of health assistance considering risks and barriers to access faced by persons with disabilities, maximizing the positive impacts of health programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights. Establish proper information sharing protocol for the disabled in a manner they can understand and survivors of abuse to avoid exposing them to further trauma and that all efforts are made to ensure confidentiality and wishes or comfort of the beneficiaries. ARC’s needs assessment tools and frameworks will be adapted to and strengthen understanding of disability inclusion ensuring that persons with disabilities are involved in any key informant interviews and focus group discussion. A diverse sample of persons with disabilities will be part of the focus group discussions including men and women, boys, and girls, of all ages and impairment types, so that a diverse insight into the risks and barriers can be understoodARC will keep beneficiaries and personal data records confidential and safe from unintended use for effective data protection measures. Clearly marked separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females accounting for persons with disabilities and children will be provided. For impartial and equal access of the basic services for all, ARC will ensure proper documentation, collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, disability and diversity to effectively inform programming and ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable. ARC requires all partners and contractors, to sign Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) policy at the time of agreement, and all staff, suppliers and consultants at the time of offer letter, so that the contracts are not valid without a signed code of conduct.To strengthen participation and empowerment, ARC will involve all categories, age or gender group, disabled and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to health needs. For purposes of transparency, ARC will set up accessible, confidential, and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment, and assist in detecting misconduct by creating complaint boxes at health center, phone lines, setting up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselvesARC teams will respond to complaints, immediately documenting lessons learnt, organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. ARC will utilize the real time beneficiary feedback system, Kuja Kuja as a tool that captures the ‘pulse’ of customer satisfaction with services and to allow service teams, technical teams, and support functions both at the local and global levels to understand real time issues being faced, see opportunities for immediate and strategic improvement to service delivery. Feedback mechanisms will be accessible to all persons including the disabled with support from the Kuj Kuja team members who are all times standby to provide necessary guidanceThrough the adoption of Do No Harm approaches, efforts will be made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of conflict situations. ARC believes that understanding the context in which it operates is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and avoids negative consequences. ARC will assess the context and conflict dynamics, especially the role of dividers and connectors to link the conflict analysis with the program cycle helping ARC to re-plan, implement and monitor the intervention in a conflict sensitive fashion

Please provide further explanation

The proposed health intervention will reduce acute humanitarian needs and excess mortality among the most vulnerable by providing life-saving and sustaining integrated assistance, prioritizing assistance to people with acute needs and in life-threatening situations, including IDPs, refugees, returnees and crisis affected host communities, and those suffering from severe food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) to minimize excess morbidity and mortality, especially among children. The proposed response will encompass access to quality basic healthcare services and focus eson an integrated approach, with projects and activities concurrently tackling multiple sectoral issues through complementing ARC protection, WASH, and nutrition interventionALIGHT(Formerly known as ARC) led intervention will be designed in an integrated method for purposes of cost effectiveness, further engaging development, resilient actors, and government for full engagement from the inception, planning, monitoring and execution for swift hand over process. At every level of project implementation, ARC will involve the DMO and RMO to enable the local health authorities have an in-depth understanding of the programming, community engagement will be undertaken to allow adequate participation and ownership of the response. ALIGHT established surveillance system in all project supported health facilities in coordination with the FMOH and RMOs, through setting up DHIS2, ONA platform and EWARN system that is regularly reported. ALIGHT will ensure the medical staff will be provided with capacity building for purposes of program sustainability. ARC has been part of the coordination mechanism sharing the necessary information and promoting integration to avoid duplication and gaps, optimizing the resources available for sustainable recovery. As and when MoH advises, however, raising discussions on the way forward ensuring sustainability of the humanitarian interventions designed in line with Somalia’s sustainable development goals.
