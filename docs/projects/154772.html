Project Summary

In line with the Cash Working Group’s Strategic Objectives, UNHCR aims to meet MPCA’s objectives for 2019 as follows by provide multi-purpose cash assistance to extremely vulnerable households affected by armed conflict and to vulnerable households affected by a shock generating humanitarian needs that cannot be covered by existing national services and facilitate vulnerable households’ access to complementary and critical humanitarian and national services, including non-contributory social protection programmes

Needs

Over 5.8 million Iraqis were displaced following the outbreak of conflict in 2014. Although the Government of Iraq declared the liberation of all Iraqi territory from the control of extremists in December 2017, 2.4 million Iraqis remain internally displaced as of 30 September 2018 according to IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). The majority of more than 4.08 million returnees face severely constrained access to basic services, continuing security risks, extensive destruction of property and critical infrastructure, and a lack of livelihood opportunities or financial resources. For many, this has led to secondary or repeated displacement. UNHCR PDM data as of September 2018, show the most prioritised needs reported by beneficiary households in the 30 days prior to the interview were debt repayment (31%), followed by health care (19%) and household needs (14%). Beneficiary households reported that these needs remained or increased 30 days after receiving cash indicating that the needs are beyond the emergency phase and increase in severity in the months following displacement or return. Immediately after receiving cash assistance beneficiary households reported an improvement in their situation, most frequently beneficiaries report that assistance improved their ability to repay debts (65%), avoid eviction (21%), send children to school (20%) and pay for medical needs (17%).CWG humanitarian needs overview highlights that 1,9 million individuals in Iraq are food insecure coupled with lack of livelihood opportunities and basic services has forced 80 per cent of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable non-displaced to adopt one or more negative copying strategies such as buying food on credit, selling assets and engaging in risky or illicit activities. In the recently concluded vulnerability scoring framework review, some of the key behaviours and household characteristics (HH indicators) that significantly explain lower consumption per capita included: a household resorting to forced marriage as a negative coping strategy is likely to have 30% less per capita consumption, those resorting to child labour - 7% less per capita consumption, and those with medium or high category of the coping strategy index are likely to have 1.5% less per capita consumption Both of HH indicators (forced marriage and child labour) fall under emergency strategies, indicating a higher level of vulnerability. UNHCR PDM findings show household decreased overall use of negative copying strategies by 21% after receiving cash assistance. Hence effective targeting remains a priority to identify extremely vulnerable populations in need of MPCA assistance. UNHCR will continue to provide technical support to CWG task force in completing the review of targeting and vulnerability framework ensuring quality of analysis, outcomes and tools.According to UNHCR vulnerability assessment data, at least 70% of households qualify for multi-month cash assistance which is significantly higher compared to 2017. 21% of UNHCR cash recipients are single female headed households. Provision of MPCA supports female-headed households to meet the basic needs but also to enhance their protection against negative coping mechanisms. Multi-cluster needs assessment (MCNA) data also show 40% increase in the need for Multi-purpose cash assistance, indicating that 60% of persons of concern live below the set amount of Minimum Expenditure Basket (480,000IQD per month). The highest percentage have been recorded in Anbar, Baghdad, Diyala, Erbil, Najaf, Ninewa, Salah Al-Din and Suleymania. HRP has identified 20 priority locations for IDPs out of camps and 15 priority locations for returnees

Activities

UNHCR will closely coordinate with the Cash Working Group to ensure a coordinated response and promote the use of agreed harmonized tools and approaches, . UNHCR has extensive Cash-Based Interventions experience, Iraq wide operational presence, established agreements with key mobile money service providers in Iraq. Through its network of partners (government, local and international) UNHCR has achieved extensive engagement with the community and is able to scale up while maintaining flexibility, accountability and minimizing fiduciary risks. UNHCR will continue to provide MPCA Iraq-wide covering all HRP priority locations, including governorates with acute and moderate humanitarian needs in which UNHCR is the only cash actor including Kerbala, Kirkuk (Daquq district), Wassit, Babylon, Basra, and all governorates in the Kurdistan region of Iraq. Limited funding and a gradual resumption of government services in most conflict affected areas highlight the need and opportunity for bridging towards more inclusive and sustainable social protection by bridging the humanitarian/development divide and strengthening the protection ecosystemTo meet the MPCA objectives, UNHCR will implement the following activities: - Cash Feasibility and Appropriateness assessments, a UNHCR requirement to implement Cash Based Interventions. These include market assessments, security assessments, supply chain and infrastructure- Beneficiary targeting though households visits, using the CWG Targeting and Vulnerability Taskforce’s Socio -Economic Vulnerability model/framework. - Beneficiary data management through (Assistance Information and Services Tracking System) ASSIST - Distribute MPCA to 40,000* households through mobile money providers: UNHCR will provide MPCA to extremely vulnerable households affected by armed conflict and to vulnerable households affected by a shock generating humanitarian needs that cannot be covered by existing national services - Referrals of vulnerable households to access complementary and critical humanitarian and national services, including non-contributory social protection programmes based on cluster-established referral pathways as well as CWG agreed referral pathways - Post Distribution Monitoring will follow every distribution with an aim to monitor the impact of MPCA on household consumption,, copying strategies as well to promote accountability- Inclusion analysis, mapping out opportunities and legal and operational challenges for conflict-affected households to access non-contributory cash and in kind assistance will be carried out by UNHCR to promote learning, impactful research and support UNHCR protection and MPCA activities - Reporting to CWG (4W, Activity info operational updates)* Please note that the project proposal and budget presented herewith do not reflect UNHCR’s comprehensive or prioritized plan for IDPs in Iraq as presented to and approved by UNHCR’s Executive Committee in October 2018. For UNHCR’s comprehensive needs and budgets for Iraq please refer to UNHCR 2019 Global Appeal.

Protection Risk Analysis

Provision of MPCA in areas which are not safely accessible Instances of coerced and forced returns, have been identified through protection monitoring and rapid protection assessment. At times these returns are occurring in areas which are not safely accessible and where implementation of MPCA may be problematic.Limited capacity of existing social protection networks Conflict affected families still face significant challenges in accessing government social protection networks. Lack of the necessary civil documentation for enrolment, limited absorption and assessment capacity of Ministry of Labor and Social Affairs (MoLSA) and complicated enrolment procedures are among the main obstacles impeding affected population to access complementary social protection programs. Abuse of power by financial service providers, partner staff, authoritiesThe increasing reliance on cash as a transfer modality to vulnerable populations potentially exposes them to higher risks of abuse of power by Financial Service Providers’ (FSP) agents, traders, money agents and other private sector stakeholders involved in such programmes. Risks include violation of consumer rights, including fraud and corruption, discrimination and sexual exploitation and abuse. Access and security during the distributionPersons with disabilities often require assistance in getting to registration centers, cash out agents, markets, so UNHCR through its partners will support the PSN to access the market (contract transporters), register/designate an alternate/next of kin or identify community based solution. There is a risk of financial service providers will share beneficiary data and beneficiary identity with third parties, resulting in protection threats.

Mitigation Measures

UNHCR will continue assessing and assisting families who have returned in involuntarily or coerced manner on a case by case basis, measuring the benefits and risks that provision of MPCA could determine for the affected population and maintaining the safety and security of UNHCR and partner staffUNHCR will continue to provide MPCA as a crucial tool to support vulnerable families unable or unwilling to return to meet their basic needs in place of displacement. For those who voluntarily returned and per CWG overview MPCA is de facto still replacing the government return package which was envisaged to support the return process in 2018-2019.UNHCR have received some reports of abuse where mobile agents charge higher fees. UNHCR alongside with UNHCR mobile money service providers has put in place robust Reactive Feedback, Complaint and Response Mechanisms and proactively sanctions instances of abuse of power by disqualifying agents charging higher or arbitrary fees to beneficiaries. UNHCR will continue to promote adherence to FSP and UNHCR code of conduct, development of public awareness programmes as well as customer satisfaction hotlines.There is also a risk of potential insecurity during cash distributions, thus UNHCR will make sure that adequate crowd control measures are in place. There also might be persons that are not familiar with mobile technology, and thus will have troubles accessing the funds. Additional awareness raising and support will be required for these groups To mitigate potential risks of beneficiary data being shared with third parties, UNHCR has conducted a robust FSP selection process, and included data protection clauses in the contract. Respective confidentiality clauses are also included in agreements with UNHCR partners, and data protection agreements are concluded with any third party when sharing of the beneficiary data with other humanitarian actors, etcImplementation of large scale cash distribution programmes can prompt imbalances in the size of market compared to beneficiary numbers (demand exceeding supply), so it is essential to have market assessments and analysis for the large scale cash programmes.Inadequate capacity of financial institutions delivering cash or making payments (ability, reliability, reach and potential for fraud) can result in failure to pay retailers/ POS; retailers will no longer be willing to provide the service. Robust assessment of financial sector on macro and micro level is completed and analysed, when establishing contractual relationship with FSP. ETo avoid duplication of assistance UNHCR will ensure close collaboration with cash actors in Iraq (CWG, DSAs, Bio-metrics, ASSIST)Delays in the distribution, can result in persons of concern not being able to meet needs on time and might resolve to negative coping strategies. UNHCR is in the process of fine-tuning the internal standard operating procedures that articulate responsibilities and timeframes in planning, delivery, monitoring and evaluation of MPCA activities, while the responsibilities and liability of the FSP are set out in the respective contract clauses

How is your project accountable to affected population

Various tools have been put in place to monitor the accountability vis-à-vis population of concern. The UNHCR has a robust processes for its monitoring, evaluation and partner reporting. UNHCR has a dedicated Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation Partner and CBI focal points for each governorate to ensure an overarching cross-programmatic oversight. Outcomes are monitored against indicators on a continuing basis. Third party monitors are used to monitor progress in hard-to-reach areas in addition to direct monitoring by agency’s staff. In addition, Post distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys are conducted with beneficiaries in order to provide information on the effectiveness of the project in relation to beneficiary satisfaction, registration and distribution process. UNHCR regularly reviews the findings of the PDM’s to ensure corrective measures are put in place to address issues identified in the course of post distribution monitoring. Among other, the purpose of the PDM is to ascertain how useful cash assistance is to meet the basic needs and what beneficiaries experience is with the processes, i.e. identification, cash delivery and its utilization. Another accountability framework used is the Feedback and Response Mechanism (FRM) and Communication with Communities (CwC). Beneficiary Communication, Feedback Complaint and Response Mechanism applies for MPCA assistance aiming to inform beneficiaries qualified for the MPCA (multi-purpose cash assistance) on the programme phases and modalities, allow beneficiaries to report issues, difficulties, challenges, or express their opinion about the assistance. Moreover, the mechanism is designed to assist the MPCA staff to find the gaps in the services, fix or correct issues, and/or improve the programme services to meet beneficiary needs. This system acts as a control mechanism to ensure transparency and integrity in services provided to beneficiaries. Feedback and complaints are collected through three channels: field visits – and via telephone - and via Cash Focal Points (CFP). Community Outreach Volunteers (COVs): reach out to vulnerable families on monthly basis. The COVs identify cases with special needs including, women at risk, physical disabilities, drop-outs, psychological problems. UNHCR closely coordinates with IDP Call center to ensure that issues, difficulties, challenges reported by beneficiaries served by UNHCR are addressed. Referrals from IDP call centres are considered for assessment and inclusion in MPCA if they meet CWG eligibility criteria
