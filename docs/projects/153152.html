Project Summary


This project will target an estimation of 8,135 ( 4200F, 3935M) children and young people among the host, IDP and refugee community in Juba, Maban and Yambio.
The intervention will be aligned with the 3 Strategic Objectives for the Education cluster (1) increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education, (2) improve the quality of education (3) and strengthen the response capacity of communities. JRS will contribute to this Objectives through:
(1) Teacher training program, which will provide the country with certified well trained teachers who are part of the education system. With their knowledge and professional skills this teachers will improve the quality of education and also strengthen the system. Taking into account that a large number of teachers who currently work in the South Sudanese educational system do not have a certification or related studies, it is clear that this activity can have a great impact and make a difference.
(2) Affirmative action focused on female students that will help to retain them at school and make sure they continue their studies in the next critical school years when the number of drop outs of female increases dramatically. This will be a way of promoting equity access to education for this vulnerable group.
(3) Provision of Early Childhood Care and Education, will ensure access and quality care and education for pre-primary children, but will also strengthen the education system through training the ECE management comities.
(4) Training on school management; this will include School Management Committees, Parent Teachers Associations and Board of Governors of primary and secondary schools
(5) Construction of permanent and temporary classrooms
(6) Basic training courses in English and computer focused on young people to help them improve their working opportunities
JRS will work closely with the community to ensure the sustainability of its services, with a strong approach of capacity building.

Needs

MABAN: JRS annually conducts a comprehensive needs assessment reaching out to members from both the host and the refugee community. JRS also participates and relies on the AGDM (Age, Gender, Diversity and Mainstreaming) Assessment coordinated by UNHCR in the four refugee camps, and other sectoral assessments (education, livelihoods. etc.).The findings in the needs assessment point that provision of quality education is still a major challenge. The difference between the Sudanese curriculum (in Arabic) and the South Sudanese curriculum (in English) is one of the major challenges. 60 % of the camp population are below 18 years of age and thus at school going age. Professional skills and qualification of Primary teachers, and lack of enough female teachers are some of the major concerns raised by most stakeholders. If these are properly addressed they can raise the standard of education. Limited access to quality pre-primary education and high number of dropouts of female primary students are also big issues among the community. YAMBIO:2018 needs assessment, identified a gap to respond to the internally displaced educational needs such as limited access to scholastic materials, number of teachers to manage the extended class-levels, bad conditions of learning spaces, inadequate teacher training and competency, lack of capacity building for school management such as Board of Governors(BoG)/Parents Teachers Association(PTA) members and high number of female dropouts.2018 needs assessment, identified a gap to respond to the internally displaced educational needs such as limited access to scholastic materials, number of teachers to manage the extended class-levels, bad conditions of learning spaces, teacher training and competency, lack of capacity building for school management such as Board of Governors(BoG)/Parents Teachers Association(PTA) members and high number of female dropouts. JUBA:High number of young people among urban refugees, IDPs and returnees, are unemployed and with very low levels of education. Lack of English and computer skills make it difficult for them to find a job and be economically self-sufficient.

Activities

This project will contribute on the following areas according to some of the planned Education Cluster activities: 1. EQUIP TEACHERS WITH EIE SKILLS TO PROVIDE AN IMPROVED TEACHING AND LEARNING EXPERIENCE FOR EMERGENCY-AFFECTED CHILDREN. The Teacher Training activities which JRS begun implementing in March 2016 will continue with more focus being on improving quality. In 2019, we will continue with the Pre-Service and In-Service Teacher Training models in Maban whereby the former will take 2-years to complete and the In-Service model will take 4 years.Teachers will also receive psychosocial support and trainings on how to support students. 2. AFFIRMATIVE ACTION TO RETAIN AND ENCOURAGE GIRL’S ENROLMENT In Yambio, the project will provide termly 75% percent tuition fees to vulnerable female secondary students 15 years and above. This is aimed at promoting equal access to education for the girls and to reduce the rate of early marriages and pregnancies which are major challenges affecting girls’ education in this region. Additionally, sanitary comfort kits will be provided to primary school girls from p4-p8 and secondary school girls twice a year and soap on a monthly basis. IN Maban, female students will be directly supported to participate in periodic clubs and mentorship activities in the schools. They will also receive psychosocial support and periodic follow up visits by their mentor who will be a female student of JRS teacher training. The main aim is to ensure that the girls’ attendance remains consistent and their scores improve in class. This will eventually ensure retainment of girls in school. 3. PROVIDE YOUNG CHILDREN WITH ECD KITS AND SCHOOL-AGED CHILDREN WITH FORTIFIED SCHOOL MEALS In the direct support to the host and internally displaced communities in Maban, JRS Education will be supporting Early Childhood Development Centres run by the community. The centres will aim towards ensuring that children between the ages of 3 to 7 years are developed holistically in readiness to join basic schooling. The children’s emotional, social and physical development will be professionally handled to guarantee make the most of their future well-being.School feeding will also be provided in this centers on daily basis. 4. ESTABLISH AND REHABILITATE TEMPORARY LEARNING SPACES, CLASSROOMS AND GENDER SEGREGATED LATRINES JRS is planning to construct 2 temporary learning spaces and 1 permanent classroom for ECDs in Maban. As well as 3 permanent classrooms and 3 segregated latrines in primary and secondary schools in Yambio. 5. PROVIDE EMERGENCY-AFFECTED CHILDREN WITH SCHOLASTIC MATERIALS Specific scholastic materials and curriculum textbooks will be provided to JRS supported primary and secondary schools in Yambio to improve the level of teaching and learning. 6. EQUIP SMCS/PTAS AND EDUCATION AUTHORITIES ON SCHOOL MANAGEMENT AND GOVERNANCETraining on school management will be carried out in Yambio and Maban (ECDs); this will include School Management Committees, Parent Teachers Associations and Board of Governors of primary and secondary schools in order to promote and improve the quality of school management. 7. BASIC TRAINING COURSES IN ENGLISH AND COMPUTERAdult Literacy and English Language courses will be rolled out in both Maban and Yambio. Including a female-only English Course.The JRS Computer Program will also be rolled out in both locations to the host, refugee, IDPs and returnees communities with very special attention being given to female participants.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

JRS has established standards for protection mainstreaming into programs based on inter-agency protection standards. Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and child protection are core concerns of JRS that are reflected in its strategies and programming including in JRS partnership agreements. JRS has developed security protocols at the international and regional levels for staff working in conflict and remote sites. Through the principle of subsidiarity, JRS leadership exercises a participative, accountable and transparent decision-making process. JRS has continued the training of staff on PSEA, Child Safeguarding and Protection Policy. These efforts are led by the Human Resources Department (HR) and is integrated into staff recruitment, orientation and project implementation. Reporting mechanisms regarding these issues and concerns, including assigned focal persons, have been identified at all levels—from the project, country, regional to international level.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The main assessment methods included a 12-day participatory needs assessment exercise with the affected population (refugees, IDPs and Host Community). The main participatory assessment techniques used was free listing technique, Key Informant Interviews and Focus Group Discussions (FDG). Sufficient information regarding the current context and representation across all locations were given due attention in selection of designing the methodology. Different categories of POC were targeted in the assessment: women, men, youth, children, leaders, teachers, school committee members..

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

JRS establishes clear inter-departmental mechanisms and structures for receiving and responding to feedback and complaints in its projects. JRS values the engagement of people served in planning and implementing projects and in seeking ways to improve its services. While the HR team leads response to certain types of feedback and complaints that involves JRS staff, the Programs department is responsible for ensuring that the critical engagement of people served is robust in managing and improving the project. Strict confidentiality and privacy for receiving and responding to feedback are maintained for the protection of all people concerned. Through its accompaniment, JRS ensures that refugees are able to express their needs, hopes and concerns and that JRS is able to address them in an appropriate manner based on institutional mechanisms as well as respect for socio-cultural contexts. JRS has established and implemented feedback and complaints mechanisms both for one-time and ongoing services in the project. Weekly meetings with incentive refugee staff is the main fora for refugees to be involved in the program implementation and design of corrections needed. Regular feedback from participants and beneficiaries will be used to adjust and improve trainings and services provided.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

JRS promotes the active participation of the people in the management cycle of the project including monitorung and evaluation. It includes qualified refugees, IDPs and Host Community memebers as part of its personnel to provide valuable presence and input to the work and to work closely with refugee communities and organizations in developing and implementing programs. JRS encourages co-responsibility, discernment and participative decision-making. Women has a strong contribution to JRS programs especially in psychosocial related activities. Participation of refugees, IDPs and host community starts with their involvement in the assessment. Also there are some support groups composed of community members to aid project design and implementation. This project incorporates the service of incentive workers who carry out most of the activities in their respective communities. All of them are trained to ensure that they acquire the skills and competencies needed to make an impact in their communities.
