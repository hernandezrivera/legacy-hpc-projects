Project Summary

This project will support its beneficiaries with child protection and education services, through this project Girls and boys will benefit from timely and appropriate humanitarian protection and assistance and efforts to reduce long term vulnerability, to achieve this objective the student will enrolled in education centres in project locations and these schools will provided with WASH infrastructure for safe return of students and teachers and general infrastructure rehabilitation, student and teachers will receive teaching and learning kits, parents and student caregivers will engaged in this project through participation in parent -teachers associations and teachers will meet on monthly basis to exchange experience and find mitigations for challenging issues, in addition all children who are in critical risks will reached out by case management services, children participating in this project will receive recreational activities in the established CFSs,and will participating in HEART , youth resilience program and child youth clubs, caregivers will receiving Safe Families program and mother toddler sessions, and will receiving mother toddler and PSS kits during program implementation.
Key program activities: case management, child-friendly spaces, safe families programming, temporary learning spaces, teacher learning circles, teacher capacity building, support for child protection committees
Needs: Throughout 2023, in NES, the humanitarian situation continued to deteriorate driven by a deepening economic crisis, losses in human capital, destroyed infrastructure and degraded basic services along with shrinking international fund and a complicated regional crisis. Food and basic supplies and fuel prices have risen, leading to a substantial decline in the population’s purchasing power, a crucial factor to the humanitarian needs in the country. In October and December 2023, the Government of Türkiye escalated hostilities in NES. These included shelling, artillery, and drone strikes, causing civilian casualties' large-scale destruction of civilian infrastructure, causing power cuts and lack of water and fuel in parts of NES. This disruption further affected economic activity reliant on electricity and internet access. A range of interrelated drivers has sharply reduced economic activity in NES. In Al Hol Camp, where the project has been implemented, the situation remains dire amid characterized by poor living conditions, inadequate services, and violence. Children are at highest risk in this context.
Of 4.5 million living in NWS, 2 million people are currently displaced with 800,000 living in tents. The devastating earthquakes that struck northern Syria and Türkiye in February 2023 exacerbated an already dire situation, uprooting hundreds of thousands of families and leaving millions more vulnerable. In north-west Syria, out of the 278,200 individuals living in camps, 57% are female and 54% are children. Sadly, children in IDP sites face significant barriers to accessing basic education rights, with 57% lacking access to primary schools and 80% to secondary schools. The economic crisis and deficient services force many to resort to negative coping mechanisms, including child labour, particularly prevalent in in-camp populations. Living conditions in camps and similar settings are unsafe and undignified, heightening risks of gender-based violence (GBV). A staggering 73% of IDPs in camps report areas where women and girls feel unsafe, while privacy inadequacies affect 65% of shelters, rising to 86% in informal settlements. Formal, in-person learning remains the dominant education format, with only 2% of children accessing non-formal education. However, schools struggle with limited absorption and retention capacities, insufficient resources, and poor infrastructure, hindering effective learning environments.

Protection Risk AnalysisRisk 1: Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. child rights messaging perceived as a threat). Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: Train key staff as resources to act as trainers/facilitators; Regular communication with communities. Risk 2: Risk of increasing violence in the home and community for girls and boys receiving specialized services. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: Includ self-care strategies in programs and HR policies; training on ‘do no harm’; ensure support of relevant authorities to provision of specialized services. Risk 3: Inappropriate response to individual cases in line with case management guidelines which can lead to further harm to the child, due to lack of trained staff working with children. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policies, supported by refresher trainings. Risk 4: Risk to the wellbeing of child protection staff due to exposure to violence (e.g. secondary or vicarious trauma). Likelihood: high, Impact: high. Mitigation: Include Self-care strategies in programs and HR policies; establish peer support mechanisms. Risk 5: Risk to physical safety of staff if perceived to interfere in family-based concerns. Likelihood: high, Impact: High. Mitigation: Continue involvement and coordination with relevant authorities and stakeholders in place; Seek informed consent of beneficiaries.
