Project Summary

The conflict in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon continues to pose multitudes of physical, social, and psychological threats to children's protection and wellbeing. This project will provide life-saving immediate support to the extremely vulnerable and conflict-affected children and their caregivers in the South West Region of Cameroon who are exposed to abuse, exploitation, neglect, and violence to support them heal, recover and feel safe again when they do experience harm. This will be an integrated response in Meme, Ndian and Kupe Muanenguba Divisions of the South West Region. Comprehensive case management services will be provided to children with protection concerns, family tracing and reunification (FTR) services, and alternative care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and cash assistance to meet the basic need of children. The mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) service will be provided for children and caregivers with psychological distress. The project further proposes to protect children from all forms of violence (including abuse, neglect, and GBV), raise awareness at the household and community level, strengthen preventive and response measures (including parenting and caregiver training, teacher training and school capacity building, provision of adolescent life skills courses, and functioning and effective community based measures such as community mobilizers, help desks, and reporting and referral mechanisms), and establish and expand child friendly spaces (specifically to increase enrollment and participation amongst girls and boys, living with disabilities, and to incorporate the involvement of community members to expand the protection of all children.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Six years of continuous fighting between state security forces and non-state armed groups in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon have brought untold suffering to the people of these two regions. Children bear the brunt of the resulting humanitarian crisis. Family separation continues to be the main child protection concern, as many children have been separated from their families because of death of parents, voluntary separation to attend school, insecurity, destruction of homes, poverty, etc. Children, especially boys, are reportedly being recruited and used by armed groups. This in turn exposes other children to the danger of arbitrary arrest on suspicion of being members or potential members of armed groups. With most schools closed or under attack due to the crisis, many children lack access to education and are instead engaged in work for food and to supplement their families’ incomes. Those who have managed to secure places in the few schools that remain operational, work on the streets to pay their school fees. As this conflict continues, many cases of sexual violence, harassment, and child marriage accompanied by unwanted early pregnancies, are being reported. Reports by field staff confirm that girls and young women are resorting to transactional survival sex. The lack or loss of birth certificates continues to result in other child protection risks as it hinders young boys’ and girls’ access to basic services, when available, and exposes them to further risks of exploitation and of arbitrary arrest and detention. Additionally, the crisis has led to the breaking of existing social bonds and community structures that used to protect children. The violence continues to negatively impact children’s psychosocial wellbeing and mental health. Witnessing killings, destruction of homes, and loss of family and friends are events that are beyond processing by the young minds. Existing gaps in protection prevention and response services need to be addressed to prevent any further deterioration and embark on the path to recovery. The proposed interventions are aimed at facilitating safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services by the affected populations. They call for immediate efforts to respond to the situation, including psychosocial support services, case management services and the strengthening of community-based structures to prevent and respond to child protection risks. The project will ensure quality programming, inter-cluster synergies, Accountability to Affected Populations, regular context analysis and advocacy on environmental conservation, ensuring conflict-sensitive programming and adhere to the principle of Do No Harm. The following threats have been identified to be prioritized: child exploitation and abuse, family separation, physical violence, forced marriages, early pregnancies, child Labour, birth registration in the context of forced displacement, arbitrary arrests of older children and youths, children associated with armed groups and sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian actors. The project is to target 40,000 direct and indirect beneficiaries (15,000 girls, 15,000 boys, 5,000 women, and 5,000 women) in Meme, Ndian and parts of Kupe Muanenguba Divisions of South West Region. In carrying out these interventions, Caritas Kumba will collaborate with other humanitarian actors as well as local authorities and actively participate in CP AoR coordination meetings, assessments, and the submission of 5Ws. All these with aim of building and maintaining an environment in which child protection services within IDP and hosts communities help to foster adaptation, recovery, growth and promote dialogue, survival, and protection.
