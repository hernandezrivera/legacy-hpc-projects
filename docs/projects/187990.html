Project Summary

The project intends to expand its provision of Inclusive Non-Formal Education to populations across NWS.
IAC has access to targeted sites through its team on ground due to the implementation of a FSL activities in same targeted camps, IAC team in Turkey has good experience in the education sector through the implementation of many educational activities within Turkey over the past three years, and is currently planning to carry out educational activities targeting displaced people inside Syria.
IAC will provide Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for IDP children who have been displaced to Dana (severity 4) and Maaret Tamsrin (severity 5) according severity scale of HNO 2022-2023, the targeted children are 2,734 (1,504g/1,230b-3% Children with Disability (CwD) aged between (8-16) (grades 1-9). IAC will establish 15 TECs (accessible to CwD) – 6 classrooms and an admin room in each – in 15 camps in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts. A preparation period of 1 - 2 months to establish the TECs, hire the staff and lightly train them. Trained staff and members of EAs in Harim will apply ASER to decide the levels of students. Two cycles of ALP to be conducted (one-month set up followed by 5 sessions /day, 5 days/week to cover 1 cycle in 5 months.). ALP is the best fit for children who have been out of school for 2 years or more, and are older than their classmates who are in formal education. One academic semester is accelerated and covers two semesters, and final goal is to transition of students to formal education, as defined by NFE Task Force of Education Cluster.
146 teachers and education personnel 45%m, 55%f, 60%IDPs, 40%HC) will be provided with incentives and trained on ASER, TICC and ALP. All of them will get orientation about COVID-19 prevention, CP, child safeguarding policies, and PSEA, and let them sign IAC`s Code of Conduct.
2 blocks of 2 gender-sensitive and disability-friendly toilets will be constructed in each TEC.
Classrooms in TECs will be lightly decorated to make TECs safer and more welcoming Adapted Curriculum B will be printed and distributed
TECs will be provided with school supplies, teaching & learning materials and furniture
Safety and security plans will be established and drilled in TECs which will be provided with safety equipment
TECs will be supported with hygiene supplies to support prevention of COVID-19 infection.
TECs will be provided with operational running costs
15 Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) will be established
Project will target IDP students and their parents through mobile outreach teams covering 17 communities surrounding the TECs in followıng activities:
- Conduct BTL campaigns
- Run recreational, psychosocial and resilience activities in TECs
- Awareness raising activities for children and parents focusing on Education related issues, Child Labour, Family tracing and reunification, Basic CP, and Child Rights
- Preparedness training such as fire drills - self-care - first aid
- Providing psychosocial support and referral mechanisms based on needs
- Conducting awareness campaigns about COVID-19 prevention.
The project will be implemented during 2022-2023, the project will be prepared by equipping TECs and recruiting and training the educational staff within one or two months and then starting education activities for two years.
The project will continue in 2023 by implementing two new cycles of ALP; providing teachers and education personnel with incentives; providing TECs with Curriculum and educational supplies, hygiene supplies and operational running costs; conducting recreational, psychosocial and resilience activities, providing psychological support and referral mechanisms, and conducting awareness campaigns about the necessary topics.
The beneficiaries of project are the students (2734), teachers and education personnel (146), Parents in 15 PTAs (1000), children and parents benefiting of BTL campaigns and various awareness raising campaigns (7568)

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, there are over 7 M children 3-17 years in need of education support, the rate of attendance for pre-tertiary education (formal and non-formal learning) for 2020-2021 academic year was 82%.As in past years’ girls and boys tended to attend at the same rates with both having a sharp dropout rate in adolescents with boys dropping out at a higher rate than girls. With a self-reported non-attendance rate of 36% IDP children living in camps were much less likely to attend than resident children (15% non-attendance), COVID-19 has led to increase the number of out-of-school children.IAC has conducted a baseline assessment in 17 camps in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin, hosting 19,172 IDPs(4960m,5123w,4517b,4572g).There are 2,817 (1540 g, 1277 b) school-aged children, 77 (30g, 47b)of them are with disability.2,511(1390g, 1121b) grades 1-6, 223 (114g, 109b) grades 7-9, 83 (36g, 47b) grades 10-12). There are 92 (50f, 42m) teachers as well.16 of 17 assessed camps have no schools, a minority of students are attending a school which is far away and not supported by any NGO. The camps managers confirmed presence of spaces to establish TECs and WASH facilities. also the sensitization campaigns and recreation activities are not available. There is various anxiety levels among some families due to presence of some cases resulted of absence of education mainly girls and low level of awareness of parents-cases like early marriage, child labor and ignorance among some families.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

To prevent any PSEA issue from occurring during the implementation period of the project activities, especially the activities in which the project staff are in direct contact with the beneficiaries. All field activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors for PSEA with the use of the PSEA Network Field and Organizational Mitigation and Prevention Checklists and the following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken: 1- IAC will make sure that All staff, contractor, and volunteers understand and sign the Code of Conduct.2- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive PSEA Training.3- IAC will review internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff know about reporting mechanisms.4- Activating complaints mechanisms to protect PSEA in particular, placing them in easily accessible places, designing awareness-raising banners, placing boxes for complaints and suggestions, and activating the hotline number (WhatsApp).5 - provide awareness-raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA and methods for reporting them and gaining the trust of society and spreading humanitarian principles and stick to them.6- Contractors and their workers will not have direct contact with beneficiaries


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption A:- Parents support children's enrollment.- Out of school children show interest to back to school.Risk1: Children aged 8-16 are not interested in program because they are affected with child labor, early marriage and other exploitation form.Mitigation1:-Outreach mobile team will conduct awareness sessions about the value of education.-Back to learning campaign will be launched to explain project to communities.Assumption B: Qualified teachers (females/males) will be hired to launch the education process.Risk2: Lack of qualified teachers (females/males) for non-formal education.Mitigation2: Training teachers (females/males) on non-formal education modality and curricula.Risk 3: Lack of qualified trainers.Mitigation 3: Providing training online and sending trainers from Turkey to Syria.Assumption C: School supplies are delivered to bene¦ciaries (girls/boys) on timely manner.Risk4: Closing the border-gates between Syria and Turkey hindered the education supplies to be delivered on timely manner.Mitigation4: Procure the supplies and print textbooks and brochures in Syria.Assumption D: Project will not cause increasing of COVID-19 cases.Risk 5: Increased number of cases infected with COVID in NW Mitigation 5: Providing sterilization (sterilization + masks), instructions brochures, and awareness sessions.
