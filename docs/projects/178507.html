Project Summary

The proposed project will provide assistance to conflict-affected populations in NWS by increasing the availability of protection mechanisms and improve the overall protective environment and resilience of the most vulnerable internally displaced people and host community members in Idleb (Idleb, Kafer Yahmoul, Kafr Takharim, Deir Hassan, Dana, Salqin and Atmeh) and Aleppo (Jandairis, Akhtarin, and AlRayan). The NGO will provide protection services, including gender-based violence and child protection case management to those who are survivors or at risk of gender-based violence and abuse – especially women and children. The program will target at least 34,880 beneficiaries (7714B, 5642M, 8428W, 13096G)
Case management services will be tailored to beneficiaries’ individual needs, taking into consideration family and caregivers. Psychosocial support will help beneficiaries to improve their coping mechanisms, manage stress, and find solutions to their challenges. Group work will provide space for beneficiaries with similar issues, problems or circumstances to come together for mutual support. Outreach services will spread information on positive mental health and protection risks, as well as assist in reaching the most vulnerable in communities and making appropriate referrals to services. Training will target both community members and front-line service providers to increase their capacity to help and support others. Awareness-raising activities aim to decrease stigma around accessing services by encouraging people to be more aware of protection risks faced.
Protection services will be accessible by all, psychosocial support, community outreach, group work, awareness-raising activities, training for service providers and community members on mental health and protection topics, and integrated mental health services for the most vulnerable people, including people with disabilities, people facing mental conditions, psychological distress and trauma, also will target children and women assessed to be at high risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect or violence, children found to be associated with armed forces and armed groups, unaccompanied or separated children, persons with mental disorders or psychological distress, the elderly, chronically ill and people with disabilities.

4 - Needs

NGO’s 2021 MSNA shows that protection services which include integrated (CP), GBV and PSS remain a major need across NWS, and there is a need to expand them. GBV concerns, continue to increase. Based on data collected through the NGO’s current GBV implementation, psychological abuse is the most prevalent type of GBV case (at 40%), followed by physical assault (28 %); denial of resources/opportunities and services (18%); sexual abuse including rape (8%), early and forced marriage (7%). Per the MSNA 2021, 66% of household (HH) survey respondents indicated there are no services available for women in the camp or community. Economic deprivation, lack of livelihood opportunities, discrimination, exclusion, and socio-cultural norms (HNO, 2021) have led to the continued normalization of GBV and severely impacted the psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights, including those with disabilities. Based on the findings of CP HNO 2021, children comprise almost half of the affected population. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused. The NGO’s 2021 MSNA, 92.2% (319) of the HH respondents mentioned different dangers facing children where the top three dangers facing children cited by respondents were kidnapping 56.4% (195), dropping out of school 55.8% (193), and neglect 35.8% (124). MHPSSTWG estimates that every two hours one person tries to or commits suicide in NWS.The NGO 2021 MSNA shows that there is still a need to provide MHPSS services targeting the most at-risk and vulnerable individuals.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Exploitation, sexual exploitation and abuse is seen as a possible risk when providing individual services such as case management and psychosocial support for vulnerable people, especially women, children and persons with disabilities and mental disorders. Low – moderate, based upon prior experience, high stress levels and difficult life conditions for both staff and beneficiariesThe organization maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and follows internationally established protocols and standards for child safeguarding. The organizations staff and partner staff are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training in child safeguarding and SEA protection with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA of beneficiaries, staff and children. The organization has an established community based feedback and response mechanism to including a PSEA hotline to report suspected cases. All community centres and facilities have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback including reporting child protection concerns anonymously. The organization considers maintaining a gender balance in its outreach teams and at static centre, in order to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable with the services provided.

Close security monitoring. Security training for staff which to limit/lessen exposure. Support to evacuation/withdrawal of local staff to alternative locations to continue managing program or revise targeting. The NGO will consider supporting affected staff through Duty of Care, including financial compensation.• The NGO uses joint efforts of procurement and legal staff to clarify details of hosting countries regulations. The NGO applies for appropriate certifications, licenses and permits to import and/or use bonded transportation and warehouses for humanitarian assistanceThe NGO will explore options to continue support through local partner NGOs registered in Turkey in the case of GoT takeover to maintain support.
