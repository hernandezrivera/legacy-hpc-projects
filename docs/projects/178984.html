Project Summary

It is reported that the situation of war-born injuries and resulting impairment and disability are one of the most prioritized areas of intervention for humanitarian response in Syria, as more than 3 million people are living with Disabilities (PWDs) . The transition from injury to impairment is happening at an increased pace under a less-favourable medical care environment due to the declining of health services coverage where 30 per cent of public hospitals were not functioning , and the transition to disability was inevitable within the exclusive and disabling environment. In fact, 3.7 million or 27 per cent of the total population (aged 12+) in Syria are disabled. Previous reports estimated that roughly 45 percent of the injured population, due to the crisis, develop permanent disabilities of whom 15% undergo amputation.
The provision of lower limb prosthetic is a medical intervention that, when delivered properly, carries a high rate of successful impact that transform the life of the benefiting person from dependent and excluded to independent and empowered person that can participate again fully and meaningfully in socioeconomic life.
Under its program to support the resilience of PWDs towards socio-economic inclusion, UNDP has been contributing to filling the gaps of lack of services specially in the field of prosthesis. Building on its experience and in-house technical capacities, and to expand the support to underserved areas.
The project will focus on delivering lower limb prosthetic devices to persons with disabilities (amputation cases) with focus on women and youth being part of the most vulnerable groups. The project will benefit from the UNDP ‘s established prosthetic centers to deliver the services in 5 governorates (Damascus, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Homs, Tartous, Qamishli) with possible extension to other governorates such as Dara’a, Hama, Latakia, and Deir-Ez-Zor.

4 - Needs

While many components of the Syrian society suffered from the current crises, persons with disabilities and their families are among the most affected and marginalized, of which situation leads to their exclusion from many humanitarian and socioeconomic support. Despite the various efforts of many actors (governmental, UN and NGOs) to respond to the needs of persons with disabilities, the demands are still tremendous compared to the available human or financial resources, resulting in being not fully covered. Moreover, the cases of persons with severe disabilities present one of the greatest challenges as they suffer from higher mortality rate and deeply depressed socioeconomic situation. There are no exact data reporting the exact number of persons with disabilities in Syria. It is estimated that 27% of the population now in Syria live with a level with disabilities of which 30% is linked to permanent disabilities In general, there is a lack and limitation on the acquisition of precise numerical data in all areas related to disability. This is also the case for amputation and prosthetic services. One of the earliest reports in 2014 stated the presence of more than 80,000 traumatic amputation cases in needs of prosthetic fitting in Syria. Other studies estimated that 15% of all reported injuries are amputation. Other anecdotal data mentioned that in previously besieged areas and currently newly accessible areas such as (East Ghouta, Deir-ez-Zor and East Aleppo), the rate of amputation is one out of eight people. Despite the efforts of many parties in delivering prosthetic services, the nationally available capacities and services being public, civil or even private sector, are still limited on covering needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Disability Inclusion Programme manager will provide technical support and oversight on the quality implementation and will lead the implementation of the project. The team will consist of programme management and technical teams that develop a detailed plan based on implementation strategies. The project is divided into:1. Survey & evaluation: collect preliminary data on amputation cases in the region, which will result in a list of beneficiaries--according to the project's targeting criteria2. Physical rehabilitation & PSS services: which includes the following:o Design individual comprehensive physical rehabilitation and PSS plan for selected beneficiaries.o Provision of physical rehabilitation and PSS to beneficiaries (200 PWDs)3. Evaluation and follow-up of beneficiaries after service delivery: evaluation of the level of service provided and the effectiveness of the project.The project will include regular monitoring and follow-up for the outcome of the physical rehabilitation process and provide the necessary maintenance in case of any malfunction of the prosthesis resulting from poor manufacture for 6 months from providing the prosthesis. UNDP will carry out monitoring visits to follow-up on cases at project site and/or in their place of residence as part of the evaluation mechanism.To further ensure the participation of vulnerable groups, UNDP will follow-up with beneficiaries who did not benefit from activities to ensure that selection criteria are implemented correctly. The project team will provide regular, impartial monitoring through direct field monitoring visits, remote telephone monitoring, and indicator data tracking mechanisms to provide a regular assessment for course corrective actions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

the protection and safety of beneficiaries will be promoted through applying different protection elements, i.e. individual data collection will be done discretely for each beneficiary, a dedicated team will work on analyzing the data which might help in detecting any GBV cases and\or discrimination acts. The data collected will be handled carefully to avoid any exposure or danger. The data will be accessed only by the project management team. The project will have female and male members to handle sensitive cases especially for women & children. awareness sessions will be provided for all beneficiaries to raise their knowledge about PWD rights and the privileges the Syrian government provides. Adding to tailored PSS activities for PWDs with referring paths for persons in need of more in-depth consultations. UNDP has PSEA/SH policies, guidelines and procedures for “prohibited conduct” that hold UNDP staff accountable for any misconduct.UNDP has a hotline for reporting PSEA and any false doing and abuse of power. The hotline number is visible to all IP staff and beneficiaries inside the prosthetic workshops. Finally, UNDP will activate complaint policy and ensure that the beneficiaries could raise their complaint and get feedback indicating that their complaint has been taken seriously, there are different channels to collect feedback and complaints:1. PSS staff: We have noticed PWDs tend to be more open with PSS personnel while conducting individual sessions. PSS personnel report any positive or negative feedback to administrative staff and UNDP monitors.2 UNDP staff: PWDs provide feedback during their meetings with UNDP staff. 3.UNDP hotline

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: - Risks:- Overlapping with other similar interventions due to lack of national coordination mechanism to track services and/or people deliberately seeking multiple support - The drastic drop in the exchange rate for the Syrian pound that affects the volume of services provided, hence the number of beneficiaries. - The resistance from beneficiaries to reach for the provided services due to personal and/or security reasons- Deterioration of the security situation in the targeted areas,- Delay in the provision of the services, Mitigation - Establish early coordination through physical rehabilitation working group and other stakeholders and service providers in order to avoid overlapping, and conduct a thorough assessment of targeted group to identify people who have been already served.- Communicate with donors at the early stages in case there is a necessity for any changes- identify the cases of sensitivity and develop customized solutions and support to access the service while ensuring safety and no-harm-The project will coordinate with national authorities and the local community to facilitate the operation and report early in case of any challenges to take the necessary measures, which could be further reliance on local community support. - The security situation will be analysed and followed-up closely and reported duly to make any necessary changes.
