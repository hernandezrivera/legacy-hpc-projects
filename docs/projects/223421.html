Project Summary

Beneficiary Identification, Selection, Verification, and Registration
The IRC will use a mixed approach combining referrals and community-based targeting (CBT). Beneficiaries will be referred by existing partners in the nutrition, protection, and health sectors for families requiring food assistance to receive multipurpose cash support. IRC will establish or rely on existing referral pathways in the project locations for cash assistance referrals. Community-based targeting will be guided by Affected Population Committees (AAP), either newly established or strengthened from existing structures. The IRC will use vulnerability criteria including; at-risk pregnant and breastfeeding women, caregivers, or families with children with severe acute malnutrition, households with persons with a permanent disability; elderly (+60 years) who are chronically sick, survivors of SGBV, and unaccompanied and separated children. Overall IRC planned to target 3,397 HHs (20,383 People)
Provision of MPCA to vulnerable households
The IRC proposes multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to enhance the purchasing power of the most vulnerable households to address the multiple needs relating to food insecurity, malnutrition, protection, and other critical gaps. The cash assistance will be delivered during the critical floods and lean season period (April – June 2025) when households are facing severe food gaps classified under IPC 4 and 5 in Aweil East, Aweil South counties of Northern Bahr el Ghazal State Rubkona and Panyijiar Counties of Unity State, South Sudan. The IRC proposed three rounds of cash transfers will be provided aiming to cover 70% of the SMEB for target households. The JMMI -SMEB for November 2024 was used to determine the amount of cash assistance to be delivered. The 70% MEB is considered with a 30% inflation buffer to ensure households meet their basic needs and maintain purchasing power. Each household expected to receive in Aweil East ($133), Aweil South ($107), Rubkona ($175), and Panyijiar ($157), note JMMI data for Leer was used as JMMI data for Panyijair was not collected in November. On cash delivery mechanism, The IRC already has a pre-existing contract with FSPs, including Alpha Commercial Bank, Juba Express Money Transfer, and MTN (MoMO) with cash to be delivered cash in hand and mobile transfer. Overall, IRC will deliver cash to 3,397 HHs (20,383 People) to meet their basic needs, such as food and other basic needs, and boost local markets.
Gender and protection
The response will disseminate key messages on the importance of CVA in food security, GBV, and PSEA prevention, as well as assess protection concerns related to cash assistance utilization. The FSL team will direct households with identified protection risks to the IRC's Women Protection and Empowerment (WPE) team for case management. The IRC’s Safer Cash Toolkit integrated into the PDMs will guide to capture minimum amount of protection-related information in an emergency setting to make informed decisions on how to prevent and minimize harm.
Monitoring and evaluation, compliants and feedback mechanism
IRC's MEAL Team will conduct relevant assessments following the cash distribution, including PDM, baseline and endline. The PDM will integrate relevant questions from the IRC’s Safer Cash Toolkit to identify and develop mitigation strategies for key protection-related risks. The IRC will recruit experienced field enumerators to conduct PDM exercises two weeks after every distribution to determine cash utilization levels, beneficiaries' satisfaction, and the results of the support towards meeting households' basic needs.
A baseline and endline exercise will be conducted to measure the progress of the key indicators. These assessments will be conducted by the IRC MEAL team. The evaluation will look at whether the support provided was appropriate, timely, and accessible for all beneficiaries and whether the intended improvements in the economic wellbeing

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face multiple shocks and stressors, including insecurity, intra-communal violence, and conflict, low agricultural productivity, poor macroeconomic conditions compounded by recurrent climatic shocks (flooding and prolonged dry spells leading to low agricultural productivity), and the ongoing conflict in Sudan. The continued influx of returnees and refugees has driven high humanitarian needs, putting an additional burden on host communities who are sharing scarce resources with new arrivals. According to UNHCR/IOM Sudan Crisis Displacement tracker, As of November 27, 2024, South Sudan received 878,719 returnees and refugees from Sudan, of which 57,644 people are in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and 29,338 people in Unity states. Food insecurity is likely to worsen in 2025 in South Sudan. According to the Integrated Food Security Classification Phase Classification (IPC, 2025), food and nutrition insecurity in South Sudan is expected to worsen at the peak of the lean season (May – July, 2024). An estimated 218,000 people will experience emergency or Catastrophe food insecurity (IPC 4) in Aweil South (37,000), Panyijiar (25,000) , Rubkona (141,000), Aweil East (89,000) counties. With huge food gaps among returnees and refugees. This is likely to increase severe and harmful coping mechanisms like begging and borrowing, leading to the accumulation of debts; forced migration in search of jobs; the sale of productive assets, reducing the frequency of meals; and eating wild fruits, among others. The lack of food among refugees and returnees and host communities is likely to increase the strain on host communities in counties as they turn to their hosts for support. It is also likely to increase tensions between the refugees, returnees, and host communities, with the risk of exacerbating conflict over available food and natural resources. According to the OCHA flood situation report, as of November 2024, about 1.4 million people are affected by flooding in 43 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area, with Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states comprising more than 51 percent of the affected population. The Recurrent climatic shocks continue to destroy farmlands, reducing annual crop yields. Other sources of livelihood, like fishing, experience loss and damage of fishing equipment, thereby reducing fish trade, which is one of the key income sources. Pastoralist livestock continues to be affected by pests and diseases, significant damage to pasture/grazing land, and decreased milk production. Further, communities and local authorities lack the capacity, knowledge, and skills to respond to climate shocks and new emerging disasters. Thus, there is a need to provide emergency cash, food, and livelihood response to the vulnerable affected people's needs.
