Project Summary

In 2023, multiple causes of Humanitarian crises continued to affect access to healthcare services across South Sudan leading to an estimated 8.9 million people being in need of humanitarian assistance across the Country. This is due to the devastating effects of continued flooding from last year, continued skirmishes of insecurity from armed groups, communal conflicts arising from tribal clashes due to cattle rustling, armed conflict in Sudan forcing thousands of people (returnees and refugees) fleeing the crises for safety in South Sudan and economic difficulties and disease outbreaks. Similarly, from April 2022 up to date, HealthNet TPO (HNTPO) experienced widened gaps in delivering essential health care services across its current project areas since several health facilities that it had been supporting with funding from HPF were dropped from the funding mechanism by HPF due to funding gaps from the donors that pool funds into the HPF. These challenges are further compounded by the inadequacy of motivated and qualified staff, inadequacy of medical drugs and equipment, and lack of funding for health services delivery that essential health services delivery is heavily dependent on international aid. This reduction left many communities vulnerable especially women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities, in their quest for accessing equitable essential maternal child health, sexual and reproductive health care services, and mental health and psychosocial support services. In order to meet the health needs of the vulnerable People in Need, HNTPO proposes a 4 -way approach to increase access to comprehensive essential healthcare services including curative and preventive services in health facilities and in the communities with integrated outreach services, providing basic mental health and psychosocial care support and clinical management of victims of sexual violence and rape. These approaches include; 1. Continued support to health care services in, Aweil North, Aweil West, Gogrial West, Gogrial East, and Twic counties, extending services to the communities that are distant and hard to reach, and fill gaps created by increased demand on health services due to IPDs, returnees and refugees from the Sudan crises in those communities, in addition to strengthening referral pathways in the for continuum of care. 2. Establish a unit for Case Management of Rape Survivors in Raja, Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties. 3. Establish a unit for delivery of Minimum Service Package (MSP) in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services in Raja Hospital, Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties, and 4. Strengthening referral systems in the target locations to complete the continuum of care. These interventions will contribute to achieving the overall South Sudan Health Cluster 2022's objective of decreasing excess morbidity and mortality rates through Improving equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality healthcare services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV services to crisis-affected populations. The project targets 50,000 PIN. These include 38,244 residents, 3,269 IDPs, and 8,487 returnees in the different age groups of under-five years, 5-17 years, 18- 60 years, and above 60 years of age, in the targeted communities of Terekeka, Aweil North, Aweil West, Raja, Gogrial East, Gogrial West and Twic counties of South Sudan by the end of 2024.

1. Needs

At present, the health systems in South Sudan is weak, mostly hard to reach and insecure communities hardly access basic live-saving services. Perennial flooding, cattle-rustling and communal conflicts have displaced communities and perished their livelihoods, thus worsening the situation by causing massive deaths, injury and disability as a consequence of increased susceptibility to hunger, diseases, trauma and mental illnesses due to inadequate accessibility to health, nutrition, WASH and mental health care services. In HealthNet TPO’s current areas of operation (Terekeka, Raja, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties), only the Health Pooled Fund (HPF) supported Health Facilities are functional but are also not able to provide a complete package of services as required by the BPHNS guidelines of South Sudan due to funding gaps. A total of 61 health facilities from Raja, Terekeka, Aweil North, Aweil West and Gogrial East, Gogrial West and Twic counties which are not supported under HPF 3 funding have ceased functioning due to lack of supplies, skilled staff and limited general support from the government. The number of unsupported health facilities increased since year 4 of HPF3 (April 2022- March 2023) to 105 as HPF dropped support to additional 31 health facilities including state hospitals. in the above-mentioned locations. These facilities are mostly located in distant and extremely hard to reach locations. Communities in the catchment of the unsupported and non-functional facilities are isolated and experience frequent tribal fighting and extreme weather conditions (floods and drought). The situation is compounded by high morbidity levels with many treatable illnesses observed among children under 5-years-old, mostly malaria, respiratory infections and diarrhea and malnutrition. In addition, the few accessible health facilities have inadequate health workers presently in facilities, some communities are located in extremely hard to reach and insecure areas with no health facilities and many communities often do not feel safe to trek long distances and hours to access the health services from the existing health facilities thus affecting health seeking behaviors and put many vulnerable populations especially women, children, disabled and the elderly at high risk of morbidity and mortality. Further, young girls and women often are victims of rape while trekking the long, insecure roads given the fluidity of security in these areas. To respond to these needs in a holistic approach, HNTPO intends to employ a 4-way approach to increase access to essential primary health care services with emphasis on maternal, child and adolescent sexual and reproductive health, SGBV/CMR and MHPSS services, neglected tropical and non-communicable diseases especially among the elderly. These approaches include; 1. Continued support to health care services in Terekeka, Aweil North, Aweil West, Raja, Gogrial West, Gogrial East and Twic counties, extending services to the communities that are distant and hard to reach, and fill gaps created by increased demand on health services due to IPDs, returnees and refugees from the Sudan crises in those communities, in addition to strengthening referral pathways in the for continuum of care. 2. Establish a unit for Case Management of Rape Survivors in Raja, Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties. 3. Establish a unit for delivery of Minimum Service Package (MSP) in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services in Raja Hospital, Terekeka, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties, and 4. Strengthening referral systems in the target locations to complete the continuum of care. HealthNet TPO’s 4 way approach fits into the health cluster goal of providing quality healthcare to crisis-affected people, prevent and respond to health threats, and strengthen the health system and overall HRP 2024’s objective of saving lives, reducing health risks, and enhancing coping and recovery by focusing on delivering essentia

2. Project activities

This intervention will contribute to reduction of excess morbidity and mortality in the targeted vulnerable communities, thereby complementing and strengthening what is being done in health facilities in the target communities. Activities will broadly fall into the priority activities of the sector and they will be a combination of curative consultations, preventive interventions, and emergency response interventions which will be implemented in the targeted communities. Specifically, provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through fixed and integrated outreach healthcare services, procurement and provision of essential medical kits and training of health workers. Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response. Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives and Psychosocial support networks in the communities of the target locations. IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities, enhancing referral pathways between the communities and static health facilities for critical conditions. The project will specifically: 1). Conduct integrated healthcare outreaches to the hardly inaccessible communities especially those displaced by floods and in Payams where health facilities are non-functional. An integrated services package including antenatal, postnatal, immunization, curative consultations and growth monitoring, nutrition screening and health awareness sessions will be provided in the outreaches. Linkages will be established between the communities and the nearest PHCCs and hospitals where severe cases will be referred for further management and appropriate care provided. Women in labor presenting in the mobile clinics will be referred to functioning PHCCs for skilled birth services. These services will be provided at no cost to all age groups. 2). Immunisation services for children will be provided in the targeted communities to ensure that no child is left behind. All antigens in the Expanded Program on Immunisation according to South Sudan guidelines will be available and provided as per the agreed schedule. Cold chains established in the fixed sites will provide the mobile clinic teams with the vaccines in cool boxes and vaccine carriers. At the community level, Boma Health Workers will be helpful in defaulter tracing and referral to the nearest mobile clinic points and immunization centers 3). Using the essential medicines list of South Sudan, a list of essential commodities including psychotropics will be procured for health care services provided through the proposed service points. Mechanisms for rational use will be instituted and stock closely monitored to ensure that stock ruptures are minimized during the project period. 4). Medical and paramedical staff within the targeted communities will be trained and refresher trained on various health topics including, Clinical management of rape and mhGAP in MHPSS. Beyond training, mentorship, coaching, and supportive supervision will further be used to enhance staff capacity. 5). During outreaches, health education will be provided daily on various topics. Other health awareness sessions and campaigns will be organized to promote health and prevent diseases. 6). Surveillance mechanisms will also be instituted to monitor the trend of epidemic-prone diseases. Friendly and private spaces will be created within the PHCCs and Hospitals to provide counseling support for victims of sexual violence.
