Project Summary

Since 2017, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has been a key responder to the Rohingya crisis, supporting both host and refugee communities. CB has run education programs for children, adolescents, and youth since 2020. The project aims to continue into the upcoming year, 2025. It focuses on providing education for children aged 3 to 14 and 15-18, following the Myanmar Curriculum. The strategy involves converting six Multipurpose Children and Adolescent Centres (MCACs) into four LCs in Camps 4 and 20 Extension, focusing on Grade 6 for eligible adolescents not enrolled in other LCs. Placement will be determined through sector-aligned tests. The project will run LCs in 2025, following Education Sector-endorsed materials and guidelines. It ensures equal gender balance.
In collaboration with Library Without Boarder (Bibliothèques Sans Frontières- BSF) CB has been implementing education activities for Rohingya Adolescent and Youths of aged 15-18, focusing on Access to information, education, and learning through setting-up a library space with an Ideas Box, a multi-media hub that provides educational and cultural resources to vulnerable groups to meet and engage in activities together in a safe space. Through its modularity and the variety of contents and tools it offers, Ideas box increases the scope of existing activities and fosters local initiatives. Another innovation of the project is Ideas Cubes to conduct outreach sessions to improve access to information and learning for extremely vulnerable or isolated people who are restricted in their movement due to social or other barriers. Two Youth and Adolescent Centres in Camp 4 Extension, staffed by five Field Officers and eight volunteers, run from 8:45 AM to 4:00 PM to maximize Idea Box use. There will be specific sessions especially geared towards adolescents and youth of 15-18 years of age. The library will also have dedicated times for free use and provide open access to its content for the targeted group for IT equipment is using as a tool to facilitate sessions with the beneficiaries, as well as to overcome limitations. Digital devices will be used in offline purposefully in guided sessions for education and recreation, with scheduled access, content filtering, and participant feedback to ensure safe and effective use.
The project encompasses the following key components:
Pre-primary Education (KG): Children aged 3-5 years will receive pre-primary education, focusing on Early Childhood Education (ECE) through interactive and joyful activities, play, and audio-visual sessions, preparing them for learning centres.
Primary and Secondary Education: Children aged 6 to 18 will engage in primary and secondary education, spanning Grades I to IV and VI-VII following the MC
Inclusive facilities will address access barriers for children with disabilities and girls, with remote and IT-based learning as alternatives for hard-to-reach learners.
Access to information, education, and learning for adolescents and youth: Providing meaningful and functional numeracy, literacy, and life skills education as part of pre-vocational education to individuals aged 15-18. Including communication, teamwork, critical thinking, time management, literacy, numeracy, and practical skills development. Basic literacy and numeracy courses, available in both Myanmar and English languages, will equip adolescents and youths with the competencies to function effectively in their daily lives and pave the way for pursuing studies in academic and vocational areas as they desire
Support to the Host Community: CB will collaborate with government and community-run schools to provide technical assistance and materials for Early Childhood Development (ECD) programs and classes 1 and 2.
Teacher Professional Development (TPD): CB will organize TPD activities, including training in basic pedagogy, subject-specific instruction, classroom management, refresher courses, and materials development, to enhance teaching quality.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Rohingya population in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh has suffered from genocide, with their children denied education since 1982 in Myanmar. Currently, there are 994,124 Rohingya refugees reside in 33 overcrowded camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, including 52% children (UNHCR August 31, 2024). The universal right to education is upheld by international agreements that host countries must honour. UN Sustainable Development Goals 4 (Quality Education) and 5 (Gender Equality) stress the importance of education for Rohingya children, whether in Bangladesh or elsewhere.According to the ISNA 2024 there are significant educational needs, with particular attention to gender considerations. Enrolment gaps and gender disparities are evident, as 22% of households with children aged 3–18 lack access to education. Among enrolled students, boys exhibit a higher enrolment rate (62%) compared to girls (57%), and this gap is most pronounced for adolescent girls aged 15–18, where only 8% are enrolled. It suggests a strong need for targeted interventions to support adolescent girls’ education, particularly at the secondary level and beyond.Safety concerns and accessibility barriers further impact enrolment. Although 93% of learners report feeling safe traveling to educational facilities, the remaining 7% face safety issues, primarily fear of abduction and threats from armed groups. Girls are disproportionately affected by these safety concerns, highlighting the need for gender-specific measures to secure safe passage to and from learning centres. Additionally, logistical barriers such as distance and unsafe infrastructure, like broken bridges, restrict access, emphasizing the need for improved and resilient transport options to educational facilities.Cultural and gender-specific challenges also play a crucial role in education accessibility, especially for girls. Factors such as early marriage, household responsibilities, and menstruation are commonly cited reasons for non-enrolment. For adolescent girls, especially those over 15, issues like being too old for education or being married lead to high dropout rates. This indicates a need for flexible and adaptive educational programs tailored for older girls who may otherwise face social or familial constraints. Requests for gender-segregated classrooms and appropriate sanitary facilities underscore the importance of a supportive, inclusive learning environment that respects cultural expectations and enhances comfort for female students.Quality and retention challenges further hinder educational engagement. A lack of adequate teaching materials, qualified teachers, and gender-sensitive facilities were noted as factors limiting interest in school attendance, particularly among girls. Addressing these issues could significantly improve retention and engagement for both male and female students by ensuring that learning environments are more appealing and adequately resourced.An assessment of Library Without Borders and Caritas Bangladesh indicates the need for innovative community engagement and social-emotional learning (SEL) initiatives to improve outcomes. The Ideas Box and Ideas Cube enhance educational impact by addressing social-emotional needs, promoting better attendance, retention, and reducing negative behaviours and stress.According to Education Sector data as of September 2024 shows only 22% coverage for the host community, leaving a substantial gap. Recognizing this urgency, Caritas Bangladesh will continue its education program into 2025, prioritizing advocacy for gender-sensitive facilities, alternative learning paths for adolescent girls, and durable educational infrastructure to expand access, improve safety, and create an inclusive, supportive environment for the underserved populations.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) prioritizes the safety, dignity, and rights of vulnerable Rohingya populations, including women, children, persons with disabilities, and the elderly, by aligning with protection mainstreaming principles. Safety and Dignity: CB strategically locates learning facilities in safe zones, away from hazards, and provides separate, labelled latrines for girls and boys. Safeguarding and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures align with CB’s Protection Policy and Code of Conduct. Meaningful Access: CB ensures inclusivity, addressing barriers for all, including girls, boys, and individuals with disabilities. Girls-only sessions, led by female teachers, create safe learning spaces. Participation and Empowerment: CB fosters community ownership through inclusive decision-making, ensuring women are represented in committees and empowering communities to strengthen resilience and reduce risks. Accountability: CB maintains an Accountability to Affected People (AAP) framework, supported by Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) systems and a Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM). Adaptive programming ensures responsiveness to evolving needs. CB also follows its Gender Policy to create gender-sensitive and accessible learning environments, with dedicated spaces for girls and considerations for children with disabilities. And CB trains staff and volunteers in child protection and maintains robust mechanisms to mitigate risks and handle complaints, ensuring safe and supportive facilities. By embedding protection mainstreaming across all initiatives, CB ensures the well-being, inclusion, and empowerment of vulnerable populations in this project.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) adheres to a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy, customized for the humanitarian context. CB has established a Safeguarding Committee and designated a Safeguarding Focal Point in all offices, including the Cox's Bazar office. Training in Safeguarding, the Code of Conduct, and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) orientation is conducted annually for all staff and volunteers. To ensure compliance, staff and volunteers are required to sign a Safeguarding Policy, Code of Conduct, and self-declaration form upon joining, and their references are checked before employment. Beneficiaries are introduced to the safeguarding policy through orientation sessions prior to service delivery and distribution activities. Safeguarding is integrated into the agendas of all meetings, trainings, and workshops. The both version (English and Bengali) of the Safeguarding policy and a one-pager are distributed among staff and host volunteers. Furthermore, all visitors, advisors, and vendors are required to read and sign the safeguarding form before visiting the camps.The same obligations and protocols apply to the staff, teachers, and volunteers working under the proposed project.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

A comprehensive MEAL (Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning) mechanism is in place to uphold Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, incorporate the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and engage in regular stakeholder consultations. These measures are designed to enhance the quality of education within the project. CB is committed to building the capacity of its staff and volunteers, empowering them to facilitate culturally sensitive programming that promotes sustainability and can potentially be replicated within the community. All policies and guidelines of CB will be strictly maintained to ensure compliance with emergency response standards as well as Education Sector standards. The project actively involves parents, Learning Centre based Community Education Support Group (CESG), and Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) in the education process. Community members participate in the needs assessment through Participatory Action Research (PAR) methods. Impact findings are shared with beneficiaries, and monthly meetings with CESGs and parents are held to collaboratively decide on ways to ensure quality education for their children. Feedback from diverse learner populations, including children, adolescents, and youths, is given significant importance, and the project encourages community participation in all stages of implementation. Community suggestions at the field level are highly valued.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) has a robust Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy in place and is an active member of the PSEA network. In line with CB's established practices, the following measures will be undertaken within this project to ensure the mainstreaming of PSEA:Strong Adherence to PSEA Policy: The existing PSEA policy will be rigorously maintained and enforced throughout the project. Code of Conduct: To further strengthen the commitment to PSEA, a Code of Conduct will be implemented within the project. This code will be applicable to all project stakeholders, including staff, volunteers, vendors, and suppliers, emphasizing the importance of appropriate behaviour and ethics.Establishment of Reporting Mechanism: A confidential and accessible reporting mechanism will be established to facilitate the tracking and mainstreaming of PSEA. Investigations and Response Mechanism: A clear and functional mechanism for investigating and responding to reported incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse will be in place. Incidents will be handled promptly, impartially, and in compliance with legal requirements.Accountability: All project staff and partners will be held accountable for adhering to the PSEA policy and the Code of Conduct. PSEA Focal Point: To oversee and ensure the effective mainstreaming of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, a dedicated PSEA Focal Point will be appointed. This individual will be responsible for coordinating PSEA activities and upholding the organization's commitment to PSEA within the project.Caritas Bangladesh is committed to maintaining the highest standards of protection and accountability in all its humanitarian efforts. Through these PSEA measures, CB aims to create a protective environment, prevent sexual exploitation and abuse, and ensure the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries involved in the project.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project incorporates climate actions. Emergency preparedness and response activities are integral to the proposed project. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is committed to providing services that ensure each learning facility has both a Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) contingency plan and a development plan in place. These plans are implemented in close collaboration with the community. A key aspect of the proposed project is capacity building for various stakeholders, including teachers, community volunteers, Community Education Support Group (CESG) members, and other community stakeholders, within the context of Preparedness for Emergency Response and Urgency (PERU) and DRR program management. Their active engagement in these initiatives is essential for preventing and mitigating potential crises. By enhancing their knowledge and skills and involving them in decision-making and program implementation, the community is empowered to build sustainability and resilience. To further strengthen preparedness, hazard mapping orientations will be provided to project staff and volunteers. The results of the hazard mapping exercise will inform the development of comprehensive development and contingency plans for all learning facilities. These plans will be continuously updated throughout 2025, ensuring that the project remains ready to respond effectively to any emergencies or crises that may arise. Additionally, the project will promote tree planting around the learning facilities and mobilize community members through various meetings, such as community consultations, parents’ meetings, and CESG meetings, to encourage tree planting for environmental protection. This topic will be included as a cross-cutting issue in all meetings.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project has robust Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures embedded. It includes a comprehensive Business Continuity Plan, an Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedure (SOP), and a contingency fund to ensure swift and efficient responses in case of emergencies. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is committed to equipping each learning facility with a Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) contingency plan and development plan. These plans are developed and maintained in close collaboration with the community, ensuring that they are relevant, practical, and widely supported. Key stakeholders, including teachers, Community Volunteers, Community Education Support Group (CESG) members, and other community stakeholders, receive regular training on these EPR measures to enhance local capacity. Additionally, hazard mapping orientations and regular updates of the contingency and development plans ensure ongoing preparedness and responsiveness. These embedded EPR measures make the project resilient and responsive to potential risks, enhancing safety and continuity for all beneficiaries.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project actively includes and engages youth aged 15–24. Recognizing the critical role of youth in building resilient communities, the project provides tailored educational programs, life skills training, awareness sessions, and leadership opportunities. It also offers short and long courses, such as “English for Daily Life” and “Basic Literacy and Numeracy.” Additionally, specific awareness sessions for young women include women’s safety kits and education on protection against violence and harm. To promote cultural engagement, the project organizes indoor and outdoor tournaments, including football, fostering teamwork and a sense of community. Youth are further mobilized to identify community-level issues and develop practical solutions. In support of this, we assist them in implementing micro-projects aimed at addressing local challenges. These initiatives enhance personal development while equipping youth with skills to contribute meaningfully to their communities. Youth also actively participate in community outreach and serve as volunteers, promoting peer learning and fostering a culture of responsibility and empowerment.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, our project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees in the camps through various initiatives. We offer soft skills development and pre-vocational training programs that empower refugees to gain employment after repatriation, fostering economic independence. By ensuring access to quality education for children and youth, we equip them with essential skills and knowledge that enhance their future employment opportunities. Additionally, we actively involve refugees in project planning and implementation, encouraging their participation in decision-making processes, which strengthens their sense of ownership and community vital components for resilience. Our project also includes mental health and psychosocial support services, helping refugees cope with trauma and build social connections, thereby enhancing their overall well-being.
