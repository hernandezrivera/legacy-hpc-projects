Project Summary

According to the Health cluster strategy for 2020, it is estimated that 3,296,823 people in South Sudan will require humanitarian healthcare services in the whole of 2020. However, the health cluster can realistically reach 2,049,990 people with various health care services in different parts of the country.
In 2020, the health cluster intends to reach this target population using three main objectives in 2020: 1) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities; 2) Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations; and 3) Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. These objectives are in alignment with HRP 2020 objectives and with UNFPA’s country programme outputs, especially the ones on humanitarian response 1) Increased access to Integrated Sexual Reproductive Health /Gender-Based Violence services including in humanitarian Response and gender and gender-based violence 4) Prevention and response to GBV and harmful practices.
The situation of reproductive health, including the availability of commodities and supplies for the population affected by the crisis, remains dare. The implementation of the minimum initial services package for SRH is varied in scale and quality as you move across the country. Overall access to SRH services is limited due to poor health infrastructure and road network, limited availability of SRH commodities and limited skilled staff, among others. Antenatal visits and skilled delivery remain a significant challenge, with only 17% of pregnant women attending four visits and a chronic shortage of basic emergency obstetrics and neonatal services. The unmet need for family planning remains high at 24%. The needs for clinical management of rape and psychosocial services for survivors of sexual violence remains high while the mix of frequent population movements, lack of awareness among others provide a conducive environment for new HIV infections.
Through this project, UNFPA seeks to serve two categories of affected populations namely: 491,998 women of reproductive age group (WRA) of whom we expect 71,000 deliveries, 34,900 WRA attempt to use modern contraceptives, 29,000 people seeking care for STI syndromes and nearly 9,000 cases of sexual violence who will seek care. On the other hand, UNFPA will seek to reach 471,498 girls and boy with HIV prevention services and information, adolescent sexual and reproductive health services and information among others Overall, UNFPA seeks to reach 963,496 affected people with various forms of SRH services and information. The targets will be achieved through a well-coordinated mechanism for SRH at both the national and local levels.
The main interventions to achieve the project purpose include, among others:
a) Ensuring availability of skilled staff and good quality infrastructure for the provision of integrated SRH/GBV, including emergency obstetric care services at static health facilities and through outreaches.
b) Conducting community dialogues and health education for women, men, families and community to increase awareness on benefits of safe sex, HIV prevention, family planning, ANC, PNC and delivery in a health facility by skilled birth attendants;
c) Procuring, prepositioning, and distributing a whole range of RH kits and non-kit RH medicines and sundries essential for the delivery of RH services at the community, and health facilities including RH equipment for EmONC (delivery beds, examination couches, lighting, and others)
d) Training service providers on essential lifesaving service areas like supply chain management and rational use of RH kits, MISP, CMR, PAC, FP, HIV prevention and universal precautions, and other lifesaving interventions.
e) Producing and disseminating IEC materials.
f) Supporting 24/7 referral system of complicated obstetric cases and GBV cases.
g) Engage men and boys

1 - Needs

Despite the ongoing peace process, the humanitarian situation in the country continues unabated. The country continues to experience frequent and unexpected acute localised crises amidst the major protracted crisis. This stretches available resources considerably. The health cluster estimated to target 2,049,990 affected people with health services and information. Among whom will be 491,998 women of reproductive age, 471,498 adolescent girls and boys in a dire humanitarian health care need in 2020. Women and girls have been severely affected by the conflict-related sexual violence with an estimated 9,840 cases of rape who seek care in 2020. Below is a table providing the summary needs in 2020 for reproductive health. Best available estimates Units491,998 Women of reproductive age471,498 Adolescents (10-19)71,342 Live births in the next 12 months34,932 WRA who use modern contraceptives11,808 People living with HIV29,416 People seeking care for STI syndromes 9,840 Number of cases of sexual violence who will seek care Through this project, UNFPA will endeavour to reach all the third of women of reproductive health age group ( 163,999) and a third of adolescents (157,166) with a total of 321,165 beneficiaries. Maternal mortality ratio still ranks one of the highest in the world (789 deaths per 100.000 live births), as a result of lack of access and limited availability of reproductive health care services across the country. Pregnant mothers continued to die while giving life. The UNFPA assessment on the implementation of the Minimum Initial Services Package for reproductive health services conducted in 2018 highlighted the significant breakdown of functioning health facilities. The local health systems in remote rural areas, which have also the worst of the crisis, are overwhelmed, and in some cases collapsed. 80% of health service provision is delivered through non-governmental agencies. Focus group discussions and key informants’ interviews highlighted a high demand for specialised care for reproductive health, which includes family planning, Post Abortion Care, Gender-Based Violence services and others. Access to comprehensive obstetric surgical care in many parts of the country, the limited number of skilled birth attendants and the lack of ambulance services as well as access to essential medicines and medical supplies is still critical in humanitarian protection of civilians sites and government facilities across the country. This project will contribute to addressing the immediate needs of pregnant women, who want to access antenatal, perinatal and postnatal care, including post-partum family planning and services for a complicated pregnancy, PAC and delivery by ensuring that they benefit from basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric care. GBV risks will be mitigated by ensuring that women, girls and boys have access to services through community awareness sessions and protection networks, mainly to engage them in HIV prevention and combating gender-based violence; and Youth friendly service, including recreational spaces, for young people (both male and female) to be able to get some positive outlets for their energies and to tap into them as a positive source of role models and peer educators within their communities

2 - Activities

Activities This project is intended to actualise one of the core strategies of the health cluster of ensuring availability of life-saving maternal health services, including commodities and supplies in all service delivery points among affected populations. Therefore, the activities below are intended to ensure that RH services are available and accessible in all the locations serving the affected population. These are; 1. Identify and deploy skilled staff for the provision of integrated SRH/GBV, including emergency obstetric care services at static health facilities and through outreaches. 2. Maintain, renovate, and whenever possible, re-open infrastructure for the provision of integrated SRH/GBV including emergency obstetric care services at static health facilities and through outreaches. 3. Conduct community dialogues and health education for women, men, families and community to increase awareness on benefits of safe sex, HIV prevention, family planning, ANC, PNC and delivery in a health facility by skilled birth attendants;4. Procure, preposition, and distributes a whole range of RH kits and non-kit RH medicines and sundries essential for the delivery of RH services at the community, and health facilities including RH equipment for EmONC (delivery beds, examination couches, lighting, and others)5. Train service providers on essential lifesaving service areas like supply chain management and rational use of RH kits, MISP, CMR, PAC, FP, HIV prevention and universal precautions, and other lifesaving interventions.6. Produce and disseminate IEC materials.7. Support 24/7 referral system of complicated obstetric cases and GBV cases.8. Engage men and boys in awareness sessions and protection networks for HIV and gender-based violence prevention;9. Monitor, maintain and scale up data collection and reporting on SRH services

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

A majority of project beneficiaries are women and girls since reproductive health issues disproportionally affect more women and girls than any other segment of the populations. Therefore not consulting women and girls would pause a significant risk to the project. Women and girls input into the project design would enhance ownership and improve service uptake. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence and exploitation during access to humanitarian assistance would have a devastating impact on the project. The women and girls would be gravely affected physically and psychologically, among others, while the image of the humanitarian system as a whole would be tainted. The consequences of sexual violence and exploitation are life long and can have a severe impact on the community as a whole. Unsafe and unethical management of data, especially in the provision of health services, can result in severe consequences. In some situations in the past, health workers have been attacked and injured due to it, while beneficiaries have shunned services due to such practices. Data management is, therefore, an important part of project management and all those involved in the implementation of the project have to commit to managing data safely and ethically.
