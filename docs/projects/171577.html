Project Summary

The proposed project is designed to respond to the most essential need for child protection responses children with and without disabilities. Based on Atfaluna needs assessment it becomes clear that children with and without disabilities are of need child protection activities. They need to enjoy a safe environment within their homes, schools, and the whole surroundings. The project tackles the problems faced by the children that threatens their protection and mental-wellbeing. Children protection and psychosocial support is of significance to mitigate the effects of child violations in family surrounding and schools. The project will provide equal access to children with and without disabilities through distributed service points across the Gaza Strip. Medical care will be provided to children whose families cannot afford the cost of treatment. Children who were exposed to severe harmful act will receive legal counselling and representation. Children will learn to have a voice and express and report child protection issue that they encounter. Parents will be targeted to help them cope with life burdens and turn improve their parenting skills which will create a safe environment for children at their homes. Service providers, teachers, and community members will be sensitized on child protection issues such as child labor, schools’ dropout, and abuse to that they integrate the child protection mechanisms to mitigate the risks that children face in the community.
The project will be implemented in partnership with five childhood centres across the Gaza Strip.
Due to current COVID19 situation, the protection and psychosocial support will be implemented in remote and blended modalities that involves including technological solutions to make sure that most children have access to protection interventions. Families who do not have internet access will be reached through the field team taking all needed precautionary measures.

1 - Needs

Children in Palestine face considerable challenges to fulfil their real potential and to become active citizens. Many are exposed to unacceptable levels of violence, exploitation and grave violations on the way to and from school, during school, and in their homes. Exposure to violence in the short and long term negatively impacts on the mental, emotional, behavioral, social and physical well-being of children and unless they receive counselling these effects can become worse over time. There are two main sources of violence: i) violence resulting from the occupation and ongoing Israeli-Palestinian conflict; and ii) violence within Palestinian families and communities, including domestic violence, harsh corporal punishment in homes and schools, sexual abuse, early marriage, and child labor. These two sources of violence are interconnected in many complex ways. Based on WHO projections of mental disorders in populations affected by emergencies, it is estimated that approximately 10,400 people will have severe mental health problems and 41,700 will have mild to moderate problems requiring MHPSS, including at least 26,000 children in Gaza under current conditions. These numbers are expected to increase as mental health incidents tend to be manifested at a later stage after the events. According to international literature, CWDs are at-higher risks to be victims of violence, abuse and exploitation than their peers without disabilities. The Gaza Child Protection Working Group (CPWG) and Protection Cluster estimated that 323,000 children in the Gaza Strip are in need of protection interventions in 2019, including psychosocial support interventions. WHO projected more than 26,049 children are in need of MHPSS (severe and mild) in Gaza. In a very recent needs assessment conducted by Atfaluna in March, 2020 targeting 63 of parents(24men, 39 women ), and 312 children (144 boys, 168 girls) with the ages 6-17 years old, out of them 87 children with disability from all areas in the Gaza Strip the following was reported from the interviewed key informants: 52% of the parents reported that they use violence with their children on their daily interactions with them, while 55% of the children (61% boys, 39% girls) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that they are subjected to the violence. The violence is increased against with the children with the disability to reach 64%. Neglect was one of the major things reported by children and their parents as in 32% of the parents(men, women) reported that they have poor communication with their children, while 22% of the children (50% boys, 50% girls) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that their parents do not consider their complaints, the neglect increased to 28% among the children with disabilities. About 19 % of the children (64% boys, 36% girls) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that they didn’t receive the medical care when they are in need for the medical services, and about 36% of interviewed CWDs reported that they do not have access to medical intervention. While 5% of the interviewed children are forced to work to earn money for their families. About 27% of the children (60% buys, 40% girls) subjected to bullying at schools, among them 48% CWDs. 29% of the children (55% boys, 45% girls) reported that the school are not safe for them where their classmate brings sharp tools at the schools. 37% of the parents reported that the teachers are aggressive, while 40% of the children with and without disabilities (56% boys, 44% girls) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that the subjected to the violence from the teachers at the schools. 65% of the parents reported that there are not adapted safe spaces for their children, among them 52% of the children with and without disabilities (56% boys, 44% girls) with the ages 6-17 years. 61% of the parents reported that they have the fears to subject to death or injury due to military escalation, and 69% of the children with and without disabilities (45% boys, 54% girls) with the ages 6-17 years, while 60% of the children with disability reported their fear and anxiety of any escalations. And about 41% of the parents reported that their children didn’t have access to psychosocial interventions. And 26% of the children (42% boys, 58% girls) reported lack of school counseling support, this percentage increased to 40% when interviewing children with disability of the children with disabilities. The COVID19 crisis and emergency situation has double the suffering of families and increased the psychosocial distress that lies on parents and the children. According to Atfaluna’s factsheet, April 2020 related to the impact of COVID19 on the socioeconomic status of families in the Gaza Strip, it was indicated that 94% of the interviewed families reported to suffer from deteriorated livelihood conditions, where 75% of them reported that they struggle in securing the basic nutrition supplies. In addition, 84% of the key informants reported increased anxious due to COVID19 outbreak, and 82% of them stated that their children psychological status is affected by the COVID19 crisis situation.

During the lockdown, Atfaluna has managed in a quality manner to maintaining its PSS and awareness raising intervention going on, the team will replicate the best practices of those experience in this project as well.
