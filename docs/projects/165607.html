Project Summary

The objective/outcome of this project is “population (especially vulnerable groups) in the intervention areas are more resilient to environmental, social and economic shocks” in the Far North region through the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus.
Output 1: Displaced persons and host communities are economically empowered
Activity 1.1 Support economic initiatives (including by 3x6 approach of UNDP, cash for work) for youth and women among refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities
Activity 1.2 Strengthen the capacity in agricultural value chain development
Activity 1.3 Rehabilitate socio-economic infrastructure for local Economy
Activity 1.4 Reinforce the capacity of economic institutions
Output 2: Peace culture is promoted by justice, governance, dialogues and conflict prevention/management
Activity 2.1 Strengthen the capacity of fair justice delivery to manage conflicts, including through traditional courts
Activity 2.2 Reinforce the capacity of local governance and accountability including civil registration/documentation
Activity 2.3 Reinforce the capacity of existing community structures and existing dialogue platforms with inclusive approach
Activity 2.4 Reinforce local initiatives for prevention/resolution of conflicts and social cohesion
Output 3: Human and community security is strengthened
Activity 3.1 Reinforce the capacity of national/municipal police and vigilante committee (esp. human rights and SGBV)
Main target: youth (18-34 years old) and women of Nigerian refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities, authorities, religious/community leaders

Besoins / Needs

The crisis led by armed groups, esp. Boko Haram has negatively affected people and communities in the Far North region as limited economic opportunities, down-sized local economy and limited access to basic social services and natural resources. The majority of displaced persons could bring minimum goods/assets when they fled and they have lost means for their livelihoods. Moreover, many of them have also faced difficulty to change livelihoods due to available inputs in displaced places. The capacity of existing mechanisms has also been limited to prevent and manage conflicts which could be increased due to the massive displacement of people, in particular refugees, IDPs and returnees. There is risk to deteriorate social cohesion among people in communities. Therefore, displaced persons including Nigerian refugees, IDPs and returnees, and vulnerable host communities need to be economically empowered and social cohesion among them needs to be strengthened.
