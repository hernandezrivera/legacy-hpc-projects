Project Summary

This six month action will target the newly displaced vulnerable residents’ and new returneesaged between 6-18 years as well as otherwise vulnerable or marginalized girls and boys at risk including children with disabilities(CWDs) that flee their homes from Khartoum after the conflict that started in the 15th of April of exclusion from education to have better equitable access to essential services, and promote the protection, safety and dignity of affected people in improved protective learning environments and enhance structured lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being within a protective environment .This action priority response aims to prioritize immediate critical life-saving education needs security, access and children safety .During the school recess (June to September), the school building and other public facilities will be used as safe and protective child-friendly spaces to foster and prioritize children’s’ well-being and safety. Once the schools re-open ensuring continued access to education services and retention of both the displaced and non-displaced vulnerable girls and boys, children with disabilities affected by the conflict, poor households, girls, hard to reach areas children SCI through this project will intervene in Blue Nile (Al Kurmuk- Ar Rusayris –Baw – Geissan) localities, South Kordofan in (Dilling- Kadugli –Rashad –Abujibiha) localities, Sinnar in (East Sinnar –Alsokey-Abuhajar-Aldaly WalMazmom-AlDinar) ,Red Sea in Port Sudan localities ,Aljaziera state in (Wadmadani-Hashish-Om-AlGora-Almanagil-South aljaziera-AlKamleen-Sharg Aljaziera- Rofaa) s targeting a total of 407,372 compromise of 400,000 vulnerable and newly displaced children (196,000 boys ,204,000 girls) 5% out of them are children with disabilities ,778 teachers (2741 male , 2853 female) and 5594 OOSC(2741 boy ,2853 girl).This action will be executed into two phases, phase one from June to September 2023 through provision of services to the most vulnerable children and supports water and sanitation infrastructure in schools with an integrated package of education and protection assistance and integrated interventions in Wash in school and provision of clean water and distribution of hygiene kits and provision of psychosocial support activities which includes access to life-saving information and survival skills, including awareness of hazards, better nutrition, protection from SEA and other forms of GBV, and child protection and well-being awareness. To support the traumatized children and enhance their psychological wellbeing. Phase 2 of this action will be from September until end of December, a mobilization campaigns will be conducted to register and enroll the newly displaced and vulnerable children into the established temporary learning spaces and schools near their gathering points and to facilitate children’s safe return to school in the longer-term, there is an intervention of construction of new gender sensitive latrines following the universal design in order to accommodate all children including CWDs and construction temporary handwashing facilities, in addition , children will be provided with feeding programs in close coordination with SCI feeding interventions in the targeted localities. .These interventions are combined with specific Education in Emergencies support including providing temporary learning spaces for displaced children, distributing teaching and learning materials, training teachers and facilitators and enhance their professional development and trained on Psychosocial, safe schools, GBV, children’s rights and violence free learning, including child protection. During phase 2, this action will support the innovative alternative learning modalities through ALP classes, which will be established in the newly constructed learning spaces. Mapping of referral pathways will be conducted to identify existing ones to support the most vulnerable children .

1 - Needs

Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted on 15 April 2023 are having devastating consequences for civilians. The conflict also spread in other parts of the country (such as Darfur and North Kordofan). As of May 4, at least 736,000 people have reportedly been displaced inside Sudan since 15 April, according to estimates by IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). In West Darfur, most of the residents of Ag Geneina town have left due to the fighting that erupted on 24 April. Although figures are not confirmed, about 156,500 people are estimated to be displaced in the state. Overall Fourteen (15) out of Sudan’s 18 states have been affected by displacement. 177,000 people have crossed in neighboring countries (SUDAN CONFLICT RAPID NEED ASSESSMENT – SC-May 2023). Since the outbreak of the conflict, an estimated 285,000 school aged children have been displaced. This includes 208,000 school going children, 106,000 girls and an estimated 15 per cent of children with disabilities. The conflict has negatively affected the education of affected girls, boys and adolescents, including children with disabilities who face challenges in accessing inclusive quality education in a safe and protective learning environment. In addition, the conflict exposes vulnerable children to a range of life-threatening risks such as GBV including child marriage; female genital mutilation (FGM); human / sex trafficking and SEA recruitment by armed actors and child labor. Structured learning programs protect children from exploitation and abuse, and involuntary recruitment into armed groups. This combined with school closures, will exacerbate and negatively affect school attendance for the new academic year that starts in September. In addition to pre-existing challenges related to insufficient learning resources and supplies, the crisis has left learners and teacher displaced and traumatized; damaged learning infrastructure and school spaces, and constrained access to schools (updated HRP May 2023). According to Sudan conflict rapid need assessment conducted by SCI and UNICEF on May 2023 in Aljaziera state, 736,223 individuals (149,599 households) displaced across the country, the conflict is deeply affecting important part of children’s lives. In Khartoum and greater Darfur, learning has been limited or even non-existent. Some schools in safer areas are sheltering displaced families and examination is interrupted in a number of areas. There have been reports of grave child rights violations including killing, maiming, and exploitation - recruitment of children in armed groups (esp. street children) and SGBV are frequently reported. Findings of the assessments indicated that conflict has severe impacts on learning opportunities. , Schools s in Khartoum and in the Greater Darfur are either not functioning or closed, Some schools in safer areas such as White Nile, South Kordofan and Gezira are currently occupied by families fleeing Khartoum and Darfur regions, thus schools cannot be used for learning purposes, examinations interrupted in certain states due to the occupation of schools as well as conflict restricting movement and access to schools. , forced displacement of teachers and children. According to Acted global Emergency Response unit coordinated with Monitoring, Evaluation rapid need assessment in BN, Aljaziera, items is not sufficient and has grown worse since the crisis began. With major supply chain disruptions throughout the country, the supply of hygiene items has dwindled, and KII respondents reported that costs are rising significantly, particularly in Gezira, Blue Nile. In terms of sanitation, assessment findings indicate that latrines and handwashing facilities have not been subject to significant damage following the crisis, but latrines are overcrowded in camps and collective centers in Blue Nile and Gezira state.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The implementation of the project activities will be fully guided by and the updated HRP 2023, SCI HRP 2023 and Save the Children Quality Learning framework (QLF) that developed in consideration of INEE and Sphere. The response strategy of this action will adopt a two-pronged approach: first to support displaced children and second to support children where they live (original settlements) once security, access and children safety allows. The first set of activities are towards ensuring the accessibility of vulnerable or marginalized children (ages 6-18) to safe and protective learning environments through construction of more 20 learning spaces and WASH facilities through construction of 30 latrines, establishment of 80 temporary hand washing facilities and provision of safe drinking water to 36,000 children. I addition,20,000 child will be supported with feeding programs and . 2-Provision of all supplies that includes learning and teaching materials to 400,000 children ,45,000 children will benefit from distribution of recreational materials, hygiene sets to 20,000 vulnarable children.. Provision of clean water through establishment of 80 hand washing stations in the newly established. 20 TLSs will be constructed following the universal design and enroll 20,000 children from newly displaced and marginalized children including CWDs. In order to encourage teachers and ensure they will continue teaching, this action will provide monthly incentive to 1778 teachers, and this will massively contribute to the sustainability of the learning process and improvement of the learning outcomes. This action will ensure the resilient of the newly displaced and returnees and empower the capacity of the teachers through the training of 1778 teachers(871 male,907 female) in on Psychosocial, safe schools, GBV, children’s rights and violence free learning, including child protection issues to support the traumatized children to access to life-saving information and survival skills, including awareness of hazards, better nutrition, protection from SEA and other forms of GBV, and child protection and well-being awareness. Mapping of referral pathways will be conducted to identify existing ones to support the most vulnerable children that will be connecting the schools with diversity of services for the children, this will be supported by stablihment of 17 referal pathway to support the teachers in refering the identified vulnaable and traumatized children. will focus on the GBV issues through a contiguous gender analysis and awareness raising of all stakeholders on this topic in an inception meeting during a kickoff one day workshop, PSEA will ensure to be inserted in this workshop as well.
