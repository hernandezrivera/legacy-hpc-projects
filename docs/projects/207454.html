Project Summary

The overall aim of this project is to contribute to improving the overall health, well-being, gender equality and resilience of Rohingya Refugees and vulnerable people in host communities of Cox’s Bazar District. In the Rohingya camps, the project will continue to support routine repair, operation, and maintenance of existing piped water supply systems and hand pump wells. In addition, new water network supply systems will be constructed and upgraded to cover gaps and enhance the resilience of the facilities against extreme weather events. The capacity and performance of water networks and handpumps will be assessed and improved. UNICEF will transit from supporting quarterly water quality monitoring/surveillance through DPHE to engage its partners testing water-networks more frequently than tube-wells.
The project will support operations, maintenance and improvement of existing latrine and bathing spaces to ensure functionality, quality, safety, and privacy and address the needs of women, girls, Persons with Disabilities (PwD), older adults and other vulnerable people in the camps. The project will upgrade latrines and bathing facilities to ensure inclusive access for women, girls, older adults, children, and persons with disability. New latrines, solid waste, and (Fecal Sludge Management ( FSM) sites will be constructed to fill critical gaps, while efforts will be made to improve FSM effluent quality in all camps. In line with the approved FSM strategy, UNICEF will endeavour to review and reduce the number of FSTP in few camps to minimize the operational cost. The operation and maintenance of Solid waste management sites will be supported, while the project will strengthen integration with nutrition and livelihood for the use of compost. House-to-house waste collection will be scaled up in all eight camps. UNICEF will assess the cost and efficiency of plastic recycling plants for continuation. With the funding cuts, this project will focus on community engagement for behaviour change and sustainability of WASH services.: The participatory approaches such as CCCPLTH (Clean Camp Campaign-People Let Total Hygiene) and RANAS (Risk, Attitudes, Norms, Abilities and Self-Regulation) will be supported for changing identified high-risk behaviours. The project will continue working and engaging community WASH structures/groups to maintain WASH services. The project will work with other sectors for the harmonization and rationalization of the engagement of volunteers. UNICEF will support participatory community monitoring and feedback mechanisms to contribute to AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations). Gender, GVB and Disability inclusion will be mainstreamed in all interventions. The project will support the provision of WASH supplies such as soap, Aquatab and MHM materials and emergency stock of WASH supplies.

UNICEF will implement WASH services for host community people to strengthen humanitarian, development and peace nexus. Actions include community approach for total sanitation (CATS) and Sanitation Marketing (SanMark) for creating open defecation free (ODF) and water safe communities as well as private sector development. Facilitating hygiene promotion for improving personal behaviour and environmental health is an integral action in the project. The project supports WASH in Institutes (school & health) following a 3-star approach and WASH Fit. As Cox’s Bazar is historically climate threatened, the project will support emergency coordination, assessment, planning, and responses as an integral part of the overall districtwide WASH programme. The project will also support WASH emergency preparedness and response, mainly targeting extremely poor adjacent host communities. As part of system strengthening, the project will support government counterparts and local government on evidence generation, WASH planning, demonstration of good practices, monitoring, reporting and capacity building.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The percentage of households reporting insufficient access to water has slightly decreased from 88 per cent in 2021 to 86 per cent in 2023. However, the functionality of hand pumps and the low water level are also concerns. Additionally, water scarcity remains a challenge in Teknaf camps and host communities. Public health risks have increased. According to WHO, 40 per cent of the Rohingya population currently has scabies, and there was an upsurge in acute watery diarrhoea (AWD)/cholera and dengue cases in 2023. Latrine coverage in most camps is below the WASH sector standard of one latrine per 20 users - 22 persons per toilet. Only 62 per cent of female family members feel safe using the communal latrine at night. The number of private latrines is increasing, with 7 per cent of households reporting having their latrines. Segregation of waste at the household level remains a challenge. Around 29 per cent of households do not segregate waste at the household level. Furthermore, the WASH infrastructure is ageing and temporary in nature, and most need to be designed to integrate long-term climate risks and shocks, leaving the WASH services vulnerable. While hygiene knowledge and access to soap are high, access to menstrual hygiene management (MHM) materials remains challenging for many women and girls. The Rohingya refugee crisis created significant pressures on social cohesion and service disparities, having the worst water, sanitation, and hygiene indicators in Cox’s Bazar district. Following the SDG-6 progress in Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts, only 8 per cent of households have access to safely managed drinking water services. Only one-third of households have access to safely managed latrines and properly dispose of children’s faeces under the age of five. Half the population reports regular handwashing at critical times, and 42 per cent of women practice menstrual hygiene management (MHM). On average, 59 per cent of schools have access to safely managed water supply services, and 69 per cent have safely managed sanitation services. However, only 3 per cent of schools provide improved toilets for persons with disabilities. Additionally, just 32 percent of schools offer separate toilets for girls, and MHM facilities are available in only 4 per cent of the schools.Notably, 94 percent of schools have handwashing facilities, and 87 percent of students practice handwashing during school hours. However, there is a reported 10 per cent absenteeism among girls during menstruation. Only 19 per cent of healthcare facilities have safely managed water systems services, and only 2 per cent have access to adequate sanitation facilities. These findings underscore significant WASH gaps within the host community, further exacerbated by frequent climate change threats, including floods, cyclones, and landslides.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Given the recent and anticipated decline in funding, it is essential to improve the effectiveness of the refugee response, gain efficiencies, and reduce duplication. UNICEF has made multiple efforts to streamline and rationalise the delivery of WASH services to ensure efficiency. As part of the rationalization process, UNICEF introduced new approaches to more efficiently use resources. This involved implementing several interventions, including the 'one partner- one camp' modality, where six partners were assigned responsibility for delivering all WASH services for the eight camps. This streamlined approach replaced the previous practice of having multiple partners working in each camp. One partner for each camp has taken over the operation and maintenance of all WASH services and hygiene promotion, including DPHE/ADB water networks. This transition has reduced costs and enabled more consistent rates across partners in UNICEF's area of responsibility, which includes eight camps. Harmonising approaches with other UN agencies, UNICEF has started shifting from communal solid waste collection to household waste collection and segregation, which has reduced the need for additional costs and volunteers for waste segregation. In the host communities, Upazillas level WASH sector coordination groups have been set up to provide WASH services to refugee crisis-affected people in a coordinated and equitable way.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will ensure the protection, dignity, and privacy concerns of affected populations, especially women and girls, including persons with disability (PwD), to access WASH services without facing maltreatment. The project will also work on supporting women and girls in addressing gender and protection needs and empowering them to actively participate in the decision-making process toward safe and sustainable WASH services. Regular monitoring will be continued through Community Feedback and Response System, establishing Community Structures and MHM facilitators groups, etc. Gender-specific topics will be incorporated into WASH awareness messaging, training, and orientation of partner staff and front-line workers. Partners staff, as well as community people, will be oriented on gender-based violence (GBV) in WASH programs to ensure protection issues appropriately. UNICEF and its partners will work closely with the GBV sub-sector.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNICEF and partners have been working to overcome barriers to easy access to WASH facilities for children, older people, and persons with disabilities. All UNICEF partners must have their Child Safeguarding Policies and ensure their staff sign to adhere to the provisions therein. UNICEF and its partners will take measures to reduce barriers so that children of all ages and those at risk of discrimination participate in planning, implementing, and monitoring the WASH interventions. Partners, contractors, and vendors will be trained to take appropriate actions related to age and inclusion.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNICEF continues to promote compliance with AAP commitments on participation, partnership, information, feedback, and action. The UNICEF WASH programme has a strong AAP focus through community engagement, where the "user is at the centre and activities are adapted to maximise outputs of the Project. Community consultations will be prioritised at stages of the WASH programme with the active participation of men, women, girls, boys and persons with disability. UNICEF will ensure that an AAP feedback and response mechanism is established following the AAP principles of participation, inclusion, communication, and transparency. UNICEF and its partners will take measures to reduce barriers so that children of all ages and those at risk of discrimination are participating in the planning, implementation and monitoring process while implementing the projects by the partners. UNICEF will support partners in strengthening organisational child safeguarding policies and increasing staff capacity to take appropriate measures. Moreover, UNICEF will ensure that community feedback and complaints are received and addressed. These include a complaints/feedback /comments box, focus group discussions, and house visits by the CBVs. Feedback analysis and reports, which reflect critical complaints and frequently asked questions from Rohingya about unmet needs and services delivered by sectors and implementing partners, are shared monthly to ensure an effective AAP.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNICEF will continue to support partners in strengthening their institutional PSEA policies and procedures. UNICEF and partners will continue to ensure mandatory prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) orientation is conducted for all staff, volunteers, consultants, vendors, and contractors. Communication materials have been developed and disseminated to create awareness of SEA among the affected population, including prevention, reporting, and victim assistance. All the partners have an awareness-raising plan to strengthen community-based protection mechanisms. Complaint mechanisms, such as the UNICEF-supported Information and Feedback Centre (IFC) and partners ' mechanisms, will be strengthened to facilitate safe/confidential reporting and referral of victims for the required and appropriate services UNICEF has completed a PSEA risk assessment for all the partners and currently, has no partner with high risk. All the partners have PSEA action plans to prevent and mitigate the risk of SEA. All UNICEF partners must have their Child Protection Safeguarding Policies and ensure their staff sign to adhere to the provisions therein. Through field visits and reporting, UNICEF will closely monitor partner organizations' implementation of approved PSEA action plans.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The WASH project will strongly integrate DRR and climate change while raising awareness of WASH issues and designing implementation facilities. Relevant environmental aspects will be considered in terms of forestation and groundwater issues. DRR and climate change will be integrated with WASH prior to their implementation as Cox's Bazar has historically been threatened by climate change and disasters, and two JRP Upazila and the camps are the most vulnerable considering their coastal locations. These efforts aim to safeguard the well-being of the refugee camps and host communities, preserve the local environment, and contribute to climate change mitigation initiatives.
