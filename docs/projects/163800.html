Project Summary

The objective of the proposed action is to respond comprehensively to the protection risks that vulnerable populations face in the South of Libya. The proposed action will focus on identifying and addressing the needs of vulnerable communities namely, IDP, Returnee and host communities, as well as migrants and refugees. The geographic scope of intervention for the proposed action will largely focus on the South.
ACTED will adopt a community-based protection approach for this project, notably by advocating for the establishment of community protection committees (CPCs). Through the CPCs, ACTED will conduct awareness raising sessions around sensitive protection risks encountered by vulnerable and marginalized population and to strengthen existing community owned protection models. ACTED will also establish a referral service and conduct protection monitoring to reach out to the most vulnerable populations and the most vulnerable areas. Complementary to the referral services, ACTED will provide direct assistance to the most vulnerable cases through a special needs fund (cash for protection). ACTED will provide capacity building to CSOs/local actors on protection principles and practical protection activities, as well as financial support (subgrants) for the implementation of activities aimed at the protection of vulnerable populations.
Concerning protection monitoring, at least 240 HHs or 1,344 individual beneficiaries will be identified and will benefit from protection monitoring services and referral to service providers as required. ACTED also plans to reach an average of 150 individuals through awareness raising sessions and the training of at least 10 local CSO protection staff and 1 community-based protection structures. Through the sub grants, ACTED estimates to reach out to at a minimum 1000 individuals, especially in the refugee and migrant communities.
ACTED has been continuously involved in Libya since 2011, establishing a strong field presence with bases in Tripoli, Benghazi, and Sebha. This has allowed ACTED to develop a cross-regional level of acceptance. The ACTED Libya mission employs 52 national staff and 12 international staff to support operational and strategic implementation. ACTED will rely on a team of international and national staff to implement its protection intervention, under the close oversight of its Protection Coordinator. ACTED has strong project management capacities, including accountability mechanisms, sound financial management, and the ability to deliver quality reporting and adhere to contractual reporting requirements. ACTED also has stringent anti-fraud and code of conduct policies and delivers trainings to field personnel on a regular basis. ACTED likewise has a demonstrated ability to undertake large-scale procurement in Libya. Program staff will be supported by a dedicated team of coordination, grant management, finance, logistics, compliance and security staff in country who will be responsible for sound financial and contractual management of the project. ACTED’s functional logistics team, based in Libya and in Tunis, closely follows the written standard procurement procedures as outlined in the ACTED procurement manual, and has a strong operational vendor management system. ACTED also has well-trained program and AME staff with experience in implementing and monitoring the activities which are proposed in this project.
ACTED actively engages with the humanitarian coordination system in Libya, notably the UNHCR-led Protection Working Group (WG), as well as the Child Protection and GBV Sub-Sector WGs, and the education sector WG. In 2018 and 2019/20, ACTED was accepted as member of the Protection Cluster’s core Strategic Advisory Group (SAG), which addresses critical protection issues in Libya. ACTED will report all activities conducted in the framework of the proposed project within the protection sector and the INGO Forum ‘4Ws’ to ensure a coordinated response.

1 - Needs

Since 2011, Libya has witnessed several waves of conflict, with episodic escalation across different regions. The year 2019 witnessed a notable deterioration in the security situation, with the conflict escalating in Tripoli in April 2019 as a result of the Libyan National Army’s military advances, resulting in a dramatic increase in humanitarian needs in Tripoli. Although the frontlines of the clashes remain in the Tripoli region, the southern areas of Libya in particular have felt increasing pressure as a consequence of the Tripoli clashes. Consequences include a deterioration with regard to safety and security, associated production and transportation risks, supply chain disruption, and price increases, as well as power and water shortages. In the South of Libya, inter-communal tensions between the al-Ahali and Tebu communities in Murzuq have grown increasingly worse since early 2019, escalating into a violent conflict starting on August 4th, 2019. The conflict has led to intense urban fighting, identity-based civilian killing, as well as mass displacement to neighboring areas in Libya. An estimated 26,465 individuals from Murzuq had fled to other cities in Libya by late September 2019. Displacement has been based on tribal inclinations and support – with the Tebu IDPs moving to areas under the protection of their tribe such as Hamarah, Al Gatroud, and southern Sebha, and al-Ahali IDPs moving to areas under the protection of their tribe, notably Wadi Atbah, Wadi Al Hatat, northern Sebha, and Wadi Shati. Tensions in the South are also tied to questions surrounding migration, citizenship rights, identity and entitlement for certain minority groups – particularly affecting Libyans of Undetermined Legal Status.The proposed action aims to benefit members of migrant, IDP, returnee and host communities in the Fezzan region. Host communities, IDPs, and returnees likewise face significant protection concerns in the South of Libya. According to data from the 2019 MSNA, 22.8% of households in Sebha and 9% in Ubari had an unmet need in the protection sector. In the REACH Rapid Assessment from August 2019, many IDPs were reported to be under intense psychological stress as a result of their experiences and general lack of security. Across all cities, KIs consistently mentioned that children were experiencing psychological distress and lacked psychosocial support to help them cope. Other protection issues include movement restrictions due to activities of armed groups and general violence, and access to legal documentation. ACTED’s protection team in Benghazi identified severe protection needs among recently displaced persons from Murzuq and the South of Libya, with most households having faced numerous violations to their rights, safety, and dignity.The 2019 HNO highlights refugees and migrants as among the most vulnerable population groups in Libya. Regarding protection concerns, notably freedom of movement, the vast majority of refugee and migrant respondents in Sebha reported feeling in danger in the 2019 MSNA. According to the 2019 HNO, refugees or migrants are subject to arbitrary detention, torture, forced labour, extortion, unlawful killings, sexual exploitation and a variety of other human rights abuses – in the face of any access to rule of law or judicial processes. Refugee and migrant women face specific vulnerabilities, linked to their migration status, and social issues linked to sexism and racism.Despite acute needs and an important migrant population in southern Libya, most of the interventions are currently concentrated in northern Libya, creating a substantial gap in providing adequate response to protection risks and to social exclusion.

2 - Activities

1. Community level assessmentsACTED team will contextualize community knowledge to guide the identification of particularly vulnerable households for interview.2. Awareness raising sessions and community-based protectionACTED will adopt a community-based approach for the implementation of protection and social cohesion activities by promoting the creation of community protection committees (CPCs). ACTED will train the CPCs on protection and social cohesion and will support them financially and technically to develop community-based activities. ACTED will also conduct awareness raising sessions around sensitive protection risks encountered by vulnerable and marginalized population and to strengthen existing community owned protection models.3. Protection monitoring and referral systemACTED will create a referral network of service providers to respond to the protection risks faced by vulnerable populations. ACTED will conduct protection monitoring to ensure an effective identification of vulnerable beneficiaries with protection needs, who will receive assistance through the referral pathway. Data will be collected at the community and HH level to understand the humanitarian situation and to identify specific protection needs that warrant individual-level follow-up. ACTED will establish a ‘Special Needs Fund’ to provide direct assistance addressing the basic needs of vulnerable beneficiaries.4. Capacity-building and sub-granting to local CSOs working on protectionACTED will provide capacity building to CSOs/local actors on protection principles and practical protection activities such as vulnerability case identification, case management and referral pathways, communication with communities tools, PFA, Emergency Response Reporting, SGBV Incident Reporting and Monitoring, and PIM. The trained CSOs will be eligible to receive sub-grants for the implementation of activities aimed at the protection of vulnerable populations.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Risk 1: The Security Situation in Libya Prevents Access to Beneficiaries and Endangers Field Staff: As the security situation remains unpredictable in Libya, a sudden escalation of the conflict could limit the access to areas of implementation. Should the situation escalate, it is possible that direct implementation would no longer be possible without risk to staff or beneficiaries. Risk 2: Access Constraints Posed by Local Actors: There is a risk that local authorities place constraints on programming, reducing access to target areas. While ACTED has so far been able to secure permissions for access, both local and national authorities are rather unstable in Libya, and regular changes in leadership may affect the attitude of local authorities towards international NGOs and their activities even if agreements were previously signed. Risk 3: Risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Given the vulnerable set of population that ACTED will work with on this protection project, it is important to acknowledge the risk for sexual exploitation as well as other forms of abuses. Risk 4: Data Protection: All beneficiaries of the protection programming might face stigma as a result of their participation to ACTED activities if it was to be known within the local communities. Given the that these population are already vulnerable as a result of SGBV they faced, it is essential that ACTED put in place mechanisms of information management to ensure confidentiality. This applies even more to the populations that are in situation of migration, who might face a double stigma, as well as the burden of their status legality. Risk 5. ‘Do No Harm’ and the Well-Being of Survivors: Working with vulnerable populations implies that consent should be sought at each step of the case management process – from initial assessment through to service provision.

5 - Mitigation measures

Risk 1. Should changes in conflict dynamics cause a deterioration of the security situation, threatening the safety of staff or beneficiaries, ACTED will activate its Security Response Plan and potentially adapt the program if neededRisk 2. ACTED will work closely with local authorities to identify vulnerable HHs in order to avoid any confusion regarding the selection of beneficiaries. In the case in which national authorities impede the work of INGOs, ACTED may have no choice but to comply with the regulation put in place in order not to endanger its staff or beneficiaries. Risk 3. ACTED will be cautiously applying its Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse as well as its Child Protection Policy. Selection criteria for program activities and the principles of humanitarian action will be explained to beneficiaries. ACTED will put in place an anonymous Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM). Risk 4. The project requires robust data protection. Data will be password-protected and all data shared will have all personal information redacted in order to ensure the data protection of complaints. The linkage with the referral system will consider full respect for safe reporting systems, guaranteed confidentiality and safe storage of records. Training will be provided to staff on steps to follow when receiving disclosures in group settings or doing public activities. Risk 5. ACTED will utilize survivor consent form for all GBV cases that it manages. Should ACTED wish to interview any GBV survivors to evaluate the impact of the trainings and network on service provision, consent will be sought from all individuals. ACTED also acknowledges the importance of maintaining a balance between visibility and ensuring the physical security and safety of all the actors. The potential impact of each activity will be carefully assessed to ensure the application of the “do not harm” principle in each phase of the project implementation.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) will be a key means of ensuring accountability towards beneficiaries and external stakeholders. The CRM is managed by AME staff to ensure independence from program teams and for which confidentiality is a fundamental principle. Complaints are received via a dedicated CRM phoneline, WhatsApp, Viber and email in both Tripoli and Benghazi. Complaints are also received in the field by AME and program staff to be recorded and transferred to the CRM confidentially. All complaints are registered and recorded by the CRM Focal Point in a password-protected database and categorized based on the level of sensitivity. The CRM Focal Point leads to resolve complaints raised, with the involvement of program, AME or senior management staff, depending on the case and its severity. Information is shared on a strictly need-to-know basis and restricted as much as possible. The CRM Focal Point responds to all individuals who have made a complaint to inform them of the outcome of their complaint, within 15 days or sooner. ACTED ensured that all beneficiaries are made aware of the CRM process and that they could raise any issues and activities are planned to disseminate the CRM information to all project beneficiaries and participants. Within this project, ACTED will actively work to ensure that the CRM is able to be accessed by any community member regardless of their level of literacy, to ensure that marginalized groups can access it—including female headed households. This served as an active line of communication for ACTED to receive any grievances from community members regarding the implementation of the project activities or any suggestions for changes. These calls therefore directly translate into recommendations to programs for improvement of programming, and the resolution of such complaints communicated directly back to the beneficiaries.
