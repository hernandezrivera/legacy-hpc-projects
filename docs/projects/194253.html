Project Summary

The duration of this project will be 12 months staring from 1st January 2022 to 31st December 2022. The education core pipeline supplies project will contribute to the Education Cluster objectives CLEDU/CO1 and CLEDU/CO2 through the provision and management o teaching and learning materials for emergency-affected boys and girls aged 3-17 years (40% girls) to increase equitable access to safe and protective quality education service in South Sudan. Essential education supplies play a critical role in effective humanitarian response and the demand for teaching and learning materials will always remain high especially during emergencies.
Through the project, essential education materials will be procured, prepositioned and distributed and efficiently managed to ensure children's right for equitable access to quality education for all children (boys and girls) in South Sudan, particularly the most vulnerable and those affected by conflict and floods are safeguarded. Without education supplies in the hands of the learners, the teaching and learning process at the school level
will be at stake. As most parents cannot afford to provide learning materials to their children, the need for the provision of education supplies remains high in the education system.
UNICEF has adopted the multi-sectoral approach for project implementation. The provision of supplies will complement integrated WASH and Child Protection interventions in the target schools. Monitoring of the interventions will be conducted jointly with other sectors and lessons learnt documented for future planning.

As the Education Cluster lead and one of the most influential actors with field presence in most of the states, UNICEF will provide support to national NGOs with capacity building in supplies distribution, warehousing, and end-user monitoring.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is faced with numerous challenges including conflict, displacement, floods, COVID-19, poverty, and insecurity to mention but a few. These challenges have exacerbated the dire education situation, which is characterized by poor learning outcomes and high number of out-of-school children particularly the vulnerable groups including those affected by emergencies, girls, and children with disabilities. UNICEF South Sudan (2021): Getting girls back to the classroom after COVID-19 school closures cited in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2022) reports that prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. Due to the high poverty levels, coupled with instability and long distances to school, some parents prefer not to send their children to school but rather keep them at home to help with economic activities and domestic chores. The education crisis increases the risk that more out-of-school boys will be recruited by armed actors while girls face an increased risk of early marriage. The Ministry of General Education and Instruction (2019), Preliminary report on National catchment mapping for out of school children in South Sudan estimates 2.8 million, or over 70 per cent children aged 6 -17 are out-of-school in South Sudan, putting at risk their futures and the future of the country. According to SSAMS (November 2021), the gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7% between 2020 (2,727,209) and 2021 (2,523,204). Therefore, the proportion of out-of-school children in South Sudan is likely to remain high. These figures are even higher in pastoralist communities, as traditional schools are not effective in reaching out to them since the communities are often on constant move with their cattle across the country. The trend in female enrolment and drop out is particularly disconcerting with the Gender Parity Index (GPI) going from 0.86 in pre-primary to 0.75 at primary and, ultimately, 0.54 at secondary level. In the strict patriarchal society of South Sudan, the destiny of the girl-child is often determined from birth with the boy-child as the preferred heir to preserve family lineage and pride. Girls are raised to accommodate their male family members; cooking, cleaning and serving in preparation for their role as wives and mothers. This early childhood socialization process along with other societal, environmental and economic challenges increases the risk of girls being victims of sexual and gender-based violence both within and outside of school settings, pregnancy, early and forced marriages. Thus, in turn leading to persistent obstacles affecting girls’ education opportunities and learning experiences as they transition from adolescence to adulthood. According to education statistics, only 33 per cent of all girls in the country attend primary school, with a high dropout rate of 13 per cent. These trends have led to a low rate of female literacy and inhibit girls from achieving their full potential. The Education Cluster (2021) South Sudan Education Needs Assessment cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding and county education officials reported over 190 schools closed due to flooding. The quality of education has been affected by poor service conditions for teachers; limited opportunities for professional development, very low and delayed salaries . The inadequate learning environment and shortage of basic teaching and learning materials further hinder teachers from providing quality education. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (202, Children with Disabilities (CwDs) face numerous challenges in accessing quality education. In the Education Needs Assessment, most schools reported having no mobility or assistive devices.

2 - Project activities

The UNICEF education core pipeline supplies project will provide teaching and learning materials for emergency-affected children (boys and girls) and teachers (males and females) through implementation of the following activities:2.1) Procure teaching and learning materials (ECD kits, teacher kits, student kits, recreation kits, dignity kits.2.2) Preposition and distribute teaching and learning materials for emergency affected boys and girls aged 3-17 years and teachers.2.3) Conduct end-user monitoring of teaching and learning materials to ensure effective utilization. The affected population will participate in and influence the project through a feedback mechanism. Boys and girls aged 3-17 years will be involved throughout project cycle from needs assessment, design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. The project will strengthen SMCs and PTAs especially in management of the education supplies at the school level and conducting end-user monitoring to ensure proper use of the supplies.

3 - Project indicators

The success of the project will be measure through the following indicators: 3.1) # of children and youth (6-17 years) who benefited from recreational material supplies. 3.2) # of children (3-5 years) benefited from Early Childhood and Development (ECD) kits.3.3) # of children (5-17) and teachers provided with teaching and learning materials. 3.4) # of end-user monitoring visits to project sites conducted.

Information about the project and feedback mechanism will be provided to the beneficiaries through the existing school-based structures. Boys and girls aged 3-17 years will be involved throughout the project cycle from needs assessment, design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will provide beneficiaries with information about Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to raise awareness on the issue through posters and social mobilization sessions. Headteachers, teachers, SMCs, and PTAs will be sensitized on PSEA and the Teachers' Code of Conduct. Linkages with other sectors, especially the Protection Cluster, will be enhanced to create awareness on PSEA at the community level.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries will be gathered through the existing school-based structures including SMCs and PTAs. The information will be analyzed and used to adapt project assistance.
