Project Summary

Chronic WASH needs continue to emerge in parts of Upper Nile and Jonglei States, which are likely to be exacerbated by disease burden, non-ending COVID 19 restrictions, ragging flood waters for the last 3 years, food insecurity as well as logistical challenges. The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, and intensified existing vulnerabilities of affected people,
which include high poverty rates, limited access to basic services including health and education, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement (HNO 2022). Provision of adequate WASH services is key to achieving sustainable solutions to communities in emergency. Medicair is targeting the locations of Jonglei and Upper Nile envisages a targeted response aimed at reaching internally displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable host communities with water, sanitation and hygiene services, integrated with nutrition, health and protection programming. Spanning a period of 12 months, the project targets the locations of Ayod, Akobo, Duk, and Maiwut (Upper Nile) with WASH temporary and sustainable services, to address the vital needs, public health and protection issues. At the national level, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence for children 0-59 months was 11.3 per cent while the Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was at 1.7 per cent. The presence of water-borne diseases in children under 5, for example diarrhoea, is also indicative of poor WASH conditions. (Humanitarian Needs Overview,2022).
Flooding has hit hard these targeted regions of greater Upper Nile for the last 3 years leaving the community vulnerable of using unsafe water sources, open defecation, exposed to waterborne diseases, populations displaced as well. The displaced population in Akobo expressed that there are few available water sources, 95 % of the population are surviving on surface/seasonal ponds and swamps for example Wechlul, Kaikuiny and Kuerkew Boma has no boreholes, a Duba pump shared by the whole boma and even hard to pump but most of the boreholes are not functioning( Akobo West Flood Vulnerability IRNA, SCI 2021).
The response aims at reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programing with Nutrition and Health, Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5, also provide WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation and communities exposed to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant infrastructure. Persistence of food insecurity, signaled by high GAM rates, with <5s and PLW mainly affected will be mitigated by the provision of WASH in Nutrition services, through targeted WASH minimum packages at OTP/SC mainly for mother/caretaker – malnourished children with SAM cases. Water related disease outbreaks have been traced to health facilities and community infrastructures/settings without adequate WASH facilities. A WASH in health outbreak control response is planned especially in disease prone counties as well as hygiene promotion .

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan continues to increase despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement 3 years ago. In states, such as Jonglei and Upper Nile, 33 per cent of households travel for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities. Difficulty in securing enough water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49 per cent of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. The situation is particularly concerning in Upper Nile where as many as 71 per cent of households reportedly use rivers or puddles as their main water source, while in certain counties, such as Canal/Pigi, Ayod,and Manyo virtually all households relied on surface water. Certain rural areas, such as in Upper Nile State, are particularly at risk as they are unreachable in the wet season due to roads being washed away and existing networks cut off. This is likely to force displaced people into urban or already populated areas, causing a knock-on effect to existing WASH infrastructure that are already operating over capacity. (hno,2022).Interagency rapid flood assessment conducted the assessment from 28th – 30th Sept 2021 in Akobo West after heavy rainfall, flood displaced a huge number of approximately 9,120 population and some households were sleeping inside of wet houses, (Akobo West Flood vulnerability Needs Assessment,2021). During Maiwut assessment about 17,394 individuals (10,089 F: 7,305M) were reported to be displaced at different Payams of Maiwut. The population contains 11181 Returnees, 6212 IDPs. About 29% of the populations are children between 0-18 years while 52% are adult aging from 19-59 years and the remaining 19% are elderly people. Amongst the assessed communities, about 2,345 individuals were identified to be vulnerable meeting at least one of the Nine protection cluster criteria such as; 35% are elderly at risk, 5% are separated children, 12% are unaccompanied children, 20% are Women at risk, 5% are people living with Disabilities, 15% are single parents at risk and 8% are people with critical medical conditions. (IRNA Report, Maiwut Upper Nile, 2021). Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points in Greater Upper Nile, with 18 per cent of households, both rurally and in urban settings, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water in the two weeks prior to data collection. Of those, 12 per cent reported of women and girls avoiding water points due to GBV concerns. The issue was particularly acute in certain counties, such as Akobo, Mayom and Baliet where more than 40 per cent of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water. Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. Overall, 80 per cent of households did not access a latrine. Moreover floods regularly damage existing sanitation infrastructure and given their annual occurrence and paired with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by communities to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities, (HNO,2022). in locations like Jonglei and Upper Nile people prefer walk long distance to do open defection. Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence for children 0-59 months was 11.3 per cent while the Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was at 1.7 per cent. The presence of water-borne diseases in children under 5, for example diarrheas', is also indicative of poor WASH conditions, (IPC, July,2021). In 2020, 16% of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns. The COVID 19 pandemic also increased GBV incidences as a result of the measures used to curb the pandemic with 2 million people estimated to be affected by GBV in 2021. Therefore, support for critical WASH needs to continue and the provision of life saving WASH services in communities of Upper Ni and Jonglei is crucial in building and maintaining co-existence and gradual behavior.

2 - Project activities

The response will be based on these cluster activities 1. 18 pump mechanics given refresher training on hand pump maintenance and repair • Drilling of 10 boreholes, Repair and rehabilitate 30 boreholes each with trained water management committee members-WMCs • Organize and conduct participatory hygiene promotion, and conduct training for 40 community Hygiene promoters • Community mobilization and sensitization on good hygiene practices and health education through a participatory approach undertaken in health facilities, churches and markets on good behavior targeting 16,500people • Carry out sanitation education and awareness to enable vulnerable and affected population to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living through secure dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services, construction of 10 sanitation facilities in communities with consideration for gender separated latrines and provision of latrine digging tools to the affected community and training of the community on how to construct the HH latrine using available local materials as an exit strategy. Undertake cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities. Distribution of WASH NFIs, capacity building in terms of preparedness through; training of pump mechanics and provision of borehole spare parts, training of Water Management Committees for resilience, water quality testing both at water points and community level. Medicair shall apply sustainable solutions, such as installing water sources propelled by solar power, where feasible. Medicair has its own drilling equipment and team with geologist to conduct geophysics survey before drilling. Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among affected population to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through promotion activities, particularly hand washing for displaced and vulnerable communities and institutions. Medicair will mostly focus on health workers, teachers, pupils, and women group leaders while conducting household visits, focus group discussions and training for Community Hygiene Promoters.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will coordinate with other actors on ground involved in other sectors like protection and health. With the mainstreaming of GBV into WASH, SGBV and child protection specialists are consulted to identify safe, confidential and appropriate systems of care (i.e. referral pathways) for survivors who tell WASH staff that they have experienced violence. All staff and volunteers involved in the WASH response carry an up- dated list and contact details of agencies and professionals for SGBV, child protection, medical care, legal and psychosocial support services to which they can refer survivors of SGBV or children who reveal an incident of violence to them. Efforts should be made to identify agencies or pro- fessionals experienced in responding to specific risks in each context. Additionally, the project will ensure WASH facilities have maximum privacy and dignity like people have access to latrines according to their gender identity, separate latrines are accessible for persons of different genders with mobility limitations, lockable latrines,Culturally appropriate sanitary materials and underwear are distributed to women and girls of reproductive age in sensitive ways, water points are located so that people do not have to walk unreasonable distances or gradients, and they are located in areas that the community deems safe, consulting with the affected community to ensure WASH facilities and related activities are located in areas that women, girls, boys and men, and individuals from different groups deem safe.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will ensure displaced population have access to durable, resilient WASH facilities which are gender, sex and persons with disability sensitivity to elevate their living conditions.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Making clean and safe water available and accessible for the targeted population decreases the prevalence of water borne diseases and the spread of COVID-19 when used for drinking and hand washing. The construction of sanitation facilities in key places like Nutrition/Health centers, schools and the sensitization of the communities to construct their own House hold latrines using distributed latrine digging tools and locally available construction materials would reduce the practice of open defecation which in turn would decrease the risk of cholera , diarrhea and other vector-borne diseases. Continuous hygiene education will help in behavior change which is a very important element especially in the context of COVID-19.
