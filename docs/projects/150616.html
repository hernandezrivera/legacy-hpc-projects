Project Summary

The project will target 1000 food insecure families to improve their access to food. This includes distribution of E vouchers for food (vegetables, groceries, protein source), in addition to enhance community-based preparedness mechanisms with an E voucher system adopted.

Needs

In July 2018, DCA/NCA partners had conducted a need assessment targeting the IDP’s across Gaza strip in addition to the families of malnourished children in Al Mughraqa and Al Shoka. The assessment aims at examining the socio-economic vulnerabilities, nutritional health status for children under 5 years and protection concerns related to lack of access to food which resulted in alarming levels of food insecurity among the targeted population. IDP’s: The assessment targeted 224 families who are still internally displaced due to 2014 conflict. The population of the assessment included 1,173 individuals (254 men, 264 women, 881 children, 16 elderlies, 30 persons with disabilities). The assessment has included site visits to the residency places of the targeted families and consultations with the affected population, especially house-headed women and men and people with disabilities to assess the protection risks that affect them. The assessment has revealed that all targeted families are vulnerable to high protection risks, some of which are life threatening, due to sever lack of access to basic needs across all sectors. In addition, 96% of the targeted families are either poor or abject poor which affected their ability to access their basic needs of healthy and diverse food resulting in alarming levels of food insecurity. 24% of the targeted families have no access to any source of income and are totally dependent on aid, while almost 62% of families that have an access to income rely mainly on government jobs or irregular paid work in private sector as a source of income. Al Shoka and Al Moghraqa: are two of the most vulnerable localities in the Gaza Strip where most families are poor and food insecure. An assessment has been conducted to address the nutritional health situation for children under 5 years and the socio-economic vulnerabilities of the children’s families. The assessment has relied on capillary blood hemoglobin tests to check the health status of the targeted families’ children and the face-to-face interviews with the children’s caregivers/mothers to identify the socioeconomic characteristics. The assessment found that 51.4% of 821 screened children are anemic/malnourished due to poor food intake and unhealthy food habits. The deteriorated nutritional status in the surveyed localities is mainly attributed to poverty (95% of the families are considered as abject poor), limited access to food, inadequate food habits, deterioration of sanitary and environmental conditions and exposure to parasitic infestation. The assessment has also revealed that 81% of surveyed families have reported no access to any source of income and are totally dependent on aid. According to the assessment results, 44% of women and 14% of children were exposed to at least one type of violence by the time of the assessment due to harsh socioeconomic conditions. The bad socioeconomic situation and food insecurity have reflections on the social relations and affecting adversely the psychological wellbeing of women. Women have the responsibility of taking care of children, preparing and consuming food, where they expressed high concerns of children nutrition. Supporting the access to nutrient food is a priority for women to enjoy well-being and improve nutritional health situation for children. The assessment revealed that the most affected groups by the food insecurity are women (especially lactating, neonates and pregnant), children especially those under 5 years and elderlies. Women and elderlies sometimes reduce the no. of meals per day to make food available for other family members. Considering the recurrent crisis in Gaza, it has been clear that there is a need for a stronger focus on emergency preparedness to build resilient communities that are prepared to cope with emergencies, with specific focus to address their access to food and water.

Activities

Improved access to food: This activity will target 1000 vulnerable families. From IDP’s across Gaza strip as well as families of malnourished children in Al Shoka and Mughraqa. Food insecurity and poverty will be the main criteria for targeting. Female headed HH’s, families including children under five years, pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized. At least 12% of the targeted HH’s will be female headed. E voucher program and mobile technology systems will be adopted.Locations of the distribution points and times of distribution will be determined upon consultation with the beneficiaries, in particular women to ensure safe and accessible distribution points are identified. The diversity and content of vouchers will be identified in consultation with DCA/NCA nutrition partner, nutrition cluster and with beneficiaries, in particular women as they are responsible for cooking and are more aware of the family preferences and children needs. Voucher content will address needs of most vulnerable groups, in particular, lactating, neonates and pregnant women, the children under 5 years and elderly. Addressing emergency preparedness gaps of the partner CBOs through capacity building program: A set of gaps in the preparedness capacities of the community based structures were identified through an assessment conducted by DCA/NCA emergency experts. The intervention will improve the capacity of MAAN and 15 CBOs to promote CTP (E vouchers for food and water) as part of emergency response. An E-voucher system is currently in place in which MAAN, CBO’s emergency teams (more than 30% are women) and potential suppliers were trained on. MA’AN and DCA/NCA will conduct a simulation exercise to ensure the readiness of the emergency response system where all emergency response structures will participate in the simulation. The intervention has been coordinated with Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and Ministry of Agriculture. Further coordination with MoSD for beneficiary selection will be ensured. The project fits with the cluster response plan & complement other clusters’ actions.

(target: 100% of the targeted women) SO2: To improve the community-based humanitarian response capacity in Gaza. - 15 Partner CBOs, 90 members of community-based emergencies committees (38% Females, 62% males) and 250 volunteers (54% Females, 46% males) are trained on emergency humanitarian response to address gaps in their emergency preparedness. - 15 partner CBOs are provided with tools and inputs to enhance their emergency response capacities. - 1 emergency simulation exercise is implemented. The indicator measurement tools will include baseline/ end line surveys, records of receipts, vouchers, beneficiaries lists, monitoring field visits and perception surveys with beneficiaries. Onsite observation and monitoring of the distribution process through field representatives will be conducted for each distribution location ensuring the quality of the service. Results-based monitoring will be applied and the partners will keep clear project reporting to be shared with DCA-NCA by oral and written reports. Gender balance will be ensured in the composition of project staff to address the gender-sensitive aspects. A female monitoring staff will assist to collect information and monitor project implementation with women beneficiaries. An accessible complaints and feedback handling system will be developed in consultation with the community engagement committee. This will ensure the accountability and transparency of the provided aid As part of information sharing mechanisms, start-up/orientation workshops and community meetings will be conducted for the target group to introduce project Incorporating mobile data collection technology for data collection and monitoring will ensure effective project monitoring, quality management, and beneficiary accountability system for the project. DCA and MAAN are active members in the FSS cluster which is the main forums for coordination. Community engagement committee will be established in order to ensure effective and accountable participation of the target community in the project implementation and monitoring processes. The representation of various community groups in the committees will be ensured including women, youth representatives for PWD’s, children caregivers and elderlies. FGD’s and perception surveys will be conducted with the target group (including women, men, youth, elderlies and children caregivers) during the implementation phase to assess the quality and accessibility of the response provided and take the proper corrective measures as needed.
