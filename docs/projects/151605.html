Needs

One of the most pressing priority needs in Somalia is the provision of basic and life saving healthcare which up to now remains beyond the reach of many rural, urban areas, where Somalia remains among the worst affected countries due to complex emergencies and most protracted compared to the other countries throughout the world. Currently over 2.3 million people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 802,000 are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through December 2017, totalling over 3.1 million people who need urgent humanitarian assistance.Additionally, approximately 3.1 million people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people inneed of emergency food or livelihood support (IPC Phases 2, 3 and 4 combined) through the end of 2017 to 6.2 million. The Conflict, drought, flood and fluid displacements of the population. Access to life saving health services remains badly limited or disrupted due to insufficient health resources and lack of functioning health facilities results from lack of funding, shortage of medical supplies, conflict, displacements, droughts and floods. Frontline health care providers and key health partners accessible to most areas are only NGOs however due to their limited capacities, they are not commonly given priority in access to external funding, this has been a considerable negative factor limiting access to Needs health services among the most vulnerable populations in newly accessible areas, in underserved locations and hard to reach rural areas. The most common health risk in Somalia is a high prevalence of communicable diseases including acute diarrheal disease (AWD/Cholera), malaria (mainly after Gu and Deyr raining seasons) and measles according to Somalia health cluster bulletin on April Limited health infrastructure and low investment in the health sector of Somalia leaves the community exposed to health risks and results in ill-health therefore Direct Aid Society organization is currently running 3 MCHs in south central
