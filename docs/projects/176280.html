Project Summary

The project intends to deliver life saving static and mobile services to the most vulnerable men women, boys and girls affected by HLP issues attributed to conflict , displacement , economic woes and COVID 19. This comprises of returnees, Host community and internally displaced population ( men women, boys , girls , young people ,child headed households, female headed households and adolescents) living in Jonglei state ( Pibor county),Central Equatoria State ( Lainya and Morobo counties) and Western Bhar-El -Ghazal State ( Wau county ) The total target population is 20,000 most vulnerable individuals ( 1,000 girls, 1000 boys, 8000 women and 8000 men). The primary purpose of the project is to address compounding effects of converging factors related to land and property ownership i.e loss of livelihoods/land, displacements, protection threats and lack of services which contributes and aggravates HLP issues.This project will focus on both preventive and responsive aspects of HLP programming and work closely with GBV , FSL ,S/NFI WASH and Education partners to ensure an integrated approach for a holistic service delivery that entrenches resilience and sustainability of the services rendered to the affected population. The intervention will be guided by the IASC guidelines for HLP ,national SOPs and cluster strategy. The response is strategic to the attainment of Cluster objectives and align to activities, indicators, and outcomes of the HRP 2021.The project activities focus on establishment of legal clinics, strengthening Land dispute resolution mechanisms,legal awareness and counselling, case management , Cash based Intervention and integrated sectoral activities which contribute to mitigation of HLP issues
In addressing such issues and other Housing, Land and Property matters, CIDO will establish accountability to affected population Committees in all project locations, who will conduct meetings with Local authorities, Chiefs and Protection Partners to map existing and potential HLP issues in the proposed project locations. Thereafter, CIDO in close collaboration with Chiefs, AAP committees and other Protection partners will conduct HLP messaging while at the same time addressing existing and emerging HLP issues throughout project implementation in line with the cluster humanitarian shelter and land rights due diligence guidelines in South Sudan .
CIDO will conduct rapid HLP assessments with focus on the immediate shelter and protection needs of affected people and the potential risks they face, including their access to different shelter types, security of tenure, including risk of forced eviction. Assessments at this stage may also be able to get an overview of the level of damaged/destroyed HLP and any (secondary) occupation trends. This will define ways of engaging the local authority and raising awareness on HLP rights among crisis-affected populations, including the services available to support claiming such rights (e.g., legal assistance, dispute resolution and shelter assistance) and local rules and regulations on HLP matters (e.g., rental/lease agreements). CIDO will also consult with all groups within affected populations to identify most effective and appropriate methods of awareness-raising. CIDO will be guided by the cluster safety audit while mainstreaming conflict sensitivity and AAP.
CIDO is a national NGO which has 6 years experience of implementing humanitarian Projects in South Sudan. CIDO's has gained wide experience in implementing HLP activities attributed to the HLP mainstreaming aspect in all CIDO's Shelter and NFI activities implemented in Twic East. Bor, Pibor, Akobo counties of Jonglei State and in Yei, Morobo , Lainya counties of Central Equatoria state. CIDO is a Shelter and NFI cluster site focal point for Pibor county, Jonglei state and works closely with Protection partners to address HLP issues.

1 - Needs

According to Report produced by the shelter and NFI cluster ,Urban and peri-urban areas of South Sudan are witnessing an often systematic take-over of land and properties belonging to displaced populations by actors to the conflict. This also includes the unlawful transfer and sale of confiscated properties for profit. Likewise, many IDPs and returnees, for lack of other shelter options, are now occupying HLP left behind by those displaced with over 80% reporting to be involved in land disputes. Pibor county has witnessed multiple shocks which cause displacements ,arson attacks and damage to property. There is always difficulty in relocating IDPS because of land rights in this county.Similarily,Majority of houses and property in Morobo and Lainya counties were burnt destroyed and looted ,few that remained are occupied by IDPS and though the revitalized peace agreement was signed in August 2018 , uncertainty of the peace holding and Corona virus pandemic with its associated travel restrictions and lockdowns has not allowed refugees to return to South Sudan. The protection cluster reports of February 2021 indicates that the process of redesignating PoC sites into IDP camps, i.e. transferring responsibility from UNMISS to the Government of South Sudan, has created further risks associated with HLP issues, including issues regarding established IDP camps. Voluntary return and relocation for IDPs faces multiple issues connected to both HLP rights and wider security South Sudan’s customary authorities have been greatly weakened by the killings and disappearances of chiefs, and political appointments of customary leaders by the respective state leadership. In many areas, chiefs have been appointed to areas to which they do not have a prior connection and of which they have no prior knowledge limiting their capacity to resolve HLP issues.While land commercialization steadily increased prior to the conflict, resulting in significant surveying and demarcation efforts, 84% of all land in South Sudan still remains un-surveyed without formalized land titling. As such, the 2009 Land Act is widely irrelevant for day-to-day land management of land relations in the country. This is particularly the case in rural areas, where the state is often effectively absent.Majority of population still do not understand their housing, land and property rights and do not know where to seek redress in case of violation. Most vulnerable households cannot afford to take cases of land to court or even pay for a service of a legal counsel. Host communities have also seen a lot of unprocedural encouragement on their land by IDPS and also government . A shelter and NFI need analysis report of December 2020 conducted in Wau county indicates the need for awareness on HLP rights among beneficiaries.However, while evidence points to increasing acceptance of women’s rights to property, 2019 gender analysis research by CARE international shows that there has been little change with regards to their ability to independently obtain and exert tenure rights, both in rural and urban areas for widowed women. Despite equal rights to own land under statutory law, the majority of women continue to access holdings exclusively through a male relative as required by customary law.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During AAP sessions , CIDO team and committee on AAP and GBV will provide information on PSEA to all beneficiaries including how to identify and report PSEA cases. The beneficiaries will be made aware that vendors of CVI, CIDO contractors, CIDO staff and volunteers are all required to sign codes of conduct and commit to protect beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse. IEC materials on PSEA will be sourced from the Lead (UNFPA) and translated and distributed in project locations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CIDO will establish a Complaint and Feedback mechanism and establish Help desk during distributions. There will be Community communication and engagement (CCE) before the project begins to harmonise the expectations of the community in regards to the project activities vs the available services. During CCE , consultations will be made with communities to establish committees which will oversee the distribution, GBV issues, and AAP issues. The committee on AAP will ensure that voices of the most vulnerable are heard including people with disability who may be faced with access challenges. The committee will ensure that location of static services like legal clinics does not bar access to PWDs .The team will be careful to provide opportunity to people living with disability, women and girls to air their views through separate FGDs and ensure that they are included in various leadership roles like being part of the committees. Feedback from People with Disability will be used to tailor services to meet their most critical needs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) is committed to ensuring that frontline staff engage in timely and proper Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) while being guided by the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will conduct entry and exit meetings prior to the end of the project implementation to have discussions with not only community leaders but also directly with all targeted beneficiaries who are women, men girls and boys to capture their interests and recommendations and asses the vulnerability. In addition, CIDO will conduct awareness sessions on AAP rights and how to use the established Complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs). The AAP committee composed of community members representing men, women, elderly, youth , people with disability will be established to ensure communities rights and dignity is respected by all stakeholders.There will be an open-door policy, complaints, response, and feedback mechanism (CRFM) boxes, hotline and CRFM desks set up at each distribution site where beneficiaries will be given opportunity to air their feedback to the project team through using both leaders and open-door policy. The hotline will be linked directly to the management. This will facilitate Communication, transparency, and Community full engagement.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CIDO team will compile feedback from the commitees and beneficiaries and present to the management in Juba who will confirm the grievances and compliments . Feedback on how the complaints have been addressed on a daily basis on ground will also be shared with the management and will be used to define further actions to improve service delivery. There will be project review meetings where project staff and management will analyse the usefulness of the CRFM , information gathered and how the system can be made better to encourage all beneficiaries to use it freely. Feedback from beneficiaries will be used to improve programming and adjust weaker areas accordingly.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

CIDO team will coordinate with protection partners to ensure beneficiaries are aware of the services available and the referral system/pathways. Notably referral systems and frameworks contain vital information on Health, Psychosocial support/Mental Health, Legal, livelihood, safety etc and contacts of service providers. Joint activities will be carried out with protection partners in all project locations which will include safety audits, dissemination of referral pathways and outreach messages conducted at all distribution sites. CIDO staff will be trained on safe reporting of GBV cases and will be provided with GBV pocket guides for non-GBV actors to equip them with basic information for identifying and assisting GBV survivors.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This will be achieved through coordination with other sectoral partners to integrate disaster risk mitigation strategies known to cause displacements and aggravates food insecurity like conflict and floods. CIDO will coordinate capacity building activities on life-skills, coping strategies and durable solutions with partners while promoting actionable risk mitigation strategies like building flood control structures (dykes), conflict sensitivity and promoting peaceful co-existence among communities.In addition, CIDO will conduct joint outreach messages with other sectoral partners on community led disaster risk mitigation , early warning and response mechanisms and protection of livelihoods from loss.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The cluster , ICCG and OCHA encourages joint assessments with other partners and sectors and the cluster /project assessment tools capture the needs of beneficiaries based on gender, age and disability. CIDO will participate in Joint interventions with WASH, FSL, S/NFI and Nutrition to provide cross-sectoral basic services . The project team will use the existing referral pathways to refer beneficiaries to other sectoral services in circumstances where the services cannot be provided. In addition, the project will make use of gender analysis reports to adjust activities accordingly and meet the needs of both men and women, boys and girls in dignity and safety.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

• Tackling the compounding effects which exposes children to diseases and worsen malnutrition i.e lack of land for farming and loss of livelihoods which limits access to food security and thus usually progresses Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) to severe acute malnutrition (SAM). • CIDO team will make use of the referral system and ensure that malnourished children who accompany their mothers to legal clinics are referred appropriately for services.
