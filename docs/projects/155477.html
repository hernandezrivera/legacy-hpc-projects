Project Summary

GOAL ZW is currently operational in Chipinge and Mbare districts implementing a Livelihoods/Nutrition intervention and a Cholera response respectively. GOAL ZW will also re-enter Buhera district for a three- year livelihoods and nutrition program as of January 2019. These districts have been identified as being amongst the most vulnerable in the country in view of the predicted El Nino in 2019.
This action will complement GOAL’s ongoing activities in the targeted districts, by providing support to the Ministry of Health in the following areas;
- Implementation of active screening for early identification and referral of children with acute malnutrition through community-based health workers and community volunteers.
- Capacity building of facility-based health workers on treatment, management and reporting of acute malnutrition.
- Integrated refresher training for Village Health Workers (VHWs) and Community Volunteers on active screening through MUAC and odema testing, HIV prevention and treatment follow up, IYCF, ECD, child protection, micronutrients and positive parenting and Vitamin A supplementation.
The trained VHW/Community Volunteers will do the following:
- Screen 74,896 children in 3 districts for acute malnutrition.
- Reach 73,076 children in 3 districts with Vitamin A supplementation (VAS) and deworming
- Reach 22,836 children with micro nutrient powders
- Reach 58,200 caregivers in 3 districts of children < 5 years with integrated messages on nutrition, WASH, HIV, Child protection through social mobilisation activities.
- Establish/Support mother support groups in targeted districts continuation to the existing mother support groups in the 3 targeted districts, by reaching xxx caregivers with IYCF messages, messages on positive parenting practices and cooking demonstrations.
- Create linkages with the Food Security Sector for provision of supplementary feeding for PLW and children under 5 in targeted districts
In coordination with the WASH and food security cluster the action will provide Community IYCF-E support and counselling specifically where cash and/or food assistance intervention are provided
Early detection of Moderate Malnutrition cases will ensure avoidance of these cases deteriorating to Severe Acute Malnutrition.

Needs

The Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee assessment report of June 2018 reported a deterioration in the food security situation during the peak hunger period (January to March 2019). According to the report, over 1.1 million people are at risk of being food insecure during the peak hunger period. Adding to that, the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) which was conducted in May 2018 projected that over 2.4 million people will be food insecure during the peak hunger period and at least 1.7 million people are projected to be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). The country is likely to experience an increase in numbers of children with acute malnutrition resulting from food insecurity. The drought situation could be further exacerbated by the recent cholera outbreak and on-going poor macro-economic situation affecting access to basic food commodities. It is anticipated that 72,228 children could be affected by acute malnutrition (25,280 SAM and 46,948 MAM) in all 63 districts in the country between now and May 2019. In addition, 360,000 young children (0 to 23 months), 905,933 children aged 6 to 59 months and 575,384 pregnant and lactating women with elevated nutrition needs (or vulnerability) in 24 selected districts are likely to be immediately affected by the prevailing and up-coming food insecurity including through the looming El-Nino drought (90% likelihood according to the most recent SARCOF analysis), The targeted districts of Chipinge and Buhera of Manicaland Province and Harare are districts that have been identified in need of support for the upcoming El Nino season. According to the National Nutrition Survey conducted in 2018, Manicaland Province had the highest prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition at (0.53%) and the Minimum Acceptable Diet was at 4%. The ZIMVAC assessment conducted in 2018 also shows that Manicaland Province has the highest stunting rate in the country at 31.2%. In the urban areas the situation is as alarming, the Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Report (Urban) published in (2018) demonstrates that there is a looming food security crisis in Zimbabwe’s urban area. The assessment indicates that in the urban areas surveyed, 38% of the population is food insecure, Harare in particular was recorded as having a projected stunting rate for 2018 of 28.9%. GOAL ZW and World Vision conducted a vulnerability urban assessment in Harare in October 2018, data collected across the six locations (Mbare, Epworth, Tafara Mavuku, Highfields, Norton) showed an estimated 70% of households to be food insecure. With some locations having a higher proportions of food insecure households compared to others. The food insecure households ranged from 52% to 88% across the locations. Negative coping mechanisms vary from reducing the size and number of meals, engaging in casual work/ piece jobs, vending and hawking, urban farming, early marriages, selling household assets to buy food, begging, waste collection for recycling. Food prices have escalated in the past 3 months as a result of further dwindling of foreign currency, in October 2018, basic commodities have become beyond the reach of poor urban residents.Respondents stated that priority is being given to food purchases, thus neglecting health and education of their families. In Mbare District some families would forego payment of school fees thereby affecting regular school attendance by children leading to poor performance. Children are also going to school hungry due to lack of proper meals and this also affects their concentration in class. Key informant interviews held at some schools in Mbare highlighted increased cases of absenteeism as well as children dropping out of school because of the food shortages at home. The above challenges were further compounded by the cholera outbreak in September 2018 which has so far claimed 59 lives and 10,598 suspected cases of which over 90% are from Harare district.
