Project Summary

More than half of Somalis lack of access to adequate sanitation, with critical gaps in Lower Shabelle, Lower Juba, Gedo and Bakool and in most IDP settlements, resulting in widespread open defecation; Hand washing with soap is practiced by very few because of the limited availability of soap and functional handwashing points.
In South West State, the Gu’ rain season was delayed and where rains fell in Bay, Bakool and Lower Shabelle regions, they are mainly light showers and poorly distributed. According to the WASH cluster, the price of water in most areas of the state has almost tripled. For instance, in Baidoa, the cost of water per 200 litre barrel has increased to more than $3 up from $1 in normal conditions. In some cases, it is much higher if the distance from the water source is more. In Buur Hakaba, the price has gone up from 20,000 to 80,000 Somali Shillings for a 200 litre barrel in the same period. Increasing demand for water and long queues have been observed at many water points across the state. According to Ministry of Health (MOH), World Health Organisation (WHO) and health partners, a total of 415 cases of suspected of acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) and cholera have been reported from several health facilities in South West State since the beginning of 2019.
In the Banadir region, delayed Gu’ rains and the prolonged dry season led to increased water shortage for most IDPs. WASH partner agencies in Banadir region estimate that between 150,000 - 180,000 IDPs are facing acute water shortages. Camp elders stated that the cost of 20 litters of water had risen by approximately 50 percent i.e. from $0.50 to $0.75 for 20 liters, equivalent to households spending US$3 to US$4 per day on water. IDPs also spend an average 3 to 4 hours queuing to obtain water and sometimes fail to get the water as they get discouraged due to hunger. Between March and mid-April 2019, some 10,000 new IDPs (1,665 households) moved from Lower and Middle Shabelle regions to Banadir region largely due to the prolonged dry season, including armed conflict and military operations. They settled in Kahda, Hordan, Dharkenley, Waaberi, Wadajir, and Daynille districts of Banadir. The impact of drought is further compounded by insecurity that hampers the free movement of IDPs and limits their ability to engage in livelihood and income generating activities outside the camps. This is happening against the backdrop of prevalence of AWD/Cholera, high rates of malnutrition and increasing evictions.
Project objective is to improve access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene to the emergency affected populations living in Banadir, Lower Shebelle, Bay and Bakol regions of Somalia. This shall be achieved through the provision of access to safe water, adequate and appropriate sanitation facilities and services. The project also aims to ensure that the targeted IDP and Host mmunities have the necessary information, knowledge and understanding to prevent water and sanitation related diseases. The project shall promote the equitable participation of both men and women in the planning and decision making processes, this will ultimately help to ensure that the entire affected population has safe and easy access to water supply, sanitation facilities and services, and that such services and facilities are appropriate. Special focus shall be given to the needs of women and children who bear greater risk of SGBV in the use of communal sanitation facilities. The intervention shall support gender-sensitive participatory processes for operation, maintenance and management of the sanitation facilities and services. The project also seeks to promote hygiene by conducting hand washing demonstrations, and promote water treatment and storage practices.
The project intends to target a total of 12500 (6375 Men, 6125 Women, 6875 children) including 3000 IDPs

Project Activities

Supply of clean, safe and treated water through water construction of elevated water tanks in the IDP, camps. 6375 men, 6125 women, and 6875 children making up 12500 households shall have access to approximately 15 liters of safe drinking water per person per day for twelve months Water distribution point construction and rehabilitation; with the agreement of borehole owners; 11 boreholes shall be fitted with an appropriate solar pumping system to reduce maintenance and operating costs long-term. Distribution of 3,000 hygiene kits to the most vulnerable households; 6125 women and 6375 men targeted by Community hygiene outreach activities, implemented by 50 (men and women) selected and trained community hygiene promoters. Participatory methods of hygiene education shall be employed to enable targeted beneficiaries to develop the knowledge, attitudes, and skills required for them to adopt healthy lifestyles. The promotion of positive behavior towards general environmental cleanliness, hand-washing with soap (at least before eating and after using the latrine). The correct use of latrines, cleaning of latrines, collection and proper disposal of solid waste shall also be promoted. Selected Community Hygiene Promoters shall help to monitor and encourage proper hygiene practice, as well as provide positive role models by consistently demonstrating appropriate hygiene behavior Construction of latrines to serve the needs of 12500 men, women and children. Latrines shall be fitted with lockable doors; lockable from the inside to protect people while they are using them. An appropriate hand-washing point with soap, water, and adequate drainage shall be made available. Latrines will also be separated into males and females.
