Project Summary

The action strives to achieve durable solutions and contribute to peace building and social cohesion amongst displacement-affected populations, including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and host populations in the North West, South West, West and Littoral regions of Cameroon. To this end, the project will address underlying housing, land and property issues in an integrated manner including access to essential services to prevent disputes that arise over exploitation.
The programme will do so through an integrated, multi-sectorial approach comprising Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA), Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS), and Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WaSH).
The project is aimed at assisting crisis-affected populations to achieve increased security of tenure and enjoy HLP rights, including accees to property documentation. NRC and its local partners will enhance capacities of IDPs, returnees and host populations to prevent and address disputes linked to access to HLP, and work with communities to address social tensions. This will be undertaken through three specific interventions :
• Promotion of HLP rights of IDPs, returnees and host populations through information sessions that will cover peaceful co-existence and collaborative dispute resolution, and access to essential services such as markets, financial services and healthcare.
• Training of local and regional authorities and humanitarian actors, local partners and community leaders on social cohesion, HLP rights and CDR to enhance their capacity to protect HLP rights of crisis-affected populations.
• Setup of Community Peace and Social Cohesion Centres (CPSCC) to serve as a ICLA Centres and gathering points for nurturing relationships and distracting young people from engaging in violent behaviour.
More over, NRC will work with communities to develop social cohesion projects and support them to implement these projects. These projects include games and cultural events that will bring displaced persons and their hosts together during which ICLA teams will raise awareness of participants on HLP rights and collaborative disputes resolution. Risks of loosing properties through illegal sales, including sales or lease of land to more than one person will be mitigated for displacement-affected populatios through due diligence to ensure that transactions are carried out with rightful owners of the property. NRC will advocate for the HLP rights of conflict-affected populations to be respected. Round tables with relevant government officials and humanitarian actors at national and regional levels will be held with the objective of developing a plan of action to follow-up on the challenges and recommendations identified.
Moreover, the project will support the coordination of HLP interventions in the NWSW, Littoral and West Regions for quality and timely reponse to the affected populations.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In its sixth year, the socio-political crisis in the North West and South West has led to a massive displacement of populations and further increased community vulnerabilities compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic. The security context remains volatile, marked by continuous violence, abductions, kidnappings, killings, unlawful arrests, and destruction of property. This is aggravating humanitarian needs, as affected people continue to flee their homes, seeking safety in the bushes and neighbouring communities. In March 2022, the number of IDPs in the NWSW was estimated at 592,600 while 77,487 people from these two restive regions have moved to Nigeria as refugees . The fighting in these regions has also caused many to move to other regions considered to be safer, mainly the Littoral and West regions. The number of IDPs in the Littoral regions is estimated at 81,298 individuals while in the West region, there are 84,927 IDPs . Tribal conflict in Nigeria’s Taraba state between the Jukum and the Icheng ethnic groups and clashes between herders and farmers also led to the influx of more than 2500 refugees into Donga Mantung Division, North West Region . The rights of displacement-affected populations have been greatly affected as they face daily violations of their HLP rights from armed groups. Destruction of houses, land-grabbing, deprivation of inheritance (mostly affecting women and girls) and forced displacement leave many families without access to decent housing and farmland. Land grabbing is common and perpetrated by armed groups and some local authorities. Returnees struggle to recover their lands and houses upon return, as secondary occupation is a major preoccupation with no documents and weakened dispute resolution mechanism. Forced eviction from houses and lands is common due to the absence of valid documentation to protect rights, with this mostly affecting IDPs. According to multi-sectoral needs assessment carried out by NRC and partners in August 2021, the lack of access to arable land for displaced persons further increases their need for livelihood. While it is found that the majority ) 64%) of the people in these regions practice agriculture as a source of livelihood, only 57% actually have access to arable land with IDPs in West and Littoral regions mostly affected. IDPs face heightened discriminations when it concerns to land access, with some declaring that land was even taken away from them when their crops were ready for harvest. Over 63% of IDPs, 36% of returnees and 27% of host community members declared having access to rented land, though with most of them facing eviction risks as tenancy agreements are informal thereby leaving the tenants with insecure tenure. Moreover, recurrent tensions between IDPs and host populations, and between local farmers and breeders over natural resources and access to essential services have contributed to affecting the enjoyment of HLP rights. An HLP research carried out by NRC in the NWSW in December 2020 finds that the inability of conflict-affected populations to access their HLP rights shapes decision-making about returning to IDPs’ place of origin, and, in some cases, prevents successful return.Through this project, NRC seeks to address the challenges and ensure that persons affected by displacement can claim and exercise their HLP rights and achieve durable solutions.
