Project Summary

As a result of the continues crashes between Government forces and Opposition forces in Wau payam of Ayod county, Yuai of Uror County and sequence influx of IDPs from Unity State to Fangak County of Jonglei State. This positioned Jonglei State often as more vulnerable due to its exposure both to natural and man-man disaster like flood and displacements from nearby states, tribal attacks and intensity of conflict between warring parties. This state has been always experiencing a massive displacement of people, killing of children, women, elderly and loss of livestock and property, including burning of houses. An estimated 51,000 individuals from Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) households are quarterly displaced 24,000 individuals Returnees from Bor PoC, Malakal PoC, UN House PoC, Ethiopia and Sudan making a total of 75,000 individuals, who were both displaced and returnees. These IDPs and returnees currently settled in Old Fangak, new Fangak, Juaibor, Paguir, Keew, Toch, Mareang, Nyadin and Diel villages of Fangak County, Other settled in Guwai, Nor Manyang, Jiech, Pagil, Wiechdeng, Wichdieng, Mugok, Riang, Guduk, Bilgak and Thanyni villages of Ayod county. Many more settled in Motot, Peiri, Pulchol, Karam, Pathai, Wechjal, Wechwal, Walgak and Motdit villages of Uror County of Jonglei State. However, several joint assessment were conducted from 20th June 2018 to 20th October 2018, IDPs reported lack of essential NFIs service. Other IDPs reported health related issues, especially diarrhea, vomiting, skin diseases, and fever, especially amongst children. IDPs also sleep on Kangas and shawls, with about ten or more individuals sharing with the host community. Many children who were attending school are now out of school in the locations where IDPs have settled. Children are also at risk of neglect, child labour, gender based violence (GBV), recruitment into armed groups, forced marriage (for girls) and family separation especially after going through the violence when fighting broke out. There is no markets in the villages where IDPs are currently located. In regard to the above needs, there is urgent need for an emergency humanitarian response to 30,000 individuals from the displaced communities in shelter and Non-food Items. This project therefore aims at providing an Integrated Emergency response for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) through Non-Food Items (NFIs) in Fangak, Ayod and Uror county of Jonglei State. This project will not only target the IDPs in Old Fangak, new Fangak, Juaibor, Paguir, Keew, Toch, Mareang, Nyadin and Diel villages of Fangak County and Gowai, Nor Manyang, Jiech, Pagil, Wiechdeng, Wichdieng, Mugok, Riang, Guduk, Bilgak and Thanyni villages of Ayod county. Motot, Peiri, Pulchol, Karam, Pathai, Wechjal, Wechwal, Walgak and Motdit villages of Uror County. ADA will expand it operation into more “hard to reach location” in greater Jonglei this may include Haat, Menime of Ayod county,Pigi, Canal, Korfulus of Fangak county and more locations in greater Akobo county of Jonglei State.The project would aims at responding to the current emergency situation in hard to reach areas of Fangak, Ayod and Uror county of Jonglei state.

Needs

Jonglei State is often more vulnerable due to it exposure both to natural and man-man disaster like flood and displacements from nearby states, tribal attacks and intensity of conflict between warring parties. According to an inter-agency assessment done in Jonglei state in August, 2018 where ADA participated. The report revealed that 5,000 Households or about 30,000 Individual IDPs are in dire need of emergency shelter and NFIs. Most of these IDPs explained their feared for the ongoing hostilities around and within Unity state which made them more vulnerable, as they had no access to food and essential NFIs. An serious alarm was seen during the need assessments; lack of basic services was observed. numerous of joint mission were conducted by different inter-agency and a significant number of the IDPs population without any shelter and basic NFIs is observed. An slight to no access to these items in local markets and the lack of a basic household income or livelihoods means purchasing these is unfeasible. This observed lack of basic necessities is a clear indicator that both Fangak, Ayod and Uror are pre- remote and challenging areas. ADA aims to strengthen resilience copying mechanism to the affected population through training and provision of lifesaving assistance. Since many IDPs are those that have suffered multiple displacements. In 2019, ADA will prioritise those with such multiple displacement this includes female headed households, people with disabilities, households with children under 5, pregnant and lactating women, elderly people and children with protection concerns including child headed households. This project will, provide blankets, mosquito nets, sleeping mats, and kitchen set (saucepan, spoons, plate, cups and ladle) to the IDPs. Given the average household size of six individuals per household, two blankets, two mosquito nets, two sleeping mats and one kitchen set will be distributed per household. Furthermore, few IDPs were observed relying on the support from host community.The permanent presence of ADA on the ground would significantly support mobile response partners in coordination, monitoring and lay down the overview context of areas far betterment of other partners. During the assessment due to the lack of humanitarian action in the location boreholes are often in a state of disrepair and ADA would refer cases related to such to the relevant humanitarian actors through essential recommendation on the assessment report. The provision of training in a number of areas of protection mainstreaming is required to ensure the communities are participating in their own protection and are supporting humanitarian action in their location. The targeted locations are difficult to access and transport issues are prevalent. The lack of roads and transport via boat provides many challenges to the community in accessing basic services where they are even available. The intervention aims to contribute in improving a protective environment of vulnerable and conflict affected women, men, boys and girls among the IDPs, returnees and host community population through distribution of life –saving emergency shelter and NFIs including provision of survival kits to on-the-move IDPs while observing the “do no harm” humanitarian principle. The ADA would lay-down other strategies to deploy a integrated team composed of protection, WASH and NFIs staff to carry out standard need and fact assessment.
