Project Summary

The intervention aims to support vulnerable agriculture-based livelihoods, mainlyin Area C, to provide more sustainable access to food while working with farmers from these families to build their resilience, and coping mechanism and enhance the productivity of agricultural and herding assets for vulnerable communities in the south Hebron Governorate, namely; the governorate of Yatta. In parallel, the intervention aims to strengthen ability of acutely vulnerable Palestinian households, particularly female headed households, across fourteen targeted areas to cope with protracted threats and shocks.
The intervention will achieve this through a multi- pronged approach to supporting the steadfastness, protection and resilience of vulnerable families livelihoods mainly through: (i) creation of project support committees to ensure transparency, accountability and engagement in the monitoring of the project and selection of beneficiaries. Particularly the PSC will report on violence incidents and advise for timely and effective implementation ensuring “Do no harm” principles. (ii) Contribute to restoring and enhancing the productive capacity of vulnerable and affected HH by protecting and promoting their livelihood and assets through rehabilitation of agricultural lands through cash transfer modalities including cash for work. (iii) rehabilitation and provision of basic assets, and increasing access to resources through rehabilitation of agricultural roads , rehabilitation of water storage and livestock shelters. Moreover, the intervention will prioritize identification of and response to gender-based vulnerabilities in order to support the eroded coping capacities of female- headed households in particular.
The range of interventions outlined in activities and outputs below reflect specific community needs and align with two HRP Country Strategic Objectives. Beyond expanding access to basic resources for which the occupying power and other authorities have failed to adequately assume responsibility (CSO2), the project emboldens highly vulnerable Palestinian communities to cope with shocks including demolitions carried out by Israeli forces and damages to land caused by settler violence, (CSO3). Targeted communities are located in a highly contested zone, where communities live between multiple settlements, settlers’ cultivated agriculture land, and Israeli-controlled nature reserves.
Uncultivated land is a major attraction to settlers’ activities, which in many cases lead to confrontations with local communities over land and water resources. Developing and maintaining access to food-productive assets is of paramount importance to these communities. Availability of agriculture roads leading to farm lands means secure access to highly-needed food and water resources. Rehabilitation of farm lands and animal shelters provides communities with higher levels of resilience and allow them to withstand attacks by Israeli forces and settlers. The intervention further contributes the FSS cluster key objectives (FSSO2) & (FSSO3) through by improving access to, and the productive potential of various resources, and by improving productivity of farmers agricultural lands and water sources, rehabilitation of agricultural roads and rehabilitation of livestock sheds.

Needs

A needs assessment was conducted by MA’AN in Sep 2018 covering 22 South Hebron communities of: Khashem al Karem, An Najada, Mantiqat Shi’b al Butum, Qawawis, Khirbet at Tabban, Khirbet al Majaz, Maghayer al Abeed, Khirbet al Fakheit, Saadet Tha’lah, Isfey al Fauqa, Isfey al Tihta, Dkaika, Jinba, Mirkez, Halaweh, Tuba, Umm al Kheir, Azzuwedien, Khirbet Ghuwein, Khirbet ar Ratheem, Wedadie, Zif, Khirbet al Kharab; revealing that communities suffer from increased levels of Israeli forces and settlers violence and food insecurity. The assessment recommended the need to rehabilitate productive assets, provide agricultural production inputs, and enhance the resilience of agricultural livelihoods. The most recent assessment was conducted by MA’AN, LRC in consultation with the MoA, village councils, and PA directorates in Yatta. The assessment confirmed the aforementioned needs in the assessed communities impacting 794 farming and herding households demonstrating a need for the rehabilitation of 49 cisterns, 1390 dunums of agricultural land, 40 km of agricultural roads, and 556 livestock shelters and shed. Findings showed the average HH lives on less than 571 USD/month and spends more than half of their income on food and medicine. The majority are herders earning fluctuating income. Isolated by restrictions on their development and subjected to violence, the communities suffer from limited access to water, their grazing areas and demonstrate an inability to maintain livestock shelter. Israeli forces continued to impose sanctions and restrictions on the targeted communities, and in some cases, inflected direct damage to land and water resources. In 2017, the village of Khashem al Karem had their agricultural cistern demolished by Israeli authorities, and in January, the Karmel settlement extended their sewage network for runoff to flow to Um al Kheir farming lands, resulting in vast land degradation.Broadly, many farmers are unable to cover the running cost of raising their animals, and occasionally, they sell productive ewes to purchase fodders or household living commodities. Herders often fail to maintain their animal sheds, relying on plastic sheeting for inadequate protection for the livestock in general and newborns in particular. Farmers also suffer from limited access to agricultural lands and the high prices of agricultural inputs, especially the seeds of field crops and fodder to feed livestock as financial resources previously dedicated to these inputs are becoming increasingly depleted to cover internal HHs' needs.Low precipitation in recent years have greatly impacted agriculture and herding-dependent livelihoods. Coupled with unequal water regime and restrictions, this has caused Palestinian farmers to switch from irrigated to rain fed agriculture, collecting rainwater and purchased water in cisterns that are in great need of rehabilitation. The needs of women were determined through separate interviews and analysis. Results indicate that women are disproportionately affected by violence, poverty, and food insecurity, having to cope with the responsibility of supporting the HH socially and financially. The project will enhance the personal security of targeted females by providing secure access to land and water resources through rehabilitation of safe agriculture roads, rehabilitation of nearby water cisterns eliminating the need for females to walk through uncharted terrains to access water and other food inputs exposing them to Israeli settlers’ violence. The project will also enhance food security conditions for the entire family; pregnant women and those nursing small babies will benefit from improved HH income as a result of the project. Some activities like the animal shelter rehabilitation will especially benefit women who typically conduct the milking and cleaning of sheep pens. Moreover, targeted HHs will be able to produce more food for consumption, and will get to store food instead of purchasing it.

Activities

The project team will supervise the public outreach in the targeted communities. Local project committees will be formed and include representatives of the communities and relevant stakeholders including the MoA who will participate in the application process. Women of these communities will play an integral part in the selection of beneficiaries through participation in the application selection process. A comprehensive analysis of the candidates will be conducted to select final beneficiaries according to the objective and pre-arranged set of criteria. The criteria will focus on the socio economic conditions of the households, shelter conditions and the gender makeup of the households. The number of females in the household will also be an important part of the application process as women headed households are disproportionately affected by poverty and food insecurity, such as those in the selected villages in Hebron. Female headed households will be a priority during the selection of beneficiaries. Further, the project will put a complaint mechanism in place to ensure that any complaints or concerns will be properly addressed during the life of the project. Output 1: 780 farmers and herders benefit from Improved productivity of 300 dunums (900 3 yrs) of uncultivated and under cultivated agricultural lands Activity 1.1: Rehabilitation of Agricultural lands including uncultivated land and planting with local varieties of cereal crops wheat, barley, and other seasonal crops. Thus, increasing access to additional crops for consumption and food fodder supply, decreasing their reliance on purchasing some major crops and decreased fodder bill and reducing poor coping mechanisms for maintenance of livestock size. The activity will be implemented through a conditional and restricted Cash for Work modality with each of the selected farmers. In the cases that this is not possible, MA’AN and LRC will lead the rehabilitation of lands on a case to case basis. Output 2: 700 farmers and herders benefit from expanded access to shrinking agricultural lands with 8 km rehabilitated agricultural roads (24 3yrs)Activity 2.1: Rehabilitation of 8 km of agricultural roads to decrease the transportation cost, vehicle maintenance, and frequency of visiting the land. Output 3: Increased availability of rainwater harvesting for agricultural uses through rehabilitation of 8 cisterns (24 3yrs) with an avg total quantity of harvested water around 1200 m3.Activity 3.1: Rehabilitate 8 cisterns to enhance water availability for farming. Will allow farmers to have enough water stored throughout the year for planting different crops. Additionally, the cisterns will provide additional water for livestock use and will decrease the water bill for the farmers. Output 4: 52 HH benefit from Enhanced protection of main source of livelihood through rehabilitation of 52 livestock sheds (156 3yrs) Activity 4.1 Rehabilitate/maintenance of 52 livestock shelters to reduce disease and mortality due to exposure to severe weather conditions to decreased mortality among the livestock and increase protection of women responsible for the tending to the animals. Taking advantage of the 3 year response plan, the project will work in 22 of the assessed communities based on the individual needs of each of the selected farmers/herders. The same activities will be implemented throughout the three years in the sixteen communities however the intervention per community will be phased out based on the majority needs per community. This will allow for addressing a majority of identified needs in these communities by both MA’AN and LRC and based on the communities most pressing priorities.

Indicators

MA’ANs M&E unit be in charge of gathering and analyzing all monitoring data in order to provide the following information products, in addition to measuring progress against indicators and provide clusters and OCHA with all data requested.The project will ensure that women participate and benefit from the project planning and implementation to maximize gender equality. Seventeen female members from targeted communities have already participated in the needs assessment that was conducted, and these women helped to highlight the challenges that their communities are facing. A project committee to be established at the project outset will ensure women’s representation and participation. Moreover, the needs of female headed households will be prioritized in all activities because they are especially vulnerable in targeted communities. Some of the female headed households, depending on their needs, may be targeted with more than one activity (i.e. land rehabilitation and sheep pen maintenance). Some activities will be especially beneficial and will make life easier for women. For example, the animal shelter maintenance will make milking processes and cleaning the shelter easier for women, who regularly perform these tasks. Gender disaggregation will be ensured in the selection of beneficiaries, and extra points will be given to female headed households and families with larger number of females. All activities will be designed and implemented so that men and women have equal access and participation with the participation of gender balanced committees representing each community. The following indicators will serve as part of the project MEAL framework in addition to the FSS cluster objectives and activities depended indicators: # female headed households benefiting from activities # of female headed households who report positive changes in their daily routine, such as more time to do other tasks, less exhaustion when tending to livestock, etc. # of female headed households receiving animal shelter rehabilitation.% of women engaged in the decision-making process of land/seed management# of females trained on key skills in disease control and farm management The benefits to these groups will be determined through MEAL activities for the project including PSC ( project support committees) meetings, focus groups, surveys, complain mechanism, details of beneficiary applications for land rehabilitation , and selection matrix data, and the findings of the rapid technical assessments, and endline assessment. Futheremore, Ma’an will ensure that female staff are present in the field and leading sessions with females during meetings and focus groups to identify protection barriers to the daily work and transparent expression of their needs. Additional indicators under the guidance of FSS, specifically utilizing the Coping Strategy Index ( CSI) will potentially be adopted upon availability of resources and consolidation of good practices by the FSS Cluster.
