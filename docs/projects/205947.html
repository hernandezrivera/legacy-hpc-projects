Project Summary

According to the Health cluster inputs for the South Sudan Humanitarian response plan, about 8.9 million people in the country need one or more forms of humanitarian assistance, especially in the counties most affected by floods, conflicts, IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons), returnees and disease outbreaks. Additional shocks levied by the COVID-19 pandemic and on-going epidemic disease outbreaks including measles, cholera and Hepatitis E have further stretched existing coping mechanisms and increased vulnerabilities. IOM's DTM have reported a mixed population movement owing to increasing returns of IDPs and refugees in some of the counties including Raja, Morobo and Kajokeji counties. Health service delivery for vulnerable populations remains heavily reliant on humanitarian assistance where populations remain displaced in IDP camps or far off in hard-to-reach locations due to flooding. In 2023, IOM provided life-saving health and MHPSS support to vulnerable IDPs and host community in Abyei, Wau, Rubkhona and Malakal counties and through deployment of 9 Rapid Response Missions to crisis affected locations of Abyei, Twic, Ezo, Nagero,Raja, Renk, Yei, Morobo, Kajokeji. IOM specifically responded to the Sudan crisis by reinforcing its existing operations at the points of entries in Wau (Wau Airport), Juba (Juba International Airport), Rubkona (Panakuach PoE and Roriak PHCU), Renk( Wunthou PoE, Wunthou PHCU and Abayok PHCU) and in Malakal (Bulkat and Malakal Airport) to support South Sudanese returneses with emergency medical care, pre-departure medical screening and to provide referral services to available health facilities for patients requiring secondary level medical care. IOM specifically stepped-up vaccination at PoEs in response to on-going measles outbreaks in Sudan and continued to support health cluster efforts to prevent, detect and respond to various public health threats and emergencies at the respective PoEs.
IOM’s HRP health project for 2024 aligns with the 3 health cluster strategic objectives to Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services, reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms targeting a total of 865,237 beneficiaries including 129,786 persons with disabilities so as to continue to provide integrated emergency life-saving primary health care, including outpatient consultations, routine immunization, nutrition screening for children under five years, sexual reproductive health services, clinical case management of sGBV survivors including psychosocial support while increasing surveillance and response to disease outbreaks to underserved and conflict-affected IDPs, returnees, migrants/travelers, and host communities.
IOM will provide health services through 17 static and 7 mobile clinics and 12 additional health facilities through 6 Rapid Response mission deployments ensuring to maintain its comparative advantage in rapidly deploying rapid response teams to address emerging health needs throughout the country. MHPSS services will be provided through existing recreational and counselling centers.
IOM will focus on leveraging existing community systems for sustainable solutions and strengthening both community-based health initiatives and mental health and psychosocial support. Therefore, IOM will provide direct service delivery in partnership with the respective county health departments, while continuing to explore options of partnerships with local NGOs and CBOs towards realizing the localization agenda of the health cluster.

1. Needs

The current health status of the population is poor and characterized by high malaria morbidity and mortality, with 50.2% of cases and 39.4% of deaths attributed to malaria; low measles vaccination coverage, with only 66% of children vaccinated and measles outbreaks reported since December 2022; cholera outbreak in Malakal; Hepatitis E in Bentiu IDP (Internally Displaced Persons) sites, Western Bahr El Ghazal and Jonglei states (Draft 2024 Health cluster HNO).Access to primary health care (PHC) services continued to be unavailable for a large majority of South Sudanese, with only 56 percent of the population able to access health facilities in an hour or less while Only 12% of health facilities in South Sudan are providing the full package of BPHNS, whilst 53% are moderately functional, with many poorly equipped and staffed, with humanitarian and development partners responsible for provision of support for a significant proportion of healthcare services in the country. Prevailing health system challenges are exacerbated by a lack of safe water supplies, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management, as well as widespread malnutrition, and poor infrastructure to procure, transport, and store essential pharmaceutical and vaccines. As a result, South Sudan continues to have some of the worst health outcome indicators globally, including outbreaks of measles and polio, high instances of maternal and under- five mortality, and extremely low immunization coverage. The psychosocial consequences for displaced and conflict-affected populations – such as socio-economic hardship, disrupted community structures and lack of social support, as well as elevated levels of psychological distress and issues such as developed anxiety, depression and trauma – can lead to an increase in conflicts and violence at family and community level, alcohol and substance use, and other harmful coping mechanisms, and the development of suicidal thoughts and behaviors. According to 2020 data from the Ministry of Health outpatient department (OPD), rates of mental health attendance is extremely high; 272 per 100,000, compared to the high-income country average of 44 per100,000. The prevalence of clinical conditions such as depression is also high, ranging from 36 to up-to 50 per cent of the population. High rates of depression were concerned in 2021, as IOM rolled out the WHO Well-Being Index (WHO-5 WBI) to assess the psychosocial wellbeing of IOM MHPSS beneficiaries in IDP camps in Unity, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Upper Nile where 90 per cent scored below the ‘red ag’ threshold, whereupon it is recommended to screen for depression. Despite significant needs, qualified mental health capacity in South Sudan is extremely scarce, with only three psychiatrists, two nurses, two clinicians and 24 non-clinical psychologists operating at governmental facilities. There are only 12 beds for mental health patients available at Juba Teaching Hospital, and no psychiatric hospital elsewhere in the country. The source of information is the directorate of mental health at Ministry of Health. Assessments conducted by IOM in Bentiu, Wau, and Malakal from 2015 to 2021 indicate persistent experiences of cumulative distress, grieving of multiple losses, accumulative stress, loss of self-esteem, and fatigue and persisting trauma. Negative coping mechanisms such as abuse of alcohol, withdrawal and isolation, or aggressive behaviors are prevailing, further aggravated by negative implications of COVID-19, which had an additional negative impact on the level of distress observed in the general population and particularly on persons living in IDP sites, at which preventative actions such as physical distancing and regular handwashing presented a challenge.

2. Project activities

IOM’s health interventions will provide a comprehensive package of primary healthcare through 17 static and 7 mobile clinics while maintaining its comparative advantage in rapidly deploying Rapid Response Teams throughout the country and strengthening its community health response through a network of community health workers to provide integrated community case management of childhood illnesses alongside providing health promotion and community-based surveillance services. These efforts are implemented in coordination with the community leadership within the IDP camps and with the Boma Health workers in locations outside the IDP camps. In line with the priority for integrated healthcare, IOM will continue to provide focused, non-specialized, community-based, remote and mobile MHPSS services, in need across the country, including counselling for mild and moderate case and refer the severe cases to appropriate actors; support group discussions, reinforcement of capacities to community stakeholders on various MHPSS issues, and care for caretakers, to be supplemented through coordination of MHPSS Technical Working Groups at the national and sub-national level. IOM will continue its health response within IDP camp in Bentiu Sector 3 Primary Healthcare clinic and MHPSS center, while conducting mobile outreach to Dingding, Yoangyang, Roriak, Pakur and Budang (Rubkona). In Malakal IOM will continue to provide comprehensive Primary Healthcare and MHPSS services at the IOM Malakal Primary Health center (PHCC) in Malakal POC and at Malakia PHCC within Malakal town while in Wau IOM will continue to support 7 static health facilities within Wau town and in the locations of Bazia, Baggari and Beselia outside Wau town. IOM will respond in counties of Raja, Morobo and Kajokeji, where there are mixed displacements and returns. Deployment of country-wide integrated Emergency Rapid Response Teams and response to disease outbreaks and other acute public health emergencies: IOM will maintain a surge team of emergency responders within its regular staff for rapid deployment nationally, as part of the Health Cluster Rapid Emergency Response Missions, and in close coordination with national and sub-national health cluster, SMoH and County Health Departments (CHDs). IOM will ensure integration of health, nutrition and WASH into its programming through coordination with respective clusters and partners within locations of IOM's implementation. Additional focus will be to strengthen IOM's response at the various points of entries (PoEs) within the country so as to strengthen its capacity to prevent, detect and respond to any diseases of public health concern along the borders and to provide medical assistance to returnees and migrants through its Migration Health Assistance programes. Activities: Preventive and curative health services for common morbidities; Surveillance, early warning, alert and response to epidemic prone diseases; Routine immunization for children; Nutrition screening; Sexual and reproductive health services; Basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care services; antenatal and postnatal care, PMTCT, family planning services; Clinical management of rape; HIV and TB testing and treatment; Integrated MHPSS through focused services in recreational and counselling centres associated with IOM clinics and via tele-communication; Non- communicable disease management; Emergency reactive mass vaccination campaigns and case management; Improving WASH in health facilities; Health promotion and development of key messages in collaboration with MHPSS teams; and referral to secondary level of care; Training for health personnel, from both the Ministry of Health (MoH) and national NGOs; Capacity building for MHPSS mobile teams, for social worker trainees of the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare; MHPSS mainstreaming for health workers, protection actors, and (I)NGOs; Coordination of MHPSS Technical Working Groups.
