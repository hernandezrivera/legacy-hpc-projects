Project Summary

Save a Life International (SLI) seeks to improve access to safe, lifesaving and appropriate S/NFIs to returnees, newly IDPs, and affected non displaced persons in Northern Bahr El Ghazal counties of Aweil Center, Aweil East, Aweil West, Aweil South and Aweil North. The response will seek to target 3623 households (1623 returnees, 1000 IDPs and 1000 host community). The assistance will be in the form of Plastic Sheet, blankets, rubber ropes, mosquito nets, Sleeping Mat, cooking set, kanga, NFIs bag and solar lamp. The project will equip beneficiaries with skills and knowledge on disaster risk reduction to enhance their resilience to cope with new shocks. SLI will also support affected communities with durable S/NFIs solutions to rebuild their lives including training women and girls on making Fuel Efficient Stoves (FES) and flood resilient shelters. Vulnerable youth will be trained on making bricks, beds, chairs and tables using local materials. S/NFIs Solutions training will be complemented with environment awareness to mitigate negative effects of climate change. The project will adopt Cash Based Intervention in areas with functional markets and general in-kind distributions in locations without markets. GIK will target locations with new displacements and no functional markets. Due to high level and concentration of S/NFIs needs in these locations, SLI project staffs will dedicate 8 weeks in each location with the communities to better understand their pressing S/NFIs needs and coping mechanisms, jointly craft alternative S/NFIs durable solutions, train community-based S/NFIs Committees on resilience- enhancing messaging such as Housing Land and Property (HLP). The project prioritizes safety, dignity, inclusive participation and encouraging complaints and feedback. A key focus for this intervention is AAP through CFM embedded in project activities. CFM will be established upon contact with the affected community and during implementation process to receive, acknowledge and address complaints. Protection is mainstreamed through HLP Messaging, safety audits, sharing GBV information and referrals, training women and girls on FES and engaging protection partners while implementing CBI activities to mitigate potential violence as a result of CBI.SLI will liaise with the SFPs and NFIs partners to coordinate the response, share experience and ideas for better and cost-effective response. To integrate the response, SLI work closely with CCM, FSL, Nutrition, Health, Gender Based Violence, Wash and Protection partners and SFPs based in Aweil and Juba to avoid duplication of activities, assistance and efforts. Save a Life International has been actively engaged in addressing shelter and non-food item (SNFI) needs in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBEG), collaborating with various partners and the cluster at both the state and national levels. As a cluster state focal point, Save a Life has developed a deep understanding of the specific SNFI needs in the region, allowing the organization to tailor its response effectively. SNFI efforts are focused on coordinating resources, ensuring timely distribution of aid, and improving the living conditions of vulnerable communities in the region, in close collaboration with other humanitarian actors.

1. Needs

The heavy rains in August and early September resulted in flooding that have damaged homes, critical infrastructure, and crop fields; displaced over 102,000 people and disrupted livelihoods; and disrupted health services, increasing the risk of disease outbreaks in NBEG. Of particular concern are the low-lying and flood-prone areas where rising waters have uprooted families from their homes. Affected counties in the state include Aweil South: 37,500; Aweil East: 53,100; Aweil North: 5,100; Aweil West: 4,900 and Aweil Center: 1,400 people – where large populations are displaced, crop lands submerged, and road are becoming increasingly impassable. The ongoing war in Sudan has continued to displace more people into South Sudan, adding to the 2 million protracted internally displaced people and the newly flood-displaced. As of September 9, nearly 59,586 returnees and refugee have crossed into NBEG. The economic conditions have remained very severe in September with rising cost of living and significant decline in household purchasing capacity. The SSP depreciation has reached a record-high in September, trading at 5,000 SSP per USD, losing 383 percent of its value compared to same time in 2023 on the parallel markets and nearly 200 percent on the official market. As such the cost of living as expressed by cost of minimum expenditure basket (CMEB) in August 2024 were 43,156, and 173 percent higher than in February 2024. The situation is expected to worsen in the last quarter of 2024 due to prevailing floods that have affected supply routes and functionality of markets. Food insecurity in NBEG remained acute in the third quarter of 2024. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes remained widespread in the state with all the five counties remaining in IPC4 (emergency) or worse food insecurity. Emergency (IPC4) outcomes are likely to persist among returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities. During floods and conflicts, the IDPs and returnees fled their homes in a hurry and left behind basic household items, clothes and food. As a result, the returnees and IDPs lack cooking or serving utensils and are forced to share the little they have with their neighbors leading to delayed meals preparation. According to the IRNA reports conducted in Aweil counties, lactating and pregnant women, children and elderly persons are sleeping on hard grounds and sleep with no blankets and mosquito nets. As a result, numerous incidences of respiratory infections and back pains were reported among IDPs who were sleeping on the ground. Incidences of malaria infections were also on the rise among the IDPs due to lack of mosquito nets. Sleeping under trees, in open spaces, abandoned shelters, churches and other abandoned buildings is the norm in locations where the IDPs are settled. Women, girls and children were seen living in crowded or close spaces with men, exposing them to Gender Based Violence Risks. To ensure SNFI outcomes, conditional and restricted cash assistance will be strategically integrated into the project. Conditional cash will be linked to specific shelter-related tasks, such as repair or construction, with cash disbursements contingent upon completion of these actions. Restricted cash will be provided through vouchers or digital transfers, limited to approved shelter materials or services, ensuring funds are spent solely on SNFI needs. Beneficiaries will be trained on proper use, and progress will be monitored to ensure effective use of funds, aligning cash assistance with tangible shelter improvements for vulnerable households.As a result of these glaring humanitarian needs and vulnerabilities faced by the returnees, IDPs and host communities, SLI is proposing a SNFIs response aimed at improving access to safe and appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving non-food items to returnees, newly displaced and vulnerable host community in NBEG. The response will seek to target 3623 households.

2. Project activities

1.0: Inception and end-of-project meetings will be held with returnees, IDPs, host community representatives, RRC, and local authorities. These meetings aim to provide project information, including objectives, assistance types, and the role of affected people in implementation, while encouraging support for monitoring and evaluation. Save a Life International will form project committees of 15 members (8 females) in each county to oversee the project, assist with beneficiary selection, distribution, mobilizers, enumerators, and address complaints. A one-day training will be conducted for PIC members across the five target counties to familiarize them with implementation methods, criteria, and reporting.2.0: Selecting and training of 55 locally hired enumerators (26 Males/29 Females)Save a Life International will collaborate closely with the RRC and Project Implementation Committee to select, train and support eleven (11) locally enumerators in each of the target counties who will assist in mobilization of affected communities, selection of beneficiaries, distribution of S-NFIs, post distribution monitoring and project evaluation. The enumerators will be residents and able to communicate in English, Arabic, and local dialect. To ensure “Do No Harm”, enumerators will be oriented on SLI’s Code of Conduct, PSEA, Conflict of Interest, GBV, Child Protection, conflict and cultural sensitivity prior to being deployed. 4.0: Identification and registration of beneficiaries for NFIsSave a Life International through county level Project Implementation Committee members and enumerators will conduct identification, registration, and selection of beneficiaries (IDPs, host communities and returnees) using a pre-agreed selection criterion. 5.0. Collection of NFIs items from Core Pipeline The SNFIs will be collected from Wau Core pipeline and transported by road to Aweil Center, for onward distribution to Aweil West, Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South. Requested items will include Plastic Sheet, blankets, rubber ropes, mosquito nets, Sleeping Mat, cooking set, kanga, NFIs bag and solar lamps. SLI has an MOU with the IOM and will work closely with the Core Pipeline and S-NFIs teams on availability of the items (types, sizes, quality, volumes and quantities) requests and collection of the SNFIs. 6.0: Pre-positioning and distribution of S-NFIs to the affected persons.Pre-positioning and distribution of S-NFIs items to affected households will be done in distribution locations selected jointly with the project implementation committee, IDPs/Returnees and host communities, enumerators.7.0: Conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring on S-NFIs distribution.Once the distribution exercise is successfully completed, save a Life International Project’s Coordinator, Monitoring and Evaluation Officer, SNFIs Officers, safeguarding officer in conjunction with the Project Implementation Committee, Local authorities, returnees, IDPs and Host community’s representatives will conduct a post distribution monitoring 1-2 weeks after distribution of the SNFIs. The PDM will seek to obtain information on timeliness, quantity and quality control, appropriateness of items, effectiveness of items in addressing needs, selection control (methods, quality, coverage, fairness, participation of beneficiaries), dissemination of information, participation of beneficiaries, distribution (methods, quality, site, accessibility), Protection, accountability, and impact. Random sampling of HHs (20% of total targeted HHs) will be employed. Restricted cash and voucher assistance will support shelter upgrades (1,150 individuals), NFI replacement (1,500 individuals), and shelter reconstruction (500 individuals). SALI's expertise ensures market-sensitive interventions, including market assessments through interviews and focus groups, and regular price monitoring to prevent market distortions and mitigate risks.
