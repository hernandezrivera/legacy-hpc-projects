Project Summary

The project will ensure that 7,200 (3,200 girls and 4000 boys) children, adolescents, and youths (3-17 years old) will have access to educational programming and attend schools in Rubkona County, Unity State. About 10 temporary learning spaces will be established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and 10 temporary learning spaces or schools rehabilitated. About 2000 (800 girls and 1,200 boys) will access to new gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls) another 5,940 (2,673 girls and 3,267 boys) children will benefit from rehabilitated gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls.
Furthermore, about 5,940 (2,673 girls and 3,267 boys) children and youth (6-17 years) will benefit from recreational material supplies and children (3-5 years) benefited from Early Childhood and Development (ECD) kits, and another 5940 (2,673 girls and 3,267 boys) children (5-17) and teachers provided with teaching and learning materials. About 2000 pupils (800 girls and 1,200 boys) youth and adolescents (14-17 years) will also benefit from life skills and basic vocational training.
The adult direct beneficiaries are teachers’ who will be trained on EiE, basic pedagogy, and learner-centered methodologies, targeting 140 teachers (90 males and 40 females), and they will also be trained on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms. Other adult beneficiaries are community education committee members and local education officials who will be trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS), and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) targeting 140 people (80 males and 60 females)
Active mobilization for school campaigns will be organized to launch back to school/learning campaigns at least once per term

1 - Needs

Decades of civil war have negatively impacted the pace of development and the government’s capacity to deliver basic services remains low. South Sudanese continue to return from Sudan in large numbers, requiring significant emergency support. Rising food insecurity, disease outbreaks, and seasonal flooding continue to impact humanitarian conditions on the ground.The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Feb 2022, about 8.9 million people require humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 compared to the year 2021. The country remained a protection crisis due to a lack of durable peace and investment in basic services holding people back from stability and sustainable development mostly as a result of intensified inter-communal conflict, consecutive years of flooding, and the COVID-19. The Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) projections May-July 2021 estimated that Rubkona is expected to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4+).A previous needs assessment conducted by J4CO in the field -Bentiu indicates that; boys, girls, adolescents, and youths in Rubkona County lack basic primary education or drop out of school at the early primary level due to cultural related factors such as early marriages, early pregnancies, and demand for bridewealth. Pupils attending schools in Rubkona County are showing some very poor educational performance that has been necessitated by the factors mentioned above especially the girls at which they are disproportionately most affected; other factors are that include insufficient school feeding programs for hungry children, inadequate teaching and learning materials, inconsistent teacher payment, inadequate teacher training, limited sanitation facilities at schools, low enrolment and attendance, and high drop-out rates, especially among girls. Existing community infrastructures such as schools serve as a unique entry point for the protection of children and other lifesaving services, including access to safe drinking water, food, AWD/cholera prevention, and protection mechanisms reaching one of the most vulnerable groups - children. So far, the government-supported education services are extremely inadequate and the children are left in the villages doing nothing while others look after cattle. After many schools closed due to covid 19 and current flooding, most schools need renovation while others will need establishing of new Temporary Learning Spaces including sex-segregated latrines for safe sanitation especially doubly-benefitting adolescent girls to change the sanitary pads. Need assessment conducted before submission of this proposal in Rubkona conducted by J4CO and partners revealed that the majority of the schools assessed have deplorable learning spaces and environments due to flooding and lack of maintenance. Therefore, needs scholastic materials, blackboards, textbooks, and other stationery, training of teachers, PTAs, and parents on both GBV and inclusion topics, and most importantly the establishment of TLS with sex-segregated latrines including the rehabilitation of existing TLS with their sex-segregated latrines, which helps girls’ high dropout, due to the elevated risk of sexual harassment and lack of safe spaces for changing menstrual pads. Other recommendations are the distribution of essential school supplies/materials and support of volunteer teachers with monthly incentives as motivationThere is a need to integrate child protection/safeguarding in the schools due to as children are exposed to risks both at schools and the surrounding communities by creating awareness. Such as PSS, GBV awareness, conducting child-friendly spaces activities. The target beneficiaries of this project are boys and girls, aged-3-17 years, adolescent girls, adults, men and women, IDPs, host communities children, returnees, children with special needs among others.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

J4CO has developed a lot of tools to track the progress of the project reflecting how the project is being implemented and adjusting accordingly to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The organization uses field visits to interact with the beneficiaries to collect their views regarding the projects and make informed decisions based on that. Samples of questionnaires are printed and shared with them to complete to find their ideas about the way the project is implemented and how they want it to be done differently. Focus Group Discussions formed by the project officer is central to the project implementation cycles as they meet and share their views about the project and how it has impacted them positively and negatively. This helps the organization to make informed decisions to align the project to meet the needs of the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The Project Log- frame and Work plan will be used to plan and implement activities. The selected indicators and targets will be used to effectively track the progress of project implementation by the program team and the Monitoring and Evaluation team in coordination with UNOCHA. They will continue to monitor the ongoing project implementation process: institutional monitoring (financial, physical, and organizational issues that affect the project), context, objectives, and outcomes. Guidelines on education cluster reporting will be adhered to as per mid-term and End of project submission. Financial reporting will be analyzed every month and shared with the education cluster at the end of the project. Monitoring visits will be conducted quarterly to ensure that staff is kept abreast of their achievements and failures during implementation and adjustments are done to ensure that targets are met as planned. Cluster M&E tools (FGDs, Key Informant Interviews, support supervision assessments, site visitations, and accountability to affected population modalities) will be used to evaluate project responses and performances. J4CO will revise its monitoring tools to adapt to measuring the project objectives and results with the active participation of communities. In terms of staffing, there will be a Programme Coordinator, Project Manager, Project Officer, Community Workers, and Volunteers. The Director of Programs will also provide programmatic oversight to ensure activities and spending are on track in line with the work plan and budget.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection mainstreaming is a priority in J4CO programming throughout the project cycle to respond to the protection needs of the beneficiaries and promote their dignity, integrity, and beneficiary security. Mainstreaming protection into other sectors will be done to minimize risks of harm to children by programs designed without proper consideration for children’s safety and well-being. It is an essential part of compliance with the “Not harm principle” that applies to all humanitarian actions.J4CO has given priority to the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and considered the principles of Do No Harm, in the proposed project. The protection department will work closely with other Education sectors being this is an integrated project and other sectors, WASH, Nutrition, Health, Education, and FSL giving attention to the different needs of girls, boys, men, and women without causing more harm or victimization. To promote the prevention of child protection risks. J4CO will enhance the staff of other service providers on the integration of child protection and protection principles in their day-to-day work and ensure that they build a protective environment for boys, girls, men, and women. The mainstreaming sessions will ensure that they gain an understanding of the distinct nature and the extent of neglect, violence, exploitation, and abuse that girls, boys, men, and women experience. It also involves ensuring that all response activities take into account the different needs, concerns, and capacities of girls and boys. During the workshops with services providers, remedial actions may be identified to enhance safety, dignity, and access to the services provided. J4CO will brief various stakeholders (local authorities, non-state actors) on protection principles and protection mainstreaming to raise awareness and influence changes in practices, policies, and procedures to assist in solving the protection concerns through service delivery. The organization will conduct robust awareness and sensitization campaigns to raise awareness on protection concerns and human rights. Persons with specific needs like unaccompanied and separated children, elderly men and women, and people living with disabilities will be given opportunities to fully participate in the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will ensure that the rights, dignity, and safety of the affected populations will be respected. Men, women, boys, and girls targeted in this project are the primary stakeholders and will participate in all the decisions that affect their lives. Information will be shared so that they make informed decisions and complain if they feel the help they receive is not adequate or unwelcome. Participatory assessments will be conducted at the project's inception to identify locations for interventions. J4CO will always ensure that communities are engaged from the initiation of the project to the closure so that they take ownership of the project. Stakeholder involvement through workshops and meetings will be organized in the project locations and communities will be consulted and provided with information on the project. These will include local authorities, existing community-based structures, youth and adolescent groups, women groups, and representatives of affected communities.In terms of the Complaints and Feedback mechanism, reviews and feedback will be collected from community beneficiaries, school authorities, teachers, and children on an ongoing basis as part of all program implementation. Feedback and complaints will generally be collected through suggestion boxes, physically reporting to the focal point person. The team will ensure that confidentiality is upheld at all times and will always strive to share the results of the complaint after investigations.J4CO will be transparent in all its operations throughout the planning, implementation, monitoring, reporting, and evaluation while involving the local authorities. All stakeholders, especially children and the most excluded will be consulted during assessments involving IDPs, returnees, host communities, and other interest groups. It will also try and produce monthly analytical reports reflecting feedback and complaints, as well as more comprehensive quarterly reports. The organization will therefore ensure that the most vulnerable community members give their feedback before, during, and after the project. A feedback and complaints mechanism will be put in place and a focal point person nominated. A Committee will be set up that represents communities. This will comprise men, women, boys, and girls. Awareness-raising sessions will be conducted for the community to be aware of PSEA and that it constitutes one of the most serious breaches of accountability. Staff will receive training so that they ensure that they avoid engaging in activities that will erode the trust and confidence of the beneficiaries and the country. Suggestion boxes, as well as a telephone number for the focal point person, will be provided for community members to report. A register will also be provided to register complaints and feedback. This will have a timeline so that in the end they will be able to see if complaints and feedback were handled appropriately and in time. The project will engage stakeholders in coordination meetings that will review the implementation progress of the project. A committee that will receive complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries will be formed and be tasked to address those complaints and share the feedback with the community (beneficiaries). There will also be some suggestion boxes that will be put up at public places for easy access by the beneficiaries, especially those who can read and write to put their suggestions on written and put them into the suggestion boxes which the committee will have to collect at the end of every month, read and address them and give the final feedback to the beneficiaries. The project will ensure that all gender groups such as men, women, boys, and girls are equally served and their specific needs addressed and cultural beliefs are respected
