Project Summary

The project aims to provide Survival Kits /Emergency shelter/Non Food items both in-kind and in cash modalities to the most vulnerable persons, newly displaced/host communities and returnees. The project will focus on the life saving shelter/NFIs needs, as identified through assessment to protect the affected displaced people from harsh weather conditions and protection risks targeting all states of South Sudan. The intervention will be implemented through mobile response as well as using its static teams based in Kajo Keji, Juba, Koch, Mayom, Leer, Bor, Duk, Twic East, Akobo, Aweil, Wau and Alek. However the major focus areas will be Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahrel Ghazal, Central, Western and Eastern Equatoria states. Cash programming will also be considered in areas where markets are fully or semi-functional and shelter/NFI items are available and accessible by the community. When blanket targeting is not applicable, the intervention will target mainly lactating and pregnant women, households with malnourished children, and households with members who have certain health complications. Besides unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and persons with no community linkages will also be targeted.
The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2022 HRP's. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and within 22 days for full-fledged delivery of NFIs and emergency shelters and other activities per location. The project will also support returnees in settlement as part of the local integration or as they settle into their areas of origin. The project will benefit 120,000 individuals directly.

1 - Needs

UN OCHA South Sudan new IDPs baseline report for 2022 shows the number of internally displaced persons in South Sudan as 2.2 million while the Displacement Tracking Matrix indicated 2.017 M displaced people in 3335 locations upto September 2021. While the DTM is still in the process of finalizing the exact number of IDPs, the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview reported that the number of internaly displacced people was 1.8 million. On the other hand, the S/NFI cluster need analysis shows that an estimated 2.4M individuals require S/NFIs assistance in 2022 which includes the IDPs, returnees and the Host Communities. Consequently, the cluster targets 1.4 M individuals, of which NRC will be supporting 120,000 individuals across the country.The cluster differentiates the context into different contexts; including collective sites and other spontaneous sites; and relatively stable locations where people are returning to their former habitual residence. The cluster assumes 70 per cent of the displaced population in the country are in need of S/NFIs assistance. The targeted caseloads lack access to essential lifesaving NFI items and often times unable to support themselves through communal connections or coping mechanisms through other local available resources. In cases of returnees, where the areas of returns are stable, the returnees assumed to stay, rebuild their lives and able to cope with the existing/available resources. The Cluster will look into alternative support modalities, such as cash vouchers, training, and other engagement with communities People’s S/NFIs needs are primarily linked to conflict and natural disasters induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. While people outside of camp-like settings have better access to shelter materials, their overall Shelter and NFI needs are traditionally broader due to the absence of functional and accessible markets and lack of communal connections or coping mechanisms from other resources. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacements. Any new outbreaks of violence are expected to discourage or prevent people from moving out of camp-like settings in large numbers such as the ongoing violence in Western Equatoria. The 2021 HRP estimated that 2.4 Million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Most of the affected population especially those in hard to reach areas have limited access to basic humanitarian assistance. The limited assistance provided initially was intended to sustain the IDPs for a short period of time, with the anticipation that the conflicts would cease and the IDPs return to their villages of origin. However, the renewed and emerging conflicts in various states points to a protracted conflict situation which will continue to displace populations. Further, the rainy season has strained humanitarian efforts as well as exposing the IDPs to greater vulnerability as most of the land in South Sudan is prone to flooding such as in Unity, Jonglei, Bar el Ghazal and other parts of Greater Equatoria leading to further displacement and also hindering accessibility to some areas. The climatic extremes experienced both the rainy and dry season create high need for NFIs to strengthen the populations copying mechanism. The emergency shelter kits provided are not adequate enough and will soon need to be replaced / changed. Most adults still sleep in the open, and occasionally congesting in one shelter to cover against the night rains, while the other available shelters are used to accommodate children, the sick and the old during the night.

2 - Project activities

1. The intervention will provide access to emergency shelter (in-kind or cash modality) to newly displacedpeople in a secondary displacement. The project will target 30,000 individuals foremergency shelters including those who will be supported by cash depending on the marketfunctionality. 2. The project provides access to life-saving NFIs (in-kind or cash) for newly displacedpeople in a secondary displacement. It will target 90,000 individuals forprovision of NFIs including those who will be supported through cash. 3. Shelter upgrades (In-kind or cash) for returnees /protracted IDPs/Host Community and people in secondary displacement. The project will target 400 families. 4. Mainstream Housing, Land and Properties (HLP) and Collaborative Disputes Resolution (CDR) approaches throughout S/NFI response.5. Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response. 6. Training communities/stakeholders on Accountability to affected population (AAP). 7. Establishment of Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM).8. Carry out Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) in two phases; Rapid Monitoring while distribution is carried out to receive immediate feedback from beneficiaries while the second one will take place two to four weeks after the distribution.

3 - Project indicators

NRC Shelter Emergency team will be responding to newly displaced people, returnees and the most vulnerable host community by providing access to emergency shelter (in-kind or cash modality) and life-saving NFIs (in-kind or cash). This project will contribute to the following Strategic Objectives;SO2 (Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action.), Specific Objective SO2.2 (Provide shelter and non-food items for displaced people in situations of emergency and transition in an ability-challenged accessible, gender-responsive manner) and Sectoral Objective 1 (Percentage of the targeted population able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition).The success of this project will be measured through the following indicators: 1. # of people reached with in-kind standard emergency shelter2. # of people reached with in kind light emergency shelter3. # of people reached with in-kind standard lifesaving NFIs4. # of people reached with in kind light NFI kits5. # of people reached with flood kits6. # of people reached with individuals Shelter/NFI items7. # of people reached with cash and voucher for essential emergency shelter upgrades8. # of people reached with cash and voucher for essential NFI replacements Furthermore, Post distribution monitoring (PDM) missions will assess and measure the actual impact of this project.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In South Sudan, the practice has shown that the market functionality varies considerably over time and ranges according to seasons and movement of populations. NRC conducts Multi-sectoral assessment in locations of displacement which usually is accompanied by market assessment in locations of response to determine market functionality.NRC team uses the internal Market assessment tool to capture the market functionality, availability of items, prices, market capacity to absorb high demands and supply chain and risks.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher Assistance is an efficient and effective mode of humanitarian assistance especially in upholding human dignity through upholding the individual agency of the beneficiaries. It also strengthens local economies by stimulating local markets.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The Project will incorporate feedback and complaints mechanisms that will be understandable by older people, women, girls, people with disabilities addressing the feelings of these vulnerable individuals on what they feel are the safest and most appropriate ways for them to provide feedback and make complaints. This includes the use of simple language and easy to use feedback forms and provide support to these populations in providing feedback, including using alternative authorized persons (like caregivers, family members who is literate etc.) to give their feedback. Older persons, women, girls, people with disabilities will be sensitized on the importance of complaints and feedback mechanisms to address their specific needs and on what they can expect from humanitarian organizations. NRC will act on feedback and complaints from Older persons, women, girls, people with disabilities, etc. in a way that respects their safety, dignity, and rights and adapts organizational policies if they are not inclusive of these groups.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project is designed in a way that gives beneficiaries information regarding the project and Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) through the following:1. Accountability to Affected population training will be done for all stakeholders and the community groups representatives. Through the training, community members will be encouraged to indicate on their preferred way of communication and complaint and feedback sharing mechanisms. NRC will adopt the feedback to design the project, accordingly.2. Within the AAP training, NRC will form a committee which will include representatives from each community groups. This committee supervises NRC activities and will work as a link between NRC and the community.3. NRC will have a visible and accessible complaint and feedback desk throughout the project phases. 4. NRC will conduct household interview, Key Informant Interview (KII) and Focus group discussion (FGD) with the community groups and will inform about the project and CFMs.5. Community leaders, representatives and partners on ground will facilitate community awareness using mega phones and other materials.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

NRC takes the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse very seriously. All NRC staff engaged in the project will be trained in PSEA; further, staff will be trained in PFA and identification of potential survivors of SEA, and receive training and mentoring in how to respond appropriately and with empathy. All communications with affected populations will stress their right to receive services without coercion or exploitation. NRC will work with communities to develop confidential, appropriate complaints and feedback mechanisms, and respond to any complaints promptly and transparently, to the extent that we are still able to assume both the survivor and perpetrator of their safety. NRC will strive to recruit gender-balanced teams and take every effort to ensure that vulnerable women are able to work directly with competent woman staff.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All beneficiaries’ feedback will be documented, analysed and taken into consideration to adapt the project implementation modality. Areas of potential adaptation include:1- Selection criteria and identification of the most vulnerable2- Number of registration points and corresponding locations3- Number of distribution sites, location, specific date and time4- Type of items or assistance requested by the beneficiaries (Needs of each group)5- Management of registration and distribution sites 6- Advocacy with other cluster and partners

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

NRC has robust Policy frame work that guides its operation especially on ensuring protection of persons in Need we serve. For example, to address the risk of beneficiaries’ exposure to violence during registration/ distribution as a result of distance to or location of humanitarian assistance services, NRC ensures that the staff moves closure to the beneficiaries and staff are trained to observe protection and safeguarding issues throughout the project at all times. To address the risk of exclusion of minority and marginalized groups during the need assessment and response, a separate focus group discussion will be held with women and girls where there will be consultation on the needs.Risks of SEA are also present within any provision of humanitarian services. Project staff will therefore be trained on PSEA including refresher training for the number of staff members who have already participated in PSEA awareness and training. Awareness-raising on SEA and accountability of humanitarian organizations will also be conducted in targeted communities to mitigate this. A safe Feedback Complaints and Reporting Mechanism will be established within target communities. Beyond this, NRC will conduct regular activity and location-specific protection risk assessments considering the specific risks associated with each activity and coordinating mitigation efforts. It is also acknowledged that the current COVID-19 pandemic environment poses a risk to individuals' physical and emotional safety and access to project activities. COVID-19 prevention measures will be adhered to within project activities.
