Project Summary

The project will support 11020 vulnerable conflict-affected households and community members in northeast Nigeria through targeted and needs-based support to build resilient, safe, and dignified livelihoods in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States.
The project will prioritise the most at-risk households, particularly female, child, disability, or GBV/protection survivor-headed households (or those under multiple categories of risk), utilising community-validated vulnerability assessments. Street Child will provide identified beneficiaries with cash grants, business skill/management mentoring, and vocational training opportunities that will enable start-up or rehabilitation of Income-Generating Activities (IGA). Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLA) will serve a supportive function both by improving access to cash and liquidity, and by promoting culture of savings and investment among community members that will strengthen household-level resilience.
Street Child aims to operate in the following LGAs under the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan:
Borno: Bama, Damboa, Gwoza, Mobbar, Monguno, Ngala
Adamawa: Hong, Michika, Mubi N, Mubi S
Yobe: Gujba, Yunusari, Yusufari

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria's livelihood crisis has long-term causes of historic poverty and subsistence level livelihood, which have been sharply intensified by the 13 year insurgency and repeated cycles of conflict and displacement. In 2022, the after-effects of the COVID-19 pandemic, national/international inflation of food and fuel costs, and historic flooding across Nigeria have also severely disrupted livelihood activities and needs are expected to rise considerably from the 3.3 million identified in the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview. The Borno State Government strategy of closing displacement camps in Maiduguri and elsewhere, and relocating displaced people to their LGA of origin, will strain limited services in areas of reception and poses a risk to intercommunal social harmony. Children face considerable risk of safeguarding and Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) breaches including SGBV, early and forced marriage, child labour, recruitment by armed actors, and other forms of exploitation. These risks are magnified in households affected by poverty and loss of livelihood or income, particularly households displaced by armed violence, as children are forced into income-generating activities that are often exploitative to support the family unit. In addition to utilizing children as sources of income generation, other negative coping mechanisms employed by households in financial distress include sale of productive assets, purchasing of food on credit, and borrowing of money from informal sources. Cash support, particularly when accompanied by IGA grants and training, address the most urgent needs identified by community members in recent surveys that their most urgent need is financial support to boost liquidity and allow them to rebuild inventories and repurchase assets.

2 - Activities

Street Child will implement several livelihood strengthening modalities within the project, dependent upon the local socioeconomic context of the community of implementation. Activities are harmonised and intended to strengthen local economies and thus build communities which are more resilient to sudden, sharp shocks such as natural disaster or household expenses. All business support training will be informed by a market analysis, held before training takes place, that identifies key areas for training relevant to the current market needs of the community where intervention is taking place.* Most vulnerable households (particularly female, disability, GBV survivor, etc headed households) will receive an NGN 50,000 cash grant to start up or rehabilitate an income generating activity/small business. Livelihood case workers will support the beneficiary with basic business management skills mentoring and encouraging culture of savings. After a period of saving and mentorship, an additional N10,000 matching grant is provided.* Street Child will establish and support Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs) in the target communities to provide communities with access to credit and communal financial services. Beneficiary households will benefit from increased wellbeing with improved ability to budget and save for out-of-pocket expenses, and reduced risks of negative economic coping strategies involving children. VSLA groups will receive resources and materials as well as training on how to effectively run the VSLA.* Community-facing dialogues and discussions will take place regularly to ensure social harmony and cohesion, particularly between returnee-displaced-host community groups. These sessions will be facilitated through SC staff and key community religious and social stakeholders, to enable airing of grievances and find consensus for solving challenges. Communities will self-decide the format and structure of the dialogues based on their own context, with support from SC teams.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The prioritisation of protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising unintended negative impacts ensure that the communities where Street Child works can access meaningful, dignified support. Street Child is committed to centring and mainstreaming protection throughout its activities and projects, and has pre-emptively identified potential protection risks within the project as well as taking active steps to integrate mitigation and management strategies. Protection reporting, response and prevention mechanisms are integrated into the design, and protection mainstreaming indicators are included within the monitoring and evaluation framework. At the beneficiary selection stage, Street Child will use tested vulnerability assessment tools and clear selection process to ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on need and through predetermined and clearly communicated criteria of vulnerability. In order to ensure participation and minimise inter-group tensions, targets have been set to include different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process and criteria during the inception stages, and continuous sensitisation of the selection criteria will be used to ensure that possible intra- and inter-group tensions are minimised. Sensitisation on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past projects, where dissatisfaction about not being included in the project has been reduced by clearly explaining the aims of the project and how beneficiaries were selected. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, Street Child commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we work and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Street Child has a clear safeguarding policy in place which applies to all personnel and affiliates, including implementing partners and community members who are affiliated to our work. All Street Child staff, partner staff, and project-affiliated community members such as volunteers and committee members receive comprehensive training of the Street Child safeguarding policy. This comprehensive approach, which is also intended to be rolled out to vendors, ensures that all actors within SC projects are operating under the same rules and procedures and creates a harmonised safeguarding environment. In addition to training key community members on the Street Child safeguarding policy, wider community outreach and sensitisation is carried out via hosting of community forums and meetings, or use of visual aids including posters and murals which offer accessible and culturally relevant and appropriate key messages to empower community members on understanding and reporting concerns. Finally, Street Child is an active participant of the PSEA working group in Borno, as an extra level of accountability, and follows the best practices and lessons learned shared through this working group.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback mechanisms will be contextualised to the community in question. Street Child has a dedicated CRM Officer with the responsibility of implementing and managing accessible and relevant feedback mechanisms, based on community information. Previous interventions have utilised toll free lines, complaint desks, anonymous boxes, and similar modalities. Safeguarding and complaint mechanisms can be contextualised through altering images and languages to suit the community, both for audio and print material. Project staff will also attend regular coordination meetings with community structures. M&E teams will conduct baseline, endline, and regular monitoring, including FGDs/KIIs with community members, to gauge satisfaction with the implementation. All feedback received will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.
