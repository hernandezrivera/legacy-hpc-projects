Project Summary

The project will feature the following activities and implementation strategies in alignment with the relevant policies and guidelines of UNESCO:
i. Collection/Contextualization/adaptation of accelerated integrated literacy livelihood skills training materials – The integrated literacy and livelihood skills training materials will cover functional literacy and livelihood skills training in mixed languages of English and Burmese and math with an introduction to soft and life skills. Gender mainstreaming and Environmental sustainability will be one of the critical aspects to be integrated into life skills. Youth-led climate actions will be promoted throughout the learning programme. First priority will be given to utilizing the functional literacy materials that are under the process of development under UNESCO’s existing project with support from UNHCR.

ii. Development of a handout for teachers and trainers – The handout will be developed for literacy teachers and livelihood skills trainers. Multilingual pedagogies will be included in response to the multilingual environment in the refugee camps. The materials will be for mixed use of Burmese and English for popular words/terminologies that promote the vocabulary and terminologies of literacy and occupational trades.
iii. Training of teachers and livelihood skills trainers – There will be 15 literacy teachers cum livelihood skills trainers who will be trained on integrated andragogy for 3 days. Priority will be given to developing a mixed group of trainers (Host Community and Rohingya Refugee). The orientation will be conducted by existing Master Trainers under the UNHCR-funded project. The teachers cum trainers including Master Trainers will be selected from existing partner agencies working in the Rohingya Refugee Camps.
iv. Enrolment of learners and imparting literacy and livelihood skills training – 300 (Female 150) learners will be enrolled based on placement tests. Since priority will be given to livelihood skills, the learners who have minimum literacy skills equivalent to functional literacy courses will be enrolled. The Learning Centres (LCs)/CBLFs/training venues of existing partner agencies working in the Rohingya Refugee Camps will be used for this purpose. There is no scope to establish new LCs/CBLFs and Host Community. The learning/training sessions will be held for 3.5 hours per day and 5 days a week.
v. Learning/training assessment – Formative and summative assessment systems will be followed. Existing learning assessment tools (currently in the process of development) of UNESCO’s project supported by UNHCR and the Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector (LSDS) will be used. The personalized learning progress tracking system will be developed to ensure personalized learning needs.
vi. The AAL will be provided to 300 (female 50%) youth in the camps followed by off-firm/on-firm non-formal technical training (NFTT). Diversified livelihood opportunities will be created for 200 AAL-NFTT graduates out of 300 ALL-NFTT graduates. The remaining 100 will be included in next year. Non-formal technical training (NFTT) to 50 (female 50%) youth in the Host Community. Existing/adapted/new modules will be used for NFTT.
AAL-NFTT graduates followed by livelihood-trained youth in the Camps will be linked with voluntary economic engagement while NFTT graduates of HC to be linked with relevant self-employment/wage-employment. Priority will be given to the following trades:
• Barbering
• Carpenters (wooden/bamboo handy craft)
• Jute products (handy craft)
• Weaving, knitting, repairing household items (handy craft)
Camp Location: Camp 1E, 13, 15 and 17. There will be 75 beneficiaries in each of the camps (on an average)
Host Community: Ukhiya Upazila (Union: Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong, Ratna Palong, Palong Khali, Raja Palong)
vii. Documentation and reporting – UNESCO will collect and preserve project-related data and prepare a project implementation report.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to UNHCR Report (2023), almost three-quarters of the refugee population reported never having had access to formalised education institutions as they are reported either to have only received informal education or have no education at all.Literacy is a key component of adult learning and education, which allows a person to engage in lifelong learning and participate fully in the community, workplace and broader society. Literacy includes the ability to read and write, identify, understand, interpret, create, communicate, and compute, using printed and written materials, as well as the ability to solve problems.The National Skills Development Framework for Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities opened space for building the capacity of refugees by developing skills that are portable, commensurate with livelihood opportunities in Rakhine State and in support of their voluntary return and sustainable reintegration in Myanmar. Volunteering services allow refugees to utilize skills gained within the camps by engaging with implementing partners’ activities or through the relevant sectors such as the education sector and livelihoods and skills development sector within the camps.To enable refugees’ better access to vocational skills training and utilization of skills, (to those individuals who have no, or limited literacy and numeracy skills), Integrated Literacy and Livelihoods Skills Development Training UNHCR approached UNESCO for support in developing and implementing accelerated adult literacy learning programmes equivalent to the Myanmar Curriculum for the age group of 18-24years which are linked to specific vocations in which refugees will be trained.Competency Based Training (Vocational Training) was introduced and piloted in the Camps in 2023 (curriculum adapted from MNQF or AQRF). However, the low level of literacy of the population age 18+ affects enrolment into vocational training. The existing literacy programs are not suitable for those above the age of 18 (targeted age group of LSDS beneficiaries is 18+).As per MSNA conducted in 2023, 71% of refugee households reported facing barriers to income opportunities. However, out of the remaining 29%, only 5% reported volunteer engagement as their source of household income, and 14% reported income from small-business and own production. MSNA 2023 confirmed a high unemployment rate remaining among the workforce in the host communities (32%), particularly youth and women.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will promote common approaches and common systems (volunteers’ ToRs; guidelines for selection of beneficiaries; minimum standard packages…) in consultation with LSDS. UNESCO will ensure the convergence of service packages sector-wide harmonized literacy materials (AAL), placement tests and learning assessment systems for all partners to avoid duplication and overlapping. In addition, UNESCO will take initiatives for an integrated AAL, skills development programme including ensuring volunteer engagement or employment/self-employment opportunities. Established referral pathways between the sectors in 2024 including participatory monitoring with 5W tracker.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNESCO will adopt a capacity development approach to strengthen the capacities of target learners and other key community members to support literacy and livelihood skills development for all youth and adults, understand issues related to gender equality, and disability inclusion, and advocate for further government and donor commitments to literacy, numeracy, vocational, soft and life skills development and education resourcing. Owing to the project interventions, community members and other key stakeholders are expected to gain ownership and capacity. They can continue their contribution to the literacy and vocational/livelihood skills development of Rohingya refugee youth and adults. The equipment and inventory (project goods and property) acquired under the project will be disposed of in discussion with LSDS, prioritizing donations to the local communities. In conclusion, UNESCO will ensure the sustainability of the interventions by practicing the following five principles: community ownership, partnering, transformed relationships, and community resilience and social accountability. In addition, based on the existing project with the collaboration of UNHCR, UNESCO will promote learning assessment system, placement test mechanism and harmonized accelerated adult learning materials through this project.
