Project Summary

Among the worst recent events to affect the country was the 2015–2016 El Niño drought, the most devastating in 35 years for Mozambique and much of southern Africa. The drought affected southern and central regions in the country. Those kind of natural changes aggravate the situation of chronic malnutrition present in the Country.
The 81 per cent of the population in Mozambique relies on agriculture for livelihood and over 95 per cent of food crops are produced under rain-fed conditions in a country that suffers frequent drought. The effects of the drought continue to worsen water access, nutrition and food insecurity, increasing poverty.
In Namaacha and Manhiça districts the main objectives identified by the project are:
- Provide lifesaving services for the treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among boys and girls aged under 5 years, pregnant and lactating women including nutrition education for pregnant and lactating women to promote the adoption of adequate nutritional practices within the communities.
- Provide access to maternal nutrition, infant and young child feeding programmes and Screening of all children from 6 to 59 months, for early identification of cases of malnutrition
- Strengthen nutrition and health services capacity, monitoring and surveillance, establishing a nutrition monitor system to respond and treat acute malnutrition cases
