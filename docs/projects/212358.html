Project Summary

DRC acknowledges the significant need to scale-up resilience programming to limit future aid dependency and provide dignified, sustainable, and cost-effective solutions for fragile communities. In this view, DRC plans to implement a comprehensive package of Economic Recovery interventions in Aleppo, Homs, Rural Damascus, and Daraa governorates to address the underlying causes of vulnerability. To do so, DRC will support crisis-affected men, women, and unemployed youth in their transition to self-reliance and resilience by promoting decent wage and self-employment opportunities. Livelihood interventions are intended to complement other sectoral activities, and will include a combination of Business Development and Vocational Support.
Under the first component, Business Development Skills training will be delivered to approximately 280 individuals, with the aim of strengthening foundational business management knowledge and skills to ultimately improve business’ prospects and development opportunities. The five-day course will cover a range of topics, including human resource management, financial management (bookkeeping), demand and competition in the market, procurement, quality control, product development, preparation of business plans, and marketing. The training will be delivered through a mixed-methodology approach of both theoretical and practical modules. Upon completion of the course, DRC will invite all participants to submit final business plans, inclusive of BoQs for the items and/or services they deem necessary to effectively grow their enterprise or for the creation of new scalable ones. A review committee will conduct a technical and feasibility assessment of the proposed business plans to ensure that the most promising and lucrative business strategies are selected with maximum multiplier effects. Based on the findings, an estimated 200 individuals will be supported with Business Support Grants (BSGs), directly benefitting a further 800 household members through improved income. As a control measure, the cash grants will be disbursed in two instalments, with the first instalment not exceeding 70 percent of the total grant amount. The second and final instalment will only be released upon physical verification by DRC to ensure adherence to the business plan and the authentication of receipts linked to the expensed amount.
Under the second component, an estimated 300 individuals will engage in vocational trainings, informed by a comprehensive Labour Market Gap Analysis. Vocational trainings will be delivered through accredited institutes, taking into account vocations with the greatest potential for employment as well as participants' pre-existing competencies, skills, and interests. Subsidized job placements will also be prioritized for a portion of participants (approximately 200 individuals) to enable on-the-job learning and to support them in securing sustainable, decent employment in the longer-term.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Within the complexities of the protracted Syria crisis, the economic collapse together with the destruction of productive sectors has had a devastating effect on livelihoods and living conditions in the country. An overwhelming 90 percent of the population is now living below the poverty line, and 12.1 million Syrians are food insecure. While subject to extraordinarily high uncertainty, the Syrian economy, which was estimated to contract by 3.2 percent in 2023 prior to the earthquake, is now projected to contract by 5.5 percent and remains one of the primary drivers of needs. The severe macro-economic deterioration, resulting from years of conflict and instability, a sharp decline in domestic production, and crippling international sanctions is characterized by rampant inflation and currency depreciation, rising commodity prices, and diminished purchasing power. The rapid and constant depreciation of the currency has made life increasingly unaffordable for many Syrians, driving them towards poverty and cyclical reliance on humanitarian aid. Inflation has had a direct effect on the purchasing power of households and individuals who cannot keep up with the rising market prices, with approximately 90 percent of households in Syria consistently experiencing a gap between their income earned and the expenditure required to cover basic needs. According to available data, MSMEs contribute approximately 40 percent to Syria's GDP, have remained dominant in private entrepreneurship, and continue to serve as a main source of income and employment creation for large segments of the population. Specifically, MSMEs support the livelihoods of approximately 70 percent of the Syrian workforce within a context where formal job opportunities are severely limited. However, the rapid economic downturn has significantly impacted MSMEs that were already suffering from crisis-induced gaps in human capital and skills, limited access to finance, a loss of productive assets, and an overall ecosystem that is not conducive to private sector growth and investment. Risk Management:1) Ineffective PSEA measures result in staff or field support personnel harming members in the community: Safeguarding measures are embedded in DRC’s mandatory training packages, policies (Code of Conduct, PSEA, child protection), and procedures for the recruitment of staff, reporting and investigation routines, as well as in the roles/responsibilities of senior management and managers. Staff are aware of their obligation to report suspected misconduct and to direct all SEA-related cases to the HQ-based Code of Conduct team for investigation and management. DRC will use in-house capacities to continue the provision of trainings to all staff and contractors under this project.2) Increased inflation and price instability: DRC will conduct and regularly update market assessments in order to monitor price increases and parallel exchange rates, as well as their fluctuation.
