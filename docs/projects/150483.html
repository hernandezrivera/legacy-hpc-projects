Project Summary

The idea of the project came as a response to the deteriorated humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip especially in the health sector, in connection with the continuation of the Great Return of Marches event.
The project will target the trauma cases from the women, men, girls, boys, elderly and people with disabilities, who are at high risk of morbidity and mortality.
This intervention will support UHWC to expand the provision of emergency health services to trauma cases through the following trauma pathway that includes: (i) deploying UHWC volunteers at MoH (TSP), (ii) transportation of patients from TSPs to Al Awda Hospital, (iii) Provision of hospital care including emergency services, surgical operations and hospitalization, (iv) post-injury wound care such as dressing will be provided to trauma cases even in the hospital or at home to cases who are facing difficulties to access the hospital, (v) providing physiotherapy services even at hospital or at home, (vi) providing psychological support.
From 30/3/2018 to 8/10/2018, Al-Awda Hospital provided emergency services to 941 trauma cases (162 out of them underwent surgical operations) with support from Humanitarian Fund that will be ended on 12/11/2018. This intervention is an extension of the previous project and it will support the treatment of 2,000 trauma cases during 2019 and will enable Al Awda hospital to provide new life-saving services for the injured in general and patients with limbs and vascular injury in particular.
Thus, this project will enable Al-Awda Hospital to perform new trauma health services that weren’t previously provided, and this includes:
- Conducting vascular surgical operations to trauma cases, by contracting a consultant specialized in vascular surgery. These cases were previously referred to MoH hospitals, which are already overloaded. Since 30/3/2018, 120 vascular injured has been referred by Al Awda Hospital to MoH Hospitals. Here lies the importance of this project that it will reduce the number of cases referred to the MoH through conducting 200 vascular operations to trauma cases at Al Awda Hospital.
- Moreover, this project will enable Al awda Hospital to provide physiotherapy services even at hospital or at home to patients who couldn’t reach the hospital.
- Psychological support will also be provided to the patients and their families inside and outside the hospital in order to provide comprehensive health services.
It is estimated that 2000 cases will benefit from theses services (7 % female and 17% children). All the project cases will be documented and desegregated by gender and age according to the unified models, and information will be shared with MoH and health cluster.
In order to ensure the continuation of the provision of the lifesaving health services to the trauma cases through Al Awda Hospital; essential lists of medication, medical disposable, lab reagents in addition to fuel should be guaranteed.
All the above mentioned services will be provided to the affected people in a safety and dignity manner, and without any discrimination. Gender and age needs have been taken into consideration, besides all data will be disaggregated by gender and age group.
Protection mainstreaming will be taken into consideration, as the services will be available to all beneficiaries who needs them, trauma services will be provided totally free of charge. Female and paediatric staff will be available as a response to gender and age needs, privacy and confidentiality will be respected.
In order to enhance preparedness and to better deal with causalities, 10 of UHWC health workers (60% female) will received training in management of emergency cases and lifesaving interventions.
Community engagement and accountability will be ensured through active participation of the affected population in all the project stages through focus groups, sharing information, measuring patients’ satisfaction and receiving feedback through accountability sessions.

Needs

Since 30 March 2018, the Gaza Strip has witnessed a significant increase in casualties due to the continuation of the Great Return Marches event, resulting in an increase in the number of injuries to 22,267 cases in addition to 205 martyrs according to WHO & HC report 6/10/2018. While the health providers suffering from severe shortages of medication, medical disposable and fuel needed to operate the generators, where power cuts reach 20 hours a day.Based on HNO 2019, there is 40851 trauma cases (2860 F , 37,991 M) will be in need to receive trauma health services to save their lives. The medical teams of the UHWC have provided emergency services to 941 trauma cases until 8/10/2018. And through this project it is estimated to provide trauma services to 2000 cases including (men, women, boys, girls). Based on our experience in emergency management, it was noted that there is an important need for the operation of vascular surgery in connection with the lack of this service currently in Al Awda Hospital, forcing the hospital administration to refer these cases to MoH hospitals and this leads to increase the burden on MoH. Hence, conducting vascular surgery at Al Awda Hospital will reduce the risk to the lives of patients and reduce the burden on the Ministry of Health.In addition, the provision of physiotherapy and psychological support services will provide comprehensive service for causalities. To ensure the provision of trauma services, essential medicines, medical consumables, fuel to run the generators should be available, as well as training medical personnel on emergency response and lifesaving intervention to better deal with trauma cases. Protection mainstreaming will be taken into consideration, as the services will be available to all beneficiaries who needs them, home visits services will be provided to patients who face difficulties to access the hospital, all trauma services will be provided totally free of charge. Female and paediatric staff will be available as a response to gender and age needs, privacy and confidentiality will be respected. In a comprehensive view of the target groups in the project, it was observed that the majority of participants in the Great Return Marches are males aged 14 to 34 years old, who usually are in the front lines, so they are most vulnerable to injuries and disabilities resulting from amputation of limbs. While the percentage of females’ participation is 30%, they are more affected by toxic gas inhalation, as well as with the categories of the children and elderly.Therefore, the principles of protection will be taken into consideration during the provision of emergency services by taking into account the category of females in providing the services through stabilization process and field paramedics and during the provision of emergency health services, besides the allocation of special space for the reception and treatment of women to ensure their privacy during the provision of emergency health services.In order to ensure protection for children, a paediatrician will be presented in the emergency department to ensure that appropriate treatment is provided according to the doses allocated to each child by age group.Al Awda Hospital building is designed to facilitate the physical disabilities movement in term of ramp, the availability of wheelchairs and lift to facilitate their movement between floors. Community engagement and accountability will be ensured through active participation of the affected population (women, men, boys, girls and PWD) in all the project stages through conducting 4 FG for assessment, 2 FG for monitory at the midterm of the project, sharing information via UHWC website and social media, measuring patients’ satisfaction through questionnaires and receiving feedback through 4 accountability FG sessions.
