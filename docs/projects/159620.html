Project Summary

In 2020, Action Against Hunger will provide life-saving WASH support to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable host and IDP communities in Somalia. The communities affected by undernutrition, WASH related disease outbreaks and climate/human induced disasters will be specifically targeted for emergency WASH support to reduce the WASH-related morbidity and mortality. Action Against Hunger will employ a multi-sectoral approach where the WASH intervention will be integrated with nutrition, health and food security and livelihoods to sustainably increase access to potable water, sanitation and hygiene services. A minimum WASH package will be ensured at nutrition and health facilities while households with malnourished children will be targeted for WASH support through the mother/child dyad. The WASH project will provide water treatment kits to targeted households with malnourished children and newly displaced IDPs, distribute hygiene kits including menstrual hygiene materials to the most vulnerable people among the newly displaced households and provide emergency water support through community water vouchers for displaced households and communities affected by persistent drought. The project will also rehabilitate strategic water points in areas with the highest malnutrition and AWD caseloads. In addition, Action Against Hunger will construct emergency sanitation facilities in IDP settlements, rehabilitate latrines in institutions (nutrition, health centers and schools) and conduct hygiene promotion at the institutions and households with a special focus on households with malnourished children.
To reduce the risk of violence against women, children and other vulnerable groups, Action Against Hunger will employ a strong protection lens in WASH programming. This will be done through consultations with women, men and children in the targeted villages to determine the best locations and designs for WASH facilities and the adaptation of the facilities for use by persons living with disabilities.
The capacity of the bene􀂡ciary communities will be enhanced through regular trainings of WASH governance groups and recruitment of WASH staff from the bene􀂡ciary communities. Further, Action Against Hunger will promote the use of cash and vouchers in the provision of WASH services to the targeted individuals, households and communities.

Project Activities

Action Against Hunger will provide both emergency and sustainable WASH services in the targeted locations prioritizing cholera/AWD prone areas, communities persistently affected by drought and IDP settlements with low WASH coverage. This is in line with the needs identified through assessments conducted by ACF in the specific project locations, the WASH cluster and other stakeholders in 2019. The temporary WASH services delivered during acute emergency will address life-saving needs aimed at reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality, particularly among children. This will include emergency water trucking through community vouchers for newly displaced households and communities affected by drought, provision of household water treatment and/or point of use water treatment, bucket chlorination, safe water storage at HH level targeting villages with the highest SAM caseloads, closely followed by rehabilitation of water sources and establishment of water point management structures. Access to sanitation will be improved through construction of emergency latrines with handwashing points provided directly or through the cash for latrines approach and through the Community Led Total Sanitation as a sustainable solution in IDP sites more likely to become permanent settlements. To ensure proper use of the newly installed and the existing WASH infrastructure, Action Against Hunger will recruit and train community hygiene volunteers from the targeted communities to carry out robust hygiene promotion targteting behaviours identified through Barrier Analysis studies. Hygiene promotion will be conducted at health and nutrition facilities, households, schools and community levels through the established network of community volunteers who will be provided with materials including IECs to conduct health education and hygiene promotion activities at the three levels. Hygiene promotion will also utilize the increasing capacity of the government departments including joint trainings for hygiene promoters with MoH. To improve access to potable water, Action against Hunger will support the rehabilitation of strategic dilapidated water points or construction of new, equipped with elevated water storage tanks and distribution systems to communal water kiosks. The use of solar powered water pumping systems will be promoted for both rehabilitated and new water points. Harvesting of surface water will also be promoted including surface storage like berkads and water pans and securing of catchment systems to increase water percolation and increase shallow aquifers recharge. Gender balanced Water Management committees will be established and their capacity enhanced through scheduled trainings by both Action Against Hunger staff, line ministry staff and technical expertise in the private sector in Somalia. As such, trade fares will be organized to bring together the committees, local authorities, line ministry staff and private sector service providers. During the trade fares, the traders will be supported to showcase the spare parts, technologies and technical support available for the maintenance of water and sanitation infrastructure.Detailed Activities include:A total of 17,207 people will benefit from emergency water supply in vulnerable settlements and communities. This will be done through vouchers for 15l/p/d of potable water (45litres per household) for a period of 60 days. As far as possible, ACF will make use of community water vouchers to take advantage of the existing emergency water supply arrangements existing at community level. At the same time, ACF will work with the beneficiary communities to construct/rehabilitate multiuse water points to ensure sustainability of water supply for the targeted communities. ACF will also mobilize the targeted communities to establish water management committees to oversee operation and management of the water points.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed intervention is aligned to the WASH, Food Security and livelihoods and Health and Nutrition cluster HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building activities in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. The regions where the interventions will be implemented are characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to deforestation, land degradation, increasing aridity, overgrazing and water scarcity. By increasing access to water through rehabilitation of essential water sources and construction of new ones, the project reduces abnormal migration of humanand livestock to other regions where they inflict significant environmental damages through over grazing and exerting added pressure on scarce water sources. In the absence of latrines, in the new areas of displacement, open defecation would increase posing serious risks to the environmental wellbeing. Portions of land are usually left open to be used by the displaced population for human waste disposal. This poses serious health risks due to the spread of faecal matter, especially during the rainy season when there is seepage into water wells used for drinking water. The proposed project is in line with the WASH cluster objectives and aims to improve the well being and living standards of the most vulnerable while ensuring that the ACF WASH project maximizes its positive impacts on people’s safety and dignity and supports the affected populations to access and enjoy their rights to the full. The project involves providing WASH infrastructure that is easy to operate, environmentally friendly coupled with building strong local capacity to maintain during and after the project life. This includes establishment of community-run gender balanced water committees and strengthening their capacity to ensure proper maintenance, orderly and hygienic supply of water. The project will be linked to ACF resilience programs to ensure replication and sustainability of the intervention. ACF will maintain a participatory approach with beneficiaries and the local authorities throughout the project design and implementation cycle and will remain committed to being accountable to the beneficiaries and authorities in an inclusive manner. Consultations will be done extensively with the local community including women, men, youth, children, religious leaders, minority groups and people living with disabilities to better understand their needs as a prerequisite to designing interventions that are responsive to the identified needs. During the implementation of the project, ACF will use inclusive targeting strategies to reach the most vulnerable individuals, households and communities for the WASH activities. Communities will be involved at various levels through the gender balanced water management committees, an integral structure for decision-making, management of the water infrastructure including water sharing modalities and mobilization of communities for sanitation and hygiene promotion activities.
