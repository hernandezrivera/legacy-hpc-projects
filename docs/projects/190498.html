Project Summary

The northern conflict started in November 2020, and the tension spilled over to Amhara and Afar regions. As a result, hundreds of thousands of people are displaced and lose their assets. The situation in these regions remains volatile and unpredictable despite reported limited improvements in the security situation in some areas where fighting has recently erupted in the border areas between Afar and Amhara regions. According to the UNOCHA report, on March 31, 2022, in Amhara, the situation along areas bordering Tigray, namely in Wag Hamra, North Wollo, and North Gondar zones, remains volatile. Several locations remain hard to reach for humanitarian operations including Abergele, Tsagibji kebeles as well as some parts of Zequala kebeles in Wag Hamra Zone, large parts of Kobo in North Wollo Zone, and Addi Arkay in North Gondar. In Amhara, the regional Government authorities continued with the relocation of IDPs.Since the 14th of March 2022, it has relocated at least 12,000 people out of an estimated 58,000 registered IDPs planned for relocation from Kobo in North Wollo Zone to Jara, near Woldiya, the zonal capital. While regional authorities and a few humanitarian partners are constructing basic facilities at the relocation sites and providing essential assistance including food, there is still a huge need and setting-up proper camp structures mainly emergency shelter, water, sanitation and hygiene, health and nutrition services, and non-food items. Meanwhile, in Afar, a needs assessment was conducted in Afdera and Silsa Guyha IDP sites, where there are currently six sites managed by humanitarian partners in the two woredas, with a total of more than 34,000 people. While limited shelters and non-food items (NFIs) are provided to the IDPs, a huge gap persists with the provision of food, shelter, drinking water, sanitation and hygiene services, education service, primary health care, and NFIs, including dignity kits for women and girls, are urgently required.
CARE has a longstanding presence in Afar and the Amhara region with different programs. Since the emergency happened, CARE has provided emergency shelter and NFI assistance for the conflict-affected peoples in South wollo, North wollo, South Gondar, and North Gondar zones.
Hence, CARE proposed an emergency in-kind or cash for shelter and NFI and shelter repairing response for the conflict-affected peoples in Amhara and Afar regions. The objectives of the project are to improve the protection risks by ensuring the conflict-affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions; and to strengthen access to appropriate live-saving Emergency Shelter and Non-food Items (NFI) to safeguard the health and protection of the displacement affected population. Through this project, 49,471 people will be targeted in Afar, and Amhara, and assisted with Emergency shelter, ESNFI and NFI and Shelter repairing kits.

1. - Projects activities

The primary activity would be to conduct introductory meetings with the local stakeholders to share the selection criteria and ensure that all stakeholders understand the project. The meetings will involve zonal, Woreda, Kebele, and crises affected IDPs representatives and CARE. Then, in consultation with woredas, the sites will be selected in the target woredas, prioritizing those with a higher number of IDPs concentration with high shelter and NFI needs and protection risks. Beneficiary’s identification and registration committee will be established that comprised of the relevant parties from woreda DRMO, kebele officials, representatives from crises affected peoples, and CARE will work with the committees. Then, the beneficiaries will be targeted considering the vulnerability criteria, including separated children in need of shelter, large families with six children or more and no livelihood or other means of survival households with a high dependency ratio, Women at risk: Women of 18 years old or above who are at risk because of their gender (Single mothers or caregivers, a household headed by a single female, widowed, divorced, older women, survivors of violence, pregnant and lactating. In polygamous families, the female lead of the household will be considered an independent household), Child-Headed Household, Person with physical or mental disability and/or chronic illness, older Person-Headed Household, single parent: Man/Woman of 18 years old or above, head of household with children, families who didn’t receive assistance. CARE will conduct a verification assessment to ensure that the targeting is done according to the criteria.Finally, CARE will provide in-kind or cash Emergency shelter, shelter repairing kits, nonfood items and ESNFI items for 49,475 individuals (8026 Households in Amhara and 2428 HHs in Afar regions. The number of households who will receive Emergency shelter, shelter repairing kits, Non-food items, and ESNFI items is listed as follows: 1.Provision of emergency shelter for 1986 HHs (9421 individuals) in Afder (486 HHs), and in Sekota town and zequala woreda (1500HHs).2.Provision of in-kind or cash shelter repairing kits for 3126 HHs in Mersa Town(988HHs), AddiArkay(1237HHs),Meket(173HHs) and Afdera(728HHs).3.Provision of in-kind or cash ESNFI for 4006 individuals (728 HHs) in Afar region zone 2, Afdera woreda.4.Provision of non-food items for 21,251 individuals or 4614HHs in Addi Arkay(1237HH), Meket(404HHs), Mersa Town(988HHS), Sekota (750HHs), Zequala (750HHs) and Afdera(486HH)While in items distributions and the beneficiary selection and registration, COVID-19 mitigation protocols will put in place. CARE will provide PSEA and GBV awareness training for the front-line staff and major stakeholders who will involve in beneficiary targeting and registration. Conduct Post distribution monitoring: PDM will be conducted two weeks after the distribution.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash has a multiplier impact on the local economy by initiating the market system, and it increases the choice of the target beneficiaries and gives them the flexibility to decide based on their priorities needs. Similarly, the cash improves the control power of the target and allows for greater dignity and personal security. CVA can be implemented relatively quickly and is not reliant on logistics-heavy systems. Distribution costs for CVA are usually lower than those for in-kind items. Transfers of cash also eliminate the possibility that beneficiaries sell distributed in-kind items to buy preferred goods and services. In addition, the CVA program enhances the financial inclusion of the project beneficiaries by using financial services and products such as savings and payments.
