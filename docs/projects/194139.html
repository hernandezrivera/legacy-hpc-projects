Project Summary

This projecThis project aims to address critical humanitarian needs across three sectors to reach 64,885 IDPs and host communities in Roseries, Damazine, Wad-Almahi, and Gissan localities. The project will respond to needs and gaps created by the recent inter communal conflict in Blue Nile erupted in July 2022 and escalated in October 2022. This conflict resulted in the displacement of about 127,961 people from Wad-Almahi and Gissan localities to different areas within and outside the region including to Damazine and Roseries localities. The project aims to save lives and improve the food and nutrition security, access to safe water and hygiene services, increase protection prevention and response capacities through implementing the following activities:
1) FSL: provision with farming inputs and services to 5000 farming HHs; support the vaccination and treatment of 500,000 heads of animals that belong to 5,800 HHs against prioritized diseased
The FSL interventions target a total of 64,885 individual including 14,924 men; 15,572 women; 16,870 boys; and 17,519 girls
1) FSL: provision with farming inputs and services to 5000 farming HHs; support the vaccination of 500,000 heads of animals against prioritized diseased, support 3000 agro-pastoral HHs to access emergency livestock treatment services
The FSL interventions target a total of 42,750 individual including 9,833 men; 10,260 women; 11,115 boys; and 11,542 girls
2) WASH: upgrade of 4 hand pumps to solar operated mini water yards, and maintenance of 30 malfunctioning hand pumps to reach 23,500 people with clean and safe water for domestic use, provide hygiene promotion awareness and WASH NFIs to 4000 HHs
The WASH interventions target a total of 37,900 individual including 8717 men; 9,096 women; 9,854 boys; and 10,233 girls
3) Protection: support community-based protection networks to facilitate referral of GBV and child protection cases to healthcare, awareness raising on GBV prevention and response to 1500 people, support foster care to unaccompanied and separated children
The Protection interventions target a total of 9000 individual including 2,070 men; 2,160 women; 2,340 boys; and 2,430 girls

1 - Needs

Between July and October 2022, inter-communal clashes between members of the Hausa tribe and the Hamaj and Berta tribes in several localities of Blue Nile caused the displacement of at least 127,961 people across Blue Nile and to other states. Inter-Agency Assessment led by OCHA in November 2022 and covered four localities affected by the conflict namely: Wad-Almahi, Damazine, Roseries and Gissan. The assessment report reflects that the security situation is calm, but unpredictable due to the limited presence of the Government of Sudan security forces to ensure the safety and security of vulnerable population. This led to restricted movement of affected people within the villages, with limited access to farmland or basic services nearby. Markets were partially functional with limited availability and high food and non-food items prices. It was determined that: Food, WASH, Health, S/NFI, and health are the priority needs of affected people and IDPs in the assessed localities. According to CFSVA (June 2022), the level of food insecurity in Blue Nile state increased by 19%, from 31% in 2021 to 50% in 2022. Moreover, water quality is poor with high turbidity. There is no water treatment or chlorination and jerry cans for water storage at the household level. The primary water source is surface water from the river. Water yards need to be rehabilitated. Additionally, Poor hygiene conditions and there is no soap available. There are no hand washing facilities in the schools. Several types of GBV, including physical violence, were reported by interviewed women during attacks. GBV continues to be unreported, and survivors cannot receive support due to insecurity, fear, stigma, and a lack of community awareness. In addition, there is also a significant shortage of PSS and case management services. Community-based protection networks (CBPNs) are not functional, and there is a critical need for PSS and counseling services. 560,000 people in Blue Nile are in need of humanitarian assistance. It is estimated that 37% of the region residents need immediate FSL assistance (HNO 2023). In the proposed localities, among conflict affected communities VSF Germany intends to address critical FSL needs to 64,885 individual including 14,924 men; 15,572 women; 16,870 boys; and 17,519 girls. In regards to WASH the project will target 37,900 people affected by limited access to safe and clean water for domestic use and WASH NFIs especially among IDPs including 8717 men; 9,096 women; 9,854 boys; and 10,233 girls. GBV prevention and response awareness, support community-based protection network and PSS, as well as referral services is intended to target 9000 people.Fierce clashes erupted between SAF and RSF in multiple cities across Sudan (April 15, 2023) including Khartoum, Al-Fashir, Al-Obeid, Geneina, etc. This devastating development in Sudan resulted in killing of more than 3000 people, injury of 6,000 people, and displacement of about 2 million people inside and outside the country. The international community is calling for a ceasefire and opening humanitarian passages to deliver aid to those affected, however so far there has been no positive reponse. The current disruption to lives, livelihoods, institutions, systems and programs will definitely have an impact on the fragile humanitarian situation in all of Sudan with expected impacts on food security. Accorrding to the revised HRP issued on 17 May 2023 people in need of humanitarian assitance in Sudan increased fom 15.8 m to 24.7 m and people in need of FSL services from 11.7 m to 19.9 m. There are shortages in access to water, fuel, cash, and health services, which will exacerbate the dire situation of people directly affected by conflict and all across Sudan if this crisis continues. The proposed support is planned to provide life-saving services to people affected by the current conflict and to mitigate worsening of their humanitatian conditions.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF-G will carry out intensive community mobilization and sensitization campaigns for the target beneficiaries along with the local partners, to effectively inform the target communities on the project and ensure participation and adoption of the project activities. The working methodology will orient the target people on accountability/CFMs and basic protection concepts. As well as the project beneficiaries will be sensitized on gender inclusion (at least 50% women participation in project activities will be ensured). Participation of girls/women and boys/men of all age groups with and without disabilities will be considered through: (a) beneficiary targeting for the different activities (i.e. beneficiary selection criteria which will be developed employing community participation approaches, to reach those who are most vulnerable among affected people including IDPs, returnees, pastoralists and farmers. With focus on families with malnourished children, female-headed households and families with disabled member(s)); (b) selection of water points (sites) to be rehabilitated or upgraded taking into account the safety of women and girls and other vulnerable members of the communities; (c) ensuring women representation in all community committees (at least 30%); (d) CFMs will assist in early detection of problems such as targeting (exclusion/inclusion) and misconduct; (e) the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be prioritized through assessing and identifying risks associated with different locations/activities, to ensure implementation/distribution sites and roads leading to these sites are safe for all people involved (particularly girls, women and disabled persons), and that beneficiaries will not face problems with theft, intimidation and extortion; (f) and special arrangements will be made for people with special needs, as appropriate. Moreover, VSF-G always tries to create equal access and opportunities for all community members to benefit from humanitarian interventions through adequate community sensitization; inclusive participatory beneficiary selection and verification processes; and implementing Accountability to the affected Population (AAP) and Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFMs). Through the abovementioned approaches we expected to be able to reach girls/women and boys/men of all age groups with and without disabilities equally with the planned project activities. VSF-G fully considers “do no harm” in its activities through application of conflict-sensitivity, inclusive targeting of people from different ethnic groups, pastoralists and farmers, selection of distribution sites, and sites for different facilities (water supply infrastructure). The integrated project design addresses needs across multiple sectors including protection mainstreaming in WASH and FSL.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Planning of this project interventions is majorly based on the feedback from the target communities and other key stakeholders in the target locations. VSF-G adopts Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. VSF-G approach focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and marginalized groups. Community participation will be promoted through all the stages of the project (planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation), community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure the project is well understood by target communities, raise the communities’ awareness with regard to their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the program partners towards them, as well as to identify the most appropriate channels for communication. Community-based committees including representatives from a cross-section (with respect to age, gender and diversity) will be formed to participate in developing an effective communication strategy including appropriate complaint and feedback mechanisms (e.g. hotline, complaints/suggestions boxes), taking into consideration literacy rates, availability of communication devices and accessibility to VSF-G’s ad partners’ offices; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Community committees will also be involved in developing beneficiary selection process, follow-up of activity implementation and project evaluation
