Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Pochalla, Akobo, and Pibor Counties of Jonglei State targeting 40,000 beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees, and host community), due to continued conflict, the population has been disrupted and trapped in villages of Pochalla, Pibor, and Akobo not able to access basic facilities including market where they can buy food or replenish farm tools and equipment, while community fully depends on fishing, there is currently limited fishing gear to carry out this activity. community is struggling to make their needs fit within little available resources has resulted in destitution and malnutrition among young children and elderly persons among these communities, every effort is made to stabilize the situation has never been successful since the community lacks basic tools and seeds leading to very severe food insecurity. This project contributes to cluster objective NO.2 and 3 which is set to increase vulnerable household food production to protect the erosion of livelihoods through agricultural seeds and fishing inputs. The project aims at improving food security by increasing the capacities of the affected people through training on best modern farming practices besides, increase kitchen gardens distribution of improved quick maturing vegetable crops alongside hand tools in Pochalla, Pibor, and Akobo counties. During the project 40,000 beneficiaries (30,000 females and 10,000 male) will be provided with assorted vegetable kits out of which 1650 (1000 women and 650 men) will be trained on kitchen gardening and expected to act as farmer promoters in kitchen gardening practices, the training will as well take care of on the nutritious value of vegetables. Other 6,000 beneficiaries (2,500 women and 3,500 men) will be provided with fishing kits out of which 520 beneficiaries (310 women and 210 men) will be trained on post-harvest management-fish processing, preservation, hygienic handling of fish to minimize spoilage. During distribution, CIDO will ensure that complaint and feedback desk is made available for the beneficiaries, and post-distribution monitoring will be done after. The project implementation strategies will include capacity development for sustainability; protection programming; area-based development approach; Do no harm, conflict sensitivity planning, and gender and youth mainstreaming and empowerment. This project will also be integrated with other cluster projects such as Nutrition and Shelter/NFIs, child protection, and health for joint missions and other projects that CIDO are undertaking in the same locations such a multi-sectoral (Protection, FSL, Nutrition, Health, and WASH) in Akobo East supported by SSHF, a livestock project in Akobo East supported by UNFAO, a Shelter/NFI project in Akobo West supported by SSHF and a GBV, Child Protection and Shelter/NFI project in Pochalla and Pibor supported by SSHF, among others. The project will be guided by the following principles and in relation to AAP: leadership and empowerment of women and girls; women and girls engaged in all aspects of the space; safety and accessibility; integrated into the community and contextually relevant; inclusivity - all women and girls consulted – especially marginalized; coordination for multi-sectorial support and services – and referral; outreach to communities to encourage women and girls to participate – and activities organized in communities; feedback mechanisms in place for women and girls – and for the community; safety and ethically data collection and management; sustainability of FFS – including transition and exit strategy.

1 - Needs

A recent Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Report (October 2020 – July 2021), issued on 18th December 2020, projected that between December 2020 and March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11 000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County in Pibor Administrative Area. In the second projection period of April to July 2021, the report indicates an estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with 31,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pochalla and Akobo County (11,000). Around 1.4 million children below five years of age, the highest in three years, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021. According to the recent Humanitarian Needs Overview (2021), Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or above, with 1.7 million people (85 percent). Contrary to the IPC official release, the IPC Global Support Unit released two reports from the Famine Review Committee on 11th December 2020. One was a Real-Time Quality Review report indicating a likelihood of populations in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo, showing higher levels of Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) households consistent with the data in the FSNMS+ and the documented contributory factors; and the other was a Famine Review Committee report that classified four Payams (Pibor, Likuangole, Gumuruk, and Verteth) in Pibor County as ‘Famine Likely’ for the current and extending into the peak of the lean season.South Sudan Humanitarian Situation Report of December 2020 recommends an immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance has been recommended to save lives and avert the total collapse of livelihoods in the severely food insecure (IPC Phase 5) populations in Pibor and Akobo. Households who are in IPC Phase 5 will need immediate emergency food assistance complemented with emergency livelihood and livestock support across farming, agricultural and pastoral communities, for the medium- and longer-term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity. There are extreme levels of food insecurity with the exhaustion of emergency level coping strategies.The magnitude and severity of food insecurity are anticipated to increase and worsen respectively in 2021 compared to 2020. The direct and indirect impact of COVID-19 and related pressure put on markets and the movement of goods; an upsurge in sub-national violence, especially in Pochalla, Pibor, and Akobo; desert locust invasions in the east of the country; recurring flooding in areas still recovering from the 2019 floods; a protracted macro-economic crisis; higher food prices; and a devaluating local currency are all threatening the food security of people. Other factors that will influence the food security situation in the project locations include the seasonality of livelihoods, grain stocks, prices, floods movement of livestock, nutrition status of people, and ongoing conflict.According to REACH Port and Road Monitoring (PRM) of September 2020, in Akobo Town, the number of recorded inbound individuals from Ethiopia increased slightly between March and September. Continued inbound population movements may place additional pressure on already limited sources of food. Returnees, (both IDP and refugee), reportedly had a negative impact on the ability of most people to access enough food in 90% of the assessed settlements in which the presence of returnees was reported, in September (41% in March). They need livelihood opportunities to improve their resilience. The REACH report of September 2020 also indicated that clashes in Pibor resulted in widespread displacement and livelihood collapse.

2 - Project activities

CIDO will intervene in cluster objectives 2 and 3 with the following activities: Community mobilization and sensitization- CIDO will inform and sensitize the community of the forth-coming distribution of livelihood kits ahead of distribution. Beneficiary identification and Registration- CIDO will carry out the beneficiary selection in close collaboration with the main stakeholders including FSL Cluster, local authorities, rural communities, and any other relevant authority as per cluster beneficiary identification criteria targeting the most vulnerable households from IDPs and host communities in Akobo and Pibor. Preposition of seeds and tools – CIDO will receive seeds and tools from the FSL cluster and preposition the supplies for distribution to the affected most vulnerable communities in Akobo and Pibor. Distribution of livelihood kits, seeds, and tools – The kits will be distributed to the affected community considering the needs of the most vulnerable and people with special needs first to avoid keeping them in long queues Training on Kitchen gardening – CIDO will give kitchen gardening orientation to beneficiaries both in Akobo and Pibor, the trained persons will act as lead farmers and train other community members on handling a new variety of vegetable seeds including carrots, kales, and onions. Post Distribution monitoring – CIDO will work with 40% of total beneficiaries to carry out PDM to determine views of the beneficiaries and intention of items distributed. Protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods: Potential livelihood options specifically targeting women will be restored to increase food availability and improve household diet diversification. Livelihoods kits will be provided to 40,000 beneficiaries: Indicators • Provide 40,000 beneficiaries (10,000 women and 10,000 men) with assorted vegetable kits • Train 1650 (1000 women and 650 men) farmer promoters in kitchen gardening • Train vegetable beneficiaries on the nutritious value of vegetables. • Train women beneficiaries on infant and young child feeding using locally available foods • Train 650 farmers (410 women and 240 men) on improved farming practices, post-harvest management-preservation, hygienic handling to minimize spoilage.Output Vegetable Kits: • 15,000 beneficiaries (9,000 women and 6,000 men) provided with assorted vegetable kits • 1650 (1000 women and 650 men) farmer promoters trained in kitchen gardening • Vegetable beneficiaries trained on the nutritious value of vegetables. • Women beneficiaries trained on infant and young child feeding using locally available foods Fishing Kits: • 6,000 beneficiaries (2,500 women and 3,500 men) provided with fishing kits • 520 fishmongers (310 women and 210 men) trained on post-harvest management-fish processing, preservation, hygienic handling of fish to minimize spoilage Animal health:• Vaccinate 15,000 animals and treat 1,000 of them. • Establish and maintain 6 livestock vaccine cold chains. • Train 100 CAHWs and support through cost recovery. Resilience• Train 500 HHs on natural resource management • Conduct monthly peace meetings with signed resolutions. • Support the formation of 6 savings and loan groups.

3 - Project indicators

The success of the project will be measured through various monitoring activities which will include site visits, use of cluster monitoring tools, and use of CIDO’s MEAL plan to be developed for this project. The M&E exercise will be guided by the Logical framework and the in-built standard indicators for this response set by the cluster. CIDO will be keen to capture progress from means of verification (MOV) and where applicable monitoring from the baseline surveys. The M&E team will collect data using the cluster tools and conduct data analysis focusing on receiving project feedback from all people of both gender, all ages, and people living with disabilities in a participatory manner. The M&E tools are tailored to capture information from people with disabilities, women, men, boys, girls, and the elderly on how the project has impacted their lives. Both qualitative and quantitative data collection methods will be used in all project locations and will include the use of HH questionnaires, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII), and observations. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all project locations to ascertain the impact of the assistance in terms of quality of items distributed, access and safety of distribution sites, the safety of women and girls traveling to and going back from distribution sites, and use of the received inputs. The M&E team will compile reports and share with state focal points, national cluster, and other stakeholders. The report recommendations will be used to adjust the project where applicable and inform future programming.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During AAP sessions , CIDO team and committee on AAP and GBV will provide information on PSEA to all beneficiaries including how to identify and report PSEA cases. The beneficiaries will be made aware that vendors ,CIDO contractors, CIDO staff and volunteers are all required to sign codes of conduct and commit to protect beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse. IEC materials on PSEA will be sourced from the Lead (UNFPA) and translated and distributed in project locations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CIDO will establish a Complaint and Feedback mechanism and establish Help desk during distributions. There will be Community communication and engagement (CCE) before the project begins to harmonise the expectations of the community in regards to the project activities vs the available services. During CCE , consultations will be made with communities to establish committees which will oversee the distribution, GBV issues, and AAP issues. The committee on AAP will ensure that voices of the most vulnerable are heard .The team will be careful to provide opportunity to people living with disability, women and girls to air their views in their language through separate FGDs and ensure that they are included in various leadership roles like being part of the complaint committees.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) is committed to ensuring that frontline staff engage in timely and proper Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) while being guided by the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will conduct entry and exit meetings prior to the end of the project implementation to have discussions with not only community leaders but also directly with all targeted beneficiaries who are women, men girls and boys to capture their interests and recommendations and asses the vulnerability. In addition, CIDO will conduct awareness sessions on AAP rights and how to use the established Complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs). The AAP committee composed of community members representing men, women, elderly, youth , people with disability will be established to ensure communities rights and dignity is respected by all stakeholders.There will be an open-door policy, complaints, response, and feedback mechanism (CRFM) boxes, hotline and CRFM desks set up at each distribution site where beneficiaries will be given opportunity to air their feedback to the project team through using both leaders and open-door policy. The hotline will be linked directly to the management. This will facilitate Communication, transparency, and Community full engagement.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CIDO team will compile feedback from the commitees and beneficiaries and present to the management in Juba who will confirm the grievances and compliments . Feedback on how the complaints have been addressed on a daily basis on ground will also be shared with the management and will be used to define further actions to improve service delivery. There will be project review meetings where project staff and management will analyse the usefulness of the CRFM , information gathered and how the system can be made better to encourage all beneficiaries to use it freely. Feedback from beneficiaries will be used to improve programming and adjust weaker areas accordingly.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The CIDO team will coordinate with protection partners to ensure beneficiaries are aware of the services available and the referral system/pathways. Notably, referral systems and frameworks contain vital information on Health, Psychosocial support/Mental Health, Legal, livelihood, safety, etc, and contacts of service providers. Joint activities will be carried out with protection partners in all project locations which will include safety audits, dissemination of referral pathways, and outreach messages conducted at all distribution sites. CIDO staff will be trained on safe reporting of GBV cases and will be provided with GBV pocket guides for non-GBV actors to equip them with basic information for identifying and assisting GBV survivors. Women facing HLP issues will be assisted and referred to HLP partners and legal actors for further support.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This will be achieved through coordination with other sectoral partners to integrate disaster risk mitigation strategies known to cause displacements and aggravates food insecurity like conflict and floods. CIDO will coordinate capacity-building activities on life-skills, coping strategies, and durable solutions with partners while promoting actionable risk mitigation strategies like building flood control structures (dykes), conflict sensitivity, and promoting peaceful co-existence among communities.This project will coordinate with Shelter and NFI partners to conduct joint distributions of NFIS with FSL inputs i.e fishing kits, Survival kits with clear messages on durable solutions. In addition, CIDO will conduct joint outreach messages with other sectoral partners on community-led disaster risk mitigation, early warning, and response mechanisms, and protection of livelihoods from loss.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The cluster, ICCG, and OCHA encourage joint assessments with other partners and sectors and the cluster/project assessment tools capture the needs of beneficiaries based on gender, age, and disability to inform equitable intervention of cross-sectoral services. Safety audits will be conducted by CIDO to ensure meeting or distribution locations are safe to vulnerable populations including women, older persons, children, and persons with disabilities. Consultations will be done using FGDs separately to women, the elderly, persons with disabilities, etc when deciding meeting or distribution locations that are comfortable/accessible to everyone. CIDO will participate in Joint interventions with WASH, Health, Protection, and Nutrition to provide cross-sectoral basic services. The project team will use the existing referral pathways to refer beneficiaries to other sectoral services in circumstances where the services cannot be provided. CIDO will coordinate with other agencies on the ground so that services/activities such as distribution are done at the same time in the same location. This will not only save on time and movement but will ensure persons with disabilities elderly, women can access different cross-sectoral services equitably. In addition, the project will make use of gender analysis reports to adjust activities accordingly and meet the needs of both men and women, boys and girls in dignity and safety.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

• The project will conduct Joint assessments and joint outreach campaigns with other sectoral partners in Health, Nutrition, and WASH to provide clear messaging on the linkages of FSL services to reducing malnutrition among children under 5 years. The activities will also allow communities to provide useful feedback to be used by partners in advocacy for nutrition.• CIDO team will make use of the referral system and ensure that malnourished children who accompany their mothers to distribution sites are referred appropriately for services.• Kitchen gardens and demonstration on cooking nutritious foods will be jointly coordinated with Nutrition partners and will reduce cases of malnutrition• Distribution of farm inputs including seeds will promote food security and considerably reduce malnutrition cases• Capacity building of fishmongers and farmers on good farming practices will ensure that farm products retain the nutritional value and reduce post-harvest losses.
