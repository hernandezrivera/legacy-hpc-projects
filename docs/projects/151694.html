Project Summary

This programme is designed to improve the immediate food needs for the people affected by floods, drought, conflict and diseases, that are in the acute food insecurity category in South Central Somalia regions. The purpose is supporting them to protect their livelihoods, enable local food production and adoption of resilient strategies to mitigate against the recurrent shocks. The project conception is that the beneficiary targeted will be determined based on the needs, which shall majorly be determined by IPC levels, and mostly IDPs, with host community. The interventions will focus on emergency response for 2000 dis-aggregated acutely affected households, and these will benefit from unconditional cash transfer, with the amount of distribution being MEB-based per region of target. Transitional interventions will also be implemented targeting communities that need to restore their lost livelihoods through building of assets while participating in conditional and restricted cash transfers. These include agriculture input to 200 farmers through introduction to new affordable solar powered drip irrigation system for non-riverine small-scale farmers, fodder production by the destitute agro-pastoralists, animal health support targeting 6,000 pastoralists and provision of productive assets and enterprise development and skills enhancement for 200 youth. ZZF will mainstream protection in its programming ensuring that only deserving cases are selected to gain in the project. In addition, gender will be a major consideration through determination of intervention participants. A PDM will be conducted for all distribution activities to measure effectiveness of the project.

Needs

FSNAU’s Post Gu 2018 assessment estimates 4.6 million people are acutely food insecure (IPC phase 2 or worse), including 2.5 million children, continue to require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 1.5 million are in crisis or emergency. Communities that lost most of their livestock during the 2016-2017 drought and those affected by conflict, as well as this year’s devastating floods and cyclone, will take longer to recover. Immediate access to food for populations affected by floods, cyclones, drought, conflict and diseases to address acute food insecurity and support to flood- and drought-affected populations to protect their livelihoods, enable local food production and prevent adoption of “crisis” strategies are necessary. Livestock herd depletion and degradation of agricultural land have reduced traditional male livelihood activities, whilst forced displacement into IDP settlements has further limited opportunities for income generation. Internally displaced are the most vulnerable and in dire need of immediate and long-term assistance. UNHCR estimates the total number of internally displaced Somalis to be 2.6 million. REACH (2018) reports overall loss of livelihood and income that drought-induced displacement caused major protection concerns within households, with respondents reporting loss of livestock and other economic assets. However, the adverse impacts of the 2016-2017 severe drought are expected to persist, especially among people who became destitute and got displaced due to the drought and among pastoralists who lost most of their animals. Those facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support. Livelihood support is also required for people who are Stressed (IPC Phase 2). Between August and December 2018 and onwards, an estimated 294 200 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including 55 200 who are likely to be severely malnourished.

Activities

1.1: Provision of food vouchers for 2500HH IDPs and affected people for three months to be able to rebuild their own coping capacity. 1.2: Provide unconditional and multi-purpose Cash Transfer 2000 HH through mobile money transfer to IPC 3& 4 people in targeted areas. A1.3: Provide Conditional cash transfers (Cash for Assets) to 1500 HH drought affected population that live in vulnerability criteria that meet the classification of IPC 2&3. 2.1: Provision of complete package of agriculture inputs to 100 non-riverine small-scale farmers including (seeds, solar powered pumping system and drip irrigation kit ) to build community resilience to shocks. 3.1: Conduct animal health best practice campaigns for 10,000 pastoralist communities through livestock deworming and pastoralist field schools to protect livestock and improve production4.1 Provide business grants to 200 vulnerable IDP households to initiate SMEs. These will be in different forms targeting economic empowerment of the IDPs in the informal settlements to increase their household income for the vulnerable and food insecure households.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The targeted areas of operation are areas, where ZZF has an acceptance and experience in working over the past 15 years to effectively design and implement programs that lead to durable/resilience outcomes by building on experiences in the context, services and assistance provided in the past and technical capacities developed over this period of operation. The action has conditional cash-based interventions which aim at building community assets which assure resilience of the communities to shocks. The project also aims at diversifying livelihood options for the affected households and this directly contributes to the beneficiary households’ resilience to recurrent shocks, affecting the traditional livelihood sources. The project seeks to build the capacities of the beneficiaries in various approaches to production.The project will introduce to the beneficiary a very easy, affordable and sustainable farming technology by providing drip irrigation kit and solar powered pumps for non-riverine farmers where the scarcity of water is common. This will help community to produce crops with out need for huge water. ZZF will ensure participation of all cadres of the affected communities in the project interventions. Collective decision-making will be at the centre of the implementation processes to ensure ownership, which is a precise for sustainability.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Conflict and violence has taken a toll on the project plan target populations and the design of this project consider reduction and mitigation altogether on the effects of conflict and violence. The project aims to build the capacities of the affected communities on Natural resource management. This will entail training on management of land, water and pasture resources, which are majorly the source of conflicts in Somalia. Restoration of community assets and diversification of livelihood sources will also reduce conflicts emanating from completion on scarce resources. Having broad experience in peacebuilding, ZZF works very closely with communities and local authorities, ensuring that local laws, customs and culture are adhered to, and avoiding action and advocacy that may be perceived as offensive. Rigorously apply ‘do no harm’ principles. Programme informed by regular conflict analysis and understanding of politics at different levels
