Project Summary

Humanitarian crises affect each person differently depending on their gender, age, disability and other personal characteristics. Older people and people with disabilities are often overlooked in humanitarian relief and response and they may find it harder than others to access age appropriate health services.
There are clear gaps in treatment of Non-Communicable Diseases and palliative care across the camps, older people and those with disabilities are struggling to get full access to health services.
Based on HelpAge study (covered 6 camps only) September 2018, 46% of all older people self-reported to have serious psychological distress and access to mental health services and psychosocial support at Primary Health Care (PHC) level as well as community-based prevention, screening and early referral will be considered a priority to improve the mental well-being of the most vulnerable segment of population such as older people and those with disabilities. the study also reviled that 45% of all older people require to take medicine for long term condition and struggle to get medicine and follow up by medical staff in particular those with NCD,s and have multi-morbidity. For the proper geriatric care, older people need regular home-based care, outreach services, accompaniment support, proper counseling of caregiver, organizing health camp, need to support them for emergency health service like surgery or hospital treatment.
HelpAge and its partners RIC and Youth Power in Social Action (YPSA) currently run 10 Age Friendly Spaces (AFS) in Ukhiya Rohingya camp and host community. The AFSs are static centers where older people can access basic Health screening and referral, Psychosocial Support, Physiotherapy, medicines etc. Older people has physical challenges specially the intersection of PwD. They have limited mobility. They are facing mountainous challenges in accessing health services from static center. To ensure their services, 50% health staff will work as a mobile medical team to provide outreach and homebased services.
Age Friendly Spaces, outreach teams and home-based care will provide integrated care for older people as well as promoting inclusion and protection of older people at all levels. Activities include:
• Providing preventative, curative and health promotion services for older people and those with disabilities through direct service provision and/or referrals both at the Age Friendly Spaces and through the Health Outreach Teams
• Home based care with a specific focus on older people and people with disabilities who are shelter-bound
• Partnership and technical capacity building with other service providers and ensuring a strong referral pathway and monitoring care plans with other service providers
• Older people’s participation and community engagement in designing interventions, monitoring and evaluation.
• Capacity building of local staff and agencies to support older people and those with disabilities
• Organizing Health camp and awareness campaign
• Support older people for emergency health services from hospital.

Needs

There are clear gaps in treatment of Non-Communicable Diseases and palliative care across the camps, older people and those with disabilities are struggling to get full access to health services. As the majority of non- communicable daises are among older people HelpAge international and partners plan to expand the services of AFS (Age Friendly Spaces) to other camps where access to health care and other services limited for older people. AFS also become the places where older people socialize and interact with different people and get information on available services. Older people already participating in designing, implementing and monitoring the projects. Their role has been extremely important to address the complaint and feedback from community members. AFS has been listed also one of the model that Rohyngya Repatriation office considered as priority to keep functioning. HelpAge International and partners tailored all the services around AFS so older people and those with disabilities can get the best services when they need through home-based care. More than 20% of older people has single or multiple disabilities. Project play pivotal role in supporting older people and those with disability as well as had an impact to capacity building of local staff. We are confident that expanding services to other camps will allow us to support older people and those with disabilities and this can have huge impact to individual as well support to achieve SDG goal. Health needs are expected to exacerbate significantly during the monsoon season. Based on risk mapping conducted, large parts of the camp based population are directly affected by floods and landslides. In addition, there is significant risk of a cyclone causing major damage to the refugee camps, limiting access, causing casualties and fatalities, and impacting existing health facilities. Through technical support we are planning to work with both national and international partners to mainstreaming disability in the humanitarian agenda as a strategy for achieving equality for persons with disabilities and older people in terms of accessing the available resources and where possible link them with other comprehensive services. Study carried out by HelpAge International in September 2018 including FGD with older FDMP in 5 camps, both genders highlighted that the needs of older people are not addressed, and their participation limited there are many reasons why organizations fail to incorporate the needs of older people in their emergency policies and programmes. The main reasons highlighted are: lack of experience in dealing with old-age issues, lack of technical expertise; resources are limited, assessments are not designed to capture the needs of the older people. Many organizations not considered older people as vulnerable, and often data on them are not collected. The high turnover and busy schedules of humanitarian staff leave limited time to learn or address issues that are not seen to fall directly within an organisation’s mandate or priorities’’ Global Heath Advisor and Global Health Advisor will work and develop training materials/package how to address the needs of older people and those with disabilities use inclusion and protection as an umbrella. Strong advocacy message will be developed to make sure inclusion of all older people and those with disabilities through multi-sectoral interventions.

Key project/performance indicators

90% older people and those with disability have access to lifesaving heath services; 85% older people and those with disabilities have access to non-communicable medicine both through AFS, outreach team and supported by community volunteers; 60% older people able to access PSS and referral to secondary care on MHPSS; 100% local staff trained on geriatric heath and ageing population needs as part of capacity building; 4000 home based care visits carried out; 10200 older people outreach by paramedics and community health Volunteer
