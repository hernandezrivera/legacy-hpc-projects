Project Summary


According to FSNAU post Gu 2020 assessment report indicated that 5.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance across Somalia this was due to the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks , In addition, 849,900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. The 2020 Gu season exhibited erratic performance. The rains started as early as late March in many parts of Somalia, then intensified and expanded in April, with heavy rain leading to riverine and flash floods. However, rainfall in May was widely below average. Severe riverine and flash floods in April and early May caused significant population displacement and damage to property, infrastructure, farmland, and crops.
Despite multiple shocks in 2020, high levels of sustained humanitarian assistance and government support have played a critical role in stabilizing and preventing worse acute food insecurity outcomes in Somalia. According to the Somalia Food Security Cluster, an average of 1.85 million people received food assistance monthly from April to August. Approximately 400 000 people also benefitted from government-led rural and urban programmes. In April, government has also enacted a 20-100 percent tax exemption on imported food commodities in order to mitigate the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 on the population. Based on results of assessments conducted in July and August 2020, approximately 1.3 million people are currently facing Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or 1) higher) outcomes through September 2020 in the presence of humanitarian food assistance. An additional 2.5 million people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people experiencing acute food insecurity to 3.8 million
sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance and government support are currently preventing more severe outcomes in many areas. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly.
SRC project will therefore, intends to provide immediate access to unconditional and conditional cash assistance to 6,400 most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in IDPs and host communities, it will also provide capacity enhancement, farming tools as well as farm inputs to these target farmers in Awdhegle-Afgooye in lower Shabelle and Dhusamareeb district in Galgadud as well as well as agricultural seed inputs and tools to increase their farming production thus improving the nutrition of the target population. The project will provide through cash for work will enable to provide immediate access to household income thus improving the overall well-being of the target communities. SRC will ensure to coordinates the foods security and livelihood activities closely with other humanitarian actors and concern ministries at regional and federal level to ensure better information sharing among the south west and Galgadud regional states in the target district

Project Activities

1.Provision of emergency unconditional cash transfer to 6,400 (3,500 IDPs and 3,000 HC) vulnerable IDP and host community households considering 60% of the beneficiaries registered as vulnerable women and disable children household in Lower Shabelle and Galgduud regions. Each household will have received 95 USD per months for 3 months’ period. The unconditional cash transfer will provide immediate life-saving and life-sustaining opportunity among the vulnerable communities in emergencies hence this will be enhancing the household food basket thus improving the livelihood. 2.Provide cash for work activities to 200 men and women household heads and enable create short term job opportunities and create income to the vulnerable drought displaced and AWD affected IDPs and host communities to enable improve the community livelihood structure hence provide alternative livelihood. The CFW beneficiaries will work 26 working days per month for three months and they will be provided 6 USD per person per day.3.Sensitize vulnerable household benefiting from unconditional cash transfer on nutrition, health and hygiene promotion for food preparation to enable reduce malnutrition and disease hence improve community living standard. 4.Conduct training to capacity building farmers (30 men and 40 women household headed) on proper preparing, harvesting as well as management of farm inputs storage hence enhancing their knowledge and thus inculcate skills for their livelihood, the farmers will be provided both farm input and farming tools to ensure enhanced capacity and management of their farming, the farm input and the farming tools distribution will largely target female headed household as well as the disable group to ensure inclusivity of the affected population

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

During the flash floods, drought and displacement community assets such as homestead, animals, which are mostly sole communal economic back borne eroded. This has impact on household level food consumption patterns, and the power balance within the families. Elderly people have also been affected due to lack of essential food and medicines and reduced availability of care. Therefore, the project will particularly target these vulnerable groups, which includes women, children, elderly persons and ethnic minority groups in Lower Shabelle and Galgadud regions in Somalia, people with disability, and provide them with lifesaving assistance to protect them from further harm and refraining them from adopting negative coping mechanisms. Direct beneficiary’s selection will be ‘inclusive’ and based on the consultation with communities by the respective local administration following the humanitarian principles. A proposed list of beneficiaries (from community consultations) will be validated by the project in a door-to-door beneficiary verification exercise. Beneficiary selection shall not be restricted by documentation provided available with the beneficiaries and solely the selection criteria shall be followed. Local authorities and relevant government departments will be consulted for final selection of beneficiaries at the targeted areas. Additionally, affected communities and local government authorities will be engaged in identification of the beneficiaries most in need. The selected beneficiaries will be registered and provided with unique cards for future verification and monitoring purposes.The cash will be transferred through mobile company/financial service provider which will be selected based on availability of network and/or services, readiness and ability to provide a prompt delivery, and reasonable service charge. This service will be purchased locally and in accordance with SRC policies, SRC propose that providing unconditional cash assistance and conditional cash grant is the most appropriate intervention at this immediate response stage and will allow beneficiaries to priorities their most pressing need

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

SRC market bases intervention aims to responds to IDPs and host communities affected by drought coupled with the COVID-19 pandemic and locust infestation that have hindered the livelihood of the already in crisis communities in South central Somalia, SRC will ensure provision of life-saving and life sustaining cash response to the affected population in Lower Shabelle and Galgadud region target location through vouchers for unconditional and conditional cash transfer to vulnerable household. Providing a market based approach also strengthens the sustainability of the action by ensuring the operation and maintenance of the assets by either the targeted communities’ relief Committees.

Mitigation Measures

With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing permanent structure or own a land for food security and livelihood center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, SRC will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing cordial relationship between them and with the host communities and local authorities. Based on the food security and livelihood Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the IDPs and host communities. Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. SRC intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneficiaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health and food security and livelihood infrastructure, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, SRC will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identification and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the cash collection sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women SRC will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement on better livelihood. Building community structures including local temporary spaces will have provided in consultation with community/member who owns the land to ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities seeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination.
