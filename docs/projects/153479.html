Project Summary

Under the proposed project, farming inputs and training on better agricultural methods for staple crops and vegetable production will be provided to 5000 vulnerable households (30,000 people). Within this number, 300 female headed households (1,800 people) will be assisted with a cash intervention in addition to the kit mentioned above by virtue of their high CSI scores. At the same time, 1000 fisher folk households (6,000 people) will receive training on better fishing methods and also receive a fishing kit. In total, 6000 households (36,000 people) will be targeted.
The intervention strategy will be based on the livelihood activities of target beneficiaries and aligned to the seasonal calendar. The response plan aims at ensuring that at any given time, household needs are covered. Lean periods and planting seasons should be well covered to discourage consumption of crop seeds or engaging in negative coping practices in order to fulfill household needs.
Livelihood activities and support through this project will be coordinated with the food security cluster at county, state and national level to avoid duplication. Targeting of beneficiaries for this activity will be done based on the Food Consumption Score (FCS) as well as Coping Strategy Index (CSI).
In addition to rebuilding of livelihood assets, proposed activities are intended to have a multiplier effect on improving household dietary intake and livelihood coping capacity. In the short run, income from any of the proposed activities can be used to acquire inputs for other activities in addition to providing for basic household needs. This will in turn contribute to strengthening the resilience of the target population.
From a protection point of view, in order to combat negative coping strategies, this action proposes to use livelihood activities to attain education and protection objectives. As an entry point, targeting of beneficiaries will be based on the Food Consumption Score (FCS). However, to ensure that households most a risk of resulting to negative coping mechanisms are included in the intervention, the Coping Strategy Index will also be used simultaneously. WFP and cluster approved tools will be used for these analyses.

Needs

Pibor County, Jonglei State, is among the most underdeveloped regions in South Sudan, with poor or no road access, limited basic services and lack of livelihood opportunities for years. Successive years of insecurity, disease and insufficient rains have resulted in severe food insecurity ever since civil strife broke out in 2013. To add onto this, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) for October - December 2018 is expected to be Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) as well as projections for January - March 2019. This is according to Pibor County Food Security and Livelihoods Brief conducted by REACH in April 2018. The same report confirms that in October 2017, the Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey indicated the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) at 26.8% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) at 8%. Frequent cattle raids have also been linked to food insecurity. Border disputes as well as access to water and grazing areas have been the primary conflict drivers in the region. Tensions between communities are aggravated by ethnic grievances and perceptions, social and political marginalization, absence of infrastructure and basic services. In addition to all these factors, disease, insufficient rains and a reduction in available natural resources have resulted in cattle loss and failed harvests and consequentially severe food insecurity since 2013.Culturally, it is considered that men can fend for themselves by hunting and collecting wild fruits when food is scarce. However, children and elderly persons depend on the women for provision of food. Women will only eat when all others have eaten, if there is something left over, which makes them vulnerable compared to other groups. The loss of household assets has steadily been contributing to increasing levels of poverty and food insecurity making it more difficult for affected populations to sustain themselves. In addition, majority of the affected populations are increasingly resorting to the use of negative coping strategies; 60% reducing meals, 30% going without food in some days of the week, 45% selling their last female animal, 65% relies on less expensive food and relatives. This is according to a survey carried out by FCA in 2015.All these factors underpin Pibor as one of the most food insecure counties in the entire country. It is evident that affected populations in the proposed are of intervention are in dire need of humanitarian assistance and thus FCA’s intervention seeks to support target communities access food and rebuild their assets base in the short run.

Activities

Provision of farming inputs and training on better agricultural methods for staple crops and vegetable production:Before distribution of livelihood inputs, 5,000 targeted households will receive training on best agronomic practices including but not limited to land preparation, use of farm tools, vegetable production, cereal production, water for production & irrigation, post-harvest control, pest & disease control. FCA project officers and community mobilizers will provide continuous support to farmers throughout project implementation. The farmers will be organized into groups of 25 so as to maximize economies of scale realized from group dynamics. The following minimum package of seeds and tools will be provided; Vegetable seeds: 50g onion seeds, 50g okra seeds, 50g egg plants seeds, maize crops, and sorghum, 50g watermelon seeds, 50g kale (sukumawiki) seeds and 50g tomato seeds. Fast maturing crop seeds: 3kg maize seeds and 3 kg sorghum seeds. Farming tools: 1 hoe, 1 rake, 1 maluda, 1 axe and 1 watering can.Nevertheless, the community will still be consulted on the choice of preferred seeds and tools. Provision of training on better fishing methods and fishing gear:Before distribution of fishing gear, 1000 fisher folk households will receive training on designed to complement traditional fishing methods for an increased productivity. Training will include but not limited to fishing methods, use of fishing equipment, fish handling and preservation techniques. Regular follow-up from project staff will ensure that distributed fishing gear is put to the best use for better productivity. A fishing kit comprising of 2 fishing twines, 1 box of fishing hooks, and 1 fishing net. Nevertheless, the target group will also be consulted in the selection of the exact fishing kit. The fisher folk will be organized into fishing camps to promote group dynamics for better results. Cash intervention supporting female headed households:As a protection concern, women headed households are especially vulnerable to engage in negative coping strategies, just to provide for their families. Therefore 300 female headed households will receive an unconditional cash transfer of $80 in addition to the minimum kit mentioned above. The main selection criterion in this case will be the CSI. Cross cutting issues:Gender; 1. Gender dis-aggregated information and vulnerability criteria (FCS, CSI) will be at the base of the beneficiary selection process for all project activities. 2. Female headed households will be deliberately targeted.3. Follow-up assessments will be designed so as to gather gender and age specific information to effectively design and report on project activities. 4. Focus group discussions (FGDs) will be encouraged and held separately for the different groups to effectively identify gender specific needs.5. Project activities will consider the different roles played by different sexes, age groups and age sets.6. Project activities will encourage equal access to and participation in project activities. 7. Emphasis will be placed on encouraging women and the youth to be more involved in agricultural activities.Environment;1. Issues such as grazing areas, water points, farming land, irrigation for off season production will be included in project activities to address climate change and adaptation. 2. FCA has a well-defined climate tool that acts as a guideline on how to incorporate climate change and adaptation in projects. 3. Rain water conservation will be promoted to cater for lean periods.4. Soil conservation will be taken into consideration while farming.5. Proper irrigation methods will be encouraged to maximize water and minimize wastage.6. The use of animal manure and domestic waste (compost) will be encouraged.7. The use of traditional (organic) means of pest and weed control will also be encouraged as a means to protecting the environment.HIV/AIDS;1. Awareness raising and referencing of cases.
