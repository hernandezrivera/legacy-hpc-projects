Project Summary

In 2024, NRC Shelter & Settlement Programme aims to be part of the response to the Sector's two objectives. First, NRC will strive to improve the living conditions on refugee by providing assistance on Shelter and Non-food Items . Secondly, NRC will contribute Disaster Risk Management, solar support for better access and safety of Rohingya refugees in the camps, and vulnerable crisis-affected populations in the host communities of Ukhiya & Teknaf Upazilla, Cox's Bazar District.
Under the SCCCM Sector Objective 3, NRC will implement: Reinforcement of existing shelters technical support, provision of materials, transportation including HH-level site improvement and adaptation of shelter and surroundings for people with disabilities. Provision of new Mid Term Shelters (MTS) including repair and maintenance including household-level site improvement as well as porter and construction labor support for EVHHs; NRC will also provide NFI item to the households and provide porter support to EVHHs. ; Awareness sessions and technical support to increase community engagement and participation of women, older people, people with disabilities and gender diverse individuals in shelter and NFI activities; Provision of energy kits, which includes solar lamps, fan, panel and battery; Provision of NFIs, which will include essential household items (blankets, kitchen sets, clothes, among others) in coordination with camp-level sector FP and including porter support for EVHHs; NRC M&E will provide access to an independent and impartial complaints feedback mechanism. NRC Shelter & Settlement program will work closely through an integrated approach with NRC’s WASH, ICLA and Education teams and undertake all new construction, repair and maintenance works. Shelter beneficiaries will be engaged through awareness campaigns and capacity building trainings on: shelter upgrade and maintenance, small scale HHs level site improvement, Shelter strengthening including bracing and proper use of TDK, healthy living environments including the ventilation and aligned with Sectors minimum standards, self-improvement oriented to reduce protection risks for people with special vulnerabilities, safe cooking space improvements and gather knowledge on pre-monsoon. NRC will undertake these activities using a professional training team who has specialty in gender, disability inclusion and PSEA
Under the SCCCM Sector Objective 4, NRC’s main activities will provide support to refugees and the host communities to increase their resilience with equipment and tools against different natural disaster. NRC will provide solar streetlight for better access and safety of the project participants.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The needs of the Rohingya refugees and host communities have been identified from Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessments 2023, which identified shelter materials as the critical priority needs in the Rohingya camps and the energy fourth most critical need in the host communities; The Rohingya are acutely vulnerable to the effects of climate change as they are living in fragile shelters made of tarpaulin and bamboo. Existing shelter materials provided to the displaced population and improvements in camp infrastructure remain insufficient for protection against adverse weather events. Untreated bamboo has deteriorated due to beetle infestation. The J-MSNA 2023 found that 32% of refugees received Shelter assistance in last 12 months. Furthermore, 41% of refugee households not improving their shelters, the majority reported not improving it due to lack of humanitarian support (60%) or insufficient support (34%). Additionally, The 2022 NFI assessment conducted by the support of NPM showed 70% of assessed (3683) HHs had no portable solar lamps, 26% HHs had one portable solar lamp, and 4% had two portable solar lamps. Lack of light is especially concerning regarding Protection issues. For 83% HHs surveyed, the kitchen set items are insufficient for their daily use, and 17% HHs reported the items are sufficient.

NRC have the established CFM desk where BNF can share their complaints and feedback for improving the program. NRC always focus on BNF needs and BNF oriented service to quality delivery with a transparent and equitable manner with adapted and integrated approach. BNF engagement and participation are ensured for decision making while initiative the activities in terms of location, contribution and promote ownership. Moreover, NRC will make available its global expertise in Shelter and experience gained in Bangladesh in shelter/NFI response on these thematic areas.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The context presents specific risks for women, girls, persons with disabilities (PWD),religious minority groups, and gender-diverse populations, who continue to encounter heightened barriers in accessing Shelter services. To address this issue, meaningful participation of project participants is crucial. The project aims to enhance the involvement of these diverse groups and NRC is committed to ensure inclusivity throughout the project. All NRC Shelter invention will incorporate sex, age, and disability disaggregated data (SADDD). NRC recognizes the gender impacts, distinct needs, and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys, and girls.The organization acknowledges that women in particular, face socio-cultural barriers limiting their access to service provided for Shelter intervention. Additionally, special attention is given to the elderly and persons with disabilities, ensuring their specific vulnerabilities are taken into account through tailored assistance and accessibility measures. This holistic approach acknowledges that different age groups have distinct requirements, and the program is structured to cater to the specific needs of each demographic, fostering inclusivity and resilience within the community. To mitigate the risks of delivering inequitable assistance or reinforcing existing inequalities, NRC will implement community-led creative interventions, fostering meaningful participation of beneficiaries in decision-making processes. During distributions and training, NRC will create designated area for different age and gender group. Volunteers comprehensive training in child safeguarding, child protection, PSEA, and a code of conduct to minimize risks. The project aims to improve access to services for persons with disabilities through activities and referrals, with coordination maintained through meetings and capacity building with the Age & Disability Working Group

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

NRC prioritizes the safety and wellbeing of project participants by ensuring that its staff and volunteers undergo training on child safeguarding. NRC strictly adhere to organizational policies designed to protect project participants and stakeholders from sexual abuse and uphold the principles of the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The organization has established a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) with the goal of encouraging beneficiary feedback on interventions and methodologies employed. The CFM not only addresses general feedback but also handles reported cases of sexual abuse and PSEA. To support these efforts, a protection referral pathway is in place, ensuring that reported cases receive appropriate and timely case management. In order to effectively mainstream protection, NRC is committed to fostering community engagement. Communities will be empowered and equipped to actively participate in ensuring that they have access to services provided by NRC. This collaborative approach underscores NRC's dedication to creating an environment that is not only protective but actively involves the community in the safeguarding process. NRC has a designated PSEA focal point for reports concerning NRC staff and is engaged with the PSEA Network for regular training for the staff and new camp volunteers at the beginning of each project. PSEA, is one of the key elements for NRC way training that regular staffs, volunteers and partners are sensitized about.
