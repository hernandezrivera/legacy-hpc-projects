Project Summary

Project Goal: To create an enabling, supportive and protective community environment for the older men, women, persons with disability and their families where they feel safe and live with dignity. This project will target 15000 Participants among them 12000 will be from 14 Rohingya camps and 3000 from 2 unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf. This goal will be achieved through achieving the following four expected outcomes.
1. Community based protection services including Age-friendly spaces (AFS) and Community Based protection (CBP) structures are self-managed, accessible, supportive, and providing a protective environment for older people, persons with disabilities and their families.
Activities- 1.1). Conduct protection risks assessment of older people, persons with disability and their families.
1.2). Formation and capacity building of Community Based Protection Groups (40 groups* 25 persons=1000
1.3). Conduct training on community-based protection services to the CBPG members.
1.4). Provide a stipend/honorarium for members of community-based protection groups after they have received training:
1.5). Conduct monthly CBPG coordination and sharing meeting
1.6). Organize coordination meeting with protection focal points in the camps
1.7). Organize Mock drill for Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
2. A functional, responsive community-based complaint and feedback response mechanism is available
2.1). Established 16 community-based feedback and complaint mechanisms called “OP-PWD HELP DESK”: HelpAge have a long-established Complaint Response Mechanisms (CFRM) and other systems for ensuring accountability to the affected populations, which need to transform into Community Based Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism (CBCFRM), called “OP-PWD HELP DESK”.
2.2). Conduct awareness session on how to use CFRM and disseminate information and messages through loudspeakers on CFRM: Will disseminate information and messages through loudspeakers to create awareness about right to complaint and feedback and knowledge on how to use CFRM. Persons with disabilities, older people with disability (People of Concern-POCs) have access to required services as per their specific needs.
3. Persons with disabilities, older people with disability (People of Concern-POCs) have access to required services as per their specific needs
3.1: Assess specific needs and provision of assistive products to 1000 persons
3.2. Provide caregiver support to the 50-bed ridden older people:
3.3. Provide assistive products such as dignity kits, hygiene kits and NFIs to 2000 older people and person with disabilities.
3.4. Conduct life skills training to the persons with disability and older people (1000)-
3.5. Conduct Accessibility Audit: Conduct accessibility audit to identify specific barriers regarding access to facility-based services and specific features that needs to incorporate to make facilities accessible for the older persons and persons with disability which will help them to actively participate in the programme with safety and dignity.
3.6. Build capacity of humanitarian actors on age and disability inclusive programming:
3.7. Provide input support to the service facilities to make facilities accessible to persons with disability (30).
3.8. Organize coordination and advocacy meeting with others humanitarian actors:
HelpAge will implement this project at 14 camps (2E, 8E, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 18, 19, 24, 26 and 27) as well as 2 unions (Ratnapalong and Hnila) of Ukhiya and Teknaf sub- district. Project will focus on the specific areas of protection risk assessment of the older people as well as the person with disabilities, conduct awareness session to uphold their rights and dignity, strengthen and functional inclusive CFRM mechanism for older people as well as person with disabilities along with rehabilitation and dignity kit support (Hygiene kit, winter kit) etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"HelpAge International is an international non-governmental organization and the secretariat to a global network of 160 organizations whose goal is to create a fairer world for older people so they can live safe, healthy and dignified lives. In Cox’s Bazar Bangladesh, HelpAge has been working since 2017 in response to the Rohingya crisis by implementing Health, Nutrition, WASH, Livelihoods and Protection activities. In 2022, HelpAge adapted its work to respond to the emerging needs of older people and their family members through the provision of community-based protection services, establishing OP-HELP DESK, Disability Inclusion and Prevention of GBV response and services. This year HelpAge International is proposing 13 camps and 2 unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf which was 10 camps in the last joint response plan. The project is designed based on experiences and lessons learned from the last 5 years of providing humanitarian assistance in Cox’s Bazar. HelpAge International and its partners has designed and has been implementing a highly agile, age and disability-friendly and adaptable model which addresses the significant protection challenges to the older men, women, persons with disability and their family members in the 14 Rohingya camps (2E, 8E, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 18, 19, 24, 26 and 27) In Cox’s Bazar, there are numerous protection concerns for older people including exclusion, access to information and services, exploitation, violence, and disaster risks. According to HelpAge’s Needs and Gaps Assessment 2022, only 19% of older people participated and represented in the community-based activities and community-based committees. 41% of the older people stated that they are coping without any support. Most of the older people reported to being victims different forms of GBV including violence, abuse and neglect. Only 6% of older women believe that there are risks of sexual abuse and 35% considered isolation as one of the big risks they face compared to men. The role of women in the humanitarian response is particularly challenging due to the conservative culture of the affected community, exacerbated by the community reaction to the significant SGBV nature of the violence they fled. For older women, the intersection of age, disability and gender issues makes meaningful access to services particularly challenging. About 70% of the older people don’t know how to use Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism, 18% used the CFRM and only 1% received response. Due to lack of knowledge, capacity and awareness among the older people, service providers and community people regarding the age and disability inclusive services, issues and challenges the community people are not supportive and responsive as well. Through the work of community-based protection teams, HelpAge proactively seeks the inputs and advice of older women on how to better reach them, while remaining sensitive to the fact that it is often males of the family and community who make decisions about women’s activities. Lack of confidence, comfort, and proper platforms to raise issues of protection among community people limits the offer of protection service delivery, especially in hard-to-reach locations in camps. Rapport building with the community via outreach services, home-based protection activities, and close observations by OP Representatives have been increased by the project both to understand OP issues, and to build their confidence in reporting protection issues like trafficking. There is widespread recognition that the lack of statistical data disaggregated by age and gender makes it difficult to identify gendered patterns of inequality and discrimination in older age. Such timely and quality gender-age data can play a key role in improving situation ensuring that older women’s needs, and interests are holistically and adequately covered in the response mechanism.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

HelpAge International is an international non-governmental organization and the secretariat to a global network that wants is to create a fairer world where older people can live safe, healthy and dignified life. It has been working since 2017 in response to the Rohingya crisis by implementing Health, Nutrition, WASH, Livelihoods and Protection activities. In 2022, HelpAge adapted its work to respond to the emerging needs of older people and their family members through the provision of community-based protection services, establishing OP-HELP DESK and Disability Inclusion. Immediately after the influx of Rohingya refugees, from 2017 it has been implementing humanitarian programme including self-reliance programmes in 12 camps and adjacent host community to provide support to older people and persons with disability. HelpAge International is very familiar with the implementation process in the camp and has strong representation and acceptance to the ISCG, RRRC and different sectoral working groups. HelpAge provided support to 30,000 Older people and People with Disabilities in 10 camps and 5 host community locations within Ukhiya and Ramu Upazila. Since 2021, HelpAge has been implementing self-reliance projects with an integrated approach through funding from WFP. There are numerous successful strategies approaches already adapted in the project e.g. introduce sign language in the training curriculum, doing accessibility audit for the facilities and screening and providing necessary assistive device to the person with disabilities. Also, lessons learned have been incorporated in the design of this project. HelpAge and its partners have designed and are already implementing a highly agile and adaptable model which addresses the significant challenges of older men, women and People with Disabilities have in accessing in humanitarian response through gaining skills and input support. All successful strategies and approaches will be replicated in the proposed project.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protections concerns for older people including exclusion, access to information and services, exploitation, violence, and disaster risk. This project will be implemented in the 14 camps and 2 Unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf with an integrated approach of maintaining do no harm and social cohesion for the most vulnerable groups of people such as older persons and person with disability. HelpAge will enhance support to other humanitarian actors on accessibility as well as mainstreaming disability and older people in the humanitarian actions as a core member of Age and disability working group. It will take initiatives through this project to share information and awareness raising on humanitarian services provided to OP via AFS and CBP structure, Age Friendly kit distribution (Walking stick, prayer mat,urine pot, winter clothes), support to accessing humanitarian services without discrimination and exploitation and closely work with other protection focal organization.

3.2 - Gender

Gender equality is a programmatic priority, and gender-sensitive and appropriate interventions have been designed to ensure gender mainstreaming following HelpAge’s gender policy. 50% of beneficiaries will be women and their inter-section of persons with disability. HelpAge has designed this project to provide protection services to 7500 women and 7500 men. Gender, age and disability issues are duly considered in all steps of HelpAge Project’s design, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.HelpAge and its partners strictly follow a referral pathway set for each camp that includes creating a trusted platform where survivors feel safe to disclose the case, maintain a code of conduct at working area, and always practice the survivor-centered approach. After receiving a case, we provide urgent health services, ensuring safety and security and response to other needs in line with the wishes of survivors, ensuring their decision on accessing services and sharing information.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The core objective of HelpAge is to provide inclusive humanitarian supports to older people aged from 50 and above. However, to include person with disabilities HelpAge will consider aged from 18 and above (only for person with disability inclusion). In the Age friendly Space, it will create the safe environments for the children those who come along with the participants. HelpAge has dedicated staffs and volunteers as well as baby care corner to accompany the children in the Age friendly Space with toys and other facilities. It will also organize orientation to staffs, volunteers and service provider on safeguarding and PSEA to ensure safe environments for all. It will not tolerate staff or representatives carrying out any form of harm and abuse, including sexual harassment, abuse, and exploitation and will take appropriate action. It protects the rights of adults to live in safety, free from abuse and neglect and children from abuse and maltreatment.

3.4 - Diversity

HelpAge respects the diversity of its staff. Valuing diversity means that we recognise that we all have complex identities made up of many strands and that these can include but are not limited to gender, race, disability, age, nationality, ethnic/national origin, sexual orientation, religious beliefs, marital status, pregnancy, social status and political beliefs. The project will target 15000 participants from Rohingya and Host community where 50 percent will be older women and and their inter-section of persons with disability. According the Reach Age and disability inclusion needs assessment-2022, 51 percent persons with disabilities identified among older persons (aged 60 and above). HelpAge is committed to provide humanitarian services to the diversified population. This project will ensure the participation of both women and men among the older people and person with disabilities. HelpAge will ensure the access of person with disabilities from aged 18 and above in this project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

HelpAge is committed to ensure appropriate, need based contextualized and accountable humanitarian services for the crisis affected older people. To strengthen the accountability process HelpAge highlight the Complaints and Feedback response mechanism process as this creates the opportunity for communities (both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to provide their feedback and complaints to HelpAge, and in exchange to receive feedback or response from HelpAge, is an important part of being accountable. Participants will receive awareness session of CFRM, PSEA as well as service mapping of respective targeted camps and will engage in the project management cycle. HelpAge always ensures beneficiaries dignity and respect in its all activities and share all the project entitlements to the participants so that they can participate in the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project considering accessibility all including hearing and visual impairment persons.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, The project has activities on awareness session where all participants will receive awareness sessions on disaster risk reduction considering the camp and host context. HelpAge has life skills module and each project participants received sessions on cyclone preparedness, early warning, fire rescue & response, landslide, lightening etc. HelpAge will organize mock drill at camp and host level on cyclone, fire etc to strengthen the existing capacity of targeted participants.
