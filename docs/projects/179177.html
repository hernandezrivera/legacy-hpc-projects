Project Summary

The project will ensure enhanced, principled, and effective coordination inside Syria, North West Syria and NES.
The project will support the sector coordination functions which includes:
• Delivery of coordinated and high-quality nutrition humanitarian response in Syria.
• Real time information is made available for planning, advocacy reporting and advising decisions by the HCT.
• Technical Support and Capacity building of various nutrition actors for effective nutrition response.
• Decentralization of cluster coordination and support localization of cluster coordination in conflict affected areas through building authorities and partners capacity on coordination and program planning and management as well as risk management.
• Strengthening the cross-sectoral linkages of nutrition programming to positively impact child and mother nutrition status.

4 - Needs

SMART Surveys conducted in Syria shows that 12% of children are experiencing chronic malnutrition. This proportion could be as severe as 30% among displaced children 6-59 months in North West Syria and North East Syria. Strongly linked to chronic malnutrition are poor infant feeding practices, with a low average of exclusive breastfeeding rate of 28 per cent and a minimum acceptable diet of 26.5 %. The entire population in Syria is significantly underserved with IYCF interventions, which need to be immediately scaled up. The global acute malnutrition rate for children 6-59 months across Syria is low, at 1.7 per cent, but higher rates of acute malnutrition are spread within various sub-districts, demonstrating the need for close monitoring of the nutrition situation at sub-district level. The national prevalence of acute malnutrition in pregnant and lactating women, based on Mid Upper-Arm Circumference (MUAC), is 4.2 percent but it could go as high as 28% among IDPs in NWS. SMART surveys shows that one out of each three mothers and children are anemic at the moment With the dynamic situation in Syria there is a severe need to strengthen coordination at national , sub-national levels as well as among local actors especially in NWS and NES to ensure continuum of care.

5 - Implementation of activities

• Whole of Syria partners’ coordination structure leads development of sector strategies, protocols, and guidance for harmonization of nutrition response strategies and supports upholding quality of the response, assessments, and analysis as well as mobilization of new partners. • Undertake regular coverage – gap analysis of the response and generation of response updates and infographics on response progress. • Facilitate and supports response planning and sector fund raising to meet the humanitarian needs. • Strengthen capacity of sub-sector coordination structures.• Strengthen national, sub-national and the hubs local capacity for information management for coordinated nutrition response, cluster coordination and effective risk management. • Provide technical and strategic nutrition guidance to nutrition sector coordination platforms across North West, North East and inside Syria to deliver a principled nutrition intervention.• Joint Capacity development for nutrition and food security partners on nutrition sensitive agriculture. • Capacity Development of public health and NGOs staff on rapid nutrition assessment/ SMART surveys/IYCF-E and Nutrition in Emergencies.• Capacity Development of public health and NGOs staff on cluster coordination.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

UNICEF is the cluster lead agency for the Nutrition cluster. UNICEF must ensure that capacity is in place for the Whole of Syria Nutrition cluster coordinator, as well as for each of the hubs in Damascus, and Gaziantep. For Nutrition cluster leadership accountability, UNICEF led the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC) for the development and review of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and the Humanitarian Response Plan. This approach will ensure that assessments, gap analysis, responses and preparedness plans are provided fully online with humanitarian principles in general and in considering centrality and mainstreaming of protection.
