Project Summary

The aim of this project is to provide improved access to safe and dignified learning environment for 1500 Girls through life saving alternative basic education (Integrated Qur’anic Education and accelerated learning programmes) and inclusive Education for 3500 conflict affectcted boys, girls and children with impairment (IDPs, Returnees and Host community) in Monguno, Jere, Konduga, Gwoza of Borno State

1 - Needs

Report by UN OCHA indicates that the humanitarian crisis in the BAY states remains one of the most severe in the world today. According to the International Organization of Migration’s (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) round XXXIII (August 2020) report, there are 2,118,550 internal displaced persons (IDPs) spread across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states. This represents an increase of 30,426 persons or 2.0% compared to the population identified in Round XXXII (June 2020). Compare to round XXXII, there was no increase in coverage of locations in 2 wards of Gubio while Abadam, Marte, Guzamala, Nganzai and Kukawa LGAs remain inaccessible. Lack of infrastructure and resources, damaged buildings, overcrowding, and misuse of education facilities have negatively impacted the quality and safety of educational environments. The crisis in Northeast Nigeria exacerbate by COVID-19 has further worsen an already fragile education system , resulting in increasing the number of out of school children especially girls, A further area of concern is the lack of education opportunities and school adaptation for children with disabilities. Between 23 October and 6 November 2019, education sector partners conducted an education needs assessment across the three states. The findings indicated that poor infrastructure such as classrooms, school furniture and WASH facilities was recorded as a critical need, the report further states that School furniture was reported as a gap with just 33% of schools in Borno having adequate furniture (chairs, desks, mats), 24% in Yobe and 20% in Adamawa. Access to teaching and learning materials as more limited. In Yobe state, children in just 24% of schools had access to sufficient learning material, with even lower proportions in Borno (14%) and Adamawa (10%) which is among a major factor contributing to poor attendance and retention of learners in schools. Enrollment of girls tends to decrease in comparison to boys at the JSS level, whilst children from lower-income families are often unable to afford education-related costs (both materials and school levies) or are required to work to supplement their family income. Analysis of attendance barriers indicates a need to focus on integrating education programming with livelihoods and protection, to enable families to be able to afford to send their children to school, as well as reduce the prevalence of children working and early marriage. In Borno, Adamawa and Yobe around 40 percent of the displaced population is comprised of school aged children between three and seventeen years with Borno having high population of girls out of School, this was reported in (MICS 2016-17 Survey) as having the highest number of girls out of school in Nigeria at 71% which still remain a concern till date coupled with the COVID- 19 Pandemic compared with 27.2% of the estimated children out of school in the whole of Nigeria. The JENA Assessment 2019 further report that across all states, around a quarter of schools were unable to enrol children in the 2018-2019, typically due either to overcrowding or refusing to enrol children unable to pay for school levies or uniforms. This was particularly problematic in Yobe, where 39% of schools reported being unable to enroll some children. There are no known referral mechanisms operating to assist children who have been unable to enroll in schools. Education sector faces an upward task of infrastructural rehabilitation and expansion, education management stretching and the need to address emerging education needs such as skills acquisition and protection of education against attacks, across the three states. Children and teachers in all three affected states are in dire need of psycho-social support to cope with the negative effects of the conflict. There is an urgent need to engage the local communities in supporting education as a longer-term strategy for recovery and peace building. (JENA 2019 REPORT)

2 - Activities

Activities to be implemented to achieve the objectives and accomplish the goal of the project are as follows: i) Conduct community sensitization fora on girls’ and conflict affected children non-school attendance in communities. They will provide the opportunity for the community people to be enlightened on government education policies and improve their knowledge on plight of the girl child and conflict affected children without basic education. ii) Organise meetings with PTAs and SBMCs in communities on identifying families with conflict affected girls and Children missing out of school. The aim is to work with the community education stakeholders to identify and profile families with conflict affected children missing out of school in the communities. iii) Hold dialogue meetings with families with girls not attending school. Through this intervention, the affected children in need of support will be identified in collaboration with their respective families. iv) Organise interaction forum with school management ( PTAs and SBMCs) to prepare for enrolment and integration of girls. This will offer an opportunity to know the number of schools and vacancies available for their enrolment. v) Provision of support and counselling to girls out of school to start or continue schooling. Number of girls supported to start or go back to school will be known, thereby facilitating appraisal of the project. vi) Establishment of temporary class rooms with appropriate WASH facilities vii) Enrollment of Boys and girls learners into schools viii) Provision of classroom equipment and teaching Materials ix) Emergency provision of learning supplies, degnity and hygiene kits, x) implementation of Early Childhood Development ix) Distribution of self learning materials for Boys and girls who are unable to access school xii) Distribution of COVID-19 Prevention kits, Hand washing station, disinfectants and IEC Materials
