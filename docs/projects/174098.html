Project Summary

WASH needs, mainly access to clean safe water for drinking, hygiene and sanitation have continued to increase in scale and severity across North-East Nigeria. The complexity of needs due to the COVID 19 outbreak continues to impact on needs for hygiene promotion and awareness. Access to safe water has been constrained as a result of new and protracted displacement as well as returns (self-organised and government-led) resulting in host communities registering a reduced resilience due to high pressure on the existing and already overburdened water points and sanitation facilities. Water supply intervention will focus on 1) maintenance and operation/ upgrading of exiting wash facilities including hybrid boreholes and hand pumps in protracted congested IDP camps and rehabilitation, 2) extension or construction of new facilities in support to returned communities and vulnerable host communities.
Access to sanitation continues to be diminished due to wear and tear of emergency type construction, continuous displacements and seasonal flooding leading to high open defecation and reduced handwashing practices. Sanitation needs for persons with special needs, women and children to ensure safety and equitable access are a critical gap as the sector which requires additional resources as other issues of proximity and design of the facilities are considered.
WASH and Health continue to work together on disease early warning and cholera prevention campaigns coupled with environmental hygiene to prevent high AWD cases and mitigate cholera outbreaks. Recently, the WASH sector has further rationalized partners operations to ensure more accountability to beneficiaries and better coverage of needs with reduced funding, which has improved the quality of the response.
The high need for WASH services amongst IDPs and returnees has had an increase from last year, mainly due to continuous displacement and the high volume of expected returns.
New reported acute diarrhoea cases, with average bi-weekly data of 1,500-2,000 individuals amongst populations further increases the needs category. While there is high vulnerability across all groups, available data shows that majority of women and children (over 51%) have reported severe needs for water, hygiene and sanitation.

1 - Needs

WASH & Shelter needs vary from location, (including status – IDP, returnee or host community). Across the IDP camps, their main needs across the water, sanitation and hygiene remained closely similar to returnees and some areas with vulnerable host communities. In summary;1. Access to clean safe water: an average of 40-60% of beneficiaries confirm to having access to an improved water source. The level of service and gains need to be maintained for the operation and maintenance of the facilities. A significant (40) percentage still use insufficient water (below 15 ltrs/person/day) which results in households using water from unprotected sources (rivers, open channels etc). Frequent breakdown of facilities requires round the clock maintenance for improved functionality. The highest proportion of beneficiaries using unsafe water are mainly from the LGAs; Gwoza, Bama, Dikwa and Marte, as a result of long walking distances as well lack of adequate water sources. In Michika, Madagali and Chibok LGAs and others the water stress results in seasonal cholera outbreak.2. Access to Sanitation: Overcrowding in camps, resulting in reduced space to put up sanitation blocks and land owners not willing to grant access to their lands for the construction of new sanitation facilities has constrained access to sanitation. In LGAs, MMC, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Monguno and Maiduguri, high sanitation needs are persistent, with existing ratios of above 1 latrine for 20 persons still largey exceeded. Lack of space, adverse weather, wear and tear of emergency design of facilities and high dislodging have forced vulnerable persons to resort to open defecation, crowding and associated protection issues around facilities. New arrivals increase pressue on existing facilies while a lack of policy shift and investment to improve designs have left thousands of sanitation facilities not functional. 3. Hygiene Promotion and WASH NFIs: Knowledge of disease prevention and practices associated with improved hygiene is good, however the lack of resources hinders implementation at household level. Hygiene awareness remains a gap in locations without WASH partner coverage. Besides, the constant need for NFIs due to factors of availability and capacity of IDPs to provide for self in the settlements come with negative coping mechamisms. Women and girls face critical shortages in MHM kits (Menstrual Hygiene Management) including soap for personal hygiene. The WASH core pipeline has further supported access to lifesaving NFIs but gaps remain in areas with high needs. WASH sector continues to work with all partners to ensure that IEC materials for environmental and personal hygiene awareness are available to the population.

2 - Activities

The project will support protracted IDPs in congested camps, returnees communities and most vulnerable host communities in Borno and Adamawa with assistance in water supply, sanitation and Hygiene, Shelter and NFIsActivities will include:1) Improve access to long-term safe water facilities and services as per sector’s standards through new construction and rehabilitation of water supply systems WASH:2) Provide Assistance to water systems’ functionality by sustained operation and maintenance services (e.g. boreholes, pumps, solar panels, overhead tanks, piped networks, taps, chlorination including monitoring of free residual chlorine)3) Support IDPs in camps having access to gender-segregated sanitation facilities and services (latrines, showers, handwashing stations) as per sector’s standards4) provide to community-informed hygiene messages promoting hygienic attitudes, behaviors and practices, including, Menstrual Hygiene Management5) Provide basic hygiene items including regular top-ups to IDP and vulnerable host communities. Shelter & NFIs:1) Provide Emergency shelter Assistance to conflict affected households 2) Provide assistance in Transitional shelter to conflict affected households3) Provide settlement NFI assistance conflict affected households.
