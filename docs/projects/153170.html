Project Summary

WHO proposes to reduce the health impact of the nutrition crisis through integrated health/nutrition/ protection/WASH/education interventions and nutrition surveillance, focusing on quality live-saving services, in areas with high burden of acute malnutrition, in South Sudan. In the effort to close child survival gaps, the project directly targets 15,000 SAM/MC children (58% of the SAM/MC annual caseload), their caretakers, and at least 100 health/nutrition staff working in stabilization centres and 50 staff in nutrition surveillance sites, in areas with high burden of acute malnutrition, food insecurity and morbidity. WHO will employ a two-pronged approach:
1) SAM/MC PACKAGE:
a) Implement a capacity building program designed by WHO to improve the quality of inpatient management of SAM/MC. Trainings to be conducted at the ToT and facility levels, combined with MIYCN component.
b) Distribution of essential medical supplies (SAM kit), specifically designed by WHO to attend medical complications associated with SAM, to be used in stabilization centres.
c) WASH services, which include distribution of equipment (life-straw) to mitigate the risk of infections in stabilization centres and WASH training.
d) Conduct standardized trainings on MHPSS for health and nutrition staff working in stabilization centres and outpatient nutrition programs.
e) Distribution of Early Childhood Development kits (procured in collaboration with UNICEF Education Division and Supply Division) and train SC staff on techniques on child stimulation, play with children, supervise play sessions, and make toy , and counselling caretakers on techniques to continue care, play and stimulation ant home.
f) Regular on-site supportive supervision missions to reinforce the quality of live-saving services provided, focusing on on-the-job mentorship on clinical management based on the ITP guidelines
2) INFORMATION PACKAGE: strengthening early detection of acute malnutrition in health facilities through expanded MUAC screening and surveillance, to early detect and timely refer patients at an earlier stage of acute malnutrition. Through MUAC surveillance WHO is targeting to screen at least 50,000 children under five, and train at least 50 health staff. WHO will:
a) establish additional MUAC sentinel sites at the health facility level
b) strengthen the reporting capacity of existing sites, to rapidly detect hot spots, encourage timely case referral
c) disseminate weekly analysis to alert partners and trigger rapid response
d) The health and nutrition staff will be supported remotely and through field mentorship and regular supportive supervision missions.
WHO’s mandate focuses on improving inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition and establishing nutrition surveillance. Since the beginning of the humanitarian crisis in 2013, WHO in South Sudan has put intensified efforts and operations, catalysed technical and financial resources to support improved inpatient therapeutic programs (ITP) for SAM/MC, as well as rolling out facility-based nutrition surveillance. Through this project, WHO will ensure that quality lifesaving medical treatments are provided for SAM/MC children so to prevent unnecessary deaths and ensuring quality across the continuum of care. Capacity building of medical doctors, nurses and other health workers responsible for case management of severely malnourished children is essential to achieve reductions in mortality. Supplies will provide a stop gap measure and ensure sustainability of treatments. WASH services will be extended to stabilization centres in priority locations. Nutrition surveillance sentinel sites will be established in high food insecure areas with alarming GAM rates. The existing sentinel sites will be closely supervised and supported to improve efficiency, case referral and reporting performance. WHO plays a unique role in emergency nutrition, provides technical support, builds sustainable institutional capacity, monitors the health situation and trends. Therefore the added value of this project is critical and very distinctive within the CMAM.

Needs

High level of acute malnutrition is persistently reported across South Sudan with an overall selective feeding program admissions remaining elevated. In 2018, over 1 million children under 5 were projected to be acutely malnourished, of which 261,424 to be suffering from SAM. Diseases, skin and bacterial infections, danger signs and medical complications are intimately interlinked with SAM, affecting at least 10% (with peaks of 20%) of the total number of children with SAM. This resulted in an estimated caseload in 2018 of at least 26,100 children with SAM/MC. However, due to inaccessibility to services, late referral and underreporting the total admission for 2018 (as of Sep 2018) is less than 7,000 children (source NIS). Overall, the mortality rate was kept below the 5%, as per the NIS data. In 2019, the burden of SAM is expected to be 259,732, with an estimated SAM/MC at 10% (around 26,000) and the highest burden in Unity, Warrap, Jonglei and NB&G. About 64% of the 33 SMART surveys 2018, reported critical nutrition situation, confirmed by FSNMS 2018results, which showed critical levels of GAM in about 61% of the 46 of the counties and IPC findings, warning of pockets of populations in catastrophic humanitarian situations in 8 counties, between Sept 2018 and March 2019. These figures reflect lack of significant improvement of drivers of acute malnutrition: food insecurity, poor child care and feeding practices, morbidity, lack of safe WASH, internal displacement, conflict. Children under five years are among the most vulnerable groups due to their specific needs; this condition is exacerbated among children in distressed conditions, living in active conflict or access restricted areas. The greater Bahr el Ghazal and Greater Upper Nile regions have been showing the highest prevalence of GAM for the last four years accounting for 37% of the Jan-August 2018 cumulative SAM new admissions. SAM/MC admission rates show significant seasonal and geographic variations: highest in Central Equatoria, Warrap and Lakes (cumulative 2018), and overall highest in May (rainy season typically bringing infections) in Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal, during this time case fatality rate is observed higher in Central Equatoria and Western Bahr el Ghazal, where defaulter rate is registered at 25.8% and confirming emerging needs and vulnerability of Greater equatoria. The drivers of malnutrition (food insecurity, sub optional child care and feeding practices, rampant morbidity, lack of safe water and sanitation, internal displacement and conflict) continued to worsen in 2018. Health service coverage is sub optimal and intertwined with disease outbreak such as malaria, which exceeded the threshold in 15 counties in Sept. The MOH/WHO EWARS surveillance indicated that malaria cases accounted for 62 % of the total consultations, with an overall higher trend in 2018 compared to 2017. Prevalence of acute watery diarrhea also remains high. The risk of increased water born related morbidities in the nutrition sites remain high with only 40% of the 625 OTP/TSFP sites having safe water for appetite test and an estimated 20% of SC sites (assessed in 2018 though field visit). Malaria, acute watery diarrhoea, measles will continue to be the main drivers of acute malnutrition in South Sudan in 2019. MIYCN practices are sub-optimal: only 51% children 0-6 months exclusively breastfed (FSNMS 2017); the incidence of malnutrition among children 6-23 months is higher. The unrelenting conflict in Great Upper Nile, WBeG and parts of greater equatorial is persistently restricting access to nutrition services and disrupting the already fragile referral pathway with impact on late referrals to SC. A total of 4,525 children with SAM and MAM were lost of follow up and 6,123 could not access services in Leer, Mayendit, Wau, Akobo, Yambio, Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasir, Maban, Ulang, Negero and Kouch counties between Jan and June 2018.

Activities

• Conduct standard 6-day trainings on Inpatient Management of Severe Acute Malnutrition with Medical Complications, specifically designed for nutrition and medical staff working in stabilization centre staff, prioritizing high burden counties. The training program will also cover early child stimulation in Stabilization centres and MIYCN.• Distribute WHO SAM kits to stabilization centres where staff is trained in the management of SAM/MC, to provide the treatment for at least 15,000 children.• Provision of standardized WHO psycho-social support training and WHO Basic Detection and Referral of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Cases in Emergency setting, designed for nutrition staff, targeting SC and OTP staff.• Distribute Early Childhood Development Kits, in collaboration with partners, to SC; provide training on techniques for SC staff on emotional and physical stimulation, crucial for child’s recovery to reduce risk of developmental/emotional problems, techniques for caregivers to provide continuous stimulation including proper care of the child and stimulation using play/homemade toys.• Increase availability of methods to improve WASH, water quality for safe preparation of F75 and F100 at nutrition stabilization centers through provision of water quality control services (WASH equipment: life-straw) and training on their use• Deploy WHO/partner mentorship teams to provide supportive supervision on quality case management of SAM/MC in nutrition stabilization centres in the targeted counties; to ensure quality service delivery compliant with the QAAP approach; to provide technical support on MHPSS case detection and referral, to provide technical assistance on WASH in stabilization centres. The role of WHO as the provider of TA to partners focuses on technical assistance on-site (to medical/nutrition staff in the facility) through field missions. Supportive supervision to stabilization centers and on-the-job mentor-ship is based on the ITP guidelines; the staff's degree of adherence to the guidelines is evaluated and better oriented to meet quality standards. On-the-job training is provided on identified weaknesses, focusing on the clinical management, initial management, correct daily care, correct use of monitoring tools including growth monitoring, planning, preparing, monitoring feedings. The availability of drugs will be assessed and follow up actions taken, linked to restock and orientation on proper use of drugs. TA is provided on MIYCN, focusing on breastfeeding techniques, optimized IYCF and promoting breastfeeding in the facility and at home. A dedicated support on play therapy and psycho-social support will be provided based on the WHO Training course. This practice has been highly encouraged by WHO, through dedicated capacity building of staff and a specific Early Childhood development kit, currently in the pipeline to be distributed to stabilization centers. A review of registers is also conducted to increase reporting performance.• Provide training to the nutrition programme staff to understand how to serve best the beneficiaries using the QAAP approach• Raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the nutrition sites county (ies), through skilled stabilization center staff• Establish and equip new sentinel sites for facility-based MUAC surveillance and referral so as to scale up the number of existing sentinel sites, covering high burden spots (based on population movement and emerging hunger hotspots)• Strengthening the reporting capacity and performance of the existing the network of sentinel sites.• Coordinate data flow and collection of incoming data at central level, ensure proper database management, provide timely analysis, raising alert and triggering response, sharing outputs (weekly and monthly bulletin)• Provide on-site supportive supervision to sentinel sites via field visits, coordination and technical workshops.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

Indirect consultation with affected population has been conducted in 2018 by the project management. Feedback from beneficiaries (mothers/caretakers of children admitted in stabilization centres) has been received by 103 health and nutrition staff working in stabilization centres and reported to the project management. Feedback has been integrated in the current project design to improve the quality of services provided, and enhance accountability to beneficiaries. In the large majority of cases (virtually 100%), health and nutrition staff observed that verbal communication with mothers and caretakers should be strongly supported and preferred t written complain mechanisms. Based on that, WHO has specifically integrated this component thought the following mechanism: identify one nurse in charge of 1) informing mothers/caretakers on the treatment length, procedures and roles of caretakers, 2) receiving feedback/complains from mothers and address them through verbal explanations and/or treatment improvement and/or reporting to SC staff and management. This element has been now fully integrated in WHO trainings delivered through this project, and it is part of the capacity building, monitoring and supportive supervision activities.
