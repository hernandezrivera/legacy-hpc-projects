Project Summary

According to the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) and the Somali Water and Land Management Unit (SWALIM), drought conditions in most parts of northern and central Somalia are projected to worsen due to below normal rainfall forecasts in the coming months. The humanitarian impact of the deteriorating drought conditions is concerning. There are likely to be adverse effects on water for human consumption; livestock and irrigation (including increased costs); agriculture and livestock in terms of production and reproduction, herd size, products and market price; food security and nutrition, leading to increased numbers of people in IPC 3 and IPC 4 (by 10 percent). Migration or displacement to areas where humanitarian assistance may be available; and security / increased conflict due to scarcity of resources such as water and pasture are key concerns. In addition, according to the Protection and Return Monitoring Network (PRMN) 240,000 people have been displaced across Somalia since the start of 2019 by drought. In PL state most departures were reported from Sanaag and Bari regions and most of drought-triggered arrivals came from Bari, and Muduug regions. IDPs come mainly from marginalized groups and thus face more hurdles accessing humanitarian assistance as they lack representation of gatekeepers and land owners. In addition, admission trends so far in 2019 have steadily increased for moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) while severe acute malnutrition (SAM) admission appear lower compared to same periods of the previous year due to under reporting.
Somalia women vision will aim at addressing the short-term needs in emergencies, the medium term needs through CT programmes and the longer term strategic needs of the poor through our long-term rehabilitation interventions. Our emergency interventions will stabilize HH who do not, in the short term, have the capacity to meet their basic food needs. For the most part in our target area, this will targeted food insecure HH with conditional and unconditional CTs, distribution of seeds & tools, interventions in water and public health targeting those suffering the effects of drought, flush floods and other disasters. Through working in chronic food deficit areas and in the most disadvantaged areas Sool and Nugaal, SWV will ensure that the project target those who are most vulnerable in the most disadvantaged areas. These programmes include food security and livelihoods, Nutrition, Water and Sanitation, Child-welfare and other special programmes through conducting a community based targeting vulnerability assessment

Mitigation Measures

SWV will enable marginalized people and communities to gain more control over decisions that affect them and increasing their access to services and opportunities, can help them develop more secure and resilient livelihoods. To achieve this, SWV will generate greater awareness of the social-equality dimension of environmental problems and help resilience programmes to include marginalized groupsViolence against women and girls is a daily reality in Somalia's local communities. SWV will have well-established protection principles and processes exist to help address and prevent VAWG throughout the project especially for internally displaced populations the interventions will focus to provide a long-term sustainable response to the daily violence experienced by women and girls in local communities by mainstreaming gender-based violence awareness in project meetings and forums. • Through participatory assessments that will involve men, women, girls, boys various groups such as older persons, different ethnic groups, youths, and persons with mental and physical disabilities SWV will Involve these members of the community in analyzing root causes of the risks, and whether these come from within or outside the community. Provide support in tackling both internal and external threats through targeted training:• Work with community leaders to inform all members about their responsibilities as duty-bearers and as right holders. For example, train special focal points within the population to disseminate information on rights and obligations.• Build the capacity of the community to handle procedures for civil documentation, land titling, and compensation. To do so, develop the expertise of selected focal points that will assist their peers and make related information accessible to the public.• Encourage direct meetings among the IDP community and external stakeholders involved in mitigating protection risks of durable solutions, including local authorities, police, registrars, and host communities.• SWV will provide guidance to community members on documenting existing good practices and participatory methods and on how to collect, verify and analyze information on specific protection issues to determine a course of action• SWV will keep an open dialogue and agree with the community on the mechanisms that will be established to address identified protection concerns and ensure follow-up and monitoring.• SWV will generate debates on durable solutions envisaged by the displaced communities, ensure that all members have access to objective and accurate information on the various options available and provide regular feedback on agencies’ interventions, progress, and difficulties.

Please provide further explanation

SWV intends to address displacement in a way that goes beyond ‘care and maintenance’ of displaced communities to one that builds resilience and the ability to withstand future shocks, preserves safety and dignity in order to improve the lives and self-reliance of displaced populations while simultaneously addressing the impact on host communities through creation and restoration of water reservoirs/dams for irrigating crops and animal consumption during dry season. The project will provide an approach to implement rights and needs-based programming that addresses physical safety (safety and security), material safety (an adequate standard of living and access to livelihoods) and legal safety (access to documentation, family reunification, participation in public affairs, and access to effective remedies and justice) of communities in protracted displacement as in Sool and the communities which are hosting them. SWV through the project will advocate for a multi-sectoral response to displacement that bridges the humanitarian-development divide, involves all key line-ministries, firmly embeds the protection and assistance needs of the displaced, host communities and other drought-affected communities, paying attention to the particular needs of men, women,boys,girls, the elderly, disabled, youth and children within the region’s and national development plans, promote joint planning, responsibility and commitment amongst governmental and nongovernmental (humanitarian and development) actors towards implementation of durable solutions, strengthen the normative, legal and institutional framework for identification and provision of durable solutions for those in ongoing and protracted displacement provide a mechanism that enables key decision-makers at regional and national levels to develop a nationwide approach to finding timely and durable solutions in response to ongoing and future displacements.
