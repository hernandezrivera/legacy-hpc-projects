Project Summary

The proposed intervention will contribute to safe access & inclusive quality education for vulnerable school children in the Khan Younis
The overall objective is to contribute to the improvement of the quality of education for vulnerable primary school students in Khan Younis through a holistic, all-encompassing approach supporting their right to a safe and positive learning environment at schools.
The specific objective is to improve the academic performance, psychosocial well being and building the resilience of 400 school children.
Primarily through the provision of psychosocial support and accessible remedial classes in Arabic, Science, Mathematics, & English will be provided to 400 children of both genders (200 girls and 200 boys, including persons with disabilities). This will be through creative teaching methods and tools that are designed to respond to the needs of each child. It will be implemented at CFTA's centers & school. Also, structured psychosocial support that includes the child and his/her caretakers like parents or big brothers and sisters, school teachers, etc. This will consist of individual & group counseling, referrals, and stress release activities.
Special attention will be given to students with disabilities as well as the students who were affected by the Great March of Return (GMR) events. So far, CFTA has worked with 28 students, including 3 girls .who were injured and directly affected by the GMR, in addition to 200 students who were affected indirectly by the GMR. In this project, CFTA will continue to target this group who is considered more vulnerable. The target group of the project will be 400 students (200 girls & 200 boys, including students with disabilities). They are between the age of 8 to 15 years old. it is essential to mention that a sign interpreter is available for children with hearing impairment
Children enrolled in the project will implement child-led advocacy initiatives that will include child-led research, child-led fact sheet production, child-led photography, child-led radio online, videos, and other tools that can support children in highlighting the issue of (students at the risk/and are dropped out of schools). The project will ensure active participation of most vulnerable students (girls and students with disabilities etc.) at all levels of the activities including the leadership of the advocacy actions. Child-led advocacy will involve MoE, schools, and families. The selection criteria for students to be enrolled will guarantee that most fragile and vulnerable students are prioritized, this will include gender-balanced selection (200 girls/200 boys including students with disabilities), students from vulnerable geographical areas like Al Mawasi, eastern villages including ARAs, students affected by the GMR and students who are at the risk of dropping out/and or are dropped out of schools. An already designed screening/assignment tool will be used to identify the prioritized needs of each child and will tailor the educational, psychosocial accessibility within the project framework. A baseline for each child will be set at the start of the project. It will include the results of their last exam of 2019. Post results will be compared later for each child to examine progress. The project will give special attention to the 4th-grade students who are in the process of being in a transformation level at school. Also, UNRWA students who no more have access to remedial classes and education support because of the current crises.
The project will involve students in different activities like the peer to peer education support, the design and implantation of advocacy even at the remedial classes where children will support each other. To effectively address the poor educational achievement and the psychological well-being of the student; families and teachers will b targeted through regular meetings and involvement in the different activities.

1 - Needs

Given the deterioration of the humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip lately, Education has been undermined and directly affected. The main challenges for school children are lack of access to safe child-friendly learning environments, quality schooling, and educational materials. Schools continue to be overcrowded. According to the Ministry of Education and Higher Education for the academic year 2016–2017, there were approximately 38 students per classroom, which is considered high compared to international standards that average 25 per classroom. Added to that, overcrowding in the same classroom affects students focusing on their studies, teachers losing control of the class, and may raise the levels of violence. Also, there is limited time available to support slow learners and provide remedial Education or extracurricular activities. The situation continues to be worsened by the population growth resulting in an average of 16,394 additional students joining public schools each year. In the entire generation of Gaza, students have grown under conflict and on-going deterioration of their humanitarian situation. The electricity cuts of up to 20 hours a day have negatively impacted children's rights and ability to access Education at school and home and increased the drop-out rate according to the OCHA report (May 2018). The social and cultural context of the south is considered more conservative than other areas, which has a direct impact on girls.Also, attitude towards disability is still challenged with stigma. The project will ensure that girls' needs, especially at the age of early adolescents as well as students with disabilities, are met. Child labor has risen sharply in Gaza as a result of the deterioration of the humanitarian situation. According to the latest fact sheet issued by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) 2017 Child labor marked 3.4% of children aged 10-17 years in Palestine. It is estimated as 9,700 working children in the Gaza Strip, which in the opinion of experts consulted, is likely a conservative figure and under-reported. Indeed, child labor is very much affected by the deterioration of the humanitarian situation in Gaza. It is used as an alternative household's loss of income, poverty, or loss of primary wages.Unfortunately, children are doing types of work that are considered harsh or dangerous. According to Terre Des Hommes 2018 Child Protection Rapid Assessment Report, 81% of the children included in the assessment are involved in selling goods as street vendors, 66.5% collecting waste (66.5%,), 60.7 collecting rubble, 59.2% are doing farm work and begging is 59% which is considered as child labor. The report also stated that approximately half of all critical informants were aware of children working to transport other people and goods, cleaning cars, working in factories, digging tunnels, and engaging in construction work. Less than a quarter of all Gaza key informants said they were aware of children employed in domestic labor. The project will give priority to students at risk of dropping out. A study conducted the defense of children's rights in June 2018 revealed that 95% of the young people surveyed in Gaza had symptoms of profound psychological distress: depression, hyperactivity, aggression, sleep disorders, incontinence, a preference for being alone, sometimes going into a vegetative state. In mostly peaceful demonstrations associated with the "Great March of Return started on 30th March 2018; the State of Palestine: Humanitarian Situation Report, January – June 2018 indicated that 135 people had been killed, including 17 children. A total of 2,947 children were injured, comprising 19% of total injured, with 50.6% of injuries were to the lower limbs, 7.2% to the head and neck, and 48.9% of injuries were from live bullets. CFTA psychosocial reports over the last eight months show an increasing need for psychosocial interventions in ARA areas among children.

2 - Activities

Preparing the baseline for each student enrolled in the project by CFTA and the school. Preparing the selection criteria for the remedial classes ensuring access of students in the ARA/Al-Mawasi area, students with disabilities and children at risk of being dropped out of schools. Carry out remedial classes for 400 school children throughout the project; each of the remedial classes will last for 2 hours, including a break and refreshment. Teachers will use informal education methods to implement the class and will provide the students with all educational tools, materials, and stationary needed to conduct the class.Peer to peer learning support will be part of the class techniques, - Producing creative and interactive accessible education tools/games by CFTA’s educators, the school teachers, and leaders students. Carry out refreshing interactive and in-job learning, including the use of new creative educational tools based on the module developed and tested by CFTA. Monthly monitoring of each child in the remedial classes based on the monthly results exams and the overall pre-test exam at the start of the project. Each child will have a file that includes the progress of her/his result, projects, monthly exams, and feedback from the teachers. Implementing an individual psycho-social assessment for students to determine the intervention needed.Providing psycho-social support activities, including individual and group counseling. Provide groups psycho-social support: 4 months for each group, including 15 children in each. This will include Drama as Therapy and expressive art activities such as puppetry, drawing, and creative writing. Provide case management for those who need referrals. Integrating extracurricular recreational activities for a more enjoyable experience for students at the school. Conducting regular meetings and sessions with parents and teachers aiming at supporting the children enrolled in the project. Scanning and distribution of assistive devices and accessible means for students with disabilities. Organizing child-led advocacy with three different groups of students 45 students. The advocacy will target the MoEHE, UNRWA. Each group will include 15 students who will be working together on an education advocacy campaign. This will consist of child-led research, child-led photography and child-led fact sheet production, child-led Radio production online.

9.1 - Do No Harm

The current humanitarian crisis in Gaza is directly related to the protracted protection crisis characterized by violations of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and fundamental human rights. This intervention aims to address the urgent educational needs that are outcomes of IHL and IHR violations. The project directs to supporting the most affected and vulnerable categories of people by strengthening protection mechanisms. By consolidating a protection element, vulnerable communities and students will be better equipped to deal with IHL violations and ongoing and future crises.CFTA has the protection manual, which contains the particular guidelines to its beneficiaries. These guidelines are incorporated in the CFTAs' programs and activities and are being followed up professionally. Through this project, the proposed remedial classes, considered part of the CFTAs' regular programs, have policies in terms of technical support, supervision, incorporated rules, regulation, and manuals. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The protracted humanitarian crisis in the Gaza Strip has undermined the protection of all people, especially women and children, and exposed them to various risks that put their safety and security in danger. Coordination mechanisms through the Education Cluster will continue between CFTA and other organizations and actors working in similar fields. CFTAs’ project has clear criteria for those children who enrolled and for the staff working within the project. CFTAs’ follow the HRBA, which ensures equal inclusion for all beneficiaries, including equal access to gender and persons of disabilities. CFTA follows the inclusive approach, including the physical as well as attitude interventions. The project is a quick response as priority is given to those children who are affected by the Great March of Return, and children who are at risk of dropping out.The project will primarily focus on the provision of psychosocial support and accessible remedial classes that will target 400 children of both genders (200 girls and 200 boys, including a minimum of persons with disabilities. The project will include the developing of alternative teaching methodologies and tools, centers & school-based education support, and structured psychosocial support that consists of the child and his/her caretakers like parents or big brothers and sisters, school teachers, etc. Special attention will be given to students with disabilities as well as the students who were affected by the March of Return events. In the process of providing these activities, teachers and parents will be provided with interactive skills development support (learn while doing). Children enrolled in the project will implement child-led advocacy initiatives that will include child-led research, child-led fact sheet production, child-led photography, child-led radio online, videos and other tools that can support children in highlighting the message which will focus on education (students at the risk of dropping out of schools) as the central theme.
