Project Summary

Republic of South Sudan continued to be affected by complex emergencies resulting from prolonged conflict, broken health system, emergence of communicable diseases and climate change affecting livelihoods of the citizens. The signing of the Revitalised ARCISS on September 12 2018 is hoped to pave ways to provision of basic emergency services including healthcare services to the IDPs, host community and returnees who are in dire needs to access live saving emergency health services. This Project aim to address the major causes of morbidity and mortality through ensuring lifesaving emergency healthcare meeting 2019 HRP and health cluster objectives through mobile outreaches, RRM, and facility-based services deliveries. THESO has resolved to continue providing live saving emergency healthcare with dignity to IDPs, returnees and host community in 2019 following the humanitarian code of conducts across the country despite the documented high number of attacked on healthcare and extensive looting and damage of health infrastructure.
Assessments made by The Health Support Organisation (THESO) and corroborated by other humanitarian agency reports (UNICEF, IOM, and WHO) indicated that there is a big gap at reaching the IDPs, returnees and host communities both under government and opposition control areas in Panyikang, Mundri West, Twic East and Uror counties. Insufficient access to health, water, and sanitation services combined with gaps in livelihoods and food security brought major contributing factors towards the high prevalence rate of malnutrition in the area and malnutrition associated illness (e.g. ARI, Measles, Diarrhoea and Malaria). The weekly IDSR reports and monthly HMIS reports shows increase in prevalence of endemic and communicable disease such as malaria, measles, and Hepatitis E in various location such as Mayom, Rubkona, Yirol East, Fangak, Aweil. (WHO). There is doubt in having sustainable funding for health facilities in Twic East and Panyikang counties in 2019, and will not cover all health facilities if funded RRHP 2018). Moreover, presence of increased number of returnees and Internally Displaced Peoples (IDPs) from North Sudan, Ethiopia, and Uganda to Panyikang, Mundri West, Uror and Twic East Counties can contribute for occurrence of emerging and re-emerging diseases due to breakdown in immunisation services and weak diseases prevention and promotion services.
The effect of South Sudanese poor health situation increasingly contributed for recurrent occurrence of morbidity, mortality and sufferings among vulnerable people in under 5 children and pregnant and lactating mothers continue to be unacceptably high. A high proportion of childhood mortality is rooted in malnutrition which is the underlying cause of death for neonatal, pneumonia, malaria, diarrhoea, and measles related cases. South Sudan is acknowledged to have some of the worst health indicators in the world. The under-five mortality rate is 135 per 1,000 live births, whilst maternal mortality ratio is the highest in the world at 2,054 per 100,000 live births (SSHS 2006).
THESO in South Sudan, in collaboration with the Government structures at State and County level, are responding to these primary health care activities through delivery of static and mobile health service to identified returnees, host community and internally displaced persons. In recent years, THESO has been at the forefront of the development and implementation of live saving emergency primary health care through static, mobile outreaches and rapid response mechanism approaches in Twic East, Nyirol, Uror, Bor South, Ikotos, Magwi, Morobo, Kajo Keji, Lainya, Tonj North, Gogrial East, Abyei, Guit, Koch and Pariang Counties; these programmes strategic approaches will be used to provide live saving emergency healthcare services to the IDPs, returnees and host communities in dire needs of humanitarian assistance in Panyikang, Mundri West, Uror and Twic East Counties.

Activities

Ensuring lifesaving healthcare services: THESO will apply three strategies of static health facilities, mobile team outreaches, and rapid response mechanism in provision of live saving healthcare services meeting needs of target communities particularly those of under 5 children, host community and returnees in the five counties. By addressing these illnesses at the community level while simultaneously strengthening facility-based MCH services THESO will continue to ensure <5 children, pregnant and lactating mothers, adult host community and IDPs in Panyikang, Twic East, Uror and Mundri West Counties having increased access to life saving health services. These are the focus areas: Objective 1: Improve access and scale-up responsiveness to integrated essential healthcare needs of the vulnerable populations focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality• Maintain adequate supplies, equipment and staff at health facilities, Mobile team outreaches and RRM responses • Curative and preventive measures for adult host community, returnees and IDPs through out-patient and in-patient modality • Provision of reproductive health services to pregnant & lactating mothers- e.g. Focused ANC, clean delivery kit, PNC, FP, BEmOC and TT immunisation• Pentavalent vaccines and measles immunization for under 1-year old children.• Prevention and management of common childhood diseases including malaria, pneumonia, diarrhoea, measles and malnutrition for under 5 children• Provide daily maternal care and out-of-hour services to all pregnant women, including antenatal care, postnatal care, family planning, mosquito net distribution, and intermittent preventive treatment for malaria. • Distribute long-lasting insecticide treated mosquito nets to pregnant mothers and under-fives attending for health services.• Monthly supervision to all facilities, sites and when inaccessible, conduct Community-level identification, treatment and referral of severe cases (danger sign) during mobile outreaches and RRM • Capacity building of health service providers through initial training, on-the-job training, supportive supervision, equipment procurement and material support remote supervision through monthly meetings with the CHWs. Objective 2: Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks• Procure and preposition buffer stocks of essential drugs and supplies to ensure immediate response during emergencies. • Continue active, efficient routine monitoring of disease outbreak and IDSR weekly and DHIS monthly reporting to CHD, SMOH, WHO and RSS MoH.• Provide training on case management of outbreak and communicable diseases (measles, malaria, AWD)• Assess and respond to potential outbreaks and humanitarian emergencies within the project areas. E.g. measles vaccination campaigns, rapid MUAC assessments. • Provide health education to community members (direct beneficiaries -women and girls, men and boys) through existing community structures using ‘key messages’ approach. Target groups include clinic populations, women’s groups, school, church and youth groups. Objective 4: Improve resilience among vulnerable populations by increasing access to mental health and psychosocial support services and increase access to essential clinical health services among vulnerable populations• Ensure GBV referral pathways are in place (for women, children and men) and PEP and rape kits are available at all sites. • Designed behaviour change (DBC) – focusing on identified gaps to provide designed continues health information to host community, returnees and IDPs e.g. IYCF approach, optimal hygiene/health behaviours, appropriate home care and care seeking practices for sick children• Provide additional malnutrition preventative care from each clinic in the form of Vitamin A provision and de-worming for all under-fives as per WHO guidelines.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

THESO uses different communication and information sharing strategies such as focused group discussion and face to face, mass media, and group sessions. Uses means such as health education sessions, OPD consultations, ANC sessions, Immunisation services sessions to provide information to beneficiaries.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

THESO uses both context-specific and tailored messages to it beneficiaries and the mode and means of information sharing also varies on gender and age groups of the target individuals or groups. For example messages on important of immunisation and ANC services can be pass to adults of both sexes however messages on exclusive breastfeeding will majorly focus on pregnant and lactating mothers.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

THESO is committed to the global eradication of sexual exploitation and abuse and has signed the UN document on the Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abused (PSEA). THESO has established a clear structures and procedures to ensure adherence to the policy and has trained all it staff on PSEA. There are PSEA focal point persons at head office and field level and all line of reporting and referral are well operational. All new project staff and people associated with project will be trained on PSEA and will adhere to the regulations and provisions in PSEA guidelines as they will be monitored by their PSEA focal point persons.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Project consultations with communities have been very broad and representative, beyond the community leaders and usual constituencies as THESO staff engaged beneficiaries. Consultations where made with community leaders, pregnant and lactating women, TBAs, Men, women, boys and girls to assess their opinions on existing health problems, project design, implementation, monitoring and reporting. THESO employed focused group discussions, and face face discussions to gage views of the target groups and all their feedback were collected, collated, analysed and included reflected in the project.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

THESO approach to feedback and complaints handling is part of a broader organisational commitment to accountability, that promotes information sharing, transparency, participation and learning with project participants. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. All feedback is recorded, responses are given to community members or groups and a monthly report of the feedback received and responses given is sent to THESO head office in Juba. Many project teams include staff with specific responsibility for supporting the mechanism, such as Quality Assurance Officer, Accountability Officers or Community Animators.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Meaningful, effective community engagement is often essential for all stages of project. Despite substantial variation in the types of resources available (purpose, scope, tools, targeted audience, resources required, etc.) THESO follows holistic, systematic consideration and specific guidance on how community engagement approaches can best be incorporated into each step of the project implementation cycle. THESO always provide transparent and effective processes that enable community involvement in decision-making in all it projects cycle. Increasingly, public input and participation is expected and, in most cases, demanded at the earliest stages of a project’s design. Host communities need to understand the full implications of a project at concept stage, so that there is opportunity for concerns to be raised and addressed.Planning and design processes are more likely to be aligned with community views as these views are directly reflected in concept development, and feedback is provided on how the input influenced the decision. Such early engagement always assists THESO in establishing strong relationships that can continue throughout the implementation and operational stages of projects. Regular engagement at each stage of a project is more likely to reveal important issues and provide valuable feedback in all stages of project cycle and THESO engage community in a participatory process during project monitoring to address stressors, challenges and threats during implementations.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project hold to the universal right based approach to provide protection while providing lifesaving emergency healthcare to it beneficiaries. The following human rights article are the most relevant when THESO provides healthcare services to it beneficiaries:• article 8 - the right to respect for private and family life• article 3 - the right not to be tortured or treated in an inhuman or degrading way• article 5 - the right to liberty• article 2 - the right to life• article 14 - the right not to be discriminated against.We ensure that all our beneficiaries are protected from risks that might affect them while accessing or utilising THESO healthcare services.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project identified bureaucratic impediments by the warring parties signatories to the recently signed revitalised ARCISS agreement and poor road access as the major barriers to access to lifesaving emergency healthcare services. THESO will use static health facilities in areas with good road access and mobile team to reach populations with no access to healthcare services in deep locations with road network even though very poor. RRM team can be deployed by air flights to specific locations where the project is for a period of three (3) to six months to provide the much needed lifesaving services to the beneficiaries. All the three strategies will address barriers to access to healthcare services that will be provided by THESO.
