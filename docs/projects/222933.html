Project Summary

The project mainly focuses on the Treatment and Prevention of Malnourished Children and Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women and to improved Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), Hygiene Practice. Following the nutrition specific and sensitive approach, the project contributes in the reduction of mortality and morbidity among boys and girls under 5 years old, Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women (PBW), and other vulnerable groups among Rohingya and host community in Cox’s Bazar. Furthermore, to improve awareness in the community, the project will ensure dissemination of awareness rising messages on health, nutrition for PBW, husbands of the PBW, adolescent girls.
Activities includes:
I. Promotion of nutrition practices and awareness rising for pregnant and breastfeeding women, husbands & adolescent girls through support group approach.
II. Facilitate participatory cooking sessions to develop innovative nutritious recipes.
III. Conduct community outreach activities (screening, referral, and follow up, mobilization) through volunteers and mother-led MUAC approach
IV. Provide MAM & SAM treatment services for children and PBW at the integrated nutrition facilities.
V. Carry out comprehensive growth monitoring and promotion services to children under 5 years (0-59 month).
VI. Community Management of at-risk Mothers and Infants age <6 months (CMAMI),
VII. Prevention of Anemia among adolescent girls through micronutrient supplementation.
VIII. Blanket Supplementary feeding program (BSFP), Nutrition sensitive E voucher program (NSEP),
IX. IYCF counselling and messaging among caregiver of children 6-23 month and pregnant women
X. Ensure crosscutting issues including gender, disability, AAP etc.
XI. Capacity building of staff and volunteers
Implementation plan and modality:
> Support group activities focused on the standard guidance from sector funded by BPRM as well co-funded by Concern with UNICEF/UNHCR-WFP. Concern ensures proper communication and coordination with nutrition facilities (Concern / other partners) to implement the support group activities in the camps ensuring no overlapping and duplications of programme participants.
> All other nutrition treatment and preventive activities mentioned above will follow the nutrition sector standard implementation protocol, guidelines; co-funded by Concern with UNICEF/UNHCR-WFP by incorporating resources as staff and volunteers, capacity-building support, IEC materials/stationaries, vehicles rental costs and operation costs. As the appealing partner, UNICEF and WFP will provide necessary nutrition supplies to implement the project. Thus, the project is not overlapping, rather supporting for better cost sharing and implementation.
> Concern regularly coordinates with nutrition sector, UN agencies regarding the implementation of activities, ensuring quality standards of services. As, Concern is leading CMAM technical working group, and as a member of SAG, contributes actively to the decision-making process of sector.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the latest SENS survey (conducted in November 2023 by UNHCR and Concern Worldwide), Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) identified as 15.4% (SAM prevalence 2.1% and MAM prevalence 13.3%). The data indicated that GAM rate has been increased significantly from 2022 SENS survey (12.4%) reaching to the WHO-UNICEF emergency threshold (<15%). The GAM prevalence among girls found relatively high as 17.1% compared to the boys as 15.4 indicates more vulnerability among girls. The 2023 SENS survey also indicated that the stunting prevalence among under-five children remained persistent with the prevalence rate of 41.2%, which is very high/critical (≥ 30%). The host community also faces significant malnutrition challenges, with a GAM rate of 11.1% and stunting at 23.3%. Infant and young child feeding practices remain sub-optimal, with exclusive breastfeeding rates below WHO standards. The overall deterioration in nutrition situation is likely due to a number of factors, including the ration cut in 2023, high rates of child morbidities such as diarrhea, poor WASH facilities and recurrent natural disasters. Although anemia prevalence has reduced slightly compared to 2022; children under five (6-59 months) 38.2% and women of reproductive age 24.1%, it is still surpasses the medium level public health concern threshold. The minimum acceptable diet (MAD) has dropped to 16.5%, and minimum dietary diversity (MDD) has decreased to 26.9%, compared to 2022’s survey result, respectively. This decline highlights inadequate care practices, limited access to a variety of nutrient-rich foods, insufficient health services, poor water and sanitation conditions, and overcrowding in the Rohingya camps. These factors contribute to the high rates of both acute and chronic malnutrition.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Concern ensures accountability to program participants through a transparent Feedback Complaint and Response Mechanism (FCRM) that operates in every camp via AAP/CFM assistants. Concern utilized various channels to reach affected populations as part of its community engagement and accountability efforts. From the end of 2024, Concern will launch its digital FCRM system. The comprehensive tool is designed to capture feedback and complaints from various sources including help desk, complaint box, hotline number, email, community level feedback collection, exit interview etc. By systematically recording essential details such as the date of receipt, source of the complaint, nature of the issue, and location information, the form enables efficient tracking and analysis of complaints. Additionally, features like anonymous reporting and classification of complaint types enhance the form's versatility and effectiveness in addressing diverse issues. Overall, it's an easy-to-use solution that facilitates transparent and systematic complaint management. All data within the FCRM digital system will be encrypted to safeguard against unauthorized access and ensure confidentiality. All personnel with access to sensitive data will receive training on data protection protocols and the importance of maintaining confidentiality. Regular awareness campaigns will also be conducted to reinforce data protection practices.Following the standard mechanism, concern address the complaints / feedback and response back to the complainant informing the final decision. In case of involvement to other agencies / stakeholders, Concern refer the cases and follow up, whether the loop is closed or not.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project focuses on empowering youth and adolescent girls through nutrition-sensitive training and active project involvement on the adolescent group activities. Specific activities include providing awareness sessions on essential nutrition knowledge, dietary diversity, and menstrual hygiene to enhance their understanding of balanced diets and healthy life style. Additionally, they are encouraged to participate in outreach activities, spreading awareness on healthy eating habits, sanitation, early marriage, and personal hygiene within their communities. These activities aim to build their capacity, develop leadership skills, and create a supportive network for sustainable health improvements. The knowledge and practice level will be assessed through graduation assessment and denote them as community nutrition leaders for the sustainable behavior changes in the community.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project on empowering refugees with the skills and resources they need to support themselves and become more independent. To enhance knowledge and practice, adolescent, adult women and men are engaged in support group activities. After receiving training / sessions, men are sensitized and support to their spouses in the household chores and ensure caring of mothers and children which results improve health, nutrition and food security for the household.
