Project Summary

This intervention is designed to address different protection, child protection, and GBV issues affecting girls, boys and women and men through strengthen community based structures that enabled to identify and address different protection needs of the IDPs, returnees, most vulnerable host communities in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile.

1 - Needs

Children, women and girls, people at risk of landmines, and host communities remain exposed to protection risks inSudan. Other issues of concern are tensions caused by land occupation and conflict between farmers and herders duringthe harvest season, as outlined by the Secretary General’s United Nations – African Union Mission in Darfur (UNAMID)report of October 2018. As is the case worldwide, people living in protracted displacement remain exposed to risks ofharassment, rape, or violence in and around camps. As UNAMID reduces its presence in Darfur, protection is one of thekey areas of concern, which will require close monitoring during 2019 (HNO-2019)Women and girls are disproportionately affected by theimpact of the humanitarian situation in Sudan due to definedgender inequalities, combined with other factors such asdiscrimination and disadvantage, including age, ethnicity,marital status, etc. Existing gender inequalities are furthercompounded by the breakdown of protection mechanisms,destruction of essential services and economic structuresand the lack of recognition of their crucial contribution to thesurvival and recovery of crisis-affected communities. Theposition of women and girls is further diminished due totheir lack of access to and control of resources and viablecoping strategies needed for survival as well the protectionof their basic human rights and recourse to justice. Thedeterioration of gender-based power-relations during crises,heightens exposure to gender-based violence (GBV) in all itsforms. Furthermore, impunity of perpetrators and barriers tojustice and redress are even more difficult in situations whereprotections mechanisms are not functioning well. (HNO-2019)Gender-Based Violence (GBV) is common among IDPs and refugees in Sudan. Throughout the lengthy conflict, the rape of women and young girls, often by multiple militiamen, has been reported at staggering rates. So too, the prevalence of female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C) was 87% among women aged 15-49 and 32% among girls aged 0-14 in 2014 in the country. Almost 38% of women were first married before age 18 (12% before age 15). There are also severe shortages of specialized response services (e.g. psychosocial support) for GBV survivors, which are unavailable in over 80% of affected localities. Girls and boys (who represent 60% of displaced people in camps), face numerous child protection risks including becoming separated from their families, lack of birth registration, recruitment by armed groups, sexual and physical violence, child labor, child exploitation, and risky coping mechanisms. The report published by the Secretary General on children and armed conflict (May 2018) outlined the following grave violations in Darfur: 24 documented cases of recruitment and use of children in armed groups, 94 verified incidents of killing and maiming children (116 boys, 30 girls), and 36 verified incidents of rape and other forms of sexual violence which affected 44 girls. Community safety nets in the country have been weakened and overstretched social institutions cannot provide adequate protection services for those affected (HNO, 2018).

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is going to address the environmental issues by considering the local environmental context of the targeted area, and a continuous assessment for potential environmental impacts will be carried throughout the project life. The project is also going to do mitigation measures by compensating for negative impacts (if any), and enhance environmental benefits in the project by practicing some measurements, the followings are part of it: • Replace the use of fired bricks with Soil-Stabilized-Blocks (SSB) or Cement-Stabilized-Blocks (CSB). • Locally available material is recommended provided that it is not impacting negatively on the environment. • Include environmental awareness sessions in projects, and support solid waste management schemes • Reduce packaging material and discourage use of plastic bags to reduce the amount of waste generated. • Promote alternatives such as Fuel-Efficient Stoves to reduce use of wood, solar panels for lighting and pumps. Include fuel efficient cooking techniques (e.g. pre-soaking beans, sheltering cooking fires, etc.) in training. • Raise awareness on environmental protection and conservation • Reduce wood consumption by supplying iron pipes and using environmentally friendly material for constructions and rehabilitation; and promoting alternatives such as Fuel Efficient Stoves, and fuel efficient cooking techniques. Consider also alternative energy sources such as solar panels for lighting and pumps.• Enhance environmental benefits in the project by Tree planting, etc

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed into the project. As mainstreaming issue, the project will ensure that each activity will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults.Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staffs or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children.To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staffs will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries
