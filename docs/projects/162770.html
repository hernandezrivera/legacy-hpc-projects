Project Summary

The proposed intervention links with and contributes to 2020 HRP Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 by supporting displaced populations, returnees and host communities to meet their basic needs and/or access to essential basic services by providing emergency lifesaving assistance (O1), and focusing on vulnerable residents to improve their nutrition status and increased resilience (O2) in West Darfur, West Kordofan and South Kordofan States. This intervention will also contribute to FSL sector outputs and activities to achieve the FSL sector strategic objectives by increase equal access to quality basic food, and income option (output 1.1., 2.1., and 2.2.), and improved capacity to prevent malnutrition through community based livelihood interventions.
In the three states, the project will support vulnerable households affected by manmade and natural disasters, refugees, IDPs, returnee communities with limited access and availability of adequate, sufficient and nutritious food and small scale farmers who face production and market constraints as projected by HNO 2020 report. There will be deliberate focus on women, beneficiaries as they are marginalized and socially excluded and thus bear the greatest burden of food insecurity.
The purpose of the project is to support food insecure households to improve food production and increase access to incomes and livelihood opportunities. The intervention seeks to provide material support (inputs) and increase the knowledge and skills of the targeted households in agricultural production, income generation and livelihood opportunities in order to increase household food availability, improve food diversity and have diversified income and livelihood opportunities.
The four interrelated pillars of the proposed project:
1) increased food production through timely access to farm input (seeds, tools, irrigation inputs) and extension services, using the Farmers Field School (FFS) approach and promoting Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) practices linked to the inputs provided.
2) increased the access to essential livelihood services mostly for women, through income generating activities and safety net, including capacity building and linking them to locality/state level existing market systems. The activities will include; identification and facilitating small businesses within the urban centres linked to agricultural production, the establishment of community village saving groups/associations, community vegetable gardening, skills/vocation training targeting especially the most vulnerable to earn incomes.
3) improved nutrition and food diversification for vulnerable households, especially women through home gardening, vegetable production, food processing and related skills development. This project component will be linked with the first pillar and provide an improved diversity and quality of seeds for beneficiaries with a climate smart agricultural techniques (e.g. minimum tillage, organic composting, the use of organic pesticides). This project component also will train beneficiaries in food processing and food preservation that will contribute to the positive coping mechanisms and reduce the huger gap period, during the lean seasons in May to September each year.
4) developed skills in forest/tree planting and management to increase regeneration of trees linking to environment protection. This component will strengthen resilience against climate related shocks and stresses for the entire community through tree planning and tree management in and outside of the community. There will be focus on fruit trees as well to improve access to fruits thus increasing the diversity of food available to households.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan is a result of long standing protracted crises from repeated and ongoing displacements (due to conflicts, climate related shocks and stresses), poverty and deteriorating economic situation. According the IPC report released in July 2019, an estimated 6.3 million people (14% of the total population) was experiencing crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and were in need urgent action, the highest on record since the introduction of the IPC analysis in Sudan. In 2020, it is estimated that the number of individuals will face food crisis is projected to be 6.2 million people Sudan (OCHA appeal launched in December 2019). These individuals will need urgent and timely lifesaving food and livelihoods security support. The draft Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) report for 2020 indicates that South Kordofan, which is one of the states targeted by this project will have one of the highest prevalence of food security at 40%. West Darfur state, another targeted state is expected to have a prevalence of 21%. This project will particularly target hard to reach localities, where access challenges reduce the availability of food and livelihood opportunities, limit the households to generate food stocks especially during the lean season. The draft HNO report identifies vulnerable communities as those affected by man-made and natural disasters, refugees, IDPs, returnee communities with limited access and availability of adequate, sufficient and nutritious food and small scale farmers who face production and market constraints. In most cases, vulnerable people, particularly women and children suffer from limited dietary diversity which is the main cause of malnutrition, vitamin deficiency, and related health problems. Due to the nature of the target groups (conflict affected and poor households), the majority of the them don’t have the resources, the skills, and the knowledge to improve their coping mechanisms against climate driven shocks e.g. flooding in West Kordofan, or man-made stresses e.g. conflict over grazing/land in Jabel Moon, Sirba in West Darfur. The situation has been deteriorating with the devaluation of the currency, lack of basic food, and the cash crisis. According to WFP market monitor report released in July 2019, the national average price of sorghum (staple food) was 227 percent higher than the five-year average of the same month, underlining the dire food security situation. The areas targeted also have high levels of malnutrition and the intervention will be integrated with the other sectors for a comprehensive and maximum impact. Food insecurity has far reaching implications on the wellbeing and overall health and nutritional status of the individuals.In West Darfur, the continuing insecurity in Jabel Moon, results in forced migration that will certainly increase the number of food insecure people, particularly youth, women and children, as a result of lack of land cultivation and husbandry, income. El Geneina and Kerneik localities also have populations of people in need. In South Kordofan state, the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) report shows that over 5,000 IDPs arrived in different localities in October and November 2019 and more are expected to arrive in 2020. In addition, Abujubaiha, Alabbasiya, Kadugli and Rashsad are some of the localities with large caseloads of people in need, mainly refugees from South Sudan. Moreover, depending on how the implementation of the peace agreement goes in South Sudan, there may be a further influx of South Sudanese refugees in the South Kordofan state if the parties to the agreement fail to agree. There is also a need for an integrated emergency response in locations such as El Nehoud, El Odaya and Wad Banda in West Kordofan by providing basic agriculture inputs (seeds, and tools) and capacity building of the beneficiaries to improve and sustain their food security and livelihood status.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Activities HRP 2019 - approx. 25% beneficiary overlapping between the difference activities. The project will prioritize vulnerable females across all activities. Outcome 1: Ensure disaster (man-maid/natural) affected individuals receive timely and required assistance during and in the aftermath of a shock (conflict, floods, drought) based on assessments. Targeting 40,000 persons: Agriculture inputs and activities to support emergency responses: 1) Distribution 100 MT of short duration and drought tolerant seeds of Millet, Sorghum and Groundnut to 5000 farmers. 2) Distribution of assorted hand tools (digging and weeding hoes, sickles etc.) for cultivation to 5000 farmers. 3) Distribution of 25 MT of seeds of traditional legumes4) Unconditional cash transfer to 1,500 to People Living with Disability (PLWD), the old and terminal ill5) Vaccination of 3,500 livestock mainly small livestock, goats, sheep and chicken.6) Training and capacity building of 7,500 farmers in climate agricultural practices, including compost making, pest and disease control and soil fertility management.Outcome 2: Improve availability and accessibility to sufficient, quality and nutritious food to individuals impacted by protracted crisis who suffer from food insecurity levels for enhanced resilience. Targeting 75,000 persons.1) Local chicken restocking and training to 5,000 farmers 2) Distribution of goats through vouchers and fairs and training to 7,500 farmers3) Distribution of fishing equipment (nets and hooks), training, marketing and preservation (particularly in West Kordofan) to 1,000 people.4) Distribution 5,000 kg of assorted vegetable seeds and tools for household crop and vegetable farms 5) Support 180 farmers to start communal vegetable gardens under irrigation6) Small business management (i.e. petty trading, food processing) for income generation to 1,5007) Support and strengthen saving groups, build their capacities in saving/income management with a membership of 2,5008) Distribution of 5 seedlings each to each of the 15,000 households, including fruit tree seedlings in collaboration with Forestry National Corporation (FNC) and MoA9) Environmental awareness raising through public information and Radio Programme and Early Warning on from SMA on radio targeting approximately 100,000 people.

5 - Environment Marker

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The proposed project has been designed in a way that the activities will not have any significant impact on the environment. However, steps will be taken to ensure that environmental protection and climate change adaptation measures are taken into account through three pillars: 1) all agriculture inputs (seeds, seedlings, tools, pesticide etc.) will follow the minimum standards for environment protection. Improved seeds and seedlings will be procured through certified suppliers that are recognized by the Ministry of Agriculture in country, and the products are safe for human consumption and are not harmful for the environment. As much as possible, the project will use natural/organic methods of pest and disease control but in case of pesticides, they will be procured based on MoA standards. 2) Capacity building training, skills training, IGA training will have components to address environment management within the given context of the activities being promoted. The availability of local resources will be also highlighted and participants will be trained how to use these resources in a sustainable way (e.g. land use with minimal tillage, reducing waste around water sources, using natural repellents, tinctures and mixtures for crop management, instead of chemicals). These trainings will also focus on forest protection and the project also has one activity that addresses tree planting within/outside of the communities. 3) This intervention has a strong linkage to resilience and climate change adaptation practices through improved seeds and seedlings distribution that have already been tested and succeeded. The establishment of Farmers Field School will ensure that the improved seeds and related farming methods will be disseminated and adopted by the selected farmers. The project will also address environment protection through community campaigns, marking international days e.g. Int. Tree Days, Earth Day etc.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of accountability to affected people, Concern has put in place a robust Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM). The CRM spells out how the project beneficiaries are supposed to file their complaints, anaylysis of the complaints, reporting, feedback and actions to be taken if required. The implementation of the CRM system starts at the project design stage where the beneficiaries are informed about the platforms available to resport any cases of misconduct by project staff, including partner staff and contractors. At the implementation stage, Concern usually rolls out the CRM at the lowest level of project implementation, with the establishment CRM committees with membership from the community, including women to ensure that the system is implemented at the grassroots. The CRM system is monitored and overseen by the senior management staff of the organisation. The feedback from the CRM system are analysed, reported downwards to the beneficiaries, including the actions taken and upwards to the senior management in country and the headquarters in Dublin.
