Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates that the number of crisis-affected population in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 14.2 million (48.7% male and 51.3% female), of which 6.0 million are in acute need. This figure of people in need, includes 6.7 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated 320,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 438,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. More than 70% of the population in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria.
Overall, 12.4 million of people are estimated to be food insecure (including 1.27 million severely food insecure and 9.5 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 192,000 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 1,152,000 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve access to staple foods for vulnerable farmers through increased cereal and pulse production. The project outputs include:
Output 1: Access to agricultural inputs to facilitate production of cereals and pulses for 185,500 households increased.
The response package under this output will be in line with sector recommendations for provision of 200 kg of wheat/barley seeds (adequate to plant 1 ha), 50 kg NPK 10:10:10 (adequate for 1 ha). An additional 40 to 50 kg of various legume seeds (adequate to plant approximately 0.25 0.4 ha) or 200 kg per dunum for potatoes will be provided per household.
Output 2: Skills for cereal and pulses production of 185,500 targeted households enhanced.
This output aims at building the capacity of the 185,500 households that will benefit from cereal and pulses production inputs. Beneficiaries will receive training to support use of the inputs and the training programme will be built on the following FAO concepts: “Save & Grow”, climate-smart agriculture and conservation agriculture.
Output 3: Enhanced production and preservation of high quality wheat seed for 6,500 households.
This output aims to enhance wheat seed production and preservation capacity through training and input provision.
It is expected that the introduction of agriculture-based livelihood protection support in current protracted nearly ten years crisis will be able to increase local availability of food, self-reliance and improve food and nutrition security of the Syrian people, especially the most vulnerable ones.

4 - Needs

Cereal and pulse production is vital to ensuring food security for millions of Syrians. It increases food availability at household level, boosts and increases local supplies of staple food and puts money in the hands of farmers of different ages and genders, thereby enabling them to increase their food purchases in local markets. Although wheat production increased in 2020 at 2.8 million tons due to relatively better rainfall, the current production is still much lower than the pre-crisis average of 4.1 million tonnes. The implication of the current level of production is that there is a significant shortfall to meet national cereal requirements even after considering potential government, commercial and food assistance imports, especially since the wheat produced in 2020 did not fully reach state institutions, because the anti-state authorities prevented farmers (within its areas of control, which is the greatest source of grain in Syria) from selling their wheat grain production to state institutions.Agricultural production has been hampered by insecurity and other shocks to production such as floods, fires, frost, high temperature, and crop pests and diseases which have affected large number of farmers across the country. The major underlying constraints to agricultural production include dependency on rain-fed farming, decreasing availability of quality farming inputs coupled with increased prices, severe disruption of input and output markets, destruction of infrastructure and services (irrigation, storage, veterinary services and roads), contamination, land degradation and sanctions.Syria grain balance sheet for 2020 prepared by FAO indicates that there is huge shortage of quality seed in Syria. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries’ selection. M.M: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. M.M: Establishing local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. M.M: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do-no-harm principles. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because of the being selected for the project. Women and youth will be prioritized, inputs and trainings will take account of the different requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted so that re-settlement will be aided. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible friction between the two groups. The implementation of field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by the Protection Cluster.
