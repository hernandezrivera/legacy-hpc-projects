Project Summary

The project ‘Enhancing Resilience Against Forced Displacement: Monitoring, Documentation, Research and Advocacy on Israeli Strategies to Displace West Bank Palestinians’ addresses SO 1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation… are protected, respected, and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL. The project will produce credible documentation and analysis about Israeli policies that aim to force Palestinians off their lands. The project will address four key Israeli methods and strategies that create a coercive environment in the West Bank: 1) the Israeli planning policies that threaten to displace communities Palestinian communities from their homes and lands 2) Israeli policies that prevent Palestinians from receiving an adequate water supply from the water system, 3) the increasing inviability of Palestinian shepherding sector resulting from Israeli policies, and, 4) settler violence that aims to force Palestinians off of their lands and expedites Israeli takeover of Palestinian lands. The geographic focus of the project will be 1) the areas of Area C where Israel seeks to forcibly transfer Palestinian communities by creating coercive environment, particularly the South Hebron Hills, the Jerusalem-Jericho corridor, and the Jordan Valley; 2) Palestinian communities that lack adequate water resources, especially shepherding communities; 3) Areas where the occupation of shepherding is threatened due to Israel’s policies and 4) Areas suffering rampant settler violence, including the city of Hebron, the south Hebron Hills, the Jordan Valley, the Nablus region, and the Ramallah area.
The project will produce credible, effective, timely and usable documentation and analysis about Israel's attempts to expel Palestinians from their lands or homes. The project will produce outputs such as:
A short research report about the impact of Israeli policies on Palestinian shepherding in the West Bank; short research reports about attempts to expel Palestinians from their lands though planning policies, water deprivation, or settler violence; a comprehensive research report about the Israeli water distribution policy in one area of the West Bank; video documentation about Israeli attempts to expel Palestinians from their land; updates on Facing Expulsion blog documenting threats to Area C communities; updates on Settler Violence Backed by the State blog; social media posts sharing documentation and data with the public; press releases distributed to the media; media articles citing B’Tselem on these issues; meetings, briefings and field visits to Palestinian communities facing water deprivation, threats of displacement or settler violence.
The principles of Do No Harm are mainstreamed into B’Tselem’s working practices and procedures. B’Tselem maintains relationships with the Palestinian communities we work in through our field researchers, headed by our field research director, who are residents of the region, and through our video camera project. Our field researchers and video staff represent B’Tselem towards the Palestinian public, and these relationships serve as open lines of communication between B’Tselem and our beneficiary communities. Any information, feedback, criticism and complaints can be communicated to our field staff, and the field research director will relay them to the rest of the staff. The project will also implement development of B’Tselem’s existing procedures and policies for safeguarding and protecting the communities that we work in against sexual exploitation and abuse. B’Tselem will conduct staff training on PSEA and safeguarding, and will further develop and implement a complaint mechanism for Palestinian volunteers, witnesses who give testimony, or other Palestinians who come into contact with B’Tselem.
B’Tselem, and this project, prioritizes the safety and dignity of its volunteers in the video camera project and the communities in which we work.

1 - Needs

The project will address elements of the MSNA by analyzing the strategies which Israel employs to displace Palestinians from their homes and lands: Israeli planning policies: Dozens of farming-shepherding communities, home to thousands of Palestinians, dot the landscape of Area C, which comprises 60% of the West Bank. For decades, Israeli authorities sought to drive out some of these communities by making living conditions intolerable to compel residents to leave, ostensibly of their own volition. Israeli authorities prohibit construction, refuse to hook them up to water and power grids, and won’t pave roads. When residents build without permits, the CA issues demolition orders. While demolition orders are not always carried out, the threat of demolition looms constantly. In some communities, families have had their homes demolished several times. The CA also destroys rainwater cisterns, roads, and solar panels, confiscates water tanks or cuts water pipes.The authorities’ efforts center on three areas: The South Hebron Hills: About a thousand Palestinians, half of whom are minors, face the threat of expulsion from their homes and the destruction of their villages. The military expelled residents from this area in 1999, citing declaration of the area as a “firing zone” in the 1980s. The villagers were allowed to return to their homes while petitions were before the HCJ. New petitions challenging the expulsion are still before the HCJ, and the threat of expulsion remains.Ma’ale Adumim Area: In the 1980-90s, the CA expelled hundreds of Jahalin Bedouins from the areas they lived in to establish and expand the settlement of Ma’ale Adumim, and relocated them to a permanent site near the Abu Dis landfill. `3 K people in the area still face the threat of expulsion. The Jordan Valley: `2,700 Palestinians live in `twenty shepherding communities in or near areas that the military declared firing zones. The CA employs measures to compel them to leave, including repeated home demolitions, relocations for military training, and confiscation of water pipes, tanks and solar panels. Water: In 1967, Israel seized control of all water resources in the territories it occupied. and retains exclusive control over the water resources between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea, except for a section of the coastal aquifer under Gaza. Israel uses the water to serve Israeli interests, ignoring Palestinians needs, and Palestinians all over the oPt suffer from severe and pervasive water shortages that are primarily man-made. The 9.21 UN OHCHR A/HRC/48/43 report notes that water in the West Bank is unavailable in a sufficient and continuous manner. It is estimated that nearly 660 K Palestinians have limited access to water, with 420 K consuming less than 50 litres on average daily per capita, half of the 100 litres recommended by WHO. 14 K Palestinians in approximately 180 communities in Area C have no connection to a water network, lack water infrastructure, and are considered at high risk for water scarcity. The communities that Israel prevents from hooking up to a water grid must buy water from tankers at considerable cost, which rises when the water must be transported across rough terrain because Israel does not allow paved roads. Residents buy water at several times the price of the public service, yet it is often not safe to drink due to poor sanitary conditions in the tankers that transport it. Israel’s restrictions on developing water infrastructure were exacerbated by settlers and Israeli authorities taking over natural water sources the residents relied on for years, destroying or blocking cisterns and spring-fed pools. All these actions are part of an ongoing effort by Israeli authorities to drive Palestinians from their homes. This reality demonstrates how Israel sees water as its exclusive property, to be doubly exploited: both for Israeli needs – especially those of settlements – and to dispossess Palestinians. Settler Violence: Violence by settlers toward Palestinians has long since become part of life under occupation in the West Bank. These actions range from blocking roads, throwing stones at cars and houses, raiding villages and farmland, torching fields and olive groves, damaging crops and property to physical assault, sometimes hurling Molotov cocktails or using live fire. Widespread violence toward Palestinians has resulted in injuries to life and limb, and damage to property and land. B’Tselem views settler violence not as sporadic acts of violence by ideologically motivated extremists, nor simply a failure of law enforcement, but a deliberate strategy to force Palestinians off of their land to clear the way for Israel to take control of it.Under international law, Israel has a duty to protect Palestinians in the West Bank from settler violence. However, Israeli authorities routinely shirk this responsibility. Thousands of testimonies, videos and reports, and many years of monitoring by B’Tselem reveal that Israeli security forces not only allow settlers to harm Palestinians and their property – they often provide the perpetrators escort and back-up. In some cases, they even join in on the attack. In other instances, security forces prevented anticipated harm by removing targeted Palestinians, rather than the Israeli assailants. Countless attacks have left their traumatic mark on Palestinian collective memory. Many Palestinians avoid “danger zones” near settlements. As a result, in some plots, the yield has become so poor that the owners have given up farming the land. This dynamic created invisible walls, beyond which Palestinians know they face violence to the point of risking their lives. The result of settler violence is the dispossession of Palestinians from more and more areas in the WB, easing Israel's seizure of land and resources. All these factors above contribute to significant decline in the viability of Palestinian shepherding in the WB.

2 - Activities

Short research report on Palestinian shepherding sector: B’Tselem will research harm caused by Israeli policies to Palestinian shepherding in the WB. In B'Tselem's upcoming report on settler violence, and its recent settlement policy report, B’Tselem presents anecdotal evidence that raises concerns that Palestinian shepherding sector is close to collapse in three areas that are intensively targeted by shepherds' outposts–the South Hebron Hills, the Jordan Valley, and northeast of Ramallah, east of the “Alon” highway. Huge allocations of grazing land for agricultural settlement outposts blocked shepherding lands for many communities, and Palestinians reported that they had to reduce their herds considerably. B’Tselem may focus on one area or seek more general data to shed light on the state of the herding sector. Water Research Report: B’Tselem will research the Israeli water distribution policy and the disparities in water allocations for Palestinians and Israelis in the WB. Israel today is a water powerhouse, following the desalination revolution, and runs an advanced, efficient water economy, while perpetuating underdevelopment of the Palestinian population in the WB through water deprivation. Israeli citizens enjoy almost unlimited water, while Palestinians are bound to the water allocations from Oslo II in 1995, which has not reflected the needs of the Palestinian population for years, if ever. In order to illustrate how the Israeli apartheid regime exploits the water resources, we will research: 1) A peripheral area of the West Bank: the SHH or the JV, illustrating how Israel uses water resources to expel communities from their homes and take over their land. Israel prevents the communities from connecting to water networks, destroys water and sanitation facilities, and allows settlers to take over water sources used by residents; 2) An Area A city, Ramallah or Nablus, which was supposedly transferred to PA control, yet its inhabitants suffer from a constant shortage of water. Monitoring and documentation of Israeli Strategies to Displace Palestinians: B'Tselem will employ 8 Palestinian field researchers around the WB to monitor and document the human rights impact of the Israeli strategies described above in their region. One of B’Tselem’s primary sources of information are testimonies from victims and eyewitnesses to violations. B’Tselem’s field researchers gather photo and video documentation, medical records, data from Palestinian DCO offices, authorities, the WHO, and UN agencies. The field researchers will work with volunteer Palestinian videographers in the video camera project, which trains and equips Palestinian volunteers to document violations that they experience or witness, to obtain video documentation that illustrates these Israeli polices, that will be distributed to the public. The data coordination department will verify and substantiate the information and documentation, then decide which violations will be published and which archived. The data coordinators will cross reference and supplement the information with press reports, information published by Israeli authorities, and other organizations. When there is sufficient evidence of a violation about the issues identified for this project, it will be written into short research reports which will be widely distributed on B’Tselem’s website, on social media, or press releases. B’Tselem will document two types of violations in blogs. B’Tselem will report threats to Palestinian communities at risk of expulsion, including demolitions, confiscations, and military training evacuations on the Facing Expulsion Blog. Incidents of state-backed settler-violence will be published in the Settler Violence Blog. Video Camera Project: The volunteers are human rights defenders and constitute a protective presence in their communities. The volunteers will document attempts by Israeli authorities to displace Palestinian communities through settler violence or planning policies. The documentation will be researched, catalogued, contextualized, and distributed to Israeli and international audiences to illustrate the impact of coercive Israeli policies on Palestinians. Public Outreach: The project will enhance the communities resilience by drawing attention to Israel’s policies using B’Tselem's communication and distribution capabilities. The documentation and analysis produced will be distributed to wide audiences in short research reports and its video page on its website, which was viewed 1.17 million times by 520 K users in 2020, and You Tube channel, and on its social media platforms with 355 K followers and digital newsletters sent to 35 K. International Advocacy: B’Tselem will provide diplomats, decision-makers and policymakers, international orgs, and faith groups with information and analysis about the practices and strategies that Israel employs in attempts to displace Palestinians from their lands and homes. B’Tselem will inform international audiences about the displacement strategies identified in this project through briefings to international audiences, field visits to communities threatened with displacement, e-mail updates to targeted recipients, and advocacy missions. Coordination with OHCHR Protection Cluster: B’Tselem will continue to engage with the PC, provide, and seek information from UN bodies on the field research, data coordination, research, and advocacy levels. B’Tselem will also continue to engage with the Palestinian and Israeli human rights community and conduct joint research and advocacy. PSEA and Safeguarding: The project will include further development of procedures and policies for safeguarding that protect our volunteers and the communities that we work in. B’Tselem will conduct staff training on PSEA and safeguarding and will further develop and implement a complaint mechanism for Palestinian volunteers, witnesses who give testimony, or other Palestinians beneficiaries.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

B'Tselem works according to a human rights approach in which the Palestinian population participates in monitoring and documenting violations of their own human rights through collection of testimonies from people who witness or experience violations, which are used to understand and analyze Israeli policies in the oPt, and through our video camera project, which trains and equips Palestinians to document the reality of occupation they experience. We make every effort to be accountable to the Palestinian population. B’Tselem maintains relationships with the Palestinian communities we work in through our field researchers, who are residents of the communities in which they work, and through our video camera project. Our field researchers and video staff represent B’Tselem towards the Palestinian public, and these relationships serve as open lines of communication between B’Tselem and its beneficiary communities. B’Tselem listens and is informed by information and feedback from Palestinian communities sent in from field researchers and video department staff. B’Tselem shares information with the communities in which we work about our professional standards of conduct when the field researchers collect testimonies from witnesses or victims of violations, and in our video training workshops.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Cases of internal complaints, by either employees or service providers, are handled by a person appointed to deal with such cases. Complaints by service providers are handled by the financial manager who also serves as Deputy Director. She reviews all correspondence and inquiries and then reviews the complaint. Complaints by workers are initially handled by the financial manager, and then referred to the organization's Executive Director or B'Tselem's audit committee. If concerns are raised by field researchers, these concerns are directly addressed by the relevant B'Tselem staff. The board of directors are ultimately responsible for the organization's actions and conduct, and could become involved in the handling of complaints and grievances if necessary. Each complaint is handled carefully and thoroughly, and is answered by a solution or response to the complainant. In case of sexual harassment complaints, B'Tselem appointed an attorney from within the organization to handle sexual harassment claims. B’Tselem workers are unionized, and complaints concerning compensation or rights can be addressed to the employees committee or to the management. Palestinian volunteers in the video camera project can address complaints and feedback to the camera project manager, local field researcher, the field research director or to the organization's executive director. In the case of external complaints, such as cases of publishing alleged libel, related to the names of people allegedly involved in human rights abuses, B’Tselem retains the legal advice of esteemed human rights lawyers and/or other experts to professionally accompany the organization –when and if necessary.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

B'Tselem identifies, considers and mitigates risks to stakeholders’ safety and wellbeing. B'Tselem always prioritizes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries, and considers the principles of Do No Harm within its analysis of the protection context. B’Tselem has policies to ensure that our work does no harm to minors, women, LGBTQ+ or other groups. B’Tselem ensures all materials collected are secure before publication, and ensures that materials that we publish cannot potentially endanger beneficiaries from the Israeli authorities. B'Tselem works according to principles of informed consent. All witnesses and volunteers give informed and documented consent for their testimonies and/or pictures to be collected and/or published, and all materials meet consent legal requirements from the beneficiaries depicted or their legal guardian. Child Protection Measures: B'Tselem not only invests efforts to document and publisize the violations of the rights of minors by Israeli authorities but implements best practices to guarantee that minors’ rights are protected where B’Tselem’s activities involve minors. B’Tselem’s work is guided by the principle of parental consent, and follows these guidelines: a. The organization does not use pictures or footage of minors without their legal guardian’s consent. Consent is obtained only after the legal guardian received an explanation in his or her native language, about the planned and possible use of images. b. B’Tselem does not collect testimonies – verbal, filmed or written – from minors, without obtaining parental consent. c. B’Tselem strives to minimize the use of testimonies obtained from minors, and collects testimonies primarily, and almost exclusively, to document and report human rights violations directed against minors (such as prevention of access to education, or in cases of the arrest or interrogation of minors, or in cases of bodily injury or death). When testimonies from minors are used, use is subject to written consent by the minor’s legal guardian. d. B’Tselem’s camera project does not provide video cameras or training to minors. B’Tselem further commits to: • Prohibition of staff volunteers or affiliate from causing any physical or emotional harm to children. • Prohibition of staff volunteers or affiliate from the exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors. • Prohibition of staff volunteers or affiliate from any form of humiliating, degrading, or aggressive behavior toward children. • Prohibition of staff volunteers or affiliates from having sexual relations with minors. • Prohibition of child labor, which is a form of exploitation and abuse. • Prohibition of staff volunteers or affiliate from using their power or position to withhold assistance or services, or to give preferential treatment. • Prohibition of staff volunteers or affiliate from using their power or position to request payment, privilege, or any other benefit. • Prohibition of aiding another person to exploit or abuse. • Responsibility to report, and follow up, any concern or suspicion of exploitation or abuse of a child. B’Tselem further implements safeguarding and protection measures in its Staff Code of Conduct: •Safety rules should be adhered to at all times. •Threatening, aggressive or violent behavior or language - during working hours and outside of work - is forbidden and may lead to dismissal. •Discriminatory behavior, language, or non-verbal language is forbidden. • Harassment, sexual or otherwise, is forbidden. • Bullying in any form is forbidden. •Behavior that jeopardize the safety or well-being of other employees is forbidden. •Unnecessary disclosure of confidential information concerning B’Tselem or its employees is forbidden •Employees will not bring sexually explicit material into the workplace or engage in inappropriate sexually explicit conversation.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

B’Tselem strives to amplify voices of women and girls, and to bring to light the unique ways in which they are impacted by the occupation. B’Tselem’s data collection and research employs gender sensitive practices and, when possible, collects and presents gender disaggregated data. B’Tselem collects gender disaggregated data about numbers of testimonies collected from Palestinian women and men, minor girls and boys, and has collected this data since its establishment in 1989. B'Tselem ensures that testimonies about incidents we document and issues we address reflect a gender perspective. B’Tselem’s video camera project builds the capacity of women to monitor and document human rights violations they witness and to assume prominent roles within their communities in advocating for human rights through video documentation of the reality around them. B'Tselem actively recruits women participants, and provides women-only training to address their unique concerns. B’Tselem is an equal opportunity employer. Job recruitment ads are gender neutral, and no gender is preferred for any role. Men and women are recruited in equal numbers, and our staff is nearly gender balanced. The staff salary scale is based on experience, qualifications and seniority, and men and women are paid the same salaries for the same roles. B’Tselem currently has a staff of 37, 20 men and 17 women. The deputy director, 3/6 department directors and the spokesperson are women. Resources, training, and capacity development are equally available to men and women.

Risk analysis: Staff security: B’Tselem’s staff are subject to various degrees of threats to personal security, depending, primarily, on their nationality and location. There is an ongoing risk that B’Tselem’s WB field researchers will be arrested, or their camera or video memory cards confiscated, because of their work with B’Tselem. The field researchers are also subject to ongoing violence as Palestinian residents of the WB. In the past, B’Tselem staff have been detained, arrested, shot at and wounded, and threatened in various ways. Mitigation: B'Tselem never exposes our staff to excessive risk and will always prioritize their safety over any other consideration. B’Tselem’s field researchers have been trained on how to deal with Israeli security forces and are experienced in navigating the complex challenges they face.B’Tselem provides its field researchers with legal consultation or representation, if necessary, as well as insurance and support throughout any obstacles they may face because of their work with B'Tselem. Risk: Volunteer security: There is also an ongoing risk that volunteers in our video camera project can be arrested, their camera or video memory cards confiscated, or their home raided by Israeli security forces because of their work with B’Tselem. Mitigation: B'Tselem never exposes volunteers to excessive physical risk and always prioritizes their safety over any other consideration. Volunteers receive training on how to exercise their legal rights to film in complex circumstances and are given a letter to present to security forces explaining the legality of filming in the WB, and that they cannot be legally detained for filming events in the WB, and their cameras or memory cards can’t be confiscated. Volunteers are instructed not to confront Israeli security forces or settlers or get involved in events that they are documenting, and to leave dangerous situations that threaten their safety. B’Tselem provides its volunteers with insurance, legal consultation, and representation when necessary, and support throughout any obstacles they face because of their volunteering. B’Tselem is considering additional technologies to enhance the safety and security of the volunteers and the documentation they capture, such as uploading documentation to a cloud.
