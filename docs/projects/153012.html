Project Summary

Maiwut county (Upper Nile State) & Gogrial West (Warrap State) are both categorised under emergency and crisis phases of food insecurity by the latest IPC reports (IPC Jan-Mar’19 projections). With a total combined population of 403,911 both counties are threatened with shortage of basic facilities that include education. Maiwut has been severely affected by conflicts with the most recent clashes leaving behind a high number of people estimated at 22,000 displaced in safer locations near Longechuk and in Jekow areas within Maiwut. In Gogrial, inter-ethnic clashes between the Agwok and Apuk communities have continued to frustrate efforts towards education in the county. In 2017, an armed conflict between the two rival communities left 100 people dead and over 14,000 displaced persons fleeing safety. The tense relations have made it difficult for children to access the limited education facilities as they are confined in specific locations – most of which are not serviced with education facilities. In these conflict affected communities, children continue to be the most affected as schools are destroyed and as the population relocates to relatively secure areas. This challenge has been compounded by Murle attackers who continue to pursue child abductions. The most recent case in Maiwut reported was in April when about 15 children were abducted after the population ran away from Fagak (where political clashes had earlier on displaced the population).
Throughout 2018, only one agency, Nile Hope has been providing education services in 5 schools in Maiwut County. The second agency that had been complementing Nile Hope pulled out leaving a vacuum that has not been filled todate. A total of 16 schools remain non-operational in Maiwut (Joint Inter-agency assessment 14-18 May 2018). This is besides IDP concentrations that in addition are in dire need of temporary learning shelters. In Gogrial over 17 schools remain non-operational due to lack of external support. Government direct support has proved inadequate to resuscitate the non-functioning schools. UNKEA considers that the gap in education faced in the two counties require imminent attention – which this proposal intends to address.
There is a notable absence of school facilities that include learning and instructional materials, shelter for children, and limited support to school management and volunteer teachers. Out of the 42 schools that were functioning before the political crisis in South Sudan, only a handful of 9 schools are functioning in both counties with very irregular support to sustain normal operations. UNKEA plans to provide learning & instructional materials to 10 schools, provide temporary learning shelter in 10 locations in the period of January 2019 to December 2019. From UNKEA assessment – confirmed by other independent assessments, girls bear the brunt of absence of functioning schools. This exposes them to abuse, early marriage, and child labor. The proposed intervention will ensure 4,400 girls are in schools while a total of 3,600 boys will be kept in schools - and have a chance to escape conscription in to war outfits as child soldiers.

Needs

Based on the inter-agency assessment and UNKEA's internal reports through its presence in the two counties, these are the notable needs that have been identified in the two counties of Maiwut and Gogrial West;- Abscense of learning shelters. In almost all areas where child learning is taking place, children are learning under trees which becomes complicated during rainy season- Lack of learning and instruction materials. Children and teachers have no access to books, pens, boards, text books, and reference materials to aid learning. This is true in 33 non functioning schools. However, UNKEA plans to roll out its suppport to 20 out of the 33 non-functional schools.- Teachers (voluntary) have not attended the requisite pedagogy trainings to deliver learning.- There is notable absence of oversight or effective management of learning. School management committees are either non existent or don't have sufficient knowledge to provide oversigt in the management of schools.- Recreational facilities that critical to aiding learning amongst children is unavailable. This suffocates the creative thinking process that nurtures learning and skills in children- As a result of food insecurity in both locations classified as emmergency and crisis for Maiwut and Gogrial West respectively, there is shortage of meals in schools. This has continued to affect learning and pupil attendance in schools. - There is poor protection measures for children. No campaigns that champion child rights in the schools. Girls need empowerment in order to counter peculiarities that affect the girl child- In almost all the schools, UNKEA and other agencies noted absence of sanitation and hygiene facilities. No washing facilities were observed while those that had latrines/toilets did not have provisions that allocate sides for girls and that of boys. Such critical and vital information that reduces incidences of GBV are required and the assessment noted that these were lacking in the visited functional learning sites.

Activities

UNKEA proposes to address the above challences through provision of the following emergency support services;1) Construct temporary learning shelters in locations with population concentrations. A total of 20 temporary learning shelters shall be erected using majorly locally available materials with additional project support. Ten will be established in each county.2) The project will also provide training to teachers on basic skill packages. UNKEA has a professional teacher whose experience will be utilised to deliver training to all site volunteer teachers in both counties3) As a basic component for learning, the project will utilise existing education pipeline support to supply learning and instruction materials to 20 schools in the two earmarked counties. Teachers will be guided on how to use and apply instructional materials to pupils.4) Specific trainings will be delievered to both the primary teachers association and school management committees. UNKEA has training materials that have been tested and used in previous education projects. These shall be reviewed utilised for training of the PTAs and SMCs.5) The project will also provide training on protection through child clubs and through teachers who will facilitate continous support to pupils in ensuring there is follow up within each school.6) UNKEA shall also provide school meals to functional schools in order to sustain learning and maintain pupil attendance in the schools. Studies have proved that presence of food or meals in schools maintains pupil attenance and improves concentration of learners.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project shall be guided by UNKEA's internal policies in regared to sexual exploitation and abuse. UNKEA has adopted the Core Humanitarian Standard and incorporated provisions that protect staff, beneficiaries, members from the benefiary community, and humanitarian workers from abuse at all times. All project staff, contractors, and persons engaged directly in the project implementation shall sign up to the code of conduct that restrict behaviors to avoid harm. At field level, project officer is a recipient for cases where instances do not directly target staff but entities the project engages to contribute to project objectives. Cases involving have provisions where Payam oversight committees communicate directly with national office through senior management staff that travel to field on quarterly basis to interact with the committees.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The project has two layers of complain mechanisms:First layer is the project steering committee that directly handles the complaints desk in the regular undertaking of project activities. They are comprised of beneficiary representatives constitution of reprentatives of IDPs, Returnees, host communities, women leaders, religious leaders, and local authority. The second tier is the Payam level oversight committee that does not handle any activities but observe and recieve complains on all actors including on project staff, steering committee members, and all actors in project execution. The oversight committee reports directly to Juba based senior manager who enagages with the field on quarterly basis.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through the project design, communities are engaged both at Project Steering Committee levels and at Payam oversight committee. In addition, beneficiary selection, local procurement, local staff recruitment are done with direct input and supervision of local authorities. Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project has acknowledge risks that exists and part of the risk challenges is to be solved by this proposal. In Gogrial, rival communities have constrained free movement of people including learnings from crossing conflict bordes to access education facilities in other communities. This project considers that provision of temporary learning facilities with requisite facilities to aid learning will reduce on the risk exposure for children moving longer distances to access education from hostile rival communities. In Maiwut, possible tensions of access to limited resources between IDPs and host communities have been factored in the project engagements with key stakeholders whom UNKEA is already currently engaging through other projects in the county. Past experience shows that when prior engaged, local authorities are willing to protect IDP populations to access resources. The evidence of similarities in ethnic backgrounds between IDPs and host communities have also played a vital factor in easing tensions that could otherwise emerge from competition for limited resources.
