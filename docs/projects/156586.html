Project Summary

Building on the expertise and operational capacity of ACTED staff and its partner Ihsan Relief and Development (Ihsan) in Syria, the intervention will provide targeted and effective WASH support to those most vulnerable across the country. To ensure that needs are met comprehensively, ACTED and its partners will provide WASH assistance both in and out-of-camp in Northwest Syria. The action will focus on restoring cost-efficient, sustained and safe access to quality water to populations. Temporary sources of safe water will be installed where no alternatives to water trucking exist and under performing water and sanitation infrastructure will be rehabilitated. Rehabilitated assets will be handed over to local actors to increase community resilience. Community sensitization on hygiene practices and water usage will be mainstreamed to heighten the impact of the intervention.
Under the WASH sector, ACTED will build on its expertise conducting multi-faceted WASH programming and will combine elements of emergency response while establishing durable solutions. In Syria, ACTED's WASH approach is based on three layers of intervention. First, immediate interventions aim to meet the emergency needs of those most vulnerable through the distribution of WASH NFIs and the establishment of emergency water treatment units and distribution points. Key WASH-related awareness raising is mainstreamed throughout the approach. A second layer of interventions focuses on improving access to sustainable safe water sources through existing infrastructure. The initial emergency response is thus paired with the rehabilitation of WASH assets such as canals, water stations, and boreholes. Additionally, ACTED provides technical and operational support to the authorities managing the rehabilitated sites. A third layer focuses on ensuring the handover of rehabilitated assets to, and establishment of cost-recovery and water safety plans with, local stakeholders to ensure the sustainability of the self-managed infrastructure.
This intervention will focus on emergency response and sustainable resilience, so as to provide the most adapted and cost-efficient assistance to those most in need. Synergies will be drawn between activities and with other ongoing ACTED interventions.
ACTED and Ihsan will deliver an integrated package of WASH assistance in Northwest Syria. Water quantity and quality will be improved to SPHERE standards, with a focus on ensuring durable and cost-efficient access to safe water. A particular effort will be put on offering sustainable alternatives to water trucking where no other sources of safe water are available. In turn, it will allow actors currently conducting water trucking to redirect emergency assistance to other areas. Mainstreamed community sensitization on water conservation, usage, and hygiene will contribute to decreasing Water Born Diseases (WBD) concerns. An effective exit strategy will be ensured by working closely with local stakeholders, building their capacity to operate and maintain rehabilitated infrastructure.

Needs

To identify the pressing needs and current gaps in WASH response in Aleppo and Idleb governorates, ACTED conducted an assessment in December 2018 to complement assessment in camp and Southern Aleppo conducted as part of ongoing projects. The prolonged conflict in Idleb governorate, and the collateral damage resulting from military operations, has deeply impacted the WASH infrastructures, making them inoperable and in need of repair. As a result, access to water has been hampered for most of the people who live in the governorate, and who have been experiencing water shortages for long time. IDPs are among the most affected groups; lack of potable drinking water affects in particular IDPs in newly established site, which often lack WASH services, including adequate latrines. WASH needs remain present in existing camps as well, particularly in light of the new arrivals following displacement, which require a continuous provision and scaling up of WASH assistance. A recent and comprehensive assessment conducted by REACH Initiative provides information on the WASH situation in the north and the west parts of Idleb governorate and confirms that the WASH situation in the governorate remains critical (REACH, HSOS, November 2018). Similarly, sewage systems including treatment plants used to serve only major cities, while other parts of the country relied on simpler technologies. The situation with regard to water supply and sanitation infrastructure is considerable worse in camps, due to overcrowded facilities, common protection issues, and increased risks. In these camps, IDPs rely entirely on the provision of daily WASH services to meet their basic needs. ACTED has been implementing a WASH response since 2013, which now encompasses 161 camps across eight camp clusters in Idleb. As of October 2018, 133,824 individuals are living across the eight camp clusters as follows: 101,161 in the 144 camps across the four camp clusters of Dana sub-district, Harim district; 28,595 in 17 camps across the four camp clusters of Khurbt Al Joz, Az-Zawf, Obein, and Darkoush located in Ash Shugur district (ACTED Project Data - Grant 720FDA18CA00041). All these individuals rely on water trucking and water distribution points to meet their daily needs of water for drinking, washing and cooking; and on sanitation and waste management services to ensure proper sanitation. For this reason, a gap in daily WASH assistance would have disastrous consequences on populations who rely entirely on humanitarian assistance. Constant inputs and maintenance are required to keep the water supply systems well-maintained and operational, while fuel cost spikes make water trucking an ever increasingly expensive activity. In addition, the latrines and the sewage system require constant cleaning and maintenance to keep functioning. Similarly, environmental health in camps is particularly critical, as the lack of income of the camp residents prevents any local authority or camp management entity to set-up a self-sufficient waste collection system based on fee recovery. In Aleppo governorate (more precisely Aghtrin, Azaz, Mare, and Suran sub-districts of Azaz) the majority of respondents reported accessing water through: (1) water network (69%); (2) water trucking (28%). 82% reported a lack of secondary water source. Key WASH priority needs were to increase the amount of water supplied, enhancing the quality of water at household level, and provide hygiene kits and chlorination tablets. The water quality was demonstrably poor, with both color, odor and suspended particles issues highlighted by assessed communities. The existing assessed water stations were found to be only semi-functional, forcing people into purchasing water from private water sources at high prices. Additionally, the damaged waste water networks were reported to negatively affect the quality of the water supplied due to leakages.

Implementation of activities

ACTED and Ihsan will focus on improving access to clean and safe water for conflict affected population. In camps• Day to day management of water supply, water quality monitoring, excreta disposal, maintenance of sewage systems and sanitation facilities, and support to daily waste collection in Leeajlekom, Qah, Salam, Karama, Obein, Az-Zawf, Khurbt Al Johz, and Darkoush camp clusters• Construction of a water supply network in Northern Karama camps cluster. Inded, ACTED completed the construction of a water supply system in the southern part of Karama camp cluster, providing access to water networks to 30 camps while reducing the camp population total dependency on water trucking from 78% to 44%. The remaining 27 camps in the camp cluster still rely on water trucking delivered at household level as their main source of water. Therefore, the construction of a water system supplying the remaining 27 camps will allow 100% access to water through water network for the camps residents. Out of campsEmergency and quick impact response• Provision of life-saving emergency water trucking and waste collection services in camps and informal settlements in areas of Azaz district with high severity of needs following new displacement;• Prepositioning of 3,000 hygiene kits in Azaz district (Aleppo governorate) to respond to expected new displacements in 2019. Rehabilitation of WASH assets - The activity will reduce the financial burden on beneficiaries and dependency on private water trucks, building community resilience. The activity will also increase the capacity of target communities to absorb further influx of populations in the face of potential large-scale population movements in 2019. All locations of interventions were identified on the basis of: the severity of needs in the area; the lack of current sustainable safe water sources; lack of planned rehabilitation works by other actors on the infrastructure; the number of vulnerable conflict-affected individuals who will benefit from the rehabilitation; possibility to handover the structure to local authorities after the completion of works.• Rehabilitation of water systems, as well as water and sanitation infrastructure in health facilities, and development of a sustainable transition plan in Idleb, Harim, and Jish Ash-Shugur districts, Idleb governorate• Rehabilitation of Baruz and Arshaf (Aghtrin sub-district), Kaljibrin (Azaz sub-district) and Baraghided (Suran sub-district) water supplies in Azaz district (Aleppo governorate). This activity will be complemented by the implementation of a Water Safety plan including 3 components: (1) large community sensitization and training campaign on water quality measures, (2) the establishment of quality monitoring measures at the water station sites and distribution points, (3) a private tanks flushing campaign in the target areas, working through daily workers recruited from the area to ensure that water storage is optimal for safe water quality.• Rehabilitation of waste water infrastructure in the 4 communities targeted in Azaz district (Aleppo governorate) to provide a comprehensive WASH assistance and improve sustainably the quality of the water Handover to the local authorities • The handover of rehabilitated assets to, and establishment of cost-recovery and water safety plans with, local stakeholders aims at ensuring the sustainability of the self-managed infrastructure;• All rehabilitations will include 3 months of support for Operation and Maintenance to ensure the development and operationalization of a sustainable exit strategy. All activities will be complemented by hygiene promotion mainstreaming in Northwest Syria.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Non-inclusion of special needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community mobilisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities. Focus Group Discussion are conducted in order to listen to vulnerable people’s preferences and needs in order to respond accordingly. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk: Inadequate intervention to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups Likelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: During the project implementation, ACTED ensures the active participation of women and girls (in separate meetings so that they can freely express their ideas and concerns) in deciding on the location and design of latrines and WASH areas to maximize safety, privacy and dignity. Special needs of women and girls will be taken into account throughout the implementation of the activities, the particular necessities of pregnant and lactating women, female head of household, and elderly or disabled women will be taken into account, and should they be unable to reach the distribution sites, they will be able to obtain the kits by proxy. ACTED ensure that water collection point and distributions site are safe and easily accessible for the most vulnerable groups, including women. Finally, all along the distributions, ACTED will constantly monitor protection and gender issues, to ensure they are adequately identified and addressed. By rehabilitating water stations and installing new water points, ACTED aims at reducing the distance between households shelters and water collection point. This mitigates protection concerns for women and accessible issues for people with disabilities. Additionally, the WASH activities will decrease the risk of water borne diseases, which often affect children more significantly. Components of the hygiene kits/vouchers and hygiene promotion will also focus on women’s personal hygiene, reducing risks for their health. Risk: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV), as well as a PSEA policy. ACTED’s PSEA policy stresses the importance of prevention but also explain ACTED’s mechanisms for report and investigation. Additionally, as part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.
