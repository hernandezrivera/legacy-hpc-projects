Project Summary

Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 180 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. PUI seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the local capacities, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs.

Given the persisting humanitarian needs within Borno State, and the increased security threats towards the civilian resulting in afflux of IDPs in Maiduguri, Monguno and other perceived safe LGA, the proposed project aims to improve access to basic services through an integrated health and nutrition response for conflict affected populations, based on a community approach.

1 - Needs

The armed conflict in North east Nigeria has continued through the year 2020 with Borno state being the worst hit. The people who are in need of humanitarian assistance has risen from 7.9 million in the beginning of the year 2020 to 10.6 million people towards the end; 80% of this vulnerable population are women and children. It has been estimated that over 300,000 children are acutely malnourished, with majority in Borno state. Also, over 40% of the health facilities have been destroyed in the region. The nutrition situation has significantly deteriorated in towards the end of the year 2020, owing to the lean season (rising food prices and low harvest) complicated by disease outbreaks and continued insecurity situation. In MMC, the residents of Bolori-II ward where (border community to Jere and konduga LGAs) have continued to report arrival of displaced people from neighbouring communities in Konduga and Magumeri LGAs due to physical and socioeconomic insecurity. About 240 households arrived the community between August to October 2020 alone. These new arrivals depend on the existing health and nutrition facilities in Bolori-II, adding to the needs to be addressed.Monguno LGA has continued to witness increased humanitarian needs due to the conflict's prolonged nature and OAGs territorial extension, especially ISWAP around this area. The overall humanitarian crisis further deteriorated due to the adverse effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and related restrictive measures . Although strict lockdown measures were not implemented in Monguno, the impact was significantly felt since movements from and to Maiduguri were not possible from the 22th April to 15th May 2020, then followed by inter-state travel restrictions that lasted till June 2020. Mass displacements of people from communities around Kukawa who are mostly farmers and herders into Monguno have been recorded during the April till September due to GF operations' scale-up and counter-attacks by OAGs. During this period, around 250 households' arrivals were reported, including people coming by their means or rescued by GF who brought them to Monguno.In the North East region, the number of children admitted into nutrition treatment services continued to rise around September 2020 compared to the same period in 2019. A 30% increase has been reported on admission caseload for nutrition treatment in the BAY region . PUI has also recorded similar dramatic increase in Bolori-II ward of MMC, with about 180% increase in the admission rate for Severe Acute Malnutrition in PUI OTPs alone in September 2020. This almost tripling of the rate from previous year. Monguno displays a steady increase in GAM rate, indicating that continuous nutrition intervention is not yet enough to meet population's needs. In September 2020, UNICEF conducted a sentinel survey following multiple nutrition alerts from various LGAs including Monguno, where the survey by MUAC revealed an increase in the GAM rate by 13.9% in the second and third quartile of the year, with GAM rate as high as 15.38% among children aged 6-23 months in Monguno.

2 - Activities

PUI nutrition intervention strategy will continue to adopt evidence based integrated multisectoral approach including health, FSL and protection towards addressing the nutrition gaps in Borno state. PUI plans to reach both host population and IDPs including returnees in Bolori (MMC) and Monguno. To ensure relevant impact on reducing malnutrition with in the community, PUI delivers integrated Nutrition package of CMAM, IYCF and Micronutrient powder (MNP) distribution to tackle all forms of malnutrition which overlap. In addition, PUI is planning to start targeted supplementary feeding program in Monguno using locally sourced nutrition rich foods such as Tom-brown to address MAM. IYCF interventions with focus on preventive Social Behavior Change Communication through a network of community outreach volunteers and Mother Care Groups as well as skilled based counseling of IYCF at health facilities mainly critical contact points in the OTP, SC, TSFP and SRH services. Alongside with IYCF messaging, PUI also target MNP distributions for 6-23 months children to improve dietary fortification of essential micronutrients. CMAM approach for timely identification and treatment of SAM (OTP and SC) and MAM (TSFP) in line with WHO and MoH guidelines while applying new sustainable innovations through the provision of essential medicines and therapeutic supplies for management of malnutrition, and extending support to complicated SAM cases in the Stabilization Center as well as empowering the community to address malnutrition using locally sourced nutritious foods. To reduce exposure of COVID-19, delivery of nutrition activities at the community level will be an important step to ensure affected population has access to the services and implement COVID-19 infection prevention and control measures. PUI will adopt the nutrition sector proposed strategies to mitigate the outbreak such as implementation of Mother-MUAC approach for continuation of screening, ensuring of protective measures are applied by Health care providers and volunteers and sensitization of the beneficiary communities on the preventive measures of Covid-19. To ensure continued screening, identification and referral of acute malnutrition to facilities, the mother-MUAC approach will be continued through training of the mothers of children <5years on how to asses acute malnutrition using MUAC screening and oedema identification and referral of identified SAM cases to the nearest service providers. The approach significantly reduces exposure between the nutrition service providers and beneficiaries by avoiding gatherings for screening at the community. The mothers trained on the Mother-MUAC approach will perform MUAC screening for their children and will refer for confirmation and treatment of the identified SAM children to the nearest OTP. In the context of restricted gathering during screening in the context of Covid-19 outbreak, this will give mothers ability to identify the earliest signs of malnutrition. All PUI and MoH staffs working in PUI supported health facilities and in the community will be trained on the prevention measures of the COVID-19 infection to protect themselves beneficiaries. Meanwhile all the facilities will adopt the infection prevention measures through applying hand washing at entrances of health facilities, social distancing of 2m between service providers and between beneficiaries and establishing screenings sites at the gates of all PUI supported health facilities. Infrared Thermometer will be used by a trained health work to screen all patients, their caretakers and staff at the gate. Those found to be having high temperatures of >38 will directed to COVID-19 screening area from where suspected cases will be put in an isolation room and state surveillance team notified immediately.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The Nutrition program, especially through the IYCF component directly empowers women and mothers in the community through: Identification of mothers to take part in the Mother Care Groups activities to raise awareness on IYCF key messages. Through community consultation at the beneficiary selection phase, PUI will ensure that the community is aware of – and in support of – the beneficiary selection criteria to reduce risk of tension arising from the assistance and that the most vulnerable populations are selected for the intervention; Creating strong community linkages between women and mothers in the community through MCGs  Promoting the election of Lead Mothers within each MCGs  Through lessons learnt from previous programming, targeted populations have provided valuable feedback into this component. Among those are creating additional MCGs as many women within the community had growing interest, better integration into PUI protection and Food Security programming, and the need for identification and treatment of MAM. Moreover, all Nutrition outreach workers are provided with protection mainstreaming trainings in addition of induction sessions on the safe identification and referral of protection issues to relevant protection officers internally, in addition to training on PUI’s code of conduct and humanitarian principles.
