Project Summary

SPEDP proposes a 12 months HRP project aiming to improve access to timely and appropriate shelter and non-food items to vulnerable populations in Central and Eastern Equatoria States through integrated and inclusive response. The intervention will protect the vulnerable populations from the harsh weather conditions and disease-causing vectors, direct impacts of Covid-19 and GBV risks. SPEDP has static presence in Lainya, Morobo, Kajo Keji and Magwi Counties with strong working relationship with the local authorities, community and partners on the ground. SPEDP has been playing the role of Co-SFP for Central Equatoria state for the last three years and it is a member of Shelter/NFI strategic advisory group (SAG). With these multiple experiences, SPEDP will collaborate and coordinate with the CCCM Cluster for the provision of shelter, non-food items and CCCM assistance to new IDPs or returnees in the proposed project locations. SPEDP will also engage with local authorities, housing land, and property (HLP) actors in resolving issues that may arise because of HLP. SPEDP will seek guidance from the Protection Cluster, leading the national HLP Technical Working Group (HLP TWG) to ensure comprehensive programming on HLP and shelter. Identification of persons in need will be accompanied by services such as legal assistance and case management. SPEDP in collaboration with the SFP will conduct training on HLP access rights and working with authorities for allocation of land for IDPs or returnees, dispute resolution, legal assistance and using KII to get feedbacks from the targeted beneficiaries.
The proposed intervention will reach a total target of 20,000 individuals among internal displaced people (IDPs), returnees, host community and people with special needs. SPEDP will engage in needs assessments to identify the needs conduct in-kind distribution of standard emergence shelter materials to 3,400 individuals and in-kind standard lifesaving NFIs to 6,100individuals among the vulnerable populations in Lainya, Morobo Kajo Keji and Magwi Counties. SPEDP has 7 years of experience in cash and voucher assistance in Central and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bhar El Ghazal. SPEDP will use the same modality to respond to 6,000 individuals with cash and voucher assistance for essential emergency shelter upgrades and 4,200 individuals with cash and voucher assistance for NFI replacement and 709 individuals with cash and voucher assistance for shelter reconstruction and rehabilitation. The project teams will also engage in post distribution monitoring to ascertain beneficiaries' satisfaction on the items distributed. Community engagement modalities will continue to form a critical part of all shelter and NFI interventions, incorporating such approaches as community consultations on feedback, complain boxes, hotlines and institutionalization of complaint and feedback mechanisms.
The project teams will highly consider consultative approach with local stakeholders during assessments, vulnerability criteria to the identification and selection of the project beneficiaries and monitoring and post distribution monitoring. The project will pay close attention to women headed households, child headed households, older people and people with disabilities incorporating gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs analysis and response recommendations.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is facing a worsening humanitarian condition, amidst rising inflation, increased insecurity, droughts, and floods compounded with direct and indirect effects of COVID-19. More than two-thirds of the South Sudanese population, 8.9 million people an increase of 600,000 since 2021 and some 300,000 refugees and asylum seekers in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection in 2022 (HNO Report 2022). At the end of 2021, an estimated 1.8 million men, women, boys, girls, and older people lived in inadequate shelter. This number represents an increase in the expected numbers of people who in 2022 will need shelter support. This increased need has resulted from the new displacement of approximately 300,000 people in 2021, in addition to the existing displaced population, and the return of more than 150,000 people from neighbouring countries. The predominant needs are access to emergency shelter, NFIs and housing, and land and property (HLP) rights to ensure security of tenure. The endemic conflict and cattle migration in parts of Central and Eastern Equatoria are the main drivers of displacement and tensions between the cattle keepers and the agrarian host communities. The February feuds between cattle keepers and farmers in Magwi led to displacement of 10,547people IRNA Report (1st March, 2022). Meanwhile, the fighting between armed factions in Lainya County, Central Equatoria displaced approximately 13,300 people (OCHA Jan 2022 Snapshot). Unfavorable conditions in the refugee camps in Uganda and Democratic Republic of Congo culminated the voluntary return of people to their homes of origin in Lainya, Morobo, Kajo keji Counties of Central Equatoria and Magwi County of Eastern Equatoria despite of the recurring conflict in the Country. There are 46,131 individuals’ returnees in Kajo Keji County in need of humanitarian assistance (OCHA Coordinated assessment report – (30th Sept to 7th November 2021). The needs for emergency shelter and NFI are so enormous among the displaced, returnees, and host communities in Lainya, Morobo, Kajo keji and Magwi counties. This also requires particular attention for HLP, which continues to drive vulnerability for women and children. Despite the legal recognition of women’s rights to land and property under formal South Sudanese law, cultural norms, customary land tenure law, and manipulations of the system pose barriers to women securing their rights.

2 - Project activities

The proposed intervention will contribute to the cluster objectives and will reach a total target of 20,000 people (4400men, 5000women, 5200boys and 5400girls) among internal displaced people (IDPs), returnees, host community and people with special needs. 1) Need Analysis: SPEDP will conduct need analysis in all the targeted project locations to ensure that the needs of the targeted beneficiaries are considered in the order of their priorities to enable the project respond appropriately. 2) Distribution of in-kind items: SPEDP will conduct distribution of in-kind standard emergence shelter materials to 3,400 individuals and in-kind standard lifesaving NFIs to 6,100 individuals among the vulnerable populations in Lainya, Morobo Kajo Keji and Magwi Counties. Before the distribution beneficiaries verification and registration will be conducted. This intervention will be coordinated with static partners for joint distribution. 3) Cash and voucher assistance: SPEDP has 7 years of experience in implementation of cash and voucher assistance in Central and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bhar El Ghazal states. SPEDP will provide 6,000 individuals with cash and voucher assistance for essential emergency shelter upgrades and 4,200 individuals with cash and voucher assistance for NFI replacement and 709 individuals with cash and voucher assistance for shelter reconstruction and rehabilitation. SPEDP will do a coordinated market assessment with partners in the targeted locations. The assessment will be conducted through Key informant interviews with a wide range of stakeholders such as households, private sector, government officials and other humanitarian actors and also conduct focus group discussions (FGD) with vendors, wholesalers, producers and households. The market assessment will focus on functional markets, the resources available and the risk that the intended response will harm the market. Where there is availability of functional markets, SPEDP will conduct regular monitoring of prices and the volume of transactions shall be carried out in order to identify market distortions at an early stage. 4) Post distribution monitoring: The project teams will also engage in post distribution monitoring to ascertain beneficiaries' satisfaction on the items distributed. PDMs normally done two weeks’ after distribution of items. 5) CFM/community engagement: Community engagement modalities will continue to form a critical part of all Shelter and NFI interventions, incorporating such approaches as community consultations on feedback, complain boxes, hotlines and institutionalization of complaint and feedback mechanisms. To demonstrate SPEDP's commitment to AAP mechanism, SPEDP will ensure strong accountability in its responses toward affected communities directly affected by disasters and conflicts by involving them directly in project design and encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. SPEDP will report to the cluster on weekly basis about the progress of the intervention.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

There are fully functional markets in Magwi County and partially functional markets in Kajo Keji and Morobo Counties that will promote this modality of response. SPEDP had multiple cash and voucher assistance responding to the food security needs of the vulnerable populations in Magwi County for the last three years with support from World Vision and Dan Church Aid. This modality will target beneficiaries in Magwi, Morobo and Kajo Keji Counties to access shelter and non food items in a timely manner. The modality will mostly target protracted internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities preferably women headed households, child headed households, older people and people with disabilities to acquire shelter materials from the market to replace their NFIs and upgrade their shelters since most of the shelters in Magwi, Morobo and Kajo Keji counties were vandalized during the 2016 conflict that displaced many people to Uganda and DRC. Cash will be provided only if no other actor can provide support, and the protection risk can only be resolved or reduced through this means (for example providing cash for transport to a life-saving service which the person would otherwise not be able to access), deemed safe for the person in need to move to a safe space/location or access life-saving services but does not have the means to do so. The cash assistance is provided on an individual basis and only following a thorough risk analysis.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The proposed location for this modality is found suitable as there are available functional markets in Magwi County. To ensure market functionality, SPEDP will do market assessment to verify the availability of the shelter and NFI materials and monitor their costs. SPEDP is also aware that vendors increase their supplies based on market demand. Most importantly, The area is blessed in terms of the availability of natural local resources for the shelters especially the construction materials which among others includes wooden poles represented by wooden poles, grass, ropes then followed by the rest of the items. In addition, there are available shelter materials that are sold in the existing shops in the market and these include; timbers, iron sheets, nails, plastic sheets and NFIs. The traders will be encouraged to restock their shops with the most needed items prior to the project implementation. Although, some traders in the markets generally may have constraints to increasing inventory due to lack of financial capital, compounded by limited access to credit; customers’ low purchasing power; price variability dependent on seasonality; and external shocks, such as conflict, Inflation and the sharp depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound (SSP).

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SPEDP will identify and support feedback channels that communities prefer while ensuring that feedback mechanisms are sensitive to age, gender and diversity (AGD) and accessible to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities and others at high risk. The feedback will inform the proposed project activities to ensure that the project responses to the community needs for women, girls, men and boys. In addition, the feedback will be incorporated for future projects if the available resources are not sufficient enough to address the identified needs. SPEDP has a functional CFRM that allows beneficiaries to give feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. SPEDP CFRM desks will be set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service providers when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. SPEDP has a standardized CFRM form where all feedback and complaints are captured. All data captured on the CFRM forms is then uploaded onto a password protected database where all feedback and complaints are tacked until they are closed. Furthermore, beneficiary complaints and feedback will also be captured when conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM). SPEDP therefore uses CFRM form to make its interventions relevant to the beneficiaries needs and adapt services that suit the needs of the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SPEDP values special interest groups (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) as an integral part of its beneficiaries and take proactive measure to ensure that they are involved in all stages of its project; from planning, designing, implementations, monitoring and evaluations. SPEDP has been implementing CFRM in all project locations, which would be capable of handling complaints related to any of its activities, and will have several entry points for lodging complaints comprising of in-person office visits, Suggestion Box and through community visits either orally at or formal written complaints, at CFRM desks, and community representatives. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. Utilizing the Women and Girls Friendly Spaces, monthly FGDs and listening sessions will be held to collect feedback from the women and girls in regards to the project. SPEDP also placed special attention to capacity building of local staff to ensure that beneficiaries are served by staff both male and females known to them, connected to them by belonging and language so that they can easily interact and register feedback in their native language. This facilitates feedback from the most vulnerable community. More importantly, SPEDP Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to SPEDP protection team to ensure sensitive issues are handled by qualified staff only.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP staff have signed a code of conduct which includes zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse policy. SPEDP commits to continuously educate beneficiaries on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and other violations and to inform them on reporting mechanisms in place when such cases occur. SPEDP will strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project period across the proposed locations. All project staff will be trained on PSEA. WGFSs are used as entry points for reporting PSEA cases for women and girls. SPEDP will support survivors of PSEA to access support in a manner that does not compromise their dignity and privacy and confidentiality. PSEA messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members are aware of what PSEA entails, the available reporting mechanisms and the importance of reporting. SPEDP will build the capacity of PSEA networks or focal point(s) who will be included in the community sensitization activities to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms. IEC materials with PSEA messages will be developed, translated into the local language, and distributed to the communities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community engagement modalities will continue to form a critical part of all shelter and NFI interventions, incorporating such approaches as community consultations on feedback, complain boxes, hotlines and institutionalization of complaint and feedback mechanisms. To demonstrate SPEDP's commitment to AAP mechanism, SPEDP will ensure strong accountability in its responses toward affected communities directly affected by disasters and conflicts by involving them directly in project design and encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. SPEDP will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the AAP mechanism. SPEDP will establish community feedback and response mechanism (CFRM) in all its operational sites through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from SPEDP. SPEDP will use periodic focus group discussions (FGDs) and an ongoing feedback/complaint collections and response mechanism throughout this project to obtain feedback from beneficiaries. Communities will be informed about the SPEDP Focal Point to handle any compliant report and feedback. SPEDP will explain its procedures, decisions from the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries to allow women, girls, men and boys to hold SPEDP accountable. SPEDP will use IEC materials that are displayed/shared in order to raise awareness on the feedback and complaint mechanism.
