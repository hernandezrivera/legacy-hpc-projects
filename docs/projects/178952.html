Project Summary

This project aims at providing the following services to IDPs sites:
a) Care and maintenance of 6 IDP sites in A’zaz, Ghandoura, Dana and Harim sub-districts in case of floods, fire incidents ...etc. This includes
1. Train 240 IDPs ( youth, women and men) on tents and roads maintenance in the six existing IDP sites in A’zaz, Ghandoura, Dana and Harim sub-districts and connecting them with the recruitment committees in the camps for the planned activities of maintenance.
2. Equip and train 240 IDPs emergency responders and IDP committees in the six existing IDP sites in A’zaz, Ghandoura, Dana and Harim sub-districts.
3. Care and maintenance for 6 existing IDP sites in A’zaz, Ghandoura, Dana and Harim sub-districts. (technicians and labor can conduct light to medium maintenance services such as for electricity, water, and sewage network in the camps).
4. Establishment of camps committees through training of 92 IDPs on camps management in 6 existing IDP sites in A’zaz, Ghandoura, Dana and Harim sub-districts.
Shafak will use the vouchers modality as a redemption method for the recruited staff from the camps. Camps selection would be through identifying the camps that are at risk of floods or have the effects of floods previously in addition to applying the vulnerability criteria where camps with higher numbers of women, elderly, children and disable persons would be prioritized.
The scope of work has been limited to training of IDPs, Care and Maintenance and camps committees formation activities as they are the top priorities in camps in order to mitigate the effects of crisis floods and fire incidents. Yet, CARE and Maintenance activities are still top priorities out of these activities. During the project, Shafak intends to communicate with the stakeholders like camps managements, beneficiaries, local authorities and donors through reporting on the displacement, floods and fire incidents and creating advocacy messages to support IDPs in the camps in addition to the meetings Shafak normally have with the camps representatives during signing MoUs and communication channels for the beneficiaries like hot line and complaints boxes and whatsapp. Shafak keeps tracking on incidents reports and displacement reports issued by CCCM and keeps close monitoring for the displacement and incident on the ground ( bi-weekly basis) which are reported to CCCM. In case of underfunding for these activities, Shafak will prioritize camps at risks with more vulnerable groups like women, elderly and the disable and will work on CARE and maintenance activities only (activity 3), reducing the number of trained people.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, over seven million people are highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in 1,116 IDP last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate in Aleppo the number of displaced people increased significantly, with many IDPs forced to seek shelter in planned camps, informal camps or settlements, transit/reception centres, and collective centres. The vast majority of these IDPs resides in informal settlements where shelter and WASH conditions as well as access to basic services, including health and protection, are inadequate or absent. Shafak conducted a rapid need assessment (multi-sectorial assessment) where 1031 households in 18 sub-districts participated and were interviewed. Major problems of shelter are ranked as the following: Shelters overcrowded (22%), shelters are not protective against weather conditions (19%), shelters do not have enough space between each other’s, and shelters are not enough available (16%). Incidents continue to impact IDP sites, exacerbating existing challenges and vulnerabilities, such as lack of proper infrastructure or site planning. In 2020, the CCCM Cluster in North-west Syria reported 101 incidents in 152 sites, that happened mainly from fires (79 incidents) and floods (12 incidents). 2,278 shelters were destroyed, and 2,761 shelters were partially damaged. According to Shafak above mentioned assessment, the specific shelter interventions are most urgently required are; 93% for cash assistance, 66% for shelter rehabilitation, 43% for electricity/lighting, 22% for temporary shelters/tents and 20% for shelter replacement.

5 - Implementation of activities

Shafak will implement the proposed activities depending on its usual practices and SOPs derived from working with different INGO and UN Agencies. Shafak will follow the following implementation steps: -Needs assessment to determine the IDP sites affected by any humanitarian disasters which are in need for maintenance, emergency services, and/or care services. -Technical assessment and preparing BoQs -Recruitment of skilled IDPs committees for leading maintenance work and train unskilled workers from the IDPs on maintenance activities in the IDPs sites linking with CFWs if applicable with a particular focus on enrolling youth and mid-age adults -Rehabilitation activities will include 1) train maintenance and emergency responders and IDPs committees 2) maintenance of alleys between tents, electricity, sewage and water network -Train IDPs groups on camps management and support them to form camps committees inclusive of youths, men and women -Monitoring and evaluation for the activity - Exit strategy for the project and hand over to the camp management. As for payment for the workers ( skilled and unskilled) , Shafak will be delivering this following Shafak SoP for cash vouchers in alignment with CWG will start with - Community mobilization - Vouchers printing - Contracting financial service providers (FSPs) - Registration of HHs and final selection of BNFs - Identification of beneficiaries through their IDs, or other personal documents- Distribution of vouchers to beneficiaries (one-off distribution)- Redemption of vouchers through FSP after cross check by finance team along with FSP- Reconciliation and PDM.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1st Risk: Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by cash for workers, male beneficiaries while registering for the services or attending the training. Mitigating Measure: - recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old) - make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA - set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children. 2nd Risk : Marginalization of most vulnerable groups like disable and women during project design and implementation. Mitigating Measure: Set a fixed quota for these groups to be involved in the camps committees, ensure that field teams have female staff and ensure that mobilization sessions for the project include all categories. 3rd Risk: Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities (PWD). Mitigating Measure: - Register all adult women, unaccompanied Register all adult women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients of services- During post-distribution follow-up, understand how women, children, and other groups accessed the services and their feedback on the process and materials 4th Risk: Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures.Mitigating Measure: Programme activities which engage women will be endorse by community and religious leaders to gain support from male community members.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1st Risk: recruitment of care and maintenance committee could create some tensions because of their fees payments. Likelihood: High. Mitigation Measures: -Include the unskilled IDPs residents in the training and maintenance activities - Ensure that skilled workers in the targeted IDPs are involved in the leading committees recruited for construction - Maintenance roles are clarified early to prevent labour-seeking vandalism - Ensure that CRM mechanism is clear and active. 2nd Risk: Trainings are not welcomed by the local authorities. Likelihood: High. Mitigation Measures: -Tailor each training module to the context, coordinate with the key representatives in the camps for reducing unexpected risks -Advocacy with local authorities on all level. 3rd Risk: Create pull-factor and expose IDPs to physical safety risks when camps and settlements are located in unsafe areas). Likelihood: High. Mitigation Measures: - Ensure that response is done to only captured camps by ISIMM tool-Ensure that Cash and tents distributions taking place are aligned with SNFI, CCCM and Cash Working Groups SoPs. Set clear camps selection criteria and mobilize them among the camps. 4th Risk: Turkey-Syria border closure and inability to transfer vouchers. Likelihood: medium. Mitigation Measures: - Include in the procurement- Announcement that the vouchers printing can be in Syria.5th Risk: tension between IDPs and HC post the distribution of MPCs for IDPs in collective centers. Likelihood: High. Mitigation Measures: Coordinate with the local authorities and explain the project scope. 6th Risk: Over-rely on IDPs committee in case of any emergency. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: Responses incorporates general awareness raising focus on individuals.
