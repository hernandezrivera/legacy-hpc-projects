Project Summary

The proposed project will be given support to the most vulnerable Rohingya community who are suffering from food shortage due to cut of WFP ration size and will also be given support to the host community to reduce food insecurity and strengthening coping up mechanism/resilience.
Food security for Rohingya Communities:
The project will contribute to increase food security of Rohingya refugees. This includes food security interventions integrating with climate adaptive technological practices like climate smart agriculture (Homestead/kitchen/vertical gardening and pit composting). Situational steps also be taken aligning with food security sector’s principles and government guideline to address food insecurity by providing livestock support. Emphasize will be on diversified food production, behavioral change, and practices. Considering the unprecedented scenario, (WFP ration cut and impact in the camps) Oxfam along with partner will emphasize homestead production (including rooftop/vertical/sack gardening) round the year and use of fellow land. Comprehensive training packages will be included according to FSS manual and guidelines. Camp dwellers will exchange their technical knowledge and practices for increasing production and consumption of nutritious food.
Food security and Livelihood for Host Communities:
The project will enhance food security and livelihood conditions of the community by increasing access to the needed foods and gain access to nutritious food as well. Considering the multi-vulnerabilities factors such as economic crisis, price increase and pre-existing natural hazards, Oxfam will provide support through food and income generating activity (IGAs) to sustain their livelihoods and to reduce vulnerability and uplift the living quality of vulnerable host communities especially vulnerable women and girls, children, the elderly, and people at-risk living in host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila.
Comprehensive Green and climate resilient gardening and pit composting will help the community to become more resilient to climate shocks, enable to maintain food availability during any emergency, meet family members' basic and nutritional needs. In addition to, the project participant will be capable to get access to fresh vegetables and employment opportunities. Oxfam will apply the principle of "do no harm" during the programme design phase and to strengthen social cohesion among the host communities using various techniques and approaches.
Apart from the lifesaving food security support, the long-term humanitarian responses need to call for a review of the response and market linkage modalities currently in place and rethink of humanitarian strategies for sustainable job market for host community. Greater emphasis needs to be placed on market linkage and strengthening linkages between sectors with a view to drive an integrated approach to enable meeting the essential needs of affected populations.
Above mentioned food security interventions (for camp and host) along with other INGO and NGOs actors may contribute to further strengthened peaceful co-existence by sharing safe food, nutritious food, and climate adaptive practices.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The influx was a shock for the already fragile subdistricts which saw their population increase by 140 percent. By December 2022, Cox’s Bazar hosted more than 923,000 Rohingya, the largest refugee settlement in the world, and the fourth largest urban centre in the country, with a population density of 33,612 inhabitants per km2, similar to the most densely populated cities in the world. According to the latest REVA-6, Around two-thirds of households in the camps (65 percent) and half of the households in the Bangladeshi community (55 percent) relied on less preferred or less expensive food as the most frequently used coping strategy for both populations. In 2022, overall vulnerability continued an upward trend in both the Rohingya and Bangladeshi communities, resulting in much higher prevalence than in pre-pandemic years. For the Rohingya, 95 percent of households were found with moderate to high vulnerability, much higher than the 55 percent Bangladeshi community level. Economic vulnerability among Bangladeshi community households, further increased to 35 percent in 2022 as compared to 32 percent in 2021, which was already higher than the 2019 pre COVID-19 level (26 percent). The overall acceptable food consumption for the Rohingya remained almost unchanged since 2019 (56 percent versus 58 percent) but worsened by 14 percentage points for the Bangladeshi community households, dropping from 79 percent to 65 percent from 2019 to 2022. Among Rohingya households, levels of poor food consumption also improved, decreasing from 4 to 3 percent between 2021 and 2022. In the Bangladeshi community, inadequate food consumption (poor and borderline) dropped from 38 to 34 percent from 2021 to 2022 mainly driven by a decrease in borderline food consumption from 37 to 32 percent Source (REVA-6) While the refugees are plagued by poverty, in March 2023, WFP had reduced its life-saving food vouchers from US$ 12 to US$ 10 per person per month. With the funding gap persisting, starting on 1 June WFP again decreased the voucher value for a second time – from US$ 10 to US$ 8. This reduction in ration will push the current food assistance provided to the Rohingyas fall far below the recognized global humanitarian standard of 2,100 kcal, entailing a significant drop in refugees’ food intake (67 percent of full assistance; only 83 percent of energy requirements per SPHERE standards). In the Bangladeshi community, far fewer households were classified as highly vulnerable (15 percent), and those households had an average monthly income of BDT 11,529 (US$115). In both communities, households were classified as highly vulnerable mainly due to their use of negative coping strategies (seeking or taking support from friends, relatives, or neighbor’s; selling assistance; begging). Access to fresh and nutritious food continues to be an obstacle for the refugee population residing in Cox’s Bazar camps and host community areas due to the ongoing Rohingya crises and disaster vulnerability. Food security needs in the Rohingya camp and host community in Bangladesh require the establishment of reliable and equitable food distribution systems, prioritizing vulnerable groups. Sustainable agriculture practices, community gardening, and access to seeds and tools are crucial for enhancing food production. Community engagement and awareness programs play a vital role in building resilience for both Rohingya refugees and host communities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

(a) Adopted an integrated approach through Effective coordination with relevant stakeholder’s: OXFAM will assist with all activities related to food security in the same camp and surrounding areas. To facilitate an integrated approach across these sectors and to guarantee seamless coordination with varying tiers of stakeholders, Oxfam will offer these various services under one roof. To find gaps and prevent duplication of aid, Oxfam programme teams actively participate in inter-agency clusters coordinated by the government and the United Nations (UN). Oxfam provides frequent updates on its operations via clusters, which are incorporated into the database "Who Does What, Where, and When. Oxfam will assist the host and Rohingya communities in all endeavors and carry out the essential coordination and fostering of mutual understanding. (b) Predictability and continuity through the relationships with the Government and local administration: Oxfam works closely with the Government department and has positive relationships with them. Oxfam meets with and reports on programme activity to the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) and the Deputy Commissioner (DC), the Upazilla Nirbahi Officer (UNO), Camp Coordination, and the Camp in Charge (CiC). Oxfam ensures that programme plans are shared with local government departments by the government's designated CiC, UNO. Oxfam established project objectives to increase production and create market linkages with relevant stakeholder to ensure long-term food security and sustainable livelihood initiatives. Furthermore, climate resilient and adaptive homestead gardening, Green Cash for Work (e.g., waste management, tree plantation) will contribute to the development of environmental sustainability in the targeted area and reduce future climate risks.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming entails incorporating protection concerns into various sectors and activities to ensure that the rights and well-being of affected populations are taken into account throughout the project lifecycle, as well as ensuring inclusive programming, capacity building, community engagement, monitoring and evaluation, coordination with stakeholders, advocacy, and awareness through internal and external other department linkage. Additionally, SAFE programming will be implemented, which is focused on detecting potential project risks and mitigating actions to prevent or mitigate those risks. The project will also ensure that raised protection issues are addressed through collaboration with the Oxfam Protection team when needed. To ensure that PSEA and safeguarding are completely understood by Oxfam workers, all new employees get protection and safe programming training, while long-term employees must attend mandatory refresher training on a regular basis.

3.3 - Age & Disability

we prioritize addressing cross-cutting issues related to age and disabilities through a multifaceted approach. 3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations is at the heart of Oxfam's interventions, accountability to project participants is included into the design of this intervention, supporting our commitment to safeguarding and community engagement. In accordance with the Core Humanitarian Standard and strategy, Oxfam is implementing a community participation-centered approach throughout the project cycle to ensure that participants are consulted during assessments, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. Oxfam will also actively involve the community in all monitoring and evaluation activities, including frequent feedback sessions via community consultation meetings and input opportunities during and after each distribution. The hotline, helpdesk, and complaints tools will also allow communities to submit input. The CPT is an additional tool that allows communities to express their opinions while also helping staff to capture, assess, and comprehend community impressions. Oxfam will be able to tailor programming to better suit the needs of the community’s using information acquired through regular feedback and consultative sessions, as well as information recorded through the CPT. Mechanisms for feedback and complaints will be used to highlight what works well and where changes are needed. Survey CTO will be used to collect feedback data, and enumerators will use cellphones to collect data from participants. Based on comments and PDM findings, a monthly accountability report will be created.
