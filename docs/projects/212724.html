Project Summary

The intervention addresses critical shelter and winterization needs for vulnerable populations in NWS. It focuses on implementing dignified shelter units for 500 households of internally displaced persons (IDPs) of the most vulnerable population and implementing disaster risk reduction activities targeting the NWS camp by rehabilitating essential infrastructure, including 15 km of roads and drainage. The project extends winterization assistance to 10,000 camp residents.
The dignified shelter activities, that focus on Idleb governorate, includes various activities aims to address shelter needs and improving living conditions for vulnerable populations in NWS. Community outreach and HLP confirmation involve engagement with local stakeholders, including IDP representatives, to ensure the proposed shelter solution aligns with beneficiaries' needs as well as, involves awareness sessions, and design selection, ensuring alignment with SNFI Cluster guidelines The HLP confirmation process includes a deep verification of ownership for the selected public land. Developing public infrastructure involves topographical studies and implementing various elements like site preparation, drainage systems, and solar lampposts. The procurement process emphasizes transparency, cost-effectiveness, and adherence to standards, with qualified contractors selected for implementation. Public infrastructure implementation includes site preparation, drainage systems, shelter foundations, and solar lampposts, with daily monitoring and quality assurance measures. The prioritization process identifies beneficiaries based on vulnerability criteria and community input. The implementation of dignified shelters involves a combination of contractor-led and beneficiary-led works, with cash disbursement tranches to project milestones. Ongoing protection measures include safety audits, awareness sessions, and referrals to other services as needed. The entire process aims to improve living conditions while involving the community in decision-making and ensuring compliance with humanitarian and protection standards. Likewise, GC will rehabilitate critical infrastructure in the NWS camps to facilitate the delivery of life-saving essential WASH services and improve the overall shelter status of the Camp residents, particularly those affected by seasonal flooding. We will gravel roads, repair and/or redirect drainage.
For winterization support activities, Global Communities will target the most vulnerable population in Dana subdistrict with Winterization assistance through voucher modality reaching 10,000 HH, prioritizing the population who lives in Atmeh camp, as GC is the main actor of SNFI activities there.
According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview Summary, Idleb has the highest concentrations of people in need across Syria, while at the same time these areas are deemed to primarily have “extreme” levels of intersectoral needs. According to the Action Plan for Dignified Shelter & Living Conditions in Northwest Syria, 800,000 people in NWS live in tents, 90% of which are more than one year old. Furthermore, 83% of IDPs live in “critically overcrowded” conditions. Only 188 of the 1,468 IDP sites in NWS are planned, and unplanned sites suffer from diminished services such as site planning, waste disposal, sewage systems, dependable water supply, and easy, reliable site access. While dignified shelter solutions cost 2-3 times as much as tents up front, recognizing the cost savings generated over 5-10 years of use and improved quality of life for residents, the Shelter Cluster, technical leaders, and communities are advocating for an investment in dignified shelter. As of December 2023, according to the OCHA North-West Syria Situation Report, over 28,500 families have been moved out of tents into dignified shelters as of mid-December. At this rate, it will take five years to reach the target as put forth in the aforementioned Action Plan unless funding increases.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

During project implementation, several anticipated risks encompass issues like fake beneficiaries, low-capacity service providers, and safety concerns. Mitigating measures include senior staff reviews, feedback mechanisms, beneficiary registration processes, and precise market assessments. Risks related to tensions in communities, substandard shelter construction, and potential non-compliance by beneficiaries, mitigated through community outreach, daily monitoring with a remote system, and a multi-layered approach for high-quality project deliverables. Registration risks, including fake or unqualified beneficiaries, are addressed through multiple teams, adherence to selection criteria, and anti-fraud training. Risks related to the quality of works in a cash-for-shelter modality are mitigated by daily monitoring, community mobilization for awareness, and cross-departmental approval processes. Protection risks associated with the dignified shelter and infrastructure activites include eviction risks due to lack of approval from the property owner and potential exposure of beneficiaries to risk due to insufficient HLP documentation. Mitigation involves abiding by HLP guidance developed by the SNFI Cluster, obtaining consent from the owner with proof of registered HLP ownership, including HLP terms in MoUs, and incorporating an HLP section in the M&E assessment form. The risk of exclusion from shelter assistance due to a lack of civil status documents/ID is addressed by working closely with communities, leaders, and authorities to identify alternative identification methods. Risks of cash assistance, such as robbery or extortion, are mitigated through clear distribution methodologies, home delivery to prevent targeting, and verified beneficiary systems. Mitigating the high risk of abuse of authority by actors (PSEA, GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion) involves ensuring staff adherence to a Code of Conduct, developing internal procedures to identify perpetrators, and maintaining an active complaint mechanism. Additionally, crowded living conditions and inadequate lighting, gender-segregated sanitation facilities may lead to increased GBV, sexual exploitation, and communicable diseases. Mitigation includes advocating for alternative accommodation, providing gender-segregated privacy in mass shelters, offering appropriate sanitation facilities, ensuring proper lighting, collaborating with protection sectors, and extending consultations with diverse population groups, including women, girls, and persons with disabilities, on the other hand, During the beneficiaries' identification, under the dignified shelter and winterization intervention, at least 5% of the selected beneficiaries will be from protection referrals through the relevant cluster and other actors in the targeted region.
