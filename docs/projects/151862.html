Project Summary

In line with the WASH objectives 1 and 2 : In the targeted areas of Jere, Maiduguri, Monguno and Kukawa LGA's affected people will have safe and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water for domestic needs. and safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities. In line with WASH objectives 3 and 4: Affected people will benefit from community tailored gender- and age-sensitive hygiene messages aiming at hygienic behavior and practices combined with the distribution of basic gender- and age-sensitive hygiene items. All provisions will be done in line with the sectors standard WASH objective 5: Affected children admitted for SAM treatment benefit from WASH NFIs as per sector’s standard

Needs

Nigeria’s North East is currently experiencing a significant humanitarian crisis, which has resulted from the on going armed conflict between AOG' s and the Nigerian security which resulted that an estimated 1,8 million people are internally displaced. However, the host populations are also affected and the Nigerian Humanitarian Response Update of August 2018 states that there are still 7,1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. There remain 500,000 people still not reached of the 2,000,000 target for the WASH sector, making this population extremely vulnerable for further deterioration in their situation such as the Cholera out break witnessed recently in Borno. Seventy seven per cent of the IDPs are in Borno State, the epi centre of the crisis, and over half are living outside IDP camps in local communities, making it harder for humanitarian assistance to reach them and putting additional pressure on the host communities. The camps are congested with limited freedom of movement owing to insecurity and women, men and children face a loss of dignity as a result of inadequate sanitation and latrine facilities and poor sustainability of WASH services. Furthermore, the low levels of WASH infrastructure that existed before in NE Nigeria now pose a significant issue for populations returning to their home villages. Assessments and ongoing monitoring in ZOA' s current areas of operation in Borno shows that many water points have been destroyed or are contaminated on purpose or by neglect. Hygiene practices in IDP's households are poor due to a lack of basic hygiene knowledge or lack of access to basic sanitation items such as soap. Sanitation facilities are either lacking or are damaged to the point of non functionality in host communities and in IDP camps. Solid waste is often disposed of indiscriminately, and without any adequate collection system. Latrines require desludging services in three to four week cycles to ensure sanitary conditions for affected populations. When latrines fill up and remain loaded for weeks, the IDPs are forced to rely on open defecation, which increases contamination of the environment and associated risks of disease outbreaks and transmission. .
