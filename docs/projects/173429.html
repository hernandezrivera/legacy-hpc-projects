Project Summary

IOM’s Protection Project for the 2021 JRP connects four strategic objectives with a series of activities to benefit the most vulnerable Rohingya and host community persons, to reach the elderly, widows, single-headed households, persons with disabilities, and persons with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity, and expression, and to target authorities and partners in a way that does no harm, assists based on needs, and prioritizes respect for human rights, safety and dignity.
IOM will be the Protection focal point lead in nine camps, directly implementing most activities. IOM’s protection monitoring activities will facilitate beneficiaries’ meaningful access to all humanitarian assistance, dignity and human rights. IOM will strengthen community-based protection through engagement, awareness raising, and sensitization; case management and specialized Protection services for individuals in need. IOM’s core counter-trafficking activities - prevention, protection and capacity building - will be implemented in 18 camps and 10 host communities. These activities strive to have sustainable impact and improve peaceful coexistence between communities.
For disaster risk reduction, IOM will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps, and relevant authorities to identify extremely vulnerable individuals or households needing specialized assistance and preparation before a disaster. IOM will verify the needs of EVIs, coordinate services, and support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. IOM will continue leading the Protection Emergency Response Units in nine camps.
IOM’s protection mainstreaming activities will promote a holistic lens, incorporating age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in Cox’s Bazar into programming through capacity building, training, as well as activity monitoring and modification. Finally, IOM will continue to co-chair the Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group, and be a proactive member of the PWG, the Protection Task Team, and other related working groups.

1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya who sought refuge in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh continue to face formidable challenges and need Protection and other life-saving humanitarian assistance, despite having found safe-haven and demonstrating resilience. Among the 889,407 Rohingya population, 80% consists of girls, boys, women, and elderly men and women; 4.3% of individuals were identified with at least one specific need; 1% are considered persons with disabilities according to population data records. The COVID-19 crisis also had significant protection, health and socio-economic implications for Rohingya and host community affected-populations. Data analysis by ACAPS highlighted the impact on vulnerability levels specifically for single female headed households and households without a working age male; households with at least one member aged five and above that require assistance to complete daily activities and large, economically vulnerable families. According to the J-MSNA, food insecurity among the affected-population grew, partly because cash-for-work schemes were paused in the camps and there was less demand for day laborers. The RRRC’s COVID-19 directives limited field presence and prompted remote work processes that made it onerous for refugees to update documentation to access aid. Protection actors reported changes about GBV incidents, particularly those occurring in the home. Boys were reportedly involved in gang-activity, trash collecting, or other forms of labor to earn money because temporary learning centres, child friendly spaces, or religious schools were closed. The COVID-19 reports by ACAPS also emphasized the pressing need to re-establish trust between beneficiaries and the humanitarian community, a trust that fractured when the latter were largely absent from the camps for three months. Reports indicated a need for humanitarian actors to target outreach campaigns to women, girls, PwD and older groups at the sub-block level and with tailored messaging for each group’s communication styles, capacities, and limitations. IOM’s protection monitoring identified changes within the safety and security environment. Petty theft, abduction, extortion, and other crime has been common in the camps. Violent conflict incidents occurred between Rohingya communities, and between Rohingya and host communities. Community/block leaders, who often used extortion, bribery, physical punishment and psychological coercion to obtain goals, grew more influential and controlling of humanitarian services. Criminal networks seemingly carried on without impunity, gaining influence and illicit profits because authorities had prioritized COVID-19 prevention activities.There is also a need to continue counter-trafficking efforts through awareness raising, capacity building of government and humanitarian actors, and victim identification and assistance because of the complicated trafficking dynamics affecting Rohingya and Bangladeshis. According to IOM’s case data, 41% of the caseload included Rohingya women, girls, and boys mainly trafficked for labor exploitation, and 59% of the cases were Bangladeshi men, women, and children trafficked for forced labor. The IOM caseload is most likely not representative of all trends, as very few Rohingya men who are victims of trafficking are represented in the entire caseload and partners report assisting Rohingya women and girls who were trafficked for sexual exploitation. Continued efforts for advocacy, community-based protection activities that promote inclusion, participation, and meaningful access, and measures to mitigate harm are necessary for Rohingya and host communities. Exacerbated by COVID-19, the vulnerabilities and thus the various needs of Bangladeshi and Rohingya men, women, boys and girls require a comprehensive and fortified Protection approach while enhancing dignity and fulfillment of human rights and capacities at the individual, household and community level.

2 - Activities or outputs

-IOM and partners will continue protection monitoring through direct observation, FGDs and KIIs with beneficiaries to collect and analyse data on risks, incidents, and trends in eleven camps. Particular attention will be given to extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs). IOM will analyze primary and secondary data collected by IOM or other partners to detect trends, such as human trafficking, abuse, exploitation and other protection risks that men, women, boys, and girls may experience differently because of their individual or household circumstances. IOM will advocate and liaise with relevant authorities and partners to address Protection issues impacting the affected-population.-IOM and partners will continue strengthening community-based protection mechanisms, using existing facilities, through a network of Rohingya volunteers, by conducting community risk mapping exercises, identifying and assisting EVIs, and engaging with the community to raise awareness on Protection risks and threats. The tailored activities that were adjusted for COVID-19 support beneficiaries to safely identify and mitigate protection concerns and threats, exchange positive strategies for reducing and managing exposure to harm. IOM will engage relevant stakeholders to exchange ideas and address sources of social conflict and promote peaceful coexistence. -IOM will facilitate the identification, referral and follow up for victims of trafficking, EVIs, and households that are particularly vulnerable. The counter-trafficking activities that IOM will conduct to strengthen systems are in line with the national legislation and plan of action, targeting Rohingya and host community members to reinforce the protection response, prevent trafficking through outreach, and capacity building of local authorities and humanitarian actors. IOM will continue technically supporting the government to lead the CT committees throughout the district and serve as the co-chair of the Protection Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group. -IOM will contribute protection technical expertise to the disaster preparedness and emergency response by collaborating with IOM sectors and agencies to consider the unique needs of EVIs, to identify EVIs in need of additional support or relocation because of disaster related risks and damage, and conducting outreach among EVIs groups or vulnerable households to develop and reaffirm individual and household preparedness and response plans, prior to disasters. IOM will serve as the focal point for the Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) for nine camps and as a member of the Rapid Protection Assessment led by the Sector. After the disaster, IOM will work to assist beneficiaries re-establish meaningful access to humanitarian services and will monitor protection trends, including any changes to the human trafficking context, for new or emerging threats and risks and apply mitigation measures.-IOM will continue protection mainstreaming by conducting regular refresher trainings on protection principles and Inclusion techniques for IOM staff, volunteers, and partners to facilitate beneficiaries’ meaningful access to humanitarian services. Regular field visits will assess how well Inclusion measures are incorporated into the design and implementation of activities by IOM sectors. IOM will continue being a key member of the Age and Diversity WG, the Gender Diverse Population WG, and the Gender in Humanitarian Action WG.-IOM will consult daily with the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) desks that are operated by IOM Site Management about Protection related complaints requiring action and promote the use of the CFM desks during community-based protection and other outreach activities. IOM shall continue supporting the PSEA Champions that are based in 18 camps, conduct training for staff and partners about sexual exploitation and abuse and the obligatory SEA reporting system for staff, and serve as the PSEA Network co-chair.
