Project Summary

Given increased risks to the effects of catastrophic droughts, especially in districts located in Matabeleland South and North Provinces, there is urgent need to demonstrate visible progress in the affected areas. The current devastating drought has resulted in a huge livestock mortality in the Southern region hence CRS is proposing the “Replenishing the Immediate Food Access” Project to ensure that communities and their respective local based institutions have the necessary crop and livestock protection, production, and marketing skills and techniques that can be used to reverse the impact of this devastating drought. CRS proposes a series of trainings in addition to the critical inputs of seeds, fodder, and livestock. The trainings will consist of vegetable production (April - May), small grains production (January OR October), fodder production (January for Beitbridge, and October for Binga and Gwanda), goat production (February - March), indigenous chicken production (February - March), and post-harvest handling and storage (April). Vegetable garden implementation will target the most vulnerable affected population with current access to a perennial stream or borehole. In view of the COVID-19 virus pandemic, the project will focus on COVID-19 risk management and infection control through Hygiene promotion and ensuring that field and front-line staff engaged in the project have adequate access to PPE and practice key risk reduction practices like social distancing.
