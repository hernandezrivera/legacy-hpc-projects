Project Summary

The Solidarités International (SI) proposal aims to continue critical water infrastructure works in the Old City of Mosul, and to extend into two areas of high need which have been under-supported in the last few years - the Muhalabiya area of Mosul district and Nimrud Camp. The team have been working closely with key authorities such as the Departments of Water and Sanitation (DoW and DoS) for Mosul, the Director of Muhalabiya and the WaSH Cluster who are coordinating the efforts of WaSH actors. The proposed programme covers a comprehensive WaSH programme, tailored to the specific needs in the three different areas.
In the Muhalabiya sub-district SI proposes to improve the water quality at the household level, which threatens health of the population and for which we are already seeing some water-related illnesses being reported. In line with the allocation strategy, SI will support water quality improvement with the training of community hygiene promoters to promote safe water handling and disease prevention, including cholera prevention. SI will also ensure the provision of water through water trucking in the southern areas of Muhalabiya sub-district for households in acute need where there is no water network connection, and explore options for a sustainable solution for this district, to be proposed in the first allocation of 2019. Additional support for the improvement of the water treatment facilities is planned to complement this activity, in conjunction with local authorities and other NGOs which work with the water station.
SI is proposing to upgrade and maintain the WaSH facilities at Nimrud Camp; improving drainage, sanitation facilities and implementing longer-term solutions for the camp. SI will also provide water trucking to the population, while exploring options to rehabilitate storage solutions and extend water networks to the site. Community-led hygiene promotion activities are being proposed, to include promoting safe water handling and improved water quality through water treatment. Assessments are underway for the planning of activities, which will also consider gender and protection risks of camp settings, and where possible SI will upgrade facilities to make a safer environment for women, boys and girls.

Needs

Muhalabiya:The September 2018 IOM Returns Index reports that the Ninewa Governorate has the largest caseload of returnees living in very high severity conditions. SI understands that the Muhalabiya sub-district is both an area of high and very high severity returns and one that is under-served by NGO or authority support. To better understand this area, SI teams carried out a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) in October 2018. This assessment, along with key informant interviews with the Director for the sub-district of Muhalabiya and the Department of Water, identified WaSH gaps and risks in the current drinking water distribution system and a significant percentage of households with reported water-related health complaints. Respondents to the MSNA also reported shelter and livelihoods as an urgent need. The IOM Returns Index also reports that areas in southern Muhalabiya do not have a functioning public water supply, and only have electricity provision in one-third of assessed areas. In the areas SI evaluated in central and northern Muhalabiya, 97% of MSNA-surveyed households are connected to the water network, however water is only available for four or five days a week for 31% of respondents, and 61% only receive water for three or two days per week. The tanks used by the community are reported not to be sufficient for the high number or people living in each house (with an average of 9 people in each household). A high proportion of households also report health issues which can be related to poor water quality; 42% households report that some family members has suffered of water related disease within the last month (diarrhea or skin disease). The poor quality of the water in that area is related to the low capacity of filtration in the Badoosh’s intake. SI teams received conflicting information on the functionality of markets and planned rehabilitation of water networks and stations, so information gathering and assessments will continue to ensure that the planned activities are addressing the most urgent needs. With the current information, it appears that the most urgent WaSH needs in these areas are (1) household level water quality management in Muhalabiya, (2) provision of water in southern Muhalabiya and (3) support for hygiene promotion across Muhalabiya, in particular as a health risk mitigation. Nimrud Camp:In the Nimrud Camp, identified by SI and the WaSH Cluster as in need of upgraded facilities, is below the international standard of sanitation coverage both in terms of quantity and quality. Nimrud Camp does not have a water network providing water inside the camp, and soap and cleaning supplies are not available to guarantee the necessary level of hygiene inside the latrines and the wash facilities to prevent disease. As a result IDPs resort to washing inside their tents, with consequences in terms of dignity and grey water drainage. This is in particular the case for female camp residents, which can be an indicator of a protection risk in the way the facilities are currently set up, and which can also lead to open defecation as a coping strategy. Seasonal variability also presents a challenge on the drainage of the camp; mixing grey water from the tents (further exacerbated by coping mechanism of washing inside tents), surface water and sludge from the cesspool. There are also some areas of the camp which are prone to flooding. If left unaddressed, this can lead to serious public health problems and lead to the spread of diarrhea and cholera. The camp does not have sufficient drainage solutions in place to cope with this level of water. Due to the elevation of water table and lack of drainage there is a need for regular desludging in the camp, increasing in winter conditions. Cooperation with local authorities is needed in order to improve the treatment of the sludge when it is removed from the camp to prevent contamination of the local environment and consequences for surrounding areas and populations.

Activities

Based on the needs assessment, four categories of activities are proposed to cover WaSH needs in Muhalabiya, Nimrud Camp and Mosul City. Outcome 1: Provide urgent support in WaSH access and support for the sub-district of MuhalabiyaOutcome 2: Identify more durable solution for WaSH needs in the southern areas of the sub-district of MuhalabiyaOutcome 3: Upgrade and support existing WaSH facilities in Nimrud Camp. Outcome 4: Returnees in West Mosul have sustained access to the municipal potable water network Outcome 1 - Muhalabiya(1) Provide temporary water trucking for southern areas of Muhalabiya. This activity is specifically targeting the households facing acute need for water due to lack of connection to the water networks and lack of storage equipment. This activity will be implemented in close partnership with the local authorities responsible for providing water. Additional support for the improvement of water treatment facilities will be planned to complement this activity.(2) For households needing storage facilities, distribution of tanks will be planned.(3) Address water quality issues at the household level. Market assessments are ongoing to identify the most appropriate solution and whether local markets could support a cash or voucher based intervention. (4) Strengthen community based hygiene interventions and conservation practices at household level. Assessments are ongoing to understand the most appropriate intervention to address health risks reported in the MSNA, and to establish/strengthen community level knowledge to mitigate the risk of cholera. Outcome 2 - Muhalabiya(1) Explore rehabilitation and extension of the water network and water treatment capacities in close partnership with local authorities for 2019. This long term intervention planning will be undertaken during the immediate project and further assessment and intervention design for a sustainable solution to the needs identified above. Outcome 3 - Nimrud: (1) Upgrading, decommissioning and/or installation of latrines, water and sanitation facilities(2) Provide drainage solution(s) for the management of grey water from tents(3) Increase the frequency of the desludging to avoid contamination of surface water, underground water and contamination of the environment(4) Improve the state of the current cesspools and install septic tanks as a safer and longer-term solution(5) Provision of potable water through water trucking while exploring options to implement alternative durable solutions. These durable solution will be designed according to the best impact and the cost efficiency. A number of camps (Salamiyah 1 to 2 and Nimrud) are dependent on the same sources of water so coordination with local authorities is key to designing and implementing a sustainable water network to phase out from water trucking and improve access to safe drinking water(6) Hygiene kit distributions to take place for the population in the camp(7) Community-led hygiene promotion activities implemented, to include promoting safe water handling and improved water quality through water treatment. Also, regular quality testing water, monitoring and reporting of water quality results will be put in place, in particular during the phasing out from water trucking.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Accountability at Solidarites International (SI) means listening to the needs, concerns and views of all stakeholders and in particular the communities that SI supports. This means ensuring that communities participate in the design and implementation planning, that populations are able to communicate with the teams, that teams act on what populations are saying and that SI is accountable to populations for the decisions that SI makes. On a practical level for this project, SI teams met with communities while conducting the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) which was designed to understand the needs and concerns of the community in general, so we can highlight broad areas of need. SI establishes complaint mechanisms for all projects and commits to ensuring that these are accessible for all populations, considering community dynamics and literacy skills, and this will be put into place for the new project areas. SI seeks feedback on projects pro-actively and ensures that all feedback is followed up in a timely manner, and ensures that there is a gender balance in the implementing team so SI teams can engage meaningfully with women and children. SI also has an independent whistle blowing policy directly linked to SI’s CEO to make sure beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders are able to voice their concerns and complaints about SI activities and receive timely feedback.
