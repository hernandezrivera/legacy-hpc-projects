Project Summary

Project overview:
In the first phase, OCARD pilot project demonstrated progressive results in terms of improved soil quality and condition, and the effectiveness of employing organic compost and natural pest control in vegetable cultivation. This was measured by the improvement in harvest quality and quantity. Additionally, OCARD I provided water-saving irrigation equipment (drip-irrigation) and promoted biodiversity.
MRD developed the proposed project in response to the recommendations and requests received from a significant number of local farmers from various nearby areas to expand the project's to their locations. To scale up the project to include new locations and increase the number of beneficiaries, the project targets in this phase farmers, plant nursery operators, and agro-pastoralists. It aims at linking these groups in an integrated network where the output of one part is used as an input for another part of the system raising its overall efficiency and creating an informal community of practice to disseminate the acquired knowledge among further interested farmers.
The project will also include the implementation of 2 community-based initiatives.
Project objective:
In an effort to support the economic resilience of farmer communities in the western and eastern countryside of Deir-ez-Zor, MRD proposes a collective approach that supports actors in the agriculture sector in the region in finding and applying alternative and environmentally friendly production methods creating a more stabilized farming system overcoming limited resources.
Enabling farmers to produce and apply organic fertilizers by themselves not only has a beneficial impact on soil health and productivity, especially combined with other sustainable practices, but it also increases their economic independence from imports and rising market prices for chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and herbicides.

Project description:
Beneficiaries:
Direct beneficiaries: 780 disaggregated into (males, females, IDPs, and residents) with 40% minimum women participation.
Indirect beneficiaries: 3900 families
The project targets 3 groups of beneficiaries. Each group includes 2 sub-groups (primary and secondary beneficiaries to serve as knowledge carriers and project impact multipliers ensuring the widespread and adoption of organic farming principles across the agriculture community).

Beneficiaries are categorized into:
Plant nurseries owners: 20 nursery owners (2 donums /BN)
Argo-pastoralists: 130 BN (3 donums/BN)
Farmers (vegetables growers): 560 farmers (3 donums/BN)
Protection, Gender mainstreaming and PSEAH consideration
Gender participation: the project is designed to increase the participation of women in agriculture with the aim of achieving a 40% female beneficiaries’ ratio. To track the progress made in promoting gender equality, we will collect sex-disaggregated data, enabling us to assess the effectiveness of our gender-related procedures, capture valuable lessons, and adapt our approaches to meet any identified gaps.
Gender-responsive project implementation: will be integral to the project implementation phases including the provision of technical support and follow-up to beneficiaries. Workshops and, training, and one-to-one coaching will be scheduled and located to accommodate female participants, and transportation will be provided as needed. Moreover, whenever feasible, these sessions will be co-conducted by female facilitators, fostering an environment of inclusivity and representation.
PSEAH consideration
MRD continuously implements PSEA policy for employees, clarifying procedures in case of SEA. In addition to mitigation strategies that are in place to address risks such as gender-based violence, including implementing PSEA policies, staff orientation, beneficiaries' verification, and providing specialized response services.
