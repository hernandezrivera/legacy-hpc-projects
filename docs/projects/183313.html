Project Summary

Children and youth who are at risk of drop-out, and out of school, are targeted in this project through giving them access to safe and quality learning environments. This will create leeway for innovation as the issues to be addressed are oftentimes complex and require a combination of various approaches and considerations.
Access to education will be mainly addressed through reducing the financial burdens of enrollment; this will include, among others, provision of learning materials and low cost connectivity solutions.
The project will target 3,500 children and youth in Beirut, Bekaa, Hermel, and North. The project will entail information sharing and awareness raising for caregivers while also supporting them with necessary tools and materials to support their children accessing schools and/or learning from home.
The project will include components of sensitization of caregivers on cyber risks and internet safety protocols in general.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

I) Out of Education Children identified and referred:RTP will work with its partners to identify children who are out of learning through community mapping activities. Identified children will be supported and referred to necessary services as deemed relevant.II) Awareness raising sessions: Awareness raising sessions including information sharing around possible Education gateways will be conducted entailing focus on the importance of education.III) Vulnerable Lebanese provided emergency access to low-cost solutions at home:This will be mainly done through distribution of connectivity devices and tablets and where possible, providing internet subsidies.V) Children & youth received age-appropriate and relevant learning materials:This will be done through the provision of learning materials including stationery, bags, and play items.VI) Caregivers are sensitized on internet safety:RTP will utilize its Internet Safety curriculum geared towards caregivers to enhance their knowledge around protection and cyber risks that their children would be exposed to while online.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

All staff at Right To Play receive Child Safeguarding training at the time of on boarding and undertake a refresher training each year. Additionally, Child Safeguarding training for Country, HQ and National staff is scheduled and conducted at least every two years. Safeguarding training is provided to RTP staff during onboarding and staff undertake refresher trainings each year. As outlined in Right To Play’s Child Safeguarding Policy, children and parents/guardians are informed of the Child Safeguarding policies and procedures, and means of contacting Right To Play directly for concerns about violence against children, abuse and/or exploitation. Throughout the life of the project, all involved stakeholders including partners will receive trainings and refreshers on Child Safeguarding. Additionally, all RTP resources are gender sensitive and takes into consideration different capacities and abilities.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RTP provides children with information on where to go locally for confidential help and advice in relation to violence, abuse, exploitation, harassment and bullying. Throughout the program, quarterly Focus Group Discussions will also be held with girls and boys participating in the program to understand their experiences of the program and support in identifying any concerns. Additionally, RTP will utilize it's referral mechanisms under the umbrella of the inter-agency and will ensure timely referrals and follow-up as need be.
