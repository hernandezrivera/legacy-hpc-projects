Project Summary

The project aims at providing safe and dignified living conditions for 24.080 Rohingya refugees through the establishment, improvement and maintenance of site infrastructure. Caritas Bangladesh intends to scale up actions on infrastructural development and maintenance, with specific focuses on disaster risk reduction, durability, safety, and community participation. The project will ensure coordination with all stakeholders, participatory community-level mapping and prioritization of interventions as well as the establishment, improvement and maintenance of community scale infrastructure works, including but not limited to bamboo bridge, retention wall, stepping, drainage, slope protection and stabilization, pathways, etc.

Needs

"Refugees are residing in makeshift settlements that are severely below basic humanitarian standards, overcrowded, and will not withstand seasonal climatic conditions. The monsoon has already severely damaged many facilities and infrastructure that were mostly built with bamboo, sand bags and rope. In addition, bamboo has also deteriorated due to beetle infestation, with many installations of extremely poor quality that now require a second investment to upgrade. Women and girls consist of 52% of the Rohingya population in Bangladesh, and 55% are under 18. Women, girls, and boys are disproportionately affected by risks within the camps including safe site accessibility. Mobility restrictions due to lack or poor quality site improvement are of particular a concern of protection, especially for women, children and vulnerable groups. In addition, lack of lighting is one of the main reasons why women are not comfortable going out at night or using showers and latrines. Some cash-for-work activities are available to men, but women and vulnerable groups have even fewer options due to cultural restrictions of working outside their home."

Activities or outputs

"Capitalizing on the activities carried out in multiple camps, Caritas Bangladesh intends to scale up actions on infrastructural development and maintenance, with specific focuses on disaster risk reduction, durability and safety, and community participation. The project will ensure having participatory process with communities to prioritize their needs, specifically ensuring meaningful inclusion of women and vulnerable groups in the project activities and in decision making. Female and male members of the family will participate in outlining the assessment, mapping, design, construction/repair and maintenance of community level site infrastructures. Segregated training and messaging for women regarding DRR and household level site improvement activities will be provided, and site improvement and site development activities will be designed to help promote gender equality and a safe environment for all refugees. Increased infrastructure across the camps will also facilitate linkages and connectivity among camps and households, strengthen the interactions and build community.Communities are at the very core of the programming, and central to this is ensuring that males and females of different ages are able to feedback and influence the project. The project will closely monitor implementation activities through gender and age disaggregated PDM, community meetings and FGDs. Feedback and complaints mechanisms will be established to ensure beneficiaries the right to express their concerns, particularly for the most vulnerable groups. Caritas has conducted assessments with men and women to understand their preferred methods for giving feedback and complaints, and set up the mechanisms accordingly.Project activities include: 1.Coordination with national authorities, sector coordinators and area coordination agencies to identify and address priority gaps and needs in assistance, ensuring the avoidance of duplication 2. Coordination with CiCs, site management agencies and communities 3.Participatory community-level mapping and prioritization of interventions, where not conducted by other actors 4.Establishment, improvement and maintenance of community scale infrastructure works, including but not limited to bamboo bridge, retention wall, stepping, drainage, slope protection and stabilization , pathway, etc. 5.Installation and maintenance of solar streetlights will contribute to ensure safety, especially for women, children and vulnerable groups. Provision of solar streetlights will be coordinated with ADB and World Bank projects to avoid duplications, and minimized to cover the limited needs.Livelihood opportunities for both men and women will be generated through cash for work. As it may be culturally challenging for women to be present in construction sites, some lighter building components (i.e bamboo mats) will be outsourced to them. "
