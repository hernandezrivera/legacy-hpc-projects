Project Summary

The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” offers a project to support undifferentiated activities of private households for the production of goods and services for their own consumption and livelihoods. The project is aimed at solving the problem of stimulating and supporting the development of personal subsidiary plots and helping the most vulnerable category of beneficiaries to create their own source of livelihood.
The main objective of the project is to provide support to IDPs and their host communities in the Sudan in ordersevere food insecurity and saving the lives of highly vulnerable populations, victims of conflicts and natural disasters.
The overall objective of the project is to restore food security and access to livelihoods for displaced populations along with vulnerable host communities through emergency agriculture support, emergency employment, income generation, and enhanced conflict prevention and mediation among the most vulnerable populations. Agricultural assistance to the population and increased access to food security and diversification of livelihoods will strengthen the self-reliance of both returnees and members of the host community, as well as foster lasting solutions for the displaced population, thereby ensuring sustainable peace and stability in targeted areas.
The project will focus on building the capacity of farmers to have access to basic farm inputs such as seeds / tools, fertilizers, livestock restocking, timely vaccinations and the provision of health kits. Farmers will also be trained to equip them with the knowledge they need to increase production, which not only can cover family consumption, but will enable them to sell surplus produce. Post-harvest management: Beneficiaries will be trained in better post-harvest management systems, as losses can be as high as 30% under normal management. Farmers will be able to sell their produce at a better price by improving post-harvest management, as well as raise awareness of living together among farmers and pastoralists, and be able to exchange goods by creating a common market.
As a result of the project activities, the standard of living of returnees and host communities will increase, social cohesion will be strengthened, contributing to peaceful coexistence and providing IDPs and returnees with a stable environment, providing them with the resources and necessary professional training, learning new skills to ensure their livelihood.

1 - Needs

The Sudan Humanitarian Needs Survey 2021 states that 13.4 million people (29 percent of the total population) are in need of humanitarian assistance.According to the Humanitarian Response Plan, Darfur is one of the priority locations for humanitarian impact. An estimated 2.55 million IDPs are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection support both in and outside camps in host communities. In these difficult conditions IDPs struggle to meet their basic needs and, with limited access to the labor market, remain highly dependent on humanitarian assistance. More than two thirds of the population lives in rural areas, where the poverty rate is 58% compared to the national average of 47%. Agriculture, including livestock and fishing, is the main sector of the Sudan economy and provides livelihoods for about two-thirds of the working-age population. According to the WFP, the average price of a local food basket is up nearly 200 percent since 2019. Inflation drives up the prices of basic foodstuffs. Food insecurity is particularly high among in Darfur. In order to avoid irreversible consequences, it is necessary to provide assistance to the affected population. In the Sudan, the vulnerability of households and individuals whose livelihood is based on agriculture is directly related to the availability of seed, the availability and condition of livestock. The persistent shortage of hard currency and frequent fluctuations in the Sudanese pound are contributing to increased costs of agricultural inputs. As hard currency shortages persist, prices will remain high, access to food will remain low, and farmers may not be able to procure inputs for the next planting season. According to FAO’s report of 2021, livestock (cattle, sheep, goats, camels and poultry) in the Sudan plays a vital, multidimensional role in the economy and in the lives of the rural population. Livestock is a key asset, serving as a source of food and nutritional security (providing milk, meat and eggs), a source of livelihoods, a buffer against crop failures and disasters, a safety net for social stability, a means of social solidarity, a means of access to power and authority, and a source of finance for crop farming where official credit is inaccessible. Moreover, livestock is a major means of transport and draught power to support crop production and processing, and a source of fertilizer and fuel. Raising livestock is also a significant field of employment.South Darfur is the largest state in greater Darfur. It has one of the highest population densities in the country, with 5 million people living in the state. Armed conflict plagued Darfur for nearly 20 years, causing high levels of forced displacement from rural to urban areas. Agriculture and animal husbandry suffered the most as a result of the conflict.According to Country Gender Assessment Of The Agriculture And Rural Sector done by FAO in 2021, access to resources has always been one of the major barriers to women’s empowerment in the Sudan. The issue is more important than ever given the current context in which growing numbers of women in rural villages and in situations of displacement are assuming the role of head of household, because of the absence of men due to economic migration or conflict, without having the necessary access to basic resources, such as land, information and technology.The circumstances determining access to agricultural services are related those that determine access to fTraditional rainfed farming is the dominant farming system, covering 60 percent of the total cultivated land and employing about 65 percent the Sudanese agricultural labour force (Ministry of Environment and Physical Development, 2014), with women constituting 17 percent of that labour force (Ahmed et al., 2012).Thus, in the current crisis, there is an urgent need to support agricultural production systems

2 - Activities/Outputs

Within the framework of the project, HADC will strive to implement an integrated approach to the implementation of the project, creating a link between programs at all stages of activity, which will contribute to ensuring awareness, safety, protection of rights, and access to social guarantees.The project is aimed at improving the quality of life of beneficiaries living in private households and having (leading) personal subsidiary plots by issuing humanitarian aid in kind: provision of seeds, agricultural implements, young farm animals, feed, veterinary drugs. Also, a series of training sessions for farmers and veterinarians will be held to raise awareness and acquire the necessary skills in housekeeping and vaccination.For the high-quality implementation of the project and achievement of the goal, the HADC plans to perform the following actions:1. In collaboration with local community leaders and in coordination with donors and local stakeholders, identify beneficiaries.2. Conduct verification and initial assessment of potential beneficiaries for compliance with the vulnerability criteria.3. Prepare information for the issuance of agricultural products.4. Make the purchase of humanitarian aid.5. Make the issuance of humanitarian aid.6. Conduct educational trainings for farmers and veterinarians.7. Conduct a post-monitoring assessment.During the implementation of the project activities, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. For a clear understanding of how the program is being implemented and the feasibility of providing this type of assistance, all project activities will be monitored and evaluated throughout the implementation period. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision. For this, communicative approaches will be used, assuming a transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information on the risks, needs and opportunities of communities will be collected. Project activities are designed in such a way that IDPs, returnees and host communities can quickly benefit from the assistance provided to continue to prevent and resolve any security issues beyond the end of the project.The project impact analysis will be used to formulate the policy and make further decisions on the mode of intervention based on the needs and requests of the beneficiaries.During the implementation of the project, the following goals are achieved:1. 1000 households are provided with various seeds.2. 1000 households are provided with agricultural implements (plow, hoe, etc.).3. Livestock vaccination services for 1000 households are provided.4. A small number of goats (5) are provided for 200 vulnerable female-headed households, with the necessary skills to improve skills in the field of animal husbandry, necessary for the success of the intervention.5. 400 households are provided with 800 head of pedigree cattle (male and female) for further breeding and providing local communities with their own livestock. The 100 most vulnerable families will be identified (for example, large families with low income).6. 400 households are provided with animal feed.7. 700 farmers are trained in basic agronomic techniques and post-harvest handling8. 10 trainings are conducted for 200 community veterinary workers with the provision of vaccination and treatment kits and drugs to reduce animal losses (animal pest and disease control, animal vaccination and treatment) and food conservation methods.10. 30 trainings are conducted for 600 beneficiaries to raise awareness of living together among farmers and pastoralists and exchange of goods of their own production by creating a common market.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” understands that communities living in countries affected by conflict are more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change. Armed conflict destroys infrastructure, weakens institutions, impedes access to basic services and erodes social cohesion - all critical to human resilience and adaptation to climate change. Communities are on a survival rope in the face of these complex crises. Given that the protection of civilians remains the highest priority in the implementation of this project, it also takes into account environmental lenses that will contribute to the effective management of natural resources, which, among other things, contribute to the restoration of basic livelihoods. In the implementation of this project, the Foundation operates with one of the humanitarian principles – "Do no harm", which is part of the concept of reducing the impact on the environment.Thus, the training programs will include mandatory items that train the project beneficiaries in waste management, waste separation and disposal, training in cleaning their local area, careful attitude to natural resources (plants, water, land). When training beneficiaries on basic agronomic techniques and post-harvest handling, the aspect of fertilizers handling and disposal will certainly be included, and when training pastoralists and farmers, using manure as fertilizer and further possibility of its exchange or sale will be included as well. In the training centers, to train opinion leaders, a separate course on environmental protection will be developed and conducted, which includes hygienic aspects, cleaning and separation of garbage, careful handling of nature, water resources, etc.In the context of climate change, the carbon cost of international deployment will also be taken into account when defining the workforce. The project will employ mainly local staff, with a limited presence of international staff. Local staff will be trained in the principles of environmental protection. Also during the project, the staff of the Foundation will conduct a systematic analysis of the cost-effectiveness of the impact of this humanitarian operation on the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

People affected by conflicts, riots and natural disasters are almost always exposed to high risks of violence (physical, psychological and sexual), rights violations, coercion and exploitation. Individual and community protection capacities, as well as national protection mechanisms, are often compromised or insufficient to cope with the protection risks posed by ever-emerging threats. Thus, the situation in the field of protection will be taken into account separately, including when interacting with stakeholders and government officials. When developing and implementing the project, the location of the facilities where the project is being implemented will be taken into account, the distance of routes from real or potential threats, such as: violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence and attacks by armed groups.Protection risks and violations will be determined by characteristics such as minority status, gender, sexual orientation, age and other diversity factors. Thus, the central role of protection in this project is, together with the communities, to identify groups at high risk in the field of protection, to find out the types and causes of such risk, and also to analyze these features, taking into account external factors that affect the protection mechanism itself. In addition, this approach involves identifying specific weaknesses that give rise to such risks, taking into account the unique experiences of men, women, girls and boys, such groups as the elderly, people with disabilities, LGBT and other groups.A wide range of representatives of the local community will have the opportunity to participate in the decision-making process in an inclusive and non-discriminatory manner that has a real impact on the provision of a humanitarian response. The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” in this direction will use communicative approaches, assuming the presence of clear and direct feedback between the staff who develop and control the program, and the communities themselves. The project also plans to improve protection by differentiating the risks (needs) of the local community, which will allow for more targeted assistance. This will help, among other things, identify the underlying drivers of conflict and thereby improve the protection of the local community, while avoiding the harm that humanitarian action could otherwise do.
