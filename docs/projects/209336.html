Project Summary

Prior to the conflict, Sudan was one of the main hosting countries in the African continent with almost 1.2 million refugees, among whom almost 71,000 Ethiopians. Despite the fighting in the country, Ethiopian refugees have continued to arrive in Camp 6, Blue Nile, where Acted has been present since 2023. Before April 2023, the camp population was of 9,016 Ethiopian refugees. As of December 2023, 1,345 individuals have come to Camp 6, bringing the total of Ethiopian residing in the camp to 10,361 with 55% children, 24% adult women and 21% adult men. Since 2023, Acted is leading an emergency multi-sectoral response in Camp 6, Blue Nile state. In 2024, Acted will continue to intervene in three sectors: Protection, SNFI and WASH. As the leading Protection agency in the camp, Acted will continue to support the established community-based protection networks (CBPNs) in the camp, and will aim at strengthening further the created referral pathways for identified at-risk GBV survivors and children. In addition, individual case management will be provided to children and GBV survivors in need, comprising individual protection assistance (IPA) as well as psychosocial sessions (PSS). In Camp 6, Acted will also ensure the maintenance of the child and youth safe space and offer weekly recreational activities for children and youth. Acted will organise awareness-raising sessions for all camp residents to sensitise to protection issues. In Camp 6, Acted will also provide assistance to the newly arrived refugees through distribution of hot meals and SNFI assistance. More specifically, Acted will provide SNFI assistance to the newly arrived refugees to the camp. Acted will distribute NFIs to new households, including among other things jerry cans, plastic sheets and mosquito nets. Shelter kits will also be given to the newly arrived families to facilitate their installation in Camp 6. To address the shelter needs in the camp, Acted will establish Shelter Committees to facilitate feedback from the camp residents on their needs, which will enable to provide timely assistance, tailored to the identified needs of the households in the camp. In addition, to Protection and SNFI aid, Acted will also conduct crucial WASH interventions to improve access to water, sanitation and hygiene for camp residents. Acted will provide daily access to water by water-trucking. With Water Committees, composed of camp residents, Acted will test the water quality every day. Vector control activities will also be undertaken to mitigate against the spread of water-borne diseases. Water distribution will be improved through rehabilitation of pipelines and construction of additional ones which will ensure all camp residents are served. Acted will also coordinate the solid waste management system by ensuring regular disposal of waste and waste collecting campaigns with camp residents. To ensure optimum sanitation and hygiene practices in the camp, Acted will maintain the latrines and showers, while distributing soap, hygiene and dignity kits every month. Complementary to this action, Acted will lead hygiene promotion campaigns, delivering key messages on best hygiene practices and how to mitigate the spread of diseases.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups and encourages a gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedded in the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase: - Before and during the intervention, Acted will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on the project’s scope and activities and informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (e.g. single women, PLWs, children, the elderly, PwDs). Program Implementation Phase: - Acted mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during community sensitisation and trainings. - Waiting time and safety during traveling will be addressed through adhoc protection measures. - First aid kits and protective wear are available on distribution sites. Accountability: - Acted has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Besides its staff deployed in the field daily, Acted will also conduct regular spot checks. Acted will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposed intervention if needed, and inform future projects’ design.Human resources: - Acted has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. Acted also complies with The Code of Conduct (CoC) of The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and UNHCR’s CoC. Acted’s CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to coordination through a standardized communication pathway and actions would be taken by the appropriate levels of coordination. - For the intervention, Acted will set up a gender-balanced implementation team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusion mechanisms. In all activities, Acted will promote an equal gender participation.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Acted has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Besides its staff deployed in the field daily, Acted will also conduct regular spot checks. Acted will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposed intervention if needed, and inform future projects’ design. Acted will also ensure community leaders are present during key decision-making process to include the feedback and needs of community members which will influence the activity implementation.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, Acted will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly through its CRM. Acted has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment: - Acted prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by Acted already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. Administration and employee cycle: - Signature of Code of Conductfor staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected - Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. - Training program related to safeguarding issues. - Acted has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7,000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. ii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. Acted Complaints and response mechanisms. - Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures -Acted has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (Acted SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; Acted Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). Acted must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. Acted also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (Acted SEA policy, article 23). Acted has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with Acted's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
