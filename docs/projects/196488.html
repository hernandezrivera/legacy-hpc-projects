Project Summary

Over ten years of the protracted crisis in Syria has created and exacerbated large-scale humanitarian needs around water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) for the country’s population.
The project will work to meet the needs of targeted families in Aleppo, Syria (A'zaz and Al-Bab districts) from the WASH assistance
ONUSR interventions aims to reduce the impact of Cholera on the NW Syria and provide sessions to increase to awareness of IDPs’ informal settlement through four component as follow:
1. Reduce morbidity and mortality associated with an increase in WASH-related diseases and environmental health risks resulting from shocks or displacement.
2. Improve access to WaSH assistance: by Construct of Latrines, Construction of 40 blocks of 2 latrines, 1 for male and 1 for female, (total 80 latrines) for informal settlements with 80 tanks, while sustainable solutions will be prioritized where feasible in the future through provide more sustainable constructions.
3. Conduction of hygiene promotion sessions: Through Provide regular physically session to targeted beneficiaries to raise the importance of (personal hygiene, How to use the sanitation materials, How to keep the water clean and safe) to protected beneficiaries form Cholera and any other disease.
4. Hygiene kits distribution: distribute a hygiene kites to the targeted comps every three months.
Whereas durable and cost-efficient interventions include operation and maintenance of water supply infrastructure in urban communities. Sanitation activities seek addressing key areas of Cholera risks stemming from arbitrary establishment of informal settlements, which necessitates increase of communal latrines’ capacity and improvement in drainage conditions. Lastly, aiming to reduce and prevent disease transmission through enabling people to practice key hygiene behaviors, distribution of hygiene items and solid waste management will be provided.
Insufficient quantities of potable water, sanitation services and supplies for improving personal hygiene, will be met through WASH activities, in addition to the improvement of public health that will reduce the diseases related to it.
Through this intervention ONSUR will target a total of 2031 HHs living in Azaz and Al Bab districts located in Aleppo governorate.
The project’s primary target groups are internally displaced persons (IDPs). Additionally, the project will target local host populations due to the fact that they live mixed with the primary target group and have similar needs.
Both IDPs and local populations in the target area are suffering from different types vulnerabilities. During the rapid needs assessment (RNA) conducted for this project the following vulnerability categories and vulnerable groups:
• Female-headed households.
• Elderly-headed households.
• Households with disabled members and/or heads.
• Households with chronically ill members and/or heads.
• Households with three or more children under the age of five

3 - Needs

In 2022, 4.1 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in the Northern West of Syria 2.9 M in Idleb, and 1.2 in Northern Aleppo. More recently, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have increasingly become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. 2.8 million IDPs live in last-resort sites in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates in Northwest Syria, of which 77 percent are women and children. With the continuation of war operations, the humanitarian situation in Northwest Syria continues to deteriorate, impacting all life aspects of the Syrian families and further increasing their struggles to meet their basic needs.Currently, the first cholera case has been confirmed in northwest Syria and the number of cases is still increasing. Furthermore, the hygiene situation, in general, became further worsened the situation let to a huge number of water-borne and vector-borne diseases in the project's subdistricts in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. So far, in 2022, about 321,849 water-borne diseases, 22,374 cases of leishmaniasis, and cholera diseases have been reported in the project's sub-districts.According to 2022 HNO, the proposed project sub district rank 4 on a final severity scale.Also, 24.4% of functioning water stations in NWS were working without Chlorination according to ACU dashboard in August 2022.

Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up. Lessons from previous experience are shared and learned.Monitoring:Liaison with Protection sectorEstablish check list for agencies to field monitoring, Bathing spaces are installed inappropriately and culturally insensitively Mitigating Measure: Consult/engage community regarding desludging services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Establish clear routes and schedule for desludging trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training (include operational safety/PPE) with truck drivers/operators;Monitoring: Establish feedback& complaint mechanism that allows reporting on issues with desludging services and follow-up on reported issues; Regularly monitor trucks routes and schedule

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Insufficient attention given to ensure inclusion of households with especially vulnerable members (gender, age, disability) Onsur teams, which include specialists in protection, canvas IDP sites to ensure households with especially vulnerable members are included when considering local access to facilities and services. Lack of access to support and protection undermines programming effectivenessDuring the selection process, the need for and availability of additional support including referrals to protection services, assured access for particularly vulnerable households, and the safety and security of service provision and permanent facilities will be addressed. At that time information on the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) approach described in the accompanying documentation submitted as part of this proposal will also be provided. In addition, Bahar field staff will be visiting households and monitoring need.Onsur will have two gender-balanced protection mainstreaming teams to provide GBV awareness raising sessions for both males and females, share information on available protection services at sub district level, identify and refer GBV and CP cases to service providers in the target locations.
