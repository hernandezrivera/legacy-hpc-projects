Project Summary

The response is to support conflicted and flood affected populations in 5 counties of NCA areas of operations which also constitute areas of high priority in the 2021 HRP for South Sudan and aims to provide support for basic WASH service provision, including COVID 19 prevention and mitigation support. This is a 9-month project that will have a strong component to support and mainstream GBV, AAP and disability inclusion into our programmes.
The project is focussed on supporting communities that have been affected by floods, intercommunal violence including displaced and host communities whose access to safe water has been constrained resulting in large populations having high prevalence for WASH related diseases high rates of malnutrition which is compounded by poor wash service and designed to have a big proportion of Cash based interventions modality. The project will mostly be focussed on improvement of access to safe water through drilling of new water points, repair and rehabilitation of existing ones, mainstreaming COVID 19 preparedness and prevention initiatives, hygiene awareness and promotion. Some of the key sectoral and cluster objectives includes cluster objectives 2, Cluster objective 3, 4, 5 and 6. The project will reach a total of 96,000 people with a budget of 713, 320 USDs.
NCA will be directly responsible for the implement this project directly but will work through local partners and is also designed to promote the localisation of aid as well as capacity building of local entities. NCA being an international ecumenical actor has commitments under the grand bargain to support local partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to a high quality and degree and this project will also ensure resilience of local communities and CBOs in WASH service provision by having components that will promote resilience through training water committees, supporting local government actors and line ministries in WASH service provision.

1 - Needs

Assessments carried out in Pibor, Tonj North, Warrap, Aweil central, Jur River and Baggari Corridor of Wau County between December 2020 and February 2021 indicate high levels of needs for Emergency WASH support services in all these locations all of which have intersecting multisectoral needs due to a combination of factors. These areas also correspond with some of the counties on the high priority list as outlined in the 2021 HRP and WASH cluster. In Baggari Corridor, for instance, assessment findings carried out by NCA in January and February 2021, shows that many villages are experiencing large numbers of returnees which also consequently places WASH services under severe strain. The cessation of hostilities and prevailing calm that has enabled many families that had been IDPS in Wau town to return. The low number of operational water points coupled with low capacity for maintaining the existing facilities means that there is overcrowding which further exacerbates the challenges of maintaining social distancing for COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures. Additionally, access to water for basic needs is severely constrained. Jur River and Warrap North are receiving large numbers of internally displaced people fleeing the conflict in neighbouring Tonj North and Tonj East. Many communities fleeing conflict in Wardalelo have sought refuge in a number of locations in Jur River where local host communities have welcomed and sheltered them, Access to safe water in Barwol , Kuajieno , RocRoch Dong is poor Increased populations due to new arrivals is causing a strain on the limited water points. COVID 19 pandemic is a real concern and crowing around water points and other public places is a big challenge, there is need to educate communities on Covid 19 prevention and awareness including hand washing. this will need that access to water is made possible as well as provision of hand washing facilities. The dry seasonal migrations from the largely cattle keeping communities into Jur River is further expected to cause seasonal displacement due to the recurrent conflict between cattle keepers and farmers in the area. Coupled with the displacements and volatile pollical climate in the area, the potential for a crisis that will cause further strain in meagre water resources is real. Children are particularly affected with an increased number of children suffering from diarrhoea diseases increasing their vulnerability to compounding effect of under-nutrition. In Aweil central, the flooding in mid to late 2020 along the Lol State and surrounding areas has affected large tracts of land and displaced communities and inundated water points and farms. The harvesting season will see a decline in food resulting in malnutrition in the area. Lack of access to safe water is a key driver that will result in infant and under 5 mortality and morbidity if not addressed. There is need to rehabilitate flood damaged water points, increase and expand access to safe water and help improve safe sanitation and hygiene awareness to reduce incidences of water borne and water related diseases. In Pibor and Grater Kubal area, flooding and intercommunal violence in mid-2020 caused a large displacement of populations towards Pibor Town. This coupled with the worst flooding in 30 years in the area has also disrupted farming and destroyed crops. The fighting between communities from Bor , Upper Nile and Pibor area also resulted in WASH infrastructure to be targeted and destroyed . over 3/4 s of the functioning water facilities in the area were destroyed during the invasion of greater Kubal . The only functioning water facility in Gumuruk that was developed and run by NCA was vandalised and the communities in the area now have no access to the only source of water for over 40,000 people in the area

2 - Project activities

NCA and partners will provide timely and equitable and access to safe water through drilling of new water points and repair and rehabilitation of existing ones in areas where IDPs are settled in order to increase the number of water points and thus improve ease of access to safe water. We will also help communities to improve on sanitation improvement by developing new latrines at the household level through vouchers , ensure that our WASH programmes have gender and AAP mainstreaming This will be achieved by carrying out a variety of activities not limited to the following;- - Carrying out assessments of needs and consultations with right holders - the training of water pump mechanics, - training water committees- drilling new water points - Monitoring and assessing progress of - Installation of handwashing facilities for Covid 19 mitigation- Supplying of spare parts for repair and maintenance of water points - Markets assessments - Carry out GBV safety audits. - Repair water points - Following up with service providers and suppliers to redeem vouchers. - Distribution of vouchers for purchase of tools for sanitation - Distribution of vouchers for purchase of spare parts for handpump repairs - Consultation with stakeholders on WASH needs.- Setting up Complaints response systems for community feedback - Training of hygiene promoters - Develop training materials for COVID 19 information on prevention. - Gender assessment - Awareness raising on appropriate Hygiene practices at household level. - Identify hygiene promotion volunteers. - Monitor sanitation improvement. - Identify people with special needs and provide targeted support. - Carrying out Knowledge awareness and practices Surveys- Water point inventories to identify water points needing repairs.- Carrying out water committee’s formation - Designing and Printing Vouchers - Identification of vulnerable populations and target groups - Carrying out monitoring of progress of implementation - Participating in Coordination mechanisms - Carrying out assessments of needs - Providing information and getting feedback from right holders and attending to complaints

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

At the design stage, we considered issues of access particularly in wet season where costs of transportation of humanitarian materials becomes difficult due to bad roads. We considered the challenges related to costs for transport as well as costs and risks associated with in kind donations and also its effects and impact on local businesses. We considered the existing markets and their ability to supply some of the goods that we transport from big town e.g., Juba and Wau. We considered the importance of promoting local bussineses , the importance of not disrupting the function of markets by introducing external goods which are already available from the markets , We considered the need for sustainable access to WASH services as well as the need to build resilience of communities and markets as well as issues and factors for safety, market supplies and costs. We considered that markets get disrupted by road access constraint but also considered that local businesses are more resilient and more adaptive to shocks. We considered the impact of the economic crisis and how humanitarian responses can also play a role in supporting markets for delivering goods.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In Gumuruk one of the locations where we are planning to implement our WASH programme, has a vibrant local market which has a variety of products including WASH supplies. We have carried out a preliminary cash and markets survey and it has shown a viability of using vouchers for targeted beneficiaries to receive WASH goods through vouchers. We have established a safe mechanism for payment of traders through the existing local Money transfer agents which would enable us to transfer cash safely after verifications. Discussions with Women and Girls has shown that there are no barriers to receiving hygiene materials from shops and that cash vouchers are generally safe. The distances and routes for right holders’ access to markets has undergone and In order to ensure a more resilient way to access sanitation materials as well as access to soap and other sanitary products, we have determined that a cash and voucher system is most ideal in this area. Traders are happy to assure of a steady resupply of goods based on market demand and have a price range that does not fluctuate beyond a certain threshold. In Jur River, we have similarly carried out an assessment and analysis of needs, including function of markets survey as well as a protection survey with right holders and we have determined that it is possible to provide soap, sanitary materials, hand washing facilities from local traders. The existence of money transfer services and accessibility of the area for monitoring shows that we would provide better services and ensure sustainability of interventions by using cash and vouchers. Overall , we have determined that using cash and vouchers will give right holders better value and choices and that we will be able to deliver our responses more quickly , with better value and more appropriately compared to in-kind assistance

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Staff will be made aware about their expected conduct while working at the community level. Any breaches to the code of conduct will be addressed in the manner prescribed in the HR and employment terms. Community members their leaders will be informed about their right to complain if any member of the staff or of partner staffs does not uphold their values, breach any policies according to the expectations of the community and according to the employment guidelines and policies. All NCA employees are requested to report allegations, concerns and suspicion of breaches related to abuse, exploitation and fraud and corruption. Deliberate non-disclosure will lead to disciplinary action. Mechanisms have been established for whistle blowing as well as for Complaints response mechanism which will help us to monitor and act on any breaches

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NCA has a very deliberate policy of community engagement at every stage of our programme cycle and this includes at various stages. For instance, at the inception stage, we will hold community sensitization sessions with community members through a variety of approaches including focussed group discussions with key informants, leaders, religious leaders, representatives of minorities and people living with disabilities, women, youth and other gender groups. This approach will help us to understand the issues and needs and gaps within the community and among the various gender groups. We will use this information to carry out a participatory and focused assessment of needs and to map vulnerability which will help us to design our programme. The information from this process will be fed back to the community and their views incorporated. We also involve and consult community members. When we need volunteers or local staff to work on projects at the community, we as the community members to identify and provider names for volunteers to work on programmes.At the monitoring and evaluation stage, we try to ensure that in all these stages of the programme that all members of the community are consulted to provide input and feedback on how the project components has affected them positively or negatively. Our accountability to affected populations strategy is based on NCAs Complaints response management strategy. NCA system at the community level involves programme staffs and community members having dialogue at the initial stages to identify how community would like to provide feedback or receive it. Community members discuss a range of options that may include email & telephone, complaints register, suggestion box, face to face or community meetings or a mix of these options. We then set up meeting with community members to discuss ways of setting up the system at the community level once the most preferred system is outlined and discussing on how it will operate. In many of the community settings, electronic systems are not possible, and communities often choose face to face meetings and complaints boxes and complaints registers. The practicalities of the system in relation to local set up is discussed. The key issues discussed include what is a valid complaint and how to handle sensitive complaints, what are sensitive and non-sensitive complaints and how they are handled. The complaints and feedback mechanism is incorporated into the NCA monitoring framework. Issues Once system has been discussed and developed, NCA, Partners and the community agree on a monitoring framework for AAP and then integrated to the NCA CRM. All complaints (sensitive and non-sensitive are registered and forwarded to the NCA focal team on CRM. This teams ensure that each and every complaint is action on, addressed and feedback provided based on agreed feedback mechanisms. To receive complaints, the community will appoint a focal person to register the complaint. We use these engagements to also provide feedback and receive back from community and we use this information to address concerns but also to ensure that the community is at the centre of our response actions. Normal community meetings are also undertaken to engage community members during normal course of programme implementation. This helps to improve our engagement with community. Data collected during monitoring and evaluation sessions is also shared with community members, leaders and stakeholders as part of feedback and to also see how their input was reflected. Information we collect from our monitoring evaluation is analysed to inform us on whether we are on track and if needed to carry out adjustments and for learning. We also use evaluation feedback for learning

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Our monitoring processes are undertaken regularly with monthly and quarterly field monitoring. While our evaluation programmes are undertaken annually. We also make our complaints response and feedback mechanism feedback part and parcel of our programme monitoring framework. We use participatory approaches and processes to ensure that the data collected has input from all sectors of the community we serve and also ensure that every individual opinion counts. We use a variety of tools to ensure that we have effective and structured community engagement so that we can obtain information that will help us to get an needs and concerns of women, girls, boys, displaced, hosts, returnees, elderly, disabled and other special interest groups but also representative. These include focused group discussions, Key informant interviews, and household surveys to engage community to provide us information on what is important to them and what they feel they need addressed or to provide feedback on how they feel our programme are going. Information obtained from right holders will be used to improve our delivery, ensure assistance is delivered in a way that the community feels benefits them. We will use the same information to reflect on our systems, approaches and strategies and adapt them to be more responsive to community needs and to make our programmes better and more targeted and of high quality.
