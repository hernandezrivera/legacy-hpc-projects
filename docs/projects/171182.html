Project Summary

According the HNO-Protection Needs 2021, Up to 66 per cent of the populations in need in Somalia are children who continue to be exposed to threats of recruitment, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation and violence. 1.87M children including 10% of children with disabilities are in need of immediate CP services. Boys and girls of all ages in Somalia continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations. CTFMR verified 1,774 grave violations, 1,613 children 303 girls of which 34% 545 & 30 girls were cases of recruitment/use, 34% 555 cases of abduction. GBVIMS 2020 data show increased intimate partner violence 55% Physical Assault/rape at 61%, 12% respectively in contract with Physical Assault and rape at 56%, 14% 2019. The proposed interventions are within the 2021 Protection Cluster Objectives/priorities. The project will be guided by the principles of DO NO HARM, PSEA and code of conduct ensuring safety and dignity of target population is upheld and at the same time providing for accountability, children’s participation and empowerment. Particular attention will be given child safeguarding through community based programming and strengthening of GBV/Child Protection response systems and promoting community cohesiveness. The proposed project is designed to improve the quality of CP services for boys and girls most at-risk and survivors of violence, exploitation and abuse in particular the child survivors of GBV, child labor, UASC with disabilities, those exhibiting severe signs of distress, and CAAFAG children and children with disabilities. This project will sustain, scale up lifesaving gender-based violence GBV and child protection services including case referral and case management particularly for GBV survivors and family separation. The intervention will respond to protection risks/concerns experienced by women, men, girls and boys affected by the floods, conflicts particularly those among the IDPs and host community in the key displacement/evacuation areas. The project targeted a total # of 61,790 vulnerable IDPs and Host community 15,336 women, 4,750 men, 25,570 children, 11,700 adults, 1,650 elders and 2784 disabled persons in Jowhar, Balcad, Dhusmareb, Kismayo/Baletweyn. INTERSOM will ensure provide case management UASC through immediate response for child and family separation as well as provide equitable, timely accessible GBV services for women, men, boys and girls through provision of medical services, identification and PSS Counselor training will be conducted to provide counseling training to traumatized women and girls affected by the conflicts, droughts/floods in the project areas. PSS /structured MHPSS activities including individual and group counseling will be provided to children and caregivers to protect and promote children’s wellbeing and full participation. INTERSOM will ensure provide immediate comprehensive child projection care packages and complementarity services for all children with protection concerns and vulnerable disabled people and children with disabilities engage and meaningfully consult and have access to community-based prevention, response MHPSS, child protection services. INTERSOM will established/strengthen the existing the community-based child protection committees by increasing the capacity of CBCPM to facilitate identification and efficient referral of children with protection concerns or needs and to prevent and mitigate CP risks through multi-layered, community based PSS support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family/community levels. Provide timely response/appropriate care to survivors of GBV. Organize house-house awareness raising that led by CBCPMs, youth and community volunteers’ networks to prevent GBV and child protection risks. The project will strengthen confidential reporting and referral mechanisms which are accessible to women and children, linked to comprehensive response mechanisms GBV.

Project Activities

1. Improving the quality of CP services for boys and girls most at-risk and survivors of violence, exploitation and abuse in particular the child survivors of GBV, child labour, unaccompanied and separated children, children with disabilities, those exhibiting severe signs of distress, and children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG)2. Increasing the capacity of community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM) to prevent and mitigate CP risks through multi-layered, community based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family and community levels. Peer to peer awareness raising will be led by CBCPMs, youth and community volunteers’ networks to prevent child protection risks, identify vulnerable children in need of support such as case management and emergency child protection care (i.e. alternative care or basic PSS activities)3. Protection and support to children at risk and child survivors including unaccompanied and separated through providing case management services that meets their unique needs, which include family tracing and reunification services, alternative care based on global learning and field evidence. The capacity of community volunteers will be strengthened to support the case management process, namely through identification of children at risk, making home monitoring visits and offering peer support to caregivers.4. Provision of inclusive community reintegration services for children associated with armed forces and groups, or those at-risk categories5. Provision of structured MHPSS activities including individual and group counselling. The Child Protection AoR also considers to developed standardized tools/modules in structured PSS services, enhancing capacity of different stakeholders in PSS, strengthening MHPSS with more focus on children in need of additional more intensive support6. Support to caregivers and families (MHPSS, positive parenting and other kind of support) of children facing protection risks in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children.7. Awareness raising activities targeting community wide on prevention messages and awareness sessions including on recruitment/CAAFAG, SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, MRE and Children’s rights.8. Strengthening Child Protection information management (CPIMS+)for regular Child Protection trends analysis and evidence-based responses through inter-agency collaboration and CPIMS+ reports9. Systematising efforts to develop sustainable local capacities for child protection.10. Strengthen child safeguarding measures including PSEA and code of conduct to ensure that all interventions are safe for children. CP partners that have at least one staff trained as a PSEA and child safeguarding focal point11. Child Protection safety audits/walks/mapping exercise will be used to identify safety and protection issues for children in camps and host communities and which allows children’s participation in identifying risks that concern them and coming up with mitigation strategies owned by children, community members, service providers and humanitarian actors.12. Children and adolescents’ survivors of sexual and gender-based violence will be supported in coordination with the Gender Based Violence AOR. The capacity of social workers/case workers will continue to be built through learning strategies including case management training, mentoring and coaching.

Mitigation Measures

INTERSOM will undertake measures to minimize risk related to beneficiary’s selection and registration process by using joint, participatory transparent verification and registration processes and assessment of gender specific needs inclusive all groups as a key requirement in the response mobilization phase and ensuring that marginalized and disabled people are inclusive using WGSS data disaggregation as well as INTERSOM will ensure commitment to accountability to affected to the affected populations, local authority, line ministries and donors by ensuring transparent feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into the project, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings, vendors et. INTERSOM will ensure that the affected community have accesses timely sufficient information, guidance, and support that enable the target population to understand and influence key decisions in the project to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices through active participation and facilitate consultation between INTERSOM and the affected populations over the project. INTERSOM staff will undertaking regular field monitoring visits to observe key the implementation of the project activities, risks and challenges. INTERSOM will also ensure to maintain regular communication with the community, local authority, line ministries, and donors to receive timely and updated information on the situation of children and women in each of the targeted project areas. As well, to mitigate risks related of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, INTERSOM will ensure avoid centralized distributions of humanitarian assistance and services such as dignity, kits etc.

Please provide further explanation

INTERSOM will identify project beneficiaries through community participatory process & selection criteria agreed with the communities including female headed households, IDPs, disabled and minority groups using WGSS for disaggregation of each gender specific needs to identify and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities in humanitarian contexts. Information will be provided on process of beneficiary selection and criteria, entitlements, complaints & feedback system including PSEA information and reporting mechanism. INTERSOM will be committed and accountable to the affected populations ensuring transparent feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into the project implementation, monitoring and evaluations during the project life. INTERSOM will ensure that the project beneficiaries have access timely and sufficient information of their entitlement, and support that enable the target population to understand and influence key decisions in the project to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices through active participation and facilitate a dialogue between INTERSOM and the beneficiaries. Beneficiary complaint committees will be established in each of the targeted project locations to act as an independent group tasked with receiving and processing queries, complaints and feedback from their community. They will also consult with INTERSOM staff to address concerns. Child Feedback Committees whom will provides other children with a safe peer to peer forum to voice concerns and collect feedback, complaints and suggestions for programme improvements from a child’s perspective will also be established in each of the targeted project locations. All these committee members will receive training on how to carry out awareness and sensitization meetings among community members in the respective villages and will also discussed how to channel community complaints on activities being implemented. Monthly community meetings were held in which CBCP, and complaints committees were also discussed. Minutes of meetings and decisions on complaints will be recorded and signed by the village chairperson. Legal and social support will be provided to GBV survivors through both traditional legal/justice systems and community protection mechanisms will put in place as secondary protection to meet the needs of women and girls who have been abused. INTERSOM will hire legal advisor who will regularly providing legal advice and assistance to citizens in target areas, with the aim of increasing access to basic rights and services for all through conducting community based activities through these networks aimed at increasing understanding and at the meantime reducing stigma and establishing mutual goals in achievement of basic rights.
