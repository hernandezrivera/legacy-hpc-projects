Project Summary

The objective of this project is to provide humanitarian assistance, and enhance the resiliencies of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in hard to reach parts of Syria. Given the incredible complexity of the situation in Aleppo and Ar Raqqa governorates, which may vary from one district to another, different modalities will be employed to provide an integrated yet tailored response.
Overall, 2000 households (12,000 individuals) will be targeted by Shelter and NFI activities.
Sector Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support.
Sector outcome indicator: Targeted crisis-affected households, including those in HTR locations, have their core and essential non-food item needs met.
Activity: 1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps, including cash/voucher for these items). Regular NFI distributions will ensure that the basic needs of displaced and/or vulnerable households are met, while supporting their dignity.
Sector outcome indicator: Targeted people are protected from seasonally harsh conditions.
Activity: 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance.
In addition to the distribution of core and essential items, the project will provide winterization assistance to households living in sub-standard shelter, i.e. living in camps, informal settlements or damaged/unfinished buildings.
Sector Objective 2: Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
Sector Outcome Indicator: Increase in adequate housing stock available to targeted households and communities.
Activity 2.1 Support to sustainably repair or rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities provided to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.).
Shelter Rehabilitation activities will be implemented through the provision of Cash for Shelter grants, which will be accompanied by regular technical support from the project Shelter and Infrastructure Team.

Needs

Now in the seventh year of the crisis, some 13.1 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance, of whom 5.3 million are children. A total of 5.6 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services. Conflict continues to be the principal driver of humanitarian needs, with the civilian population in many parts of the country, including project locations, exposed to significant protection risks which threaten life, dignity and wellbeing on a daily basis.The humanitarian situation is incredibly complex and constantly evolving, with population movements difficult to predict. Following the losses of ISIS controlled territories, large numbers of IDPs are beginning to return home and require basic humanitarian assistance. Equally large numbers of IDPs are unable to return home and require continued humanitarian assistance in camps and ‘temporary’ shelters. Host communities are over-burdened. Ar Raqqa governorate: The war has led to unprecedented levels of explosive hazard contamination as well as the destruction of infrastructure and buildings. Local communities are living in damaged buildings and makeshift shelters, with little or no access to basic services, with livelihoods destroyed, and in constant risk of explosive accidents. Both improvised and conventional explosive hazards have been reported, requiring increased integrated clearance capacities centred on the removal of explosive hazards in order to support a safe returns process and prevent death and injury, while improving humanitarian access. Aleppo governorate: High levels of IDPs are in camps or staying with overburdened host communities from Aleppo, Idleb, Ar Raqqa and Deir ez Zor. Needs are especially acute in the informal settlements. In December 2018, DCA carried out a mapping of informal settlements and provided an initial winterization response. Based on needs identified, further assistance will be provided in 2019.

Implementation of activities

Sector Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support.Indicator: Targeted crisis-affected households, including those in HTR locations, have their core and essential non-food item needs met.Activity: 1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs.Regular NFI distributions will ensure the basic needs of displaced and/or vulnerable households are met, while supporting their dignity. Based on information collected during pre-implementation assessments, gender and age specific items will be included in the NFI kits. When possible (ie, functional and accessible markets can meet the increased demand), cash and in-kind responses might be combined to also empower beneficiaries to choose the items that are the most necessary to them.(Target 6000 individuals/ 1000 kits/ USD 150 per kit, Total funds for Activity: USD 150,000) Indicator: Targeted people are protected from seasonally harsh conditions.Activity: 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance.In addition to the distribution of core and essential items, the project will provide winterization assistance to households living in sub-standard shelter, i.e. living in camps, informal settlements or damaged/unfinished buildings.When possible (ie, functional and accessible markets can meet the increased demand), cash and in-kind responses might be combined to support beneficiary empowerment. This approach will namely be privileged to the procurement of, for example, winter clothing.(Target 6000 individuals/ 1000 kits/ USD 300 per kit, Total funds for Activity: USD 300,000) Sector Objective 2: Contribute to the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.Indicator: Increase in adequate housing stock available to targeted households and communities.Activity 2.1 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.).In areas considered low threat from explosive hazards (as evaluated by the mine action sector), returnees who are living in damaged and substandard accommodation will receive cash-for-shelter assistance as well as technical guidance that will allow targeted households to carry out necessary improvements for their accommodation to meet minimum standards in terms of WASH facilities, winterization and weatherproofing. This will contribute to their dignity while also supporting the local market (through construction-related labour and material inputs). The average value per household will be USD 700. Households with high levels of vulnerability (i.e. female, or elderly households, households with people with disabilities) will receive an increased grant to ensure they are able to make use of mentioned rehabilitation works. The average additional grant for highly vulnerable households will be USD 150. While the person residing in the shelter will be in charge of conducting the rehabilitation works (either themselves or by a local contractor), the project’s Shelter and Infrastructure Team will carry out regular monitoring visits to assess the progress of the rehabilitation works and to provide technical guidance and recommendations. The Cash for Shelter grant will be disbursed in instalments, with each subsequent instalment triggered by the completion of a previously agreed portion of rehabilitation work.(Target 300 shelters for 300 households (1800 individuals) / USD 700 per shelter, Total Funds for Activity: USD 210,000). Some project beneficiaries may receive assistance from Activity 1.1, Activity 1.2 and Activity 1.3, however they will only be counted once. Hence the project expected total beneficiary count (12,000) is lower than the sum of all activity beneficiaries (13,800).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Safety risk: Safety and security of project staff; Safety and security of project beneficiariesMitigation measures: Staff live in ’low risk’ areas. Project staff report their movements to the security focal point on a regular basis. Names of project beneficiaries are protected. Multiple distribution sites are set up for simultaneous distribution to a) minimize travel distances for beneficiaries, b) minimize crowd sizes, and c) minimize the duration of distribution processes for security reasons. Different transport routes are used on different days when cash distributions are taking place. 2.Programmatic risk: Beneficiaries are not reachable because of conflict; Assistance is not used for humanitarian purposes.Mitigation measures: Money is set aside for up to a month if people are not reachable. At least 20% of beneficiaries are interviewed about their spending patterns using real-time electronic mobile monitoring tools. The identity of each beneficiary is verified, and their households are visited to confirm lack of substantial assets. The project maintains a Complaints Handling System to ensure feedback and complaints are welcomed from the project beneficiaries. Any complaints are dealt with swiftly and appropriately. 3.Financial risk: Fraud and corruptionMitigation measures: All project management staff have completed the Anti-Corruption Training, as well as the training on Common Humanitarian Standards. A project procurement committee will be set up at the start of the project. All transactions require original vouchers and receipts. The Complaints Handling Mechanism also exists for financial complaints to be raised.
