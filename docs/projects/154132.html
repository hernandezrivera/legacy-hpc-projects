Project Summary

The project will aim to address the shortage of potable water in conflict affected areas in the north and center of Mali. While the targeted regions are prone to drought, the 2012 conflict has also had its toll on preserving the available water sources. Most of the existing wells have been contaminated either during the conflict or due to prolonged lack of maintenance and water treatment. The population, especially women and young girls, heavily rely on well water as they cater to the daily needs of their families. The water wells will be rehabilitated, treated, and rendered potable in order to reduce, and if possible, eliminate the risk of poor-quality-water-related diseases.
The project being implemented in pastoralist area cattle as well will drink water from rehabilitated water source through an appropriate point designed preventing water point from further contamination. Gardens around water points will be watered as well linking WASH to livelihood and food security
The project will be carried out in Mopti (Mopti, Djenné & Douentza), Menaka, and Gao (Gao, Bourem & Ansongo) regions and in circles where shortage of potable water is most acute. IOM will address the situation in close coordination and collaboration with local population, local administrative authorities, civil society, and partner agencies. The assessment reports from the WASH cluster will be used as a guide to reconfirm the identified water sources. Evaluation will be carried out to determine the extent of the damages and the corresponding needs and actions taken to remedy the situation. The damaged water sources will be rehabilitated and, in some instances, new wells will be dug depending on the need, population density, and proximity to public infrastructure like markets, schools, and medical facilities. Water treatment equipment and facilities will be procured for use on the water sources. To maintain excellent quality of the wells and usage, local repairmen from various communities will be trained and equipped to provide regular maintenance of the wells while the capacity of water management committees will be reinforced in the target communities and at various locations. Beneficiaries will be trained on water environmental hygiene most vulnerable beneficiaries will receive WASH kit composed of Buckets, Jerrycans, soaps containers for water safe handling and purifiers( pure & quatabs). In general, the local population will be fully involved not only in the design of the project but in its implementation to ensure sustainability and ownership. IOM will build on similar project currently being implemented in the target regions where the beneficiaries are expressing their delight and satisfaction, to provide potable water to the affected communities.

Needs

Access to safe water is limited in the northern regions of Mali. Existing wells are poorly protected prone to frequent contamination risks that might lead to further increased risk of water borne diseases among populations especially women and children who are often in contact with water for daily needs. According to the WASH cluster and following the vulnerability-based research that takes into account the crisis in northern Mali, displacement, malnutrition, risks of cholera epidemic, drought, flood risk and the rate of access of populations, access to an improved and quality drinking water source is inevitable and necessary to alleviate the situation and to guarantee sanitary and better health conditions. For the cluster, over 3.1 million persons are affected by the shortage of quality drinking water of which 1.3 million persons are directly in need. The cluster is targeting over one million beneficiaries for 2019. This project will target a minimum of 30,000 individual beneficiaries in various locations where the project will be implemented. Majority of the beneficiaries are located in the regions of Gao, Kidal, Mopti, Timbuktu and Menaka and are in need of WASH assistance (HRP 2018). The 3.1 million persons affected are composed of IDPs, returnees, repatriated and host communities and others living in affected or at-risk circles in localities with quality water deficiency. Also, and in addition to residents in these localities, nationals from other West African countries that transit through the regions as migration routes to northern Africa and beyond also depend on the water for their needs. The most affected populations are children, women and the elderly. The 2012 crisis that mostly affected the northern regions has led to a deterioration of sources of water and its infrastructure. This contributed to a drastic decline in coverage of drinking water in these regions. Water is scarce for those who remained in the North due to climatic conditions as the north of the country largely suffers from acute shortage of water. According to the September 2018 report from the Commission of Movement of Population (CMP) of the Ministry of Solidarity and Humanitarian Action that tracks the movement of population, about 526,505 persons that were forcibly displaced as a result of the conflict have returned to their places of origin and mostly in the regions where the project is intended to be carried out. However, according to the same report, about 77 046 people remain displaced from recent communal conflicts in the center and north of the country. Given the population dynamics, the pressure exerted on the already existing scarce water resources is increasing. Conflicts and insecurity in Mopti & Ménaka regions have increased the number of IDPs. Better and quality water coverage in return areas is essential to reduce water-related diseases, improve health and reduce incidence of community conflicts related to the shortage of water and other meager resources whilst improving livelihood of both pastoralist and agricultural communities.

Activities

TThe activities of the project will be carried out in close consultation and collaboration with all stakeholders including the intended beneficiaries. It will be based on existing mechanisms of committees, group discussions on the existing problem and recommended solutions. The project will be carried out over a period of nine months during which an interim report will be provided, monitoring and evaluation carried out and a final narrative and financial report produced at the end of the project. Specific actions that will be carried out in addition to others as the need would arise include; 1. Carry out detailed assessments, in coordination with the Directorate of Regional Water at village levels in the target regions - Mopti, Gao, Timbuktu and Menaka to reconfirm the needs and the degree of damages, 2. Inform local administration, community leaders, target beneficiaries and partners about the project and expected outcomes, 3. In coordination with relevant stakeholders of which district mayor, identify and select the water points that need intervention and digging new water wells where needed 4. Select, through competitive bidding, the best technically suited partners of which the social interface (local NGO) for project implementation, 5. Reinforce the capacity of members of the communities in management and preservation of the water points, 6. Carry out awareness raising on hygiene promotion especially targeting women and young girls who are in constant contact with water, 7. Reinforce the capacity of community repairmen to effectively and adequately carry out repair works on the water points as would be needed to maintain the water sources in good conditions. 8. Distribute WASH kits to most vulnerable beneficiaries following the protection cluster’s vulnerability criterionThe above summary of activities will be spread over the project duration ensuring the proper approach and the stakeholders buy-in is assured for sustainability
