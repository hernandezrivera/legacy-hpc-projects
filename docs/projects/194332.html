Project Summary

PCO aims at targeting 28,800 households (Tonj East 9,600, Tonj South 9600 and Tonj North 9600), includes people with special needs and youth amongst host, returnee and IDP communities for a period of 12 months. The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 8.2 million people in need according to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview. The HNO estimate that in 2023, the FSL Cluster will target 6.0 million people (excluding refugees), who are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (May – July) and are in dire need of humanitarian life-saving assistance as shock appear to be intensifying. Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei where 79% of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity, followed by Unity (75%), Warrap (70%), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (66%), Lakes (61%) and Upper Nile (60%) with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2023.). To enhance and sustain emergency food production will be through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support. In light of resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households (CO3), this will be realized through training of farmers, setting up of kitchen garden in the 12 targeted Nutrition facilities, establishment of demo sites, fisher folks, formation of VSLA groups (20 member each), and through local procurement of seeds through seed fairs. The training will cover good and climate-smart agronomic practices, countering post-harvest losses, sustainable income generation activities, value addition with special emphasis on minimizing spread of COVID-19. Seed Fairs (SFs) for Crop Seed procurement will be done from seed producer groups/traders in Tonj South County where seeds will be procured locally from these farmers and distributed to the community farming purposes among vulnerable populations at risk of protection concerns through linkages of already instituted collaborative efforts with GBV, Nutrition, Education, Health and WASH partners, these will also be linked to create complementarities. Beneficiary targeting will be through the AAP committee with mainstreaming of gender, persons with disabilities, minority and marginalized groups.
PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead in WBeG and currently implementing UNICEF funded construction of 10 Semi - Permanent Learning Spaces in 10 schools in Wau and Jur River counties since July 2022 to January 2023, Also on-going Education project targeting 5 schools under UNOCHA 2022 SA1 funding in Twic County being supported by SSHF 2022; and Multi Year Response Education Program (MYRP) project in 46 schools in Gogrial West and Tonj North Counties funded by Education Cannot Wait (ECW) since May 2020 to December 2022. PCO is also implementing FNS REPRO FSL project in Jur River and Wau Counties of Western Bahr el Ghazal state being supported by FAO/USAID, RALP FSL Project in Wau County being funded by World Bank and also currently implementing Camp and Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Project in Pibor GPAA of Jonglei supported by SSHF. PCO in partnership with NPA has a current running FSL Program in Rumbek East County of Lake state supported by NORAD. PCO has maintained uninterrupted programmatic presence, strong network and linkages with the local authority in targeted counties since 2014 and proposed action will scale up FSL initiatives. The organization has a functional and experienced emergency response team that has been working in coordination with the states as well as National FSL, Protection, Education WASH and Nutrition Clusters and is better placed to respond.

1 - Needs

South Sudan, are likely to continue driving high levels of food insecurity into the foreseeable future, access to markets affected by the economic crisis, protracted conflict and flooding limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services, sub-national violence is expected to continue in many locations and abate in others until the peace process and governance becomes truly inclusive; a fourth year of a typical flooding is becoming the new ‘normal’ causing displacement, damage to homes and affecting agricultural livelihoods and households’ ability to produce their own food with the added effect of water logging and water borne disease on livestock and human health, persistent high food prices, periodic dry spells and continue to threaten people’s food security. The inter-communal violence has continued in many pockets of the country resulted in new displacement often many times and impeding humanitarian access. South Sudan is experiencing unprecedented levels of acute food insecurity, with an IPC Phase 3, 4&5 (Catastrophe) acute food insecurity indicators observed in Tonj East, Tonj North, and Tonj South.According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)-2023 estimated that more than two-thirds of south Sudan’s population, 8.2 million people, are in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection an increase of 500,000 since 2021. The HNO estimate that in 2023, the FSL Cluster will target 6.0 million people (excluding refugees), who are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (May – July) and are in dire need of humanitarian life-saving assistance as shock appear to be intensifying. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5 and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022.This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. The high levels of food insecurity observed in Warrap State could potentially serve as a trigger for continued organized violence, as hunger forces households to raid neighbouring communities for livestock and livelihood assets. Similarly, food insecurity will likely deteriorate should the levels and severity of organized violence observed in Counties of Tonj North, Tonj East and Tonj South of Warrap States continue into 2023. The on-going economic crisis has significantly reduced households’ purchasing power, prolonged dry spells at critical stages of crop growth, flooding, crop pests and diseases, Livestock have been looted, the recent influx of spontaneous refugee returnees from the neighbouring countries, and the returning of IDPs to their original residential areas are demanding food, humanitarian access challenges, chronic water borne diseases and malnutrition (FSNMS 24&28 report). The protracted nature of the crisis in South Sudan have resulted in significantly greater levels of food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, forcing many families to resort to negative coping strategies by reducing meals, going without food, surviving on wild fruits or selling their last livestock’s, transactional sex, sale of productive assets, early/ forced marriage and forms of child labour. These results to food consumption gaps, reduced dietary diversity and increased household hunger scores. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. This project aims at enabling the community increase food and livestock production to save life and reduce dependency on food and agriculture input to strengthen household ability to absorb shocks to conflict affected population of Tonj South, Tonj North and Tonj East Counties in Warrap of South Sudan.

2 - Project activities

The proposed Humanitarian Response Plan targets a total of 28,800 individuals and the following key activities will be implemented. Farmers training through farmer field schools and demonstration plots to farmers in Tonj North and Tonj South Counties, Training of fisher folks in Tonj North and Tonj South Counties, Conducting Seed Fairs (SFs) for local Crop Seed procurement seed producer groups/traders in Tonj North, Tonj East and Tonj South Counties, Formation and training VSLAs. Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits, Emergency food & livelihood support, fishing kits/ vegetable kits; rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed), Livelihood support to returnee households, Provision of timely food security information, Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives IRRM: food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds, Conditional cash transfers (social skills & safety nets)/ Conditionality: (investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees etc.), Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc.,Multi sectoral models: livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation, Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW & DL surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication, Reduction in post-harvest losses and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies. VSLAs shall be identified and engaged in the seed multiplication and seed saving training exercise. Establishment of demonstration plots, farmer-to-farmer learning, and on mass agricultural campaigns and conduct refresher training to County Agriculture Department Officers and CBAEWs. Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with a particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to the affected population, and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

PCO has been implementing projects for a long time and monthly monitors the prices in the market of both goods locally produced and goods that comes across the border and with previous experience in implementing CVA project, we will conduct a market feasibility and availability of inputs required both from the beneficiary’s perspectives and the different market actors. The project will only be implemented after the detail analysis of the different market actors to avoid distorting the market balance but rather reinforcing its sustainability. Guidance from the Cash Working Group, will be adapted with reference to specific market functionality assessments before the project is implemented.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Despite the report of cereal prices being the highest, this proves the functionality and availability of goods in the market, which is a pre-requisite to carry our cash or voucher assistance. Cash or voucher assistance provides the dignity of choice and decision that beneficiaries would have in deciding what they would want to acquire from the market. Most of the CVA will be done in complementarity with other partners that distribute food in kind and hence the supplement with Cash Voucher Assistance will complement and harmonize the response to the communities. CVA save time required for the response as the delays and costs related to procurement, logistics, and transportation are eliminated and communities are able to acquire from the market what they want as soon as possible within the shortest time.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

PCO has presence in all the targeted locations and hence there is already community engagement and trust with beneficiaries as well as the local authorities, who already had given some of the needs to help design this project. PCO will spend some time with beneficiaries at the inception of the project creating an awareness of the project and its goal including how to report complains and feedback and how they will be addressed. PCO will use the following Information sharing mechanisms such as monthly focus group discussions and community meetings to share information; weekly or bi-monthly announcements through notice boards, quarterly printing and distribution of leaflets and posters and monthly women and child participatory activities to share information and solicit feedback from women and children about the support they receive or the spaces they attend (using simple child participatory tools, such as the assessment to generate useful information for quick decision-making). The Project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, though, Surveys and FGDs with specific age groups, women, men, youths and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth will be conducted on a regular basis. Bi-annual public meetings will be organized to gain beneficiaries ‘feedback in a structured format, enabling different stakeholders to discuss and exchange views on specific topics, often adopting a face-to-face format which allows for brainstorming and testing ideas. In addition, in-depth interviews with individual stakeholders will be used to get a sense of stakeholders’ perspectives and key informants/stakeholder assessments will be used occasionally to explore an issue in depth and permit related issues to be introduced into the discussion. PCO will use FGD/community meetings, help desks, suggestion boxes (including information on how the boxes will be handled) and child-friendly feedback mechanism. PCO will maintain and raise awareness on its Complaints and Feedback Mechanism. This mechanism will follow PCO guidelines, ensuring multiple methods for submitting complaints and receiving responses, including CFM boxes in schools and safe spaces as well as through static helpdesks. PCO’s Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will provide continuous information and feedback on the quality of services provided, gaps and ways forward to strengthen the response. During these visits women and girls shall be given priority to air out their views in complete confidentiality. PCO’s MEAL Team will identify risks, classify any high-risk activities, and document steps being taken to reduce or remove these risks for beneficiaries, develop and implement an action plan to ensure the beneficiaries safety.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

PCO will adopt a consultative approach in the implementation of this project and this will enable the implementing partners to receive feedback on the project progress. All the beneficiaries (men, women, boys and girls) will be directly involved in the entire response cycle from inception until exit and also be informed on the duration, activities and the roles of different stakeholders in the project. All feedback regarding project-related matters will provide self-reflection and providing actionable plans to ensure the project is adjusted to suit recommendations provided during consultation sessions. This will be done by ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project. Details on recruitment and other arising issues will be communicated in forums such as public meetings and Focus Group Discussions which will be held with the different segregated groups of people (men, women, girls and boys) to ensure community gets timely information which enhances active involvement and sense of ownership of the project. The local leaders among other relevant authorities and other key stakeholders will also be invited during project launch and since they are the representatives of the community, they will give the community information about the assistance to be provided. The local leaders and other community representatives will give their opinions and complaints as well as possible recommendations. Feedback obtained from project activities will be consolidated and analysed at the state and Juba level offices and feedback into the program unit used to realign activities to planned standard.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the project life cycle. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee comprises of diverse representatives such as Community leaders, Community representatives, women representatives, Youth representatives, local authority and beneficiary representatives and with support from project staff. In order to provide beneficiaries with information about PSEA, all training will incorporate elements of PSEA, indicating the engagement levels of the project staff with the community, reporting avenues available, their rights to benefit without being compromised. The project will Identify and minimize the risk of harm to beneficiaries from staff. The beneficiaries will be reminded that PCO staff interactions with adult beneficiaries, or with personal data about such persons, will comply with the PCO’s Safeguarding Behaviour Protocols and PSEA policy. The project will Include GBV mitigation as part of staff training & conduct. Any incidents of harm or risk of harm to child headed or to adult beneficiaries will be reported immediately through channels that will be explained and demonstrated to the community. Beneficiaries will be reminded of all issues related to PSEA including reported cases being treated with utmost confidentiality, investigated and action taken. All actions taken will be discussed with the complainant after a specified time (feedback).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

PCO have developed a tool for feedback and complaint mechanism which we have been using in the previous similar projects and have always proven to be efficient. The local leaders and other community representatives will give their opinions and complaints as well as possible recommendations. PCO have locally recruited and trained Community Health Worker and Agriculture Extension Officer who vaccinate animals and train beneficiaries on agronomics respectively and are able to get feedback directly from the beneficiaries and they are addressed immediately depending on the urgency. We also have sessions with the beneficiaries during our field supervisions where we discuss the challenges, project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach. We also have suggestion boxes where beneficiaries can drop their complaints for which will be registered in the complaint book and reviewed by Project Management Committee and give feedback to respective beneficiary. All the views from the beneficiaries are then screened and processed further and we use these criteria as a complaint/ feedback mechanism.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PCO is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. PCO will develop mitigation measures for potential protection risks associated with implementing of this proposed project. PCO will mobilize and sensitize the community and stakeholders about COVID-19 disease through use of megaphones, house to house visits but maintaining the MOH standard of physical distance; develop, print and distribute appropriate IEC materials (i.e. posters, leaflets). In the event of finding difficulties for women to participate in the intervention, PCO will conduct community outreach to explain the project’s objectives and the importance of engaging women and girls. Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, Mitigation measure: staff will have to sign the code of conduct when signing the contract and before activities start all teams will receive PSEA and Child protection training. Additionally, specific PSEA complaint mechanisms will be put in place through the PSEA focal points within the CRM committee to ensure complaints reach Juba and the internal audit as early as possible. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures: in each CRM it will be ensured at least on representative of each marginalized group is represented in the committee to guarantee feedback from these vulnerable groups is received. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). PCO will establish help desks as well as EIC material aiming at raising awareness on protection risks specially GBV and CP as well as referral pathways to the services available will be made available in every school. PCO, together with other agencies will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access.
