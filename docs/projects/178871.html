Project Summary

SAMS Health program for the year 2021 comes in line with the Health Sector objectives 1&2&3: Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need; Strengthen health sector capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks, including COVID-19 and Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context. In this program, SAMS will prioritize the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving the access to quality health care (primary and secondary, specialized services, mobile clinics, and an ambulatory system, COVID-19 response) in 36 health facilities within NWS and Al Hasakeh and Ar Raqqa governorates. SAMS will support facilities on different levels including coverage of staff incentives, running costs, provision of pharmaceuticals and other medical and non-medical commodities, rehabilitation/maintenance of the HFs. SAMS will provide recruitment support, capacity building, and medical supervision to ensure the HFs are staffed with qualified medical personnel and to enhance compliance with standard treatment protocols.
SAMS will provide the following services at the primary healthcare level: RH services (ante-natal care, post-natal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), Integrated management for child illness, EPI, communicable and non-communicable diseases, Nutrition, MHPSS, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services. Services at the secondary health care level will include gynecologic surgeries and cesarean sections, trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, hemodialysis, and ICUs, in addition to COVID-19 response (CM, Triage, PPE/IPC, CHWs). This program will also provide cancer treatment to Breast, colon, Hodgkin, and non-Hodgkin Lymphoma patients, Cath Lab, Pathology, GI endoscopy, Dental services, Paediatric ALL, ICU, NICU, PICU, Kidney transplant medications. Additionally, SAMS will support the ambulatory system (10 ambulances, part of which will be dedicated to transferring COVID-19 patients). SAMS will sustain the support for rehabilitation in Sejjo, Termanin, and Afrin. In addition, as part of SAMS focus on contributing to its goal of increasing the capacity of the health care professional in NWS through advancing SAMS’ investment in its medical education program: Supporting Residents Doctors; Supporting Midwifery Education in NWS; Supporting Nursing Education.
Similar to all SAMS projects, SAMS will seek all efforts possible to coordinate with all health actors through the Health Cluster whether through the routinely gathered data in 4Ws and HeRAMS. or through its constant participation and active membership within the following TWG: COVID-19 TF, SRH, MHPSS, Non-Communicable Diseases, SIG, Midwifery Capacity Building Strategy for NWS, Secondary Health Care, and Drug Supply Line, in addition, to coordinate with other NGOs on the referral, and the contribution to DHIS2, notwithstanding its membership in the NGO Forum, SNA, and other bodies. SAMS always coordinate and consult its operations with the HDs.

4 - Needs

With just 58 % of hospitals and 53 % of PHCs fully functional, COVID19 has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system. 2020 saw 39,773 COVID-19 cases and over 1,300 deaths reported across Syria. Essential health services were disrupted as agencies and health workers alike rushed to respond to COVID-19. Due to cumulative effects of crises, cost of health services; lack of medicines, supplies or health staff; and fear of exposure to COVID-19 have replaced distance, over-crowding and long wait times as the main self-reported barriers to health access with persons with disabilities disproportionately affected. Disrupted medication supply chains, particularly for psychotropic drugs and (NCD) medicines, were previously identified as a key gap in the health response and a contributor to child mortality. Compared to 2020, the total number of people in need (PiN) for health services has increased by 4%, primarily due to the humanitarian situation in Idleb governorate where reports indicate an additional 220K PiN. However, in NWS, only 396 of the total 577 health facilities are currently functional, including only 56% of those in Idleb and 39% of those in Aleppo (HeRAMS). The shortage of qualified health workers at the HFs continues to be an issue across Syria, with HeRAMS indicating the total number of health workers in NWS is less than half of the SPHERE standard. Significant psychological trauma is evident in survivors of explosive incidents seeking mental health, psychosocial support, and physiotherapy services yet are unreported due to lack of resources to assess and address the specific needs

5 - Implementation of activities

SAMS will work on the following activities: Rehab works for health facilities, Recruitment, Capacity building for medical and non-medical staff in addition to the administrative staff, Provide PHC services according to updated EHSP including CD, NCD, child health, RH and MH-Gap, Provide trauma, SHC, and in-patients services in SAMS specialized and general hospitals including ICU and Renal dialysis, MHPSS, Referralssupport the DHIS2, provide SRH services including ANC, PNC, FP, BEmONC, CEmONC, in addition to CMR and NICU, Provide Case Management services in 3 HBIUs, Procure medical/non-medical supplies to the HFs, Raising awareness sessions, health-promoting, and medical messages to the community through CHWs, self-care and staff care activities, Provision of ambulatory and referral services, Implementation of safe and effective medical waste management protocols, Sustain services of EPI to reduce morbidity and mortality in 8 centers, Provide medical education support through two midwiferies and nursing schools, in addition to the support of the resident doctors’ project, and Provision of PSEA And Child safeguarding training. MEAL Field monitors will conduct quarterly visits using checklists/spot checks, including verifying attendance sheets, payment receipts, accuracy, and patient data entry, in order to monitor activities and assess impact. Besides SAMS CRM channels, MEAL team will conduct patient satisfaction surveys (including protection mainstreaming indicators) spot-checks according to EHSP standards, KIIs with local stakeholders including health facility staff, KAP baseline/end line. In addition to the mainstreaming of SAMS EMR (Electronic Medical Record) system in all SAMS supported facilities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Attack by GoS/ others. Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Activation of contingency plans for each center in cases of near bombing or close clashes or siege; Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation. Risk: Hostage taking/Kidnap for ransom. Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Counter Abduction; Abduction Management training for staff and partners. Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / GoS. Mitigation measures: Anti-Terrorism Check (ATC), Pause / Cessation; Information security SOP; Relocating the activities locations; Inform the partner civil authorities (Health Directorates and Civil Local Authorities), of the suspension and the reasons, in order to play their role in negotiation and advocacy, and apply the agreements of neutralizing humanitarian facilities. Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries. Mitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the trainings regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible. Risk: Theft Mitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports.
