Project Summary

Improving access to and the quality of health of the IDPs and people in host communities is important as it contributes to improved economic situation of the family, communities, and the affected states
According to Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP,2022), about 8.4 million people are need of humanitarian aid in the BAY state. Most of the areas in these conflict zones were inaccessible in the past few years but with the improved security situation and especially with the closing of IDPs camps in the city following the relocation of the IDPs to their host communities, there is an urgent need to improve the health conditions of the population in area of SRH and for women, girls, pregnant and lactating women and men as well as those who are infected with sexually transmitted infections in the hard-to-reach areas. GHIV Africa’s mandate of delivering healthcare at the doorstep of every community has SRH as one of its components and has been using community-based and facility-based approaches in programming for SRH interventions using the MISP approach. GHIV Africa through this proposed intervention will addressing gaps that exist in sexual reproductive health in selected LGAs of Borno State.

1 - Needs

More than thirteen years in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) OF Northeast Nigeria continues unabated. The impact of the conflict in the BAY states, home to some 16 million people, has created a protracted humanitarian and protection crisis affecting millions of people.Conflicts (including armed conflicts) lead to immense negative impacts on the lives of millions of people through serious repercussions, including violations of rights and freedoms. In 2018, an estimated 34 million women of reproductive age, at least 5 million of whom were pregnant, were in need of humanitarian health assistance, due to conflict. While there continues to be a need for more reliable data on maternal mortality in conflict and displacement settings, there is little doubt that conflict exacerbates maternal mortality and morbidity. A recent study noted that conflict countries have had consistently higher maternal mortality rates than non-conflict countries for the past 30 years, and these gaps persist. In addition, women and girls affected by conflict are particularly vulnerable to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), including rape; sexually transmitted infections, such as HIV/AIDS; sex trafficking and slavery; early and forced marriage; and forced and unintended pregnancy. These women, along with all women and girls living in conflict settings, therefore urgently require sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services, including obstetric care, antenatal care, contraceptives, safe abortion and postabortion care, and psychosocial counseling for trauma. In the context of conflict, however, delivery and access to these services are affected due to devastated and disintegrated health infrastructure and health facilities that are inaccessible and unable to provide reproductive health care services and information.To respond to above gaps, GHIV Africa will be responding to Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) and Mental Health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS) need of Vulnerable Groups in BAY state

2 - Activities

1. GHIV Africa will prioritize Health system strengthening and ensure planning for comprehensive SRH services by working closely with State Ministry of Health. 2.Training and capacity building of Local Health Care providers/organizations on Minimum Initial Ser Package (MISP), Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Emergency maternal and neonatal Obstetric Care, Family Planning (FP), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) within Borno and Yobe State. 3. Provision of essential specialized GBV Integrated SRH services in IDP camp clinics, Referral centers and Mobile medical outreaches in hard-to-reach communities. Services include adolescent Sexual Reproductive Health services, CMR, management of Obstetric Complications and contraceptives. 5. Provision of technical guidance on reinforcing the infection control measures and universal precautions as guided by MISP for SRH service provisions in the health facilities and the clinics to ensure the critical SRH life-saving SRH services are continued even in the context of the flood and cholera outbreak 6. Community engagement and improved awareness of Key SRH issues to create demand and promote accountability to the affected population. 7.Promotional messages in local spoken languages will be developed and shared, ensuring feedback from the community members is considered. 8. Behavioral change initiatives to mitigate harmful traditional practices and promote the acceptability of life-saving SRH services. GHIV Africa shall work with the State Government in the BAY States and partners to prioritize the SRH needs of women and girls, including adolescent girls and boys, and Persons with Disabilities (PWD) for inclusivity .

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

During the implementation of this intervention, GHIV Africa will focused on the dynamics and circumstances that threaten the safety and dignity of people affected by armed conflict, displacement, and other crisis situations especially amongst the most vulnerable groups. We will be using crisis-specific strategies that prioritize issues of greatest concern to affected groups, while investing in relationships and initiatives that safeguard the space needed to uphold humanitarian values.GHIV Africa is a women led organization that prioritizes the needs of girls and boy, women, boys, and other persons most affected by crisis or those in dire need of humanitarian assistance. In view that, girls of menstruation age will be trained on Menstruation Hygiene Management (MHM) and will be provided with dignity kits. In addition, all the latrines constructed will be gender segregated. As the sector lead for disability inclusion, the project will ensure access to learning opportunities for People with Disabilities (PWD) thus making inclusive. In this frame, a fair access to education will be emphasized during the awareness sessions on education and the classrooms in the formal schools, as well as the WASH facilities in both the TLS and the formal schools will be constructed with ramp access. To empower the different groups, GHIV Africa will identify volunteers and hygiene promotor speaking the local dialects. Furthermore, the recruitment of the volunteers and the hygiene promoters will be gender-balanced and ensure a fair access to job-opportunity and to provide culturally sensitive services to girls, boys, women, and men.GHIV Africa will follow its policies on child safeguarding and protection from sexual abuse and exploitation (PSEA) in line with International best practices. These policies set out the importance and requirements for the good conduct of staff and establishes a systematic obligation for reporting any behavior that contravenes it. More specifically, teachers and education will be trained on prevention of violence against children, positive discipline, PSEA, Psychosocial support and Psychological First Aid, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS), School GBV concepts and prevention- gender norm, safe identification and referrals, Child protection mainstreaming, child protection minimum standards, teachers code of conduct, life skills training and safe school.As previously mentioned, GHIV will put in place an Accountability to Affected Persons framework. Through this framework, community members will have the opportunity determine the appropriate and relevant mechanisms for feedback, participation, complaints, information sharing, and monitoring based on the context and to use them when necessary.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Participation of beneficiaries is rooted in GHIV Africa methodology of implementation and as such beneficiaries will be involved at all stages of the project. Beneficiaries will be deeply involved in the project implementation in order to enhance the ownership of the project, strengthen the community-based mechanisms, and reinforce sustainability. In this frame, volunteers will be identified amongst the community in consultation with SAME and/or the community leaders. Volunteers will be trained on the efficient implementation of the activities and their capacity will be enhanced to become agents of change amongst their peers. In addition, GHIV Africa will provide local structures composed of community members with trainings and on-the-job coaching to reinforce their capacity and autonomy.In this frame, SBMCs from the TLS and formal schools will be supported to implement their work plan and to share good practices through joint monthly meetings. In addition, children will take a crucial place in the implementation of the activities and the dissemination of behavioral changes. As such children clubs will be established and/or reinforced in which girls and boys will propose interventions to reduce hygiene issues they face in their environment.
