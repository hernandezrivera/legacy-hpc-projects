Project Summary

Under this project, the International Rescue Committee (IRC) proposes to improve access to safe water and sanitation facilities and promote good hygiene practices in an effort to improve the health status of vulnerable communities that are at risk to drought and disease –outbreaks in target locations of Sanaag, Nugaal, Mudug and Bari regions of Somalia. Limited levels of access to safe water and sanitation facilities, and with poor compliance of hygiene practices put both IDPs and host communities at risk to water –borne diseases. The proposed WASH interventions will address life-saving needs and reduce the burden of WASH- related mortality and morbidity. The IRC will undertake key activities including, rehabilitating and solarizing existing but non-functional water sources with potential for high output, in areas of high numbers of IDPs, including remote and hard-to-reach locations affected by the drought. Promotion of household water treatment and safe storage (HWTS) will be carried put to improve the quality of drinking water and reduce waterborne disease through the use of various available, simple, cost-effective and context-appropriate treatment technologies that improve water quality at the point of use. Furthermore, will construct/rehabilitate latrines with a focus on IDPs and ensure access to essential WASH services in health facilities to contribute to health and nutritional outcomes. In addition, the IRC will carry out hygiene and sanitation promotion and distribute household basic hygiene kits including appropriate material for menstrual management. The proposed response will be delivered under a strong protection lens, which involve efforts to ensure latrines are gender separated, provided with lockable doors, have sufficient lighting, and the distance to water points is reduced to within the agreed standards. The project will also take into account, the impact of the conflict on women and children in terms of access to WASH services and the special needs of women and girls of menstrual age. The proposed actions will address and prevent risks to gender-based violence and thus contribute to greater gender equality.

Project Activities

Needs Conflict, insecurity, drought, and famine have defined Somalia throughout more than two decades. Currently, about 2.9 million people need urgent WASH support, among the affected being IDPs and rural communities that are at risk of drought and disease –outbreaks. Below-average rains in 2018 resulted in drier conditions in 2019 while GU rains (May- June) started late and were erratic in most of the country. Furthermore, in drought-affected regions, water scarcity and inadequate quality are the leading causes of displacement and conflict, particularly in Puntland and Galmudug where 69 % and 35% of households need more than 30 minutes to reach their main drinking water points. Field reports suggest 40% of existing water sources are non-functional due to over-use, weak management, and high operation and maintenance costs forcing IDPs and host communities to buy water at prohibitive costs. In addition, 85% of existing pumping systems are based on diesel motor pumps which show high recurrent costs and are often overdesigned leading to overexploitation of aquifers and the cost of fuel is often a financial barrier to ensure access for poorest households to a reliable water supply. More than half of the population lack access to adequate sanitation. Latrine safety is worryingly high in all parts of Somalia, with the situation particularly critical (below 25%) for IDP settlements resulting users being at high risks when using facilities. Handwashing with soap at a critical moment is practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability to a functional handwashing point is limited. Important gaps persist throughout Somalia in terms of WASH programming in health facilities with more than 50% of health institutions lack access to basic WASH services impacting health operation and outcomes. Due to the cultural challenges surrounding menstruation, Women and girls face restrict mobility and have limited access to normal activities and services such as schooling or engaging in productive tasks, and are considered impure and unfit when on period. The situation is worsened by the lack of sanitary materials and spaces to help women and girls manage the menstrual discharge with dignity and safety. Project activities 1.1. The IRC will upgrade strategic boreholes by installing solar-powered pumping systems to reduce fuel costs and enhance sustainability (4). Repair broken water sources through minor maintenance activities (10). This activity will also include provision of spare parts. The IRC will also construct elevated water storage tanks, water points,and lay distribution pipelines. WMCs will be trained on operation and maintenance and on cost-recovery mechanisms to maximize sustainability and will be responsible for future maintenance and management of rehabilitated water sources. 1.2. Extension of Pipeline water connections, construction of institutional latrines and water storage tanks in health facilities to increase service provision and uptake (target: 6). 1.3: Provision of HHWT (aqua tabs and filters) for household water treatment targeting 3,000HHs in target locations with a focus on training women and girls who mostly involve in handling water and food at the household level. The IRC will also monitor water quality at tsource and randomly selected households. 1.4: Construction of 50 (25 desludgable and 25 emergency gender-sensitive) latrines with hand wash facilities, lockable from inside and shared among four most vulnerable IDP households. 1.5: Procurement of 120 sanitation kits (comprising of wheelbarrows, shovels and forks) will be distributed to target IDP households and the rural communities. 1.6. Conduct gender-sensitive hygiene promotion campaigns targeting 3,000 households with an emphasize on the participation of women. 1.7. Distribute 3,000 hygiene kits (including sanitary materials) prioritizing most vulnerable households). 1.8. Promotion and creation of awareness in disaster risk reduction

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance-- The project will consult marginalized groups to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for the location and design of WASH facilities. - Use existing feedback mechanisms to receive suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, and promote beneficiary empowerment.- The project will strengthen the participation of women and girls in terms of the decision –making while separate group discussions will be conducted to deliberate women’s issues since they are often excluded from spaces where information is exchanged or discussed. - Will ensure genuine representation of all groups in the local representative bodies such as WMCs and CHPVs. - The IRC will assess the power dynamics in target areas and use the generated information to inform monitoring of activities and identifying any barrier to access, discrimination against particular groups. 3a. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Protection concerns stemming from displacement- IRC will provide clear information to the distribution teams and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favors to anyone in exchange for receiving WASH assistance. Ensure that this information is communicated to women and girls.- The IRC will ensure WASH staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries.- The IRC will ensure that beneficiaries know about WASH services, how to obtain hygiene kits and when the distributions will take place, what they will get, how long it should last and how to use the items.- The IRC establishes mechanisms to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with a disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time.- The IRC will transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them.3c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. - The IRC will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the water points, latrines, and distribution sites are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups.- Distribution of hygiene kits will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall to avert possible gender-based violence and rape. - Will ensure that the hygiene kit size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them. - Will keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use – applying effective data protection measures.

Please provide further explanation

The IRC is committed to a gender-sensitive approach throughout the WASH programming which recognizes and addresses the different skill sets, capacities, needs, responsibilities, and vulnerabilities of affected women, girls, boys and men. The IRC will comply with the IASC Guidelines for Gender-based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Settings programming. Thereby, before construction of WASH infrastructure such as water sources, latrines and extension of pipelines. IRC will consult with men and women when identifying siting to ensure that any concerns related to utilization of such infrastructures are considered to maximize the expected impact. In addition, IRC will also ensure that all data collected during surveys, assessments, evaluation and regular reporting is disaggregated by age, sex and residency status where applicable to monitor that IRC’s services are equally responsive to the needs of different groups. In the proposed project, IRC considered the fact that fetching water and taking care of the hygiene and sanitation of the whole family are traditionally the tasks of women and girls therefore, the proposed project will increase access to improved water sources, which will particularly benefit women and girls who are typically responsible for water collection. IRC will ensure that all demographic sectors within the community are involved in determining the location of rehabilitated/upgraded water points, distribution mechanisms, and management for those points to ensure women and girls have safe access to water. IRC will involve men and women equally in the hygiene promotion strategies and community forums to counter the notion that hygiene is only a female issue.
