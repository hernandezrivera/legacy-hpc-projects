Project Summary

The project tile “Support for the prevention and treatment of malnutrition for girls and boys aged 6 to 59 months in the Far North Regions, Cameroon” is proposed as response to the nutritional situation of the Far North Region where the GAM rate is 5.2% and SAM 1.4% alert threshold (SMART 2019). Firstly to provide a package of specific nutrition interventions such as: food supplementation, micronutrient fortification, breastfeeding practices, complementary feeding and treatment of global acute malnutrition. Secondly by nutrition sensitive interventions such as: food security, agriculture, WASH, child protection, basic health care services and family planning… Among all preventive interventions in health and nutrition, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) has a great potential in child survival with an average investment of USD 175 for each life protected. Positive Deviance Household (PDH) is a community approach based on treatment of MAM (household rehabilitation) through local foods and improve food security through the support of beneficiaries on the establishment of home gardens to make vegetables and fruits available in order to better diversify their diet. Support the beneficiaries on the breeding of small cattle and IGA for their empowerment. Despite all these interventions, the problem of malnutrition is not only due to the lack of intervention or food scare in the region, it is also a matters of foot habits and behavior hence the importance of implementing all these preventive actions through social base communication change (SBCC) community-based treatment of malnutrition.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since 2013, Cameroon has been affected by attacks perpetrated by armed groups. In Far North Region, Recurring attacks and threats continue to cause displacement, thousands of people have been displaced .The Region has approximately 321,886 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), 48,769 Refugees outside the camp 123,489 of which 49.3% are girls and women, 50.7% are boys and men, with 41% girls and boys under 05 years old and 49.3% % are women who represent the most vulnerable groups, (OIM, 202019) minors between 0 and 5 years old who are the target of the project The results the nutrition survey (SMART method) conducted in November 2019 by UNICEF and government showed a GAM rate of 5.2% with 1.4% of SAM. For chronic malnutrition, the Far North had the highest prevalence with 38.2%. In addition, a MUAC screening carried out in August 2020, on a sample of 1,601 children aged 6-59 months during the survey revealed that the proportion of MAG is 6.0%. These results indicate that the nutritional situation in the region remains precarious. The proportion of SAM children was 2.4%; that of MAM was 3.6%. The departments of Logone and Chari and Mayo Sava were the most affected.In 2020, that more than 230,000 people, mainly girls and boys under the age of 5 and pregnant or lactating women (FEFA), will be vulnerable in the Far North while important interventions (HNO report).According the HNO 2020, a minimum of 125,000 people will suffer from global acute malnutrition (GAM), including 20,000 pregnant and lactating women and 105,000 girls and boys under the age of five need humanitarian assistance in Nutrition. Most of this population exposed to the lack of access to drinking water, poor access to health care, hygiene problems, epidemics, and the consequences of poor harvests which can lead to rapid deterioration of their nutritional status. Regarding the health situation, across the region, 43.6% of children show signs of malaria and 41.3% of diarrhea. The indicators related to infant and young child feeding practices are among the poorest in the country, with an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 30.8%, a timely introduction rate for complementary foods of 33.3%. The proportion of children aged 6 to 59 months with minimum dietary diversity in the region is 33%.In the Far North, about one-fifth of households headed by women are food insecure, respectively 22% and 20% of households, against 9% and 14% of those headed by men.Food security is undermined by conflicts: productive activities are disrupted, food stores are destroyed or looted, livestock are stolen, access to markets is reduced and prices increase…Reduced access to food results in changes in feeding practices (in particular, reduced consumption of vegetables, fruits and animal-source foods), food preparation and food allocation within households. Respectively 29% and 25% of households have poor or limited food consumption in the Far North.In addition the COVID-19 pandemic has contributed to exacerbating the vulnerability of the Region in addition to the unstable security situation, thus reducing access to food, and health care services. Since the appearance of the virus in Cameroon in March 2020, shortages of basic necessities (food, water, medicines, hygiene kits) have been observed in several localities the prices of these products increased in 56 percent of the localities assessed, mainly in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga (DTM June 2020). These shortages are partly explained by the travel restriction measures decided by the Government from March 18 to deal with the spread of COVID-19, leading to the breakdown of certain supply chains and therefore the shortage and the rising prices of certain commodities.
