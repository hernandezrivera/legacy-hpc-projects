Project Summary

The project will run for a period nine of months and will address the critical needs of vulnerable groups in the planned woreda, mainly IDPs and conflict-affected host communities. The project will support 18,599 IDPs and 12,816 hosting communities in Wash and protection interventions with a total budget of 573,280.44 dollars. 83 percent of the budget is allocated for WASH and 17% Percent for protection. The project's main tasks include providing access to clean water and critical protection services. Water trucking and water sources rehabilitation will be done for a sustainable solution. The project will Rehabilitate five shallow wells equipped with a hand pump, conduct water trucking through emergency live-saving and construction of twenty blocks of communal semi-permanent latrines, distribute WASH NFIs, promote sanitation and hygiene, establish and train WASHCOs, latrine cleaning committees and provide kits to strengthen the prevention of COVID-19 measures. Provide child protection services through case management, family tracing and reunification, alternative care arrangements, and individual PSS; Strengthen Child Protection and GBV Committees by providing training and capacity building support for child protection, case identification, and referral; Provide structured parenting support and parenting skills to parents.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Implementing the project in Sustainable manner is an important element of all OWDA's interventions. OWDA is consistently building capacity of local COMMUNITY as well as its government partners. Cooperation and participation with IDPs, HCs and local government at every stage of the proposed project will be key to sustainability and handover. Furthermore, capacity building and focus on resilience of the IDPs and host communities vis-a-vis future shocks lies in the heart of the intervention. OWDA has long-term and dedicated project and support teams with extensive institutional knowledge and technical expertise. Their capacities to deliver quality interventions are constantly increased through staff capacity building. All OWDA interventions will be carried out through participatory methods, capitalizing existing capacities and structures in order to ensure community ownership of the proposed action. Capacity building of the target communities will be carried out through complex and continuous training and coaching of hygiene promoters, WASHCOs and outreach workers as well as mass education in hygiene, training of IDPs. Those trained and will continue transmitting messages addressing issues of safe water management, sanitation, hygiene and GBV in the IDP sites and in the HCs (Host communities) respectively. To the extent possible (and culturally appropriate), women, men, girls and boys will be involved in all the above-mentioned structures. Special focus will be paid to the influential community members with potential to fuel and maintain the behaviour changes related to WASH and GBV.The project is designed to work with and support existing government structures that are ultimately responsible for IDP protection and host community development. The partners work in close cooperation with responsible government agencies. In the target sites WASHCOs will be engaged. In the HCs WASHCOs, health extension workers, service providers and local leaders will be engaged in the project. On completion, the institutional sanitation facilities in the HCs and sanitation facilities in the camp, including all plans and designs, will be handed over to relevant government agencies and committees. Before handing over the projects, a training session will be held with government officials in the HCs and WASHCOs in the sites, discussing the designs and maintenance. Quality sanitation and hygiene materials with a long lifespan will be provided.
