Project Summary

• The Inter-Agency Accountability Working Group-Ethiopia (IAAWG-E) is a collaborative network primarily comprised of agencies operating in Ethiopia who aim to adhere to the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Sphere Project, ISAC and the Humanitarian Partnership Standards (HSP) as a whole. The network was established in Addis Ababa in 2009.
• In 2021, IAAWG-E under the leadership of IOM undertook various consultations, including surveys, mapping of partners, capacity building over 200 focal points of members of IAAWG-E, ensuring dedicated funding for AAP, and integration of AAP in overall humanitarian response, as AAP now has a dedicated section in HNO and HRP.
• Field monitoring results and community consultations revealed that safe access to feedback and complaint mechanisms is a challenge for many affected populations by the crisis, i.e., Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), host communities and migrant returnees. Responsiveness of the feedback and complaint mechanisms has also been a point of discussion in humanitarian agencies. Initially, the effort to establish safe and accessible feedback and complaint mechanism which is tailored to the preference of the affected communities’ context is very limited in most humanitarian agencies. Secondly, in most cases, the established feedback and complaint mechanisms are not based on the results of the consultation of the affected communities and are thereby not utilized by the affected communities. Subsequently, even after the feedback and complaint mechanisms are established, they are either less or not responsive to the concerns of the affected communities. Finally, the lesson learning from the feedback and complaints that we collect is very limited.
• So, this project aims to improve the safe access and responsiveness of the feedback and complaint mechanisms of members of the Inter-Agency Working Group-Ethiopia (IAAWG-E) working in humanitarian response in Oromia, Amhara, Afar, Somali and SNNP Regions. The project also will promote collective community voices by integrating AAP into other humanitarian response communication and community engagement activities through capacity building. The project will also improve the visibility of decision-makers of the community feedback trends. In summary, the aim of the project is to improve the quality of humanitarian response in communities affected by the disaster and conflict to enhance the accountability of humanitarian action to crisis-affected people.
• To this end, the project will support the IAAWG-E in establishing and strengthening sub-regional AAP working groups in Amhara, Afar, Somali, Oromia and SNNNP where there has been an active humanitarian response since 2020.
• The project will assess the safe access and responsiveness of IAAWG-E members' feedback and complaint mechanisms in selective emergency response areas and suggest an improvement plan, and follow and record improvements through the regional AAP sub-working groups.
• The project will build IAAWG-E member staff's capacity on accountability to the affected population. One full day of AAP training tailored to the contexts on the ground informed by the results of the community consultation and assessment of humanitarian agencies’ feedback and complaint mechanism will be organized at the regional level involving all humanitarian agencies.
• The project will also assess the community’s information needs and preferences (collating and aggregating existing assessments already done by IOM and IAAWG-E members), and then design, produce and distribute information, education and communication materials about key humanitarian services and agencies providing them, and the available feedback and complaint mechanisms.
• The project will also work with other protection clusters working group partners such as PSEA and SGBV in creating and promoting safe access to feedback and complaint mechanisms for the most vulnerable population groups, women, children and elderlies.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The IAAWG-E member organizations are working towards strengthening Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) throughout its humanitarian responses in all regions of the country. The IAAWG-E's accountability to the affected population (AAP) efforts is guided by the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (ISAC) five AAP operational commitments and objective-Leadership, Information-Sharing and Transparency, Participation, Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms and Partner Coordination that were adopted in 2020. IOM Ethiopia as the Co-Chair of the Inter-Agency Accountability Working Group (IAAWG-E), thus will coordinate and facilitate the promotion and mainstreaming of AAP practices and approaches within the wider humanitarian community in the country in 2022. In this project, IAAWG-E Co-Chaired by IOM aims to strengthen and mainstream AAP activities in humanitarian response in six (6) regions including drought-affected ones. This coordinated effort of the IAAWG-E members will minimise duplication of efforts and maximize humanitarian response effectiveness and improve coordinated assistance through the learning. The project will also promote active participation of the key local civil society organizations (CSOs), and local and community leaders during the project's initial community consultation and assessment exercises to understand gaps in the existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, how communities prefer to receive and access to humanitarian life-saving information, and how they better provide feedback and raise complaints about staff and humanitarian services. IDP committees, women and youth associations, religious leaders, and local government structures will actively participate in need assessment and community consultations during the project inception phases. The project will also promote active participation of the key AAP actors during the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project activities. The project will also build the capacity building of the relevant local and national actors on accountability to the affected population to promote AAP principles and approaches. This approach of ‘community complaints committees’ has already been implemented by members of IAAWG-E, such as World Vision Ethiopia, IOM, Action Against Hunger, and Care Ethiopia and the learnings will inform replicating this practice within this project could improve the quality of the humanitarian service. The civil society organizations and local government structures will also in turn support creating awareness among the wider community about the key humanitarian services and the available feedback and complaint mechanism. IDP committees, community leaders, and women and youth associations will also serve as a channel to receive and manage feedback and complaints from communities. These actors also actively participate in setting project targets, target verification and project implementation activities that can, in turn, support the effort of the humanitarian actors in reaching the most vulnerable and affected communities by the crisis. The project will also involve the relevant actors in a periodical/quarterly joint field monitoring visit to review the effectiveness and responsiveness of the information being shared with communities and the available feedback and complaint mechanisms. The relevant actors will also support the dissemination of lessons learned that the project gained from the monitoring visit and reviewing workshops.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

IAAWG-E will assess the context and support members of IAAWG-E to establish context tailored feedback and complaint mechanism that considers the safety and accessibility concerns of the most vulnerable affected people by the crisis. The project will assess and consider the needs and preferences of girls, boys, women, men, elderlies, and persons with disabilities how they would like to provide their feedback and raise their concerns about the humanitarian response activities and staff. The project will recommend multi-entry feedback and complaint channels such as hotline phone, suggestion boxes (in school and public service areas), focus group discussions, one-to-one discussions, community representatives, dedicated established help desks, hotline emails and other preferred and trusted channels that will be safe, accessible and appropriate to the preference of the communities that the project aims to assist. The project will develop SOPs to guide the confidential management of both programmatic and sensitive feedback and complaints received from the communities. The project will also build the capacity of the community on how to access the established feedback and complaint mechanism, receive and manage feedback and complaints, and participate in reviewing the established feedback and complaint channels to improve the confidentiality and privacy of those who raise their concern.
