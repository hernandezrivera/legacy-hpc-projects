Project Summary

Overall Project Objective is to ensure timely emergency and sustainable WASH response to reduce mortality and morbidity and prevent disease to vulnerable, conflict or natural disaster affected communities of Central and West Darfur.
In the current Darfur context characterized by a complex, protracted and forgotten crisis, TGH designed a WASH intervention through the lens of the emergency- development humanitarian nexus. The project therefore includes:
- Installation and running of Emergency Water System whenever necessary (conflict or disaster related population displacement)
-Rehabilitations aiming to facilitate the transition from emergency to more durable water supply services as well as to expand and improve access to protected water sources in returnee and IDP settlements where access to safe water is below the emergency standard of 7,5 L/p/d
- Community capacity building on water supply management and cost-recovery system in order to empower community stakeholders and ensure sustainability of the water points
- Prepositioning of EPR WASH buffer stocks to mitigate disaster risks (water systems, water treatment kits, latrines, etc.)
- Sensitization on Hygiene Promotion through HKPs in order to mitigate consequences of bad hygiene behavior
- Distribution of WASH items to people with special needs and dignity kits for women and girls
TGH will pay particular attention in including women in the water committees as they are mostly the ones fetching the water they have key inputs in that matter. TGH will also ensure that at least 50% of the trained HKPs are women in order to ensure women and girls are directly targeted by hygiene promotion events.
This project will target 135,463 individuals in Central Darfur (Um Dukhun, Golo, Bindizi and Mukjar localities) and 72,577 individuals in West Darfur (Beida, Kreinik and Geneina localities).

1 - Needs

Increased return dynamics from Chad to Darfur in a context of regular new displacements provoked by local conflicts and protracted situations call for an external support to provide lifesaving services to returnees, IDPs and host communities. Chronic deficiency of basic services is aggravated by the increased pressure on natural resources induced by new arrivals of population and the increase in prices of items of first necessity due to the current economic crisis. Return dynamic is expected to continue and to potentially increase in Central and West Darfur while new population displacements are expected. This needs assessment is based on TGH WASH assessments conducted from September to December 2019 in Um Dukhun, Bindizi/Mukjar and Golo localities (Central Darfur), and Geneina, Kreinik, Beida (West Darfur) as well as field observations collected during TGH’s WASH current projects (both in WD and CD). With regards to the current economic crisis, the returns dynamics from Chad and intra-Darfur, as well as the clashes that occurred frequently in Jebel Mara area and the natural disasters, such as floods, it seems unlikely that Darfur context will improve in 2020. Therefore, a life-saving and early recovery intervention remains necessary. Needs for the prepositioning of buffer stocks in the main areas are important considering the above mentioned risks and the logistics constraints in Darfur that obstruct the timely delivery of aid if not properly anticipated. The main findings of the observations and assessments conducted by TGH team (i.e. needs in latrines, hygiene and sanitation sensitization due to high rate of open defecation; emergency and durable water points) underlined the necessity of maintaining hygiene promotion activities and water access services where TGH is already operating and implementing emergency systems where required. Moreover, capacity building activities are strongly required in order to reinforce the resilience of the population to respond to external shocks. The localities targeted by this project belong to the priority list established by the sector for 2020. Indeed, Beida (West Darfur) falls under Priority 1, Um Dukhun (Central Darfur) under Priority 2, Kreinik (west Darfur), Golo (Central Darfur) and Bindizi (Central Darfur) under Priority 3.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

TGH strategy aims at improving the sustainability of the water points constructed and/or rehabilitated. Therefore, TGH dismantles EWS (that works with fuel) to install more durable water sources, such as hand pumps or water yards, when possible. However, TGH installs when possible solar hybrid pumping systems that are more environment-friendly. Moreover, TGH implements sanitation activities, such as garbage collection system in Kreinik locality. While implementing this activity, messages on environment protection are spread among the communities. During the hygiene promotion sessions, cleaning sessions (at market places, within the schools) are also organized to raise awareness on environmental sanitation.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Throughout the project, TGH will pay attention to protection mainstreaming regarding women and girls. Gender-sensitive latrines will be constructed at market places and in the schools targeted by the project. Construction of gender-separated latrines will benefit to girls’ school enrollment and limit protection risks. TGH will pay a close attention to the participation of women within this intervention, especially within the water committees and within the HKPs selected. Specific dignity kits addressing the needs of vulnerable girl adolescents and women will be prepositioned within the buffer stock.Furthermore, people with disabilities will also be taken into account while implementing this project. One latrine per block constructed or rehabilitated will have a specific design to facilitate its use by people with disabilities and their inclusion within the education system will be tackled during teachers training and awareness campaigns.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

TGH puts in place mechanisms to enable beneficiaries and affected populations to provide feedback and complaints. At the beginning of the project implementation, TGH will organize a meeting with leaders and other relevant stakeholders to present the activities and explain that community complaints and advices are necessary to the action’s improvement. TGH will present to the beneficiaries the different ways to communicate their issues (special complains during distribution, direct contact with TGH staff, visit to TGH office, etc.). When a complaint is addressed to TGH, it is acknowledged by the sector managers who provide the necessary information to the affected population to ensure their comprehension of TGH operations, to address answers to beneficiaries concerns and complaints prioritization criteria and operational or budget limitations. According to the relevance of the concerns addressed by the community to the Base Managers and Program team in the field, these can also be analyzed in further details and considered in activities planning. While explaining the complaint process, TGH team will mention GBV and SEA and highlight the importance to raise any concerns regarding these topics. Transparency is ensured through adequate information sharing and communication to beneficiaries and affected populations have already been mentioned above and include meetings and specific complaint mechanisms and responses with affected populations, Sheikhs or other community leaders and more importantly, availability of TGH staff.
