Project Summary

Conflict-induced displacement has been common in Wollega and Kemashi Zones in the past four years. Reports indicate that at least 11 new woredas are added to IDP hosting lists making a total of 24 hosting woredas in East and West Wollega. Despite the continual displacements in these areas, responses are still minimal due to lack of partners, funding and security issues as indicated in the December response plan on Wollega and Kemashi Zones. For the most part, there is a high caseload of IDPs in Sasiga woreda where there is a high need and response gap. The IDPs are living dispersed in the host communities in different sets of clusters namely Tsige , Galo , Balo , Bareda and Anger kebeles/clusters. Reports show that there are more than 21,000 IDP caseloads in this particular woreda. These IDPs are living in the host community dependent upon the shelter provided by their hosts. Lack of basic household items such as cooking materials, water reservoirs and bedding materials are the frequently reported challenges. Due to a lack of job opportunities IDP’s can’t afford to buy basic household materials. These IDPs live congested in rented houses of the host community sharing the house expenses amongst themselves. These rented houses usually accommodate more than 4 HHs in which monthly payments from 600-800 Birr is expected depending upon the standard of the house. In West wollega zone, two of the prioritized locations Kiltu Kara and Mana Sibu woreda ; have also been identified to have high IDP caseloads. Reports show that there are about 20,105 IDPs and 14,962 IDPs in Kiltu Kara and Mana Sibu woreda respectively and there is a high need for interventions in these woredas as the living condition of the IDPs remains dire. IDPs are living in rented houses in the host community living at a price range from 800-1000 birr monthly. The living condition of the IDPs is distressing in that most can’t afford the rental price because they are not engaged in income-generating activities. Hence, due to the alarming needs of the IDPs in these areas, FIDO is proposing to provide Cash for rent assistance to 2,874 IDP HHs and NFI assistance to 507 IDP HHs living in Sasiga Woreda, of East Wollega Zone and Cash for rent assistance for 2,720 IDP HHs Kiltu Kara and 3,454 IDP HHs for Mana Sibu Woredas of West Wollega Zone, Oromia Region. The project will respond to immediate critical needs identified in Shelter & NFI sector in these two areas in line with the guideline provided by the Ethiopia Shelter cluster. The proposed project will ensure disability inclusion in that it is designed to provide assistance to HHs of family members with disability from 15% of the total intervention. It also targets 15% of the host community in an aim to maintain social cohesion. The cluster has carried out the prioritization of severity based on accessible locations, number of IDP case loads, and interventions thereby the above three woredas are prioritized in according to the ESNFI cluster. The project is designed to also addresses cross-cutting themes PSEA, gender, and accountability to the affected population.

1. - Projects activities

FIDO will first conduct a market assessment on the target areas for the availability of local markets for the NFI kits. FIDO will also conduct an assessment on the appropriateness of cash for rent intervention response for Sasiga, Mana Sibu and Kiltu Kara Woredas by performing rental market survey. The beneficiary selection and complaint handling committee will be formed and be responsible for beneficiary identification and registration according to selection criteria; female and child-headed HHs, Elderly headed HHs, HHs with a disability or chronically ill members, HHs with low income, HHs who didn’t receive any ESNFI support from other partners or government. These same committee members will involve in the distribution process. FIDO will adopt and utilize the beneficiary scorecard and Washington group data tool for the verification process. The verification process will be independently done by FIDO with the use of community volunteers. Procurement and distribution of NFI item kits (V2) for 431 IDP HHs and NFI kits (V2) _ disable for 76 IDP HHs with family members with disability living in Sasiga woreda, East wollega zone. BID process for procurement of the kits will involve both Addis Ababa and local markets. FIDO will motivate local vendors to support the local market. The value for standard package for NFI (V2) and NFI (V2) _ disable kit is 108 USD and 131 USD respectively. Provision of information on PSEA, accountability and COVID-19 mitigation measures using IEC/BCC materials for beneficiaries during distribution process. Following distribution, FIDO will perform post-distribution monitoring after 2 weeks of distribution to get beneficiary feedback on the activities, satisfaction, and utilization. Enumerators will be trained prior to conducting data collection. Participatory tools such as KII and FGDs will be employed to conduct data collection for the selected samples. Monitoring and Evaluation will also be conducted including local government stakeholders at woreda and zonal level, FIDO project staffs and sub-national shelter cluster lead. FIDO will respond through cash for rent intervention for 2,874 IDP HHs in Sasiga woreda of East Wollega Zone, 2,720 IDP HHs in Kiltu Kara, and 3,454 IDP HHs in Mana Sibu Woreda of West Wollega zone. Cash for rent assistance amount will be decided together with the local governmental stakeholders in a way that it doesn’t increase the social tension and destabilize the local rental market. Beneficiaries for assistance will be targeted based on a selection criteria but priority will be given to HHs in overcrowded shelters and HHs that are homeless.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

FIDO has well-structured program execution and monitoring mechanisms at different structural levels which is designed in a way that enhances its efficiency and effectiveness in responding to the emergency needs of the community. FIDO’s presence as operational in the proposed areas comprises professionals which are supported by the zonal cluster leads and regional leadership. FIDO has implemented RRF funded shelter project in Sasiga woreda for 2,000 IDP HHs and WASH project in Kiltu Kara and Mana Sibu Woredas. It also has an ongoing EHF funded shelter project in sasiga woreda for 900 IDP HHs in ESNFI assistance. The regional and zonal leads also work in harmony with the technical leads and units at the head office. This project will apply participatory monitoring approaches that engage all concerned bodies and beneficiary representatives. The process will engage the beneficiary representatives and local authorities from the onset of the project with special emphasis on screening. Joint monitoring with the involvement of ESNFI shelter cluster, progress and performance will be tracked and a range of data collection methods of both qualitative and quantitative will be applied. A comprehensive monitoring framework will be developed at the start of the project to ensure the existence of a well-planned monitoring schedule, partner’s involvement, required resources allocation, and supportive tools for data collection and analysis prepared.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Initially the project is designed based on the identified gaps, and as any other projects, FIDO will create an effective communication channels between the government stakeholders and the affected population prior to the implementation of any of the project activity. FIDO ensures the participation of community members throughout the life cycle of the project. It will establish clear mechanism that allows the community members to provide feedback on the project implementation. The mechanism will ensure comprehensive and confidential system set up. Site and beneficiaries selection will be take place after consultation meetings held with government stakeholders, cluster representatives and representatives of the affected population. Different meetings will be held with community members to provide information and discuss on planned interventions from beneficiary targeting, verification, prioritization, distribution process, to informing the rights and entitlements of recipients. FIDO works to integrate inclusion of people with disability in all cycles of the project. It will ensure the participation of this groups in beneficiary selection committees, complaint handling committees and monitoring framework. It will also organize consultation on planned projects with people with disabilities and understand the barrier to access to assistance. Feedback mechanisms will be designed to adequately record issues faced by persons with disabilities and staff is empowered to act on these suggestions. Information sharing and awareness creation about accountability to affected population will be held during distribution by using different printed IEC/BCC materials. Complaint handling and Beneficiary selection committee along with responsible government stakeholders and FIDO project staffs will involve handling any concerns or complaints that arises from the beneficiary and act accordingly. The committee will be comprised of women, people with disabilities, elderlies to ensure maximum community participation. Aside from the committee, FIDO will also set up alternate ways for feedback and complaint mechanism to promptly respond to concerns of beneficiary and adjust implementation. Mechanisms to receive this complaint will consider the preferred mechanism of the community but will mainly use Help desk, community feedback suggestion boxes, provision of local stakeholders of contacts of FIDO staffs and selected focal representative of the community. FIDO will use the standard checklist on incorporating accountability to affected population in the humanitarian programme cycle developed by the Global protection cluster to ensure that AAP is integrated into all cycles of the project. FIDO project staffs and community volunteers will be trained on humanitarian accountability and its importance to mainstream them on ground in every level of the project. FIDO will also ensure the collection and utilization of sex and age dis-aggregated data that adheres to recommended age groupings
