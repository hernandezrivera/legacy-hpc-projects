Project Summary

Universal Network for Knowledge & Empowerment Agency (UNKEA) will conduct lifesaving WASH activities, integrated with nutrition, GBV prevention and Health. The project aims to increase access to clean and safe water, sanitation and improve hygiene for vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host communities in Ulang, and Fashoda Counties of Upper Nile State which are severely affected by flooding and inter-communal violence/cattle raiding, and high influx of returnees who are in IDP locations. The project targets 15,000 direct beneficiaries who will be drawn from host communities, IDPs, and returnees 10% of the project beneficiaries will be people with disabilities and 7% will be the elderly. The project target direct beneficiaries are 15,000 that include 2,850 children (1368 Boys and 1482 Girls) and 12,150 Adults (5832 Men & 6,318 Women).
The main activities will include: 1) Project launch. UNKEA will conduct several inception meetings with project staff, local authorities, key stakeholders, and community representatives. 2) Establishment of Project Management Committees (PMCs). 3) Establishment of mobile sanitation team or controlled defecation field/trenches. 4) Beneficiary Selection. 5) Construction of flood-resilient water and sanitation infrastructures. 6) Distribution of water treatment tablets and water storage jerry cans. UNKEA will distribute water treatment tablets and water storage jerry cans to vulnerable households. 7) The distribution of WASH kits. 8) Construction and rehabilitation of nutrition facilities. 9) Conduct mass campaign on hygiene messaging, prioritizing handwashing, and other hygiene basic messages. 10) Conduct safety audits. UNKEA will periodically conduct safety audits to help in developing an action plan to mitigate WASH-related GBV that may a raised during project implementation period. 11) Monitoring, Evaluation and Reporting. UNKEA will conduct regular evaluation and monitoring activities to obtain information on project progress and assess whether project objectives are being met.

1. Needs

The humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 9.4 million people need humanitarian assistance in 2023, of which an estimated 6.1 million of them (65%) need water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services (HNO, 2023). The estimated 6.1 million people who need WASH services, 24% are women, 54% are children, and 15% are people with disabilities. Severe flooding and inter-communal violence/cattle raiding are the main factors behind this critical humanitarian situation in the country. Many people are exposed to WASH related diseases such acute watery diarrhoea, cholera, EVD and Hepatitis E due to floods, drought, water scarcity and dryness. In most cases, majority of the households travel long distances (more than 30 minutes) to access any type of water, an issue that is challenging especially to people with disabilities and increases the risk of women and girls facing gender-based violence such as rape and forced marriage. Most of the counties do not have sufficient water and sanitation infrastructure with most of the households relying on surface water as their main water source. Surface water is often untreated before consumption, which along with the widespread practice of open defecation heightens the risk of contracting water-borne, water-washed, and water-related diseases especially in the rainy season when latrine pits seep into waterways. Access to sanitation facilities across South Sudan is very limited: over 80% of households do not have access to latrines thus practice the open defecation method. In some states such as Unity, Jonglei, and Upper Nile, the number of households with access to latrines are less 12% (IRNA, 2022). Lack of access to latrines is associated to various factors such as cultural and societal attitudes especially in states with a larger rural and/or pastoral population who considered use of latrines as taboo. In some locations, constructed latrines are hardly used due to societal pressure, with people preferring instead to walk a great distance even at night to practice open defecation. Moreover, floods regularly damage existing sanitation infrastructure and given their annual occurrence and paired with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by communities to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities. Increasing insecurity, both ethnic and politically motivated conflict, coupled with localized violence and disputes over land, has been an underlying driver of WASH conditions in almost all states, limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services while also restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure.Lack of access to water treatment tablets, storage jerry cans, hygiene items and messaging is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Overall, only 16 per cent of the households reported owning soap and this figure was as low as 4 per cent and 11 per cent in Jonglei and Upper Nile States respectively (HNO, 2022). Limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, for instance, water points and latrines has carry-on impacts when utilizing a multi-sectoral view. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition. In Upper Nile especially in Fashoda and Ulang, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence for children 6-59 months was 26.4%. These 2 counties have the lowest immunization coverage in the state. The outcomes of these health and nutrition indicators are worsened by the poor WASH practices. The presence of water-borne diseases esp. diarrhoea as a common ailment in children under 5, is also indicative of poor WASH conditions. 2. Project activities

The main activities for this proposed WASH project shall include:i) Project launch. UNKEA will conduct several inception meetings with project staff, local authorities, key stakeholders and community representatives to introduce the project and invite these stakeholders’ input in project design, delivery, and evaluation.ii) Establishment of Project Management Committees (PMCs) The PMCs will be tasked with identification of Bomas with the worst WASH conditions for humanitarian assistance, beneficiary selection to ensure that the project reaches the most vulnerable and deserving persons in the communities, and monitoring of project. iii) Establishment of mobile sanitation team or controlled defecation field/trenches. The team will be tasked with protection of the nearby fields that are used by children for defecation both in the day times and at night. iv) Beneficiary Selection. UNKEA with the help of the established PMCs will adopt a participatory, vulnerability-informed, and rights-based approach in identifying, verifying and registering project beneficiaries. UNKEA and the PMCs will define eligibility criteria for beneficiary selection to ensure the most vulnerable women, children, youth, and people with disabilities receive the required WASH services.v) Construction of flood-resilient water and sanitation infrastructures. UNKEA will identify areas where communities where boreholes and latrines are destroyed by floods and constructed the flood-resilient water and sanitation infrastructures.vi) Distribution of water treatment tablets and water storage jerry cans. UNKEA will distribute water treatment tablets and water storage jerry cans to vulnerable households. This will ensure that in case communities draw surface water, they are able to treat it before consumption thus limiting the risk of contracting water-borne diseases. UNKEA will provide training to the households on how to use the water treatment tablets and on good water storage practices. vii) The distribution of WASH kits. UNKEA will provide the selected beneficiaries with WASH non-food items such as soap and buckets to enhance their WASH conditions. UNKEA will also provide women and adolescent girls with menstrual hygiene kits.viii) Construction and rehabilitation of nutrition facilities. UNKEA will engage youth in the construction of pit latrines at nutrition facilities as well as provision of water treatment tablets to facilities for safe access to water and adequate sanitation. ix) Conduct mass campaign on hygiene messaging, prioritizing handwashing, and other hygiene basic messages. UNKEA will provide training to community WASH stakeholders including water management committees and hygiene promoters before they go out for mass campaign on hygiene messaging. x) Conduct safety audits. UNKEA will periodically conduct safety audits to help in developing an action plan to mitigate WASH-related GBV that may a raised during project implementation period. xi) Monitoring, Evaluation and Reporting UNKEA will conduct regular evaluation and monitoring activities to obtain information on project progress and assess whether project objectives are being met. UNKEA will also conduct post-implementation monitoring after the implementation of each output to ascertain the appropriateness of the achieved response. UNKEA will periodically conduct outcome monitoring to assess the changes experienced by the beneficiaries and communities as a result of the project. Feedback from beneficiaries will also be evaluated to assess whether the project responds to the targeted needs. UNKEA will prepare and submit all the required reports, both internal and donor reports.
