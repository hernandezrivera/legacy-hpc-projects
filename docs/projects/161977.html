Project Summary


Save the Children (SC) will run eight Health Posts and one Primary Health Care Center (PHCC). These health facilities provide an integrated package of health, SRHR, nutrition, MHPSS, and WASH services in line with the Ministry of Health / Health Sector recommended minimum package of essential health services for primary health care facilities in the FDMN camps and are staffed by Doctors, Midwives, Paramedics, MHPSS specialist, Psychosocial Support Officer, Clinic Aids, Maternal Child Health and Nutrition Promoters, and Community Health Workers (CHWs). SC carefully screens vulnerable children and their families for mental health issues, prevalent amongst the targeted population, as it can contribute to deteriorating the physical health of the already vulnerable people . This is done by creating awareness and building the capacity of staff to effectively detect, support and refer those in need and ensure those affected have access to equitable, comprehensive and essential primary health care services. With the operation and management of the 9 health facilities, SC aims to reach 109,301 individuals with consultations, and to provide them the primary health care services listed above. Healthcare providers at supported health facilities will also be re-trained on child protection basic concepts, child safeguarding, child rights, confidentiality and on how to be child/adolescent-friendly providers.

Needs

The key health needs for the Rohingya and host communities are very similar; prevention and treatment of communicable diseases, antenatal and post-natal care services, emergency obstetric care services, reproductive and child health, and Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) related clinical case management. The sexual and reproductive health (SRH) needs for high-risk and special need populations including survivors of gender-based violence and adolescents are yet to be addressed. Married and pregnant adolescent girls still need to be targeted through facility and community support groups to reduce their high risks of maternal mortality and morbidity. There is a need to strengthen sexual and reproductive health care, including access to safe, voluntary family planning and maternal and new-born health services. Gaps still exist in Basic Emergency Maternal, Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) services. Unsafe home delivery practices (about 60% of the total deliveries across the camps) and delay in the uptake of Post Natal Care (PNC) follow-up also put newborns and mothers at risk of avoidable complications. Analysis of data from Save the Children’s facilities shows that up to 10% of newborns present with medical complications during PNC follow up and although no official figures are currently available, it is acknowledged that there is a high rate of early neonatal mortality. Overcrowding, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and practices, and low immunity have increased the risk of disease outbreaks, with children disproportionately affected. Gender based violence (GBV) is under-reported, and existing clinical staff have limited capacity to manage its medical and psychosocial impact. There are currently multiple communicable disease outbreaks within the Rohingya and the host population, confirming the understanding that the current setting is a “perfect storm” for disease outbreaks.

Activities or outputs

Sector Objective 1:a) Continue primary health care services in 8 Health Posts (HPs) and 1 Primary Health Care Center (PHCC) following minimum package of essential services for primary health care facilities in the campsb) SRH services to adolescents from refugee and host communitiesc) Management of post-abortion care (focusing infection prevention and control)d) Continuation of Basic Emergency Maternal, Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) services at PHCCe) Routine vaccination in fixed HPs and PHCC and ensure adequate supply of vaccines f) MHPSS services (like psychosocial first aid for common mental health problems) will be continued in HPs and PHCC by training/refresher training (e.g. mhGAP) of health service providers (Doctors/ Psychosocial Support Officer) and ensure continuous supply of relevant medicines. Additionally, trained CHWs will identify and refer clients to health facilities.g) Follow-up management of non-communicable diseases (like hypertension, diabetes) and referral of suspected cases for diagnosis and treatment of complicated casesh) Cultural sensitivities and gender norms will be maintained in service provision with special attention during MHPSS, SGBV management, SRH and Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services by ensuring appropriate gender-balanced staffing, segregated WASH, consultation, and inpatient facilities. Women and girls will be consulted through community health meetings, committees and focus group discussions to identify barriers to accessing health facilities and specific needs, thus given a voice in the implementation of health services. i) Capacity development of clinical and non-clinical staff for running of 8 health posts and 1 PHCCj) Procurement of pharmaceuticals, medical supplies, equipment and non-medical suppliesk) Support referral mechanisms for patients to access secondary care facilitiesl) Strengthening integration of WASH, Nutrition and MHPSS activities into HPs and PHCCSector Objective 2: a) Prepositioning of trained mobile medical team, supplies and equipmentb) Staff capacity development to ensure effective medical responsec) Maintenance of the Diarrheal Treatment Centre in PHCC/Camp 21 and Oral Rehydration Points (ORP) in HPs to respond to any cholera outbreakd) Participate in deworming, Vitamin A campaign and other campaigns (like MR, OCV) as part of minimize risk of disease epidemics e) Participate in emergency simulation exercises conducted to test readiness of the staff and facilities to respond to a critical eventSector Objective 3: a) Community engagement aligned with the endorsed Community Health Working Group guidelines: recruitment, training and supportive supervision of CHWsb) Training of CHWs on at least 4 core training packagec) Utilize network of trained community health workers for promotion of key MNCH practices, collection of socio-demographic information, disease surveillance and vital statistics etc.Sector Objective 4:a) Strengthen health information management system and regularly feed into Health Sector information databaseb) Continue gender–disaggregated data collection from all service points and analyze to inform any potential barriersc) Introducing individual patient tracking system to ensure continuum of care; start with pregnant womend) Regularly participate in Health Sector, Strategic Advisory Group and sub-group/working group meetings and share project lessons learned as well as contribute to development of strategies as relevante) Accountability to the beneficiaries through utilization of the complaints, feedback and response mechanisms, and the Child Safeguarding Policy
