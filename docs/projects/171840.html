Project Summary

A total of 3864 confirmed COVID-19 cases including 104 deaths reported in Somalia (10 Oct 2020), with the majority from Banadir, Somaliland and Puntland. Since the first case reported, WHO Somalia is supporting the MoH responding to the outbreak through coordination, technical support; procurement & distribution of PPEs, medicine, medical equipment; establishing four PCR laboratories; epidemiological data collection, analysis and regular information sharing for evidence-based decision making to guide the response activities.
Currently the most affected areas by Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD)/cholera include 23 flood affected districts of Hirshabelle, South-West states and Banadir region. Since January 2020, a total of 5,872 AWD/Cholera cases were reported, including 30 deaths (CFR 0.5%) in 23 flood-affected districts. Of the 5,872 cases, 2,584 (44%) are children aged below 2 years while 3,288 (56%) are female and 2,584 (44%) males. In Banadir alone, 3,374 cases, including 19 deaths, have been reported. In Baidoa, a total of 680 cases with 3 deaths have been reported amongst displaced communities since April 2020.
Since onset of the pandemic, EPI program is one of the worst affected among the health programs. There is a drop both in number and proportion of children immunized in 2020 compared to the previous year. Over the period of January to September 2020, Penta 3 and Measles vaccination coverage have reduced by approximately 10%-12%. This growing number of un-immunized children poses a greater risk of vaccine preventable disease (VPD) outbreak. As an example, an increased number of suspected measles cases (296 cases during the period of epi week 27 to 35) mostly from the IDPs were reported from Kismayo.
Mental health remains unprioritized and a significant public health challenge. One in every three people in Somalia have experienced some kind of mental health illness and 2.8 millon people are in stress among the IDPs . Only 4 hospitals in Somalia provide basic mental health services with limited integration of MH services by the PHC providers.
The project aims to provide increased access to life-saving essential health services to the most vulnerable communities in Somalia across all the states, with the focus on mitigating the negative health consequence of recurrent flooding, locust infestation, COVID-19 pandemic and communicable disease outbreaks in Somalia. Through this project WHO will continue to advocate for equity and to ensure access for the people living with disabilities.
The WHO Somalia HRP 2021 will focus in 51 high risk districts on the following strategic and specific objectives:
1. To strengthen integrated community based disease surveillance and response network
• To build capacity of the community health workers in integrated disease surveillance and response
• To strengthen the reporting network through real-time /online platform for immediate follow up and actions.
• To increase community awareness in responding to a disease by accepting and practicing the mitigation measures
2. To prevent and control the communicable disease outbreaks
• To increase the preparedness and response capacity of the health facilities responding to the communicable disease outbreak
• To increase the capacity of health care workers on case investigation and integrated management of outbreak prone diseases
• To increase the EPI coverage among the vulnerable population
• To promote public health prevention measures including improved hygiene practice, safe water and sanitation
3. To provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support
• To advocate for mental health services including de-stigmatization and inclusion of population with psychosocial disabilities during any emergencies
• To increase the capacity of health care workers, and the health system to offer appropriate psychosocial support
• To deliver adequate psychosocial support at all level of health care delivery platforms

Project Activities

Activities under strategic objective 1:• Support MoH towards implementation of integrated community surveillance and response network. This is to ensure early detection of outbreaks and contribute to the early warning system in the country• Develop user friendly training materials and tools using local language • Provide formal and informal training (supportive supervision) to build capacity of community health workers• Deploy community health workers to conduct and implement the integrated disease surveillance, identification of malnourished children and enrolment in the Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) program • Strengthen the link between community health workers and the caregivers through house hold visits, community meetings, advocacy sessions etc. • Monitor and follow up the disease trends to take immediate action. • Support the coordination mechanism at district level to link community based surveillance with the health facility and provide overall guidance. Activities under strategic objective 2: • Train and build capacity of health care workers to identify and manage communicable diseases, and severe acute malnutrition with medical complication• Prepositioning of medicines, medical/lab equipment, medical/lab supplies at the regional and major district hospitals, and personal protective equipment• Support the isolation centers within the functional hospitals to mange communicable diseases• Train and deploy rapid response teams to support field alert investigation, verification and response • Conducting risk communication and community engagement/awareness activities • Coordinate outbreak response activities at state, region and district level (e.g. targeted vaccination campaigns for the high risk population)• Support local health authorities to maintain and improve routine immunisation coverage through extending vaccination sessions in the IDP camps i.e fixed, outreach and mobile in high risk districts.• Train health care workers including community workers on health and hygiene promotion activities• Ensure provision of safe water, hygiene and sanitation products within the health facilities • Regular supportive supervision to monitor all disease prevention and control activities (e.g. EPI) Activities under strategic objective 3: • Conduct common assessments, identify the gaps to address the the population in need• Advocate and support activities for stigma reduction, rights and protection of persons with psychosocial disabilities• Conduct training on WHO Quality Rights Initiatives for traditional & spiritual healers, mental hospital health workers and community health workers.• Actor mapping, training and capacity building of health service providers to offer integrated MHPSS services for psychosocial disabilities• Support the out-patient mental health clinics in regional and district hospitals to provide MHPSS services for the high risk population• Support the referral system for mental health patients requiring in-patient care• Supportive supervision and capacity building of the spiritual healers, mental hospital health workers and community health workers for a harmonised approach to psychosocial care

Mitigation Measures

The environment in which the health sector works is often insecure or unpredictable. Mechanisms are in place to identify and respond appropriately to changes of circumstances.To mitigate impact of risks, the following contingency measures will be undertaken:1. Regular communication with health authorities and local partners will ensure dialogue and have some form of early warning to anticipate problems so immediate action can be taken.2. Discussion with government counterparts should be undertaken to ensure institutionalization plans for recommendations on policy support to introduce standards and guidelines.3. Internal project management system of WHO is under constant monitoring of administrative management so problems can be anticipated and early action taken. In case of an unforeseen natural and/or human-induced disaster of a large scale occurring in the country during the project life time, discussions will be held with the donor, local government and other partners to re-orient the project to address the immediate humanitarian needs. All of the operations of the agencies are guided by the internal security measures in line with the basic operating guidelines of the United Nations security regulations. Offices are required to comply with security requirements when conducting operations in the field.
