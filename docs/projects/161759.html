Project Summary

In line with the centrality of protection strategy, the newly developed UNHCR SGBV policy, the three GBV strategic objectives of the HRP 2020 as well as the updated “Call to Action” road map, the project aim is to strengthen protection of Women, men, boys and girls internally displaced and returnees including those with specific needs against exposure to risks of sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) and to ensure that all survivors have equitable and timely access to quality restorative services in Borno and Adamawa states.
Additionally, the project will address specific issues related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) involving humanitarian workers, Security actors and community leaders in the camps and host communities.
In order to ensure a comprehensive approach, the project will adopt UNHCR SGBV policy based on 3 core programme areas: Prevention, Risk mitigation and integrated Response.
In term of prevention, the project will work to engage all women, men, boys and girls IDPs and returnees to address root causes of SGBV such as gender inequality and unequal power dynamics between individuals using innovative approach such as the zero-tolerance village alliance (ZTVA). For this purpose, the project aims to build capacity to identify, prevent and respond to SGBV. Various trainings will target IDPs and returnees leaders (religious and traditional), peer to peer groups, community volunteers. Raising awareness through mass sensitization campaigns in and out of camps on SGBV, effective behavior and social changes, sexual exploitation and abuse.
The project will also promote innovative livelihood opportunities as a way to mitigate risk of SGVB and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Safe access to fuel and energy will be promoted through alternative source of energy as recommended by the joint NE SAFE assessment.
Livelihood activities will be use as a protection safety net to reduce economic vulnerability of IDP and returnee women, men, boys and girls against sexual exploitation and abuse.
This project will be complemented by the UNHCR “access to justice” project which fight impunity by facilitating survivors’ access to justice for via legal representation, counseling and awareness.
For risk mitigation, protection monitoring will be used as an entry points for Identification and documentation of risk points and SGBV incidents through community based protection and GBV mechanisms (monitors, Protection Action Groups, men and boys group, rapid alert system, community based complaint mechanisms, youth clubs and peer to peer support, etc.) in Borno and Adamawa States to inform humanitarian interventions and support advocacy and SGBV mainstreaming in all the sectors.
For the integrated Response, the project will ensure that an integrated package is available and all identified survivors as well as vulnerable men, women, girls, boys and adolescents have safe access to the multisectoral services (Psychosocial Support Services, referral, including for legal support, material support, case management, economic empowerment, etc.) with a special attention to women and girls identify as “BHI wifes”, women and girls in detention facilities and women and girls previously used or associated with armed group as well as those released or those that have escaped from NSAG abduction.

1 - Needs

Protection incidents particularly sexual and gender-based violence remain a major concern in the north east of Nigeria despite multiple efforts in terms of prevention and response. Women and girls remain the most affected (95%) by SGBV. According to protection monitoring reports, from January to September 2019, more than 1200 returnees and IDPs have experienced different types of SGBV (rape, abductions, child and forced marriage, undesired pregnancies, sexual exploitation, physical assault, denial of resources, etc.) perpetrated by non-state armed groups, in countries of asylum and in the IDP camps. There is a need for a tailored psychosocial support to build resilience and re-socialization among survivors. According to the HRP 2019, 3 million people are in need of GBV prevention and response in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states. Protection needs for SGBV are huge and the inter-agency capacity to respond remain insufficient, poor in quality and challenging (coverage) in the current security situation. Inaccessibility to certain areas, socio-cultural constraints and lack of adequate protection services have led to under reporting of SGBV incidents by the community. It is very difficult to determine the real scale of SGBV among IDP and returnee women, girls, men and boys. Ongoing insecurity and lack of basic services and resources (food, NFIs, shelter, clothing education and health facilities, income generating activities) in areas of return and around IDPs camps has resulted in additional challenges for female-headed households and widows, increasing their vulnerability to rape, exploitation and abuse, sexual harassment, forced marriage as well as domestic violence. According to the DTM Round 24 Report (august 2018), 73 % of displaced persons mentioned food as the main unmet need, followed by NFI and Shelter. End of 2017, UNHCR, WFP and FAO carried out an assessment on « safe access to fuel and energy » within Borno state among IDP’s, returnees and host population. The finding showed that 95% of the interviewed population depend on firewood and charcoal for their daily cooking energy needs and 85% indicated that they face risks of rape, sexual assault, killing and abductions during collection of firewood including. Vulnerability of women and girls has also increased due to many factors such as the reduction of assistance package and targeting of the most vulnerable represent a real risk of SEA and SGBV, the restriction of movement, the social status of women since most of them are widows or single due to the high number of men killed during the conflict coupled with the current detention of men for investigation process by the military. Women are now saddled with the responsibility to feeding their families. There is an urgent need to address these issues by creating more economic opportunities for both women, men and adolescents. Additionally, in many Local government areas, there is not yet the presence of civil authorities with almost a breakdown of law leading to impunity for SGBV perpetrator in hard to reach areas. Once liberated from hard to reach areas, Women and children identified during the security screening as having a link with non-state armed group are transferred in detention centre in Maiduguri where humanitarian have no access. Once released, they are transferred in the “rehabilitation centre”. The whole process showed weakness and risk points for SGBV and SEA. Many allegations of SEA in exchange for resources (money, assets, etc.) by security actors as well as influential community members and government agency staff members have been reported by IDP’s. There is an urgent need to take appropriate measures such as capacity building, sensitization and high level advocacy to prevent Sexual Exploitation Abuse.

2 - Activities

1. PREVENTION • Establish/Strengthen community-based GBV structures for awareness raising, early identification, prevention and referral for specialized services (Youth club, women committee, community volunteers, peer to peer groups, groups of men and boys engaged) • Identify key male leaders in the community (religious, traditional) and engage them in prevention of SGBV and SEA and peaceful dialogue • Implement the ground-breaking model of community mobilization using the zero-tolerance village alliance methodology to address root causes of gender-based violence in camps • The project will explore innovative way (promotion of kitchen gardening, referral for unconditional CBI multipurpose) to mitigate SEA due to lack of condiments and other basic needs. • Implement the SAFE approach to ensure IDP’s and returnees have safe access to fuel and energy by exploring distribution of fuel-efficient stoves • Livelihood activities will be used as a preventive action and alternative survival package to reduce economic vulnerability of women, men, boys and girls IDP’s and returnees against sexual exploitation and abuse. • Capacity building for security actors, humanitarian actors and IDPs and returnees on SEA and SGBV • Advocacy at state level for favorable legal framework for protection of women and children • Establish/strengthen PSEA community-based complaints mechanism from the transit/reception facilities to the camps. 2 Risk mitigation • Identify, monitor and document through protection monitoring and vulnerability screening women, girls and female adolescents at risk including unaccompanied and separated children at the transit center, in the camp and in rehabilitation center • Identify, document risk point of Sexual Gender based Violence including Sexual exploitation and Abuse • Identify and assess through vulnerability screening adolescents and women in need of life skills acquisition and livelihood support • Support identification, verification and referral of girls and women used or associated with armed group • Build capacity of SGBV actors in identification and documentation of SGBV incidents as well as risks points 3. MULTI SECTORAL RESPONSE • Provide psychosocial Support, case management and GBV critical material support at the transit/reception facilities for new arrivals and rehabilitation centre for WAAFG • Establish integrated safe spaces for women and girls in the camps • Establish/strengthen Case Management System in the safe spaces • Provide psychosocial support in the camps including life skill acquisition and recreational activities • Referral for legal support services • GBV critical Material support (dignity kits) for vulnerable girls and women including survivors living in the camps. • Contribute to the GBV Information Management System.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will establish/strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
