Project Summary

UNFPA will continue to work for the reduction of maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality, support for infection prevention and control (IPC) during COVID-19, and the provision of medical assistance and care to GBV survivors. This will be achieved by ensuring (1) the availability, access to and utilization of integrated quality SRH services and information for women, men, girls and boys; (2) building the capacity of health workers and community health workers (CHWs); (3) reactivate and activate services in areas where no SRH services exist; (4) ensure availability of essential medical and personal protective equipment (PPE), medicines, and supplies for delivery of SRH services and family planning; (5) ensuring delivery of complementary comprehensive service including MHPSS, and NCD; (6) strengthening referral mechanism and improving the coordination in national and subnational level.
UNFPA will continue to follow the WoS approach, with implementation from Damascus and cross-border operations from Turkey, ensuring a country-wide response. All 14 Syrian governorates will be targeted, with activities in NES implemented through the Damascus hub. Vulnerable population groups targeted include: women and girls of reproductive age, Internally Displaced Population (IDP), host communities and returnees, adolescents, and persons with disabilities (PwD).
Key objectives: Increasing access to lifesaving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services
for the most vulnerable and in need:
Increasing access to primary healthcare through the provision of essential primary health services to cover the health needs of vulnerable populations, including Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (RMNCH);
Building the capacity of health workers and CHWs;
Supporting essential repairs, rehabilitation and equipping of health facilities providing public and specialized services, in particular in areas of most acute need, as well as facilities that receive referrals from areas of acute need;
Strengthening and consolidating medical supply chains, ensuring availability of safe and quality medicines for essential primary health care, IPC supplies and PPE for COVID-19 and general needs, obstetric care, infectious diseases including HIV to prevent transmission from mother to child and family planning methods;
Pre-positioning of emergency supply stocks at warehouse and health facility level in line with contingency scenarios for reduced access and increased level of hostilities requiring RH kits, pharmaceutical items and supplies;
Improving access to secondary and tertiary health care for high risk pregnancy, malnourished pregnant and lactating women (PLW), emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) cases, and acute cases which cannot access treatment in the area in which they reside, particularly rural areas;
Strengthening health sector coordination and HIS, with an emphasis on enhancing protection and increasing access to health services and continuing coordination within and across hubs, including thorough joint contingency and preparedness planning for changes in context;
UNFPA will engage in activities that support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, respond to IDP movements and shifts in control; and beneficiaries’ participation all over the country;
UNFPA will continue to monitor the implementation of these activities based on the five principles of AAP as well as UNFPA’s global results-based monitoring framework. Monthly supervision visits will be done jointly with the partners at the national and subnational level by UNFPA staff and personnel subject to access and approvals. Third party monitors are used in the cross-border response. Standardized tools including the supported services and supervision checklist are used to assess service availability/quality. Clinic registration data will be collated at least monthly. Beneficiary feedback (incl. complaints box, FGDs, exit interviews) will be collected.

4 - Needs

Health needs in Syria continued to be exacerbated by access constraints, damage to HFs, economic deterioration, insecurity and conflict, displacement, and insufficient HR, with the COVID-19 pandemic adding new risks and challenges to an already fragile context. Movement restrictions, suspension of services, population fear of contracting the virus, reduced capacity to allow for social distancing, delayed procurement, and infections among health staff continue to affect availability and accessibility of SRH services. In addition to the critical health needs, women’s and girls’ SRH rights are not fully respected, with access constraints regarding contraception, safe abortion, and post-abortion care; CMR service gaps also remain widespread, particularly in government controlled areas which requires mandatory GBV incident reporting and as highlighted by health providers and community representatives. While family planning, ANC and PNC, EmONC, and STI treatment are needed, there are additional needs for female cancer screening and HIV/AIDS programmes, as there is limited scope to provide quality care for these conditions within Syria. The economic deterioration has contributed to increased need, restricted access to services, and higher malnutrition rates, with about 20% of PLW being malnourished in NWS alone. As of June 2020, 50% of HFs were not or partially functional, according to HeRAMS. Of the 12.4m people in need of health services according to the 2021 HNO, WRA have been identified as a particularly vulnerable group, making up 27% of the 2021 Health PiN, including 498,480 women who are expected to become pregnant in 2021. The 2021 HNO indicates that the prevalence of disability is estimated at 25%.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNFPA continues to prioritize improved maternal and newborn health by providing integrated SRH and GBV services through 17 CEmONCs, 22 BEmONCs, 76 PHCs, and 89 mobile clinics and partial support to 638 MoH/MoHE PHCs; building the capacity of HWs, CHWs and midwives; provide essential equipment, medicines and supplies for services and family planning; deliver complementary services; strengthen referral mechanisms to specialized health or GBV services and monitored through tracking and patient follow-up; and improve coordination. UNFPA ensures social inclusion of PwDs and the elderly by supporting transportation to facilities, ensuring facilities are disability-friendly, and through mobile clinics. UNFPA works with the SRH working groups to build capacity on needed topics such as life-saving protocols, Caesarean sections; CMR; cancer screening; menstrual hygiene management; and service adaptations for COVID-19. UNFPA Syria will introduce a new voucher programme to support vulnerable populations to access free or subsidized health care, reducing financial barriers. UNFPA will ensure meaningful participation of beneficiaries at all stages of the operation's management cycle, and will monitor activities based on the five principles of AAP and UNFPA’s global results-based monitoring framework. UNFPA Syria has suboffices and a network of field coordinators in all governorates who conduct site visits. The Turkey hub has included third party monitoring, which conducts site visits to ensure access and availability of quality services. M&E systems are in place to generate evidence on activities and make adjustments as necessary. UNFPA ensures harmonized monthly reporting by its IPs across the different hubs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:• increasing advocacy efforts, by the health sector, with all parties to the conflict. This aims to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). • Monitoring and reporting on the health situations in reconciled areas. Gender Based Violence:•Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected populations should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package.• Train specialized health staff on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.• UNFPA and other partners in the GBV sub-cluster have identified safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner which guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor.• Recruit female staff in health facilities in order to increase access of women and girls to health services. Provision of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Health Services: • Inclusion of vulnerable and minority groups needs throughout the program cycle has been done through gender, age, and disability disaggregated analysis of Beneficiaries

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risks:- The COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated PSEA related risks for displaced populations. - The deteriorating economic situation inside Syria, has led to more human rights abuses, including SEA. - Ongoing conflict enabling a context where SEA cases are inadequately addressed. Mitigation:- UNFPA conducts PSEA assessments of all its IPs and supports them in developing and validating a PSEA policy.- UNFPA has established a hotline to report any SEA related incidents.- UNFPA organizes specific trainings on PSEA for staff including IPs- UNFPA has a zero tolerance policy with regards to SEA. Also note that UNFPA’s executive director is the champion of PSEA 2021, pushing three priorities which include (1) bolstering PSEA country mechanisms, (2) improving access to quality assistance and information for victims of SEA and (3) strengthening coordination and cooperation.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

UNFPA regularly assesses risks for operations, and have identified the following key risks for 2021: Escalation of violence: includes targeting of health facilities, increased displacement and limited access to services. Lack of access: difficult to monitor programs; difficult to provide services/supplies in insecure areas; movement in Syria can be restricted. Large population displacements: service providers may be overwhelmed. Lack of partners with limited capacity: applicable inside Syria and dependent on approval from national authorities. Staffing restrictions: delayed Visa/long recruitment procedures, increased outward migration and internal displacement of service providers. High turnover of staff: can result in delay of activity implementation. Procurement risks: sanctions make local procurement difficult and international procurement can have a long lead time. Fraud / Aid Diversion. Lack of acceptance of sexual terminology in SRH programs: Sexual health is a taboo subject and is often not prioritized in emergencies. UNFPA maintains strong contingencies for these risks and has developed contingency plans within SRH/GBV coordination groups and through UNFPA’s internal risk management framework. UNFPA conducts regular monitoring of security and partner activities to respond to needs and access to provide services to the most vulnerable beneficiaries. For the CVA joint program with WFP, accountability mechanisms are communicated to those receiving CVA on how to report concerns or protection incidents to UNFPA or WFP. UNFPA also ensures its IPs and all voucher issuing agents/shop owners have a code of conduct in place signed by all staff, and also that regular staff sensitization on PSEA is conducted.
