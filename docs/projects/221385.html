Project Summary

To improve food security and enhance livelihood opportunities for IDPs and vulnerable host communities in conflict-affected districts in Cabo Delgado (including Macomia, Chiure, Nangade, Metuge, Meluco and Quissanga) by promoting sustainable agricultural practices, economic empowerment, and resilience-building initiatives.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

NPA will support newly displaced IDPs, returnees and host communities to address the immediate and medium-term needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities by providing multi-purpose cash assistance, promoting food security, strengthening livelihoods, and enhancing resilience to climate change and disasters. The aim of this project is to ensure that the most at-risk individuals in displacement-affected communities receive support, focusing on those with heightened vulnerabilities and protection concerns. Beneficiaries will be selected based on the following key criteria:Beneficiaries that present protection concerns:• Individuals or households facing significant risks or vulnerabilities due to displacement, including those exposed to violence, exploitation, or abuse.Beneficiaries with socioeconomic vulnerabilities:• Families with limited or no access to income, food, or basic necessities.• Households struggling to restart income-generating activities post-displacement.Special Focus Groups:• Women/Girls: Particular attention to women and girls facing gender-based vulnerabilities.• Single-Parent Households: Prioritization of women-headed households• Child-Headed Households: Support for children managing households due to loss of adult caregivers.• People with Disabilities• Elderly peopleDisplacement Status:• Recently displaced individuals or households affected by conflict or natural disasters.• Returnees requiring support to rebuild livelihoods in their communities.Inclusion of Host Communities:• Vulnerable members of host communities who are sharing resources with IDPs and returnees.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

NPA interventions are implemented through a locally led approach. NPA's locally led humanitarian approach contributes to an increased access to communities most in need and enhances local organizations’ response with additional resources and technical capacity strengthening. The program will target IDPs and vulnerable host communities in conflict-affected districts from Chiure, Macomia, Meluco, Metuge, Nangade and Quissanga. Key activities include - Multipurpose cash will be provided to new IDPs/returnees households prioritizing women, girls- and child headed households, the elderly and people living with disabilities (cash distribution will be conducted by a partner currently being identified through stakeholder analysis). Agricultural inputs (seeds and tools) distributions will be provided to beneficiaries in partnership with our local partner UPC. Targets will include IDPs that have either been assigned plots for cultivation by the local authorities. IDPs that have secured land through negotiations with their hosts or vulnerable host community households as recommended after conflict sensitivity analysis. NPA will also support beneficiaries in negotiating cultivation land through local authorities or negotiations with host communities who can support IDPs access land for cultivation. Land negotiations and dialogues will include members from both host communities and IDPs. Documentation and learning: Establishment of MEAL and accountability systems, including complaints response mechanisms, regular collection and analysis of data from beneficiaries of cash support to draw lessons for future programming. Training in Climate Adaptive Agriculture: IDP smallholder farmers and agricultural laborers in communities vulnerable to climate change, as well as vulnerable groups such as women farmers, youth and host communities involved in agriculture are targeted with CAA trainings that promote resilience to climate change. Training and establishment of community savings and loan groups: Provide training and resources to establish savings and rotative credit groups to enhance economic resilience, allowing access to savings mechanisms and small loan. Market Access: Assist smallholders in creating market linkages to help farmers and producers get access to markets and sell their produce at competitive conditions. Early Warning Systems: Conflict Preparedness and Protection activities are integrated to allow communities to be better prepared through the establishment of systems providing timely information on weather and potential emergencies; information will mainly reach communities through DRR committees that will be trained and provided technical support. A rapid needs assessment will be conducted in all of the mentioned 6 districts (Chiure, Macomia, Meluco, Metuge, Nangade and Quissanga) that the project aims to implement in. To determine the scope of the crisis and identify needs, vulnerabilities, and priorities of affected population and confirm number of project beneficiaries that will be covered. Multipurpose cash activity will target all 6 districts (Chiure, Macomia, Meluco, Metuge, Nangade and Quissanga) proposed by the project which equates to 16 000 people (9600 female and 6400 male). Distribution of agricultural inputs (seeds and tools) will target 4000 people (2400 female and 1600 male) in the following districts – Chiure, Macomia, Metuge and Quissanga). Training in Climate Adaptive Agriculture (CAA) will be delivered to 500 (320 female and 180 male) beneficiaries in Chiure, Macomia, Metuge and Quissanga. Training and resources to establish savings and rotative credit groups will be provided to 400 (240 females, 160 male IDPs) in Macomia, Meluco, Metuge and Nangade. Integrated programming: project beneficiaries will be provided with information from our CPP project to be to be better prepared for emergencies with DRR committees and early warning systems in place.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

By working with local partners NPA has established community acceptance over a long period of time and is able to access vulnerable populations as a consequence of the trusted position they have pre-dating the current conflict. Through this understanding and analysis NPA and its partners will ensure they do no harm and have a conflict sensitive approach. NPA and its partners have a well-established operational approach to community engagement through the ongoing work with small-scale farmers and civil society in rural and urban areas. The long-term presence of NPA’s partners in the target area and the support provided to host communities through existing programming will form part of a localized conflict mitigation approach. NPA will strengthen implementing partners’ capacity on safeguarding protection, Code of Conduct, Security policies Do No Harm principles and feedback and complaints mechanism to avoid harassment or exploitation of beneficiaries. NPA’s humanitarian programs are developed and implemented in line with internationally recognized humanitarian principles and standards. Ensuring that we ‘do-no-harm’ in humanitarian settings in Mozambique, NPA will examine how aid interacts with conflict dynamics, the power structures, gender relations or other aspects of the context we work in, and ensure that our programs, as well as staff and partner behavior, are conflict sensitive.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Mozambique has severe inequalities between men and women that characterize the country. NPA recognizes that the majority of the IDP’s are the women, children, elderly and people with disabilities, whose life dignity and protection have been completely disrupted. NPA together with its partners is committed to reduce these inequalities by implementation of the proposed project. NPA recognizes the role of women in resolution of conflicts in the resettlement areas, humanitarian response and post-conflict reconstructions, and will support women’s meaningful participation throughout the humanitarian program cycle. The different needs and vulnerabilities of men, women, boys, girls as well as elderly and persons with disabilities has been considered. Severity of needs is also considered which includes, amongst other factors; female-headed households, households with a high number of dependents, households headed by older people or people with disabilities or chronic illnesses, households with limited income. Emphasis will be to prioritize response to the needs of the most vulnerable households according to the vulnerability assessment in the proposed districts. This will prioritize particularly female-headed households, child headed households, households with a high number of dependents, households headed by older people or people with disabilities or chronic illnesses, households with limited income. The partner will ensure the use of a variety of participatory methodologies such as needs assessments and local community engagement in order to map out community structures and available services to concretely understand the context of people of concern as well as their needs. This is to ensure that the program is able to provide assistance specifically targeting and delivering services to populations with identified vulnerabilities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project is committed to ensuring equitable access to assistance for all vulnerable groups, including people with disabilities (PWDs). This will be achieved through a systematic approach to identification, engagement, and the removal of barriers to participation. The project will ensure that persons with disabilities are consulted throughout the project cycle that will ensure identifying and prioritizing of the work that needs to be done to improve their inclusivity and accessibility of the project. By proactively identifying PWDs and addressing barriers to their participation, the project ensures equitable assistance and fosters an inclusive environment that respects the dignity and rights of all beneficiaries.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project incorporates an environmental lens to minimize negative impacts while promoting sustainable practices that enhance resilience and environmental protection. The project will focus on providing training to beneficiaries on how to mitigate against impact of climate change by adopting Conversation Agricultural principles for sustainable food security and improved livelihoods. This will ensure that project beneficiaries adopt sustainable agricultural practices or measures to improve crop production whilst having a positive impact on the environment. Furthermore, the project aims to promote negotiated access to cultivation land, ensuring land agreements with host communities and respect local ecosystems to reduce conflict. The project also aims to balance immediate humanitarian needs with long-term environmental sustainability. By integrating environmental assessments and adopting mitigation measures, the project seeks to protect natural resources, support resilience, and ensure that interventions contribute positively to both people and the environment.
