Project Summary

This is a continuation of the community-based protection initiative that OXFAM started in selected camps hosting Rohingya refugees and in remote Bangladeshi communities living in proximity to Rohingya refugee camps. Building on the past years’ gains, including mobilising refugees for awareness session on protection services and the installation of public solar streetlights, OXFAM shall partner with a local NGO to implement structured capacity development activities to enhance the skills of existing volunteers, protection committees, youth, women's groups to support them into becoming active contributors to their protection. The project is a shift towards lesser reliance on individual incentivised volunteers to strengthening refugees and hosts organised into collectives or groups (women, youth groups and protection committees, solar light maintenance groups) become active representatives and leaders of their own communities in improving their protection. It is a community-based protection approach that will develop skills and confidence of community groups in protection analysis, facilitation of referrals, maintenance of solar streetlights, and negotiation with with service providers for protection. Total number of beneficiaries is 198,224. Capacity development activities will have 534 direct beneficiaries: (96 members of women’s committees, 80 protection volunteers, and 198 members of solar streetlight maintenance groups).The project will include emergency cash assistance to help 200 survivors of violence and abuse, those who face imminent threats access specialised protection services to protect themselves or their families. Awareness sessions will be for refugees and hosts, except the legal documentation awareness which is only for host communities.
The project applies a safe programming approach & will analyse potential risks the project activities to project participants including possible conflict between the beneficiaries and communities not covered in the project and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA); then mitigation measures will be adopted. Implementing staff and community groups will have refresher trainings on PSEA and informed of SEA reporting channels. As we implementing this during a global Covid-19 pandemic, the project implement measures help prevent transmission and protect participants from exposure to or passing the virus such as ensuring social distancing in group activities, use of protective masks, etc. Finally, project staff and community groups will also ensure be briefed on referral pathways for Covid-19 so they can facilitate referrals when needed.
OXFAM and its partner shall jointly agree on a MEAL system to keep track of project’s progress and developments in the context to inform adaptations needed. MEAL will include project visits, and for trainings, will do pre and post-test to know if messages were understood and what additional trainings might be needed. Other information will be used to know and act on communities’ feedback such as the Community Perception Tracker that OXFAM implements to listen to concerns being raised by communities. Post-assistance monitoring will be implemented at least two weeks after disbursement of emergency cash to a survivor, to know how the intervention helped address the protection need.
Project activities will be implemented in coordination with Government of Bangladesh through the relevant agencies in the camps and Cox’s Bazar. OXFAM and partner regularly meet’s and coordinates with and provide project updates to RRRC, Camp in Charge (CiC). Also, with the Anti-Trafficking, Protection Against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA) Network, GBV Sub-Sector, and other inter-agency groups. Identification of emergency cash for protection beneficiary will be done in coordination with case management agencies, thus OXFAM and partner will inform organisations providing case management about the emergency cash intervention so they could refer potential beneficiaries.

1 - Identified Needs

Since their displacement to Bangladesh, the Rohingya refugees have consistently faced significant protection risks. Of late, fighting between armed gangs has become more frequent, while criminality, drug trading and use continues and is feared to have also increased. The fencing of parts of the camps has little or no positive impact on the safety and security of refugees. Violence and insecurity have severely impacted women and men’s mobility and ability to work, as they fear attack, harassment, and conscription into gangs. Many criminal and violent (especially GBV) activities happen at night when it is dark and unseen. Consequently, insufficient lighting has increased fear among refugees and hosts, especially women and children. (Source: OXFAM's protection monitoring - done on an ongoing basis)Protection services are still limited and many among host communities, have little awareness of how legal services, can be accessed. Even when protection services available, some victims of abuse are reluctant to access protection services due to prevailing factors such as fear of reprisal, lack of trust in the services, or lack of accurate and updated information about how to access. Sometimes, access to protection by survivors of violence is hindered by lack of cash for transportation or professional services if free services are unavailable. Overcrowding in public transportation discourages others to continue with counselling or legal services because of fear of exposure to Covid-19. Some survivors do not have time to participate in psycholosical activities because they need to look after their families; thereby denying them the time and opportunity to heal. These needs will be addressed in this project through awareness raising on services, community-assisted referrals and provision of emergency cash for protection. In the last two years, the community-based volunteers trained and incentivised by OXFAM helped pass accurate information and increased confidence of survivors to access services by accompanying them to appropriate service providers. There is a need to upgrade the approach and equip organised groups like protection committees, women’s groups, youth groups with information and skills to help their neighbours access protection.Meanwhile, maintenance of solar lights remains a challenge. The care and monitoring of the lights requires a community-led approach to develop shared responsibility and co-ownership of community facilities like solar lights. Organizing the volunteers into solar light maintenance groups and training them to take turns in monitoring the lights, educating community members on light maintenance and care, reporting and coordinating with camp authorities on the status of the lights is a community-led initiative that addresses the need to develop ownership and shared responsibility to collectively manage communal lighting facilities. In addition the need for skills and tools for the solar lighting maintenance groups will be addressed through trainings and provision of basic soalr light repair kits. Communicating early warning of environmental/climactic hazards such as cyclones is a gap in certain areas and these will be addressed through dissemination of early warning and preparedness information by the community groups supported in this project.Many of the protection threats are beyond the communities to solve on their own and refugees and hosts, especially women and girls' concerns and proposed solutions are barely listened to. There is rarely any opportunity for communities to communicate their concerns with service providers and protection actors. Involving communities in analysing protection and finding solutions by providing space for dialogues between them, service providers and local authorities addresses the lack of communities participation in protection and is important in contributing towards evidenced-based policy advocacy led by NGOs and protection working group.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Emergency cash assistance to victims and survivors of protection abuse to enable their access to legal, psychological and other related services. The cash assistance will cover cost of transportation, legal documentation and professional fees if free specialised service is not available or limited. It is also intended to help survivors recover from the protection threat or resume their usual activities i.e. education for a person rescued from trafficking. Emergency cash is also intended to support those facing threats for their legal protection i.e. who are under threat of displacement or eviction.2. Protection monitoring through FGDs, KIIs, observation and secondary information analysis. OXFAM and partners' protection teams will work closely with its network of protection volunteers, protection committees, women's organisations and wider networks within other relevant sectors (e.g. WASH listening groups) to support refugee and host communities in identifying protection risks and developing solutions. 3.The project will organise dialogues between community groups with service providers and protection actors to enable the former to discuss protection issues and their proposed solutions. 4.Solar light monitoring and Solar Light Maintenance Groups will be formed. Earlier there were 2 key contacts per solar lamp. It will be expanded into a group where members will lead in monitoring the performance of the lights, meet with OXFAM for any concerns, and develop messages on maintenance of solar lights and reporting issues. The groups will be trained to prepare monthly report on the status of existing solar lights under project blocks. The monitoring will help community groups to feel ownership of the facilities, increase their sense of responsibility and eventually enable them to contribute to the community. Findings will help inform the community with important messages on care, maintenance and how to report different issues. The monitoring reports shall also be shared with camp authorities within the shelter sector to seek support in repair and maintenance work. At project end, recommendations will be prepared to influence a process towards finding sustainable solutions and promoting cooperation between communities, local NGOs and camp authorities on installation and management of solar lighting facilities in the camps. 5. Community volunteers, women's groups, youth groups, protection committees will pass disaster early warning messages from camp authorities and disaster management agencies to 2,000 community members. In case of disasters like floods, landslides, fire, etc. the protection committees and women's groups will also disseminate information about where people may go for help, undertake referrals and provide useful information for damage assessment, rescue, or persons of concern that may need protection. 6. Repair Solar street lights7. Community awareness sessions on available protection services and how to access them. For host communities, there will be sessions on civil documentation to help communities know the steps for obtaining and/or renewing lost documents such as marriage, divorce, National ID, birth and death certificates.8.Training of selected members of solar light maintenance group on basic repair.9.Information, education and communication materials, posters and video in particular, shall be developed and used to raise awareness about available services and referral systems. This includes materials to raise awareness on disaster early warning system and disability inclusion in coordination with others in the sector.10. Trainings on data protection and confidential referrals, psychological first aid will be conducted for for community volunteers, and local org. staffs to enable them as first responders ensure safe handling of protection information including disclosures of specific incidents, and appropriate response (such as referrals) of persons needing protection.
