Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains critical with an estimated 6.1 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and 304,560 refugees in need some form of protection from violence, abuse, exploitation and violations of fundamental human rights. Protection threats continue to intensify in scale and scope, and the population is exposed to repeated deliberate attacks on civilians and other violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. Poor rule of law continues to prevent perpetrators from being held accountable for violations, including conflict-related sexual violence, torture, killing, targeted discrimination, and occupation and destruction of property. Women and children face specific protection risks, including gender-based violence, forced recruitment, family separation, forced and early marriage (HRP2018).Numerous tribal and inter-clan conflicts have resulted in displacement of populations). 4.6 million people have been uprooted since 2013, including an estimated 2 million people still displaced and living in protection sites and IDP settlements (OCHA, 2018). Repeated displacements, the economic crisis and food insecurity have contributed to the breakdown of structures, affecting the fragile protective environment for children. Children represent over 61% of those in need, suffer the brunt of conflict and economic pressures and at risk of, or exposed to exploitation, abuse, neglect, and violence. Children and their families continue to rely on negative coping and survival strategies including early and forced child marriage, intentional family separation, school dropout, involvement in criminal groups/armed forces or groups, and child labor, although Peace Agreement provides hope. Save the Children has been implementing CPiE interventions since the beginning of crisis in 2013 and has operations in Jonglei (Akobo, Nyirol, Bor), Eastern Equatoria (Torit, Kapoeta) NBeG (Malualkon), and Maban with recent expansion of the CPiE work to Lakes, Western and EEQ through funding and technical support from UNICEF. Specific activities implemented are Case management, Coordination of FTR activities, Technical and capacity building to partners, implementing community based PSS activities, delivering CP trainings to staff and CBCPN, and targeted mass awareness and sensitization on Child Protection. As a result, 5,744 UASC (2,588 girls, 3,156 boys) have been reunified through UNICEF and CPSC support and have been reintegrated in the communities while 23 partners have benefited from capacity building on FTR and use of CPIMS. Despite the key achievements highlighted above, we continue to experience funding constrains, hash climatic conditions, inaccessibility of roads especially in hard to reach locations, increased demand for Child Protection services among others, which have hindered timely support for vulnerable children with protection risks. Since January 2014, 18,154 children have been identified as separated (11,149), unaccompanied (2,878) or missing (4,127). The current active caseload in the country stands at 7,222 UASC (CPIMS, August 2018). The protection and care of vulnerable children in the South Sudan is critical. Children’s vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by constant displacement and relocation of communities due to constant fear of attacks or revenge killings resulting into a re-emergence of protection risks to the already vulnerable girls and boys. Other consequences are the adoption s of survival mechanisms such as survival sex, exploitative work, school drop-out, and early/forced marriage of young girls. According to consultations with and feedback from beneficiaries, involuntary family separations, heightened levels of psycho-social distress, early and forced marriages, Sexual abuse, neglect, exploitation and physical abuse were some of the issues highlighted that affect children.

Activities

Save the Children has presence in most of the locations mapped and prioritized by the Child Protection Sub cluster. Save the Children is also a member of Child protection Working group, other clusters such as CAAFAG, MHPSS among other coordination groups at county and national level. We also leads the UASC working group on a monthly basis in collaboration with UNICEF and are a core chair of the Case Management Task force coordinating Case management activities and providing technical support to partners. Save the Children will continue to coordinate with key partners on ground and UNICEF to agree on specific coverage areas, coordinate with CPWG at the onset of the project for clarity on area of operations to avoid any possible duplication. Save the Children will also participate in coordination meetings and periodically review activities with all partners on ground. The 5Ws matrix will regularly be updated by the CPSC indicating who is working where for easy coordination of services and referrals. The referral pathway will also be regularly updated by the county-based Child Protection Working Groups. The activities will be structured and linked to a Child Protection help desks in communities, CFSs/schools which will support in devolving Child Protection services in the community, bring services close to the communities and encourage community participation, accountability and timely feedback. The following are the proposed activities to be implemented to address the above highlighted needs: - 1) Identification and documentation of 2,500 vulnerable children using newly launched Comprehensive Case management toolkits,2) Supporting 2,500 vulnerable girls and boys in vulnerable situations through case management and referrals to specialized services,3) Conducting trainings and capacity building for 300 CP staff on comprehensive case management processes and SOPs, toolkit, Child Protection in emergencies, Family4) Conducting Tracing, Reunification and reintegration activities for 2,000 UASCs,5) Conducting structured focussed and non-focused PSS to 20,000 boys and girls using booklet developed by the PSS working group, Child/Youth resilience building trainings using one of the successful Save the Children models piloted in South Sudan among other areas,6) Mainstreaming Child Protection in other sectors (Education, Nutrition, Health, Wash, GBV actors etc)7) Targeted structured awareness raising activities at community level to 30,000 boys, girls, men and women 8) Conducting separate adolescent’s boys and girls support groups and symposiums for 200 children. 9) conducting monitoring and evaluation activities throughout the project period

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The beneficiaries will be provided with Child Protection related information through targeted and structured awareness and sensitization activities on issues affecting children such as radios, through children activities such as structured PSS and adolescent boys and girls symposiums, community training and community forums to prevent family separations, understand beneficiary selection criteria and those with most urgent needs.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Deliberate strategies will be employed to ensure community participation, ownership of the project. Community structures will be involved to develop structured messages for different target groups (Children, adults, leaders). Lastly, through community feedback mechanism and established child Protection help desks, information on the projects will be disseminated allowing communities to to provide feedback. Therefore, messages will be context specific and structured and tailored for boys and girls, men and women, community and other leaders.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Save the Children conducts risk assessment and analysis of all project using gender analysis and Child Safeguarding lens in line with do no harm principles and best interests of children. Save the Children ensures that accountable processes are applied during recruitment of staffs, consultants and supplies. Child survivors of SGBV will be identified and supported through comprehensive case management interventions inline with CPMS standard 9, 10 and 15. therefore, at all times the project will be guided by SC’s Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct, and humanitarian principles including child participation, do no harm, and best interests of the child.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Complain response mechanismSave the Children implements complains and response mechanism as one of the models for collecting information on issues affecting beneficiaries, documenting complains or dissatisfaction of any of the activities, getting feedback and prioritization of beneficiaries needs. Similar model will be applied in this current project. Child Protection help desks Save the Children has established child focused help desks in out patient centers, schools and in the child friendly spaces. This ensures that early identification of extremely vulnerable beneficiaries is done, referrals made while community members have access to Child Protection information. similar model will be applied while strengthening the mechanisms and addressing gaps identified through a recent help desks assessment conducted in August 2018. Part of proposed activities includes capacity building of volunteers, child protection staffs, teachers and CBCPN for a robust and vibrant feedback and complaint mechanisms. Save the children also ensures that any suspected child safeguarding concerns are reported and acted in a confidential manner in line with set procedures and policies.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Save the Children has a dedicated department headed by head of MEAL providing technical support for field staffs and ensures monitoring and evaluation of all activities throughout the project cycle. The team in collaboration with the Child Protection technical team will ensure that focused group discussions, rapid assessments and, end of project evaluation is done. this will ensure community leaders, children and beneficiaries participate in the project activities and provide periodic feedback on the activities implemented. to address cultural barriers and help in shifting community attitudes and behaviors that affect children, specific activities will target women and girls such as separate symposiums for boys and girls that will help girls and women confidently raise issues affecting them for advocacy activities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on a protection risk analysis that considers specific needs of boys, girls, men and women consumerate with individual needs and vulnerabilities in line with Age Gender and diversity. The project activities are also based on beneficiaries recommendations and analysis of existing gaps. Save the Children has and internal risk management plan (RMP) conducted every quarter to identify, assess and mitigate potential programmatic risks. this is regularly tracked to ensure adequate and timely actions are implemented. In addition, Save the Children conducts child safeguarding risk assessment for all activities to ensure children and beneficiaries are protected from harm. this is also demonstrated in the beneficiaries selection criteria which ensures most at risk, marginalized and vulnerable groups are identified and supported.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

limited participation of women, girls and children in key decision making processes-Save the children will ensure that most vulnerable groups such as women, girls and boys are put in the fore front in participation of their own protection. Representation from the groups will ensure that they participate in key activities such as community awareness, sensitization and implementation of community action plans. More women and girls will be reached across all activities. community leaders and Child Protection staffs will also be sensitized on exclusivity and ensuring the vulnerable groups benefit from the interventions. In addition, Child Protection help desks will be established in key sites within the community such as schools, out patient centers and child friendly spaces to devolve child protection services and ensure vulnerable beneficiaries have access to services and receive timely support. Inaccessibility of some of the implementation locations (hard to reach areasSave the Children will ensure community groups and structures (CBCPN) are trained, mentored and supported to take a lead role in prevention and response activities as a sustainable strategy. Harmful traditional cultural practices-Key community leaders, parents, children, teachers among others will be trained on Child Protection topics and mentored to provide and strengthen family and community based support. The trained groups will act as change agent in the community by strategically challenging harmful cultural practices such as early and forced marriage among others. The Child Protection training, structured community awareness, messaging and dissemination of information will help in shifting the community perception on Child abuse and effects of harmful cultural practices.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

South Sudan crisis is exacerbating the situation for women and girls, with most experiencing at least one form of SGBV Women and girls face daily exposure to protection risks in the home and while collecting food, firewood, and water, using WASH facilities, travelling to school, and accessing community facilities. A study of the knowledge, attitudes and practices around violence against children undertaken in Jonglei indicated that violence remains pervasive in communities. Men and boys are overrepresented as perpetrators of violence which explains some power dynamics that place girls at greater risk of sexual exploitation through early and forced marriage and boys at greater risk of recruitment or other forms of violence. all the proposed activities will therefore target all the diverse groups with aim to shift negative attitudes towards women, girls and boys hence developing ephathy while rallying them to protect the vulnerable groups. The activities also ensures communities participate in the project throughout the project cycle.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety of benficiaries is a key consideration throught the project cycle in line with Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian in Action (CPMS) #5-8 on mitigation of dangers and injuries, 9 on provision of timely and dignified support to child survivors of sexual violence, 10 (assess to mental health and psycho social support, 13 on support for unaccompanied and separated children and 15 on provision of comprehensive case management. The project enhances child participation in line with individual best interest and do no more harm principles. Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

All Save the Children activities and assistance are implemented in a way that respects the rights, safety and dignity of individuals seeking support. The beneficiaries receiving assistance are primary actors in their own survival and protection and therefore, also primary stakeholder of assistance. through the complain feedback mechanism and child protection help desks, Save the Children will ensure effective information provision in a two way communication for beneficiaries to facilitate their ability to make informed decisions and to provide regular feedback on quality and effectiveness of the assistance. CPMS#9 will be applied ensuring timely, coordinated and dignified support for child survivors of sexual violence and affected families/communities.
