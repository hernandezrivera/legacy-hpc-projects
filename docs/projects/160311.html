Project Summary

Save the Children proposes this livelihood based emergency project in response to the ongoing conflict and its associated impacts in eight counties, which are known for track records of severe food insecurity and malnutrition in three States in South Sudan. The proposed action aims at saving lives, addressing the immediate and medium-term needs and improving access to improved agricultures and livestock services, therefore improving the food and nutrition security of the conflict affected communities and increasing their resilience to famine and disaster risks and shocks. The project targets primarily conflict affected children and their families in the internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities.
The key interventions proposed through this action are food assistances (conditional general food distributions, food for assets), cash/voucher assistances, agricultural inputs provisions and productions with training packages on improved agronomic practices, and livestock vaccinations and treatment against diseases greatest economic importance. The livelihoods support will help target beneficiaries in meeting lifesaving and immediate food needs and also contributing to agriculture productions and livelihoods diversifications and building disaster management capacity of vulnerable communities. The improved agricultural and livestock productions can increase the availability and affordability of nutritious food and hence improve dietary diversity to women and children. Combinations of food security and livelihoods support will help the target households to improve their food and nutrition status, build resilience and reduce vulnerability to malnutrition and other shocks.
The design of the proposed project is informed by findings of the multi-partner and multi-sectoral assessments, official appeals of the government, security and risk analysis and recommendations which have been conducted and released for the last six months. Most of the proposed interventions are accessible and currently multiple projects are being implemented by Save the Children.
The proposed counties are selected based on food insecurity and malnutrition status, SCI’s operational presence and experiences in the areas, good partnership and potential integration with ongoing multi-sectoral programs in the areas.
The proposed emergency response is also developed in line with Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) of FSL Cluster and Strategic Objectives (SO 2 and SO3) of HRP 2020, minimum guidelines, priorities, and considering the minimum standards where relevant.
Starting from 01 January 2020, the proposed action will be implemented for 12 months in three States of South Sudan, specifically Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil East), Jonglei (Akobo, Nyirol), and Eastern Equatoria (Lafon, Budi, Torit, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North) targeting a total of 234,000 people, which fall under IPC 5, 4 and 3.

1 - Needs

Exacerbated by a severe economic crisis, the armed conflict has still resulted in the worst humanitarian situation since independence, July 2011. In September 2018, there was another round of agreement signed called ‘Revitalized Peace Agreement’ on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan(R-ARCSS)" established a (TGoNU). Efforts are underway in realizing the formation of the Transitional Government of National Unity (TGoNU) in November 2019 which is assumed to pave the way to peace in the county. Despite all efforts, the problem is persisting and has resulted in internal displacement of 1.78 million (OCHA, August 2019), 2.33 million have sought refuge in neighboring countries (UNHCR, June 2019) and with some 6.35 million people (54% of total population), estimated to be severely food insecure, including 10,000 people facing famine conditions (IPC, August 2019). Refugees and displaced civilians are at imminent risk of gross human rights violations, inter-ethnic violence and subsequent migration wave. The same report indicates that 1,301,000 children (6-59 months) are acutely malnourished; and 352,000 Pregnant or lactating women acutely malnourished.The IPC August 2019 report highlights that the food security situation slightly improved during the 2019 lean season compared to the 2018 lean season. This improvement was driven by reduced conflict, and improved access to livelihoods, markets and humanitarian assistance, which said that the ongoing R-ARCSS initiative contributes to. The second projection covering January to April 2020, which is the start of the lean season, highlights that 5.50 million people (47%) are projected to face severe food insecurity with IPC 3 and above after factoring the presence of humanitarian assistances. The same report indicates that the overall nutrition situation during the lean season of 2019 is critical and worse compared to same period in 2018. Limited access and poor utilization to food has led to high malnutrition rates amongst children under five. More specifically:- • HRP targets for 2020 shows that the proposed intervention areas are reported for high caseloads falling under IPC 3 and IPC 4. For instance, Aweil East (174090, 52%), Akobo (140480, 62%), Nyirol (82910, 67%), Budi (69650, 70%), Lafon (53200, 36%), Kapoeta East (95440, 59%), Kapoeta North (99130, 65%) and Kapoeta South (50810, 53%). • IPC (August 2019) shows that out of the eight targeted areas: six counties fall under IPC4 (Aweil East, Akobo, Nyirol, Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East); whereas the other two counties under IPC 3 (Kapoeta South and Lafon) • FSNMS 24 reports shows severity of the malnutrition status in the target areas: Akobo (GAM 26.7%, SAM 9.9%); Nyirol (GAM 25.5%, SAM 5.4%); June 2019 SMART surevy in Aweil East shows GAM 15.5% and SAM 1.6%; Kapoeta East (GAM 11.7%, SAM 1.5%) as per SMART Survey December 2018; • The crop production assessment by FAO/WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission to South Sudan (CFSAM, March 2019) shows deficit of 2019: Akobo (15799 MT), Nyirol (17033 MT), Aweil East (20490MT), Kapoeta East (15968 MT), Kapoeta North (8153 MT), Kapoeta (6691 MT). - IDP/Returnees status: The IOM – Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM, June 2019) report shows the highest number of returnees have been reported. The number of returnees is expected to increase in the intervention areas because of the recent developments related to the ongoing revitalized peace agreement. Thus, it’s expected that the high population influx of people will aggravate the competition over access to land and water, basic services (health, schools), income generating/jobs, becoming a significant source of tension between the host communities and returnees. Thus, the same problem is expected in the proposed intervention areas except Aweil East where the IDP/returnee number is insignificant.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Save the Children will conduct community sensitization and information sharing sessions on project activities and explore ways of strengthening or setting up joint complain and feedback mechanisms to enhance accountability and community participation throughout the project period. Feedbacks from the community and stakeholders would also be collected during needs identifications, post-distribution monitoring, post-harvest assessments and meetings with beneficiaries. The target groups will be represented from girls, boys, women and men, people with disabilities, together with community and government leaders and community structures from onset of implementation, to ensure communities are well informed about the project and getting assistances considering their safety and consultation with identified individual needs and vulnerabilities. Any complaints from the beneficiaries about the project activities will be collected and feedback will be given by the Boma level project management committees. In addition to existing experiences, we will work with the communities to determine a most appropriate and suitable complaint response mechanism (CRM) in order to facilitate beneficiary complaint/feedback collection, complaints handling and response mechanism. We will continue to build capacities of staff as well as communities to ensure they are aware of their rights and entitlements. Issues raised could be discussed during the monthly coordination meeting for joint actions where due necessary. Feedbacks from the community and stakeholders would also be collected during needs identifications, post-distribution monitoring, post-harvest assessments and meetings with beneficiaries.
