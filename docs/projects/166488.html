Project Summary

Since 2009, the Boko Haram (herein, Armed Opposition Groups [AOGs]) insurgency has crippled Northeastern (NE) Nigeria. Borno State (Borno) has been particularly affected; of the 2.2 million people forced from their homes across the region, over half (1.5 million) are in Borno. AOGs also continue to attack LGAs in Northern Adamawa and Eastern Yobe states. The Global Humanitarian Needs Overview estimated that there are 7.9 million people in need in northeast Nigeria, of which approximately 4.3 million people live in Borno State (HNO,2020).
1.8 million People are still internally displaced across BAY states for Borno Adamawa and Yobe, and living in either a formal IDP camp or in an informal settlement with host communities which continues to overstretch the already limited water and sanitation infrastructures.
The Corona virus (COVID-19) pandemic poses a new threat to the already vulnerable population in NE Nigeria. As at 3rd May 2020, Nigeria has 2,388 confirmed COVID-19 cases with 85 deaths recorded. NE Nigeria has also seen a rapid spread of the virus and as at 3rd May 2020, cases were as follows: Borno (75 cases with 11 deaths), Adamawa (4 cases, 0 deaths) and Yobe (13 cases with 1 death).
In line with the United Nations program criticality assessment, the IRC will leverage existing experience and strong coordination with the State Ministry of water resources (SMoWR), State ministry of Health (SMoH), water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and health Partners, community leadership as well as existing community structures to help mitigate the transmission and effects of the pandemic among vulnerable populations in IDP and host communities across BAY states.
Activities will focus on Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 of the Humanitarian Response Plan (2019-2021) - Saving lives by intensifying handwashing promotion at community and household level, together with hand washing stations as well as promoting respiratory hygiene awareness in the target communities, providing timely and integrated multi-sector WASH assistance and Mainstreaming protection interventions to the most vulnerable populations. This will aim to improve safe access to water and sanitation; and increase people’s knowledge on how to identify and interrupt the transmission of COVID-19. The IRC will provide emergency water and sanitation facilities, and WASH NFIs, focusing particularly on soap. The IRC will work closely with both the SMoH run health facilities as well as those supported by health partners to improve health service delivery through the provision of adequate water, sanitation, Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) supplies which will be accompanied with the establishment and training of IPC committees. In the IDP camps, the IRC will also work closely with the CCCM and provide WASH support to established quarantine and isolation spaces managed by health partners working in these IDP camps. This will ensure the safety for both the suspected COVID-19 cases, care givers as well as health workers thus minimizing possible transmission of COVID-19.

1 - Needs

Since 2009, the Boko Haram (herein, Armed Opposition Groups [AOGs]) insurgency has crippled Northeastern (NE) Nigeria. Borno State (Borno) has been particularly affected; of the 2.2 million people forced from their homes across the region, over half are in Borno. AOGs also continue to attack LGAs in Northern Adamawa and Eastern Yobe states. The Global Humanitarian Needs Overview estimated that there are 7.9 million people in need in northeast Nigeria, of which approximately 4.3 million people live in Borno State (HNO, 2020). 1.8 million people are internally displaced across the BAY states and are living in either a formal IDP camps or in an informal settlements which has occasionally led to complaints from host community members about the increased competition for key services such as water sources. The crisis has also led to the destruction of key services and infrastructure including water sources, health facilities, sanitation systems and schools. This, combined with the congestion in the various IDP camps and host communities has led to overstraining of the already limited water and sanitation sources. Reduced access to health facilities has also led to increased caseloads for the few accessible health facilities leading to consequent overstretching of the existing water, sanitation and clinical waste management facilities. 35% of the health facilities with associated WASH services across BAY states were affected by the insurgency which makes them ill prepared to respond to COVID-19. Transmission has been is mainly through contact and air and prevention messages have encourage increased hygiene with a focus on hand washing, as well as appropriate social distancing. However, IDP camps in NE Nigeria are congested with most of the basic lifesaving services including WASH interventions provided communally, thus increasing challenges on preventing transmission.While WASH partners have continued to provide support in NE Nigeria, some gaps still exist due to increased population movements into garrison towns and existing IDP camps. According to a recent Multi-sector Needs Assessment for COVID-19 in BAY States, despite the improved water access in Borno state, 38% of population still have limited access to water leading to reduced personal and other general hygiene activities including handwashing within their homes. 25% of this population will spend between 15 to 30 minutes when collecting water, while another 25% spend more than 30 minutes during water collection times. This means prolonged exposure of the already vulnerable populations to crowds in a time when social distancing is encouraged. Lack of soap continues to endanger the health of IDP populations with 60% mentioning soap as a key item needed in their households to enhance hand washing behavior. Although Adamawa and Yobe states have experienced some stability, the populations still suffer from displacements as a result of flooding as well as from cholera outbreaks. Cholera outbreaks have continued to expose key WASH gaps in most health facilities that, though operational, are not adequately prepared to respond to the pandemic. The current capacity of the MoH to manage and treat suspected/confirmed COVID-19 cases is limited. Although the MoH has instructed all confirmed/suspected cases be referred to the MoH treatment centers, it is expected that these treatment centers will soon get overwhelmed with patients, creating a backlog of patients that will need to be isolated within Health facilities, Stabilization Centers (SCs) as well as in quarantine and isolation spaces constructed by CCCM within the IDP camps.

2 - Activities

In line with the United Nations program criticality assessment, the IRC will build on existing experience and strong coordination with key stakeholders to help mitigate the transmission and effects of the COVID-19 pandemic among vulnerable populations in IDP and host communities across BAY states. Activities will be carried out within a seven month period focused on Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 of the Humanitarian Response Plan (2019-2021). In IDP camps, the IRC will increase water supply from existing boreholes through increases in pumping hours of generator/ solar powered systems. The IRC will preposition chlorine, generator fuel, quick moving spare parts for hand pumps, and generators as well as spare pumps and piping that will ensure minimal interruption to water supply for vulnerable populations in case of breakdowns. Increasing water pumping hours will ensure the water distribution times is spread across the day so as to reduce crowding and ensure social distancing is maintained through enforcement by incentive WASHCo members stationed at each water point who will also be ensuring the handwashing station at these water points are functional and utilized.The IRC will preposition materials needed for quick rehabilitation of latrine and bathing facilities. The IRC will identify experienced team of carpenters and masons from the IDP population who will be responsible for repairs of the sanitation facilities. Also the IRC will preposition sanitation cleaning kits that will include latrine cleaning detergents, brooms and buckets to ensure continued care and maintenance of the sanitation facilities.The IRC seeks to identify and train a team of incentive cleaners and sprayers who will be responsible in the cleaning of communal latrines in the IDP camps. This team will be provided with PPE and latrine cleaning kits and sprayers and will be carry out the day to day cleaning and disinfecting of the latrines and bathing facilities until the pandemic is over. This team will also be responsible for the spraying of households with suspected COVID-19 cases and their surrounding homesteads lying within 30m radius. The IRC will coordinate with the CCCM and other WASH actors to provide water and sanitation and waste management facilities in newly constructed isolation and quarantine spaces within the IDP camps. The installation of these facilities will be followed up with training of the responsible health partners to ensure appropriate infection prevention and control (IPC) protocols are followed. The IRC will install and maintain communal handwashing stations at all main entrance gates to the IDP camps, meeting places, distribution locations, markets, latrine and bathing facilities as well as water collection points. Also the IRC will encourage the construction of household level hand washing facilities in the form of tippy taps using materials such as used water bottles that can be installed at individual households.The IRC will equip the WASH Committees in the IDP camps with sprayers, chlorine and PPE materials for disinfecting water points at least once a day and ensuring there is a functional hand washing station at the communal hand pump or water point area. The IRC will work closely with the SMoH run health facilities as well as those supported by health partners to improve health service delivery through provision of emergency safe water supply and sanitation facilities, IPC and PPE supplies as well as emergency clinical waste management facilities. Provision of these facilities and materials will be accompanied with the establishment of IPC committees. The IRC will carry out weekly monitoring to ensure IPC protocols and appropriate waste management are adhered to. COVID-19 WHO approved IEC materials will be printed and placed in the sanitation facilities, health facilities and other strategic communal places such as markets. The IRC will also prepare radio jingles to ensure large scale reach and sensitization on COVID-19

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project seeks to integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include separate sessions with women, girls, persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups, these sessions will ensure social distancing of at least 2m between participants who will also be provided with hand sanitizer, face masks and temperature taken upon coming into the meeting area. Existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums will also be used to collect beneficiary feedback on key quality markers. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s COVID-19 response WhatsApp group will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and serve as an additional reporting mechanism. Client satisfaction surveys that incorporates feedback from the vulnerable groups on water sanitation and WASH NFIs provided will be carried out on a monthly basis to ensure equitable access to WASH services among all population groups. Client exit interviews will be conducted via telephone to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allows the IRC to make rapid and informed adjustments to the project. Safety audits will also be conducted each month, where possible, with women and girls in the IDP camps to understand the protection related risks and concerns. Advocacy strategies will be developed together with other implementing partners, community structures, and female beneficiaries, to respond to identified needs.Phone interviews, text messaging and other virtual communication channels will be heavily relied on as a form of remote monitoring and client feedback mechanism
