Project Summary

The action aims to strengthen the resilience of conflict affected communities through the engagement of unemployed IDPs, returnees and host community members in income generating activities tailored to the specific needs of women and youth and other vulnerable groups. The action will be implemented through a distribution of self-employment grants to 1,950 individuals and provision of 500 self-sustenance grants for approximately 1,050 members of low income, families with many children, households with disabled members, elderly, single-headed households. Proposed activities aim to target people residing close to contact line (GCA and NGCA), and in rural areas in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts (GCA) with particular focus on agricultural businesses.

Needs

The economy in the Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA and NGCA) continues to deteriorate due to the instability, resulting in limited investment in the region’s industries and few employment opportunities, as outlined below: 1) The unemployment rate in Eastern Ukraine has increased since the onset of hostilities and the continued flow of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) further increases competition over limited employment opportunities. Economic development and boosting employment was determined as one of the four main strategies objectives and development priorities of the Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) until 2020. According to the National Monitoring System Report on the Situation of Internally Displaced Persons, June 2018, 49% of IDPs stated to be employed in Donetsk and Luhansk oblast (GCA);2) According to the latest IOM National Monitoring Survey (NMS) on the Situation of Internally Displaced Persons in Ukraine, Round 10 (covering the second quarter of 2018), 78% of individuals that have returned to the original place of residence in NGCA indicated the ownership of private property not requiring to pay rent as the main reason of return. As highlighted in the NMS report, the majority of the returnees assessed their financial situation as `enough funds for food only' (37%) or `enough funds for basic needs' (38%) and 17% have to limit their expenses even for food. As 77% of surveyed returnees plan to remain in the NGCA in the next three months, livelihood support to strengthen coping capacities and enable households to meet their urgent basic needs. Returnee households faced high level of insecurity, as 24% employed at least one negative coping strategy due to lack of food or lack of money to buy a food. Among the surveyed returnees to the NGCA within the NMS survey, the main source of income remained retirement and long service pension (68%);3) The results of the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by REACH during the period of September to October 2017 indicates an increase in the inability of the people in the NGCA to afford food and utilities. The respondents noted not to be able to cover their food needs as the price for food have increased (38%) and there is a problem with food availability in smaller settlements and settlements closest to the contact line (44%);4) Significant gender imbalances were also observed in all spheres of current employment of IDPs according to National Monitoring System (NMS), June 2018. Among IDPs actively looking for employment, 78% are women. The majority of IDPs who are actively looking for employment (51%) mentioned that lack of vacancies was one the most common difficulties they faced with, especially in rural areas. In response to identified needs, IOM targeted rural and urban areas located within 20km along the contact line and in NGCA, as well as areas that have witnessed a major process of return. Based on IOM observations and interaction with beneficiaries, many of those living in return communities in the NGCA and within 20km along the contact line face a particularly difficult situation, with extremely high unemployment rates reflecting the precarious and depressed economy. Moreover, specific locations for the implementation of project activities within Donetsk and Luhansk NGCA will be identified based on discussion with de facto authorities. To provide comprehensive support to communities that were targeted by IOM for provision winterization and hygiene support in NGCA and GCA, as well as distributes multi-purpose cash assistance among the vulnerable households residing along the contact line (GCA), districts and towns listed in the section Locations were selected for livelihood assistance.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: IDPs, returnees and members of host communities have increased access to income generation opportunities to cover their basic needs Output 1.1: Selected IDPs, returnees and host community members in areas of high concern in Luhansk and Donetsk GCA and NGCA set up or develop businesses initiatives with self-employment grants: 1) Conduct relevant assessment for livelihood activities to target locations to be supported during the project; 2) Conduct orientation sessions to assess beneficiaries’ skills and intentions, provide basic training and mentoring on self-employment and business plan preparation; 3) Disburse self-employment grants to selected beneficiaries who developed the best business plans, based on a competitive process and according to agreed-upon criteria; 4) Coaching and mentoring beneficiaries throughout project duration. The beneficiaries of self-employment trainings will be selected and prioritized based on their background and motivation, including but not limited to, the low family income-level, unemployment, ascertained IDP/returnee status, presence of members in their families who belong to vulnerability category and cannot contribute to the household income. IOM and IPs will ask selected beneficiaries to provide documents, as well as their copies for records, that proves their vulnerability to double check the data highlighted in the application forms. The selected participants will be invited for self-employment training sessions, during which they will develop their business plans and present to a panel in a two-round selection process. The first round is a presentation of individual business plans to the NGO and IOM representatives as well as other stakeholders, while the second one is an internal IOM analysis of the applications shortlisted in the first round.Trainees with the most promising business plans will receive a USD 750 in-kind grant to purchase the main assets (such as small farming kits, small greenhouse kits, coffee machines, etc.) needed for the further development of their businesses. The procurement and delivery process of the in-kind assets will be handled by ION and/or IP(s) in line with IOM procurement guidelines. Upon receipt of the necessary equipment, IOM beneficiaries will be asked to sign Beneficiary Handover Form (BAF). Output 1.2. Vulnerable households residing in Donetsk and Luhansk (NGCA) are provided with self-sustenance grants to improve economic-self-reliance and food security: 1) Select vulnerable households residing close to the contact line Donetsk and Luhansk regions (NGCA) through the verification process;2) Distribute procured assets among the selected beneficiaries; 3) Conduct implementation and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) through regular field trips, interviews and focus group discussions. The beneficiaries of household sustenance in-kind grants will be identified through community-based work conducted by IOM and the IP(s) in coordination with municipal de facto authorities and representatives of the organized civil society. This activity will prioritize the reintegration of returnees, but will also include support to local community members in vulnerable conditions but able to increase their sustenance capacities through the provision of a grant. IOM and IP will ask priory selected households to provide documents, as well as their copies, that proves their vulnerability during the verification process. The procurement of in-kind grants items for household sustenance activities will be conducted by the selected IP(s) in line with IOM procurement guidelines. All procurement will be carried out through a competitive process aiming at identifying suppliers registered and paying taxes in the GCA. The IP representatives will be responsible for at least one contact monthly with the project beneficiaries (via personal visit, telephone or skype call) to verify usage of the assets provided as grant, review progress and achievements.

Indicators & targets

Outcome: IDPs, returnees and members of host communities have increased access to income generation opportunities to cover their basic needs: 1) % of targeted beneficiaries reporting an increase in monthly income (Target 70%); 2) % of targeted beneficiaries reporting their engagement in the project supported them to meet their basic needs (Target 70%). Output 1.1: Selected IDPs, returnees and host community members in areas of high concern in Luhansk and Donetsk GCA and NGCA set up or develop micro businesses with self-employment grants - 1) 130 self-employment orientation sessions conducted throughout the project implementation period; 2) 3,250 participants (of whom an estimated 1,625 female and 1,625 male) in self-employment orientation sessions throughout the project implementation period; 3) 1,950 beneficiaries (of whom an estimated 975 female and 975 male) receiving self-employment grants, coaching and mentoring during the project lifespan. Output 1.2. Vulnerable households residing in Donetsk and Luhansk (NGCA) are provided with self-sustenance grants to improve economic-self-reliance and food security - 1) 500 self-sustenance grants distributed among the vulnerable households; 2) 1,050 members of the households (of whom an estimated 945 adults (473 female and 472 male) and 105 children (53 female and 52 male) benefited from the provision of in-kind materials and equipment. IOM will conduct regular monitoring and evaluation activities in all project locations to assess progress against set project indicators, to collect sex and age disaggregated data and to allow for necessary adjustments that might arise as lessons learnt. Phone surveys, individual interviews and monitoring on-sight visits will be carried out by IOM to collect both quantitative and qualitative data to assess the level of satisfaction and how beneficiaries are impacted by project activities. Drafting of regular and ad hoc progress reports, infographics, briefs and other documents for the donors and the broader humanitarian community, as well as a final evaluation report meant to assess performance and inform future similar programming will also be carried out.
