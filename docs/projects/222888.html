Project Summary

Climate change poses both inter- and intergenerational threats. However, children, especially in developing and least developed countries, will suffer the most due to poor socio-economic background, entrenched gender inequalities, and culture issues. For example, in Bangladesh, one in three children is directly affected by the impacts of climate change (UNICEF, 2024).
According to IPCC (2001a), countries such as Bangladesh, Maldives, etc. will be adversely affected by climate change because of their vulnerability due to different categories of disasters. Climate-related stressors, such as heat, wildfires, storms, floods, droughts, diseases, and rising sea levels, affect education outcomes. Given the varying climate challenges in different communities across Bangladesh, there is a need to sensitise learners to local climate situations. In primary and secondary schools, there could be opportunities to apply experiential learning where learners can sensitise themselves with local indigenous knowledge and livelihood practices, as well as take ownership of solving local environmental problems (https://www.dhakatribune.com/opinion/op-ed/337332/restructuring-climate-education-in-bangladesh).
Education is key to encouraging, motivating, and preparing children and young people to undertake appropriate decisions and actions on addressing climate change. The Paris Agreement recognised enhancing climate education as one of the most important elements to undertaking appropriate actions. Recognising education as an important medium of acquiring knowledge on climate change, Friendship will implement a project on educating children on climate change in the Rohingya communities in Cox’s Bazar.
The project aims to provide education to Rohingya children on acquiring knowledge and skills to undertake decisions and immediate and preparatory actions to address climate change-induced disasters. These children reside in flimsy shelters/camps and are highly vulnerable to climatic events. Diverse contents of climate education, e.g., weather, climate and climate change, greenhouse gases, the carbon cycle, the water cycle, renewable energy.
Overall objective: To ensure that all the Rohingya children and Host community children acquire basic knowledge on climate change and to promote sustainable behaviors, actions and self-sufficiency in times of emergencies.
Target beneficiaries: A total 6305 of Rohingya children and 2000 host community children with an age from 11 years to 18 years.
Expected outcomes
· Increased knowledge on climate change among the students.
· Increased self-sufficiency and a sense of awareness during emergencies.
· Increased community participation for climate education
Activities of the project:
Climate Education
· 4 Climate Education Club will be established at Camp 7, 13, 17 and 19. 5 climate education club will be established at 5 Unions of host community. These clubs will be used for learners’ facilitation.
· Organize climate education sessions covering all targeted learners. Total 5 contents will be facilitated to learners. Each learner will receive one content in two months. Learners will attend the classes after their regular session once in two months.
· Develop and disseminate educational materials on climate change such as rhyme books, photo-stories, documentary movies, cartoons etc. These activities will be physically facilitated by Climate education facilitator.
· Organize outdoor activities and field trips to have hands-experience related to climate change.
· Role-play and drama on climate change will be used/demonstrated.
Awareness -raising activities
· Organize clean-up and tree-planting campaigns and initiatives.
Capacity-building
· Arrange training, workshop, activities to equip facilitators and community members on the basics of climate change.
Monitoring and Evaluation
· Pre- and post-short assessment will be conducted to assess the knowledge gained on climate change

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Bangladesh is exposed to both disasters and climate change and is ranked seventh most disaster risk-prone country in the world as per the Global Climate Risk Index. The triggers humanitarian crises on a cyclical basis. The Rohingya crisis is now entering its seventh year and has become a protracted refugee and protection crisis. Over 967,000 Rohingya FDMN, displaced from the Rakhine state of Myanmar in 2017, reside in Myanmar, mainly in Ukhiya and the teknaf of Cox’s Bazar. Due to its location, Cox’s Bazar is particularly vulnerable to disasters like flash floods caused by erratic rain during monsoon seasons and frequent cyclones. Climate change and environmental risks are the lived reality for today’s students especially those in disadvantaged communities of coastal areas like Cox’s Bazar. There is no better future without tackling climate change. It’s therefore crucial that we support learners and their leadership so that they can pioneer the necessary solutions to the climate crisis through their values, creativity, and collaboration now and throughout their lives, and climate education needs to be at the heart of that endeavour. Yet out of 100 countries surveyed by UNESCO in 2021, only 53 national curricula make reference to climate change and environmental sustainability (UNESCO (2021), Getting Every school climate-ready). In most cases, the depth of including climate change and environmental sustainability in the curricula is minimal, and actual implementation in schools and classrooms is lagging. Only 3% of Bangladeshi grade 8 students could correctly answer a set of six basic climate change questions, said the report, titled "Choosing Our Future: Education for Climate Action." Besides, just 32% of secondary students were able to answer at least one out of the six questions, the report found, exploring the role of education in responding to climate change and the challenges climate change poses to education systems. (https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/education/bangladesh-lags-climate-education-wb-report-936241).In the circumstances of Cox’s bazaar, considering the context, geographical location, effects of climate change on host communities, nature of livelihoods along with the Rohingya crisis, dense population, and exposure to vulnerability, climate education for children is highly required to adapt. Additionally, after the transition to Myanmar curriculum, every partner is striving to provide quality education to learners. Climate change education will offer the best scope to engage the learners with the illustrated contents and videos. According to the ISNA-2024, education enrolment in particular among adolescents (12-18 years), especially girls, remained low (only 49%) in the camps in enrolment. It’s been found that adolescent girls aged 12 to 18 are more likely than boys to be missing school. J-MSNA 2023 data shows that the reason behind girls missing schools is education not being considered a priority, household chores, protection risks, cultural norms, whereas boys are missing school due to attending Madrasha. Limited data exists on the number of Rohingya adolescents who are currently engaged with education opportunities, including pre-vocational programs, especially life skills. However, the limited range of educational opportunities exposes them to exploitation, increases exposure to protection risks, and can lead to negative coping mechanisms. Gendered vulnerability to environmental hazards is compounded by systemic inequalities, poverty, and limited access to resources. Integrating these elements into this project creates a more inclusive approach that addresses systemic inequalities, elevates women’s contributions, and strengthens resilience across all communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project is intended to include protection principles to ensure access, safety, and dignity, and promote and protect the rights of FDMNs, inclusion children with disability, adolescent girls without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. A child protection risk assessment is also planned to close the gap, and appropriate action will be done based on the situation. This project intends to spread information on child protection as part of an educational program, such as separation and trafficking prevention or mine-risk education. The project will create shared referral systems with child protection professionals. The project will strongly Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended adverse effects of its intervention, to enhance people’s well-being and to reduce the vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. This project will make sure that the learning environment is physically secure, including having first aid supplies on hand and teachers who are trained in first aid, well-known emergency evacuation procedures, equipment and facilities in good condition, and staff restrooms that are separate for boys and girls. Positive discipline and non-violent classroom management techniques will be promoted through project activities. Protection mainstreaming will be ensured throughout the project by training and sensitizing the project and partner staff to equip with Knowledge and support to act in line with CP minimum standards. The safety of program activities and sites, and access to them will be assessed and monitored and activities will be adapted and/or advocacy conducted with relevant authorities to reduce risks and promote safety. Community Education Support Group (CESG) and parents will be engaged to help monitor, report, and refer identified protection cases within education interventions through the feedback mechanisms and ensure timely response as required.

2.3 - Age & Disability

By setting the following tactics into practice, this climate education initiative in Cox's Bazar that serves primary and secondary students from the host community and Rohingya community ensured inclusive of all ages and disabilities: Allow children to enter and exit the education system at different points in time to accommodate their individual needs and circumstances. Provide accelerated learning opportunities for older children who are out of school or have fallen behind due to disruptions in their education. Implement UDL principles to create a learning environment that is accessible to all learners, regardless of their abilities. Assistive technology: Support to provide assistive technology, such as hearing aids, wheelchairs, and adaptive learning software, to support learners with disabilities. Train teachers to effectively teach students with disabilities, including strategies for accommodating different learning styles and needs. Provide early identification and intervention services for children with disabilities to help them reach their full potential. Additionally, creating a safe and supportive learning environment, accessible infrastructure, reasonable accommodation as per requirement where all children feel welcome and valued, initiating culturally sensitive and respectful of the diverse backgrounds of the learners, engaging with the community to ensure that the education project is responsive to the needs of all children, including those with disabilities and those from different age groups. By implementing these strategies, the education project in Cox's Bazar can ensure that it is inclusive of all children, regardless of their age or disability. This will help to create a more equitable and just education system that benefits all members of the community.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Mainstreaming PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) in a climate education project in Cox's Bazar involves embedding PSEA principles and practices into the project's design, implementation, and monitoring. This ensures that PSEA is not just an add-on but an integral part of the project's overall approach to safeguarding children. Organization developed PSEA guideline (PSEA Policy) where clearly define PSEA, outline the project's commitment to preventing SEA, and establish procedures for reporting, investigating, and responding to SEA incidents. Project will conduct a risk assessment from which potential risks of SEA will be identified and develop mitigation strategies to address these risks. This project will ensure that all staff members, including teachers, administrators, and non-teaching staff, are trained on PSEA principles, prevention measures, reporting procedures, and their roles and responsibilities in protecting children. Implementing measures to create a safe and supportive learning environment where children feel comfortable and empowered to report SEA incidents. This includes establishing clear communication channels, promoting open dialogue, and fostering a culture of respect and inclusion. This project will designate a specific person or team to be responsible for overseeing PSEA implementation within the project. This focal point will have the authority and resources to effectively implement PSEA measures. Incorporate PSEA messaging and activities into the project's curriculum, teacher training, and community engagement initiatives. This ensures that PSEA awareness and prevention strategies are embedded throughout the project. Develop a clear and accessible reporting mechanism for children and adults to report SEA incidents without fear of reprisal. This mechanism ensure that reports are handled confidentially and promptly. Regularly monitor the implementation of PSEA measures and evaluate their effectiveness in preventing SEA incidents.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The topic of climate change and environment needs to be taught through an all-inclusive and integrated approach, addressing the topic of climate and environment as a distinct interdisciplinary subject. Building the adaptation and resilience capacity of our communities, and young people as climate leaders helping build a climate resilient future for our nation, should be one of our foremost goals. This project involves the youths aged 13-18 years as direct beneficiary. The project will support the youth by raising awareness and facilitate education about climate change and its impacts. Educational activities will be organized, including discussions, workshops, capacity building on climate change, participation in debates, drawing and essay competitions, and exposure visits. Additionally, children will contribute to community awareness through cultural movements, campaigns, public hearings, and human chains. Consequently, the climate education session and training equips the youth with the necessary skills and knowledge to provide appropriate supervision, guidance, and support to become an agent of change and involve themselves on climate action activities at the community level. Youth can make a difference in tackling the challenges posed by climate change.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Education program of Friendship in the Cox's Bazar Rohingya context actively promotes self-reliance and resilience among FDMNs. Children and adolescents receive life skills and climate education training, equipping them with practical abilities on climate education that enhance their ability. It integrates resilience-building elements, such as problem-solving, critical thinking, and conflict resolution, to help learners cope with the challenges of displacement. The program engages parents and community members in awareness sessions, training, fostering a supportive environment where education becomes a cornerstone for resilience and future opportunities. These initiatives collectively contribute to creating a more empowered FDMN community capable of adapting to ongoing challenges. Environmental education actions are arising from these initiatives in the form of training courses, field trips, or debates, among other formal and non-formal learning experiences.

Located along the Bay of Bengal in south-eastern Bangladesh, Cox’s Bazar, with a population of 2.3million people, has always been particularly susceptible to environmental disasters, including floods and landslides. One of the major objective for the host communities link to development initiatives is to Strengthening environmental/climate resilience through development of improved disaster preparedness, response and recovery mechanisms. This project aims to support 200 adolescents of Ukhiya aged 11-18 years where objective is to increase the knowledge and understanding on climate and involvement of community stakeholders and local policy makers to build resilience among the community. Climate change education can help people understand the climate crisis and develop the skills and values to take action. This can help communities become more resilient and adaptable.
