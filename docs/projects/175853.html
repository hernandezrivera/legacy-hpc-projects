Project Summary

South Sudan continues to face crises, including violent conflict, insecurity, flooding, and the impact of COVID-19 pandemic, all of which have contributed to a protracted humanitarian crisis. The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 8.3 million people in need according to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview. Raga, Pibor, Tonj North and Rumbek East Counties are among the hard to reach counties in South Sudan as stipulated by the WASH Cluster and UNOCHA with demanding WASH needs particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. The WASH condition is also demanding in these Counties with critical needs in access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services to the IDPs, returnees and the host community. Peace Corps Organization, PCO intends to scale up water sanitation and hygiene services to these vulnerable group of people through an integrated approach in Nutrition and GBV sectors. Peace Corps Organization target a total of 16,000 beneficiaries from the Pibor, Tonj North, Raga and Rumbek East Counties with Provision of safe, reliable and sustainable water will be addressed through rehabilitating water points and developing new protected hand dug wells both at the community and institution level so that the affected population can have access to safe and adequate amount (15L/person/day) of water for drinking and other domestic uses. The rehabilitation of water points will be done after a GBV risk analysis have been conducted in the focus groups discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staffs and co-chaired by a GBV partner active on ground considering the safety and best location thus reducing the risk of occurrence of GBV cases and sexual exploitation of women and girls since they are the primary beneficiary of water. Nutrition facilities such as OTP/TSFP/SC, Education facilities such as Schools and protection sites such as women and girls friendly spaces and child friendly spaces will be prioritized by PCO as part of integration among the various sectors. The water point’s rehabilitation will be done by trained hand pump mechanics selected among the locals in the community and the spares from the WASH core-pipeline hence ensuring acceptability, ownership and sustainability. Water Management Committees will take care of these water points and will teach the community on best practices to ensure apron is clean and also fencing the water points to prevent animals from defecating at the aprons.
Sanitation situation will be addressed through rehabilitation of latrines fully equipped with hand washing facilities to ensure safe disposal of human excreta and hand washing at critical times. The rehabilitation will be done after consultation through FGDs on best siting which is safe and will have doors and locks to ensure privacy, safety and dignity on usage and will prevent occurrence of GBV and other forms of abuse associated with the use of latrines. This project will ensure communities are taught by WASH staffs and Nutrition/Health staffs trained on WASH minimum package on how to improve hygiene situation through household visits, treatment and storage of drinking water to prevent occurrence and transmission of Cholera and other water borne diseases, hygiene creation and awareness campaigns with emphasis on regular hand-washing with soap and chlorinated water, wearing of mask, maintenance 1.5-2 metres social distances and use of sanitizer at critical times, safe disposal of human wastes.

1 - Needs

The cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 8.3 million people (about two thirds of the population) in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection according to South Sudan Humanitarian Response Monitoring Report 2021. According HNO 2020 an estimated 5.6 million people will negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. Many of these affected population have the highest proportion of HHs that are continually dependent on unprotected water sources, and have limited or no access to latrines and WASH NFIs. According to REACH south Sudan situation overview 2019, high proportion of HHs (74%) reporting having a water borne related diseases, with malaria the most commonly reported (74%), fever (56%) and then by AWD (20%). Women and girls sometimes are forced to walk for long distance through forests and insecure areas so as to access water exposing them to various forms of GBV and additional dangers from armed groups since majority of them are responsible for fetching water. For the households that can access safe water, a lot of time is spent in the water points with an average of 1200 households per hand pump spending more than 30 minutes and walking more than 500 meters which is below the sphere standards which may be a source of conflict between the host and the visiting community. The operational hand pumps in Raga, Pibor, Tonj North and Rumbek East Counties are overused and has led to more breakdown of pumps and increased queuing time at the viable water sources causing reduced per capita water consumption (under 15 L/person/day), hence increased consumption from unprotected water sources like untreated rivers and hand dug wells and also reduced time for other essential tasks at their homes. This competition and pressure on the scarce water is a driver to conflict between the host communities, returnees, the IDPs and even animals. Access to safe and gender sensitive latrines remains a big issue during both wet and dry seasons and thus open defecation is predominant in these areas of Warrap, Jonglei, Lakes and Western Bahr El Ghazal. Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021 estimates that about 10% of the community have safe latrines and they use it appropriately leaving an approximately 90% of population practicing open defecation. The sanitation and hygiene situation is further worsened by the fact that most of the community does not have access to a hand washing facility, poor knowledge and practices on personal hygiene and environmental conservation practices. Women have also been excluded and sidelined from leadership roles and opportunities which are associated with a little stipend or incentives. PCO intends to address this through women empowerment and to be heading committees. Community members especially the IDPs and returnees may lack capacity building and this project will address this through various trainings that will impact skills such as hand pump mechanics.

2 - Project activities

• Community mobilization to introduce the project clearly stating the objectives, duration and project implementing period, activities to be carried out, target population to the community and other stakeholders.• Training of enumerators and hand pump mechanics who will help the community in borehole mapping and repair of the boreholes and protected hand dug wells to ensure sustainability of the project.• Rehabilitation and repair of boreholes and protected hand dug wells- This will be done by trained hand pump mechanics after a GBV risk analysis through FGDs with women and girls using the in-kind supplies requested from the core pipeline.• Development of new protected hand dug wells where there is no existing borehole to provide safe water to the vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and the hosts.• Rehabilitation of latrines will also be done in or near institutions after a GBV risk analysis has been done and the latrines will have locks and doors to enhance privacy, dignity and security and hence reducing the risk of exploitation, such as sexual exploitation and abuse.• Establishment of hand washing stations and installation of the facilities at the rehabilitated sanitation facilities to ensure communities wash their hand after defecation.• Transport and distribution of WASH NFIs requested from the WASH core pipeline to the beneficiaries and monitoring their use. This will be done by WASH staff and also Nutrition/ GBV/Health staffs trained on WASH minimum packages including demonstration on usage.• Selection and training of community hygiene and sanitation promoters/ volunteers to visit households with messages of improved WASH which will help influence positive behavior change to the community.• In schools, WASH club will be established with student members and teachers as patrons. School PTA members will also be formed and trained so as to train children in schools on WASH through Child to Child (C to C) approach• Streamlining GBV Training to WASH staffs after being trained by the protection teams active on ground.• Establishing and training water management committees on best ways of managing water points including guiding the elderly, sick, people living with disabilities, animals and children at the water points to ensure sustainability of the project.• Training of Adolescent girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management both in schools and community level including demonstration on usage of menstrual hygiene management kits.• Training of Nutrition and Health staffs on WASH minimum package as part of integration among the different sectors.• Training water management committee members and Hand pump technicians on SEA policy.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will take into consideration protection from sexual exploitation and abuse issues to all the beneficiaries. Women and girls will be consulted through FGDs on the best site considering safety and accessibility before rehabilitating water sources and latrines so as to reduce their exposure to GBV and other forms of abuse when visiting these sites. In addition, the rehabilitated latrines will be gender disaggregated to avoid men and women meeting while protecting their privacy and exposing them to sexual exploitation and abuse. The water management committees will be trained and will discourage fetching water during the night since may also expose the beneficiaries to a lot of harm. PCO will train WASH staffs on cross-cutting issues such as GBV and protection to provide sensitization to the beneficiaries on GBV and other related abuses and ways to minimize their occurrence so as not to cause more harm to the beneficiaries. PCO has developed Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse policies which guides all the Organization staff and volunteers. PCO staff will sign PSEA code of conduct before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. PCO will ensure that training is conducted to all the staff members both at Juba office and field staff on PSEA before the beginning of the project implementation. The training will then be extended to beneficiaries during project inception. This training to the beneficiaries will be done by Juba based staff members and not by field teams to avoid biases, victimization or any conflict of interest. The beneficiaries will be trained on communication channels available for them to report PSEA and the contact persons. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. Distribution and training of adolescent girls on usage of menstrual hygiene kits will also be done by a PCO female staff so that the girls feel free and hence also protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life-cycle of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee comprises of diverse representatives such as pupils representative, Community Hygiene Promoters, Water Management Committee Members, Local authority representatives, Beneficiaries representatives, Community Elders with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). Communities will be encouraged to identify multiple information dissemination channels that leverage, where relevant and appropriate, multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. Low literacy options will be included, and women specifically consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. The CFM will also outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by PMCs. Information will be fed back to Project Management Team who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and work plans accordingly. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC via scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing two-way communication. PMCs will also publicize and manage a complaints and response mechanism which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

PCO will adopt a consultative approach in the implementation of this project. All the beneficiaries (men, women, boys and girls) will be directly involved in the entire response cycle from inception until exit and also be informed on the duration, activities and the roles of different stakeholders in the project. This will be done by ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project. Details on recruitment and other arising issues will be communicated in forums such as public meetings and Focus Group Discussions which will be held with the different segregated groups of people (Men, women, girls and boys) to ensure community gets timely information which enhances active involvement and sense of ownership of the project. The local leaders among other relevant authorities and other key stakeholders will also be invited during project launch and since they are the representatives of the community, they will give the community information about the assistance to be provided. PCO have developed a tool for feedback and complaint mechanism which we have been using in the previous similar projects and have always proven to be efficient. The local leaders and other community representatives will give their opinions and complaints as well as possible recommendations. PCO have locally recruited and trained Community Hygiene Promoters who conduct household visits and are able to get feedback directly from the beneficiaries and they are addressed immediately depending on the urgency. We also have sessions with the beneficiaries during our field supervisions where we discuss the challenges, project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach. All the views from the beneficiaries are then screened and processed further and we use this criteria as a complaint/ feedback mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

PCO have developed a tool for feedback and complaint mechanism which we have been using in the previous similar projects and have always proven to be efficient .The PMCs committee which composed of Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedback directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiary’s advice and lenses and they are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. PCO have locally recruited and trained Community Hygiene Promoters who conduct household visits and are able to get feedback directly from the beneficiaries and they are addressed immediately depending on the urgency. We also have sessions with the beneficiaries during our field supervisions where we discuss the challenges, project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach will be dealt with. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. PCO is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. PCO is a very active national NGO on GBV in the three States and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. PCO, together with other agencies will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools PCO will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. PCO staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. PCO is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.
