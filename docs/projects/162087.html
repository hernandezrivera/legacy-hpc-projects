Project Summary

Our proposed programme for 2020 will target Camp 4 (6428HHs), Camp 4 Extension (908HHs), Camp 5 (500HHs), Camp 19 (4351HHs), Camp 20 (1558HHs) and Camp 20 Extension (1537HHs) and Host community Ukhia-Raja palong (1000HHs),Ukhia-palongkhali(1000HHs) with a targeted Total 17282HHs for camps & host community. We aim to provide a range of more brick constructed durable Site improvement/Development interventions using a community-led approach in the aforementioned camps for both the Rohingya refugees and host community, which would deal with the varied needs arising from material deterioration, difference in topography, settlement density and individual household needs in existing self-settled areas, re-developed and newly developed areas. We believe that the interventions we have undertaken can contribute towards improving social cohesion and strengthening household resilience. Our project will ensure Coordination with national authorities, sector partners and sector coordinators to identify and address priority gaps and needs in assistance, Coordination with CiCs, site management agencies and communities . Caritas Bangladesh has planned to coordinate with SMSD Sector at Cox’s bazar level by attending sector meeting, TWIG etc. As well as with direct communication with the SMSD sectors & site management at camp level as needed. Our main activities includes HHs level with limited bamboo bridge, bamboo retention wall, bamboo stepping, bamboo drainage,bamboo slope protection and stabilization , jutebag pathway, etc. But we will be mainly focused Community level site improvement and maintenance works on Low cost brick structure such as Brick pathway,Brick drainages, Stepping by bricks, Low height brick retention wall,cement drainage etc. in the locations where previous two year bamboo structures was constructed & already damaged. Training/orientation will be provided on site improvement/DRR and maintenance with different techniques to man,women,boys & Girls considering Gender age diversity, Installation and maintenance of solar streetlights will contribute to ensure safety, especially for women, children and vulnerable groups. In addition, we will also explore more durable alternative building materials which can be considered for site improvement interventions. Caritas Bangladesh is committed to implementing Site development/improvement interventions with community led approach , as well as resilient communities who will be better able to support themselves in the longer term with maintenance.

Needs

"Refugees are residing in makeshift settlements that are severely below basic humanitarian standards, overcrowded, and will not withstand seasonal climatic conditions. The monsoon has already severely damaged many facilities and infrastructure that were mostly built with bamboo, sand bags and rope. In addition, bamboo has also deteriorated due to beetle infestation, with many installations of extremely poor quality that now require a second investment to upgrade. Women, girls, childrens and boys are disproportionately affected by risks within the camps including safe site accessibility. Mobility restrictions due to lack or poor quality site improvement are of particular a concern of protection, especially for women, children and vulnerable groups. In addition, lack of lighting is one of the main reasons why women are not comfortable going out at night or using showers and latrines. Some cash-for-work activities are available to men, but women and vulnerable groups have even fewer options due to cultural restrictions of working outside their home.Settlements are mainly located in hilly typography with exposure to risks such as landslide, cyclone, flooding, erosion, and in some locations elephant attacks. The humanitarian situation in the camps is further exacerbated by heavy rainfall during monsoon which complicates access to and within the camps which has serious impact on the physical and psychological well-being of the refugees, and especially for Young children, Adolescent girls ,Young Adults, middle age adults, older Adults and refugees/host communities with disabilities. The scale and congestion of sites, especially Kutupalong-Balukhali Expansion Site, presents serious protection risks. In the mostly unplanned site, density is as high as 8m2 of land per person, compared to the international emergency standard of 45m2. Congestion and the terrain (steep slopes and flood-prone low-lying areas) make it challenging to improve access, mitigate environmental risks, and install essential infrastructure for basic service provision. After rains the soil becomes slippery and this reduces mobility and access even further. Deforestation exacerbates the risk of landslides and flooding which occurs during monsoon season. Major weather events including rain, flooding, and wind will almost certainly cause widespread destruction across all locations. Solid waste is disposed all around, polluting the environment. Water bodies need cleaning and drainage canals often get blocked with solid waste. There are pockets in camps without adequate drainage systems. Wastewater from shelters, bathing facilities and washing sites get stagnated around the settlement causing health risks. Plot marking, plot levelling, establishment of ground cover, soil and slope stabilization, dredging of main waterways, construction of foot bridges ,pathways, drainage, retention wall construction are key site improvement elements, which need to be in place to facilitate access to and inside the camps. As the response enters its third year, the need to introduce more durable materials should be a top priority. Morever, This 2020 Caritas want to continue Low cost brick structure such as Brick pathway, Brick drainages, Stepping by bricks, Low height brick retention wall etc. in the locations where previous two year bamboo structures was constructed & already damaged. As well as training/orientation is highly needed to strengthen Capacity building & ownership."
