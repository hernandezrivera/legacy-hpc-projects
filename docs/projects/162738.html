Project Summary

Many of the IDP children and young people who live in the protracted displacement in South Kordofan do not have access to formal education and employment opportunities. Therefore, this project aims at promoting enrollment in formal elementary school and improving job skills by providing supplementary classes to the children and vocational training to the youth

1 - Needs

Since the outbreak of the conflict in 2011,　JVC has supported people affected by the conflict, about eight years have passed since the outbreak of the conflict, and many people began to make a living without relying on food assistance. The life has shown a certain level of stability. However, there are reality that many children, including displaced persons, cannot access administrative services including education. Therefore, since 2016, we have been focusing on child support, providing birth registration support and school construction necessary for school attendance. School buildings are being constructed so people who have started returning after the situation has stabilized can attend school in the original village.Along with the collapse of the former government, the interim administration has shown a stance to promote peace with anti-government organizations and is expected to create a society for social stability, but children who have not received sufficient education, It is difficult to demonstrate sufficient ability to create a social infrastructure. In particular, many children in the refugee area have lost their opportunity to enter regular elementary schools because they have reached the middle and upper grades without attending school. There are also many young people who do not have enough education and vocational skills to find jobs. Many people move to Khartoum and other cities in search of jobs, but often they have simple jobs under severe working conditions and environments. In addition, since many local NGOs that were deeply involved were ordered to suspend their activities after the collapse of the former government, there was concern about the lack of support, and the role that international NGOs will play an important role in stabilizing the community.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1-1 Support for supplementary schoolsThe number of school-age children who are not attending school in Kadugli is higher in displaced areas, accounting for 30-50% of school-age children in each region.Boys and girls who have not been able to receive education in four places of refugee settlements (Tilo, Murta, Tafuri, Shair, Gardud). Encourage students to return to school by conducting supplementary classes. In general, the age of the target children is 9-18 years. (A) Training for teachersProvide a 5-day training for teachers prior to the start of a supplementary class. In the training, teachers learn children's protection, children's rights, how to interact with children, etc. (24 people: 3 teachers x 8 schools).(B) Implementation of supplementary lessonsThere are two levels of classes at each supplementary school. For students who do not attend regular school, a six-month supplemental classes aimed at acquiring academic skills necessary for school education and adapting to school life. (320 people: 20 people x 2 levels x 8 schools). Target people are children who have never attended school or who have been attending school but are not attending school at present. In addition, teaching materials and teaching tools for providing supplementary lessons are supported by each class, and school supplies such as notebooks and pens and school lunches are provided to participating children.In addition, with supervisors dispatched from the Ministry of Education and visiting each school, reviewing meetings are conducted about once a month to share experiences and review activities among teachers.(C) Supplementary school building and equipment supportThe supplementary schools are implemented using regular elementary school buildings, but almost all schools accept capacity or more, and there is a shortage of classrooms that accept children who complete the supplementary classes and enter regular schools. . Therefore, based on the surveys and interviews conducted by the community and school, the relevant ministries and agencies will discuss the building site and obtain approval. Based on the discusstion, two classroom buildings will be constructed in two schools in the area where school buildings are lacking, and equipment such as desks and chairs will be supported (two schools in total). School supplies such as blackboards will be provided to existing classrooms.1-2 Vocational trainingVocational training under the guidance of specialists will be conducted mainly for young people over 15 years old who can read, write and calculate in the supplementary classes supported in the first year. (A) Vocational trainingTo obtain a stable income in the future, acquire skills suitable for each occupation in 3 months (100 people: 25 people x 4 groups). The contents of vocational training are scheduled to be welding, furniture manufacturing, food processing, and sewing.(B) Livelihood technology improvement workshopIn addition to vocational training, conduct workshops on communication skills, problem solving skills, and self-development necessary for employment.(C) Vocational field trainingConduct practical training in the workplace with the aim of practicing and improving the skills acquired through vocational technical training.
