Project Summary

Gender based violence (GBV) is very common throughout Somaliland due to existing conflicts, poverty, , multiple displacements, loss of livelihoods and community protection systems, and droughts. This makes impacts on the lives and well-being of women, adolescent girls and children in general. GBV risks and exposure includes distance to water points, schools, markets and other necessary service sites. Lack of lockable latrines, poor lighting in IDPs camps and overcrowded shelters/lack of privacy. Media and other programs reports of gang rapes, homicide, forced abortion of young pregnant adolescents, attempted suicide of rape survivors and increasing levels of FGM are in common place.
Women and girls living in IDP camps are more prone to sexual violence and abuse than those living in host communities with a better housing infrastructure and service sites and markets. GBV assessment made by Nagaad Network informs 13% of households were concerned that girls in host communities and 17% in IDP camps were exposed to sexual violence and harassment, while 16% (host communities and 18% (IDP) households were concerned that girls were to be forcibly married. In addition to that, it was found that 29% of respondents lacking proper bathing facilities, 26% poor lighting, and 14% privacy in shelter, 31% lacked lockable latrines; 93% use common latrines that are far away or not in proximity. The GBVIMS reports indicated that 62% of the reported GV incidents were physical assaults, 11%rape, 10%sexual assault, 8% denial of resources, while 6% psychological/emotional abuse and the remaining 4% forced and early marriage. Moreover, women and adolescent girls are in critical need of relying on direct cash and voucher assistance to meet basic survival and material needs to improve their resilience to mitigate/escape GBV. Given the widespread food insecurity in Somaliland, there may be a possibility of vulnerable women and adolescent girls exchanging sex for food. Reliance on cash support is providing a buffer for poor families but may also contribute to rising intimate partner violence with divergent priorities for families on where to invest the cash support. Increasing population in need for GBV services, limited services provision to cover the landscape of needs is a major concern for the GBV humanitarian response in Somaliland.
Therefore, to respond to the above-stated problems related to GBV, Nagaad Network (Somaliland’s biggest women organization) is intended to target the displaced and non-displaced communities in the six regions of Somaliland including all age groups, women and girls living with disabilities, widowed and divorced women, married adolescents and women and adolescent girls from minority clans. Some of Nagaad Network’s key interventions will include:
1) Support access to comprehensive multi-sectoral GBV services (medical legal, psychosocial) for 450 women and girls.
2) Strengthen the capacity/and mobilize 60 PSS counsellors; 60 health workers to provide timely, quality, and confidential PSS and rape treatment.
3) Support the establishment of 6 GBV one-stop center in every region of Somaliland.
4) Provide 2,000 dignity kits and 36 post rape kits dignity protection for women and girls and treatment of rape.
5) Support 300 women and girls’ survivors of GBV with cash voucher assistance and grants for livelihood.

1. - Projects activities

The major objective of this project is to broaden access for services for GBV survivors through direct cash and voucher assistance to meet immediate basic needs of vulnerable women and adolescent girls for GBV mitigation; strengthening the capacity of service providers to reach population in need of women and adolescent girls in remote locations; procurement and distribution of Rape kits for CMR; provision of dignity kits and menstrual hygiene kits for women and girls; and the establishment and support of service sites. Specific activities include:a. Support for the establishment and operations of 6 GBV call centers (one in every region) to provide services such as Psychosocial Support (PSS), counselling, referrals and legal aid/support for GBV survivors b. Procure and distribute 400 post rape treatment kits for treating GBV survivors and conduct orientations/training for 20 CMR actors to treat and manage rape and other survivors of GBV. c. Procure and distribute 600 dignity kits and 600 menstrual hygiene kits. d. Undertake training workshops for 60 PSS counsellors to deliver cultural appropriate, age and gender responsive PSS service. e. Support the provision of livelihoods skills trainings and marketable livelihoods options for 600 vulnerable women and girls including GBV survivors g. Support the provision of direct cash and voucher assistance to 600 vulnerable women and girls including GBV survivors, for 6 months. h. Mobilize communities to disseminate information on GBV prevention, mitigation and response through the initiatives focused on the dissemination of existing integrated referral pathways.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher assistance is important to restoring and sustaining the dignity, safety and security of women and girls affected by the humanitarian crisis. Loss of livelihoods/possibility of creating friction (IPV); traveling long distances to seek funds to procure basic needs, increase of early and forced marriage and FGM because of water and food insecurity exposes women to sexual violence, harassment and abuse. Cash in hands of vulnerable women and girls will provide them capacity to assess basic goods and service and reduce the reliance on negative coping mechanism. It will enable women and girls control to a large extent the level to which they expose themselves to GBV, harassment and abuse.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Implementation of activities proposed by the project will work in synergy with initiatives that target the promotion of safety and security of women and adolescent girls of other protection actors and complement the efforts of key cluster actors such health, Protection, WASH and Food to minimize risks of GBV in their respective Cluster activities and operations. Cash and voucher assistance will complement efforts of food security cluster and cash working group to ensure that a greater number of women and adolescent girls can access items for meet basic material and survival needs.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Nagaad Network has a PSEA policy in place for reporting and handling Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA)-related complaints and all its staff have completed the mandatory SEA training. Nagaad Network has PSEA focal points at HQ office and sub-offices which regularly conducts training and awareness sessions with its local partners and staff. As part of the project agreement, Nagaad Network as the implementing partner will sign a commitment to ensure that no SEA related offences will be committed by any of Nagaad staff, and in the event that it occurs, appropriate actions will be taken. The project will endeavor to ensure maximum consultation with marginalized communities in all phases of the program with a particular focus on including vulnerable women and girls from minority clans and those living with disabilities. The project will ensure inclusion of marginalized individuals in community-based structures to ensure their priorities are tailored into the program. Nagaad Network will closely work with community committees including GBV survivor advocates, youth organizations, organizations of persons living with disabilities to select beneficiaries for the project. Also, Nagaad Network will ensure consistent sex disaggregated programming to ensure men, women, girls, and boys are targeted adequately.
