Project Summary

"As a member of the Multisector Needs Assessment Technical Working Group (MSNA TWG), REACH will contribute to the Joint Multisector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) in Cox's Bazar to inform the 2022 response planning. The Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessments (J-MSNA) will cover both Rohingya refugee and host communities with the objectives of (1) providing a comprehensive evidence base of multi-sectoral needs to inform the 2022 Joint Response Plan (JRP); (2) providing an analysis of how needs have changed in 2021, including considering the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic; and (3) facilitating a joint multi-stakeholder analysis. To ensure the needs and experiences of vulnerable groups are included in the 2022 JRP, REACH will disaggregate the data by age and gender, and test for differing needs for vulnerable households, such as those with PWDs and female-headed households. Moreover, REACH will conduct key informant interviews with PWDs, women’s groups and the elderly to represent their voices. The J-MSNA will include indicators on social cohesion to help humanitarians identify possible flashpoints and areas for cooperation in host community/refugee relations.
REACH also proposes to co-chair the Natural Hazards Risk Analysis Technical Working Group and provide analysis to inform emergency preparedness. The NatHaz TWG aims to ensure that scientifically-based disaster risk analyses within the response are reviewed in a joint forum and translated into products that inform strategy and operations for the cyclone and monsoon seasons and macro-planning for improving living conditions and risk reduction in the camps. In 2021, REACH will increase the inclusion of vulnerable groups in DRR planning by including data from protection assessments, such as the age and disability assessment, to help ensure no group is left behind in DRR activities. The beneficiaries are calculated as the humanitarian actors, local authorities, and coordination bodies who will use this information, with a target of 150. "

1 - Identified Needs

In successive waves over four decades, Rohingya refugees have been fleeing to Bangladesh from Rakhine State, Myanmar. Since August 2017, an estimated 745,000 Rohingya refugees fled to Cox’s Bazar, increasing the total number of Rohingya refugees to more than 860,000. Most refugees rely heavily on humanitarian assistance to cover their basic needs. Moreover, the high fluidity of population movements, changing services within each settlement, and challenges presented by the monsoon and cyclone seasons require regularly updated analyses of household needs and access to services. For the host community, the presence of the refugee communities has raised concerns over local environmental degradation, falling wages and rising prices, exerting additional pressures on localities where public services and infrastructure were already lagging behind the national average. As the crisis moved beyond the initial emergency phase, comprehensive information on the needs and vulnerabilities of affected host communities is needed in order to inform the design and implementation of effective inter-sectoral programming. For both the refugee and host communities, the global COVID-19 pandemic and associated control measures have limited access to livelihoods and services. An understanding of how household-level needs and access to services have been impacted throughout 2021 will therefore be essential for 2022 response planning. Among the urgent issues facing the response, Disaster Risk Management is now recognized as vital to manage the risk of an emergency within the emergency. Cox’s Bazar district is exposed to cyclones and storm surges from the Bay of Bengal during the spring and autumn cyclone seasons, and heavy rainfall during spring low pressures and the monsoon. Sporadic rains can be expected as early as March, with the full monsoon lasting from June till the end of September. These rainfall events often lead to flash flooding in rivers and waterways in the district. Unlike most of Bangladesh, Cox’s Bazar is hilly and prone to landslides, which are also triggered by heavy rainfall. The location, living conditions and status of the Rohingya make them highly vulnerable to disastrous impacts from these hazards. Public cyclone shelter coverage in the sub-district in which the Kutupalong-Balukhali is located, is far outstripped by the size of the refugee population, and the Rohingya has not historically been included in local disaster management plans. The establishment of Natural Hazard Risk Analysis Taskforce was requested during the joint Head of Sub- Office/Sector Coordinator lessons learnt review following the first monsoon rains in 2018, building on pre-existing discussions in the Site Management Sector. Its establishment responds to a clear need for a structured forum to bring together all actors who are involved in the technical analysis of hazards and related data management. The original NatHaz TOR extended from 2018 through March 2020. As part of the TOR extension process, the TWG consulted with EPRWG, SMSD, and Shelter sectors to understand operational priorities and perceived information gaps related to disaster response as well as disaster risk reduction and mitigation strategies. The consultations culminated with a meeting and presentation to the HoSOG on 17th August 2020, where the HoSOG decided to continue the group’s coordination as a distinct TWG under the IMAWG. The feedback from all of these consultations has been integrated into future activities for the working group.

2 - Activities or outputs

Through the MSNA TWG, design and implement joint multi-sector needs assessments (J-MSNAs) to provide information on needs and gaps in the response in the refugee camps and host community.Through the MSNA TWG facilitate a joint analysis of the needs and gaps in the repsonse, drawing upon the J-MSNAs, secondary data, and inputs from each sector, which will be presented in a detailed report to inform humanitarian prioritisation.Through the NatHaz TWG, facilitate and contribute to natural hazard risk analysis initiatives, with the aim of producing information products with updates to inform monsoon and cyclone planning.Conduct assessments to inform emergency response planning, providing reports and updates to fill key information gaps identified by the NatHaz TWG, the EPRWG and other key DRR actors.
