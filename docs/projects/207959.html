Project Summary

This project aims at providing lifesaving and life-sustaining Sexual and reproductive, Maternal and Newborn Health care services (the Minimum Initial Service Package) to returnees and displaced and vulnerable women and girls (Internally Displaced, Returnees and Host communities) in Cabo Delgado, Niassa and Nampula provinces.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The 2024 Mozambique humanitarian needs analysis overview outlines people in need of essential health services including: 291,000 (31%) Internally Displaced People (IPDs) from the conflict and 368,000 returnees (39%) with similar needs in host communities. It further provides a breakdown of people in need as follows: 199,000 (21%) under-fives, 218,000 (23%) women of childbearing age, 38,000 (4%) pregnant and lactating women, 142,000 (15%) disabled and 209,000 (22%) in need of mental and emotional help. The project will be implemented in the three northern provinces of Cabo Delgado, Niassa, and Nampula. It aims at providing lifesaving and life-sustaining integrated sexual and reproductive health services including clinical GBV response interventions to internally displaced people, returnees, host communities and survivors of gender-based violence in selected affected districts. The project will inclusively focus on vulnerable women and adolescent girls with men and boys as enablers including those with disabilities (physical and mental) and the key populations (LGBTIQA) focusing on the people in need. In 2024, the health cluster will target 467,038 people (218,898 men and 237,139 women in Cabo Delgado). UNFPA, with this project, will target 37,000 (4%) pregnant women with emergency obstetric and newborn care and 104, 888 (23%)women of reproductive age with integrated sexual and reproductive health including clinic management of rape. For details, see Table 1 below with specific district indicators and targets. The project interventions will be implemented by the Government, national and International non-governmental organisations (NGOs)/CSO guided by the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health principles. Through this project, UNFPA will enhance the availability and accessibility of life-saving and sustaining SRH service through the implementation of the MISP for SRH which includes: Ensuring the availability of the coordination organization for the delivery of MISP, Prevention and Management of gender-based violence including Clinical Management of Rape, Enhancing provision of Basic and Comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) including safe abortion and essential newborn care, HIV prevention, STIs prevention and syndromic treatment, Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) and ensuring availability of condoms, Family Planning- ensuring availability of long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARC), commodities and supplies and provision of SRH interventions such as antenatal care and postnatal care where possible. MISP for SRH aims at reducing excess maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality, physical and emotional consequences resulting from the humanitarian situation including associated GBV. This further results from the limited access to SRH, GBV Clinical care and mental health services due to malfunctioning health systems during emergencies. Linkages will be formed with the Mental Health and Psychosocial Sub cluster and other clusters for referral for services that UNFPA does not have a comparative advantage on to ensure continuity of care.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

This project ensures the centrality of protection by ensuring health staff have the required capacity to provide services through client client-centered approach by ensuring the full application of the humanitarian and GBV survivor-centered guiding principles to avoid putting the target populations at further risk or harm. Privacy, dignity and confidentiality will be maintained during service provision to the best interest of the people to be served upholding medical ethical principles as well (do no harm, do good, do justice). In this project, protection-related principles will be central in all implementation phases. Interventions will a) avoid exposing the beneficiary population to further harm as a result of its actions; b) it will ensure people’s access to impartial assistance in proportion to need and without discrimination; c) protect people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; and d) assist people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuse. Linkages will be formed with other clusters as appropriate to ensure gender-responsive interventions and access to other services such as food and nutrition, water and sanitation, education for girls and shelter among others.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Although the project targets all affected populations, it will give special attention to adolescent girls and women of reproductive age (WRA) aged 15-49 years and GBV survivors. By promoting access to SRH services it will enable women and girls to exercise their SRH rights and address special needs of women and girls with information and access to services such as Family Planning, Maternal and Newborn Health (EmONC) including safe abortion, HIV and STIs prevention and treatment including PEP, prevention of GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse services among others. Women of reproductive age including adolescent girls will be reached with SRH information and services aimed at delaying sexual debut, preventing STIs including HIV, and preventing early and unwanted pregnancies by making modern contraceptives especially LARC available. The project will ensure inclusivity and make deliberate efforts to reach out to physically and mentally challenged women and girls, with men and boys as partners in achieving the objectives.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will take into consideration the needs of people with disabilities by:a) Ensuring that health facilities are disability friendly to enable access to services and information by people with various disabilities.b) Collecting disaggregated data by age, sex, disability and specific location at the targeted IDP camps and in host communities.c) Involving peer groups of people with disability in activities to reach the targets because peer groups usually know the locations and people with disability will feel more comfortable talking to their peers;d) Reaching out to people with disabilities in hard-to-reach areas through mobile brigade, home visits and community outreach services;e) Modifying and to adapting the delivery of Information and Education and communication sessions to suit the target based on the type of disability (voice messages for people with visual impairment, using sign language, etc).

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will take into consideration the Environmental Protection and Permitting Program (EP3), as unsafe health-care settings contribute to a significant proportion of health hazards and nosocomial infections. It is well-established that the humanitarian context has high risk associated with healthcare facilities, with an average proportion of healthcare-associated infections close to 10%. The capacity of health workers will be built through training in the provision of quality integrated SRH services including infection prevention and reducing environmental health hazards as part of all health training packages. Health workers will be trained on proper disposal of poor disposal of used sharps to reduce health hazards as these, although produced in small quantities, are highly infectious if not disinfected before disposal and people may get infected if in contact. Used needles and if poorly managed, expose health-care workers, waste handlers, children and the community to infections. Therefore, all identified environmental risks will be mitigated by remediation measures and cost-effective, local and innovative solutions. Awareness and actions related to environmental protection measures will be implemented to ensure project activities will not be compromised. Disposal of expired condoms, medicines and obsolete equipment procured under this project will be done in line with government guidelines on environmental protection.
