Project Summary

Oxfam’s CWC project work will aim to meaningfully engage affected women, men, girls and boys (particularly the vulnerable such as the women, elderly and disabled), in the design and delivery of communication resources adapted to their different needs to facilitate community solutions within the changing context of the crisis. The project will employ and scale-up Oxfam’s community engagement using the Community Perception Tracking (CPT) for COVID-19 and the User-Centred Community Engagement (UCCE) for increased participation on WASH project interventions by various user groups. Both approaches will be used to inform key areas and issues where gender, age and disability intersect, which are often overlooked during the project design and implementation processes. The CPT and UCCE approaches will likewise inform the adaptations with ongoing approaches in hygiene promotion such as Social Architecture (SA); Risks, Attitudes, Norms, Abilities and Self-regulation (RANAS); and Mum’s Magic Hands (MMH). WASH interventions will include addressing unequal gender power dynamics in unpaid care work, which harm women and girls and vulnerable people such as persons with disabilities. Oxfam integrates in all its WASH actions to the “3Rs of Care Work” to Recognize, to revalue it within the family and community as an important aspect of life, Reduce it through hardware and NFIs, and Redistribute it care work to men and boys to unburden women and girls. This will enable the community mobilisers and volunteers to conduct sex and age segregated community sessions on important topics such as COVID-19, AWD and other WASH aspects alongside inputs on 3Rs of care work through a campaign.
Oxfam will develop a capacity building plan for the target groups (community, CBO, and CWC partners) to deliver these interventions. Oxfam staff alongside community-based volunteers (CBVs) will build on its existing networks and collectives in the community among women, men and youth. In addition, engagement with religious and other local leaders with influence at the community level will act as links and drivers for COVID-19 prevention, mitigation and response and its relevant advocacy actions. This will include addressing different concerns based on gender, age and disability, and translating those concerns into achievable actions alongside the delivery of interventions. Based on the identified gaps and epidemiological realities over time, the CBVs will be trained and supported to facilitate quick referrals of COVID-19 suspected cases to the health facilities, as well as safe and dignified referrals for other services including GBV which has been exacerbating since lockdowns.
Internally, Oxfam WASH teams will work in an integrated manner with Protection, Gender and Livelihoods colleagues to ensure any communication done to the community incorporates all the relevant aspects from these sectors. Specifically, for COVID-19 the Community Perception Tracker captures all perceptions across sectors and actions taken jointly with the community stakeholders to address them will be done in collaboration with all sectors, within Oxfam; as well as other external ones like Health and Site management. Information sharing and advocacy will be done through the CWC, HPTWG and FSL coordination platforms.

2 - Activities or outputs

A1. Communication Resources: Content/Material/Message design & development; Content translation; and Dissemination/advocacy sessions across relevant agencies/sectors/WGs. Oxfam will design and develop contents in collaboration with CWC WG specific to the phase and context with participation of target groups and ensure gender adaptations. IEC development will include information for COVID-19 context (practices such as handwashing, masks wearing, etc), Sani-Tweak picture cards for care & maintenance of inclusive latrine/bathing facilities which will include key messages on the 3Rs (Recognition, Reduction, and Redistribution) of care work while promoting hygiene promotion. A2. Community Engagement using the CPT and UCCE approaches: By systematically capturing informal information from the communities through a mobile device (CPT), Oxfam will transform anecdotal information into more purposeful evidence. UCCE enables the co-creation of relevant and feasible solutions with community members. Generating this gender, age and disability-disaggregated data allows WASH agencies to identify and appropriately address the specific needs of each group. The CPT and UCCE approaches will link with ongoing approaches in hygiene promotion such as Social Architecture; RANAS; Care Work, and Mum’s Magic Hands.A3. Information and feedback service centres / facilities: Public health promotion campaigns using appropriate sex-segregated communication channels in consultation with women, men, girls and boys; and ensure safe space for two-way discussion (where physical distancing allows); to increase knowledge of the differential and gendered risks and preventative measures; and dispel unhelpful rumours and myths. Community Perception Tracker will be used to collect, analyse and face feedback. Oxfam will also maintain a mobile number and help desk (non-permanent/mobile) operation to generate complaint feedback database. A4. Capacity development of the staff and stakeholders being engaged: Trainings and continuous dialogues with communities (espcially CBVs) to strengthen their capacities about community engagement approaches as well as COVID-19 prevention communication & practices. All the CBVs & casual labours will be supplied with a mobilization kit (comprising of PPE) to ensure they’re protected as they carry out activities in the communities. CBVs will be trained in adapting communication styles and methods for females, males and persons who need appropriate approach. Key stakeholders will be oriented on how to provide feedback on their concerns and supported in their proposed community-led solutions.A5. Assessment/Research/Studies: [Assessment study on the Oxfam Handwashing Station and the impact on handwashing practice (alongside the care work campaign and MMH approach)]. Oxfam will collaborate with the LSHTM and a national research institution to carry out a pilot study of the OHS (with the pre-test of MMH). Disaggregated data will be collected using Mobenzi software and participatory approaches. Structured observation will be used to measure increase in handwashing practice; spot check observations will be conducted at the handwashing stations on availability and quantity of soapy water and water in person or phone survey will be used to collect data on self-reported frequency of handwashing with soap, knowledge and perceptions of COVID19 and protective measures that are recommended and exposure to and reach of program promotion.A6. Coordination with CBO/Committees, relevant government departments and agencies: By working with and through a community network, community capacities will be strengthened, advocacy will be evidence-based thus responsive, and respect for local culture and beliefs will be ensured, which are essential to enhance the appropriateness of COVID-19 interventions. Specifically, for COVID-19 the Community Perception Tracker captures all perceptions across sectors and actions taken jointly with the community stakeholders.
