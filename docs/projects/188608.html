Project Summary

For 2022, UNHCR will provide WASH services in locations receiving returnees, through the repair and rehabilitation of water supply networks and support to sewage and solid waste management systems. Sector Objective 1: Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria. The project will assist 370,000 individual / 74,000 households disaggregated as following (According to the HNO 2022), Children (19% Girls, 24% Boys), Adults (28% Female, 24% Male), Elderly +60 (2% Female, 3% Male), and 28% of Persons with Disability.
For 2023, UNHCR will continue to support returnees’ areas, enhancing resiliency, and early recovery for the targeted HHs through applying similar approaches and activities that are strongly aligned to the Sector Objective 1.
Due consideration will be given to ensure that WASH facilities are equally accessible to both returnees and affected host communities. Through provided support to target populations the project will reduce the number of persons exposed to the risks of displacement mainstream and reduce risks of GBV and PSEA. The network of over 2,800 outreach volunteers will ensure greater outreach to people in remote and hard-to-reach areas, for community engagement and consultations on project implementation to prevent SGBV and PSEA. Various participatory methods including FGDs and community/household visits will be used to consult with Syrian IDPs, returnees and host community. UNHCR works on raising awareness among UNHCR and Partner staff on PSEA, through basic PSEA trainings organized by UNHCR periodically (82 partner staff trained in 2019).
UNHCR is working to enhance capacity for the country and field offices to provide regular and timely inputs to the WASH sector 4W, also and since UNHCR is working through partnership modality to implement most of WASH interventions and activities, this would be an additional vital reporting channel to the sector as partners will be strongly encouraged to provide input to the 4W regularly and in timely manner. An assigned focal point from UNHCR will be responsible for all WASH sector related reporting and communication issues.

4 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022, the shortage of electricity and fuel forms the major issue affecting water supply systems performance with limited pumping hours, there is a need to support water supply systems with alternative energies where possible. WASH infrastructure requires significant support and investments, water supply systems and water networks are damaged and in need of rehabilitation or maintenance, 47% of the population are relying on unsafe, uncontrolled alternatives to piped water, Investment in sanitation infrastructure is critical, as at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional and in needs for rehabilitation or maintenance. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency, while the dumpsites often pose serious health, environmental and protection risks. Centralized distribution systems are the most equitable, accessible, and safer way to provide water to the maximum number of people, and similarly to public sanitation systems, despite the higher initial cost of investment activities supporting WASH systems are economically justifiable in the mid-term run and are crucial. 5 - Implementation of activities

To support areas that have shown spontaneous return and empowerment of the host community, UNHCR will work with other partners in the water and sanitation sector by implementing a simple sanitation programme in returnee areas. This programme will include different types of activities such as replacing broken pipes connecting residential buildings, schools or clinics to the main sewer system network, repairing damaged inspection rooms or increasing the number of inspection rooms to ensure better capacity.Operation, maintenance and solid waste management systems (municipal and medical) will be supported in addition to providing the necessary material to reduce overall public health risks and environmental pollution caused by the deterioration of the solid waste management system. Coordination will be maintained with other actors to perform integrated projects to support shelter objectives in coordination with the local authorities and the WASH Sector. In doing so, due consideration will be given to ensuring that WASH facilities are equally accessible to both returnees and host communities.For 2022, Comprehensively, UNHCR will support a total of 165,000 persons of concern through the distribution of 1,000 communal solid waste bins and the repair of 10,000 linear meters of sewage networks in addition to support communities/sites with storm water systems. An additional 205,000 persons will benefit from interventions to improve access to water supply.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions whichmay arise. Risk 2: Risk of theft of construction materials by armed groups and used in conflict activities. Mitigating measures: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. Risk 3: Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) Mitigating measures: Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with staff and contractors; incorporate awareness campaigns as part of the intervention. Risk 4: Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empowers or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors. Mitigating Measures: Establish strong links with authorities and partners to ensure “do no harm" principle” is applied; Promote joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard; Include communities and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 2: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price and reduced target.
