Project Summary

The proposed project aims to contribute in reduction in morbidity and mortality among the conﬂict affected IDPs and host communities in the targeted areas of Idleb and Aleppo in North-western Syria. A total of 400,000 Individuals (104,937men, 143,152 women, 77,475 boys and 74,437 girls) will receive health care through 70 Health facilities. The main project components include: • Drugs, disposables, dialysis kits provision to 56 PHC, 4 Mobile Emergency unit (MEU) and 2 dialysis centres. • Stipend support to 150 health workers and running cost to 10 targeted facilities. • Capacity building (training) for 100 health workers and facility management on health information management, protection & inclusion, nursing and midwifery. Also the project will support 2 ambulances in one facility with operational cost. These ambulances will be used for referral services. Finally out of the targeted facilities, 5 will be supported with solar panel to overcome the problem of electricity breakdown. The proposed project will provide support to those people most in need of life-saving and life-sustaining assistance. Speciﬁcally, the project will prioritize assistance to people with acute and immediate humanitarian health needs, many of whom have suffered trauma injuries or otherwise lack access to basic life-saving medical-care for both acute and chronic conditions (such as renal failures) that renders them particularly at-risk.
IR has 2 MEAL oﬃcers and 2 MEAL assistants in Syria to provide progress monitoring. Field reports in the form of monthly progress reports as well as feedback received from the main stakeholders and from the targeted community will be shared through regular Skype meetings between ﬁeld staff in Syria and management staff based in Turkey and actions will be taken accordingly. Also IR will ensure to have a robust complaint and feedback mechanism.

4 - Needs

According to the latest humanitarian needs analysis in northwest Syria, 4 million people are in living in the crisis affected areas of whom 2.8 are in urgent need humanitarian assistance. 2 million people are internally displaced in northwest Syria with 400 000 displaced since May 2019 while 669,000 IDPs are living in camps and informal sites. The first priority for the population is the health needs. In the assessed communities, 90% reported that people in their community experience challenges accessing health care services mainly related to security and transportation such as: the high cost of transportation to health facilities (45%), security concerns around travel to health facilities (42%), security concerns within the health facility (40%), and 22% cited disability or injury as a barrier for the access of health facilities. Finally, 20% of the surveyed medical professionals voiced that there were no medical items assessed in their communities. According to the same survey, the top 3 most needed healthcare services were:  chronic disease support 52% (affecting men generally slightly more than women, especially diseases of the prostate, hypertension and diabetes) antenatal care 52% (affecting PLW and girls, this type of care is essential for the wellbeing of mothers and their new-borns) provision of medicine 50% (affecting men, women, boys and girls) The 3 most common health problems reported were: severe diseases affecting children under 5 (69%)  chronic diseases (56%) acute respiratory infections (33%). People most at risk include people living with chronic diseases; people living with disabilities (PWD) children who are not vaccinated; pregnant women and neonates who have no access to life-saving obstetric and neonatal care, girls and women with poor access to essential reproductive health care; those in need of mental health and psychosocial support and; survivors of trauma, including the war wounded.

5 - Implementation of activities

Access to primary health care: The project will support 56 PHC in targeted areas through an essential package of Primary Health Services to cover health care needs of vulnerable populations including obstetric, trauma, internal and family medicine, reproductive health services and child health care. These 56 PHC will receive essential drugs and disposables. 150 health workers from these facilities will receive the monthly stipend and 10 facilities will receive the running cost. Also 5 facilities will receive solar panel. Mobile Emergency units (MEU): 4 MEU will receive drugs and disposables. In coordination with health directorates, these MEU will be strategically placed to cover the areas with high catchment population (IDP and host) and with no access to healthcare services. These MEU will also serve as mobile surgery units to provide onsite surgery support. Dialysis centre: The project will support dialysis centres with dialysis kits to conduct regular hemodialysis sessions. they will also be provided with stipend and running cost. Capacity building: 100 health workers (30 men and 70 women) will receive training on include health information management (3 training), protection & inclusion (3 training), nursing (3 training) and midwifery (3 training) . The capacity building component will help to enhance the basic skills and knowledge of the health workers. Pre and post test will be conducted to gauge the improvement in knowledge levels of the participants. After the trainings, IR will make follow up visits to facilities to ensure that the training knowledge is practically translated such as age and gender disaggregated data collection and protection and inclusion measures taken. Referral services: IR will also support operational cost of 2 ambulances of the targeted one hospital. These ambulances will ensure to create a strong referral pathway to secondary and territory health care. These ambulances will bridge the access gap between vulnerable population.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Some of the identiﬁed protection risks and mitigation measures that IR will apply include the following: 1. Data on attacks against healthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA) during the period of January - October 2019 documented 73 attacks affecting 52 health facilities. Despite extensive advocacy on the part of health actors, media and the UN, the attacks continue and violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas. Mitigation Measure: IR will ensure continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the humanitarian leadership (cluster) in their efforts to highlight protection challenges facing health workers. 2. Gender Based Violence: Health services are often the ﬁrst, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical that health providers are equipped to offer quality health services. Mitigation Measure: Identiﬁcation of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases, in a manner guarantees safety, conﬁdentiality, and privacy of the survivor. Provision of drugs and disposables required for the treatment of GBV cases. Facilitate Presence of female staff (considering female for stipend provision) in targeted health facilities to increase access of women and girls to health services. 3. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Mitigation Measure: Emplacement of effective complaint and feedback.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

some of the risks and their mitigation measures identified are as follows. 1. The closure of Bab al Hawa border crossing between Turkey and Syria. Mitigation measure: IR will use second border crossing i.e. Bab al Salamah. This will slightly increase the transport cost of the deliverables. 2. The interruption of the road between Manbej and Azaz and Idlib areas. Mitigation measure: The closure of this road will cause higher fuel prices as this road is the only source of fuel entry to the region and therefore will cause higher transportation and labor costs in general, but there are more expensive alternatives such as fuel imported from Turkey which is regularly present in the project area. 3. Theescalation of hostilities and targeting of the medical facilities supported. Mitigation measure: IR will conduct a thorough security and safety analysis of the healthcare centre to be supported or location to host a mobile unit. Once supported, the facility will be reassessed for safety and security regularly in order to prepare contingency plans to deal with any emergency situation. These contingency plans will address the relocation of the health facilities (in case of the MEUs), the staff work schedule and emergency treatment of trauma patients including their referral destinations. 4. Non availability of some medical items delaying the procurement process. Mitigation measure: IR team can identify the alternatives of the medical items non available and can initiate to replace such items with those available. Since IR is working with multiple suppliers for procurement, thereby due to long standing relationships the suppliers can also facilitate this replacement of items quickly. 5. Qualiﬁed health staff leaving area. Mitigation measure: IR will ensure rigorous coordination with the health directorates in order to ﬁnd alternatives for the health staff also stipend will be provided to encourage health workers to work with the designated health facility.
