1 - Needs

Agricultural: The Mercy Corps-led interagency livelihoods market recovery (LMR) assessment of the North East in the first half of 2017 helped to identify key agricultural market systems (cowpeas, cereals and vegetables), interconnected market systems (transport and infrastructure and labour), constraints to rural livelihoods due to the insurgency and opportunities arising with economic recovery. Several household agricultural constraints were identified in the LMR, including access and knowledge about appropriate fertilizer, availability of improved seeds, pests and disease in storage, water management and irrigation. There is a great need for restoration of agriculture businesses through provision of farming inputs and grants/access to finance. Conflict Management and Peacebuilding: Although not always stated explicitly, it was clear that there are increasing tensions where there are high concentrations of IDPs sharing host community resources and space. Discrepancies in assistance to host communities and IDPs are also causing growing grievances. It is therefore vital to establish mechanisms for ongoing dialogue and dispute resolution at community level. Adolescent and Youth Unemployment: A 2016 Mercy Corps study found that many youth and adults in Borno State viewed government- or private-sector financial services as inaccessible, and inequality drove some youth to accept risky loans or gifts from Boko Haram . Furthermore, there is growing concern about what the Civilian Joint Task Force (CJTF) and other vigilante groups will do once they are no longer needed to protect communities from Boko Haram. There are extremely high levels of unemployment and underemployment among youth, both adolescents and young men and women generally, as well as very limited vocational training opportunities. Restlessness and anger is common among young people. There is need to carry out further assessment, particularly of labour markets, to identify opportunities for unemployed youth, especially those with limited literacy. Livelihoods: Mercy Corps has already confirmed there is a demand and viability for various livelihood options for both men and women in the target areas, including in south Borno, where small-scale trade and agriculture are the most common livelihoods. Meanwhile, Mercy Corps’ March 2017 Labour Market Assessment in Maiduguri, Mafa and Damboa LGAs concludes that a relatively healthy business environment exists in these three LGAs, with many small but well-established businesses in the agriculture and livestock, retail, processing and manufacturing sectors. Access to Energy: Access to grid electricity is currently non-existent in most of the communities visited. A pre-feasibility assessment conducted by Mercy Corps / ICEED shows that access to fuelwood is rapidly declining in the target areas and other alternatives, like kerosene and LPG, are in very short supply. Further assessments of energy needs should be done for households and community facilities to determine suitable technologies for both fuel efficient cook stoves and electricity generation for the most vulnerable households.

2 - Activities

Agriculture: Restart/revitalise cooperatives through input vouchers and linkages to formal (FSPs) and informal (VSLAs) financial services. Vocational Training: Rehabilitate and re-equip Ministry of Poverty and Youth Empowerment-administered Multi-Skill and Training Resource Centres. Train youth and connect about 50% of the trained youth to cash grants or apprenticeships. Safe Spaces: Create safe spaces for adolescent girls and boys, taking them through the safe space curriculum with trained mentors and coordinators. Conflict: Establish community groups, train on conflict mitigation and management, create community action plans with the communities and use cash for work to carry out action plans. Hold social integration events that bring all members of communities together. Shelter: Construct permanent shelters. Equip shelters with home solar systems. Food Security: Vouchers for food baskets.
