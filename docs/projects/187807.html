Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements gender-sensitive life-saving WASH interventions and provide communities with access to safe water and sanitation as sustainable mitigation for hunger causes. After more than ten years of conflict, the recent economic downturn and water crisis all compounded by the COVID19 pandemic, basic services infrastructures in the country are facing unprecedented stress and deterioration confirmed with AAH needs assessments in several locations. Women, children, IDPs, elderly and disabled are often those most in need. AAH mandate consists of supporting the functional authorities in responding to crises and regaining resilience and preparedness capabilities, in this scope AAH prioritizes sustainable resilience WASH projects, however, provides life-saving rapid WASH (Water trucking, distribution of water storage containers and hygiene items) when access to the basic WASH needs becomes severe or critical. AAH operates through three field offices in Damascus, Aleppo and Hassakeh, and provides wide range of WASH services in Aleppo, Al-Hassakeh, Daraa, Hama, Idleb, Rural Damascus. AAH has MoUs with the Ministry of Water Resources, Ministry of Local Administration and Environment, Education and Health. AAH actively engages with the WASH, education, and other sectors and working groups, AAH keeps a close contact with SARC, as well as with Governorates and local administrations. In NES, AAH coordinates with UNICEF and NES WWG for its interventions in Hassakeh. AAH interventions focus on locations with WASH severity 3 and higher as per the HNO, and the identification of potential projects will be done through integrated (WASH, health, nutrition, FSA, gender, protection) and WASH-technical needs assessments to holistically investigate the inter-linkages. Gender roles and the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys, age-groups, disabled and IDPs are analysed and addressed. The identification of WASH interventions in public institutions will be based on the severity scores of the relevant sectors being Health and Education. All interventions will aim at meeting SPHERE standards, address community expressed priorities. Emergency WASH services include life-saving water trucking, water tanks distribution, water quality tests, distribution of water tanks, PWDs-sensitive latrines in camps with extension of sewage system and drainage; distribution of gender sensitive hygiene items including menstrual hygiene items in kind or through CVA. The rehabilitation of water systems carried out by AAH includes both the rehabilitation of the networks and the water stations. Greener solutions for power supply as solar panels installations are the prioritized together with connections to public electric grids, while generators are the last resort. AAH also rehabilitates sewage networks, including the drainages and the installation of septic tanks, with a significant positive environmental and public health impact, avoiding the pollution of lands and eventually of aquifers. The solid waste management system is supported through improving waste collection at different levels. All systems rehabilitation is followed with support to operation and maintenance to ensure a sustained service provision after handing over to authorities. Public health risk communication and community engagement activities tailored according to age and gender target the most at risk groups as children and PLWs. Protection and gender will be mainstreamed. A feedback mechanism will collect complaints and suggestions feeding the monitoring and learning process. This project will reach 374,360 people. All activities will be monitored to assess the impact and take corrective actions if needed. Feedback mechanisms are also in place to ensure the accountability towards beneficiaries. Activities will be implemented in 2022 and 2023 for a max of 6 months duration from the start. Estimated 40% of 2022 budget is secured.

4 - Needs

AAH conducts integrated (WASH, FSA, H&N) and WASH-technical needs assessments to holistically investigate the inter-linkages. Gender roles and the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys are analysed. AAH targets locations with a needs severity score of 3 and above with multiple vulnerabilities. Assessments in Aleppo (Maskana, Membij, Jabel Saman, Hritan), Daraa (Izra), Hama (Madiq Castle, Muradeh, Suran), Idleb (Abu Thour, Khan Shaykun), Rural Damascus (Qatana, Jirud, Sarghaya) identified dwellers have less than 30l of water/p/d, rely on costly private water trucking from unsafe water sources. Water tanks are needed (Maskane, Hama, Hassakeh) to ensure enough and safe storage of water. The sewage systems are damaged encouraging unsafe disposal practices. The solid waste management system presents several gaps with garbage often piling in the streets. Hygiene materials are unaffordable, women and girls cannot access appropriate items for menstrual hygiene management. Hassakeh city relies on humanitarian agencies’ water trucking, boreholes in Tal Hamis, Yarobie and Qamishli need rehabilitation, the water pipelines in Hassakeh town need repair. IDP camps as Areesha rely on water trucking, present gaps as sewage network and latrines for disabled people. The above issues represent serious public health risks compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic, the power crisis and the economic downturn further hindering both availability and access to WASH services as per SPHERE standards.

5 - Implementation of activities

Emergency WASH: AAH provides life-saving water trucking, water quality tests, distribution of water tanks, PWDs-sensitive latrines in camps with extension of sewage system and drainage; distribution of gender sensitive hygiene items including menstrual hygiene items in kind or through CVA. Rehabilitation of water systems: AAH rehabilitates damaged and malfunctioning water systems, which includes the repair of boreholes, solar upgrades, connections of water stations to the electric grid, repair of elevated tower tanks and repair of the water networks at community level; repair and equipment of water testing facilities. Provision of water through public networks enables greater control on both quality and quantity ensuring an affordable service. Rehabilitation of sanitation systems: AAH repairs sanitation systems at different levels as pipelines, manholes, septic tanks. Support to solid waste management includes provision of garbage bins for safe disposal in coordination with the municipalities. Operation and maintenance support includes technical capacity building for technicians, PPEs, tools and spare parts provision for sustained service provision after handing over to the local authorities. Repair of WASH facilities in public institutions as the water and sanitation systems in schools and public health centers. Public health risk communication and community engagement activities tailored according to age and gender target the most at risk groups as children and PLWs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

AAH staff is trained on gender and protection mainstreaming. Limited or unsafe access to toilets for PWD, elderly, women and girls: all the protection standards are followed, including lights (prioritizing solar energy), locks on the inside part of the toilets and the respect to minimum privacy, among others. The locations of the toilets will be agreed with women, men, girls and boys, elderly and disabled, its location will be discussed with the authorities to further ensure the users’ protection. Inadequate quality of water: When AAH rehabilitates water systems, the quality of water is closely monitored so that it complies with the WHO quality standards. Additionally, and in order to improve the capacity of the DoW on this topic, the laboratory analyzing the quality of the water will be equipped in Hassakeh. When distributing water through emergency water trucking, AAH regularly monitors the quality of water in different points of the distribution routes, not only at the consumption level. Regular cleaning of water tanks and trucks is also done. Suppliers being included in terrorism financing lists: AAH Syria has established a manual on “Prevention of Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism”. Child Labour: AAH will ensure the proper registration system for staff, consultants and contractors, and include child protection charters within contracts and HR procedures. No tolerance on child-labour, GBV or harassment will be in place.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks of sexual abuse or abuse of power have been identified as potential risks during the different stages of the project cycle. In order to prevent this, all employees, implementing partners and contractors sign AAH’s Code of Conduct, which includes AAH’s “ZERO TOLERANCE policy of sexual abuse”. Action Against Hunger also fully complies with the IASC Six Core Principles Relating to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, which is reflected in the following internal policies: “Child Protection Policy”; “Gender Policy”; and “Sexual Violence Policy”.
