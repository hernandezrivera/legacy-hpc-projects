Project Summary

Since 2019, Sudan is in a transition period and despite the recent peace agreements and efforts to stabilize the situation, the humanitarian health situation remains fragile. The main drivers of the humanitarian health crisis in Sudan include armed conflict, displacement, disease outbreaks, and floods as much as lack of staff, lack of medicines, medical supplies and equipment as much as lack of operational costs. Disease outbreaks are mostly water and sanitation related, vector-borne and vaccine preventable. The humanitarian health response project aims to address major risk factors, to provide free-of-charge essential and life-saving health services to vulnerable populations and to strengthen the intersectoral multi-hazard preparedness and response. WHO’s preparedness and response to health emergencies is in line with the national health sector and health cluster strategies and follows national and international standards. WHO will provide technical and operational support to the coordination mechanisms on all levels of the health emergency response and will strengthen and support the collection, analysis and dissemination of health related information and data in close collaboration with partners and other stakeholders. WHO will also strengthen the different pillars of disease surveillance with the aim to increase the capacities of early investigation, confirmation and immediate response to public health threats and to contribute to the health security of the targeted population groups and beyond.
WHO, as provider of last resort, will procure and distribute free of charge medicines, medical supplies, medical equipment, personal protective equipment (PPEs) and laboratory reagents to support the humanitarian health care provided by NGOs and MOH. WHO will also engage in capacity building of health workers on health emergency related subjects such as surveillance, case management and infection prevention and control for diseases with outbreak potential including Covid-19. The project will also include small repair and rehabilitation of health facilities, supporting operational costs for humanitarian health services as necessary and also health risk communication to vulnerable populations. Access to health services to all age groups and gender with their specific health needs adapted to the local cultural customs is promoted, including advocacy and synergic linkages with ongoing recovery-development interventions. 6.4 million people residing in 75 localities with severity ranking level of 4 and 5 for humanitarian health response. The activities related to outbreak control are not restricted only to the 107 targeted localities but WHO will intervene wherever the needs arise under its mandate for health security and protection of health. COVID-19 community transmission is likely to continue in all states of Sudan in 2022 in all parts of the population despite the ongoing vaccination efforts and is likely to continue its impact on access to health care. As of its mandate, WHO has integrated in this proposal interventions to contain the outbreak and to support adequate health services for the target population. This includes among others infection prevention and control, isolation center support, scale up of access to testing also in remote areas, support to scale up vaccination coverage and outbreak alert investigations. These interventions are cost intensive and contribute significantly to the requested budget.

1 - Needs

Out of the 9.1 M people in need of humanitarian health assistance in 2022, The health sector 6.5 people residing in 75 localities with severity ranking level of 4 and 5 for humanitarian health response. The main drivers of the humanitarian health crisis in Sudan include the consequences of armed conflict, displacement, disease outbreaks, and floods and the deterioration of the health system due to the ongoing socio-economic, lack of qualified health workers, repeated stock outs of medicines and medical supplies and lack of funds. Three groups of epidemics dominate the disease outbreaks in Sudan: water and sanitation related (e.g. cholera), vector borne (e.g. malaria and viral hemorrhagic fevers (VHF)) and vaccine preventable diseases (e.g. measles, polio and diphtheria). Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity with more than 1.75 Million cases reported in the country in Q 1-Q3. Anopheles stephansii, a new species of mosquitos transmitting malaria spread to 6 states of Sudan since 2019, which increases the risk of malaria transmission especially in urban settings. Aedes mosquitos, the predominant vector responsible for transmitting most of VHFs is present in 16 out of 18 States. As in the previous years, also in 2021, outbreaks of viral hemorrhagic fever including Dengue have been confirmed in 6 states and case numbers are likely to be underreported due to low access to health care and testing. Access to improved drinking water and sanitation facilities remains a challenge since 33.34 % of the population are practicing open defecation and only 14.26 % are using basic hand washing facilities. Hence, the risk of outbreaks of faecal-oral transmitted diseases including cholera remains high in most states of Sudan. Currently an outbreak of Hepatitis E is ongoing in Sudan, with 1810 suspected and confirmed cases from Gedaref, Kassala, South Kordofan, North Darfur. Hepatitis E transmission is documented since June in the refugee camps in East Sudan highlighting the still fragle WASH situation in the camps. (Attack rate: 0.6/1000, CFR: 0.17%). The vaccination coverage of measles-containing-vaccine second dose (MCV2) of less than 74% (2019) does not provide sufficient herd immunity in the vulnerable age groups to prevent regular measles outbreaks. Measles outbreaks with more than 1000 cases were observed in . Episodes of armed conflict and inter-communal clashes are regularly occurring in Darfur, Blue Nile, South Kordofan, Kassala and Red Sea State and sivil unrest in Khartoum. In areas of acute conflict and newly accessible areas present with additional need for health services to the vulnerable and marginalized communities and access to adequate trauma and referral services are a major challenge. One of the main challenges in 2021, however, is the community transmission of Covid-19 pandemic affecting entire Sudan. More than 40000 COVID-19 confirmed cases are reached including more than 3000 associated deaths, CFR: 7.0%. However, the number of cases and death are highly underreported. The high case fatality ratio (CFR) reflected the low capacity of the health system on all levels and the lack of health care especially for vulnerable populations. Given the low vaccine coverage a new wave is expected in the coming months with potentially high morbidity and mortality. There is little investment for health sector from external aid (3%) and government (12%) causing catastrophic health expenditure (76% out-of-pocket-expenditure) on the vulnerable communities. The HRP 2021 for Sudan was severely underfunded (<30%). As a result, the national population coverage with essential health services remained at 43%. The disease surveillance sentinel sites only cover 28% of the health facilities. Annually, the FMOH has been facing about 40% turnover of the Rapid Response Team (RRT) requiring continuous trainings. 2 - Activities/Outputs

To increase equitable access to health services for vulnerable communities, the project will implement the following activities. Activities, support provision of primary and secondary health care services including trauma care, EmONC, and referral services through health partner and government counterparts as appropriate. The project support procurement and propositioning of essential medicines, medical supplies, laboratory reagents, medical kits and equipment and provide training for health workers on diagnosis, case management and IPC to avoid stock out of medicines and increase access to quality of life-saving and essential health services for vulnerable communities. The project also support routine and mobile outreach immunization services and national campaigns to help boost immunization coverage to help prevent vaccine preventable disease outbreaks; support minor repairs of damaged health services to provide quality of care that respect confidentiality and privacy of clients; establish and strengthen referral pathways with special attention to vulnerable groups such as women, children, IDPs. The project mainstream COVID-19 response into existing and future emergency health responses and covers all pillars of the Covid-19 response according to WHO guidelines including coordination, infection prevention and control, case management and isolation centers, scale up of access to testing also in remote areas, surveillance, rapid response team support and alert investigation, equipment and medical supplies risk communication, essential services and vaccination. To strengthen the health sector capacity to prepare and deliver health services for vulnerable communities affected by man made and natural disasters such as armed conflict, floods, and disease outbreaks. Activities 2) Train and support RRTs at national, State and locality levels; strengthen and expand disease surveillance systems at health facility and community levels through training of health workers and community volunteers and provision of tools including cross-border and cross-line operations; strengthening surveillance at point of entries, support epidemic alert investigation and response through provision of operational costs; support sample shipment and confirmation in WHO certified labs; facilitate international notification as per IHR; support response to outbreaks vaccination campaigns; establish mobile clinics and outreach services to reach marginalized and displaced communities in remote and inaccessible locations; establish referral mechanisms to ensure continuity of care; provide life-saving medicines, medical supplies and kits including laboratory supplies, reagents and equipment; support rehabilitation of health facilities in conflict affected or flood damaged areas to restore interrupted service deliveries. Activities 3) As health cluster lead agency, the WHO, also supports coordination and leadership in health emergency preparedness and response efforts; foster interagency collaboration, support establishment of task forces, activation of Emergency Operation Centers (EOCs), co-lead health cluster coordination at national and sub-national level with FMOH/SMOH; support monitoring emergency health response through health cluster tools such as HeRAMS, 4 Ws matrix, State’s vulnerability, Cluster bulletin, updates regularly. To strengthen health systems capacity to enhance vulnerable groups and communities (including IDPs, refugees, and people living with disability) resilience to absorb and respond to shocks. Activities 4) The project support improving access to health services for vulnerable communities through mobile and outreach services when appropriate; strengthen mental health and psychosocial service delivery for communities affected by manmade and natural disasters; support coordination and collaboration with relevant stakeholders with specific mandate for vulnerable groups such as UNHCR for refugees and UNFPA for GBV related serices

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project focus is to improve access of affected population regardless of their gender and sex to essential health care services, putting the services closer to the affected communities protects the most vulnerable entering in damaging coping strategies that increases their vulnerability to exploitation and abuses (especially women and children). The need for improving the access to free-of-charge health care for the communities affected by humanitarian crisis is even greater in Sudan where the out of pocket expenditure is more than 70%. During monitoring of the health situation; WHO coordinates with community leaders committees which set to accommodate the affected communities’ concerns and satisfaction while early identification and response to ongoing outbreaks as mitigation and control measures for public health threats/outbreaks is the most efficient way to prevent emergencies became disasters, protecting not only the well-being of affected group but also the host communities, both IDPs and host communities will benefit getting access to the supported clinics as do no harm act. Proper medical waste management (incinerators, pit sharps) is included into the sector and MOH standards for the protection of staff, patients, communities and environment.All the activities that will be implemented in this project will use environmental friendly procedures and equipment. The mitigation procedures will be in line with the concepts and understanding of the beneficiaries in the way that will keep their dignity especially in the indoor vector breeding sites treatment and adult flies control using products approved by the WHO and ensuring proper disposal of chemical waste.Reducing the burden of diseases for the vulnerable household, would reduce the expenditure for health protecting the families entering damaging coping strategies that will make them more vulnerable to abuses and exploitation.
