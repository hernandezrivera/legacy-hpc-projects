Project Summary

The proposed project will provide adequate safe water, sanitation facilities and hygiene education in targeted villages in Gedo, Banadir and Baidoa Regions of Somalia. The proposed project will provide adequate safe water, sanitation facilities and hygiene education in targeted villages in Gedo, Banadir and Baidoa Regions of Somalia. The project aims to reach 4,500 households comprising 2,070 (46%) children, 1,755 comprising 2,070 (46%) children, 1,755 (39%) adults, 225 (5%) elderly and 450 (10%) people with disabilities giving a total of 27, 000 (13,250 (49%) men and 13,770 (51%) women).
The project proposes to tackle the individual identified priority needs for each area. The project will focus on the construction, upgrading and rehabilitation of existing water supply systems, focusing on simple and familiar technology options – berkads, boreholes and pipeline extensions, as well as water trucking in Gedo, the extension of water pipelines to strategic IDP areas in Banadir and the construction of latrines and sanitation facilities in Baidoa, in full compliance to cluster guidelines.
Concern will also work with the private sector (local water companies) working in Mogadishu (Banadir) and Gedo Region and Baidoa to supply water to the IDP camps identified. All installed water points at the IDP and community water collection points will be metered. Payments will based on agreed tariffs between the users and the Water Management Committees and the Private Service providers (where applicable), based on the meter readings.
Throughout the project Concern will conduct hygiene and sanitation educational training and outreach at the community level. The project will capitalize on already existing WASH committees and structures, and Community Hygiene Promoters to ensure the sustainability and maintenance of WASH infrastructure inputs. The project’s goals and plan shall be conducted in conjunction with the governmental authorities and local structures, most particularly the Ministry of Water and the Ministry of Planning, Ministry of Health (Public Health Department) and Ministry responsible for Social Services. Local Implementing Partner organizations will also participate in the implementation of the WASH Facilities.
Concern’s WASH Interventions will include cross‐cutting themes, and shall be mainstreamed in the programming, including implementing activities that enhance ways to identify and address differential forms of exclusion, including those based on societal discrimination, protection, power-structures, vulnerability, age, and gender; GBV, PSEA, DRR, disability and is in line with Accountability to Affected Populations commitments. Additionally, Concern will aim at strengthening inclusion of and ensuring accountability by community-based and other non-traditional humanitarian responders, for more effective protection to affected populations.
Concern Worldwide will work proactively in promote synergy with other Clusters and WASH partners, as WASH is mostly cross-cutting in various sectors, to ensure adequate coordination of the Project activities through regular coordination with Health, Education, Shelter/NFIs, Protection (Including CP and GBV AoR), Nutrition, CWG and FSL Clusters to add value to the overall response through integrated response.
During the project implementation period, Concern will be well aware of the need to adequately prepare the beneficiaries, local authorities and the line government departments for the Exit/Hand over of the installed systems and services. The strategy is such that the projects are designed keeping in view adequate handover arrangements with clear links to development actors and the relevant Government departments leading to durable solutions and resilience. This will be achieved through training and capacity building initiatives with the local authorities after a thorough capacity assessment including alignment with government plans.

1. - Projects activities

Concern’s water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) interventions will be deployed in emergency contexts to help reduce the risk of disease by providing safe water, reducing open defecation and promoting hygiene practices in Gedo and Banadir Regions. Concern’s WASH Emergency Response strives to implement interventions that: are efficacious, simple, well-timed, and community-driven; link relief and development; and address barriers and facilitators to use with communities. WASH interventions consistently aim to reduce the risk of disease and transmission in emergency contexts, thus, program design and beneficiary preferences are important considerations to ensure the efficacy and effectiveness of interventions targeting those facing extreme poverty. Water Supply: In Gedo Region, Concern Worldwide will construct and/or rehabilitate 2no. 1000m3 Berkads (10x20x5m deep), 3 stage silt traps (4x2.5x3m deep), using Dam Liners secured with stone garbions, and Green Mesh for evaporation control, elevated tower with 5m3 fibre glass tank, solar pump and water point (6 taps), serving 400HH (at least for 4 months). There will also be upgrading of 3no. water supply systems, by incorporating the 25m3 elevated 4m high storage tanks, HDPE pipe transmission lines extended to a maximum distance of 2km, to strategically located communal collection point (6 taps) serving 1500HH. In Banadir, Concern Worldwide plans to provide water to displaced IDPs through pipelines and water trucking (where there are no existing water systems), serving 10,000 people for 2 months. Following assessments, Concern will extend pipelines from existing public or privately owned boreholes/wells and install metered water points in the IDP settlements. Sanitation: In Baidoa, Concern Worldwide will construct 250no. latrines – 200no. emergency normal latrines (10,000 people) and 50no. (500 people) latrines for people with disabilities. The latrines will be pour flush (squat basins for normal and seats for Special Needs), with offset pits, and will be fitted with hand washing facilities. All target households will be provided with solar hand held lights to be used to provide light during the night, aiding in protecting women and girls. This will work aim to cover the existing sanitation gaps in the areas where Concern had previously worked in IDP camps in Baidoa District, Bay Region. Community Engagement - Community engagement in the WASH sector of humanitarian response will be expanded and regularly monitored or evaluated. Sanitation projects, which previously involved the community only in the construction phase as a paid labour force, or as a cash-for-work initiative, will apply the more inclusive approaches, especially the Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) (Household adults) and School Led Total Sanitation (SLTS) (school children and teachers).Engaging school children - At the community level, disaster risk prevention will start with children and youth (boys and girls), to enhance their impact in disaster risk preparedness. Children will be broadly engaged to successfully participate in mapping hazards; raising awareness through radio broadcasts and games; and influencing other children, teachers, parents, and communities on how to reduce disaster risks.Hygiene Promotion: Across all target areas the project propose hygiene promotion and awareness through trained volunteer Community Health Promoters (CHPs). The project will recruit and train hygiene promoters to disseminate hygiene messages during house-to -house visits; mass hygiene promotion awareness using radio messaging, hygiene messages on bill-boards located in strategic locations and flyers/posters. Based on assessments conducted during the project start-up, Concern proposes to distribute 3,000 hygiene kits to the most vulnerable members of the community and IDP Camps. These households will be identified on a needs bases with criteria as decided in conjunction with community representatives.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In terms of policy/programming, the strategic reconstruction goals are given more attention and systematic treatment in reconstruction planning, coordination and phasing of different modes of support to ensure sustainable, resilient, and equitable WASH facilities management and services during and after crisis situations. Concern understands that institutional failures to address WASH-related challenges can act as risk multipliers, compounding existing situations of fragility, and how improving WASH management can contribute to building resilience in the face of protracted crises. Concern recognizes the gender and protection risks associated with WASH activity provision and has robust internal policies, Complaint Response Mechanism and gender and protection mainstreaming included beneficiary selection and project activities. In addition to the above, Concern will also work in all target locations with the community and local authorities to identify and train selected community members in established Water Management/Environment Committees on management, operation and maintenance (O&M) of the water supply system. The trained committees will then be issued with basic operation and maintenance tools for the water supply systems and pumping equipment. The committee will be then continue the operations after the end of the project. This will be done in close collaboration with the local authorities and leaders, the ministry of water and other stakeholders.
