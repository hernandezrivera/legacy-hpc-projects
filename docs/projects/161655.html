Project Summary

The Ten years of conflict have made the humanitarian situation in the North East of Nigeria remains particularly challenging with an estimated 7.1 million people.
Among this affected population, WHO estimates that approximately 5 Million people (including 613,783 children aged 0 to 59 months, 3.16 Million children between 5 and 18 years old, 525,740 adult women and 301,860 elderly) from 30 LGAs across the BAY States (46% of the total number of LGAs in this region) live in areas with no or low access to health services. This lack of access to health care services is driven by various factors that affect the capacity of the individuals to access adequate health services:
• Lack of functioning health care facilities
• Security constraints
• Lack of referral mechanism
• Cost
This low or no access to health services exacerbate the risks of increased mortality and morbidity within this population due to exposure to high level of vulnerabilities (disease outbreaks, lack of immunization, lack of treatment for common and complex ailment, etc.).
Since 2016, the provision of life-saving services to the affected communities is a joint effort between the Government of Nigeria and international humanitarian community.
The lessons learned from previous and ongoing initiatives, the action will address the above mentioned context and needs with a view to reduce mortality and morbidity in areas with low and no health service coverage through increased delivery of and access to high impact health and nutrition services. This will be achieved through a three prone approach:
• Providing to 724,662 people access to free comprehensive basic lifesaving in maternal and child health, mental health, gender based violence interventions and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states through the Hard-to-Reach Mobile Health Teams (H2R MHT) and Community Resource Persons (CORPs) delivery platforms.

1 - Needs

A decade after the onset of the crisis in the North East of Nigeria, the humanitarian situation remains particularly challenging. According to the Draft 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview, it is estimated that 7.1 million people with nearly 1.8 million people in IDP camps in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states in dire need of humanitarian assistance., including 3.83 million women and girls, are in need of humanitarian assistance. Among this affected population, WHO estimates that approximately 5 Million people (including 613,783 children aged 0 to 59 months, 3.16 Million children between 5 and 18 years old, 525,740 adult women and 301,860 elderly) from 30 LGAs across the BAY States (46% of the total number of LGAs in this region) live in areas with no or low access to health services. The categorization of these areas as having no or low access to health services relies on the triangulation of several indicators collected by WHO and other partners: security level in the areas, availability of functional health facilities, number of functional health posts per 10,000 people, number of functioning health facilities with basic emergency obstetric care, percentage of immunization coverage for vaccine preventable diseases per LGA, etc. This lack of access to health care services is driven by various factors that affect the capacity of the individuals to access adequate health services:• Lack of functioning health care facilities – A recent Health Resources and Service Availability Monitoring assessment report presents baseline data from 809 assessed health facilities across 27 LGAs in Borno State. Only 41% of the health facilities in those LGAs are fully functional while up to 48% of the facilities are not functioning (including fully damaged ones) and 11% partially functioning. Additionally, 64% of the assessed facilities were either fully (31%) or partially (33%) partially damaged with only 34% of the facilities not damaged. Non-functionality of the health facilities resulted from damaged to infrastructures (60%) and lack of access due to security constraints (32%). From the fully and partially functioning facilities, just over 25% had operational in-patient services, 20% had trauma services, 25% had the necessary stocks of essential medicines and supplies.• Lack of referral mechanism – This is situation is compounded by the fact that the affected population face significant obstacles to access secondary health care and referral services, especially in hard-to-reach areas due to the critical loss of ambulance services and lack of specialized health care providers. Only 39% of health facilities have a functional referral mechanism to a higher level of care. From the fully and partially functioning facilities in these areas, only 25% had adequate referral mechanisms in place according to the September 2018 Health Sector 5W report. This hampers continuum of care and the capacity to improve the health status of the population. The delivery of quality services and referrals for secondary care to prevent maternal deaths and maternal morbidity remain limited as the availability of GBV-specialized assistance. Within the mental health sector, mental health treatment services are for instance only available at the Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital in Maiduguri despite the estimation that up to 650,000 individuals are in need mental health support. In contrast with this lack of access to health services, this population is indeed facing increasing needs for health care and treatment due to the ongoing conflict and the related risks. Data generated from the WHO Early Warning and Response System in November 2019 indicate that malaria compounded by malnutrition is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality for 45% of cases and 33% of reported deaths. This vulnerability to malaria is increased considering that 45% of the health facilities that would have provided malaria prevention or treatment are either completely or partially destroyed

2 - Activities

WHO’s action will address the critical issues with a view to reduce mortality and morbidity in areas with low and no health service coverage through increased delivery of and access to free lifesaving maternal and child health, mental health, clinical management health and nutrition services. This will be achieved through a three prone approach: 1) Providing to 724,662 people access to comprehensive and high impact health and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states through the Hard-to-Reach Mobile Health Teams (H2R MHT) and Community Resource Persons (CORPs) delivery platformsUnder this approach, WHO will deliver extended basic and essential health packages in areas with low/no access to 724,662 individuals incl. This will be achieved through a modulation of H2R MHT and CORPS health delivery platforms taking into account the situation in the targeted areas (security, number of facilities, population, etc.) to ensure comprehensive access to quality and high impact health services. These platforms are specifically designed to deliver services in very challenging environment such as protracted crisis. The robustness of those health service delivery platforms has been tested and demonstrated in other areas across the NE through previous experiences.The H2R MHT is a health service delivery platform that will provide a package of basic health services to affected population in settlements located in remote areas with low to no regular access to health services. Each mobile team is composed of Senior Community Health Extension Worker, Junior Community Health Extension Worker, Nurse/Midwife and Data encoder. At LGA level, each team is supported by LGA facilitator and Cold chain officer. Through the deployment of up to 52 H2R teams, and 32 Mental health officers, WHO will effectively address the issue of access to care for the targeted beneficiaries by delivering comprehensive free quality lifesaving health services thereby addressing immediate needs of the beneficiaries throughout the course of this action. The intervention services provided will include:- treatment of common ailments- antenatal care to pregnant women, vaccines, - provision of immunization, vitamin A and deworming to children, - screening of children for malnutrition,- Strong community engagement, risk communication, health promotion activities - outbreak detection.- Referral to the secondary level for management and.- Mental Health and gender Based violence interventional services Improving quality of GBV services provided in the health facilities through assessment of quality and readiness of the health facilities to provide GBV services. WHO will support the facilities to improve quality of services through capacity development, health system supports and supply of appropriate commodities and materials. The CORP is a health service delivery platform that provides Integrated Community Case Management (iCCM) of childhood illness. Through the deployment of 557 CORPs and 57 supervisors, WHO will provide iCCM services addressing the main vulnerabilities and risks affecting this target group including: treatment of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea, screening for malnutrition and other potential high-risk health conditions, health promotion services and referral for health and vaccination services. The basic package of health services to be provided to the beneficiaries through these platforms will be expended to include medical support for other vulnerabilities related to non-communicable diseases, notably Gender Based Violence (GBV), HIV/AIDS, sexually transmitted infections (STI) and mental health. The deployment of these specifically designed health service delivery platforms will enable WHO to reach the targeted 724,662 beneficiaries and provide them with the necessary quality and comprehensive free care to reduce mortality and morbidity in areas with low and no health service coverage.
