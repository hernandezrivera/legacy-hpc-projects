Project Summary

The project aims at supporting war affected households by replacing old tents with dignified shelter units and distributing NFI kits to displaced people in Idleb and in Al Bab in Aleppo. In total, 28,000 people will benefit from the activities of this project (14,140 males – 13,860 females) 18,000 people out of them in 2022. 150 transitional shelter units will be constructed to replace old tents in Camps. NFI kits will be distributed to 2800 HHs that have been displaced from different areas into Idleb and Aleppo and 1200 HHs will receive winterization assistance. In camps, roads of five camps will be levelled, and rain drainage established.

3 - Needs

As the war enters its twelfth year in Syria, the humanitarian situation is becoming worse. protracted displacement continues in northwest Syria. As of October 2022, the CCCM database includes 1,430 IDP sites that host 1,859,651 IDPs or 354,655 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 74,983 IDPs are reported to be persons with disabilities. Snowstorm incidents occurred in January 2022 in various IDP sites in Aleppo (22 sites) and Idleb (9 sites) governorates, mainly in the districts of Afrin, Azaz and Harim, 362 tents were affected of 2,142 individuals and 429 families, including the IDP’s belongings. According to the reports received, there is a crucial need to open snow-closed roads, moving the affected beneficiaries to safe places, securing means for heating, and replacing destroyed tents.(CCCM).Nearly 85 per cent of the overall population reported a deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs. 30 per cent of the population rely on either communal or private generators, exposing households to high fuel costs and driving a need for quality winter clothing. Consequently, basic items are further out of reach as families’ average monthly income ‘v’ expenditure gap reaches 60 per cent. The number of people in need of NFI support is expected to further increase in 2023 with a projected worsening of the economic situation. (HNO 2023).

4 - Implementation of activities

This project will compromise replacing old tent with transitional shelter units (Activity1.2.2.1), in addition to improving the infrastructure in camps (road gravelling and water culverts) (1.2.3.2), distributing 1000 core NFI kits ( Activity 1.1) distributing 1000 vouchers for winterization (2.1). The activities of the project will benefit 16,250 beneficiaries (4,030 boys under 18 , 3,608 girls under 18, 4185 men, 4428 women). Two members of the staff (a male and female) who are trained on HLP will be responsible for any HLP issues confronted during implementation of the project. A technical needs assessment of the site will be conducted before starting site improvement works. The selection of beneficiaries for the dignified Shelter activities will be based on the following criteria: Families who live in tents, Female headed households; Families with chronically ill members or with disabilities. PWD will be taken into consideration in all activities: door to door distribution for PWD, during site improvement a ramp will be constructed with handrail and other required items etc,.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two times at higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit their complaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Access restriction due to local fi ghting between different arm source groups in thetargeted location which may restrict the access. The local fi ghting might delay theimplementation of the activities. Mitigation: HIHFAD Security team will monitor thesituation on the ground and send reports when necessary. The works will be suspendedduring the fi ghting and resumed after assessment of the situation. Assumption: thesecurity situation remains relatively stable which allows people to continue living in the area and the activities of the project to take place. Risk: Closure of the border for commercial shipments or changing some regulation related to allowed items to be exported to Syria may have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities. Mitigation: 1. Explore alternative supply lines through local procurement 2. Communicateregularly with logistic cluster to have valid information about new or changing regulations. Assumption: The crossing borders are open for humanitarian and commercial shipments. Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment of women and girls) Mitigation: 1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 -Train staff on code of conduct – 3 - Include code of conduct in all staff contracts that address any SEA complaints.
