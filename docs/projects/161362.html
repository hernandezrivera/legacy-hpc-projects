Project Summary

The proposed emergency response project seeks to promote school enrollment, increase attendance and retention through providing emergency school feeding, preventing and reducing disease outbreaks in Unity State, Panyijiar and Rubkona counties as well as in Western Equatoria State Mundri West and East Counties. Project participants will be constituted of Returnees, IDP and host communities. Mercy Corps has on-going integrated emergency response programs in four sectors of education, WASH, FSL and Child Protection in the proposed counties. The proposed project will implement the following
A: Construction and Repair of classrooms: The project will construct 10 new TLS and repair 15 blocks of existing damaged classrooms to improve the learning environment for 10,500 (5,500 girls and 5,000 boys) children in the selected counties.
B: Emergency School feeding to reduce malnutrition and promote school enrollment, attendance and retention of conflict affected boys and girls in Panyijiar. Under this intervention, Mercy Corps will engage local community stakeholders, PTAs, local leadership, youth to jointly identify 10 schools and/or learning spaces to participate in emergency school feeding. Other activities that will be conducted during the inception phase (month 1) of this project include minor repairs of school kitchens and warehouses, mobilization for coordinating provision of community contributions such as labor, firewood and additional cooking utensils; training community members on nutrition, health and food handling. From month two going forward, the project will distribute hot cooked meals based on the WFP food basket to boys and girls in 10 primary schools for 5 days a week. To mitigate the risk of school drop out for girls in upper primary classes, the project will distribute take- home rations senior girls in the 10 schools.
C: The WASH in schools interventions will focus on preventing and reducing the outbreak of cholera and other water borne diseases. The project will repair school latrines and install hand washing facilities in 25 schools Unity and WES. Moreover, the project will conduct hygiene promotion session messaging on cholera symptoms, modes of transmission, prevention and referral pathways to health care centers for cholera treatment. Additionally, the project will request dignity kits for school girls and soap for handwashing from the WASH Cluster core pipeline for distribution.
D: Capacity strengthening is a cross-cutting aspect in this emergency response project. The project will mobilize and train of 325 community members (200 women; 125 men) PTAs, youth and local administration on psycho-social support, WASH, nutrition, disease prevention and life-skills. Mercy Corps will also train 200 teachers (80 F and 120 M) and 325 PTA on school management, record keeping and PSS. In addition to this training, the teachers will also be train in EiE methodologies, pedagogy and learner centered approaches. 60 % of the project will be implemented in Unity while the remaining 40% will be in WES.

1 - Needs

The education cluster estimates that some 2.8 million boys and girls of ages 3 to 18 will be out of school due to conflict and crises country wide. Additionally, more than 40,000 teachers and school management committees will require humanitarian assistance to be able to deliver quality and inclusive education services. Demotivated teachers are consistently looking for jobs hence loosing concentration of their work or duties there by deliver poor quality services. Due to insecurity in the country most schools were destroyed and furniture looted and building left with nothing to use for education purposes. Movement of communities to deeper locations where there is perceived peace and locations where there are no school infrastructures. More than two thirds of school going children are out of school in Unity and Mundri states. Access to basic education is a gap in some locations which had facilities vandalized and communities had evacuated the places but now are returning back. In most of payams were returnees are returning there are barely no schools at all. In places were schools are they are in a bad shape and not conducive for learning and some children learning under the tree which makes school difficult during rainy season. Lack on WASH facilities and psychosocial support is a gap in some of the schools. School age children and youth are loitering in the communities because of lack of adequate infrastructures and resources for quality education services. According to the IPC data released in August 2019 most counties in Unity State were in phase 3 (crisis) and 4 (emergency) and Panyijiar is singled out as one of the counties that is in critical need of food assistance. The education cluster assessment of 2018 assets that hunger is one of the top causes of school drop outs if not averted with interventions such as school meals and food aid. In addition of climatic shocks Unity state food situation is affected by persistance cattle raids that pose security threats and affect movement of food commodities to local markets. Low levels of qualifications amongst teaching staff continue to impact negatively on the education outcomes (education strategic plan 2017 -2022). This has resulted in low pass rates amongst children especially those who are in hard to reach areas. The education cluster (2018) also found out that nearly 50 % of the schools assessed do not have gender appropriate WASH facilities and this affects attendance and retention rates of menstruating girls.

2 - Activities

The project will implement a broad range of activities to improve access to education and PSS services to 10,500 children (5,500 girls and 5,000 boys) in Unity (Panyijiar and Rubkona Counties) as well as in WES (Mundri East & West Counties). Activities will be construction of TLS, repair of damaged classrooms and establishment of gender sensitive WASH facilities in schools. Furthermore, the project will conduct trainings for teachers, PTA and SMC. Teachers training will be in PSS, referral pathways for protection, GBV, nutrition and health; as well as EiE methodologies including pedagogy and learner centered approaches. 325 PTA/SMC (225F and 100M) will be trained in PSS and school management. 300 teachers (100F and 200M) will also receive appropriate trainings. In addition, the project will provide life- saving messages to 10,500 children (5,500 girls and 5,000 boys). Lessons learnt in this project will be documented through the robust monitoring system of the Program Management minimum standards of Mercy Corps. MC will employ multiple strategies namely; community mobilization, community feedback and engagement, joint monitoring and review meetings.Community mobilization: This will activate and sustain community support for education, promoting enrolment, improving learning environment and ensure safety for both boys and girls. Emphasis will be on girls and other vulnerable children with special needs especially those living with disabilities. Construction and rehabilitation of TLS & WASH facilities: There is a shortage of classrooms due to the cumulative effects of conflict, schools were looted, damaged and most of them are in a state of disrepair. To improve the learning environment, the project will construct 10 new TLS (6 in Unity & 4 in WES). Moreover 15 classroom blocks (9 in Unity and 6 in WES) will be repaired. PSS: Given the level social fabric destruction, and destitution brought about by the conflict, most children are in the state of trauma. Many children are orphaned as a result of the war, therefore, are in need of PSS. In order to improve learning outcomes, it is essential for education program to incorporate PSS in education. The project will train 200 teachers (80 F & 120M) as well as 325 PTA members (200 F & 125M) in PSS. 60% of those trained in PSS will be from Unity State while the remaining 40% will be from WES. Capacity building: One of the challenges affecting the education sector in South Sudan is low capacity levels of educators. This problem is further aggravated by low remuneration and insecurity that act as push factors out of the sector. The rate of attrition amongst the volunteer teachers is high hence the need to train newly recruited volunteers teachers. The project will train 500 teachers (200F; 300M) in EiE methodologies, basic pedagogy and learner centred methodologies. The initial training will last for 5 days and a 2-day refresher course will be conducted midway through the project.School supplies. Most of the parents are unable to provide basic school supplies to their children due to economic challenges. The project will distribute teaching and learning supplies sourced through UNICEF core-pipeline. Supplies will include exercise and text books, teacher guides, recreational kits, ECD kits, girls’ dignity kits. Monitoring and evaluation: The project will conduct a baseline survey at the beginning of the intervention. Based on the recommendations of the baseline, some indicators/targets will be adjusted and other activities revised to make them more feasible if necessary. During the project implementation phase, consultative meetings directly with participants will be carried out. In addition, the project will also conduct post distribution surveys to gauge levels of satisfaction. In addition, the project will also conduct quarterly review meetings with stakeholders. Outputs of these review meetings will be used to refocus project activities where necessary.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project works closely with the government line Ministry of General Education and Instruction at both the national and subnational levels. County education directors and their staff interface with the project on a daily basis. Mercy Corps enlists that support of Ministry of Education trainers when conducting PTA and teachers trainings. In addition to this, the project conducts community dialogues with the local leadership creating a platform where commuties directly engage with the project.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to the General Education Strategic Plan of 2017 -2022 guided by the education act of 2012. Priorites of the strategic plan are a)to ensure equitable, quality, relevant, appropriate, accessible and affordableeducation services for all.b) to provide quality education that promotes social cohesion and sustainabledevelopment.c) to ensure cost-effective education management systems at all levels.d) to mobilize and utilize financial, human and material resources effectively and efficiently.This project by providing learning spaces, wash facilities in schools, training teachers, providing school meals and conducting community engagement activities directly align with the first two priorities of the General Education Strategic Plan

The project will be implemented as part of an integrated Mercy Corps program that is comprised of WASH, Child protection, FSL and Education. Through linkages with Protection partners, the project will ensure that the principle of "do no harm" is upheld so that beneficiaries are not put in danger as a result of project interventions

This knowledge will remain with the community and remains applicable to various scenerios in the future.
