Project Summary

ATAA, based on strategic plan of 2023-2024 will continue to focus on responding to the immediate protection needs of those most at risk, special attention will be given to enhancing sustainability of interventions, as well as addressing obstacles to resilience and early recovery by implementing the following intervention.
The project aims to support the resilience of affected populations in Idleb and Aleppo through provision of integrated protection activities focusing on CP, Protection, and GBV prevention and response services based on the structure of static facilities and mobile teams will operate protection intervention in 2023 targeting the IDPs sites and host communities of Northwest Syria.
According to the needs and severity scale of 2022, ATAA will target the most severe locations in Idleb including (Dana, Maaret Tamisrin, Idleb) sub-districts, and Aleppo including (Atareb, Daret Azza, and Al bab) sub-districts by the following structure:
Three Protection community centers in Maaret Tamisrin - Kelly, Qah – Dana, Atareb – Atareb, along with 5 CFSs in Qah, and Maaret Tamisrin, and 3 mobile teams in Atmeh, Atareb, and Al Bab will provide integrated protection intervention with a total of 86 qualified protection staff members with gender balance.
ATAA is operating in all protection sub-clusters in the mentioned locations emphasizing the general goal of ATAA which focusing on community-based approach by provision of prevention and response services with focusing on integrated intervention in coordination with WASH, shelter, FS, and Education programs at ATAA to enhance the holistic approach of intervention adopted by ATAA and providing the following:
Child Protection:
ATAA will provide child protection prevention activities through the five child friendly spaces and mobile teams targeting formal and non-formal camps along with communities to reach most vulnerable children with the following services:
Awareness raising about CP concerns based on CP AR taskforce co-leaded by ATAA.
CPSM surveys
Recreational PSS sessions
Specialized case management including provision of ECF
Structured programs including (structured PSS, life skills, parenting skills)
Capacity building for humanitarian workers on child protection fundamentals.
GBV:
ATAA will continue providing GBV services within WGSS and the outreach team reaching IDPs sites and communities to provide the following set of activities:
Specialized case management for GBV survivors with the provision of ECF (cash and in-kind)
Vocational training for women and girls
Structured awareness-raising sessions based on GBV awareness-raising toolkit
Structured life skills program
Capacity building of non-protection workers on the referral of GBV survivors
Distribution of dignity kits
Safety audit in targeted camps
Protection:
ATAA within the protection centers in northwest Syria will continue providing general protection services targeting vulnerable people with high focus on elderlies and PWDs as follow:
Protection monitoring surveys
Safe identification and referrals
Legal awareness-raising sessions on CSD and HLP
Legal counseling on CSD and HLP
Individual Protection Assistance
Cash assistance for vulnerable individuals and families.
Mine Action:
ATAA will provide EORE sessions through outreach mobile teams targeting IDPs sites and affected communities through the awareness-raising messages developed by MASC.
ATAA will continue providing PSEA interventions including capacity building and awareness raising of targeted communities as a mandatory approach in all programs and activities.
ATAA will ensure gender equality during Implementation by targeting all gender groups including male, female, adolescents, elderlies, PWDs, and minorities starting from the designing phase by participating in needs assessment, and establishment of community/CP committees which will be gender balanced and will enhance the accountability to affected population approach and reaching all age and gender groups.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a protection crisis and violations of IHL and IHRL continue to drive humanitarian needs. The protracted nature of the conflict, compounded by the impact of the economic downturn, scarcity of natural resources and energies, lack of documentation, food crisis and epidemics have severely depleted the resilience of affected populations to protection threats.Based on 2023 Syria HNO-ISG, inequalities, discriminations and a rise in the frequency and variety of gender-based violence (GBV) against women and girls are paired by a social normalization and an increased perception of unsafety.Where both women and girls are increasingly exposed to GBV, in the same time the decrease in funding is jeopardizing GBV survivors’ opportunity to access lifesaving servicesDepending on Syria HRP 2023, children and adolescents, girls and boys continue to be exposed to multiple child protection risks. Grave violations against children -including risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused- remain a significant concern. Insecurity and economic hardship continue to exacerbate child protection concerns including harmful coping mechanisms such as child labor, child/early marriage putting adolescent girls and boys at risk. Separation from caregivers and absence of alternative care options is common across Syria. High psychosocial distress prevails among children and caregivers.

4 - Implementation of activities

Generally, there will be static facilities within relatively densely populated locations. Mobile units will be attached to those fixed centers to reach out to smaller populations including IDPs sites and camps. ATAA will coordinate with local authorities to ensure access and community acceptance of the proposed activities. Besides, ATAA plan to deliver the proposed services as integrated and multi-sectoral to make them more appealing to targeted communities. The proposed activities will be delivered through center based and outreach approaches by qualified protection staff targeting IDPs sites and host communities in addition to support and establish community structures (CP networks, community committees) to participate in the implementation and designing of the project.Awareness raising sessions, structured activities and training sessions will be delivered physically with provision of supportive IEC materialsAll project staff and community committees will be trained on CP, and GBV core concept in addition to specialized training set for specialized services including case management, supervision, along with PSEA and CSG training to support the protective environment and do no harm principle during the implementation.CWDs will be targeted with specialized services in addition to provide CWDs inclusion training for frontline workers to support targeting this vulnerable group after providing accessibility of PWDs to all protection facilities of the project.

Then they will sign ATAA related policies before reaching out Project BNFs.R3: Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating Measure: awareness raising session about this type of services will be provided in the targeted locations and inform community with the privacy and confidentiality of service.R4: service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral.R5: GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. R6: CPCM staff will cause harm for CP cases, L: law, I: high, Mitigation: CPCM staff will be trained on case management and supervision, and qualified staff will be hired.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy developed in June 2022 and with official approvals from PSEA network and UNICEF PSEA focal point and delivered to all staff to be signed, on the other hand ATAA became low risk partner in SEA with UNICEF for 5 years after conducting PSEA assessment.ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will get orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.ATAA is operating PSEA stand-alone project focusing on raising the awareness of targeted communities (78 community) in NW Syria about PSEA and enhance the ability of BNFs to reach reporting channels of ATAA and PSEA network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Loss of resources in the local market (L: M I: M): the resources will be bought from the Turkish market.2. Escalation in armed conflicts and displacement of civilians (L:M I: H): this will require high coordination with the cluster groups to map the services for the displaced population and refer them through service mapping and provide emergency response package.3. COVID outbreak leads to delays or suspension of activities. (L: H I: H)All activities will become very context-dependent and will be adjusted accordingly in close coordination with all stakeholders. In addition to shifting to an online module for activities using WhatsApp groups for adults and children to maintain the BNFs' maximum reach during any restrictions.4. suspension of cross boarder SC resolution. (L:M,I:H) Coordinate closely with donors to continue provide the service through handed over grants and search for alternative sources of funding. ATAA has contingency plans that are set to ensure adequate planning and preparation for a variety of potential emergency situations of deterioration of security conditions in the area of ATAA presence and provide instructions and guidance to ATAA staff involved in the mitigation planning. As well, ATAA has a risk management policy in place which forms part of the ATAA governance framework and by which ATAA processes the whole set of activities carried out to identify, assess, manage, and monitor any risks to which ATAA may be exposed.
