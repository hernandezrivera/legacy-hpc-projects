Project Summary

The project aims to mitigate deteriorating food security conditions, protect livelihood assets and improve the resilience of at least 4,000 affected community categories and segments beneficiaries H.H to recover from conflict and disaster induced shocks in 8 villages in Wadi Salih, Zalingi and Azum localities, in the Central Darfur state. The project also specifically intends to improve the livelihoods of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities, The strategy for that is through the implementation of a variety of activities that include:, distribution of agricultural inputs: improved seeds of okra and water melon, tools,(hoes, weeders and shovels), emergency livestock assistance that includes animal treatment ,drugs, vaccines, restocking of goat by provision of 4,000 goat to 800 families, and The project, too, seeks to enhance community capacity building in delivering extension on agriculture and livestock intervention, intends to economically empower women through rebuilding their destroyed asset base through distribution of goats to those women who totally lack or own very small herds (e.g. goats and sheep). The provision of these assets is believed to help improve nutritional status of the family (especially children and the elderly). Raising the skills and capacities of the communities, and their committees, in order to overcome issues that inhibit their progress and local growth - to upgraded community skills and knowledge.

Needs

Following evident impact on people’s food security and livelihoods, because of conflicts and climatic adversaries, an evident improvement on security situation is now felt by many: communities themselves and policy and decision makers. The opening up in security and stability situation invites a quick help and assistance for the affected communities to recover from impact of shocks and start to rebuild destroyed lives and livelihoods. That being said, community’s ability to do so, without external intervention, is limited as the toll of conflict is enormous and the existing available resources for the community fall far short of the needed level for self-help or assistance thus the need to request grant assistance. Without this further deterioration in community life is expected. It is important to reverse the current situation and transform it to a better one.. The project is designed to re-build the capacity of targeted IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in order to achieve their own food security and improve their livelihoods by increasing production and income to better help themselves withstand future shocks, drought and other natural disaster.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Though project’s main objective is to improve people’s quality of life through improved food security and livelihoods, food security and livelihoods of people in suggested area depends, largely on use of natural resources: farming, herding and access to forest products. Natural resources, in the area, are of course finite, with very few or no, strategies to protect them against excessive use or even extinction: massive logging for timber and charcoal making is widely practiced; wild fires cleaning areas for farming and use of forest products, for sell for income and as a livelihood means speeds destruction of natural resource, without rebuilding and construction plans in parallel. Our suggested intervention is cognizant of these realities and shall work, not only to minimize such practices, by supporting communities manage their natural resource far more better than they do now, our intervention will seek to help environment be better managed, a friend to users and shall have the laws, regulations and procedures, with the consent of users, that protect it and further its just cause for continuation to give and confer dividends expected from it. Our project design shall pay greater attention to environmental coordination, protection and legislation processes and shall train communities protect it. Our reports and monitoring processes shall also give attention to this.
