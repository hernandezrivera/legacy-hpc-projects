Project Summary

The provision of Reproductive health services including ante- and postnatal care, family planning and the management of sexually transmitted infections have all collapsed due to the ongoing conflict. There has been an alarming increase in rates of cesarean sections throughout the country. Mental health remains chronically neglected and there are only two partially functionning public mental health hospitals in the entire country. Due to the closure of health facilities and lack of health staff most of patients are now referring to private health facilities that is making out of pocket expenditure more catastrophic, especially for IDPs, migrants and people living in conflict zones.
This project is designed to provide emergency reproductive and sexual health services to 75,800 women of child bearing age among conflict affected, IDPs and migrant population and host communities. The services are planned to be provided in conflict affected municipalities / geographical areas ranked as 4 and 5 on the severity scale with a focus on un(der)served population, where the target population and host community live. EmONC services will be provided to an estimated 12,128 Pregnant women. The target population are scattered in these conflict- affected municipalities. Considering the humanitarian situation in the country, scarcity of midwives and OBGYN specialists, low capacity level and limited availability of female healthcare providers (especially specialized nurses and midwifes) to provide quality SRH services and unavailability of SRH supplies in the health facilities, there is clearaly a need for a humanitarian complemantory service package. The services that will be provided through this project will be in line with minimum initial services package (MISP) for life saving sexual and reproductive health services. UNFPA will work with its partner agencies, stakeholders, communities and local authorities to provide the following:
1. Prevent excess neonatal and maternal mortality and morbidity among conflict affected population
2. Prevent Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and HIV / AIDS
3. Provide clinical management of rape survivors and establish a center of excellence for CMR services and referral mechanism between health facilities and Tripoli forensic medicine hospital.
4. Develop the capacity of health care providers and managers (with a focus on female health cadre) on coordination, assessment, planning and provision of basic and comprehensive SRH services
5. Provision of emergency reproductive health kits, essential medicines and supplies to health facilities to maintain minimum healthcare provision.
6. Psycho-social service provision at health facility and community level through female health workers.
7. Increase awareness of migrants, IDPs and returnees through female health workers and establish a referral pathway from community to health facility and hospital levels.
8. Distribution of clean delivery kits to pregnant women on the move.
9. Strengthening referral system for emergency obstetric care services
10. Deploy mobile medical teams along with skilled birth attendance to provide basic EmONC services
11. Ensure coordination and leadership for the Reproductive Health Sub-working group under the health sector co-chaired jointly with the Ministry of Health;

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Libya has got worse with intensified armed conflicts between GNA and LNA forces. The recent clashes and airstrikes are getting closer to civilian residential areas, including detention centers. This seven-year protracted crises have left hundreds of thousands without full access to life-saving healthcare and essential medicines. IDPs, refugees, migrants, returnees and people living in conflict zones are identified as having the most severe needs. The main hospitals lack basic life support skills and equipment; in far-flung areas there are no adequate skilled health workforce. This situation is further compounded by closure of more than 25% of health facilities and among open facilities, 22% offer no services. Only 6% of 78 functioning hospitals are providing essential services. Highly skilled healthcare providers have departed from health facilities in remote areas as well as in conflict zones. As of December 2019, 75 healthcare providers were killed and 49 were injured in 60 Separate attacks. The health sector estimates 535,287 people to be in need of basic health services across Libya further exacerbated with the current conflict around Tripoli. Out of them, 170,000 are IDPs and returnees, 184,202 migrants and 28,472 refugees. The lack of rule of law and, consequently, the increased level of crimes and corruption in the country has paralyzed social services, especially health and protection services. Among the affected population of almost 1 million, there would be an average of 140 births per days, of which 15% would need emergency obstetric and neonatal care (EmONC), including at least 5% Caesarian section. Increase in other morbidities can lead to miscarriages and fetal losses; malnutrition, leading to anemia, which further increases the death toll because of post-partum hemorrhage. In the complex emergency of Libya, protective societal institutions are weakened as people flee, family dynamics are leading to domestic violence and health seeking behaviors are jeopardized. The forced displacements around Tripoli and other parts of Libya has exposed the people, especially women and girls to violence, extreme poverty, separation from families and communities. The lack of access to healthcare and community support has increased the risk of women and girls to sexual violence and exploitation. Family planning is not available in public health facilities and limitations to quality sexual reproductive health services could lead to higher mortality rates among women and children. The lack of access to emergency obstetric care and sexual health services can also increase the spread of STIs, including HIV, increase in unsafe abortions, and morbidity related to high fertility rates. The provision of Reproductive and sexual health services including ante- and postnatal care, family planning and the management of sexually transmitted infections have all collapsed due to the ongoing conflict; and there has been an alarming increase in rates of cesarean sections throughout the country. Mental health remains chronically neglected and there are only two public mental health hospitals in the entire country. Due to the closure of health facilities and lack of health staff most of patients are now referring to private health facilities (if available) that is making out of pocket expenditure more catastrophic, especially for IDPs, migrants and people living in conflict zones. Sexual reproductive health is closely related to personal dignity, choice as well as to physical and mental health. Hence the need to addresses barriers to access sexual and reproductive health with a holistic and rights based approach in the socio-cultural context of Libya.

4 - Protection risk analysis

1. Security restrictions, attacks on healthcare workers and humanitarian staff, 2. Delays in procurement of equipment and services due to disruption of public services in Libya. Due to the escalation of conflict; the social and diplomatic services may be disrupted. Similarly, local companies might not be able to provide high tech medical equipment. This could delay the procurement and dispatch of equipment and subsequently relevant activities. 3. There are challenges related to the importation, warehousing and distribution of pharmaceuticals. There are delays in Clearance of health supplies, Reproductive health kits, especially CMR kits and FP and HIV/AIDs testing and treatment commodities.4. Absence of national organizations with experience in provision of healthcare services and institutional setup. 5. Lack of interest of authorities and local communities’ engagement6. Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse;7. Some population groups may not have access to SRH services because of their ethnicity, their legal status or their tribal and political affiliation;

5 - Mitigation measures

1. UNFPA will consider best practice from its experience in other humanitarian contexts and will adhere to the safety and security policies. Mandatory field security training for all staff will be provided before going to the field. UNFPA will ensure partners are not exposed to further risk by providing security awareness, deconflicting service delivery points and coordinating with the humanitarian access working group.2. UNFPA will prioritize local procurement and early start of clearance processes in collaboration with MOH;3. A joint approach and further advocacy at a higher level with Libyan National and sub national authorities is required to speed up the import approval and custom clearance procedures. 4. Engage national non-governmental organizations in the implementation of project with opportunities for capacity building and hands on practice sessions for their staff on the application of global standards in programmatic and operational areas. 5. Increase the engagement of local actors and establish strong networking with local communities for increasing the ownership and support through stronger and smoother communication with both state and non-state actors;6. According to UNFPA policies, all staff members working for UNFPA or its partners should recieve the PSEA training. UNFPA is actively engaged and fully committed to Zero tolerance to SEA.7. UNFPA will work jointly with other protection sector members and the HCT to advocate for facilitated access to SRH services for people who face discrimination. Through its GBV program, UNFPA will conduct safety audits in collective centers and will assess access barriers to SRH services.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNFPA designed this project to address the emergency health needs of women and children in humanitarian settings. This project proposes a package of activities that ensure access of women, girls, men and boys to basic and emergency SRH services. The project meets their sexual reproductive health needs and rights equally. UNFPA will ensure accountability through coordination with MOH, consultation with the target population and feedback from the communities. In addition, UNFPA will ensure accountability to the health cluster, UNOCHA and the donors through implementation of the project activities with full transparency and integrity and in a timely manner. Reports of the project activities will be prepared in close coordination with health cluster and implementing partners. Training activities for different RMNCH component such as BEmONC, STI and MISP have been planned in cooperation with MoH and after a thorough capacity needs assessment and in consultation with health coordinators and stakeholders on the ground in the prioritized municipalities. Female and male community representatives will regularly be consulted at health facility level to reflect their opinions and advice in project implementation. Capacity building of health professionals, men and women with sufficient knowledge and skills will also increase access of conflict affected women from affected communities to basic SRH services including services for clinical management of rape survivors. Increasing awareness of men will support in reducing gender based violence incidences and will improve referral of SRH and GBV and SV survivors.Some other cross cutting issues like prevention of STI, infection prevention, safe blood transfusion in Comprehensive EmNOC facilities, will be addressed through provision of different RH Kits as part of MISP.Waste management guidelines developed and endorsed by MoH will be implemented to make sure that waste disposal is carried out in a proper manner. Monitoring checklist of relevant health facilities will be reviewed to make sure that functional incinerators exist in the health facilities.
