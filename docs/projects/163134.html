Project Summary

The ongoing crises in the South-West and North-West regions of Cameroon and the multiple displacements caused by it have affected children the most. It is estimated that more than 270.000 children are in need of psychosocial support services in the SW and NW regions, in addition to 120.000 children in the adjacent regions, namely West and Littoral. Children are also being reportedly recruited into armed forces and armed groups by both parties to the conflict to perform different tasks. As the conflict persists, more cases of sexual violence, harassment and forced and early marriages accompanied by unwanted pregnancies, particularly targeting adolescent girls, are being reported. Majority of those girls are pregnant and/or HIV positive, while they do not have access to medical care, including pre-natal care, due to the lack/loss of civil documentation (i.e. ID card or birth certificate) to go through checkpoints to access health facilities/services. Family separation is one of the major concerns identified by Child Protection actors in this crisis, with at least 2500 children are either unaccompanied or separated from their parent(s), most of which are reported to be living with other members of their communities or left alone in child-headed households in deplorable conditions in urban and peri-urban areas, including in neighboring regions of Littoral, West and Adamaoua, where very limited services available. Considering the myriad of protection issues affecting children, the purpose of the project is to:
Provide psychosocial support in child-friendly spaces/other secure spaces to 6,000 children aged 5 to 9 and 4,000 adolescents aged 10 to 19 including unaccompanied and separated (boys and girls) affected in various ways by the humanitarian crisis in the project intervention area;
Prevent future abuses by establishing 15 Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms within the project’s coverage for psycho-social support, GBV prevention, family separation prevention and reunification;
Support 15 adolescent clubs (Girls’ and Boys’ groups) in at least 45 community-based initiatives related to peace building, conflict resolution and life skills.
Strengthen local capacities to provide a protective environment and promote accountability to affected populations by training 100 child protection workers and improving their technical capacities to monitor violations and respond, sensitize communities and raise their awareness to monitor and identify violations, provide urgent psychosocial support and referrals to other services.
The project is targeting 15000 children SW (MANYU Division), NW (Boyo and Menchum Division), West (Noun Division) regions and is expected to last for 12 months
