Project Summary

The Action aims at providing life-saving core and seasonal NFIs to support highly vulnerable people, including IDPs and returnees. The Action will provide mid- to long-term sustainable resilience to those most in need for items assistance, to ensure and maintain their health, privacy and dignity. GVC plans to distribute essential and supplementary NFIs kits to 15,000 highly vulnerable adults and children, including IDPs and returnees: 1,500 HHs (7,500 people) will receive a core NFI kit (A.1.1) and other 1,500 HHs (7,500 people) will receive a seasonal NFI kit (A.1.2). The distributed items will comply with the Shelter / NFI Sector indications and specifications. Beneficiaries will be selected according to vulnerability criteria set in close consultation with the targeted population. Different stakeholders, including people in need, community leaders and other local actors will participate in setting these criteria, to ensure transparency, acceptance and accountability. Selected HHs will include female-headed HHs, HH with elderly people, and/or people with disabilities, and/or infants. The Action targets 6 sub-districts in the Governorates of Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor (Hadher in Jebel Saman District; Al Bab District; Tal Rafaat in Azaz District; Khanaser in As-Safira District; Membij District; Deir-Ez-Zor city), defined as among the most severe locations based on the severity scale (all ranked 3), People in Need and data/information provided by the Shelter / NFI Sector and recently collected. GVC has a working space in Aleppo and an operative team in Deir-Ez-Zor, able to implement and closely monitor the proposed activities. All contents of the kits are purchased from local vendors in Syria.
The Action will be implemented in cooperation and coordination with SARC.

4 - Needs

Needs have been assessed through: UNOCHA and other reports/updates; consultation with GVC field teams in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor, who periodically conduct field visits, ad-hoc meetings, observations, etc. With the crisis in its 8th year, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain deep and far-reaching. Over half of the country’s population fled their homes: 5.5 million people have sought refuge in the region and beyond; 6.1 million are IDPs. According to 2019 HNO 11.7 million people remain in need of humanitarian assistance. Of these, an estimated 5 million people face acute humanitarian needs, with around 60,000 living in IDP/last resort camps (Shelter/NFI Sector). As per 2020 HNO, 3.42 million require NFI assistance. Market functionality continues to be disrupted, reflected most clearly in the high cost of items, which renders them unaffordable for many. Over 75% of communities reported that clothing had been unaffordable or unavailable over the last 3 months, and similar levels were also reported for both cooking and heating fuel, batteries and children’s diapers. NFI assistance continues to be an essential and life-saving need, particularly for those newly displaced or forced to relocate. While market access appears to be gradually improving and NFIs are available, many essential, humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining basic items continue to be unaffordable for people. NFIs become increasingly important during winter months to ensure families can live in safety and dignity, being an essential seasonal support. Need for clothing, hygiene items and heating fuel were among the most reported NFI needs across the country by communities, with differences according to age/gender. Core relief items will be distributed in selected communities to those who experienced sudden onset displacement or who have been cut off from access to markets. Seasonal items will be distributed to sustain lives and reduce exposure to extreme weather conditions.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities link to Sector Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support.The intervention targets areas in 5 sub-districts in the Governorate of Aleppo (Hadher; Al Bab; Tal Rafaat; Khanaser; Membij) and Deir-Ez-Zor city. Due to the volatility of the context in North East and North West Syria, specific locations will be selected during the implementation phase according to needs identified.Activities: 1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs (e.g. mattress/mat, plastic sheet, blankets, jerry can, kitchen set, solar lamps) (target 7,500 people. 1 kit per HH per 1,500 HHs). A) Preparation. Coordination with SARC to obtain relevant approvals. B) Selection of beneficiaries. The identified locations have been ranked 3 in the NFI severity scale. Households will be selected and prioritized in accordance with the agreed vulnerability criteria. C) Procurement of different age/gender specific core and essential NFIs. D) Creation of detailed distribution plan. E) Distribution of items, in cooperation and coordination with SARC. F) Monitoring. 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter clothing, fuel, winter-specific shelter upgrades). (Indicator: 7,500 people. Distribution of 1 kit per HH, per 1,500 HHs). A) Preparation. Coordination with SARC to obtain relevant approvals. B) Selection of beneficiaries. The identified locations have been ranked 3 in the NFI severity scale. Households will be selected and prioritized in accordance with the agreed vulnerability criteria. C) Procurement of different gender specific seasonal and supplementary NFIs. D) Creation of detailed distribution plan. E) Distribution of items, in cooperation and coordination with SARC. F) Monitoring.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A)Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigating Measure: 1.Agree to minimum assistance package across sector. 2.Assess and validate any changes before they occur. 3.Identify/exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. B)Tension between host communities/IDPs/returnees Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigating Measure: 1.Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not status. 2.Selection criteria are transparent/groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. C)Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion) Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigating Measure: 1.Sector partners ensure that their staff read/is aware of the Code of Conduct. 2.Sector partners develop measures and internal procedures to identify staff perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. D)Risk of physical harm at distribution points Likelihood: high Impact: medium/high Mitigating Measure: 1.Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding. 2.Training to staff on establishing and managing distributions. 3.Pre-consultations with communities. 4.Ensure access to distribution points by all groups considering people with specific needs. 5.Application of checklist at distribution sites. E)Risk towards the humanitarian nature of the intervention for the presence and activities of military and non-humanitarian actors Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigating Measure: 1.Pre conciliation with community leaders 2.Increase use of coordination where necessary. F)Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population. Likelihood: low/medium Impact: high Mitigating Measure: 1.People are informed about their rights to receive assistance regardless of the documents they have. 2.Training of distribution staff about rights of affected population to receive assistance.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISKS: 1-Security situation deteriorates up to prevent implementation of some activities. 2-Shrinking of humanitarian and operational space in some intervention area due to military operation. 3-Major change in attitude of authorities and local NGOs toward planned interventions. 4-Major change in demographic in the AoI due to population movements requires modification in the strategy. 5-Diversion of aid from staff. RISK MITIGATION STRATEGY: 1-GVC ensures continuous coordination with SARC and UNDSS to monitor the situation. GVC adopts a security plan and SoP in case of any major accident. 2-Policy environment is monitored through attendance to Cluster, WGs and coordination mechanisms. 3-GVC ensures good relationship with authorities, informing on planned activities and ensuring transparent and accountable way of working. Plans of intervention are shared and discussed in order to increase acceptance. 4-GVC monitors the demography in the area and liaise with other agencies. 5-GVC directly ensures the M&E of the activities. CONTINGENCY MEASURES: 1-Security alert system in place prevents risk and different routes or timing are adopted to assure the service. In case of prolonged period of instability, activities will be stand by and information released to the donor. 2-If it is impossible to access the area, it will be studied the change of the area (any change in the implementation strategy will be discussed with the donor). 3-Any unpredictable decision of authorities on priorities to assure livelihood support and development in the target areas will be discussed and solutions found according to the final objective of the project and to the improvement of the conditions of the beneficiaries. 4-Any corrective actions will be shared with the donor and be accountable to the budget availability. 5.Performance of staff will be monitored by the Syrian and Regional coordination staff. Any breach will result in expulsion from the staff and the replacement with other personnel.
