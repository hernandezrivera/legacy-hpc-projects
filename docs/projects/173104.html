Project Summary

Protection needs are the priority concerns address by this project, owing to the protracted conflict which erupted back in 2011. Both Libyan and non-Libyan children are disproportionately affected by the consequences of the active hostilities. Namely, they are at risk of domestic violence, recruitment by armed groups, SGBV, abduction, unlawful detention, ill-treatment, killing and maiming due to the presence of UXOs. In particular, children on the move face heightened risks of human trafficking, arbitrary detention, forced labor, physical abuse and sexual exploitation. In order to effectively reduce the vulnerabilities INTERSOS has established in 2018 a Center of Excellence, called Baity center, in Tripoli and implemented the same model in Sabha in 2020. Bayti center is a multi-sectoral programme involving education and child protection. The majority of the participants are children aged 6-18 and youth up to 24 years old, both Libyans (host community and IDPs) and non-Libyans (refugees, migrants). Baity aims at creating a safe and supportive environment and providing a range of education services that encourage children and youth’s self-development and well-being. Indeed, the center is a safe space which provides non-formal education services, life skills through learning support services as well as awareness raising sessions to children and families. In fact, both children’s families and the local community are involved in the project through outreach and awareness-raising activities.

1 - Needs

In 2020, dramatic changes in the operational context, including a shift in the conflict and the COVID19 pandemic further exacerbated the humanitarian crisis in Libya. COVID19 forced the country to adopt stringent measures, including full lockdowns, roadblocks, partial or full closure of facilities (including schools and health facilities), and severe limitations in provision of services and availability of basic goods. The pandemic affected people’s mental health and psychosocial wellbeing, particularly women, children, migrants and refugees. COVID-19 exposed not only fears but also heightened distress and feelings of hopelessness and helplessness. Conflict-related displacement continued throughout 2020. During the month of June alone, nearly 28,000 people were displaced following an intensification of the conflict, bringing the total number of IDPs in Libya to 392,241.The lack of access to formal education due to the COVID-19 pandemic has negatively impacted the physical and mental health and wellbeing of children, exacerbated by living conditions characterized by insecurity, poverty, exposure to explosive ordnance, limited recreational activities and inability to access safe spaces.
