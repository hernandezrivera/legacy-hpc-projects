Project Summary

LWFs Shelter and NFI response is targeting natural disaster/flood and conflict affected most vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and host communities who have been affected over the past one year. LWF planned to reach 1150 HHs (about 6,900 people) from IDPs, Returnees communities and host communities in Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties through provision of Shelter and NFI and 11, 670 through awarenees sessions on DRR to support resilience increasing their ability to respond to shocks.
The beneficiaries’ selection will be done through community participation and lead by Community-Distribution Committees (CDCs). The process involves identification, registration and verification of beneficiaries. To ensure complains are responded timely and properly there will be Complain Response Committees (CRCs) in each payam at county level.
The project will address the shelter building through conditional cash modality to build Tukuls and restricted cash transfer (cash voucher) modality for fulfilling HHs Emergency NFI Needs. The shelter component will be addressed through conditional cash transfers to beneficiary HHs in two rounds, the first-round transfer will be made after each HHs has prepared the construction site (clearing the ground and laying foundation) and the second round will be transferred after starting building of Tukul walls. The NFI component will be addressed through cash vouchers.
LWF team will also support beneficiaries with technical assistance to ensure beneficiaries raise ‘improved shelters’ on higher grounds, with higher bases to avoid being submerged by future possible floods.
The project will ensure the sustainability of its outcome through working together with customary institution, line departments, women groups, youth groups, and local administration from the beginning of the project to ensure issues related to land ownership are discussed and solved ahead of the construction of the tukuls.
Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted to inform future action and help to take corrective measures and collect the feedbacks and level of satisfaction of the beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

According to OCHA Flood Snapshot released on 21st of October 2020, Jonglei state have been impacted by flood with an estimated 369,891 people(61,545 HHS) being affected. Among the most vulnerable: women, elderly and people with disabilities.In Duk County there has been torrential rain from July to October 2020. As a result of the flood, significant number of house/Tukuls were submerged and collapsed, people affected by flood faced displacement and loss of livelihood and livestock., 50% of planted cereal crops was damaged at early growth stage in June and at maturity stage in September. Significant number of domestic animals died. Pit latrines submerged in water collapsed. Due to flood and inter clan conflicts in Duk over 50,000 individuals verse I in sever condition (RRC Inter Agency Rapid Assessment October 2020). From 20th of July 2020 to December 2020, Twic East county had been under flood due to torrential rains across the upper Nile basin that increased the water in the river. The primary dyke got broken, and the water reached Pakeer, Ajuong, Nyuak, Lith and Kongor payams of the county. This primary dyke was constructed in early 2003/2004 by heavy machineries’ (tractors). It had been separating Jonglei from direct flooding from the swamps. With the flood, the county got completely taken by water, with few areas being still protected by the secondary dyke, for example Panyagor town has been surrounded by water becoming a port for medium and small boats. There are few still few other spots in other five payams not covered by water. Except these few spots, all areas are under water. Of the 120,000 inhabitants of the counties, majority were displaced and moved to Central Equatorial states and Lakes state. In addition to the displacement, the flood caused, almost 85% of houses/Tukuls to be under water and many collapsed,100% of planted cereal crops were damaged at early growth stage in June and at maturity stage in September, over 3000 goats died due to flood, schools, health centers, latrines had been under water, boreholes have been flooded. After the displacement at end of November’20, there were about 20,000 people remaining in Twic East county. In February 2021 reports indicate that some returnees are coming back to the county, with hope that the water level is reducing. The current residents of the county are mostly living in very confined manner and it will be the same for upcoming returnees, with hope of the water level is reducing and dry spot are coming out , there is high need of shelter and NFI to improve living condition and support resilience of the most vulnerable households in the counties Similar to Twic East county, Jalle community of Bor south county were displaced by flood in August 2020, LWF conducted a rapid assessment on 23rd and 24th October, 2020 at the places the community has been displaced to. The displaced community resettled around Bor town in places called Kondai, Wutchuei and Malual Aglobar. Kondai is about 20 km from Bor town in the north east; Wutchei is 25 km also in the north east, and Malual Aglobar is 30 km to the south east. Communities from Akuac Deng settled in Kondai; Kolmerek community settled in Wutchuei and Kuei/Jalle community settled in Malual Aglobar. Akuac Deng community counts 1,430HHs / 5,000 people displaced and settled as IDPs in Kondai. 2,114 HHs / 22,000 people from the Kolmerek community were displaced and settled as IDPs in Wutchuei. Kuei/Jalle community counts 2,810 HHs /18,265 people displaced and resettled as IDPs in Malual Aglobar. Similar to other counties there is high need for emergency shelter/NFi to improve living condition, support resilience of the most vulnerable households in the county. While ensuring community dignity through increasing their ability to respond to shocks. 40% of the target of the project will be in Duk county based on the severity 30% in Bor South and 30% will be in Twic East county.

2 - Project activities

Objective 1: Improve living condition of highly vulnerable disaster affected /protracted IDPs, returnees, host communities (all affected but not displaced) who failed to fulfill their shelter and NFI needs • Provide NFIs assistant to most vulnerable 500HHS from IDPs, Returnees and host communities through conditional cash provision (Restricted cash)• Provide Shelter assistance to most vulnerable 75 HHS from IDPs, Returnees and host communities to renovate/upgrade shelter (Tukuls) through conditional cash provision (Restricted cash) Objective 2: Improve resilience of disaster affected(flood) affected returnees (retuning to Original place) through shelter and NFI support • Support 500 most vulnerable HHs from Returnees with NFI Voucher • Support 75 most vulnerable HHs from Returnees with shelter voucher to build Shelter (with local materials) Objective 3: Enhance communities’ dignity though building their capacities to respond to shocks(disasters) • Form and Train CDC (Cash Distribution Committees) and empower them to act on Shelter NFI intervention • Form and train community complain respond mechanism and empower them to act on shelter NFI complain handling • Training of 1150 HHS on life skills, shelter building and NFI• Community led Monitoring of construction of Tukuls /upgrading of shelters• Post Cash Distribution Monitoring and Reporting• Conduct dialogue with customary institution, line departments, women groups, youth groups, and local administration from the beginning of the project to ensure issues related to land ownership

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

LWF has a zero tolerance policy against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. In line with this, all staff sign Code of Conduct which binds them to PSEA commitments. Staff have also, undergone mandatory PSEA training to ensure adherence to the policy, and regular awareness creation is done to influence behaviour change. All SEA allegations are reported and processed as per established guidelines, systems for survivor assistance have been put in place including integration of SEA in CP and GBV referral pathways. To enhance accountability, all this information will be communicated to beneficiaries at onset of the project. In addition, to scale up PSEA information to the project beneficiaries, awareness raising sessions will be conducted; focusing on building community capacities to spread PSEA messages, reduce risks of occurrence, reporting and supporting to reduce community stigma towards survivors.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most of people in targeted counties are illiterate, and among the most vulnerable selected beneficiaries several will be people living with disables, elderly and females whom are physically and culturally less prone in providing feedbacks. For this reason, LWF intends to work in close collaboration with the community-based mechanisms (Cash Distribution Committee, Complain Response Committee), staff members (local staff) who can closely work with the most in need providing immediate support and orientation throughout the project phases.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

LWF maintains a Community Based Participatory Approach in project interventions. Project inception meetings will be held with key community stakeholders and project beneficiaries; critical attention will be paid to inclusion of various groups within the beneficiary population. These meetings will be informing, as well as consultative, where methods of receiving complaints and giving feedback will be communicated, validated and agreed upon. Appropriate Complaints and response mechanisms will be established taking into consideration issues of safety, confidentiality and accessibility. The project will create awareness for the community to know how and where to report, regular coordination and feedback meetings with beneficiaries will be conducted, to provide timely response.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will actively seek beneficiaries’ feedback and use it appropriately. Feedback will be reviewed and analyzed to document learning, adjusting, improving assistance where gaps are identified, strengthen and consolidate positive learning; as well as adopting lessons learnt as part of knowledge management to inform future programming interventions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Local capacity to prevent and respond to protection risks will be built, targeting first line responders such as, community leaders, local structures as well as LWF staff. -Regular refresher trainings and awareness sessions are conducted on CoC child safeguarding, PSEA policies-Information about assistance, reporting and referrals will be centrally disseminated in languages and format understood by all project beneficiaries.The project will conduct a quick assessment on population dynamic in terms of gender relation , power and resource relationship in relation to shelter and NFI. Based on the assessment the project will developed risk mitigation measures which can range from involving community-based protection committee formation and empowering of specific individual to monitor GBV issues in the project area, consultation of girls and women to tailor the response on their needs (time, place and modalities of distribution)-The project will take targeted actions to ensure inclusion and participation of vulnerable groups including, consultations with vulnerable groups in community meetings or FGDs, through targeting representatives of vulnerable groups to receive and address specific needs, interests and concerns. -Through awareness campaigns and trainings, representation of women, girls and other vulnerable groups will be encouraged and supported. -To mitigate this risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries, LWF through community engagement strategy will ensure issues of selection criteria and entitlements to project assistance will be clearly explained. In addition, through continuous engagements and participation of stakeholders in the decision making processes related to the project interventions, any conflicts that may arise will be handled to avoid further escalation into conflicts. -Concerning HLP access and rights it is important to know that in the project areas land is owned by clan and sub clans, hence the project will work with Payam chiefdom and the local government to ensure that IDPs and returnees can access land. The project will support meetings and forums where parties can reach agreement on land property and use, written legal documentation will be ensured once decision on ownership will be taken. For returnees going back home in their land of origin, the clan will support the process of reallocation of land. The CDC will have a central role in reporting to LWF staff possible disagreement and tension on land ownership, the monitoring of shelter construction done by LWF staff together with community members will help in ensuring possible grievances are solved on spot.-The project will create awareness on participation of person with disability and elderly person. The awareness will focus on community structure and local authority to understand the situation empower persons with disability and elderly person, during distribution and shelter constructions the most vulnerable will be supported by community members to take part to the project activities.
