1 - Needs

As the 13 years old conflict in the BAY states has changed little in terms of scale and severity. The impact is profound: some 8.3 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection, mostly women and children. Of the two million IDPs in the BAY states, 83 per cent reside in Borno, living in overcrowded IDP camps or in host communities without adequate shelter and with limited access to threadbare basic services, including health care, water and sanitation, education and livelihoods. Some areas of the BAY states have become relatively stable, encouraging an estimated 1.5 million to return to their places of origin. They are returning to communities devastated by conflict, struggling to rebuild their lives. The majority of people in need (4.8 million) live in so-called host communities or communities affected by conflict. HUMANITARIAN PROGRAMME CYCLE 2023 ISSUED February 2023Conflict in the BAY states continues to drive displacement and disrupt agriculture and income-generating activities, resulting in heightened food insecurity, particularly among the 2.3 million internally displaced persons in 471,346 households and 2.1 million returnees in 341,895 households (IOM DTM Round 45). Economic shocks and adverse climate conditions further strain agricultural yields in both the northeast and northwest regions. WFP Nigeria Country Brief July - August 2023The results of the October 2023 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis on food insecurity projected that about 3.3 million people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states will face high levels of food and nutrition insecurity from October to December 2023. This is a 10 per cent increase from the 3 million people projected within the same period in 2022. The number of food-insecure people in the BAY states is expected to increase to 4.4 million during the 2024 lean season (June to August). This is slightly (2 per cent) higher than it was during the 2023 lean season when about 4.3 million people faced a severe food crisis. Over 4 million people have needed urgent food assistance annually in the BAY states since June 2020. Trend analysis indicates consistently high or rising food insecurity levels since 2018. Nigeria situation report 20 Nov 2023 Food insecurity and malnutrition are among the main drivers of humanitarian needs in the BAY states. Access to farmland is still limited in many areas because of insecurity and many of the 2.2 million displaced people do not have alternative livelihoods. They are often forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as survival sex and child labour to stay alive. Over the past year, dozens of farmers have lost their lives, and others have been abducted or injured trying to eke out a living by venturing outside the security perimeters of Borno’s garrison towns. Nigeria situation report 20 Nov 2023According to the 6th IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) analysis for Northeast Nigeria with two projections (October – December 2023 and January – April 2024), an estimated 1.53 million children aged 0-59 months are likely suffering and expected to suffer from acute malnutrition from May 2023 – April 2024. The acutely malnourished children aged 0-59 months include 511,807 Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases and 1.01 mil-lion Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases. In addition, nearly 206,779 pregnant and lactating women are likely suffering and expected to suffer from acute malnutrition from May 2023 – April 2024. Northeast Nigeria Nutrition Sector Bulletin October 2023During the first projection (October – December 2023), which corresponds to the harvest season and period of decreasing acute malnutrition, 15 of the 62 LGAs analyzed in Northeast Nigeria are likely to be in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious). During the second projection (January to April 2024), which corresponds to the post-harvest season and period of low acute malnutrition, 17 of the 62 LGAs in Northeast Nigeria are likely to be in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection mainstreaming is put in place during project implementation to ensure that human rights are respected by national and international laws. This is to ensure that the principle of Do No Harm prevails throughout our intervention to avoid increasing risk to already vulnerable populations. To ensure accountability, the project will train and refresh staff, volunteers, monitors, PMCs, and CNMs on overarching issues such as Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Accountability to Affected Population. CHRISTIAN AID will ensure the integration of accountability, and reporting mechanisms disseminated in local languages, accessible to all, with PSEA and safeguarding messages employed in ongoing awareness campaigns at the local level. CHRISTIAN AID has developed an accountability procedure and accompanying accountability guidance which clearly states that appropriate complaints and feedback channels must exist within every field office and location and response to allow children, vulnerable adults, and communities to safely and confidentially report serious complaints (including safeguarding concerns, sexual abuse, and exploitation concerns and breaches of the code of conduct), to understand and support affected population own voices by managing complaints in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages, throughout the project cycle. CHRISTIAN AID also has trained staff on safeguarding and PSEA investigations, responsible for the investigation of cases of this nature. CHRISTIAN AID actively participates in the PSEA task form in Borno state. CHRISTIAN AID safeguarding policies and code of conduct which has zero tolerance for any breach will be applied in any case, however, there will be awareness and sensitization on the prevention of such acts. In alignment with this policy, CAID Protection will be guided by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) core principles of human rights and equity, participation, doing no harm, building on available resources and capacities, integrated support systems, and multi-layered support. The focus is on mainstreaming protection through the delivery of emergency response and recovery services in participatory, safe, and socially appropriate dignified ways. To ensure the mainstreaming principles of dignity, meaningful access, and avoid causing harm, Project beneficiaries will be prioritized using the different levels of vulnerability such as persons with disabilities and female-headed households. The affected population will be included in design and implementation activities that will enhance their food security. Given the high levels of vulnerability of target groups, safety, and access issues will be constantly assessed, including safety traveling to training sites, childcare provision, location, time, and day of training. During project implementation, CHRISTIAN AID will assess local markets, food consumption, and gaps emerging within the target locations. These will be documented as reports and shared with WFP as well as other partners of the Food Security cluster. WFP will therefore seek ways of closing these gaps and increasing the space for them to ensure their protection. This is in alignment with WFP's third Protection principle: Food assistance activities will be based on context and risk analysis, including an understanding of how protection gaps contribute to food insecurity and hunger, and vice versa, and how WFP’s interventions can help close these gaps. To reduce the amount of time spent at the distribution/redemption sites and commuting to distribution and redemption points by beneficiaries, CHRISTIAN AID will develop a distribution plan that will specify when it is the turn of group beneficiaries that will be based on sex, age, size of family and geographical location to come for distribution at the designated distribution sites.
