Project Summary

Pilgrims of Hope is a local/ national NGO with operational presence in Unity and Central Equatoria and proposes to respond to critical Education activities, by targeting 5,137 children/ people (2000 girls, 3000 boys) 69 men and 68 women). In line with the 2023 HRP Allocation strategy, whereby the organization will not only work in the proposed education sector, but also link and coordinate its actions with other actors in child protection and GBV integration in addition to other sectors in health, nutrition, protection. The action, which will work hand in hand with line ministries of General Education and Instruction, Water and Irrigation and local leadership structures, Gender, Child and Social Welfare among others from state, county to Payam levels.
Proposed Education actions which will respond and support towards establishment and rehabilitation of TLS facilities, scale up Back to Learning Campaigns to increase enrolment and improve attendance, provide essential education supplies including teaching and learning materials for children and teachers, train teachers and Parent Teacher Association (PTAs) members on Education in Emergencies, basic pedagogy, and psychosocial support and provide WASH facilities including hygiene kits and establish child help desks in coordination with child protection actors.
These actions will be integrated with WASH, Child Protection ad GBV whereby the action will provide Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) and Hygiene kits among girls, female youth and women of menstruating age; through provision of psychosocial support targeting boys and girls in schools, mapping child protection services, training teachers on child protection and awareness raising on child protection risks to mitigate child abuse and to enhance children’s access and retention in education. GBV actions mainstreamed will support safety audits on schools, GBV awareness raising and reporting risk mitigation on GBV and PSEA, production of IEC materials that will address early/ forced marriages and GBV referral , trainings on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Psychological First Aid and provision of individual and group psychosocial and counselling support including to survivors of GBV. Children with disabilities will be facilitated through disability inclusive actions.
Pilgrims of Hope has in addition all staff signed a code of conduct that includes Prevention from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment. Proposed actions will also ensure that actions have an environmental mainstreaming component, use a do no harm approach, gender and disability inclusion including facilities that will ensure people with disabilities have access to proposed services, in addition to gender sensitivity where key facilities will be gender sensitive and dis-aggregated and referrals to various sectors.

1 - Needs

Waves of violence continue to impact schools and learning spaces in the country with at least five million children of school going age not having access to education and 1,095 schools damaged due to flooding and conflict. The situation of these vulnerable children is further impacted by the COVID 19 pandemic where by 2.2 million children were already out of school (Education Cluster Assessment 2021). Unity State has in the recent past witnessed escalation of subnational violence and unprecedented flooding with consequent multiple displacements of at least 25,000 boys, girls, men and women, including people with disabilities. Recent waves of violence have seen human rights violations which included killings, sexual and gender based violence affecting at least 33 women and girls, recruitment and use of children by armed actors among other grave violations with consequent mental health psychological impacts among vulnerable boys and girls (UNMISS, 2022; Protection Cluster Updates 2022; Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report, Mayendit County, 2021/ 22). Impact of floods and violence has been worse in areas such as Dablual, Mirnyal, Rubkuay, Kuok/Thakkar, Tharjiathbor, Jaguar and with consequent implications on schools and WASH facilities which have been destroyed/ submerged, which include 19 boreholes and at least 18 schools and temporary learning spaces which some have been relocated to highlands and Mayendit North, with consequent implications on increased school drop-out rates and diminished learning opportunities among girls, boys and children with disabilities. At least 15 teachers and volunteers were in addition displaced, in addition to available scholastic materials which were destroyed by floods, further compounding access to education, which also faces further challenges of limited retention and drop-out rates attributed to early pregnancies, lack of fees and lack of payment of teachers, lack of school feeding, resorting to other household livelihood activities (Education Cluster Assessment 2021). In addition, at least 18 institutional latrine facilities in schools and learning centres have collapsed due to floods, rendering women and girls at high risk of rape and other forms of gender and sexual violence. People have to wait till late to ease themselves and these put women and girls at danger (Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report, Mayendit County, 2021/ 22; IRNA Mayendit 2022). Flood induced access constraints and waves of subnational violence continue to curtail humanitarian efforts with key roads destroyed connecting various counties in Unity and access contraints in Central Equatoria. Displaced populations have had to grapple with deteriorating access to schools, high drop-out rates, limited safe water supplies, with rampant open defecation and poor hygiene practices, more so among children (girls and boys), with most vulnerable being those with disabilities, a key precursor to water-borne diseases. Majority continue to use drinking water from river swamps in addition to water sources which are often shared with livestock with resultant contamination and increasing further risks for disease transmission. Available community, schools/ temporary learning spaces, nutrition facilities, health facilities lack appropriate sanitation facilities with some sinking and need decommissioning in addition to the sanitation facilities not being gender disaggregated.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concerning protection, POH will work collaboratively with other actors (UN agencies, NGOs, FBOs and CBOs) involved in child protection issues to safeguard the delivery of protection services in the schools and communities. It will work directly with the project schools’ teachers and the PTAs to promote zero-tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse in the schools including all forms of discrimination against girls and CWDs. POH will be accountable to the beneficiaries by enabling them to participate in the project at the planning, implementation, and evaluation phases. Finally, appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints will be set up. All POH South Sudan staff members have been trained on child protection, disability and gender mainstreaming, has own policies in place on PSEA, Child Protection, Child Safeguarding, Code of Conducts that are explained and countersigned by each staff and key supplier and have capacity to share the importance of this policies with any stakeholder they interact with. On regularly basis, POH conducts community engagement meetings such as awareness campaigns, innovative youth events that allows open participation/discussion over the quality of progress of the activity conducted. POH embrace a last-mile approach through field-based personal and daily visits to the field and conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analyzed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention and beneficiaries barriers to access services. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The capillary and continuous presence that POH has in the communities targeted by the intervention POH has more than 75% of its staff, including senior positions, and the extensive use of staff recruited locally among people recognized by the communities, ensures that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the implementation of the project. All project’s beneficiaries (female and male IDPs and returnee’s children, CWDs and host communities’ children) will be considered as capable and active resources rather than as passive recipients who do not have a say in the project. To include the perspectives of the very pupils and teachers, moreover, POH conducts in the schools that supports surveys that provide a reading of people and students wellbeing, expectations, and opinion about the schools. For this goal POH has prepared specific tools adapted to the context and of proven efficiency. By running this survey, POH found that in the schools of Unity-(Panyijjiar, Mayendit and Leer), where more pronounced had been the flood and community conflict sort of displacements, and nearly 60% of pupils/students reported to have lived in refugee camps over the course of the previous two years. Thanks to a very localized approach with POH staff regularly present and usually coming from the same community of project locations, accountability is pursued also directly through its staff that can act as bridge between top management and beneficiaries. Furthermore, considering the purposely field-oriented management team (officers and also project managers are field based), beneficiaries and affected population at large as constant possibility to interact and file complain to staff at different level. Furthermore, POH periodically shares finding of monitoring and evaluation activities with SMoE, PTAs and communities at large, ensuring that project decisions are justified, shared and finally owned by the affected population. Yearly, POH conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community where we work, the county and payam officials and traditional leaders (Boma Chiefs). During these events, POH explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. POH extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, establish a case specific dialog where POHs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in order to evaluate possible challenges/ intervention failure chance. Beside the surveys, POH’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings which provided platform to address issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can find the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported thus increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design accordingly.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Pilgrims of Hope has time and time placed protection and gender issues at the center of every intervention by engaging directly with the host communities’ children, IDPs, returnees and CWDs so that their needs, their priorities and their rights are addressed within the scope of the project. On this therefore, POH will always create conducive and interactive environments that that not only allows them but also encourage them to express their needs, rights and priorities. Apart from the active involvement of the rights holders in the project, POH will ensure that the duty bearers (Government, PTAs/SMCS, institutions and non-state actors) play an effective role to promote the rights of the CWDs and girls. Activities will be developed with the active participation of women and parents in all stages of the implementation process, to ensure that girls’ priority needs are taken into consideration and that WASH infrastructures meet the right gender standards (IASC, 2015, Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action: Reducing risk, promoting resilience and also aiding recovery accordingly. More importantly, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at service access points (schools, health facilitates, distribution points and at the community. But also ensuring that, safety audits are conducted and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of beneficiaries and reducing risks. Community members, staff and stakeholder’s capacity will be strengthened through specific PSEA trainings accordingly.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

POH has in place a tested and working system of four years multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 300 questions (including question on FSL, nutrition, GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a good number of interviews more than 300 HHs that are capable of providing detailed reference at the Counties and Payam levels, an analysis centered on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge becomes the internal foundations by POH staff and thus the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. Finally, the coordination bases that POH has across the targeted communities are equipped with phones of hot-lines, suggestion boxes; focal point protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complaints whenever it occurs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

POH will ensure initial involvement of teachers, women, and men and local women groups representatives in AAP committees and during the planning activities implementation; at project implementation they will be provided with information about available GBV services and collecting feedback from women,, men,, boys and girls about their needs/safety concerns; at the time where SMcs and PTAs are meeting their responsibilities especially during the covid -19 pandemic, the project will ensure the above are trained/oriented on how to safely respond to a disclosure and that they have the most up-to-date referral pathways. With support and coordination with GBV specialists from available local actors, schools will be used as modality to proactively disseminate information on available GBV services. Teachers, SMCs/PTAs and other volunteers will be equipped with pocket cards containing relevant contact information; Representations of the GBV referral pathways will be posted in all schools. POH will ensure installing of suggestion boxes in all schools, and appropriately. Beneficiaries access to information in form of communicating complaints and receiving feedback will be enhancing through formation of AAP committees at school and in communities’ level with representatives from each location/point who will be in touch with beneficiaries to receive such complaints and feedbacks. POH will meet with the AAP committee to discuss on the complaints and feedback from time to time preferably twice in a Month, on termly basis and there will be a help desk at field sites attended by one personnel from POH at a higher position to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries accordingly.

For instance, Inclusive selection process with set % for specific population components typically victims of inequalities (min 60% of females in project implementation, inclusion of elderly and people with special needs, and returnees). Finally, POH will have a gender & protection specialist, a gender officer and a protection officer dedicated to the analysis, supervision and adjustment of activities from the gender perspective.
