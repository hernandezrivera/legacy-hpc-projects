Project Summary

Africa Development Aid intends to provide quality protection services to crisis-affected children and their caregivers in Ayod and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State and Nasir county of Upper Nile State. This project will seek to provide an immediate lifesaving response to the vulnerable crisis and flood-affected children from returnees, IDPs, and host communities of Nasir, Ayod, and Fangak counties through strengthened comprehensive case management including family tracing & reunification; referral for services; provision of critical age and gender appropriate psycho social support services including parental skills sessions in a timely, holistic and child-friendly manner as well as to the traumatized adult caregivers whose children are also facing trauma, coordination monitoring and assessments of child protection related risk. The projects will also conduct awareness-raising activities on prevention of family separation, COVID-19 prevention, and response while putting into practice the COVID-19 preventive measures.
ADA under these project will provide static and mobile critical age and gender appropriate psycho social support services in individual and group sessions including Psycho social support to caregivers of children who are traumatized and undergoing PSS activities, identification, registration, interim care, family tracing & reunification to unaccompanied and separated children, advocate for GBV related risk prevention including early marriage, Sexual abuse, exploitation and violence, referral to other services such as health, education, nutrition, shelter and NFIs among others to those at risk and other vulnerable children including those facing other forms of violence, orphans as well as Justice for children (J4C) in a timely, holistic and child-friendly manner and also promote resilience to the traumatized ones.
The project will also seek to enhance the protection environment of vulnerable crisis-affected girls, boys, and youths to enable durable solutions through the establishment of community-based child protection networks, foster family groups, youth/adolescent clubs, Child-Friendly Spaces, child rights clubs, and recreational centers while observing COVID-19 guidelines. This will create enabling conditions for the realization of solutions and prevention of further displacement which leads to family separations; identification of risks, dangers, and impediments; advocacy and mitigation measures, psycho social support, peaceful co-existence initiatives, and resilience.
In Ayod County, the project will work supplement in the areas not reached by INTERSOS e.g.,Pajiek, Wau Menime, Haat and Kuach-deng payams. In Fangak County, the project will concentrate in Mareang, Keew, Paguir, Toch and Manajang (Juaibor) and Barboi Payams which were not reached by HCO with Child Protection services. Other locations not covered will be identified on a need’s basis, based on additional partner inputs in respective locations. ADA recognizes CAO and RI as partners in child protection in Nasir County. Identified gaps have been noted in Roam, Maker, Kiechkuon, Dingkar, Dhording, Nasir and Wanding Payams of Nasir County.
A total of 20,000 people (10,00 Males and 10,000 females) will benefit from this project. Out of this, 14,000 (70%) will be children (7,000 girls; 7,000 boys) and 6,000 (30%) will be adults – 2,000 males and 4,000 females. However, 6,000 of the total beneficiaries (28%) will be from the host community while 8,000 (40%) will be from Returnees and 7,000 (32%) from IDPs.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2021, 8.3 million people are mapped to be in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan whereby, 24% are women, 54% are Children and 15% are persons with disabilities. HNO 2021 report also indicates that, after signing the Revitalized peace deal in South Sudan, the Country still remained having high protection crisis, floods, violence and COVID -19. Women and girls are still facing GBV and PSS distress across the country. Harmful traditional practices such as early or forced marriages for girls, child abduction, child labor, neglect, abuse and exploitation. Children and their caregivers` physical well being and living standard and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. The needs are vast and the 2021 locations with severity of needs include Nasir County in Upper Nile State (Severity 4), as well as Fangak and Ayod Counties in Jonglei State (Severity 3). Nasir County at the border of Ethiopia and South Sudan has experienced flooding and COVID-19, leading to displacements, death and associated child protection concerns e.g., abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation. The majority of most vulnerable are women, children, persons with disability and elderly people. According to March 2020 REACH report in Upper Nile State and September 2020 IRNA assessment conducted by different partners from different clusters like (WASH, FSL, NFIs, Nutrition, Protection, Health) in Nasir County, they indicate that there are gaps of child protection services mostly in some Payams that are lacking Child Protection partners. A number of protection concerns such us early/forced marriages, child abuse, exploitation and violence were identified and reported. ADA, CAO and RI and covering some Payams with CP activities but some Payams are having gaps in Nasir County and those Payams are in need of the following services: • CPiE (Family tracing & reunification, psychosocial support to traumatized children and their traumatized caregivers via static and mobile CFS services • Mine risk education, and provision of case management services to UASC, • Children in Conflict with law (J4C) and referrals for other services). • Community sensitization and awareness-raising on the safety of children, child right and COVID-19 prevention. • Establishment, incapacitating and strengthening community-based child protection. However, according to the REACH assessment report, between June to September 2020 in Jonglei State, the report indicates high rate of GBV related risks e.g., domestic violence to women and girls, cases of unaccompanied & separated children, missing children, child abduction, orphaned children, earlier/forced marriage, exploitation, abuse, neglect, children drowning and snakebites and trauma among children and their caregivers. These concerns are in Fangak and Ayod Counties where ADA is targeting to implement this project. Based on the report, there is high need for the implementation of CPiE activities including (Family Tracing & reunification; psycho social support via CFS both static and mobile response; comprehensive case management to the UASC children who are in critical need of basic services, and some of their caregivers facing high trauma; mainstreaming of GBV in child protection as well as community sensitization and awareness-raising on the safety of children; awareness creation on COVID-19 and child rights in all targeted locations of Ayod and Fangak counties. ADA will identify more beneficiaries especially those who have suffered multiple displacements within Nasir and those who are returning from the neighboring countries and in need of support. ADA is having field bases in Ayod, Fangak & Nasir Counties. ADA intends to target 20,000 people (M=10,000 and F = 10,000) in all three Counties through the HRP 2021.

2 - Project activities

ADA will provide comprehensive training in case management for children and their caregivers, family tracing & re-unification, Justice for children (J4C), interim care for unaccompanied children and psycho social support to traumatized children and their caregivers. The activities will be conducted in Ayod and Fangak Counties alongside with INTERSOS and HCO, but in areas with gaps e.g.,Pajiek, Wau,Kuach-deng Menime, and Haat payams in Ayod County; Mareang, Keew, Paguir, Toch, Manajang (Juabor), New Fangak and Nyadin Payams in Fangak County. HCO is having Child protection project in Old Fangak Payam while other Payams have gaps in Child protection services. ADA is also doing Child protection activities in Barboi Payam of Fangak County, this project will support in uncovered Payams. ADA is have active integreted CP and Education Project in Nasir County currently, but covering only two Payams. CAO and RI are having child protection projects in some Payams of Nasir County meanwhile this project will cover the identified CP gap in some Payams not reached by both ADA and the other CP partners to avoid duplication of services. The Payams include; Roam, Maker, Kiechkuon, Dingkar, Dhording, Nasir and Wanding Payams of Nasir County. The specific ADA interventions will include the following activities: 1: Recruit and train 30 CP staff (15M, 15F) from Nasir, Fangak and Ayod Counties on revised CPMS, CPiE, CPIMS+ and Case Management SOPs. Case workers will identify; document; assess the protection needs of children; plan and implement interventions on the identified needs including referral to other actors for services, and follow up of cases using global child protection case management minimum standards. PSS facilitators will be trained on basic skills like how to communicate with children, signs of children facing distress or other forms of abuse, psychological first aid, planning and management of mobile & static Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) activities that are age, gender and culturally appropriate. Provide psycho social support activities including parental sessions and also select and train community-based child protection networks and foster parents on CPiE for all the targeted locations. 2: Strengthen case, management approach including provision of cash-based assistance for most vulnerable cases of UASC, J4C cases as well as to their traumatized parents. 300 most vulnerable children (150 boys & 150 girls) will be supported with cash-based Assistance (CBA) as part of case management support. Identification will be done using priority list from Standard Operating Procedures for case management. The well-being of all children with critical protection concerns will be monitored through follow up visits to ensure new protection needs and risks are prevented or responded to in good time. 300 UASC/missing children will be identified/registered, documented and referred for family tracing. 3: Provide psycho social support (PSS) services to 13,175 children and some of their 6,000 traumatized caregivers through provision of mental Health and Psycho social Support Services e.g., recreational activities, livelihood services and parenting skills sessions via mobile & static response. 4: Conduct 12 awareness sessions on prevention of family’s separation, COVID19 and harmful traditional practices prevention to 19,000 adults and children, these will be the same targeted parents and children for PSS. 5: Identify/train 225 community volunteers (CBCPN & Foster Parents) on CPiE in Ayod,Fangak & Nasir Counties. 6: Establish strong referral pathway in three targeted counties of Nasir, Fangak and Ayod, this will include service mapping to know which partners is doing what to ensure children access other services that the project will not provide, eg. child survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) will be referred to GBV actors etc.

3 - Project indicators

The success of this project will be measured through the following indicators; • # of girls, boys, women and men provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g., recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills) • # of women and men who receive psychosocial support & participating in parenting sessions • # of girls, boys, women and men with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services- • # of girls, boys, women and men supported with cash based Individual Protection Assistance as part of case management and referrals • # of unaccompanied and Separated girls and boys identified, documented, and receiving family tracing and reunification services (UASC) • # of girls, boys, women and men reached through awareness-raising activities • # of women and men trained on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including Case management and PSS . # of men and women (community volunteers and staffs) trained on CPiE.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

ADA is a member of Cash Working Group in South Sudan and beside that, ADA has been supporting the vulnerable communities of Ayod, Fangak and Nasir Counties with Cash based assistance supported by other different donors such us Christian Aid. ADA has done market assessment in the targeted locations and ADA has a staff trained and will be able to carry out the work successfully. The cash provided will be unconditional cash transfer to the beneficiaries through known vendors (commercial banks) that ADA has been successfully using in other cash-based interventions. The vendor will provide cash to identified beneficiaries for unrestricted household, mostly domestic needs like food, clothing, soap, salt, sugar, etc. These items are readily available in the local markets in Ayod, Fangak, and Nasir. In Ayod and Fangak, there are functional markets and shops, with goods being supplied from mostly Juba (but on a small scale, from Sudan) through flights and boats. In Nasir traders obtain their goods supply from Ethiopia. In all targeted areas, the basic items expected to be bought by the beneficiaries are available in functional markets, with reliable supply roots, and reasonably acceptable market prices based on the context of each location.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

ADA will provide cash-based assistance to the most vulnerable UASC cases who are unable to support themselves. This case-based assistance will also be provided to some traumatized adults caregivers who are facing serious economic challenges to at least manage to get some of their basic need through the cash assistance. This is in line with case management support. This cash assistance will help the most vulnerable beneficiaries to have a chance to access multiple needs that they are not able to access. Due to the flooding, communities lost their belongings e.g., food items cultivated, livestock, and other food items for a living. The unconditional cash transfer will provide ample choice to the vulnerable persons to choose whatever they would prioritize to alleviate suffering meted by the floods.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ADA through project front line staff and community volunteers will provide awareness and sensitization in the community on Protection against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA) . ADA has staff trained on PSEA that will train and guide the front-line staff while carrying out the activities. A strong referral pathway will be built where the survivors of SEA will be referred to the concerned actors in line with cluster data management policies.ADA Juba headquarter will assist its field offices to mainstream inter-agency complaints mechanisms at community level, strengthen communication with communities, support survivors to access services through referral pathways, and related activities. The project will institutionalize PSEA minimum operating standards, and strengthen linkages to IASC thematic areas of intervention. Besides, the project will strengthen accountability to affected people (AAP) through ensuring its employees are trained and signs the PSEA code of conduct, and apply accountability and inclusion standards, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psycho-social support. ADA shall establish coordination and support functions for PSEA and sexual harassment with field level protection and child-protection sub-clusters and implementing partners. The project aims at conducting an awareness raising and sensitization on the forms of sexual abuse and exploitation to community project committees, government authorities and opinion leaders in the affected communities. The project will address stigma and cultural barriers hampering reporting of PSE or SH cases through awareness raising and also provide information on who to report to and how to report preparators. ADA will map out PSEA partners and establish safe referral pathways to services providers. Finally, ADA will ensure zero tolerance policy with regard to sexual exploitation and abuse throughout its projects as being member of the protection and child-protection sub-clusters. It is considered serious misconduct and can have severe consequences, including staff dismissal and taking legal steps against perpetrators. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiary or female employee will be reported immediately to the relevant authorities for action.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADA will setup feedback or complain mechanism including suggestion boxes that will enable beneficiaries to address their concerns on the project implementation.The front-line staff will be reaching out to the beneficiaries who are unable to reach to the ADA field offices or complaint desks as part of their daily outreach activities. This will help those with disabilities and others who cannot easily access the feedback and complaint desk to express their feedback on the services provided by ADA in the community. The outreach staff will fill for them and what they recommend to be done to at least satisfy their basic needs. A good practice that ADA has used in the past is to ensure that people with disabilities, different vulnerabilities, girls, elderly etc. are represented in the monitoring teams and specifically gather info from their own category. Special desks, lines, suggestion boxes that suit their needs are provided. Oftentimes, special people who can translate or have special ways of rapport with the various categories are identified to be representatives in a feedback loop on project matters (including complaints). These methods will be employed to ensure the project feedback mechanism is representative and inclusive of the various categories of beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ADA will use Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) tool to ensure the community have multiple ways to share complaints and information in respect to this project. ADA will use an inclusive system of interacting with communities and promote minimum standard for all mechanisms for regular tracking, reviewing and action feedback for the project. A complaints desk during project implementation will be installed in ADA field offices and Child Friendly Spaces to allow complains and feedback from the community. All cases will be recorded and addressed accordingly. Confidentiality will be assured across all the processes. Before the starting of the projects, ADA always does an initial needs assessment and engages the beneficiaries on how they can receive an assistance. ADA always provides information to beneficiaries before the implementation of projects. ADA will conduct induction meetings with the local authorities and the community members informing them on the project activities. Complaints mechanism will be provided and complains will be managed with care, dignity, confidentiality and respect while reporting to complaints desk. The beneficiaries will be encouraged to raise any complaint they like to raise. During the project implementation ADA will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, or address concerns and complaints. ADA has a robust M&E team to timely support the project team on all M&E related tasks, including the complaints raised by beneficiaries ADA will use its existing pre- and post- project implementation assessment tools/cluster tools to assess the challenges and further needs before, during, and after project implementation. ADA has staff trained on PSEA. A strong referral pathway will be built where the survivors of SEA will be referred to the concerned actors in line with cluster data management policies

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ADA has a policy on, and extensively uses feedback mechanisms in project implementation. This provides for a complete loop of communication between the field, programs and management. In essence, ADA employs four key steps in providing beneficiary feedback: design, implement, collect data and analyse; provide feedback through an agreed feedback loop. In this project: 1. ADA will use available procedures in previous projects to adapt to the feedback needs of the current project, by involving beneficiaries on how best they would like their feedbacks to be recorded, analysed, and handled in a manner that is satisfactory to them. This will be area-specific and handled with utmost confidentiality and practicality. The modalities used by ADA include suggestion boxes, phone calls, use of SMS, face-to-face meetings, focus group discussions, project implementation committee appointees, complaints and complements forms, and questionnaires. Beneficiaries make their choice and this informs the course of action. 2. At the implementation level, ADA will agree with the beneficiaries on how to participate in collecting/gather complaints and complements realistically and in a timely, realistic manner, meeting the needs of beneficiaries in specific locations (Ayod, Fangak, and Nasir) 3. ADA will agree with beneficiaries on how data collected (complaints and compliments) will be analysed, by whom and at what frequency to provide needed feedback. This stage includes concrete action points on matters raised and possible redress or way forward 4. ADA will agree in advance on how the actions generated can be shared internally or externally to create the needed change or course of action that was desired by beneficiaries. At this stage, the communities are informed of necessary changes, by when and how. ADA will use the beneficiary feedback to demonstrate its respect and promotion of human rights, foster good cooperation with affected communities, empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. Also, the beneficiaries’ feedback shall help to strengthen project ownership amongst partners, improving the chance of long-term impact. The action points will provide feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, strengthening the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for remedial action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Positive feedback motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct in beneficiary communities. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold ADA and other decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

ADA has staff trained on PSEA that will be able to capacity build other staff on how they can support the community through awareness and sensitization on prevention of PSEA in the community. Case workers and other CP staff will create more awareness on Child Protection activities and how the community should access services. ADA together with other partners in the targeted locations will sit and establish strong referral pathway systems to help the community have a clear direction on how to access services from the right partners. ADA is already having complaints and feedback mechanism system/policy in place. The system will be applied during the implementation of this project to ensure that feedbacks are received from the communities. A complaints desk will be installed at ADA PSS centres and ADA field offices to allow the community easily access them. ADA Child Protection staff are trained on data confidentiality and therefore, this will be implemented as required by the data management system. All the data and information on children and other supported adults will be kept confidential. During identification of vulnerable children, the case workers and other concerns staff plus the community volunteers will be trained on CPiE to be able to identify right people in need. This will include training on how to identify UASC and other children having high vulnerability. In summary most of the mitigation measures are dependent on ADA’s thoroughness in ensuring very good training of staff on handling people with vulnerabilities in a manner that is dignifying and satisfactory; meeting the objectives of the intervention while reducing possible risks associated with disregard of rules and procedures.
