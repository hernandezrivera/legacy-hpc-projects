Project Summary

Education is not only a powerful driver of development, and one of the strongest instruments for reducing poverty and improving peace and stability, but it can also be a key to ending conflicts. Rebuilding education systems during and after crisis envisaged by the project is crucial in conflict and fragile situations because it will help provide security, facilitate peacebuilding, and foster resilience.
Oxfam’s active presence in Lakes State (Rumbek Centre, Rumbek East, Yirol East, Yiror West, Awerial), Jonglei State (Akobo, GPAA-Pibor), Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North) has enabled to establish relationship with local authorities, School management committees, and PTA’s therefore making the task of responding to the needs of pre-primary, primary schools, ALP/FAL centers effective.
OXFAM has had extensive history of responding to pressing educational needs and has been an active HRP partner in South Sudan for several years. The need for education in Lakes, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria states is particularly urgent with an estimated 16,500 children and youths and 210 teachers in need of support. Oxfam will target 12,500 children to improve school enrolment and retention, this outcome will be reached through training teachers, PTAs, SMCs, community leaders, local education officials , and MoGEI staff in quality education delivery, improve school WASH infrastructure and practices, organize COVID-19 awareness campaigns as well as an introduction and integration of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). The proposed response plan will be implemented in the timeframe of 12 Months.

1 - Needs

South Sudan currently has an education crisis with 3.3 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 out of school, this has been partly caused by recurrent localized conflicts, increasing climate change, food insecurity, inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel and irregular and insufficient teacher payments.Quality of education services in Lakes State (Rumbek Centre, Rumbek East, Yirol East, Yiror West, Awerial), Jonglei State (Akobo, GPAA-Pibor), Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North) are categorized under priority 1-2 characterized by lack of qualified teachers with competences in gender and conflict sensitive education, limited access to learning and recreational materials. In these locations Less than half of learners were unable to access the national radio distance learning programs during the COVID-19 school closures resulting into significant learning loss (Cluster education Need assessment 2021). Across South Sudan, some schools re-closed after opening in May 2021 as teachers had not been paid, resulting in further learning loss. Lack of and delayed teacher salaries and incentives result in teacher absenteeism, apathy, high turnover, and poor-quality teaching . Teachers’ salaries in South Sudan have not been adjusted for the high inflation the country is suffering; salaries that were around 100 USD per month in 2011 due to inflation and devaluation of the local currency have now reduced to $ 5 in 2021 . In the ENA, lack of or delayed salaries was the main reason teachers were absent on the day of the assessment. Girls and young Women face protection issues and have limited opportunities to participate in decision making and economic activities. The national ratio of qualified teachers to pupils is 1:1,171 (Education Cluster, 2018). Some learning and recreational materials are available in the market in Rumbek, Yirol East, Awerial and Kapoeta Town. However, extremely low purchasing power and the high cost of transport severely limits access. For girls, early and forced marriage and pregnancy were widely reported in the ENA as reasons for drop-out. During school closures, evidence suggests daughters were married to alleviate household economic stress through the “bride price” . Children with Disabilities (CwDs) also face numerous challenges in accessing quality education. In the ENA, most schools reported having no mobility or assistive devices. Over half of schools also report their latrines are inaccessible for CwDs. Teachers are not trained to foster or deliver inclusive education and school communities are not sensitized on the need to create enabling environments for CwDs. Finally, a culture of violence and survival has caused trauma in populations, and children have been the worst hit victims of the conflict. Insecurity is a significant challenge to school functionality, in Jonglei more so in Pibor, Akobo and then in Lakes, active conflict and regular communal violence throughout 2021 led to school closures. Learners in the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021 . School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding. Jonglei and Lakes (Awerial) have been the worst impacted by the floods .

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Guided by the accountability framework of the country programme the project will have in-built feedback and/or complaint management system that is effective, accessible and safe for intended beneficiaries. Feedback and compliant collection mechanisms will be defined after discussion and agreement with people affected by the project/programme. Multiple and complementary feedback and complaints collection mechanisms will be considered to avail safe and convenient options to the community. We keep records of complaints and feedback regularly using the recording data base we have and follow up on the referral and response progress. views and requests of community members will be integrated into the programme cycle to ensure that affected people are involved in deciding on issues affecting their lives in relation to project deliverables. Feedback from beneficiaries that inform project activities or outputs will be documented for evidence and learning.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Participation of minority female and male beneficiaries including those with disabilities, illiterate among others will be ensured through various mechanisms including regular gender assessments, monthly feedback sessions via sex specific FGDs, surveys and assessments, community consultative sessions, verification exercises and others.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

As part of empowering the targeted community, we consider strong community awareness on Sexual exploitation and abuse and jointly set culturally sensitive and socially acceptable reporting mechanisms for sexual abuse, harassment and exploitation. As we have safeguarding focal points in each project location we encourage community members to report on staff behaviour to these focal persons using the most preferred and safe mechanism already set up by Oxfam and Oxfam will make sure appropriate action is taken on time after investigation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Oxfam, as an organization strives to be accountable to its beneficiaries, we always provide relevant and clear project information to all stakeholders including implementing partners, local authorities and beneficiaries. Information sharing is not a onetime activity and does not follow a one-way approach. Affected communities will are always given feedback on all relevant project related information to encourage their involvement and to accelerate informed decision making and choices. inception meetings focus group discussions, gatherings during food, cash and other scholastic material distributions will be used to share information with the community. PTAs/SMCs will also be capacitated to share relevant information in local languages, formats and media that can be easily understood by local community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Oxfam will consult the targeted communities on the appropriate locations/sites and time for distributions. Men, women, and children will be consulted on this. The project will ensure a safe, accessible and convenient locations which will create no further harm and reduce existing vulnerabilities and risks, while the elements of safe programming will be kept at the core of each stage of the response ranging from assessment to evaluation. The community feedback during consultation will help to design and deliver a safe and accessible response, while also taking into account IASC and sphere standards: - Continuous Protection and safeguarding risk analysis will be done to monitor how the project considers safety of beneficiaries.- Establishment of information and complaint desks at distribution points to enable beneficiaries report violence and concerns for appropriate action.- Participatory identification of community-based feedback and reporting mechanisms to enable the community to identify trusted focal points for safeguarding, program, and protection reports.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

This project will address protection issues through provision of learning opportunities to all the affected children, both boys and girls enrolled in schools as part of their rights to education. Also, training of teachers, community leaders and other groups on Disaster Risks Reduction and life-skills will reduce the vulnerabilities of the affected populations. The project will promote a culture of peace, through support for peace education programmes and other non-violent approaches to conflict prevention and resolution. In addition, the project will put some particular attention and adequate resources to the rehabilitation of children affected by armed conflict through counselling, trauma healing, and education, as a preventive measure and as a means of reintegrating them into society. Parents or other caregivers will be encouraged to provide good and consistent support and guidance to the affected children and also create support from friendship/community networks and teachers to the re-establishment of a normal pattern of daily life. Teachers will be encouraged to provide an educational climate which is emotionally positive, open and supportive; and appropriate role models which encourage constructive coping mechanism. Oxfam has a mandate to serve the vulnerable people around the world to fight inequality to end poverty and injustice. In doing this, it strongly incorporates protection issues into the design, implementation, and evaluation of assistance programs whenever possible and appropriate. This is done in order to assist host, IDPs and other vulnerable populations to reduce or manage risks from violence, abuse, harassment, and exploitation.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will put some particular attention and adequate resources to the engagement of the existing community structures, strengthened DRR groups and plans, timely prepositioning of supplies/resources such as plastic bags, protective materials/kits for easy access of services to all the affected children, both boys and girls enrolled in schools as part of their rights to education. Hence enhanced training of teachers, community leaders and other groups on Disaster Risks Reduction and life-skills which will reduce the vulnerabilities of the vulnerable populations.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project has been designed to contribute directly to South Sudan’s 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and Education Cluster objectives: Education activities led by OXFAM will be integrated into the ongoing GFD/BSFP and FFA activities in the targeted locations, this will ensure that learners have access to food at household level and a livelihood as a source of income. The project will work with community-based structures such as community protection committees, local authorities and chiefs and clusters such FSL, CWG and protection clusters, GBV to ensure coordinated delivery of humanitarian assistance targeting host, internally displaced and returnee population. The project will provide livelihood kits and immediate cash assistance to meet immediate basic needs including food and livelihoods needs for transitional resilience
