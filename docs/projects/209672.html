Project Summary

The deteriorating socio-economic, political and security situation witnessed in Sudan since 15 April 2023 has resulted in unprecedented levels of humanitarian needs, including those of refugees. In response, UNHCR aims to support refugees, asylum-seekers, and other persons of concern across Sudan in several different sectors including general protection, child protection, GBV, education, health and nutrition, shelter/NFI, energy and environment, food security and livelihoods, as well as WASH. UNHCR will work with a wide range of implementing partners, including government ministries and agencies, UN agencies, national NGOs and international NGOs.
UNHCR’s interventions are aimed at realizing rights-based and equal access to services and sustainable solutions for PoC to ensure they have an improved quality of life and security regardless of their nationality or where they live. Through its interventions UNHCR will continue to maintain focus on coordination and collaboration with other entities to deliver quality, AGD sensitive and evidence-based programming informed by monitoring and needs assessments, aimed at addressing priority needs, mitigating protection risks and reducing vulnerabilities. UNHCR will also strengthen efforts to achieve the inclusion of PoCs in national systems and development programs through advocacy, capacity building of national institutions, coordination, inclusive partnerships, and joint and/or complementary programming.
Further, UNHCR’s interventions aim to improve the protection environment for refugees and asylum-seekers in key areas that contribute to an environment where duty bearers are empowered to effectively fulfil their obligations and, refugees, asylum seekers and stateless persons enjoy their rights without discrimination. Sustaining a strong protection environment with effective responses to protection risks, including through empowerment of communities to mitigate protection risks, can strengthen their resilience and engagement in peaceful coexistence with host populations.
GoS capacity to effectively conduct continuous registration and documentation of refugees, including the provision of refugee identity cards will continue to be supported. UNHCR support to the GoS to strengthen civil documentation and legal identity for PoCs will be enhanced to prevent and reduce the risks of statelessness. In furtherance of the global CBI policy, UNHCR will continue to explore and expand the use of CBI as a programmatic response modality for delivering assistance to populations of concern.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNHCR aims to strengthen protection, gender mainstreaming and accountability toaffected populations as they link with the principle of “do no harm”. The particularneeds of such individuals will be reflected in NFI assessments, programme design,implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensurethe following elements are taken into account: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2)equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms,with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring that willfeed into the sector’s program cycle; and (4) sharing information, consultation,participation and community empowerment. The sector will work with partners tostrengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abusethrough awareness-raising for both partners and suppliers. UNHCR within its capacityas Shelter/NFI Sector lead will advocate to draw attention to shelter issues such ashousing, land and property rights, particularly for returnees and/or integrated IDPs.HLP due diligence tools will be set in place with cooperation of the HLP WG toharmonize the shelter responses among partners ensuring that before buildingdurable shelter partners will ensure in cooperation with the communities thatbeneficiaries’ land if free of claim and refer those conflictive cases to protectionpartners providing ICLA support through a referral pathway. This is to reduce the riskof further harm and mainstream protection on the shelter construction programs.
