Project Summary

The project will directly support the FSS objective 2 through enabling HHs suffering from limited of access to food and income opportunities to meet their basic food needs. The project will target vulnerable communities in the eastern and northern areas of the Gaza strip through provision of food e-vouchers for -4000 HHs. The most vulnerable HH will be selected based on poverty assessment results and Food Consumption Scoring. The selected HHs will receive food E-voucher for 6 months. Applying a cash-based modality will empower the food insecure through enhancing their access to sufficient food intake.

1 - Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade, the deteriorating economic situation and the recurrent conﬂict events have resulted in high rates of poverty (53%), highest ever-recorded unemployment rate (46.7 %) and Youth (19- 29 years) and female unemployment is even higher at 70% and 78% respectively (According World Bank). World Bank monitoring report (April 2019) revealed that Gaza’s economy continued to be in a deep recession during 2018; it contracted by 7 percent year-on-year due to decline of spending through PA budget and donor aid. According to FSS-HNO- 2020, more than 32,000 households have their livelihoods affected due to the increasing number of injured persons involved in Great March of Return (GMR). The families of the injured cannot afford the increasing cost of medicines, treatment and other health services which placed additional financial burden on the already poor families.The ﬁndings of the Household Expenditure and Consumption Survey, released by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) in May 2018, show a signiﬁcant increase in poverty rates in the Gaza Strip: from 38.8 per cent in 2011 (the previous time poverty was measured) to 53 per cent by the end of 2017, which is the equivalent to around 1.01 million people, including over 400,000 children. As part of SCI emergency intervention, SCI has conducted a socio-economic assessment in North Gaza and eastern Khan Younis for 1,995 poor households (HHs) farmers. The assessment has shown that the average household income has fallen to NIS 618 per month in North Gaza and only NIS 495 per month in Eastern Khan Younis. Food insecure families spend approximately half of their income on food. Other essentials are deprioritized, and a further deterioration of food security and livelihood status evolves, especially in female-headed households. The public sector salary crisis has affected a great volume of households further weakening household income stability and purchasing power which in turn has affected the local market and small businesses such as grocery owners, farmers, drivers, among others. According to UNRWA ﬁgures, approximately 80% of the population in Gaza is dependent on external assistance, primarily food assistance.The prevailing poverty, poor access to livelihood opportunities and basic services is increasing health risks and level of malnutrition. The results of multi-sectoral assessment conducted by SCI- WFP-UNICEF in 2018 showed that the poor Food Consumption scoring is worsening (23%) compared to national results in Gaza Strip (SEFSec 2016) where the poor FCS was (7%). The results also showed that 71% of surveyed HHs are reducing the number and quality of meals to cope with the crisis.

2 - Activities

The intervention will be integrated with SCI-livelihood and child protection current interventions. Beneficiary selection will prioritize vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, and dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, injured or disability, malnourished children and children suffering from multiple child protection concerns and those receiving case management services. SCI will use the data from SCI protection interventions to identify the target communities’ protection concerns and children suffering from multiple child protection concerns. SCI protection team will ensure applying protection minimum standard during application. SCI will coordinate with the existing Community Protection Committees and CBOs to ensure community active participation and acceptance of the intervention modality. The intervention will target 4,000 vulnerable HHs (approx. 22,800 people) with distribution of e-vouchers. SCI will implement the project based on the initial SMEB set at US$190 per month ($60 for basic food and 130$ for fresh fruit, fresh vegetables, fresh chicken and fresh beef meat) for an avg. family size of 5.7. SCI`s in-depth research conducted from Jun to Sept 2018 in close coordination with the in-country FS sector (incl. presentation of results to sector members) has found that the required amount per HH of average HH size to adequately meet their food needs corresponds to the above target amount. HHs that are not being served by any actors will receive the full voucher value of US$190. HHs that are already being provided with assistance by others (in the value of US$60) will receive the supplementary support (in the value of US$130). This methodology has been designed to ensure HHs` food security needs are met to the extent that the risks of malnutrition and insufficient level of food intake are as much mitigated as possible. The E-voucher value is expected to cover 100% of HHs` food needs factoring in coping strategy practices that they may use to complete their needs such as but not limited to: rely on less preferred and less expensive foods; borrowings and assistance from relatives or host families; and use of available savings. Food requirement has been calculated based on the FS sector standard of 1,800 kcal/person/day for 30 days. Food items will be based on the list of food items in the basket with the addition of fresh fruits and vegetables, fresh meat and eggs. Specic project activities will include: 1 - Rapid assessment of needs, gaps, and market functionality 2 - Kick off and preparation of partners 3 - Communication with stakeholders 4 - Conduct of poverty assessment to identify and select beneciaries through the developing beneficiaries’ selection criteria and beneficiary application in coordination with FSS and Cash working group. SC will work with the Palestinian Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) in Ramallah to nominate the potential beneficiaries from target localities. The nominated beneficiaries will fill the beneficiaries applications and SC outreach workers will conduct verification home visits. SC and partners will screen the application based on agreed criteria .SC will share the results with stakeholders FSS and Cash working group to double check with other stakeholders like WFP and UNRWA. 5-Distribution preparation (contracting with merchants and Palpay financial service provider) 6 -Orientation to beneciaries on the use of e-vouchers 7 - Distribution of E Vouchers and redemption 8 - Monitoring of e-voucher use (Post-DM) To increase the impact of the project, SCI with implementing partners will conduct 200 sensitization sessions for target HHs and key community members to explain the planned intervention and discuss the pre-selected food and non-food items. The project steering committee will include local partners and key community members, and it will conduct regular meetings to manage potential challenges and social tensions during implementation.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

SCI will ensure “Do-no-harm” through our project intervention in terms of mitigation of potential social tensions in communities resulting from community members feeling excluded. Intervention will be based on needs assessments and transparent selection criteria. Appropriate targeting of beneficiaries will be the result of quality assessment of design and planning processes, coordinated through regular meetings with the partners and communities during the course of the implementation.Through sensitization sessions with beneficiaries and non -beneficiaries, SCI will introduce SCI complain mechanism and orientation to beneficiaries to ensure best use of services. SCI will ensure that the selected selling points are safe and close to beneficiaries homes to reduce the cost of transportation. SC will provide orientation on SCI Safeguarding Policy to the partners, beneficiaries and vendors. The Safeguarding policy will be included in all agreement with vendors to ensure their commitment. SCI nutrition advisor will check the list of allowed food items to ensure the safety suitability of items for children and women in target families.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

• To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks.
