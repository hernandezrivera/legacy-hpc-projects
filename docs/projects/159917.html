Project Summary

This project seeks to build on gains made through programming in 2015–2019, meeting the needs of approximately 111,180 conflict and famine-affected individuals (53,366 males and 57,814 females) in South Sudan through an integrated Nutrition, Agriculture and Food Security, and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) response.
The areas of South Sudan targeted for this project include Mayendit, Abiemnhom and Mayom Counties. To increase adequate access to clean water, hand pumps will be rehabilitated, a manual drill will be utilized to drill additional wells, and existing water points will be upgraded to solarized systems to enhance water yields for increased beneficiary coverage. In addition, water-management committees (WMCs) will be formed and hand pump mechanics will be trained in a bid to foster community ownership and sustainability of the water infrastructures. With regard to efforts to improve health and hygiene practices, communal and HH latrines, and gender-segregated bathing shelters will be constructed, and trainings will be conducted on better health and hygiene practices.

1 - Needs

An estimated 5.7 million South Sudanese will need WASH services in 2019. In partnership with USAID/OFDA, SP has been providing WASH services in Mayendit, Abiemnhom and Mayom since 2014. To address the urgent need for access to clean water, wells have been drilled, hand pumps have been installed and repaired, and committees have been formed and trained on water management as well as health and hygiene promotion. With regard to sanitation and hygiene activities, latrines and bathing shelters have been built, hygiene kits have been distributed, and waste disposal pits have been dug. Results from the 2018 baseline study conducted by SP indicated that 76% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 91% in Mayendit collected water from improved water sources such as tap stands, hand pumps and rainwater collection. However, 20% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 35% in Mayendit, had to walk for more than 30 minutes to access a clean and safe water source. Consequently, only 35% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 23% of HHs in Mayendit met the Sphere standard for water consumption of 15 liters per person per day. Per the baseline report, the average HH water consumption was 8.7 liters per person per day in Abiemnhom and Mayom, while in Mayendit it was 10 liters per person per day. Beneficiaries reported lack of water storage containers, distant water sources and a long waiting time at the point of collection as the main contributing factors to low HH water consumption. Additionally, only 27% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom reported treating their drinking water, while in Mayendit, 44% of the HHs treated their water to make it safe for drinking. Poor sanitation and hygiene practices also remain a huge challenge in these areas. The same survey report revealed only 35% and 20% latrine coverage in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and Mayendit, respectively. As a result, up to 54% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom and 60% in Mayendit practiced OD. In addition, coverage of solid waste disposal pits was at only 39% in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 28% in Mayendit. Furthermore, although 79% and 89% of HHs surveyed in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and Mayendit, respectively, had knowledge of the critical handwashing times, the majority did not have access to soap. Only 51% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom had access to soap while in Mayendit 23% of HHs reported using soap for handwashing, leaving many HHs at risk of hygiene related diseases. Therefore, the poor sanitation and hygiene practices pose a high risk of water source contamination with fecal matter and other solid wastes, thereby exposing the population to potential outbreaks of water-borne and hygiene related diseases such as cholera and diarrhea. Good WASH practices are an integral element in ensuring that the objectives of the program´s nutrition component are successfully met and sustained. Without access to adequate potable water, target beneficiaries remain exposed to life-threatening nutrition and health related consequences. The comprehensive WASH intervention will cover the water supply infrastructure, sanitation infrastructure, hygiene promotion, and environmental health sub-sectors. Samaritan’s Purse aims to serve communities that are affected by conflict, often displaced or in areas with high population density in Abiemnhom, Mayom, and Mayendit Counties. The settings in need of humanitarian aid have been determined based on Sphere guidelines; many are lacking or have limited water supply, lack proper and sufficient sanitation and hygiene facilities, and are not well informed on matters of public health concerns related to WASH behaviors.

2 - Activities

Under the sub-sector of Water Supply: •Rehabilitate 42 water points (12 in Abiemnhom, 20 in Mayom, 10 in Mayendit). Rehabilitation works for the 42 hand pumps will target areas with a high influx of IDPs or areas that have suffered damage to water supply infrastructure because of conflict. •Upgrade one hand pump to a solarized water yard, in Mayom County. •Extend the distribution network of this water yard to two villages for a distance of 500 meters, with a special focus on serving social institutions such as schools, the market and church. •Train 61 hand pump mechanics (12 in Abiemnhom, 26 in Mayom, and 23 in Mayendit) who will be responsible for the maintenance and repair of newly rehabilitated and/or drilled water points. Under the sub-sector of Sanitation: •Construct 31 communal/institutional latrines to improve hygiene practices and contribute to the prevention of disease.•Construction of an additional 320 shared HH latrines; 80 in Abiemnhom, 120 in Mayom, and 120 in Mayendit. These latrines will be constructed only in areas where feces are present near living areas and in areas where there is a high concentration of IDPs at high risk of public health hazards or an Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) outbreak.•40 bathing shelters will be constructed; 10 in Abiemnhom, 15 in Mayom and 15 in Mayendit, to serve populations in high-density locations. Under the sub-sector of Hygiene Promotion:•Samaritan’s Purse will use techniques from the PHAST approach to reach beneficiaries. Up to 63 HHCs previously established in Abiemnhom (17), Mayom (31), and Mayendit (15) will receive refresher trainings in order to enhance their capacity for effective hygiene promotion. •Once the HHCs are established, they will be responsible for conducting Hygiene Promotion Campaigns within their respective communities. Fifty-four (54) trainings will be held, with 14 trainings in Abiemnhom, 25 in Mayom, and 15 in Mayendit. The target for each campaign will be 500 individuals; overall, an estimated 31,500 beneficiaries will be reached. •Establishment of two Hygiene Clubs in the two schools targeted for latrine construction in Abiemnhom and Mayom. Under the sub-sector of WASH NFIs•distribute soap, hard-sided jerry cans and buckets Under the sub-sector of Environmental Health•Through the HHCs, SP will conduct trainings on proper solid waste management at both the HH and community levels. •Conduct 51 community-led debris clean-up campaigns to ensure appropriate waste management.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Beneficiaries will be targeted based on the severity of WASH needs and their vulnerability to WASH-related disease outbreaks. In targeting beneficiaries, SP will pay attention to beneficiaries residing in areas with a high concentration of IDPs and/or living in areas with infrastructure damaged during the previous crisis. Additionally, programming will be integrated in payams where Food Security and Nutrition programming is implemented.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Samaritan’s Purse is a member of the WASH Cluster, and engages in regular consultations with the national and state level Ministry of Water authorities, UN OCHA, and cluster partners to ensure that acceptable standards are met. In addition, SP participates actively in coordination meetings at country and state levels to ensure that efforts are not duplicated. In past programming, SP has closely coordinated with other actors in the region to ensure a comprehensive response. In Mayendit, Abiemnhom and Mayom, SP is currently the only WASH actor.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?
6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The security situation may or may not escalate to a level that will interrupt the implementation of programs in the field locations. In instances where the security situation does escalate, personnel will be relocated temporarily, with OFDA informed of these changes in the operating environment immediately. Personnel will return to the implementation area when the situation stabilizes, following a safety assessment and consultation with key community leaders, relevant implementing partners and the broader humanitarian community.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The program in the proposed areas is designed to be a temporary intervention and focuses on emergency response. However, there are several IDP host communities within Mayendit, Abiemnhom, and Mayom, who will remain within the counties once IDPs return to their homes. Samaritan’s Purse’s primary mechanism for transition is, therefore, to work with community leaders and local government to ensure local ownership of project activities and that water and sanitation infrastructures are managed well even after the project ends. The emergency WASH intervention is in line with the 2017 U.S. Government Global Water Strategy’s strategic objective 1, and the WASH Cluster´s strategic objective 1: to promote access to safe drinking water and sanitation services, and the adoption of key hygiene behaviors. It also covers all five complementary sub-sectors, per the USAID/OFDA proposal guidelines; namely, water-supply infrastructure, sanitation infrastructure, hygiene promotion, distribution of non-food items (NFIs), and environmental health. The overarching objective for the WASH intervention is to provide emergency clean and safe water, adequate sanitation facilities, and appropriate hygiene-promotion activities for vulnerable displaced populations, all of which will be completed in an environmentally healthy and respectful manner.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.1: Conduct spot-checks to ensure that vulnerable groups such as women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities have adequate access to the WASH services provided.8.2:The proposed project will ensure that women, girls, men and boys play an equal role in the design; management and monitoring of the WASH facilities in order to empower women and girls while improving WASH outcomes.
