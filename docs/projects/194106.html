Project Summary

This project aims to support 5480 vulnerable people, internally displaced persons, returnees and resident communities in Almalha, Kabkabia and Tawila North Darfur state to safe their lives following the ongoing clashes broke out between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the para-military Rapid Response Forces in Khartoum on April 15, 2023. The support will be through the provision of food assistance and livelihoods packages that will keep the populations alive until the conditions get better in the targeted location. The project targets of the affected households in three targeted localities, the total of project target is 5,480 beneficiaries in three targeted localities, including 3,288 (60%) women and girls who will be benefiting directly from the food and livelihoods interventions, this including total of 1,173 PWDs. FSL target 2,650 individuals including 1,590 women and 385 PWDs (40% were women and girl). Protection target 2,830 individuals including 1,698 women and 788 PWDs.

1 - Needs

The situation in Sudan has been significantly worsening starting from 15 April 2023 when a fighting between the SAF and the RSF broke out resulting in the killing of hundreds of people and the forced-displacement of more than 730,000 people inside the country and other 218,000 across borders. Food production, supply chain disruptions, fuel shortages, inflation and currency depreciation have led to shortages and price hikes, making essential goods unaffordable for a lot of people. Imported staples such as wheat flour, oil and tomato paste are scarce. WFP expects the number of acutely food insecure to increase to 19.1 million people in the next 3-6 months, if the conflict persists. North Darfur state is among many other states with the highest existing and and expected food insecurity levels in the country. Almost 2,7M people living in the state are in need of humanitarian assistance according to the revised HRP 2023 which prioritizes the saving of lives and mitigation of protection risks therein. Affected people living in Al Fasher, the capital city, have been witnessing a substantial deterioration in need severity across clusters (live in ‘extreme’ or ‘catastrophic’ humanitarian conditions).The estimated 700 HHs of IDPs are displaced from several locations in El Fasher town to locations across El Fasher town, and some were displaced to the rural locations outside El Fasher, within El Fasher locality. Both Kebkabiya and Tawila localities are hosting new IDPs due to the conflict and the former hosts 200 HHs while the later has 427 HHs all distributed in hotspot areas. Women represent 49% of these households members while men represent 51% and children among them represent 55%.The project target localities had already been among those with the highest prevalence of food insecurity (71,515 in Tawila and 51,619 persons in Kabkabia) are in severe need due to many factors including the semi-desert climate, droughts and low rainfall. All indicators suggest a deteriorating food security situation faced by both the IDPs and their host communities in the project localities which are known by their repeated intercommunal violent conflicts and increased insecurity.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project aims to providing an urgent response for displacement affected population including relocated IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Elfasher, Kabkabia and Tawila localities, North Darfur (9465 individuals in total). To reduce mortality and morbidity among its targets of 1327 households, the activities of the project will include the provision of life-saving and dietary food items package (i.e. rice, dried okra (Weekah), sorghum, cooking oil, onion, peans, sugar, tea, coffee, powdered milk) to 797 households of displaced persons in the targeted localities. These households will also be provided with non-food items (household supplies/sets) to ensure healthy cooking and intake of the provided food items and improved hygiene practices at the household level. Utilizing cash and market-based approaches, FPDO will provide 200 HHs representing host communities with multi-purpose cash assistance (with special focus on women and young girls/small farmers), in addition to the provision of agricultural support (seeds/tools) to 130 HHs. The project will further include the distribution of livestock (04 goats to 200 HHs) as support to vulnerable, women headed house hold and disabilities and other at risk groups in targeted locality. On the other hand, 2830 women and girls as well as all at risk groups will further be supported through appropriate protection activities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

FPDO will prioritize safety, dignity, and avoid doing harm by analyzing the local security environment including in relation to ownership patterns, recent history of looting or raiding, husbandry practices and the need to access livestock services or markets is necessary to identify high risk practices and activities. FPDO will strive to ensure that the environment in which assistance is provided is safe for all the people concerned and make every possible effort to ease access to the point of delivery and consider the vulnerability of beneficiaries beyond food insecurity and their available coping mechanisms and other capacities. In sum, FPDO will consider doing more good with regard to the following elements:- Market effects: assistance may affect prizes, wages and profits and these will be avoided by adopting the most appropriate procurement and staffing methods and procedures.- Distributional effects: assistance targeted some groups but not others; selection of beneficiaries will be in consultation with all stakeholders on the principle of transparency. - Substitution effects: assistance can substitute for local resources and reinforce the neglect of responsibilities by Political Leaders; complementarity and accountability of to the affected people of all actors will be affirmed during the inception period`s consultations with the local authorities/leaders. - Legitimization Effects: assistance supporting people or action that pursue war or peace legitimatizes them; conflict sensitivity will observed in all the interventions.Theft: goods provided can be stolen by warriors to support the war; targeted communities ownership will be increased to protect their gains from the project and strong coordination with the law-enforcement bodies will be in place.
