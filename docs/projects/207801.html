Project Summary

The Boko Haram insurgency that started in Nigeria spread quickly in the Far-North Region of Cameroun early in 2014 and continues to date, primarily impacting the districts in the west along the border with Nigeria (Logon Chari, Mayo Sava, and Mayo Tsanaga). In 2020 and 2021, intercommunal conflict broke out in the eastern part of the Region, along the border with Chad (Logon Birni and Mayo Danay, with displacement to the Diamare as well).
The protection situation in the Far North Region is a mixed situation, complex and marked by violence and rights violations against civilians. In addition, the region is recovering from the 2021 intercommunal conflict which resulted in additional displacements and unresolved tensions between pastoralists, farmers, and fishermen. Both conflicts resulted in increased gender-based violence, abductions of civilians, destruction and looting of property, illegal or arbitrary arrests, and limitations on freedom of movement. Furthermore, seasonal flooding regularly destroys property and livelihoods, reducing the resilience of the already vulnerable populations.
This led to continual displacement of people to and within the Far North, both internally and externally, including the arrival of over 100,000 refugees from Nigeria. According to the latest figures, more than 450,000 persons are internally displaced in the Far-North Region due to armed conflict and intercommunal conflict, while another 200,000 have returned to precarious situations in their communities and are at risk of being displaced again.
According to HNO analysis, nearly 425,000 of these displaced and returned persons need a well-designed and delivered protection response.
In 2024, through a community-based approach, UNHCR will secure protection and provide solutions through a comprehensive response in 4 key areas during the year: 1) strengthening the centrality of protection and protection monitoring, 2) coordination of the protection sector, long with the respective sub-sectors of GBV, Child Protection and HLP; 3) promoting and securing solutions, and 4) meeting urgent protection needs of the affected communities.
The project is fully aligned with the objectives and activities of the protection sector, and specific activities include reinforced and expanded protection monitoring, using the Projet21 methodology and joint analysis, building community engagement around solutions, and strengthening communities’ ability to respond to protection incidents. Interventions will focus on responding to critical GBV and child protection needs and support for civil documentation. In-kind support, cash-based interventions, legal assistance, and referrals to available services will be an integral part of the response.
The search for solutions will follow an area-based approach selecting specific areas and sites in the districts most affected by the displacement and working with all stakeholders in the displaced, host community, local authorities, civil society, and agencies working in the areas. The approach will ultimately contribute to building sustainability of key services and consequently prevent displacement, develop conditions favorable to return as well and guarantee greater accountability to affected populations (AAP).

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Activity 1: Strengthening the processes of issuance of civil documentation to conflict-affected people. In collaboration with civil documentation authorities and BUNEC, UNHCR will improve access of IDPS and others to civil documentation (identity cards, birth certificates, and other vital events) by working to simplify procedures and promote improved access of vulnerable populations to civil documentation. UNHCR will involve parents, children's clubs, youth organizations, and local protection committees in community sensitizations and awareness campaigns on birth registration and vital event documentation. Activity 2: Provision of targeted essential services and individual assistance, including cash. UNHCR will provide case management and targeted assistance to IDPs and returnees with specific needs, with particular attention to disabled IDPs, survivors of GBV, and children with protection needs. Assistance will be in-kind material support to persons with physical disabilities, counseling and legal support to survivors of GBV, and cash-based support to help GBV survivors regain agency over their lives. Protection activities will include individual case management for the most vulnerable to ensure referral to appropriate services, needs are adequately assessed, and appropriate support and services are provided. Activity 3: Conduct protection monitoring in crisis-affected areas.Protection monitoring and community engagement will identify and monitor protection risks, informing all stakeholders about the protection environment and supporting joint analysis and reporting on protection risks. UNHCR will use the framework of the Projet21 approach, harmonized in several countries in West Africa. This framework, by which aspects of protection monitoring are coherently designed, promotes joint and comparative analysis between organizations, regions, and countries. Some 200,000 IDPS and returnees will be covered by protection monitoring activities, including reporting on protection incidents, systematic gathering of data on the protection environment for trends monitoring, and referral of protection cases. The analysis will be done by a group within the Protection sector dedicated group to joint analysis, comprehensive coverage, and consistent reporting. Activity 4: Sensitization on peace-building, and peaceful coexistence between IDPs, returnees, and host communities. UNHCR will support displaced and return communities with social cohesion, peaceful coexistence, durable solutions, and community engagement. UNHCR will work with authorities and other partners to profile durable solutions opportunities, including assessing conditions, intentions, and needs. The analysis will serve to develop a plan of action for durable solutions for IDPs. UNHCR will strengthen the coordination of protection activities and increase the government’s participation and ownership to ensure sustainability and access to solutions. This includes HLP awareness raising with the community, authorities, and traditional leaders, to strengthen local customary and statutory authorities in meeting HLP-related challenges in the provision of durable solutions. Activity 5: Provide holistic support to survivors of gender-based violence. UNHCR will be focused on enhancing the protection of survivors and reducing GBV risks. Women will benefit from vocational training and financial support for startups as a part of the protection response. UNHCR will provide legal support to the GBV survivors. Activity 6: Identify and implement measures to prevent risks of violence, abuse, and exploitation of children in a humanitarian context.Activities will support parents and guardians through discussion groups, social support groups, formal and informal training, and sensitizations to develop positive parenting skills and strong parent-child relationships. Case management services will be given to unaccompanied and separated children by skilled social workers.
