Project Summary

SPEDP’s 2021 shelter and NFI response plan aims to improve the well-being of the vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in Morobo, Kajo Keji and Terekeka Counties of Central Equatoria and Fangak of Jonglei state through provision of life-saving emergence shelter and non-food items to protect the affected populations from weather conditions and disease-causing vectors including Covid-19 pandemic. SPEDP with its static presence in Yei (ware house Management), Morobo, Kajo keji, Terekeka and Old Fangak, coupled with its role of Co-state focal point in Central Equatoria and a new member of the shelter and NFI strategic advisory group (SAG) will conduct its response in a holistic manner through coordination and collaboration with other partners for joint response as well as integration with CCCM, WASH, health and protection clusters in the proposed project locations. SPEDP will collaborate and coordinate with the CCCM partners or Cluster for the provision of shelter, non-food items and CCCM assistance to new IDPs or returnees in the proposed project locations. Working together with the CCCM teams or Cluster will help SPEDP's staff to acquire skills and knowledge on camp coordination and camp management. SPEDP will also engage with local authorities and housing land and property (HLP) actors in resolving issues that may arise as a result of HLP. SPEDP will seek guidance from the Protection Cluster, leading the national HLP Technical Working Group (HLP TWG) to ensure comprehensive programming on HLP and shelter. Identification of persons in need will be accompanied by services such as legal assistance and case management, but also provision of shelter kits, cash for shelter, etc. training on HLP access rights and working with authorities for allocation of land for IDPs or returnees, dispute resolution, legal assistance and using KII to get feedbacks from the targeted beneficiaries.
The proposed intervention will reach a target of 30,000 vulnerable people (6960men, 7536women, 7697boys and 7807 girls) among internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities. 750 disabled people and people with special needs among the targeted people will also be provided with shelter and NFI assistance. The intervention will target 8,696 individuals with emergency shelter materials; 19,638 individuals for non-food items; 3,000 individuals for cash and voucher for essential emergency shelter upgrades and NFI replacements. The project will highly regard a consultative approach with local stakeholders during assessments, vulnerability criteria to the identification and selection of the project beneficiaries and monitoring and post distribution monitoring. The project will pay close attention to women headed households, child headed households, older people and people with disabilities incorporating gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs analysis and response recommendations. Households not targeted will be sensitized to capitalize on livelihood opportunities as well as use of local available materials such as poles, bambos and grass for construction of transitional shelters for the purpose of resilience.
Monitoring: SPEDP's M&E officer will ensure that there is tracking of the project set indicators in each stage of the project implementation to ensure that the project is meeting the target as from needs analysis, beneficiaries’ identification and registration, distribution and post distribution monitoring. During the ES/NFI distribution, monitoring and exit interviews will be conducted to assess the quality of the distribution process and feedback gathered from beneficiaries. During the distribution, different structures will be set in place to aid monitoring like complaints and feedback system will be established at each distribution center through help desks and mobile contacts. SPEDP will report to the cluster on weekly basis about the progress of the intervention.

1 - Needs

The years of conflict coupled with economic crisis, recurrent flooding and subnational violence continue to displace communities across South Sudan. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region (HNO 2021). Insecurity, lack of basic services, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevented people from returning home in large numbers. Although people started returning voluntarily in small numbers from the refugee camps in Uganda and DRC due to the signing and slow implementation of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (RTGoNU), there is still insecurity caused by communal violence and factions that are not signatory to the 2018 peace agreement. In Morobo County, the displaced or returnees are being accommodated in churches, villages and open places with no access to basic services like access to shelter, safe water, health, food etc. The Relief and Rehabilitation Commission in Morobo County stated that Kindi Boma and Kilikili Boma are the areas of high return. The internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in Morobo County are still in their dire need for humanitarian assistance despite limited shelter and NFI assistance from partners. Similarly, there is increasing unrest, displacement and return in Kajo Keji County. According to the ROSS secretary in Korijo, estimated 24,000 people were affected by activities of cattle herders and violence between SPLA IG and SPLA IO (Kajo Keji need assessment report 1st to 5th February 2021). Half of them settled at the Ugandan villages of Logutulenge, Klagy, Mijale and Matu. Areas of high return in Kajo Keji include Mere, Liwolo, Likamero and Kansuk. The Population in Morobo and Kajo Keji is increasing and shelters were vandalized and burnt, returnees share limited shelters with the host communities. An average of 5-6 people per a shelter including children. Some of the returnees are occupying houses belonging to some people who are still in the refugee camps. Meanwhile, the population in Terekeka County is mostly affected by subnational violence and flooding that caused massive displacement of people from their homes of origin and disrupted their livelihoods. In Fangak, riverine floods have had a devastating effect on the vulnerable communities. The flooding has disrupted people’s livelihoods hence creating alarming humanitarian situation. Local authorities estimated the number of affected and displaced households to be 7,357HH comprised of 44,472 individuals in the villages along River Phow (Inter-agency spot check report 30th January 2021). In addition to above dilemmas, an estimated 6.35 of the population of South Sudan are facing severe acute food insecurity (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification [IPC] Phase 3 or above) with a projected 7.2 million people (60%), to face severe acute food insecurity between May and July 2021, of whom over 2.5 million are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Covid-19 is also one of the disaster affecting people across the Country. It is against this background that SPEDP proposes to address this situation by providing in-kind emergency shelter and non-food items to 30,000 individuals in the targeted counties of Central Equatoria and Jonglei to protect them from weather elements and disease-causing vectors. A portion of this population will require robust shelter assistance in addition to the plastic sheeting provided. SPEDP will employ restricted conditional vouchers and restricted cash for the shelter upgrades.

2 - Project activities

1. Coordination: SPEDP will ensure effective coordination with shelter/NFI cluster both at national and state level to cover existing gaps and minimize overlaps in proposed project locations.2. Needs analysis: SPEDP will conduct need analysis in all the targeted project locations to ensure that the needs of the targeted beneficiaries are considered in the order of their priorities so as to enable the project respond appropriately3. Verification and registration: SPEDP will conduct verification and the registration of the targeted beneficiaries to receive emergence shelter and NFIs assistance. This will be followed by requisition of in-kind emergency shelter and non-food items from the core pipeline in the respective project locations. Logistic cluster will also be requested to transport the supplies to the proposed project locations. 4. Distribution: SPEDP will distribute emergency shelter materials to 10,566 individuals and non-food items to 19,434 individuals. This intervention will be coordinated with static partners for joint distribution. Due to Covid-19 pandemic, SPEDP will adhere to the Covid-19 standard operating procedures (SOP) in which distributions will be in swifts to avoid overcrowding and more members of the community will also be hired as crowd controllers to maintain social distancing; face masks and hand washing facilities will be provided at distribution points. Women and other vulnerable groups like the elderly, disabled and chronically ill will be prioritized to receive their items. Distribution points will be allocated in a safe place, close, and accessible to beneficiaries so that women do not walk long distances. Distribution arrangements (time, place, schedule, size and weight, etc.) will not discriminate against vulnerable or marginalized groups. SPEDP teams will establish help desks at shelter/NFI distribution points to allow for illiterate beneficiaries to provide verbal feedback. The field teams will regularly engage with community members (including beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, men and women, adults, disabled people and youth) to invite their honest feedback. In this approach, the beneficiaries are urged to lodge complaints/express grievances prior to and after distribution of the shelter and NFIs including on the general programming of this intervention.5. Cash Voucher Assistance: SPEDP will provide 3,000 individuals with conditional restricted vouchers and restricted cash for essential emergency shelter upgrades and NFI replacements. SPEDP will do a coordinated market assessment with partners in the targeted locations. The assessment will be conducted through Key informant interviews with a wide range of stakeholders such as households, private sector, government officials and other humanitarian actors and also conduct focus group discussions (FGD) with vendors, wholesalers, producers and households. The market assessment will focus on functional markets, the resources available and the risk that the intended response will harm the market. Data will be interpreted within its level of representation. Cash Voucher Assistance Programming will be based on availability of functional markets and availability of shelter and NFI materials in the market. Where there is availability of functional markets, SPEDP will conduct regular monitoring of prices and the volume of transactions shall be carried out in order to identify market distortions at an early stage. If monitoring reveals market distortions as a result of the intervention, SPEDP will reconsider the design of the programme.6. Monitoring and post distribution monitoring: Post distribution monitoring will be carried out two weeks’ after distribution of items where enumerators, Communities local leaders and RRC will be involved in the household sampling to measure the satisfaction of the beneficiaries on the assistance received and other complaints that will help the Project adapt for future programming.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

SPEDP noted that in all the targeted locations, there is at least a partially functioning market and project staffs jointly with Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) shall monitor markets and identify opportunities for a market-based response. SPEDP is also aware that even in the deep bush people come together at established or impromptu market places provided there is demand. In Kajo Keji, morobo and Terekeka Counties, most households buy food and non-food items from the functional markets which are also significant places where they can come together peacefully although some traders in markets generally have constraints to increasing inventory due to lack of financial capital, compounded by limited access to credit; customers’ low purchasing power; price variability dependent on seasonality; and external shocks, such as flooding and conflict, Inflation and the sharp depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound (SSP). SPEDP will promote the local traders to supply shelter materials and non-food items in market so that beneficiaries can have access to meet their priority needs.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

There are partial functional markets in the proposed project locations at the moment that encourage the use of cash or vouchers to permit the targeted people to timely acquire items of their choice. The modality will mostly target vulnerable returnees, IDPs and host communities preferably women headed households, child headed households, older people and people with disabilities to acquire shelter materials from the market to upgrade their shelters since most of the shelters in Kajo Keji and Morobo have been vandalized or burnt during the 2016 conflict that displaced many people to Uganda. Most importantly, many people in Kajo Keji and Morobo are returning on daily basis due to the calm security.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP staffs are aware, trained and adheres to the UN Secretary-General's Bulletin on Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13) The project staffs will strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising sessions around PSEA with women, girls, boys and men in communities of concern as well as with other stakeholders and design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities. Beneficiaries will be made aware of their rights and entitlements through community meetings, focus group meetings and information sheets distribution to beneficiaries. In particular, SPEDP will identify and support feedback channels that communities prefer while ensuring that feedback mechanisms are sensitive to age, gender and diversity (AGD) and accessible to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities and others at high risk. Besides, SPEDP will also coordinate complaint collection and responses with other partners within these proposed project locations and establish clear tasks and duties (who will do what, where, and how) since it is critical to put in place an effective interagency complaint mechanism that permits and encourages individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially, and safely.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will be set up and made functional at community levels. The CFRM will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. SPEDP team will establish help desks at shelter/NFI distribution points to allow for illiterate beneficiaries to provide verbal feedback. The field teams will regularly engage with community members (including beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, men and women, children, old people and disabled people) to invite their honest feedback. Suggestion boxes will be placed at distribution points and brought out during meetings. Depending on the severity of the problem/complaint, the team will hold a meeting with the village committees for participatory solutions. Feedback will be provided to the community on a biweekly or monthly basis, through community meetings, on how the complaints raised have been resolved or are being handled without mentioning the individual(s) who raised the complaints. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. The project will measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints of most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls). Access barriers could be at following level i.e. within IDP, refugee returnees and host communities and age and sex.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

A complaint and feedback mechanism will be established in both Juba and field offices in order to register complaints from community and give immediate feedback to the complainers if it is a case that can be handled at field level but if not, the case will be referred to Juba. The project will utilize Community Based Response Mechanism (CBRM) as a community management mechanism that delivers collaborative livelihoods and income generating opportunities as a means of building peace and stability. The CBRM will address issues that may arise in a credible manner. CBRM will also facilitate community-based dispute resolution process to receive feedback, deal with complaints or disputes. Additionally, complaints and feedback shall be gathered through focused group discussions (FGD) with the targeted beneficiaries (women, men, children, old people and disabled people). The target groups will be consulted to give their inputs how they prefer to channel their complains to SPEDP management. The project is expected to receive and proactively respond to issues with the potential to impact community relationships. It will increase transparency and accountability of local authorities for fair, inclusive and quality service delivery to the affected populations. The project will obtain informed consent from participants and ensure confidentiality for project activities that involve any probing into participants’ experiences in project activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Views and feedback will be collected from the community liaison teams (CLTs) from the start (design) of the intervention through key information interviews. The key informant interviews will be conducted among the local leaders and community leaders over the period. All stakeholders will be involved in all assessments including assessment among IDP, host community and other interest groups. SPEDP will approach the principle of participation from equity perspective and advocate for equal participation of different groups. Based on the cluster reporting system, the project will put in place and implement and monitoring and reporting system, progress, best practices and lessons learned will be presented in cluster meetings based on the 5Ws formats developed by the cluster.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. SPEDP will ensure that registration and distribution points are located close to the community and registration and distribution time is organized early enough to allow beneficiaries to reach home early. 8.2. Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance - SPEDP has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy which includes PSEA. Ongoing orientation for new and existing staff and affiliates will be completed. SPEDP adheres to a zero tolerance policy of SEA. 8.9. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area - In order to mitigate local disputes around the land ownership following clearance operations, SPEDP will liaise with local authorities, traditional and religious leaders even before the teams start operating. A handover certiﬁcate will be issued to local leaders after the land being cleared and a community meeting will be called to ensure transparency and accountability. Additionally, SPEDP will closely monitor the socio-economic impact of its work by utilizing its own methodologies and dedicated M&E mechanism to ensure that targeted local communities remain at their locations and use the cleared land for agricultural or hunting purposes in an eﬃcient way. This will help building their resilience and pave the way for self-suﬃciency at community and household levels. It will also reduce the level of vulnerability towards humanitarian aid.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

SPEDP will ensure that the project staff and management are issued with a set of minimum standards for protection, gender and inclusion guide in emergencies. The project management will ensure that service delivery to the beneficiaries are provided with dignity, access, participation and safety for all communities affected by disasters and crises. Clear and practical guidance on how to mainstream the principles of dignity, access, participation and safety will be given most attention to the gender; women, age, disability and other diversity factors. The guide will also provide the minimum requirements across sectors to support the gender, disability and elderly without discrimination, in any given background.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Provision of adequate shelter contributes to the health and well-being of displaced populations, while essential cooking and eating utensils and fuel for cooking enable utilization of food assistance to meet nutritional needs hence reduced global malnutrition. Provision of emergency shelter and NFIs will also reduce stress and shocks among displaced and returnees populations in which they will have time and energy to participate on their daily livelihoods including agriculture to feed and cloth their family members from income generating activities.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

SPEDP will provide equitable access to shelter and NFI materials through integrated approach with protection cluster, WASH, CCCH, FSL, and health. Women, children, elderly, disabled and people with special needs will be targeted. SPEDP will collaborate and coordinate with the CCCM partners or Cluster for the provision of shelter, non-food items and CCCM assistance to the conflict affected IDPs in the proposed project locations. Working together with the CCCM teams or Cluster will help SPEDP's staff to acquire skills and knowledge on camp coordination and camp management.
