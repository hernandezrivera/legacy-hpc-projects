Project Summary

This project aims to achieve sustainable improvement in access to quality water supply, sanitation condition, hygiene practices and distribution of essential life-saving NFI and emergency shelters among IDPs and host communities in East and South Darfur states as well Khartoum State, the project will focus on subsectors of water supply, sanitation, hygiene promotion, environmental health and NFI and emergency shelters. In order to improve the water supplies in targeted area of operation, innovative and environmentally friendly technologies will be used namely water provision through installation of hybrid powered solar pumping systems and construction of roofing hafir, monitoring water quality on daily and bi-weekly bases through chlorination and bacteria tests to ensure safe drinking water is provided. Community based operation and maintenance approach will be applied to ensure long lasting water service provision. Furthermore, WASH services and facilities will be given consideration COVID-19 mitigation measures ranging from increasing handwashing facilities in high risk public places and communicating culturally appropriate messages to foster behavior changes to cash-based interventions and in-kind distribution of hygiene supplies, ARC is strengthening and adapting WASH services in camps and host community settings, healthcare facilities, and schools.
ARC will continue to provide access to safe water in communities suffering from malnutrition above emergency thresholds for IDPs as well as the vulnerable host communities impacted by the presence of IDPs through context specific technologies such as surface water treatment systems and sustainable Point of Use water treatment solutions appropriate for a highly mobile population. ARC will provide means for safe excreta disposal through the installation of gender appropriate sanitation facilities and will conduct hygiene promotion activities to support effective behavior change. For ES/NFI intervention this project seeks to provide of timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance to people affected by crisis to reduce mortality and morbidity, mitigate health threats, and improve living conditions and support the transition to recovery by improving vulnerable people’s access to shelter solutions.

1 - Needs

According to humanitarian needs overview (HNO) 2021, there are 8 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan.1.8 million are IDPs, 1.1 million are refugees, 6.1 million are vulnerable residents, and 0.3 million are returnees. In South Darfur state, an estimated 1.2 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 1.2 million people are in need of life-saving emergency shelter and household items (ES/NFIs) in Sudan, out of those 1.5 million living in East, and Khartoum sates, while 65,908 people in needs for emergency shelters and NFIs living in east Darfur. This includes those affected by conflict, floods or other hazards; those using negative coping mechanisms. The following objectives will guide the WASH interventions Objective I: 1.9 million crisis-affected population will have access to basic water services. Under this objective, ARC interventions will ensure 466,416 as direct beneficiaries will be support with drinking water from an improved source with a collection time of not more than 30 minutes’ roundtrip, including queuing.Objective II: 1.7 million crisis-affected populations will have access to adequate (basic or limited), protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services. ARC interventions will ensure the sanitation conditions for the targeted beneficiaries (396,454 people) in the rural communities and IDPs camps well maintained and have improve access to sanitation services through rehabilitation, construction of latrines with community support as much as possible. Desludging and rehabilitation of PHCC latrines. Construction of VIP latrines at PHHC in addition to Sanitation pilot social marketing approach.Objective III: 4.7 million crisis-affected populations will have access to hygiene promotion services, water quality, and environmental health interventions. 443,095 people as target beneficiaries will receive hygiene promotion messages, focusing on handwashing with soap through household visits, campaigns, and soap and jerry cans as well handwashing facilities will be fixed in public places. Environmental health interventions, such as solid waste management and vector control to be implemented in risk-prone areas.Shelter and NFI needs have increased in 2020, as result of new displacement due to inter-tribal conflict occurred in West, South Darfur and eastern Sudan. The estimate of the number of IDPs 2.55 million in 2020. The following objectives will guide the ES/NFI interventions: Access to environmentally-friendly shelter and NFIs remain critical needs. people in need of shelter and NFIs to ensure their minimum living standards, dignity, and to contribute to their physical and mental wellbeing The need for adequate lighting in camps and settlement areas, such as solar lanterns is an important factor that supports the protection and physical safety of vulnerable people. The project targeted both IDPs and host community in all our operation areas. for IDPs distribution of cash for Shelter to protracted vulnerable households, replenishment of life-saving non-food items to vulnerable households will be our main activities. In host community whereby trainings on construction techniques to mitigate the impact of natural hazards will be conducted, sensitization of community member on best practices to mitigate the impact of natural hazards will be carried out, in addition training beneficiaries on shelter material construction as a source of income generating activity. 55,000 beneficiaries from IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community will be targeted by this intervention received ES /NFI items.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ALIGHT places protection at the center of its programming by undertaking efforts for lifesaving activities throughout the project cycle and consistently working with the affected communities to ensure all services are delivered based on needs without harming the target beneficiaries. ALIGHT has comprehensive and collective approach to mainstream protection in order to enhance the overall ability of our staff to analyze, prioritize and respond effectively to respond protection needs of the affected populations including Refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities. ALIGHT provides special consideration to making its services (Livelihood, WASH, Health and Nutrition services) accessible to everyone as well as persons with disabilities and people with specific needs and ensure their accessibility across the age categories and vulnerability scenarios, with special provisions for pregnant women/ new mothers and infants, older, women headed families, widow’s orphans and severely ill and people with disability. ALIGHT has a dedicated protection team to work with all thematic sectors to strengthen the capacity of staff in protection mainstreaming but not limited to child protection, older persons, and people with disability, GBV prevention and psychosocial support. The protection team will support project teams in adopting practical ideas for, protection of identified vulnerable people in all ALIGHT programs as well as continuously monitoring protection outputs in all sectors.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ALIGHT is focused on implementing systems and methods that reveals customer experience of organizational services so that it can align a response and re-establish trust between ALIGHT, refugees, IDPs and the host communities. Trust works in both directions and must be built on the foundation of honesty and mutual accountability. ALIGHT commitment to accountability to the affected population will focus on the following; - governance, transparency, feedback, participation, design, monitoring and evaluation. ALIGHT will work with community structures in increasing the role of the community participation in maintaining the services. In addition, through the established community feedback and response mechanism, ALIGHT will continue to facilitate community consultation meetings to ensure active community participation, transparency and handling community feedback. The community members and beneficiaries provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities provided by ALIGHT and other development partners in their communities in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible to improve accountability across ALIGHT operational areas. ALIGHT’s Feedback/Complaint Response Mechanism (F/CRM) provides a formal channel through which ALIGHT beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can register voices and complaints. The F/CRM is centrally operated through feedback forms and a toll-free hotline number from all mobile phone networks companies (Sudani, Zain and MTN). ALIGHT has developed awareness materials including banners and flyers placed at ALIGHT project service delivery points such as health centers, water points etc. with the hotline number and explanations on how the FCRM works as well as information about our accountability standards and practices to which customers should hold ALIGHT accountable. In addition, through the MEAL department, ALIGHT conducts regular customer interviews and post distribution monitoring using standard survey feedback forms. As per ALIGHT procedures, the analysis of feedback information from communities is fed directly to senior management for action. These mechanisms have proven to be an effective tool in providing beneficiaries and communities with a voice and ultimately improving ALIGHT operations and identifying misconduct within the organization. A well-structured FCRM also enhances lessons learned and good practices across our network. ALIGHT is a signature to the Joint Framework for Action on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.
