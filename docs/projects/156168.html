Project Summary

Shafak's health program aims to contribute in the provision of access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health services for people in need and conflict affected population in northern Syria. The proposed intervention will prioritize internally displaced people and other vulnerable populations especially (Women ,children and disabilities) in the targeted areas. The intervention will be implemented through:
1. Delivery of services per essential primary health care (PHC) Package that includes; Child health, including EPI, reproductive health, prevention and control of communicable diseases, prevention and treatment of non-communicable diseases, provision of essential medicines and medical supplies and equipment by supporting 3 primary health care facilities center in rural Aleppo.
2. The provision of emergency obstetric and newborn care (EMONC) services and management of SRH-related diseases to the specialized hospital for women and children which provide maternal and child health services to most vulnerable people in the rural of Idlib, Marretmasrin.
3. Provision of health services through Mobile clinic for reaching communities that have a problem of access to primary health-care services in the rural of Aleppo.
4- Supporting 2 dialysis centers 1 in Idlib and 1 in Aleppo governorate with running costs and dialysis kits. In addition to conduct capacity building and training for the medical staff of the two centers.
5- Distribution of needed dialysis kits, medications and consumables adequate for a lot of months by coordination with WHO,health directorate and the health clusters.
By achieving the above components, Shafak aims to contribute in enhancing the health status in the targeted areas.

Needs

According to The Essential Primary Health Care Package northern Syria, the population in Syria is more vulnerable than ever, particularly to disease outbreaks and malnutrition. Lack of basic utility services such as electricity, fuel, safe drinking water and basic sanitation services have increased the vulnerability to diarrheal diseases, typhoid, hepatitis A as well as other vaccine preventable diseases.According to the WHO (World Health Organization), an estimated 57 % of health facilities and 51% of public health centers are either only partially functional or closed. People with life-threatening chronic diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, kidney failure, asthma, epilepsy, cancer and cardiovascular illness are at an increased risk of dying or developing complications as access to lifesaving medications and care is becoming more difficult. A severe shortage in skilled-birth attendants, including obstetricians, means that there are major obstacles to providing care to an estimated 300 000 women who are pregnant and need targeted support. According to the latest Health Resources Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS) reports and updates from the health cluster and Aleppo health directorate there is not any health facility provide EMONC services in the proposed Area (Idlib,Maret Masrin). Renal failure:In the NWS around 840 people (Men,women,Boys,girls) were reported suffering from kidney failure and need hemodialysis therapy provided by 20 dialysis centers, receiving more than 8,000 dialysis sessions on monthly basis as life-saving interventions. The cost of hemodialysis sessions, including medication and consumables, are high and can’t be afforded and sustained by NGOs running the hospitals and specialized centers. Without an urgent financial support to cover the costs of hemodialysis therapy sessions one-third to half of the affected patients will be in serious risk to lose their lives during the winter months. Furthermore, 75% of the facilities will have a shortage of dialysis supplies by the end of 2018. As per the latest reports (bulletins) shared by OCHA, ongoing hostilities and constraints (referred to in previous section) on access and commercial movement are likely to have led to further disruption of the health services and shortages of medicines, food items and fuel. The Turkey Health Cluster has identified critical health gaps which are expected to further deteriorate, among which the reduction in dialysis care for renal patients. Access to health care remains poor, especially in UN-declared besieged and hard to reach areas due to a lack of functioning facilities; shortages of health staff, drugs and medical supplies; and persistent clashes between various armed groups. Referral systems for trauma, emergency obstetric care and surgical services are insufficient, while there remains limited capacity for mobile clinics to reach out to fluid temporary settlements and host communities. The maintenance of medical equipment is also a core challenge, compounded by restrictions related to international sanctions on Syria, which prevent partners from importing critical spare parts. Shafak has conducted a rapid needs assessment about the dialysis centers across Idleb and Aleppo governorate. The results showed that 21 centers are still functioning, 11 in Idleb, 8 in Aleppo and 1 Hama areas. These centers include 135dialysis devices ranged from 4 to 20 machine per center. A total of 830 renal failure beneficiaries are receiving dialysis services through these 21 facilities (8300 sessions on monthly basis). These facilities are supported from different INGOs/NGOs such as NORWAC, SAMS, WHO, MEDICAL RELIEF, and Shafak. Shafak has filled the gap and supported two of the unsupported centers (in Babilla and Saraqab) through HF 2nd Allocation that was assessed in last July. According to results of this needs assessment, it was observed that Hepatitis C disease (HCV) was seen among the reported renal-failure patients.

Implementation of activities

In accordance with Shafak’s cross-border strategy, we will be responsible for direct implementation in the target locations coordinating with and gaining approval from local authorities in Syria, building on their extensive network, and facilitating access. Local communities will be involved in Shafak’s capacity development efforts and suitable training. Shfak will be responsible for ensuring that quality and accountability standards are met, will provide required training to staff and ensure that contractual requirement are adhered to and met. Further we will be responsible for all program and financial reporting to the donor. Shafak will also continue to coordinate with relevant cluster and other coordination bodies in Turkey. This joint program will be conducted through three main phases as the following:Phase I:- Recruitment of needed staff for each project.- Procurement of needed equipment, devices, drugs, consumables, tools for training and tools for implementation. Phase II: Launch of: - Outpatient services, comprehensive reproductive health services and chronic disease treatment in all facilities.- Emergency surgery, emergency obstetric and new-born services in the hospital.- PHC services.- Coordinate with health partners to identify locations of mobile clinics.- Providing specialist care for renal failure patients. Phase III:- Reporting, data analysis, monitoring and evaluation.

Protection Risk Analysis

The project overall objective aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual where they need it the most. Throughout the project life cycle the principles of protection mainstreaming are incorporated. All activities are designed to avoiding causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity of targeted populations, insuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation through several, action such as:• The project is designed to meet the needs of the community especially women, boys and girls considering the context, tradition, and norms in the community• Location of facilities will be accessible and easy to reach for all people., also safe and away from any military location.• Community will be clearly and widely informed about the date, location and timing of the service provision, and their opinion will be highly considered. • Team staff will be trained to be skilled in delivering PFA, identifying CP&GBV cases and refer them to adequate services.• Insure that the location is suitable for women, girls, people with disabilities, children headed household, old people, and other affected categories in the community---outreach. • Distribution and providing services for specific categories mentioned above will be to the door. • Teams will be trained on PESA, Child safeguarding, and will sign the code of conducted related. • project team will set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need, provide feedback on the activities. accountability officer will be responsible for information provision, collecting and responding to feedback and will report directly to HQ to ensure transparency • Feedback will also be continuously gathered in line with standardized center management protocol and monitoring and evaluation procedures, and doing so shall help improve protection mainstreaming. Shafak has its own Safety and Security Plan that includes, among other measures, safe in cash for rapid evacuation needs, mapping of an alternative road to avoid main road blockage. In case of security constraints, activities will be suspended, field staff hibernated, and to be restarted as soon as situation allows it.- For Shafak Organization, life proceeds over materials, and therefore no staff should endanger their own lives, or the lives of others, whilst attempting to protect Shafak Organization property, equipment, financial resources, documents or infrastructure.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- The border crossing between Turkey and Syria remains open for humanitarian assistance and staff- Target areas districts remain free of armed conflict- The situation is stable and IDPs movement fit with project objective. Risks:- Intensification of the conflict resulting drastic changes of the context, including further population movements into or out of the area- Theft of project equipment by warring parties following the change of front lines in the project area- An exodus of professional staff
