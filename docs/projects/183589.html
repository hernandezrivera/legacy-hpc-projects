Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect the livelihoods, improve the resilience,
productive capacity and enhance the food security of vulnerable farmers and herders who are living in Area C in north of West Bank and in Jordan valley.
The objective is drawn from the FSS country Strategic Objective: SO1 The resilience of
vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced.
Food security in Palestine continues to be affected by protracted nature of the crisis,
repeated shocks and continued restriction of freedom of movement, mainly for the most vulnerable families under the national social safety net (SSN), in term and type of
frequency assistance.
The proposed project goes in line with the first impact/ result “Agricultural assets and
productive capacities of farms and damaged productive assets (wells, irrigation systems, cisterns, greenhouses) restored, and the clusters plans of the ministry of agriculture in Area C in north of West Bank and in Jordan valley especially in the Jenin, Nablus, Tubas and Jericho governorates. Where more than 10,324 living in 41 communities will be
directly supported through the project’ activities.
The main activities will focus on rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and
inputs, for farmers and herders including promotion of technical capacities and small
scale production units, and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased
resilience.
To strengthen its accountability toward beneficiaries and partners, CARE applies and has an easily accessible, community-based Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM), which also attends to more sensitive, SEA-related complaints. CARE has a global policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection explicitly outlining unacceptable behavior, and investigation processes, and follow up, as well as the Safeguarding Code of Conduct. All project’s team will receive an orientation on the PSEA related issues.

1 - Needs

Palestine’s context became more complicated as result of the deterioration in the political situations accompanied by dramatic deterioration in the health situation as a result of the COVID 19 pandemic. The challenges originating from the protracted conflict, economic stagnation, high unemployment, gender inequalities and high rates of poverty and food insecurity continued to deteriorate. In the WB, the prolonged period of slow economic growth, restrictions on movement, trade, investment and access to land and water resources, together with higher food prices, continue to erode the fragile livelihoods of poor Palestinians. According to the MSNA 2021, Approximately 26 per cent of the People in need are suffering from severe, extreme or catastrophic need. The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of the targeted communities, based on a number of key sources, including the data and analysis exercise led by the FSS needs analysis for most vulnerable locations that are in need of livelihood/food security support in developing the HNO. CARE and partners were among the group who worked with the HNC to develop and update the matrix of the locations all over West Bank, MSNA results/findings. In the West Bank, the demolition and confiscation of agricultural structures and restrictions on the maintenance and productive assets, movement restrictions and settlement expansion, all contribute to livelihood instability and deterioration of the socioeconomic environment. As of September 2021 a total of 233 structures were demolished, which represent more than 34% of the total structures demolished (OCHA). Palestinian Bedouin and herding communities are disproportionately affected due to their location in sensitive areas and their reliance of agriculture-based livelihoods, as their sole or main source of income. The pandemic is placing significant stress on all dimensions of the food system, threatening food security and livelihood of thousand people in the West Bank. CARE/PARC have conducted needs assessment that included several meetings and discussions with targeted community members including PWD, male and female herders/farmers from different age groups were engaged like adolescents, youth and elderly where their inputs guided the design of the intervention. MoA offices in the respected governorates were consulted and guided the targeting activities/locations. The assessments conducted in late September took into considerations all new needs/implications of COVID 19 crisis. According to all assessments including the FSS/HNO and CARE’s quick assessment for the targeted communities, the small breeders https://projects.hpc.tools/project/183589/edit/review2/11 10/16/21, 6:07 PM HPC - Project Module: Edit Projectdon’t have access to natural grazing areas or services, suffer from high production cost and low market value, lack of vaccines, high inputs prices and lack of veterinary and extension services, high prices of fodder and inputs and poor farming practices, thus those small scale breeder became more aid dependent and the most affected groups noting that at least 80% of small ruminants breeders are small scale dominated by male farmers, most of them are Bedouins living mainly in Area C. According to the assessments; Small scale farmers especially women and PWD have access difficulties to agricultural assets/inputs and productive capacities of farms and damaged/non maintained productive assets (land, wells, irrigation systems, cisterns, greenhouses, etc) as well access obstacles to access the markets. Based on the assessments/findings, CARE/PARC identified 41 vulnerable communities in Jenin (11), Tubas (16), Jericho (4) and Nablus (10) to be targeted. The proposed activities will focus to improve the access to the essential resources (water, lands, etc) and provide inputs to increase the production capacities of the targeted farmers and herders in the selected areas through tailored activities that will focus on rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for farmers and herders including promotion of technical capacities and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience. As part of CARE strategy, and given the nature of the protracted crisis, CARE work closely with and through AIDA toward coordinated advocacy, for accountability for IHL violations in Area C. The project targeted communities are located in most vulnerable areas in Area C in West Bank and in Jordan valley who most of them were under threat of displacement, annexation, confiscation, demolition of assets and/ or soldiers’/settler’s violence. Women & children in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers in this sector, reflected in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility, and limited technical preparation. As a result, food insecurity has also disproportionately impacted women/children and marginalized groups. ALL proposed activities were developed in close coordination with MOA in each district.

2 - Activities

The project was designed in coordination with many actors mainly MoA and with cooperation between CARE/ PARC, FSS, and other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication, and maximize the number of beneficiaries who are in need. The activities will be implemented in partnership with PARC, who will be responsible for the technical implementation at field level. CARE will be responsible for the overall quality management, technical support in certain areas like marketing, gender and protection, GBV, PSEAH, ME, reporting, as well as for the feedback and complaint mechanism, and will act as the main contact for donors/actors. The project will target female and male small-scale farming /herding HHS/communities who are living in in Area C in the targeted districts and It directly responds to the actual needs of female/male small-scale farming & herding /Bedouin HHs and communities including PWD through providing initial production inputs, tools; rehabilitating their individual and/or community-wide livelihood assets & infrastructure (Water carries, agricultural road, etc.); and with existing basic knowledge needed to preserve & sustain their farming/herding activities. The organization will strictly coordinate the final selection of communities & beneficiaries with line ministries & with organizations work in the same area. The project team will finalize, validate the selection criteria per activity to ensure transparency. The criteria will look into socioeconomic status/ vulnerability, access, small-scale farmers, women headed households, availability of water, availability of agricultural services, previous benefit from previous projects. The main results/activities respond to the CSO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued and FSS objective1: The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced through the following activities. 1)Supporting Small-scale male and female livestock herders who are living Area C in north of West Bank to protect their livelihood ,improve their resilience and improving their production capacity by provision of required inputs for protecting, sustaining & increasing livestock holders’ resilience through providing shelters / Barns rehabilitation inputs: 67 in Jenin, Nablus, 62 in Tubas and 46 in Jericho, and 75 in Nablus governorates (220 males and 30 female farmers including 8 PWD). 2) Supporting Small-scale plant production female and male farmers in Area C in north of West Bank and in Jordan valley in Jenin, Nablus, Tubas and Jericho governorates to improve their resilience and livelihoods through increasing the quality & quantity of their production for self and market consumption & protection/rehabilitation of agriculture productive assets & essential infrastructure for farmers, including A. CARE Palestine’s confidential feedback and accountability mechanism (FAM), designed incorporating the Core Humanitarian Standards on Quality and Accountability, and CARE’s own Feedback and Accountability Working Group’s 2020 Guidance, will be shared with the communities and the teams, enabling targeted population to reach out to CARE. The FAM ensures that the interests and/or issues of the beneficiaries are continuously addressed safely and in a timely manner, and are in line with CARE's 'Do No Harm' approach and Humanitarian Accountability Framework, which protect CARE's beneficiaries from any unintended harm caused by the project implementation, and it enables community members to provide feedback that will be used to improve the services. In the projetc locations, Arabic instructions for lodging complaints are posted in clear view of community members. CARE and partner staff also distribute the FAM contact cards to the farmers, so any feedback and/or complaints can be brought forth directly to CARE. To ensure FAM’s continuous relevance, inclusion and accessibility, CARE is currently planning to conduct a community survey to further assess the community members’ preferences around the communication and reporting modalities. Moreover, a quick post distribution survey will be conducted in different locations to inquire about the quality of inputs/services and gaps to enable CARE to respond accordingly.

3 - Indicators

Indicators and targets: 1) 250 barns for small scale female and male herders will be rehabilitated (30 females and 220 male including 7 PWD) 2) 350 dunums of greenhouses rehabilitated for 350 small scale female and male farmers (50 females and 300 male including 11 PWD) 3) 15 km of water carrier lines / pipes installed and provided and will connect with at least six sources of water which will provide at least 310 famers (40 Female and 270 male including 10 PWD) with water to plant at least 1500 dunums of irrigated lands 4) 25 km of agricultural roads rehabilitated to improve access to 1000 farmers (920 male and 80 female including 30 PWD) who will have access to least 6250 dunums 5) 2500 cubic meters of 5 water metallic pools installed / or rehabilitated and supplied water for at least 125 small female and male farmers (20 Female and 105 Male including 4 PWD) and 18 soil pools (average 250 m3 each )will be rehabilitated serving 72 farmers (64 male and 8 female including 2 PWD). CARE/PARC will finalize the selection criteria, complaint and feedback mechanisms (documenting, processing and responding to complaints to ensure transparency and accountability to affected population), and will conduct a baseline survey using participatory approaches engaging CBOs and local committees. Monitoring data will be collected from the field and reported on a monthly basis. CARE/PARC will also conduct field visits and regularly meet with communities’ representatives/beneficiaries/etc. to ensure effective implementation, ownership, vulnerability etc. over project results and progress. An end line evaluation will also be conducted. In addition, protection will be mainstreamed in the project delivery (including respect for principles of non- discrimination, do no harm, etc.); and cross-cutting issues, including gender, age, environment and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). Furthermore, the project will be incorporated into CARE’s broader Impact, Learning and Accountability System (ILAS), which has overarching quality control standards and process for monitoring, promoting reflection and capturing learning, evidence, outcomes, and impact. Finally, there is a potential adoption of indicators under the guidance of the FSS, upon the availability of resources and consolidation of good practices. CARE has zero tolerance policy towards (1) sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse of program participants and community members and (2) sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse within the organization and has put in place measures for its prevention and reporting. Concretely, CARE’s global policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection explicitly outlines unacceptable behavior, and what we will do to investigate allegations, support people who have experienced sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and discipline perpetrators, including referring them to the relevant authorities. Internally as well, CARE has the Safeguarding Code of Conduct to which all employees and related personnel must adhere. CARE oPt’s FAM, which will be implemented during the life of the project, ensures that such reports are identified and community members’ complaints addressed and that steps are taken toward redressing any power imbalances. Additionally, CARE has a “Tell Us” whistle-blower policy and has mechanisms in place to report abuse or harassment of any kind, including anonymously to a third party via the internet or phone call.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

In Kind delivery is the most suitable modality to address the identified needs in the targeted communities as this is the fastest and safest way to ensure access to inputs and resources (water, Land), collective purchasing and distribution of the supplies/inputs proved to be more efficient and effective modality especially in the targeted areas that face serious access issues. This was also reflected in the MSNA data as well as CARE’s assessment that confirmed no potential harm of SEA. Also, CARE will distribute the FAM contact cards through which beneficiaries, partners and project’s gender-balanced team members can bring forth any programmatic or SEA-related complaints/feedback in a confidential manner, and CARE will also monitor distribution modality. All identified barriers including physical access for most of the targeted communities due to settlers and ISF violence or the financial/economic burden of lack of affordable inputs are addressed through the direct provision of in kind support.

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

CARE is committed to assuring accountability to stakeholders in all aspects and phases of the project. At the inception stage, community members helped inform the design of project activities, the composition of the teams, type of the support, urgent issues/needs, etc. CARE took into consideration the different needs of the different groups residing in the targeted communities (sex and age). Monitoring has been carried out through the on sight monitoring of distributions and activities, and it always includes segregation of data by sex and age (SADD). Partners follow the same monitoring guidelines and reporting. CARE’s Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) builds on our previous experience with humanitarian accountability, and it is in-formed by standards and guidance to ensure quality and accountability to our partners and stakeholders, and to support existing organizational and technical standards, with which to bring about sustainable change to communities in need. It was designed and piloted in other projects within CARE allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE for any feedback/complaint about the project activities, criteria, inputs, behavior by any of the project’s staff (CARE and Partners) including SEA-related issues. CARE will continue to raise community awareness regarding FAM, regularly reporting on feedback/complaints as part of CARE’s continuous learning agenda. CARE has started using the FAM cards in targeted communities and started to receive feedback/complaints via identified emails and phone numbers. Feedback/complaints are documented in a log sheet by the Focal Point / Accountability Officer responsible for monitoring the progress of each input until the feedback loop is closed. The data will be reported and used for management decisions and analyzing trends. CARE also is planning to conduct a community-based survey that will further assess accessibility of the mechanism, as well as preferred channels of communication.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

To create an effective and easily accessible mechanism for all stakeholders, CARE Palestine WBG considered the existing challenges and constraints that participants may face in the local context, including the continuous restrictions on movement, security risks and sudden changes to security, etc., as well as the social mores and gender norms. Before fully launching the mechanism, CARE piloted the FAM to gain deeper insight into beneficiary / stakeholders’ satisfaction levels about the quality of and access to services , as well as about the extent to which the most vulnerable community members were being reached and their needs were being met, to promote community empowerment, participation, and ownership of the feedback mechanism, and to help improve monitoring and evaluation activities by incorporating beneficiaries’ perspectives into reporting practices through real time analysis of CARE’s work on the ground. CARE continues to evolve FAM by soliciting stakeholders’ opinions about preferences with respect to access, methods / channels through which to provide feedback and complaints. With respect to the project’s teams work, Arabic instructions (cards) for lodging complaints are posted in clear view of community members, and contact cards are distributed to participants via through local CBOs, local councils, focal points in the communities, projects activities, etc. CARE will distribute the FAM cards in the communities including the beneficiaries so they are informed about the mechanism which also includes SEA-related complainant mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

CARE’s ‘Do No Harm’ principles are embedded in all approaches and interventions by CARE, from the ideation process of a project, throughout its duration and after the project is finished. CARE has identified risks, their potential impact and mitigation strategies, and all activities proposed are designed based on numerous discussions with community members, partners and other stakeholders, as well as on needs analyses (i.e. rapid / gender needs assessment) that are sensitive to the complexities of the local context to ensure that people are not exposed to additional risks through our action. CARE is committed to protection and ensuring safety and security of participants, dignity and accessibility of services/project activities; CARE’s activities are inclusive, and designed with the community members’ feedback, and the selection criteria for activities are transparent; the data collection processes are sensitive to the context, power dynamics in communities, and also the management of sensitive information is conducted with caution and care. CARE will also FAM that would include any SEA related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PWD/women and other vulnerable groups are considered as main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project, as they were identified by as vulnerable groups who needs special consideration, for that the project team will work with the communities’ representatives and actors in the different communities to ensure equal access of PWD/women/others to all projects’ activities and will ensure the full inclusion of PWD/women/other in all project activities. PWD/women/others will have special attention when identifying/selecting the farmers/beneficiaries. The team will ensure that meetings locations in the different communities are accessible for PWD/women/other. All project activities will be implemented based on a clear criteria that ensure transparency, equal and impartial access to the aid. CARE will also FAM that would include any SEA related issues.
