Project Summary

This intervention takes a participatory, community-based approach to recovery programming, ensuring market-sensitive livelihoods support is provided to vulnerable Syrian households. Vocational training and on-the-job training through apprenticeships will be provided to youth and women, who will be offered a selection of enterprise and employment pathways. For those seeking to set up their own small/micro enterprise, a further package of business skills training and business grant will be provided, based on their business proposition. Other vocational training graduates will be supported to seek employment with small-medium businesses within the community. All skills training will be based on a rigorous assessment of the labour market, identifying local sectors of economic growth or gaps in available goods/ services. Small and micro-business owners before the crisis whose business has been negatively affected by the conflict will be supported to relaunch their businesses through a combination of skills strengthening, business grants & coaching. Life skills training will be provided, particularly for those new to the labour market such as youth, women & people with functional limitations. Community acceptance for targeted livelihoods activities will be ensured through community involvement in selection of types of businesses needed in the community as well as beneficiary selection and monitoring project progress. Projects aimed at benefiting the entire community will also be supported through cash-for-work activities.
Inclusion of with functional limitations will be ensured through community mapping and assessment of their specific needs, awareness-raising on the rights and capacities of PWD and intensive coaching support. Participation of women will be supported through awareness and advocacy with their families, presence of female trainers and apprenticeship providers and skills training for home-based businesses. Complementary services such as Mine Risk Education (MRE) and Psychosocial Support (PSS) will be provided based on community needs & vulnerability assessments.
the Organization also supports international actors on the implementation of their respective livelihood activities in order to strengthen the inclusiveness of the designed interventions and to ensure the full participation of persons with disabilities within the activities.
This range and combination of activities will result in vulnerable Syrian households becoming self-reliant in meeting their basic needs & more resilient to conflict-related shocks and stresses.

Needs

With 9 million people in Syria in need of Early Recovery & Livelihoods (ERL) support in 2019 , the Syria ERL Cluster estimates that 4 out of 5 Syrians live in poverty . The conflict has decimated the Syrian economy. 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity, and a further 6.5 million are food insecure. Unemployment rates are high - estimated at 55% by the Food Security Sector, but likely to be even higher in recently reconciled areas and amongst specific vulnerable groups such as women, youth, older people and people with functional limitations. Depleted assets and savings, widespread destruction of productive infrastructure and years of protracted displacement, have resulted in the use of negative coping strategies by Syrian households. According to Food Security Sector data, an estimated 40% of Syrians spend more than 65% of their expenditure on food. 65% of food insecure people are reportedly adopting emergency negative coping strategies. This reliance on harmful coping strategies could be further exacerbated by continuing low purchasing power and declining incomes in 2019. Given the recent reduction in hostilities following Governement of Syria (GoS) taking control over many opposition-controlled areas in 2018, access to livelihoods is now amongst the top priorities for Syrian households . The crisis has also led to a severe contraction of skilled workers available within the country. A recent study conducted by UNDP in Syria estimates that a mere 51% of the pre-crisis workforce is now available inside Syria . According to assessments undertaken by the INGO in South Syria in November and December 2018, one of the main challenges reported by small/micro business owners both to manage and expand their business was a lack of skilled and experienced workers. They attributed this to high levels of migration, lack of training availability and lack of experience, particularly amongst the youth. Low purchasing power amongst the Syrian population, depopulation and limited employment opportunities further corresponds to low market demand and high prices, which is another key challenge for the running and expansion of small businesses . Financial services are non-existent across Syria. the Organization’s assessments as well as other Labour Market Assessments conducted in Syria in 2018 , confirm that the priorities for Micro Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) expansion are affordable financing, business training /technical support, and marketing/sales skill building. Women face specific challenges when entering the workforce. Women felt that they don’t have as much freedom as men to work: some because their husband won’t allow them to work, others because they are afraid of the situation. Women and men both reported traditions and norms as barriers for women to work in certain professions, such as carpentry and butchery. Women reported preferring home-based businesses in sectors such as tailoring, hairdressing, cooking. A Viable Livelihoods study conducted by a consortium of INGO also found that the inclusion of people with functional limitations depends on an overall misperception of disabilities among potential employers, rather than of actual work opportunities and highlights the need for awareness-raising and advocy for inclusion within the work force. A recent report from the Health Cluster found the rate of disability in Northern Syria to be 30% - double that of the global prevalence rate estimated by WHO, which is 15%. People with functional limitations face systematic exclusion from the labour market, due to a range of factors such as attitudinal barriers amongst employers, poor accessibility, and lack of information.

Implementation of activities

ERL Objective 1: Strengthen access to livelihood by creating income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery- relates to SO3 1.1 Short-term work opportunities created Sub-activity 1.1.1 Area selection for implementation of livelihoods activities will be conducted based on criteria including number and percentage of population in need at risk of food insecurity, and community severity ranking according to HNO PiN dataset, with communities scored 3 or lower being prioritised for livelihoods. Other criteria include level of coverage by other actors, market feasibility, and presence of other services to enable area-based recovery programming. Sub-Activity 1.1.2 Cash-for-Work (CFW) will be prioritised for newly or recently reconciled areas that have faced a high level of destruction. These conditional cash grants will be provided to mostly self-selecting households willing to work for a wage slightly lower than the normal wage rate. Projects will be selected in consultation with the community, covering mostly light rehabilitation of productive infrastructure, such as markets, irrigation infrastructure, etc. Participation of women (minimum 25%) and people with functional limitations (minimum 10%) will be ensured through complementary activities if needed. 1.3 Vocational and skills training provided Provision of technical & vocational skills, life skills and business skills training will be offered to eligible applicants based on their selection of employment or enterprise pathways. Technical/ vocational skills trainings will be based on the LMA findings of sectors of economic growth or greatest demand in the local market. Vocational skills training (VT) will be certified where feasible, and include an apprenticeship period for on-the-job training and establishing links to local employment opportunities. Young people and women will be prioritised for VT & apprenticeships. Both male and female trainers will be ensured as far as possible, to give women a wide choice in preferred skills training. Life skills training will be provided for target groups who may be new to the labour market, such as young people, women and people with functional limitations. Once beneficiaries have graduated from the VT + apprenticeship pathway, they will have the opportunity to apply for the micro-business training and grant scheme if they prefer to set up their own business rather than seeking employment. 1.4 Entrepreneurship supported Sub-activity 2.4.1: Localised Labour Market Assessments (LMA) will be conducted in each selected community in order to establish local-level economic growth sectors, new economic opportunities, and technical/ vocational skills in greatest demand. This data will feed into a response options analysis for livelihoods programme design in line with the needs of the local labour market. LMAs will also identify specific barriers faced by women, men, young & older people and people with functional limitations in accessing employment (including self-employment) and make recommendations for addressing them. Sub-activity 2.4.2: Provision of business skills training, grants & coaching for small & micro business recovery for re-establishing businesses will be offered to eligible applicants. Types of businesses supported will be based on the LMA findings, which fill a gap in the market (e.g. offer affordable agricultural inputs) or offer high economic growth potential (e.g. construction sector professions such as carpentry, metalwork). Priority will be given to those who had a business before the crisis, but which was lostdue to the conflict. On-going coaching to restart or grow businesses will be provided for a period of at least 3 months

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Community tensions arising from the beneficiary selection Mitigation measures: - Vulnerability criteria & weighting will be validated with the community through a participatory Community Steering Committee - Community-based beneficiary selection – Community Steering Committees will participate in beneficiary selection - Feedback & Response mechanism will be set up to elicit beneficiary & community feedback Risk: Inclusion/ exclusion errors in targeting result in tension or sexual exploitation and abuse of women (partner staff + beneficiaries) Mitigation measures: - Community-based beneficiary selection – Community Steering Committees will participate in beneficiary selection - Targeting will be vulnerability-based and not based on status (IDP/ host community) - Clear processes and guidelines for beneficiary selection are outlined and provided to partner staff Risk: Support to women within a vulnerable household results in intra-family tensions Mitigation measures: - Close monitoring of family dynamics & awareness sessions with families if needed - Provision of incentives for training such as cash or food allowance that benefits the whole household Risk: Women and/or people with functional limitations are targeted for sexual exploitation and abuse Mitigation measures: - Ensure safe transportation of women & people with functional limitations to/ from training, distribution and CFW sites - Awareness-raising of the rights and capacities of people with functional limitations targeting both families and community - Close monitoring by female staff Risk: People with functional limitations are excluded from the project due to lack of visibility and/or lack of resources to support their full participation Mitigation measures: - Inclusion workers will be embedded within project team to provide specialist support to people with functional limitations - Separate mapping of people with functional limitations based on Washington Group Questions may be undertaken if needed - Awareness-raising of the rights and capacities of people with functional limitations targeting both families and community Risk: CFW beneficiaries are exposed to explosive remnants of war whilst undertaking rehabilitation/ rubble clearance activities Mitigation measures: - Provision of Risk Education sessions to CFW beneficiaries undertaking activities deemed to be risky

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Aid diversion/ misuse of funds by the partner Mitigation: - Partner assessment and background checks - M&E system with Post-Distribution Monitoring Risk: Renewed conflict disrupts markets and/or prevents the project activities from continuing Mitigation: - Prioritise livelihoods programming in relatively stable areas, in communities with severity raking 3 or below - Project implementation through partners, whose staff are embedded in the community and may be able to retain access for longer Risk: Grants/ assets provided to beneficiaries are used for non-livelihood related activities Mitigation: - Agreements will be signed with beneficiaries outlining the restriction of the cash grant to livelihoods activities only - Cash will be provided in two tranches - Monitoring will be conducted following each cash tranche to ensure that cash is being spent in line with the business plan developed Risk: Community leaders/ local authorities do not accept project activities Mitigation: - Community leaders and local authorities consulted from the project assessment and design phase to gain buy-in - Evidence-based participatory approach to programme design, ensuring community views are taken into account may be used to influence local leaders & authorities positively. - CFW projects to have community benefits to mitigate the selectiveness of longer-term livelihoods activities Risk: Intended beneficiaries are psycho-socially not ready to participate in productive activities Mitigation: - Basic PSS activities such as Psychological First Aid, group PSS sessions to be integrated into project activities - Mental Health Service mapping if relevant to the area for referrals - Additional PSS support may be provided to beneficiaries most in need
