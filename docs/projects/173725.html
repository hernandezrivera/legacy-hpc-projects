Project Summary

Street Child will address the critical barriers to accessing quality learning for 9,300 children affected by the conflict in NWSW Cameroon, focusing on out-of-school children in NWSW and IDP children in the West and Littoral. Working with schools and communities, Street Child will also seek to improve the capacity of education stakeholders to ensure schools are safe, protective environments for all children, regardless of age, gender or disability status. Overall, the project targets 22,080 people.
In the NWSW, the focus will be on improving access to quality non-formal education by working with existing community-based learning centres, supporting community volunteer teachers to meet the foundational education and social emotional learning needs of out-of-school children. In addition, in areas where schools are open, Street Child will seek to improve access to quality, protective education which meets the differing needs of conflict-affected and displaced children – in addition to pedagogical methods to meet differing learning levels and provide catch-up classes, teachers will be trained on PSS and child protection and school disaster risk reduction plans will be put in place to ensure schools are protective spaces. In the West and Littoral, Street Child will seek to improve access to education for displaced children, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), through a similar approach focusing on teacher training and the provision of catch-up classes where needed.

1 - Besoins / Needs

As a result of the ongoing conflict, targeting of education, and displacement, the educations needs in the NW and SW regions are acute: only 13% of schools across the region are functional, leaving over 850,000 children without access to education (MSNA 2020). High numbers of children and families have been displaced to the West and Littoral regions as a result of the conflict, and in these regions over 74% of the school-aged IDP population remains out of school. In the majority of NWSW communities, schools are not open and children have limited access to learning through ‘bush schools’. Here, the focus will be on improving access to safe, quality non-formal education by working with existing community-based learning centres. Community teachers will be trained to provide basic literacy and numeracy through a low-cost, low-resource and play-based approach, targeting children who have been out of school and lack foundational skills. Locally available teaching and learning materials will be distributed. Learning outcomes will be tracked, with the aim of iteratively building an evidence base as a foundation to scaling up an effective approach to quickly improving foundational literacy and numeracy outcomes in community schools. Teachers and community education committees will be trained on psychosocial support, child protection and positive pedagogies to ensure that the learning environment is safe and protective for all children (taking into account the different barriers that different gender, age and disability groups face), with child protection referral pathways mapped out and promoted. In areas where schools are open, many struggle to meet the differing learning needs of children who have been out of school, with overcrowded classrooms, lack of appropriate learning materials, and lack of teacher lack capacity to meet the psychosocial needs of conflict-affected children. Here, Street Child will similarly work with teachers to improve their capacity to meet the different needs of children with different learning levels in overcrowded classrooms, and to be able to identify and refer children at-risk of child protection concerns such as SGBV. Teachers will be trained to provide catch-up classes to children who have been out of school, to allow them to access the curriculum for their age/grade level. Teaching and learning materials will be distributed on the basis of need, as well as WASH materials to ensure that schools are protective for girls and uphold minimum hygiene and COVID-19 prevention measures. Street Child will work with PTAs and school-based management committees to develop and implement school safety/disaster risk reduction plans, to make the school environments safer. In the West and Littoral, where the majority of displaced children are not accessing education, Street Child will support IDP children, including unaccompanied and separated children, to enable them to access quality education that meets their social emotional learning needs. This will include the provision of catch-up classes for children who have been out of school, to allow them to mainstream properly, the training of teachers to be able to meet their needs in the classroom (including psychosocial support needs), and the provision of teaching and learning and WASH materials. Given the scale of the education needs, and identified limited technical capacity of local partners, Street Child aims to build the capacity of local partners on innovative education in emergencies approaches – low-resource, scalable approaches which can meet the foundational learning needs of out-of-school and conflict-affected children in a protective manner. Street Child will work closely with local partners as well as with community stakeholders to build the capacity of those working in education in the region, contributing to longer-term systems strengthening even with the current access barriers and challenges facing education programming in NWSW.
