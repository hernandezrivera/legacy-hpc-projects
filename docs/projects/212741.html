Project Summary

After 13 years of conflict, Syria remains one of the most complex humanitarian emergencies, with an estimated 14.9 million people in need of life-saving health and nutrition services. Malnutrition remains a significant public health challenge across Northeast Syria (NES), with an estimated 5.5 million children under five years (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in need of nutrition support. The combination of a protracted conflict for over a decade, persistent drought, and low water levels has severely affected livelihoods and agricultural productivity, leading to soaring food prices. As a result, vulnerability to communicable diseases (CDs) has increased, along with high rates of malnutrition for CU5 and PLW, while nutritional anemia and micronutrient deficiencies have become prevalent. As per the NES November 2023 SMART Survey, stunting increased from 23% in 2022 to 32% in 2023, considered to be extreme as per the GNC severity scale. Aggravating factors for malnutrition such as food insecurity, high prevalence of CDs and sub-optimal infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices continue to deteriorate the nutrition situation of the most vulnerable households.
Leveraging its 10 years of sustained service provision in Syria, Relief international (RI) proposes a comprehensive nutrition intervention in NES with health as an entry point, with a sub-implementing partner (IP) to reduce nutrition related mortality and morbidity among CU5 and PLW in DeZ Governorate. This project contributes to the prevention and treatment of acute and chronic malnutrition in DeZ, with particular attention to CU5 and PLW through the implementation of quality community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM). Distinct focus is also placed on addressing underlying determinants of malnutrition by improving child-feeding and care practices through IYCF counseling and nutrition educations messages, as well as supplementation of micronutrients.
RI will continue strengthening community health activities through training and support of community health workers (CHWs), who will deliver health, nutrition, and hygiene promotion messages through home visits and in community gatherings, facilitate referrals to HFs for preventive and curative health services, conduct Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screenings, refer malnourished children and PLWs to CMAM clinics and undertake disease surveillance and reporting of CDs.
SAM cases with medical complications will be referred to the nearest nutrition stabilization centers. Behavior change communication will aim to improve MIYCN practices, through individual counselling and group sessions at nutrition sites and community level. The individualized counseling sessions will be based on identified gaps in MIYCN practices and poor outcome during the follow-up. Both women and men will be targeted with health, nutrition and hygiene messages through one-to-one counselling or group health promotion sessions. 
Partnership with the sub-IP will allow RI to access hard-to reach areas and community acceptance, while facilitating a more sustainable exit strategy through partner capacity strengthening and advocacy for secured funding.
There is a risk that PLW lacks access to HFs, and therefore RI will invest in community mobilization with CHWs reaching out to PWL at community level, conducting home visits and facilitating tracking defaulters under the CMAM programme. 
To mitigate the risk of exposing women and girls to harassment/abuse while accessing nutrition services, RI will provide separate spaces for women and girls to access nutrition services and conduct awareness raising session on the importance of nutrition services at community level to prevent access constraints and stigmatization.
Additionally, increased risk of intimate partner violence due to stressors related to food insecurity will be mitigated by training staff on GBV identification and referral pathways to the required services.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In Syria, most functioning HFs’ operations remain dependent on humanitarian aid, therefore, decrease in funding as of 2024 poses a significant challenge to the provision of critical and lifesaving services. The lack of functioning HFs in NES presents a considerable challenge to meeting the heightened needs, including for moderate and acute malnutrition. Without intervention, an increase in morbidity and mortality will occur as a result of the high rate of acute malnutrition and CDs, coupled with lack of adequate access to health services. The Syria Management Team (SMT) provides technical support to RI’s overall Syria response, led by the Country director. The implementation team will be led by the Area Director and Health Program Manager based in Syria, while the Amman-based SMT will supervise and support the team through oversight management, monitoring, and guidance on the quality of services. The program teams will manage the implementation under the technical leadership of the Health Program Manager, supported by the Medical Coordinator. RI and the Sub-IP will identify focal persons from the supported HFs who will be responsible for the day-to-day interaction and monitoring of CHW activities while the RI Medical Officers and Nutrition Officers will be responsible for regular monitoring, capacity assessment and strengthening, including on-the-job training. The CHWs will also be equipped with health promotion aid materials such as flip charts, posters, and brochures. Monitoring and evaluation will be conducted with internal MEAL tools and is also firmly embedded in the project design through a Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism to account in correction actions and future planning. Additionally, at the WGSS, women will be screened for acute malnutrition, and provided with IYCF, health and nutrition key messages to facilitate access for PLW to peer groups, while benefiting from support services such as day care, free transportation, and joint awareness-raising. Conflict-affected populations and individuals may have limited access to nutrition services due to living in hard-to-reach areas due to security concerns. To mitigate this risk, RI will conduct up-to-date security assessments, coordinate and strengthen relations with local authorities and community leaders to ensure acceptance and safe access to nutrition services, particularly in conflict-affected areas, establish SOPs to ensure uninterrupted provision of lifesaving nutrition services in the event of security escalations, and continue its partnership with the sub-IP for service continuity. Safeguarding incidents (SEA and GBV) could increase due to a rise in negative coping mechanisms as living conditions deteriorate. To mitigate this risk, RI will mainstream protection across activities, assure that service-users/staff are aware of their rights, what constitutes exploitation and abuse, and how to report safeguarding incidents and violation of Code of Conduct provisions.
