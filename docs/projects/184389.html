Project Summary

Mercy Corps proposes a project on Livelihood Support targeting vulnerable IDPs and poor host communities in Mudug and Galgadud Regions of Galmudug State of Somalia, with the aim to contribute to the achievement of FSC strategies and objectives aligned to the HRP 2022. The project will benefit a total of 4,120vulnerable and marginalized households (1,100 Men, 1,500 women, 600 boys, 920 girls) in IDPs and host communities in Galkayo, Hobyo, Caabudwaq and Caadado Districts. The proposed intervention will improve immediate access to food, provide livelihood inputs (Livestock), and skills training for new livelihoods for the communities. Access to immediate food assistance will be provided through Conditional and Unconditional Cash transfers in forms of the multipurpose cash assistance and food vouchers to meet their basic needs and to protect and restore livelihoods, related food, and income sources. Communities will also participate in the rehabilitation and establishment of community infrastructure such as water catchments, and feeder roads, whereby they will receive cash-for-work and pastoral households will receive livelihood assets in form of livestock restocking for restoration. Technical vocational skills training will also be provided as a form of building new livelihoods. The project targets the most vulnerable populations categorized as facing IPC Phases 3 and 4.
The proposed project is cognizant of the current food security context of Galmudug region of Somalia. According to FSNAU and FEWS NET, Post-Gu Assessment in September 2021 projected that nearly 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. That includes 1.1 million internally displaced persons who will face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021. Out of the total 3.5 million people in need of urgent food assistance, 640,000 people face significant food consumption gaps. In addition, 3.7 million people are classified in IPC phase 2 (Stressed), engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs, bringing the total number of Somalis experiencing acute food insecurity to 7.2 million by late 2021. In addition to, majority of the estimated 2.9 million IDPs across Somalia are poor with limited livelihood assets, few income earning opportunities, low communal support, and rely heavily on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, one-third of internally displaced persons (in both rural and urban settlements) face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021. The COVID-19 pandemic and related containment measures seem to affect the urban poor population more than rural poor population. As a result, the urban poor face moderate to large food consumption gaps, partly due to a slowdown in economic activities in urban areas and the rising costs of food and other essential non-food items.
Mercy Corps field assessments in target regions revealed the deepening effect of frequent droughts and conflicts, which have led to high food consumption gaps and loss of livelihood assets. There is urgent need to address the food insecurity and livelihood threats for the IDPs and communities in the region. As the food security situation in Galmudug state remains tenuous, Mercy Corps sees an urgent need to respond and save lives, alleviate human suffering, and reduce the social and economic impact of the humanitarian crisis. This will reduce acute humanitarian needs and excess mortality among the most vulnerable by providing lifesaving and sustaining integrated assistance. The project will consider all gender groups and disability needs in implementation and will maintain accountability to the population through ensuring community feedback mechanisms and adherence to policies.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfers are inherently unrestricted, meaning each transfer can be spent according to recipient’s priorities; and potentially address multiple needs. Mercy Corps has implemented cash transfer programming in past projects because of its convenience and safety for participants. Despite the perceived risk of cash, Mercy Corps has not faced any security or accountability issues related to using cash. Additionally, given the lack of financial services in the target areas, cash is required to ensure convenience for the individuals. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted by the monitoring and evaluation teams to ensure accountability and registration of any complaints or issues that have arose through the activity. Moreover, Guided by the National Commission for Refugees and IDPs (NCRI), in coordination with the Cash Working Group and Somalia Cash Consortium, MPCA continues to be a proven way to effectively support vulnerable households to meet their immediate non-food and food needs. The cash distributions will be aimed towards funding immediate food and non-food items such as rent, fuel, household goods, WASH costs, and transportation. MPCA Transfers will be delivered for a period of four months, with each transfer pegged to the locally adjusted Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB), and determined by the national Cash Working Group.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Given the lack of financial services in the target areas in the Galmudug region (Based on Mercy Corps previous Market assessments), cash is required to ensure convenience for the individuals. Of course at the start of the project, Mercy Corps will undertake a market analysis to determine the availability and price of food and NFIs before any intervention. This market analysis may help in deciding if in CBI or in-kind will be sought. Mercy Corps also recognizes that, market functionality may also be affected by extreme disasters events like drought or floods but also by insecurity. All these factors will be considered during intervention.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 Consultation with local administration, community leaders, and the beneficiaries will ensure that the standard selection criteria benefit the most vulnerable, including women. Consultation in selection of beneficiaries will include local community and religious leaders, and households (women, men, and youth) of different age groups including the elderly and disabled. The consultation will be fair by including persons with disabilities, minority groups, marginalized communities, and all people from different social, economic statuses or origins to ensure there is no discrimination based on gender, physical ability, status, or sexual orientation in the selection of beneficiaries. 4.2 : Staff, and communities will be informed of Mercy Corps’ Code of Conduct, including the PSEA Policy, and how to report any violations. Beneficiaries will have an opportunity to register complaints, provide feedback, and get a response. This will be through Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM), which is designed to familiarize individuals with the conduct policies in place within our organization. They can report feedback or violations and seek assistance daily. Any identified risks or violations will be addressed on time as per the Mercy Corps policies. Mercy Corps will work with GBV actors in the IDP camps in the identification of participants to conduct confidential verification processes to ensure the inclusion and privacy of GBV survivors. To avoid stigmatization and privacy breaches, the participants will not be outwardly identified as GBV survivors. Throughout the process and where needs arise, Mercy Corps will work with protection actors for support. Mercy Corps has a gender focal point that supports all programs including the SEAM II program in ensuring gender mainstreaming and protection.
