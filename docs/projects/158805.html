Project Summary

This proposal is fully in line with the strategic objective1 and 2 of Education Cluster, SDG4 and CRC art.28 and29, aiming to guarantee accessibility to qualitative education for children living in H2. TCC in partnership with WeWorld-GVC aims to reach children in the most vulnerable area in West Bank working in 4 primary schools in located in Hebron H2area, which was identified as a priority area in the 2019HPF second standard allocation strategy.
TCC and WW-GVC are active members of the Education cluster and are committed to promoting the quality education in Palestine, supporting the MoEHE in providing adequate education services as rehabilitation of education infrastructure responding to the lack from duty bearers’ implementation foreseen due to the fiscal crisis and financial cuts from major donors.
“The humanitarian situation in the H2 area” (April 2019) stated that 88% of the school children residing in H2 must cross a checkpoint to reach school, almost 90% of the HHs interviewed reported that their children faced at least one incident of delay, physical search, harassment or detention on their way to school. Therefore, the proposed intervention aims to increase equitable safe access to inclusive and quality education in 4schools in H2 restricted area (Yakoubiya, Muhammadiyah, Al Hajeria and Al Nahda), through a combination of hard and soft components in line with the mandates, strategies, and expertise of TCC and WW-GVC.
During the need assessment lead by TCC and WW-GVC school staff and teachers highlighted the huge gap left in human rights monitoring and protection especially for school children following the withdrawal of over 60international TIPH monitors in early 2019. “TipH used to fund extracurricular activities for children in H2 during holidays and this gap needs to be filled” Mr.Rawhi Alzaro–Principal of Al Nahda boys school. Moreover, the proposed project will contribute to the creation of a safe, inclusive and interactive learning environment for the most vulnerable boys and girls students in restricted areas of H2.
The project proposal includes soft and hard components as followed:1) rehabilitation of 4schools infrastructure to ensure a safe and protective environment for quality education;2) Building the capacities of targeted 32 teachers in psycho -social support for children; 3) improving the socio-academic performance of 60 girls and boys in the 2nd, 3rd and 4th, 5th and 6thgrades suffering from socio-academic dysfunction using after school remedial education and extracurricular activities on Saturdays ;4) Organizing 4 raising awareness and Psycho-education workshops for 40 parents and 4 school community committees on how to develop strategies of interventions with children inside the schools 5) advocacy activities to increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children in H2 by duty bearers and third parties.
Education experts commissioned by TCC and the Ministry of Education (Hebron Directorate) will organize monitoring visits to the 4 schools to the end of monitoring and evaluating the performance of the teachers while introducing their classes.
In line with their expertise TCC will take the lead on the psychosocial and extracurricular activities with the support of qualified sub-contract, and WW-GVC, already working on shelter rehabilitation in H2 area, will be responsible for emergency rehabilitation and upgrading of 4 schools. The action will target 1161students (1021 boys,140girls), 90Teachers and counsellors
(34men,34women), 80caregivers (40men, 40women) and reach 230decision-makers, media and donors through advocacy actions and 50,000members of civil society at national and international level with communication outputs.

1 - Needs

According to MoE and the Education Cluster, more than 12,484 students in Hebron H2 are facing restrictions on their access to education (HNO2020). Schools in the restricted H2 area are under the threat of settlers’ violence, suffering from the impacts of daily clashes in the area (AAH Assessment, Feb2019).Through the Community-based Protection Approach WeWorld-GVC has been collecting quantitative and qualitative data in West Bank, including Education sector, to collect protection related information on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities and coping strategies, with a community engagement method. The assessment on H2 area (March 2019), specifically covering Old Souq area including AlAqqaba, As Sawakna and AlQazzazin neighbourhoods, underline the persisting difficult environment, continuously challenged protection threats, restriction of movements and access to education.Girls and boys students are considered a vulnerable group due to the high exposure to Israelis attacks, IF and settlers, while accessing to education services and schools. They are generally not safe due to exposure to the threat of tear gas and sound bombs thrown by the IF, and at risk of inspections in cases of security escalation. The IF violence actions faced by the girls and boys students include: detainment, access restriction, several schools’ evacuations; physical attacks; regular verbal harassment and running people over in the street.Girls and boys accessing schools or even staying inside are always intimidated and threatened by the high Israeli presence. Different challenges are faced by boys and girls: especially male teenagers who are more exposed to IF attacks as a way to increase the violence not only among the students but among their families. Girls face challenges related to social norms such as harassments. Harassing girls is sometimes used as a provocation method to trigger confrontations with the IF.“The humanitarian situation in the H2 area” (April 2019) stated that 88% of students residing in H2 area must cross a checkpoint to reach their school, 90% of the HHs interviewed reported that their children faced at least one incident of delay, physical search, harassment or detention on their way to school. As the vehicles’ access in the area is limited due to other restrictions imposed, by the hotspot area and the lack of spaces, there is a limited possibility to realize responsive action. Moreover, the permit to operate in H2 has been denied for some international protection groups that protect students’ access to schools and other similar groups have decided to limit its actions gradually due to security issues.To tackle the challenges and difficulties mentioned above families often adopt a coping mechanism that have a negative impact on girls and boys, including withdrawing children from school, this practice particularly affects girls. To avoid the increase of dropouts and the negation of the right to education (CRCart.28-29), schools need to guarantee a safe and protected environment for access to quality education, where children do not feel threated or intimidated.The joint assessment carried out by TCC and WeWorld-GVC during the proposal elaboration phase, showed: schools in H2 are host in old rental building with strong needs of rehabilitation, with persistent dampness especially in the underground rooms, leaking and open roof, and lack of emergency exit; inadequate spaces for learning activities; need of replacement of doors and windows to prevent the entrance of tears gas and mitigate the harassment from Israeli forces and settlers. Psychosocial support and extra-curricular activities can support the stress release of the students and school staff responding to the need identified in the ICCG household survey that stated 70% of the children do not have access to extra curriculum activities or after school activities.

2 - Activities

During the design of the project, the different needs of boys, girls, men and women collected through the above-mentioned assessments and related with the rehabilitation of schools, implementation of psychosocial support, extra-curricular activities and advocacy actions have been taken into account.The project aims to increase equitable safe access to inclusive and quality education services for vulnerable children, through a combination of hard and soft components in line with the mandates, strategies, and expertise of TCC and WeWorld-GVC. The action will entail 3 integrated components: psychosocial support and extracurricular activities (WP1), rehabilitation of the 4 schools in H2 area (WP2) and advocacy action (WP3) ensuring safe, accessible, interactive and inclusive education environment, in line with the applicable standards and the parameters of the Education Cluster insuring gender and age-appropriate solutions. The schools, selected in coordination the Ministry of Education and the Directorate of Hebron, are the following: Yakoubiya, Muhammadiyah, Al Hajeria and Al Nahda.WP1.Capacity building and extra-curricular activities on cognitive and socio-emotional learning for school staff, caregivers and students. The activities, structured in coordination with teachers and counsellors, will support students affected by violence and enforce cognitive and socio-emotional skills. School staff and caregivers will enhance their knowledge of how to face psychosocial and learning challenges.1.1 Capacity building training for teachers promoting cognitive and socio-emotional learning outputs among children: a. screening the educational cadres, b. identification of participants through interview and monitoring visit b. 24hours TOT training on quality and recreation in education, cognitive and emotional learning giving tools and strategy to support the students targeting 12 teachers; c. 18 hours peer to peer training targeting 20 teachers.1.2 Organization of a workshop on intervention strategies to identify and overcome cognitive and socio-emotional learning improving the knowledge of caregivers and school community committees: a. 4 raising awareness workshops for 40 caregivers and 4 school community committees; b.4psycho-education workshops for 40 caregivers.1.3 Remedial education and extracurricular activities reducing cognitive and socio-emotional learning challenges: a. pre/post needs assessment and identification of affected children; b. elaboration of Students’ Cognitive and Socio-emotional Intervention Plans; c. implementation of remedial classes (2hoursx4daysx8months) targeting 60students; d. organization of extra-curricular activities (4 interactive days monthly and summer activities) targeting 60students.WP2. Rehabilitation of 4 schools’ infrastructure to ensure a safe and protective environment for quality education: a. technical assessment and support; b. emergency rehabilitation to increase protection from IF and settlers’ attacks (i.e. protecting the school from non-authorized access completing the wall surrounding the building, insulate the building, and preventing tear gas coming inside the structure); c. rehabilitation of stress release spaces to support the right to leisure and recreational activities (art.31 CRC) to promote a positive environment and face bullying and violent behaviours.WP3: Mobilization of different stakeholders for immediate protection and support to vulnerable communities, enforcing the incident report and alert mechanism. a. elaboration of 1policy briefing on violation in education; b. organization of field visit involving policymakers at local and national level; c. production of 1case study, 1 video and 1 infographic reaching strategic audience at the local and international level.All the activities will be implemented in coordination with the Ministry of Education, the Directorate of Education in Hebron and the Hebron Rehabilitation Committee to join the effort and avoid overlappin

3 - Indicators

The purpose of this action is to address the communities’ humanitarian and protection needs while improving their access to education and contributing to safety and dignity. Specifically, the action will lead to the achievement of the specific objective following these indicators: - # education infrastructure in H2 improved through emergency rehabilitation; - # students and teachers benefiting from emergency school rehabilitation; - # of women and men teachers and counsellors trained and equipped with knowledge and capacities in terms of socio-emotional learning - # targeted teachers enhanced their knowledge on recreation in education, cognitive and socio-emotional learning and international mechanism and tools to assess and counteract cognitive and socio-emotional learning. - % of targeted caregivers have improved their knowledge of cognitive and socio-emotional learning challenge and psychological support and intervention strategies.- # of students, parents and school staff benefiting from school-based psychosocial support services.- # of students benefiting from remedial education services- % of students have improved their socio-academic performance after the implementation of remedial education services.- # stakeholders participating in awareness-raising sessions, increased their awareness on the coercive environment and violations in the education sector. - # advocacy initiative realised (# policy brief, #field visit, #video, #case study and #infographic produced). The achievement of the indicators will be measured through an in-depth baseline technical assessment. The evaluation will measure the impact and benefit related to the activities’ implementation for the different target group with a gender-age sensitive approach. After the completion of the intervention, an overall evaluation will be conducted, including quantitative data collection (post specific survey) and qualitative analysis through separated focus groups (women/girls and men/boys) focusing on access to education/ safety/ dignity and integrity.

Complaint response mechanism is part of WW-GVC accountability and commitment strategy to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneficiaries. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

To avoid negative impacts TCC and WeWorld-GVC analyze the context and the interaction between the intervention and the context where the action will be implemented. The organization implements projects in line with the principle of Do No Harm, improving the effectiveness tailoring specific context action; the accountability responding to any unintentional negative impact created by the intervention. The efficiency is guaranteed by incorporating the Do No Harm principle encouraging local actors and community involvement on the overall steps of the suggested intervention and prevent unintended negative impacts.The proposal elaborates a remedial action to hazards that caused a change in educational needs and affects the ability of girls and boys to attend school. Which has an impact on impact on the levels of literacy, numeracy and wellbeing of the affected population. TCC and WW-GVC aim to reduce the vulnerability of the beneficiaries caused by the systematic settler and IF abuse violence creating an increase of protection risks. The core action aims to create a safe, security and inclusiveness environment in schools and realize supporting action that focus on the need for physical protection, resilience building and psychosocial-emotional protection.The Do No Harm principle is assured during all the project cycle, guaranteeing that every project- phases and steps are endorsed by the beneficiaries themselves or their families, to prevent -in so doing- any potential negative consequence. The assessment lead by WW-GVC on the H2 area in March 2019 and the needs assessment carried out for the project writing focus on the understanding of the needs of the communities specifically girls and boys. Particular attention is given to input/feedback provided by vulnerable groups such as women, whose participation is strongly encouraged to any decision-making process, and girls and boys in line with article 12 of the Convention on the Right of the Child. Specific attention will be also given to the lesson learned coming from previous similar projects of both partners. The elaboration of each single activity will take into account the different needs and investigate how girls and boys are differently affected.In the implementation phase, monitoring mechanism will guarantee direct interaction with the beneficiaries to make sure that their concerns and needs are heard and to guarantee their right to participate and be agents of change.Moreover, WeWorld-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint mechanism system, allowing beneficiaries and affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will then -if needed- influence management decisions and concrete implementation. Safety and dignity have been taken into consideration in the design of tailored activities to address the different needs and risks that girls, women, boys and men are currently facing.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project follows a sustainable humanitarian approach aiming to provide remedial and environmental-building action, ensuring equity, sustainability and impartial access to education. Impartiality principle is carried throughout the action on the basis of need alone, giving priority to the most urgent cases of distress and making no distinction on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious, belief, class or political opinion. Impartiality principle will be recognized throughout the actions' entire phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring. This is illustrated by the fact that assessments have been conducted through the same tools and methods in every school targeted, collecting the data directly from the duty-bearers as a way of accountability and transparency. On another note, schools targeted for needs assessments have been collected through trustable sources, as public data from OCHA or the Ministry of Education, avoiding random targeting. During the design phase, several schools have been assessed by TCC and WeWorld-GVC staff in coordination with the Ministry of Education, to identify the different needs and potential activities and beneficiaries. The identification of the beneficiaries and activities followed the criteria of higher needs and higher vulnerability. The selection of the 4 schools has been led by the identification of a higher level of exposition of boys, specifically teenagers, due to IF and settlers’ attacks. The girls school selected show similar threats due to the location in a hotspot area, also targeted by the IF as a way to provoke boys and family members. The proposal target girls and boys students, considered as vulnerable groups due to the high exposure to Israelis attacks while accessing to education services and schools, they are generally not safe as are exposed to the threat of tear gas and sound bombs thrown by the IF, and the risk of inspections in cases of security escalation. Children with disabilities will be also targeted by the project and their specific needs will be analysed and address. During the previous need assessment, no cases of severe disability have been identified but to guarantee the right to education for all, the activities will be implemented with an inclusive approach.
