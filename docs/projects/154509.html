Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at improving the protection environment in conflict-affected areas in Ninewa, Anbar and SAD. The project addresses protection concerns and protection-related needs of vulnerable IDPs out-of-camps, returnees and host communities. The project will be offering a variety of protection assistance services spanning protection monitoring through mobile teams, legal protection and HLP legal assistance, community-based protection, GBV response and PSS activities for children and adolescents through 5 static women’s centres established in vulnerable communities in West Mosul, Ana, Haditha and Tikrit. Overall, the project will target around 52,460 IDP, returnee and host community women, men, boys and girls in order to improve their access to protection, legal protection and HLP, GBV and PSS services.

Needs

The proposed project is an extension of the current programming Human Appeal (HA) has been implementing around protection needs in partnership with UNHCR and through IHF funding in 2018. HA has been providing access to protection services to IDPs out-of-camps, returnees and host communities/stayees in Ninewa, Salah ad-Din (SAD) and Anbar through protection monitoring and legal assistance. HA has provided protection response in hard-to-reach locations in Ninewa including Baaj, Hatra, Tal Afar, Sinjar, Sinuni, Mosul, and Qayyarah, Tikrit, Baiji, Shirqat, and Ana. Human Appeal’s team on the ground has found through needs assessments, FGDs and KIIs, that many of the families who were displaced are without male members (female or child-headed) or have protection concerns, including exploitation, human rights abuses, or having specific needs resulting from various factors including disability, age, gender, family circumstances, etc., leading to the number of persons with specialized protection needs being very high. Families living in hard-to-reach locations and recently retaken areas have reported having limited or no services, including humanitarian services and government services. In addition to these basic needs not being met, the protection environment in recently retaken areas is fragile and merit protection monitoring and protection services from humanitarian actors, as particularly vulnerable populations such as persons with disabilities or FHHs disproportionally reported being unable to support their families. According to the Multi Cluster Needs Assessment by REACH Initiative (September 2018), 40% of IDP out-of-camp households (HHs), 23% of returnee HHs, and 20% of non-displaced HHs are in need of protection assistance. Further to that, the assessment shows that 8% of IDPs out-of-camps, 8% of returnee HHs, and 6% of non displaced HHs are missing civil documentation. 7% of IDPs out-of-camps, 4% returnees, and 4% of non-displaced are at risk of eviction. 46% of IDP HHs cited shelter damage, secondary occupation or unresolved HLP ownership as the main reason for not planning to return to the places of origin. The proposed protection programming will address the needs of beneficiaries without sufficient access to protection services, including those lacking civil documentation and information on their rights and entitlements as well as HLP issues (also complementary to our proposed shelter interventions) and will ensure affected communities are able to receive the specialized assistance they require. By providing community- based protection services for particularly vulnerable individuals, the HA protection team intends to empower the affected community to strengthen and develop new community protection mechanisms. Furthermore, displaced women and children are more vulnerable to GBV and become systematic targets of exploitation, abuse, and face discrimination while accessing humanitarian assistance. Through FGDs, KIIs and SGBV assessments in Ninewa, Salah ad-Din and Anbar, including in HA-run women’s centre in West Mosul, HA’s teams have identified that women suffer a wide range of threats including domestic violence, harassment, expulsion, and have difficulties in finding employment opportunities, often forcing them into negative coping mechanisms, lack of legal assistance and civil and ownership documents, as well as suffer from psychosocial and trauma-related distress. HA will seek to address these needs through the establishment of Women’s Centres in Ninewa, Anbar and SAD, and through this static approach HA will provide safe spaces to learn, recover from trauma, obtain legal and psychosocial support, as well as provide programming for vulnerable adolescent girls and children.

Activities

Overall, protection activities will include provision of four main types of services: protection monitoring, legal assistance and legal counselling in Protection Assistance and Reintegration Centres integrated within MoDM offices as well as HLP legal assistance, Women’s Centres for GBV, PSS and livelihood/vocational activities, and protection monitoring and community-based protection programming. HA plans to provide protection responses in Mosul and hard-to-reach locations in Ninewa governorate, as well as Baiji and Tikrit in SAD, and Ana, Qaim and Haditha in Anbar governorate. Protection programming will include deploying mobile protection and legal teams into the areas of concern, identifying cases of PoC not having sufficient access to necessary protection services, including the specialized needs regarding lack of civil documentation. Through case management, counselling, referrals to specialized services, provision of safe and supportive spaces HA expects to contribute to a more favourable protection environment. Through the establishment and running of the Women’s Centres in Mosul (2), Tikrit (1), Qaim (1) and Ana (1), aiming at the achievement of EO 3, HA will aim to empower vulnerable women by building their capacity in various skills through vocational and recreational trainings, provide them with PSS, particularly to those who suffered from GBV, through case management, therapeutic activities and support groups. The centres will also extend their access to legal services and other protection services. Moreover, the centres will focus on encourage the participation of women by offering PSS activities for their children including informal education and awareness sessions, and implement youth-centred programming for adolescent girls. The programme will be able to reach around 520 children per centre. This programme overall intends to see a significant decrease of GBV cases in communities accessing the Women’s Centres, positively shift the conversation within the communities around GBV and women’s empowerment, incorporate community information, and mobilisation programme to ensure men and boys understand and are supportive of combating GBV by Engaging Men and boys through Accountable Practice (EMAP) - this will include awareness raising sessions with the wider community and training on GBV prevention and referrals to selected community members.In order to improve the protection environment in the target areas, the proposed project will implement activities around legal assistance, particularly HLP issues and community-based protection. HLP activities will include legal counselling, rights awareness raising, legal assistance to restore and facilitate the production of missing/non-existent occupancy or ownership documents and to resolve issues related to secondary occupation and facilitate compensation claims (including mediation support). Further capacity building for service providers and local authorities, and advocacy sessions with the government in order to highlight the HLP needs and issues will be conducted. Further in-depth eviction monitoring in non-camp settings where returnees households have been identified as being at risk of eviction. The HLP team will also gather and compile up-to-date technical information on relevant HLP laws, procedures, and practices operative in Iraq and respective governorates. The team will advocate for recognition of the HLP rights of displaced/returnees persons when engaging with building owners, local authorities and humanitarian partners. Moreover, Human Appeal plans to implement one mobile CBP team in hard-to-reach locations in Anbar, Salahadin and Ninewa governorates. The CBP team will focus on urban locations and areas with high protection needs such as areas highly affected by displacement and returns, and will conduct household level and community level assessments, refer individuals to specialized programmes. The CBP team will regularly conduct capacity-building trainings for protection actors as well as to establish and monitor voluntary Protection Awareness Teams (PATs) in the community. The PATs will work on the ground to assist protection actors in building comprehensive referral pathways from the community to actors, and provide key protection information to the community. The CBP programme will recruit Outreach Volunteers (OVs), train them on key protection concepts, GBV core concepts, Child Protection core concepts, how to conduct household visits, individual, and community level protection assessments, referrals and using protection tools to encourage the increased favourability of the protection environment of communities. Upon receiving these trainings, the OVs will use their skills to conduct protection activities in their communities, including conducting household visits, individual and community level assessments, complete referrals, and provide key protection messaging.

Protection Risk Analysis

The key protection risks facing beneficiaries in Ninewa, Anbar and Salah ad-Din are related to security, safety, and human rights violation as well as perceived affiliation with extremist/terrorist groups. Key risks in the project implementation are: increased lack of social cohesion due to assistance not being able to be offered to all community members (only the most vulnerable) and as the project will offer assistance to women and children with perceived extremist affiliation who might also face further abuse, exploitation and exclusion by the community. General protection and legal protection mobile teams might face hostility from the communities, security threats due to presence of ISIS sleeper cells, high contamination with explosive hazards, improvised and unexploded ordnances present in areas of interventions. Community-based protection programme might face the risk of community not willing to volunteer with the project. The WPE and CP elements to the project in the form of Women’s centres might face access risks for women and children. For example, women and children being prevented from attending and participating, geographical /logistical access or cultural limitations, particularly hostility to activities from the male members of the community. Lack of cooperation from the government departments in the targeted areas can also affect the smooth execution of the project interventions. In addition, inadequacy, low capacity or lack of specialised service providers in the areas of implementation can also hinder the provision of services to the PoCs and hinder the referrals process.

Mitigation Measures

Human Appeal has a strong element of PSEA integrated into its Code of Conduct and programming. Human Appeal Iraq has a number of measurements to report cases of sexual exploitation abuse (SEA), it is mandatory that all cases are reported. Further to that: • Human Appeal adheres to the core principles of the UN Task Force on Preventing Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises 2002. These principles apply in any project with women, men, boys and girls, irrespective of whether there is an emergency situation or not. • Sexual exploitation and abuse by project workers constitute acts of gross misconduct and are therefore grounds for the termination of employment. • Sexual activity with children is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. A mistaken belief about a child’s age is not a defense. • Exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior are prohibited. • Sexual relationships between project workers and beneficiaries are strongly discouraged since they are based on inherently unequal power dynamics. Such relationships would undermine the integrity of work to help vulnerable and excluded children. Concerns or suspicions regarding sexual abuse or exploitation by a colleague, whether in the same organization or not, must be reported in accordance with Human Appeal’s PSEA Policy. • Human Appeal Iraq follows the guidelines of the Iraq PSEA Network and abides by national laws related to mandatory reporting, while respecting the SEA principles of confidentiality, informed consent, and survivor centered approach. HA will ensure that its entire field and office staff are trained on PSEA, and that PSEA focal points of different genders and from different departments are trained and available to receive complaints and follow up. To mitigate protection risks around the WPE and CP programming community awareness/ information session will be conducted and male community mobilizers will be deployed into the targeted communities to ensure men, women and children are well informed about the services and programing offered by the Women’s centers. Men engagement programme for the GBV prevention and response will also allow the male community mobilizers to raise awareness amongst the male members of the communities regarding GBV and transform their attitudes, this will help reducing the barrier for the women to access the services. In addition to this mini buses have been included in the budget to support transport and access to the centers both for safety (decrease the chance of harassment experiences when travelling to the centers) and to ensure those who have geographical and/or financial limitations can access support services. Additionally, Human Appeal will arrange consultation meetings will be held before the initiation of the project with all the relevant stakeholders including the government departments to take them onboard. Coordination meetings will be held on frequent basis to further strengthen the relations with government departments and authorities to ensure smooth implementation of all protection programming. Community Based Protection Volunteers will be provided with small stipend on monthly basis keeping in view the fact that most of the people in the targeted areas are either unemployed or have extremely low income. The small stipend will not only help them manage their expenses like transportation or communication while performing their job responsibilities but will also keep them motivated and ensure their commitment. Human Appeal has strong inter-department referral mechanism to provide services like shelter, livelihoods, legal assistance, case management services and child protection services through Orphan Sponsorship Programme. Unavailability of services providers in the proposed locations will be mitigated through internal referral mechanism.

How is your project accountable to affected population

HA takes the accountability to affected populations (AAP) as a fundamental principle of its programming. HA adheres to the age, gender and diversity policy of UNHCR and treats it as a general approach throughout its programming which also enables the implementation and adherence to HA's commitment to upholding IASC's commitments on AAP and our commitment to the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) which stress the participatory approach and a strong beneficiary feedback mechanism. HA has a dedicated MEAL department which implements activities to strengthen the quality of our programming, increase the transparency and accountability to donors and POCs we serve. HA’s MEAL team established an effective beneficiary feedback mechanism, which includes complaints and feedback boxes, the nation-wide hotline with the number displayed on all information dissemination materials, and the IDP Call Center (nationwide, UNOPS established AAP mechanism). Through this mechanism, we gather views, feedback and complaints of the affected populations in addition to regular participatory assessments, needs assessments, feedback and complaints mechanism, FGDs, KIIs, etc. Project performance and activities will be monitored both qualitatively and quantitatively throughout the project period utilizing a result-based monitoring system structured to consider the overall objective, outcome, outputs and the deliverables. A sample of beneficiaries will be visited and interviewed, and MEAL team will regularly perform random satisfaction surveys by the dedicated Accountability Officer. The lessons learnt are utilized by programmes teams to improve the quality of the programming and promptly respond to community-needs making our interventions relevant, timely, and accurate.
