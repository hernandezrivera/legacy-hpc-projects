Project Summary

Renk is one of the counties in Upper Nile State, located in the northern part of South Sudan and bordering Sudan to the Northern direction. The county is accessible through different routings; by air, by road from Palouch to Renk, Maban to Renk and from Melut to Kaka and then to Renk via the water means. The county is made up of 7 Payams and has an estimated population of about 194,155 persons according to South Sudan IPC report released in October 2022. Trends of global acute malnutrition (GAM) at the location have been consistently above the emergency threshold even before the onset of the Sudan crisis. The onset of war in Khartoum, Sudan led to the movement of people fleeing war to neighboring countries. The northern counties of South Sudan in receiving a share of the returnees and refugees from Sudan. According to the UNHCR/IOM database as of November 25th 2024, there are around 703,912 Refugees who have arrived Renk County and are in need of Humanitarian Assistance. The people fleeing the war include South Sudanese refugees who are now returnees, Sudanese citizens, and other nationalities like Ethiopians and Eritreans. The influx of people into Renk created a humanitarian crisis and lead to emergency teams from various humanitarian actors including Malteser International to be deployed. Apart from the crisis from the Sudan Conflict, The Ministry of health in October 2024 declared an outbreak of Cholera disease in the County which increases the needs for the provision of the health services in this region. Therefore, this project aims to provide lifesaving essential comprehensive health care services to the crisis affected people in Renk County of Upper Nile state through supply of drugs and medical equipment's, rehabilitation of existing health infrastructure, child immunization, health education and treatment of the most communicable diseases. The intervention will target 99,013 people including men, women and children from the 4 payams of Renk, Geger,Jalhak and Shomedi of Renk County.

1. Needs

South Sudan is facing a dire health crisis due to a lack of adequate health services and infrastructure. The country relies on international humanitarian assistance to provide basic health care, but this is not enough to address the multiple and complex health emergencies facing the country. Floods, food insecurity, conflict and disease outbreaks have worsened the health situation and increased the vulnerability of the population, especially women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities. The interagency need assessment report conducted in Geger payam of Renk County on 1st November 2024 highlighted a huge humanitarian needs to support the people affected by crisis in Renk County. Most of the needs identified during the assessment was, Shortages of Essential Medicines and Equipment, Inadequate Immunization and Preventive Supplies, Insufficient Sanitation Facilities and Community Outreach. Malnutrition was identified to be at 16% which is above the emergency level of 15%. This is backup by the IPC scale, where Renk in Upper Nile state was classified as IPC AMN Phase 5 which is at critical need for Humanitarian Aids.According to the UNHCR/IOM database as of November 25th, 2024, there are around 703,912 Refugees who have arrived Renk County and are in dire need of Humanitarian Assistance. The people fleeing the war include South Sudanese refugees who are now returnees, Sudanese citizens, and other nationalities like Ethiopians and Eritreans. The influx of people into Renk created a humanitarian crisis and lead to emergency teams from various humanitarian actors including Malteser International to be deployed. Apart from the crisis from the Sudan Conflict, The Minstry of health in October 2024 declared an outbreak of Cholera in Renk County which increases the needs for the provision of the health services in this region. Therefore, this project aims to provide lifesaving essential comprehensive health care services to the crisis affected people in Renk County of Upper Nile state. IMPLEMENTATION STRATEGY:Malteser International plan to implement static health implementation strategies where it will support 1 hospital and 1 PHCU through supply of drugs and medical equipment, rehabilitation of existing health infrastructure, rehabilitation of existing WASH facilities in the health centers, child immunization, maternal health, health education and treatment of the most communicable diseases. it will also implement community health programs in 2 payams of Renk County where it will focus on conducting health and Hygiene awareness, child immunization services, referral of pregnant and lactating mothers to the health facilities. The intervention will target 99,013 people including men, women and children from the 4 payams of Renk, Geger,Jalhak and Shomedi of Renk County

2. Project activities

Building on past and current experiences in the health sector, especially mental health, and with the support of Malteser International's Global Mental Health Advisor, Malteser International is currently implementing mental health and psychosocial support services in 4 PHCUs and Yei Hospital in Yei River County. Therefore, this response will provide quality health services to the affected population of Renk County including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, general and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and gender-based violence (GBV). The response will also improve sexual and reproductive health services in EmONC (Emergency Newborn and Obstetric Care), family planning, PMTCT (Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission), clinical management of rape and supplies for vulnerable women and girls. The response will use referral mechanisms to improve efficiency, integration and quality of care. The response will follow the principles of universal health coverage and priorities prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation for vulnerable groups. The response will also continue Cholera and HEV Disease prevention, preparedness and response activities as Renk County faces the threat of Cholera and other viral hemorrhagic fevers. The response will support health facilities and community-based systems to care for people in need. The response will also strengthen technical, operational and field coordination with partners and authorities. Finally, the response will also support the disease surveillance and reporting system and pre-position medical supplies and equipment in Renk County.
