Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh has proposed to conduct Shelter/NFI program with concrete planning of 2023 and tentative planning of 2024 & 2025.Total target in camps is 15,707 HHs (78,841 Individuals) including camp 4 (500 HHs), Camp 4 Ext. (150 HHs), Camp 5 (500 HHs), Camp 13 (8,910 HHs), Camp 17 (500 HHs), Camp 19 (5,147 HH) as well as in the host community 300 HHs (Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong and Raja Palong union). The activities are proposed based on the identified needs and gaps in the J-MSNA, shelter standard assessment report and internal monitoring and needs assessment conducted by CB. The activities proposed in JRP 2023 entail emergency NFI (500HHs), RDV (2,530 HHs), TDK (14,057 HHs) for emergency situation and HH level SI (5,600 HHs), New MTS (400HHs), NFI (2,100HHs), regular shelter reinforcement (TSA, SUM and/or R&M) (7,500 HHs), R&M of MTS (500HHs) and solar lamps distribution/repair (200 HHs) in the camp and Shelter Repair (300HHs) in the host community.This year CB is constructing few MTS in Camp 17 and few other partners previously constructed MTS needed some repair by CB.Moreover,CB did not considered regular shelter assistance at block A,B,C of camp 13 because this year CB is providing SUM in this three blocks.This 3 blocks will receive regular shelter assistance in FY24 again.
Emergency NFI support, RDV shelter materials, relocation shelter construction, and emergency TDK will be ensured.Materials will be collected from IOM and distributed with technical support for all the HHs. Labor support to the HwSN will be provided by Caritas in the IOM operated camps.Assignment of catchment area/camp focal point is included for all the targeted camps for emergency support and shelter/protection related referral services. In all the activities, persons with disabilities and extremely vulnerable individuals will receive porter and construction support.Community-based approach for each intervention will be carried out whether male, female, adolescent girls, boys, elderly aged peoples, person with disabilities will be engaged all through the program cycle and women & elderly aged people will be engaged as daily labor with light weight work.Inclusion of women in gender-segregated training, and recruitment women volunteers will be ensured.For shelter and site improvement activities, reuse and recycle of the used materials will be emphasized and participants will be sensitized.Also, treated bamboo from IOM pipeline and UNHCR will be used to increase the durability of materials. Potential use of alternative materials will also be explored in the process. The overall program will include COVID-19 safety,PSEA,child protection/safeguarding,complain/feedback mechanism,enhancing Protection/gender Mainstreaming for all Shelter/NFI activities.Technical support for design and implementation of specific accessibility and provision of additional materials for persons with disability and LGBTQ people which will be coordinated with ADWG/Protection sector. CRS will provide all kinds of technical support to Caritas throughout the project. Overall aim of the program is to achieve more durable, safe, secure, dignified, and green environment focused shelter Program through capacity building of individuals, community-based approach, social cohesion.
The shelter/NFI program will follow a participatory and integrated approach with maximum community engagement in all phases(assessment,decision making,implementation and monitoring).Priority will be given to female headed households, elderly, HHs with special needs and persons with disabilities in both shelter and CFW program. CB will ensure effective coordination and communication with Shelter/NFI sector,CiC,RRRC,CMO,DC Office,Site Management,UNO Office,UP and other humanitarian actors. CB will also actively participate in SNFI sector coordination meeting, CwC, TWiG, SAG, CiC coordination meeting and submit the corresponding reports (i.e. 4W, monthly report, JRP financial updates, donor reports etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Five years after the Rohingya influx and settlement in congested camps in Cox’s Bazar district, the gaps and needs of shelter, NFI and site improvement still remain the almost the same due to use of non-durable and fragile materials, the shelters in the camps remain especially vulnerable to natural hazards. Cox’s Bazar is already a high-risk area for both the small, medium and large-scale weather events like cyclone, flooding, landslide, heavy rain, heavy wind etc. whereas fire risk also very vital. The J-MSNA published in August 2021 revealed that 74% of the refugees prioritized their need for shelter materials over food and self-reliance activities. The major reason behind this is the reduced shelter programming in both 2020 and 2021 in the face of COVID-19 pandemic and corresponding lockdowns which resulted in the need for increased shelter assistance and household level site improvement activities by shelter partners. The following years, the need for increased budget to provide repairs and maintenance of shelters became prominent. Furthermore, in 2022, around 30% of refugees responded that they had not received any kinds of non-emergency shelter assistance since 2020-2021 due to COVID-19 indicated from the 4W data from Shelter/NFI sector. lack of regular shelter reinforcement in 2020 and 2021 prior to monsoon led to significant increased shelter damages and increased in the emergency response as shelters were not prepared and temporary nature materials were not replaced. Due to use of untreated bamboo and tarpaulin, more than 56% of the previously constructed MTS need to repair to ensure peoples safety and security. Moreover, 66% (J-MSNA 2021) of refugees reported as insufficient household-level lighting and The 2022 Shelter/NFI Sector assessment showed that the average number of portable solar lamps per household is 0,35, which is far below the minimum standard which is especially concerning regarding Protection issues. Household level site improvement activities like drainage,retention wall, pathways, stair etc. also needed as previously mostly non-durable materials was used.However, the GoB has restricted the shelter assistance to non-durable materials like bamboo, tarpaulin and ropes requiring the need for annual replacement of materials due to their limited lifespan and exposure to flood, landslide, fire and cyclone risks. To slow down the deterioration of rate of bamboo and reduce the need for replacement, the procedures for bamboo treatment were put in place by the UN & INGOs/NGOs. There is also a need for exploring alternative materials for shelter construction which was previously explored by SNFI Sector, Caritas Bangladesh, CRS and ICDDR,B for use of JUTIN sheets. Besides, use of steel posts, beams, CGI sheets, brick lower walls, mud plastering etc. was also explored by different partners of shelter/NFI sector. Furthermore, the repurposing and recycling of shelter materials is essential to reduce wastage and maximization of resources. Coordination across different sectors is needed to improve such as Energy & Environment, WASH and Shelter TWiG/coordination meeting by the shelter partners. To complement all shelter activities, micro-level site planning for new shelters and HH level site improvement is necessary for reducing the additional risks faced by the shelters. Conversely, the need for NFI items has changed over the years as a number of items are already possessed by the Rohingya community. Priority for items include solar fan, mosquito net, kitchen set, portable solar light etc. Shelters in host community also face significant risks to damages due to above-mentioned hazards as 9% of households live in Jhupri shelters of which 71% HHs reported having issues with their shelters specially roofing. Primary need for support in host community include shelter re-construction or repair, training of build back safer shelter repair and maintenance, household level lighting etc.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Caritas Bangladesh has already taken steps towards rationalization since 2018 through working in camp 4 in different sectors like shelter, site improvement, protection, and WASH ensuring multiyear and multi sectoral program design with the support of their constant donors. In proposed activities of 2023, CB will also promote principles of rationalization while working in camp 4, 4Ext, 5, 13, 17 and 19 (complementing UNHCR activities like MTS, MTS repair, existing shelter & maintenance etc. in camp 4, 4Ext., 5 & 17 with CB’s other donors’ funds). This process enables a common strategy and process, proper coordination & communication, cost sharing, minimization of operation costs, and maximization of resources. CB always participate in camp-level, Upazila level, District level coordination platforms and maintain smooth relationship with all stakeholders including targeted hhs for the camp/HC ensuring transparency of data and alignment of strategies of both parties. CB is an active member of the Cox’s Bazar PSEA Network and have appointed PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points. for each sector, the sectoral standard package of services will be ensured reducing the identified gaps with avoiding overlapping by proper approval in a timely manner. The process will be systematic, accountable, and transparent and carried out in collaboration with different sectors, CIC, RRRC, UNO, DC, ISCG, UN agencies, international and national organizations. Subsequently, partnership and relationship between humanitarian community and GoB.In this regard, activities of both camp level and host community level will also be conducted under the comprehensive strategy to maximize the impacts of rationalization. The operating principles will include consultation with relevant sectors, complying with the minimum standards, adopting an integrated approach, ensure continuity, flexibility and predictability, timely response and transparency of information shared with the partners.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection mainstreaming will be ensured through participation and accountability to affected populations, meaningful access, safety, privacy and security throughout the Project cycle. The targeted HHs will be selected with specific criteria based on protection needs, assessment, referrals from protection sector as well as concerns raised in CFRM platform will be prioritized. Confidentiality of data will ensured following the protection principles of humanitarian response. CB will also coordinate with PSEA network, CPSS, GBVSS and protection sector to receive and follow guidance and instructions in time as CB already has an PSEA , child protection & Safeguarding focal all the guidelines and policies must be followed by all the employees. Transparency and accountability towards beneficiaries will also be a high priority. Needs of vulnerable groups like EVIs, FHHs, HHwSN, LGBTQ will be addressed. Stair with handrails ,ramps, partition wall etc. will be ensured for the related HHs.

3.2 - Gender

CB will ensure gender mainstreaming in the projects through inclusion of women in gender-segregated trainings, recruitment of women volunteers, engaging women in CFW and providing a safe space for expressing their needs and concerns. Women participation in training will be ensured by engaging one women from each HH and facilitator will be gender based. Needs of vulnerable groups will also be addressed in adapting the modality of program (porter/labor support,breastfeeding corner,separate queues etc.)with a view to achieving safe, secure, and dignified shelter, sites and access.The activities will be culturally appropriate and following do-no-harm approach. Coordination with GiHA,different sectors of ISCG and UN agencies will also be ensured in the process. CB will Ensure equal and inclusive access to shelter and NFI that addresses the specific needs of women, girls,men,boys and other at-risk populations during any assessment,FGD,implementation, monitoring phase and in decision making.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

CB will not engage any child labor (i.e. below 18 years old) and promote child rights for activities in camps and HC. Child safety and security will be ensured in construction and repair works. CB will ensure labour/portar for pregnant & women with child, elderly etc. and will attend in any meeting,training,assessment ,implementation & decision making. Protection team and site management will work together to ensure safe haven for unaccompanied children if found during construction. Also, during distribution and training, CB will provide a children playing space for ensuring their safety. Also, during technical assistance and material selection, CB shelter team will ensure that children are not exposed to any potential harm. All staffs and volunteers are trained in child safeguarding, child protection, PSEA and code of conduct to reduce risks. Construction and porter labors age for CFW engagement is between 18-59 years old, but 59+ can engage in light work if he/she has fitness.

3.4 - Diversity

CB promotes equal opportunities to all groups in the activities to be conducted in the camps and host community.Discrimination against all groups will be avoided by maintaining transparency and accountability in information sharing and decision making. CB will use Sex, Age and Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) to identify the specific needs different groups. For meaningful participation of the all the groups in the community, specific arrangements will be ensured. Also, their opinions will be taken in the decision-making process community sensitization,messaging,involvement of the needs and preference of elderly people and adolescents will be ensured. Improvement of access to all services for persons with disability through project activities and referrals will also take place. Coordination with Age & Disability Working Group (ADWG) will be ensured through meetings,capacity building etc.CB will also put inclusive budget addressing the specific needs of persons with disabilities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CB will ensure accountability to affected populations and all other stakeholders in the program implementation process. CHS principles will be followed in all the activities and program plan will be shared with the community and inception will be done before starting of project activities. In CB's community-based approach, community is involved in needs assessment, project planning, implementation, monitoring and sustainability of the project. CB will also follow internal and camp level Complain feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) process through complain/feedback box, hotline number, help desk etc. to collect and timely address the complaints and feedback. CB will share back result message to Rohingya & HC beneficiaries. For referral from Site management/Protection team, CB will respond with addressed/verified results to them through coordination after verification by CFRM & Safeguarding team. Community people will be aware about CFRM process ensured by CB MEAL team.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

CB follows the DRR elements and strategies to all of its shelter and site improvement activities. Shelters are constructed and maintained to ensure that they are able to withstand moderate level adverse weather conditions.The site improvement activities like retention walls, stairs, pathway, drainage etc. are directed towards reducing the physical risks to frequently occurring natural physical risks(i.e. landslide, flooding, heavy wind etc).CB conducts participatory hazard risk assessments with the community that also takes into account the risk perception of the community.In host community activities,the National Disaster Management Plan of GoB is aligned with the activities and coordination with local level Disaster Management Committees (DMCs) is ensured.shelter support is ensured as per HHs Risk Reduction Action Plan and DRR awareness is inbuilt in any training.For ensuring environmental sustainability,use of locally available materials is used & include zero carbon emission.
