Project Summary

Due to protracted conflict and effects of climate changes, most of the South Sudan, returnees and refugees’ population have been pushed into suffering, creating a reliance on humanitarian aid. STEWARDWOMEN therefore intends to implement a proposed 12 months project to improve provision of critical life-saving and specialized protection services through addressing general protection, GBV and emergency protection concerns of returnees, host communities and refugees in Malual North Payam in Aweil North County (Northern Bahr Ghazal), Roriak-Rubkona Payam in Rubkona County (Unity State) and Raga Payam in Raja County (Western Bahr Ghazal) in alignment with the strategic objectives of the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP).
The project will cost $885,000 i.e ($450,000 for GP & $435,000 for GBV) and will be implemented in Rubkona with severity level 4; Aweil North with severity level 4 and Raja County with severity level 3. The critical protection services will provide general protection to target groups through protection monitoring with KI Interviews at payam levels; protection awareness raising messaging among persons with protection concerns or identified needs; identify and refer them for services through static or mobile means; conduct individual cash-based protection interventions intended for durable solutions including legal aid, access to documentation, reintegration and other protection support.
GBV activities will include awareness-raising sessions on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting; GBV prevention initiatives particularly community cares programming; GBV case management; training of staff/frontline workers on various GBViE minimum standards; establishing functional GBV referral pathways in counties; provision of referral services; provision of legal aid services; renovate WGFs; provide individual and group psychosocial support including reactional activities; provide women and adolescent girls with dignity kits; support cash and voucher assistance; training and supporting women and girls with Fuel Efficient Stoves; non-GBV humanitarian workers trained on GBV integration in their sectoral response.
The overall target for protection cluster is 5.53million, out of which the project will target 76,600 beneficiaries where 17,500 will benefit from GP and 7500 will benefit from GBV and 51,600 will benefit from the emergency interventions, comprising of 16,285 men, 32,080 women, 9,580 boys and 18,655 girls and 2593 persons with disabilities. Of the 76.600 target IDPs will comprise of 15,320, HC will be 38,300 and returnees will be 22,980.
STW has presence in Rubkona, Aweil North and recently signed a contract to implement GBV programming in Raja for 3 years. In Rubkona STW has an existing WGFs and provides case management and PSS activities in Bentiu town. The legal aid officer further supports the legal aid components in Rubkona. In Aweil North, STW is implementing multi sectoral protection, FSL & NFI activities until May 2024 with a target of 2500 beneficiaries. The proposed intervention will provide critical protection and GBV services with increased targets among returnees, host communities and refugees including persons with disabilities (PWDs).
STEWARDWOEMN is a vibrant woman led organization and a very active member of the protection and GBV AoRs. STEWARDWOMEN also chairs the rule of law technical reference group of the GBV AoR and is an advisory board member of the SSHF.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges and climatic shocks. Protection concerns remain high, especially for women and girls. The humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 9.4 million people will need humanitarian assistance in 2024. The projected number includes 1.9 million IDPs, 1.4 million returnees, 5.8 million host community/non-displaced people and 337,000 refugees. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. Nearly 15 per cent of the total people in need are PWD. In Rubkona County, the local authorities said, there are about 58,772 individuals in the host community/IDPs and per DRC Camp Management, there is an estimated 5,000 returnees in the Rotriak-Rubkona Payam-Rubkona County (Coordinated GBV Safety Audit, July 2023). Further, returnees in Roriak complained of lack lights in the areas of settlement which heightened the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV risks and can restrict their movement. Returnees further expressed the feeling of discrimination from the host community when they are collecting water from the waterpoints. Fighting amongst the women at water collection points; women and girls move very far for firewood collection in the forest/bush. Rape, physical assault and robbery are the GBV risks women and girls are facing during firewood collection and cultivation. Teenage pregnancies are on increase which can be attributed to lack of information and many cases of unaccompanied and separated children, many of whom have resorted to early marriage. In Raja County, the UN OCHA estimates that 34,200 out of 59,638 of the population have humanitarian needs, over half of them are returnees fleeing the conflict from Sudan and have been registered in Raja County (UNHCR/IOM2023). The report of an assessment conducted by STEWARDWOMEN in Raja County in October 2023 further indicates that domestic violence, teenage pregnancy, rape cases are high. It is also very common practice for boys to waylay a girl for 1 boy, beat her up and allow her lover to rape her as a form of courtship rape. Sexual exploitation by men working in the NGOs is common. Women hardly get jobs from male directors unless they offer sex in exchange. Teachers are sexually exploiting girls in schools leading to teenage pregnancies and many school drop outs. Poverty and harmful cultural and gender norms continue to perpetuate GBV. There is no WGFs and no partner providing legal aid services in Raja. There is no statutory court/judiciary in Raja. The available court is “C” court and handles GBV cases. In Aweil North County, the interagency assessment conducted in May 2023 noted that refugees are staying near the military barracks and this may expose women to the risk of rape, child marriage and all other form of exploitation; congestion in the camp with no social distances remain a big threat; there was large presence of orphans, widows, pregnant mothers, separated children and elderly; people are psychologically affected since they have nothing left, levels of stress are high among women, elderly, and children due to lack of food, shelter; 62 children are reported missing with 47 boys and 15 girls; further 8 girls were abducted; some refugees are not willing to be reallocated to new areas; issues of exploitation and sexual harassment by cattle harder’s was reported;; the social cohesion between the host community, returnees and refugees is complicated due to cultural differences.

2. Project activities

1. STW will provide general protection services through protection awareness raising messaging among women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns or identified needs; identify and refer them for services through static or mobile means; conduct individual cash-based protection interventions among women, men, girls and boys intended for durable solutions including legal aid, access to documentation, reintegration and other protection support; establish community based protection structures with community leaders; conduct community-based protection activities including resilience and PSS activities, PSEA, peace-building activities and trainings through AGD approaches. These activities are aimed at ensuring vulnerable crises affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs and vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action informed by communities’ priorities in line with SO1 & SO2 (GP).2. STW will implement GBV activities through awareness-raising sessions conducted with women, men, boys and girls on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting; women, men, girls and boys reached through GBV prevention initiatives particularly community cares programming; GBV case management to women, men, boys and girls; training of staff/frontline workers on various GBViE minimum standards; establishing functional GBV referral pathways in counties; provision of referral services to women, men, boys and girls; provision of legal aid services to women, men, boys and girls; renovate WGFs; provide individual and group Psychosocial support including reactional activities to women, men, boys and girls; provide women and adolescent girls with dignity kits; support cash and voucher assistance; training and supporting women and girls with Fuel Efficient Stoves; Training of non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral response. The GBV activities will further benefit 1500 IDPs, 3750 host communities and 2250 returnees. These activities are in line with SO1, SO2 & SO3 (GBV). 3. STW will further supported the emergency component of the project through monitoring the arrival to returnees and refugees at the entry points to South Sudan; conduct protection awareness raising messaging to women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns or specific needs; identify and provide referrals through static or mobile means to women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns or specific needs; conduct protection interventions intended for durable solutions (other than CP, HLP and GBV) including legal aid, access to documentation, reintegration and other protection support that can also be cash-based to women, men, girls and boys among others.
