Project Summary

IOM's 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) project for health will contribute to improved equitable access and utilization of emergency and essential lifesaving primary healthcare services among the most vulnerable and crisis-affected populations including IDPs, migrants, and non-IDP host population in the targeted locations in the country. IOM will support operation of 33 fixed health care facilities with integrated community outreach, and expand 18 mobile teams in targeted areas, many of which are in hard-to-reach, remote, or conflict-affected areas with limited or no health actors and thus the population does not have access to basic life-saving health services. The project will benefit directly 596,700 individuals (51% female; 49% males), including 361,535 children under 18 years in the areas of implementation. Health services will be provided in line with the national Essential Package of Health Services (EPHS) framework for service delivery. IOM will strengthen the capacity of health workers to ensure delivery of high-quality medical service through provision of trainings on relevant health topics including outbreak response, and national treatment guidelines among other. The project will also support basic infrastructure rehabilitation and renovation to existing health facilities to ensure HF functionality and safe environment. IOM will also preposition essential medicines, medical supplies in particular for common outbreaks such as AWD/Cholera and measles to ensure uninterrupted supply. Medical equipment, PPEs and IPC supplies will also be prepositioned for swift response in the event of an emergency. To mitigate against recurrent and expected shocks and the effects of continued conflict, IOM will conduct mass vaccination at the entry points to IDP sites, thus increasing immunization coverage and reducing outbreaks of communicable diseases. IOM will also facilitate deployment of rapid response teams at key points of entry to enhance surveillance at points of entry through screening and referral in the event of a public health emergency especially one of international concern. IOM will support provision of maternal health care and newborn care including safe deliveries, emergency obstetric (BEmONC and CeMOC), and sexual and reproductive health services (including management of STD / HIV treatment and SGBV particularly in geographic areas where there are no other health facilities for safe deliveries. IOM will provide health education at the health facilities and during mobile and outreach sessions in the catchment communities to raise awareness and enhance behavior change in health and hygiene practices that contribute to excess morbidity and mortality, and to increase demand and utilization of the emergency and life saving health services level. IOM will integrate mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) at facility level and the community (in IDP settlements and non -IDP surrounding host communities). The range of MHPSS activities will aim to improve the local capacity response to meet the MHPSS needs of Somalia’s crisis-affected populations by building the capacity and skillset of first responders and community gatekeepers. The project will establish and support referral pathways including at the community level through the use of trained community volunteers and Community Health Workers (CHWs). This project will be part of the wider IOM’s integrated emergency response program.
Staff and office costs associated with implementing a project are allocated through projectization, which allows for proportionate allocation of staff and office costs to projects based on the amount of time staff spend working on these projects and to ensure staff have the tools they need to ensure they can fulfil their responsibilities (see budget - International staff costs, National staff costs, Office costs).

Project Activities

IOM proposes to implement the following activities across Jubaland, Southwest State, Hirshebelle, Puntland, Somaliland, and Banadir: Activity 1: Operate 33 fixed health facilities with integrated community outreach, and 18 mobile mobile teams in response to acute emergencies and to larger, hard-to reach areas with dispersed populations. The HFs and mobile outreach clinics will provide emergency and essential lifesaving primary healthcare (PHC) services in crisis-affected communities, prioritizing hard-to-reach, newly liberated areas or underserved regions which lack consistent access to primary healthcare services. IOM will also support rehabilitation and renovation of health facilities infrastructure including entrance rumps (where there is need) to facilitate and improve access to facility services for people with reduced mobility and vulnerable population including elderly persons.Activity 2: Preposition essential medicines, medical supplies and equipment including PPEs and IPC supplies in 6 strategically located regional hubs which will supply all of IOM’s PHC service delivery sites (33 fixed/static health facilities, with integrated outreach services and 18 mobile clinics). The regional hubs will always carry minimum buffer stock of three months, enabling immediate and fluid response to health emergencies in any of the targeted regions.Activity 3: Support fixed health facilities to have and provide Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BeMONC) especially in locations lacking maternity and delivery services in target locations.Activity 4: Conduct training and capacity building for health workers (207 including 106 women and 101 men) on disease outbreak response, national treatment guidelines, SGBV, IPC, trauma and emergency management, maternal child healthcare, immunization, obstetric care, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and referral mechanism, travel health, and health promotion and education approaches. Activity 5: Provide health education at fixed health facilities, and mobile outreach services at the community level to 367,200 individuals to effect behavior change in health and hygiene practices that contribute to excess morbidity and mortality, and to increase demand for preventative services (at fixed/ static HFs and mobile clinics, outreaches services, targeted campaigns).Activity 6: Conduct various trainings and supportive supervision on MHPSS (basic PSS skills, SGBV referrals, etc.) for 150 primary health care workers (76 women, 74 men) at 10 static health facilities and mobile outreach clinics; and 50 essential community volunteers /workers (25 women and 25 men; to include protection actors, community leaders, etc.); to mainstream MHPSS, raise awareness on importance of appropriate diagnosis, care and treatment of people with mental illness accessing primary healthcare services at health facilities in IDP settlements and to promote health-seeking behaviors. Activity 7: Provide mass vaccination of IDP children arriving at entry points at 6 large IDP sites (2 in Dollow, 2 in Baidoa, 2 in Kismayo) to increase immunization coverage among IDP populations and help mitigate against communicable disease outbreaks. Children missing immunizations will be vaccinated at the entry point to the IDP sites and linked to the nearest fixed health facility for follow-up and completion of immunization series. Activity 8: Support travel health services including provision of health screening transit healthcare and fitness to travel for emergency movement of 10,000 displaced and IDP returnees (3,500 girls, 3000 boys, 2000 women and 1500 men).

Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance:i. IOM will gather data (age, sex, diversities) of communities and beneficiaries for effective and informative programing.This will help identify vulnerable and marginalized persons for planning, inclusion purposes and engagement. ii. A systematic outreach process will be done to reach marginalized/minority vulnerable groups with health promotion to avoid exclusion. iii. Efforts will be made to ensure that information regarding provision of health services will be disseminated to all members of communities (including women, girls, boys, men) through appropriate channel of communication such as radio.. iv. An inclusive feedback mechanism that will include all beneficiaries will be put in place which help evaluate selection criteria and overall services gaps. v. In all health service delivery points, a mechanism will be established that will give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time. Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes: i. Women, girls and boys will be consulted during programme design to gather accurate information about their specific health needs and preferences for location and design of health clinical and non-clinical services. Issues of privacy, confidentiality and access to persons with disability will be discussed and considered during project design and implementaation. ii. Community health committees (CHCs) will be comprised of minimum 55% females, including young women. iii. Efforts will be made to ensure that women are included as healthy and hygienepromoters so that they actively contribute to the overall program implementation atcommunity level. iv. The project will hire female staff members at project locations to ensure that women and girls will feel more comfortable talking to them and this will also provide a feedback mechanism.v. Girls and boys will actively be engaged in children health hygiene promotion groups/campaigns. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance:i. Health service delivery locations (static facilities and mobile units) will be put in central, visible places and not more 500 meters away from settlements, and ideally within settlement areas. This will help protect women and girls from risks of sexual violence/exploitation when accessing services. ii. Separate toilet facilities with internal locks and secondary enclosures will be in place for women and men in health facilities with clear labels such pictorial/signs to ensure that they are used by intended gender to promote safe, confidential access.iii. In setups where threats are anticipated frequency of health and hygiene kits distribution will be modified and door-to-door distribution will be considered, to avoid sexual abuse and extortion while carrying health and hygiene kits.iv. Coordination will be done with Shelter/NFI partners to put solar light installations at health centers to deter any risk of violence against women and girls v. Community outreach medical and promotive services will be done during daylight hours to allow beneficiaries who are often women and girls to reach home early. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. IOM will support establishment of feedback mechanism. A hotline number will be provided which beneficiaries can call in to provide complaints or feedback. It will be posted at strategic and easily visible locations at the health facilities. Directions will be printed in the local language and made easily discernable using infographics for those who cannot read.

Please provide further explanation

This project leverages on existing government and community structures. It will be implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Health and will utilize Ministry of Health workforce. The project is based on national humanitarian and development response frameworks, and includes community engagement and participation throughout the project process including accountability and transparency to affected populations This project has inbuilt sustainability as primary healthcare services are implemented through Ministry of Health staff, building their longterm capacity and in the long-run enabling full government ownership/operation of the clinics, and reduction of donor dependency. The entitlement of beneficiaries to health and related services in this project is generally legally recognized in government-controlled areas, but not necessarily for all groups of population (e.g. migrants, refugees).
