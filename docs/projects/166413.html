Project Summary

"CAID with its partners realigned the activities for focusing COVID 19. The project will try to mitigate the risk of spreading at community level by disseminating messages. The project will disseminate the messages in two forms one will be printed and the other one will be audio/video. The project will disseminate printed messages during IPC and community consultation focusing on women, disable and elder person. The project will also play audio messages at the health service facilities.
CAID through its partners will engage the religious leaders i.e. Imams who will disseminate the messages during daily prayers and Friday khutba. Imams are targeted because they are influential leaders of Rohingya community, and community has strong faith on them. Imams can work for alleviating social stigma and influence the community in reducing COVID 19 risk. In addition, the project will track rumours about COVID 19 through COMPASS (an automated system). These will help to manage the programme or projects and will assist in realigning or reprioritising the activities for dealing with such ill information.
Also, the project will disseminate audio messages from mobile loudspeaker or hand mike. The project will engage volunteers for disseminating messages through WhatsApp and promote hotline number to collect feedback and provide feedback where 3G or 4G signals available, because in worst case scenario it may appear hard to reach the community physically. In those situation WhatsApp messaging could work effectively. And the Rohingya volunteers will disseminate the messages to their neighbours. The response is targeting both Rohingya and host communities, so both the communities will get benefit out of it. The response plan will continue through out the cyclone and monsoon season for DRR purpose. Mutual benefit will ensure social cohesion among the communities. "

Needs

"To ensure key messages are disseminated and reach all, partners need to engage with various groups including religious leaders and existing camp governance structures and non-formal structures through small community consultations to compliment on going mass massaging on hygiene to mitigate the risk of spreading. Continuous technical inputs will be needed on developing messages and contents of various issues including isolation and quarantine and mitigating rumours through rumour tracking mechanisms. Engagement with local government, social and religious leaders, youth and women groups as well as dissemination of basic hygiene and health messages is a primary need for COVID-19 in host community. Strengthened capacity will lead to mainstreaming of Accountability in COVID 19 response and activities, ensuring that the communities are actively involved and at the centre of every intervention, and their needs and contributions are listened to addressed. There is a need to continuously engage the women, men, elderly, boys and girls and know their perception on the COVID 19 and identify the gaps of to modify the response. Tailor messages and make them actionable for particular living conditions (including assisted living facilities), and health status. Engage the elderly to address their specific feedback with appropriate messages to explain the risk for elderly and how to care for them, especially in homecare with the help of family members, health care providers and caregivers.Limited access to timely and lifesaving information, especially for women, girls, and elderly in camps and host community, due to cultural movement restrictions. In some cases, whilst information is available, literacy levels and those living with Special Needs must be factored in. There is need to disseminate audio/video and pictorial print information (for display) to be provided in various ways, relevant to the community context. Women and girls need to be provided with information in settings culturally accessible to them.Focused and strengthened technical support need to be provided to all sectors, especially to those involved in Health and WASH activities to ensure that activities on the ground are in line with the community needs specially the needs of vulnerable population (elderly, people with pre-existing health concerns and people with special needs) and as part of accountability to affected populations. To engage host community also to ensure rumours and gaps are addressed and correct messages are disseminated through mass messaging tools and interpersonal communication channels. Both traditional and non-traditional forms of communication are needed to support the communication measures undertaken by the Government.Growing physical limitations in regard to movement in the camps, as well as poor phone/internet network is posing as a massive threat to information dissemination and limited humanitarian presence means that this will become even bigger concern in the coming weeks. Upcoming monsoon and cyclone season pose an additional threat to information dissemination in the camps, engaging the communities through various innovative ways, such as audio-video messages to ensure refugees are aware and well informed should cyclone signal be raised. "

Activities or outputs

Capacity Development: Conduct training for staff/volunteers for improving their capacity on communication skill during COVID response. Community Engagement: • Disseminate the messages with printed IEC material through inter-personal communication or small group community session. Disseminate the audio/video messages through hand mike/loudspeakers by using auto-rickshaw or tom-tom. All the community engagement activities will be conducted under the guidance of camp authority and in close coordination and collaboration with the other agencies active in the COVID 19 response for making it harmonise and avoid duplication. In the case of the host community, the activity will be coordinated with the local government. For preparing cyclone and monsoon season, this activity will support in DRR. • Play audio/video messages at static health post through the audiovisual system for the audience, who are coming (both from camp and host community) to receive health services. In the health post, the response will support to play audio/video message on the treatment protocol of COVID 19 and illustrative documents on isolation process. • Engage religious and community leaders for disseminating messages during prayers. They are targeted because they are influential leaders of Rohingya and community has strong faith in them. The leaders can work for alleviating social stigma and influence the community in reducing COVID 19 risk. The religious and community leaders will also work to raise awareness on DRR during cyclone and monsoon season. Research/Assessment/ Studies: • Tracking the rumours or myths and feedback on COVID through an automated system called COMPASS. At the moment, CAID is tracking rumours about COVID 19 through an excel tracking sheet and is planning to introduce a digital system developed by CAID global team to track the rumours, perception, concern, and feedback related to COVID 19. This digital system will help the response to act appropriately by incorporating the needs of the Rohingya and host communities, and it will also give the advantage of operating remotely. The outcome will be shared with the sector to consider the community perception in the response design. "
