Project Summary

Needs:
There are 19 million school aged children in Sudan, before the war broke out in April 2023, only 12.5 million children attended school. After the war education was suspended over all Sudan left 6.5 million children (51 per cent girls) with no access to education services, with 10,400 schools (54 per cent of the total 19,302 schools) forcefully closed. Attacks on schools and their use as emergency shelters further compromise the safety of education spaces and put enormous pressure on the already fragile education system for the host communities.
An estimated two million school-aged children are internally displaced, while half a million school-aged children have crossed international borders. Displacement has overwhelmed host communities, straining their fragile education systems and limited services. Access barriers remain significant, with an additional 6.9 million children out-of-school including children with disabilities, many living in vulnerable communities. Inadequate access to clean water and sanitation in schools remains a significant. Gender disparity is highly varied across states in Sudan, and after the current situation the case would be worsen with a fear of girls to drop out of schools and boys to engage in armed groups.
There are concerns of the impact on Sudan’s human capital-skills and knowledge required for productivity and survival face a real threat, eroding individual and household resilience. Fears of an increase in drop-out rates for both boys and girls is expected. Children from affected states and poor families, those in hard-to-reach areas are the most vulnerable.
intervention: The school feeding program in Sudan seeks to enhance enrollment and retention, regularize attendance and address short term hunger through the provision of a daily meals in schools and through take home rations leading to overall better learning outcomes for extremely vulnerable and food insecure children, while working closely with other development partners.
Students whose families rely on their children receiving daily school meals are unable to access these meals for several months, as a result of schools being closed. This hot meal served at school often compromises the first and only proper meal eaten per day by students enrolled in schools supported by the school feeding programme. Moreover, the meal provides one third of a child’s daily caloric needs. Therefore, it is crucial for this support to continue, WFP plans to re-introduce an Emergency School Feeding once schools re-open. WFP will integrate the school feeding into its ongoing humanitarian response, to the extent possible, while also taking advantage of the education sector response by overlaying school feeding with other activities such as psychosocial counselling, WASH and learning to be implemented by other agencies. School feeding program under the HRP will provide meals to about 420,414 students ( 214,411 girls and 206,000 boys) across 3 states.
