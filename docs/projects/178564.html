Project Summary

The project aims at providing integrated services to enhance resilience and protection from fire risks, and protection risks and to promote safe and dignified living conditions for IDPs living in camps and IDP settlements, especially for women, adolescent girls, the elderly and people with disabilities in NW Syria, sub-districts of Dana, Maaret tamsrin in Idleb and subdistrict of Azaz in Aleppo.
The project will target 35 IDP sites (which are also targeted by other shelter and protection components to ensure complementarity) with 9636 HHs, 54683 individuals, mostly women and children disagregated as 11695 men, 12782 women, 14348 boys and 14581 girls with about 3512 PWDs, 14217 children under 5 and 2197 elderly people above 60 years old.
Selection of sites is done in coordination with SNFI and CCCM clusters, local authorities and camp management and based on needs assessment after ensuring HLP rights through due diligence process according to relevant clusters guidelines.
SDI will be keen to adhere to IASC standards and international principles in relation to GBV, PSEA, and inclusion and accessibility of people with disabilities. The project will ensure the formation of IDP committees in these sites including the participation of women and PWDs. Coordination with all the abovementioned stakeholders and committees will be maintained throughout the project cycles to ensure active participation and accountability. Camp management and different committees in the camps with receive awareness-raising on safety & security, Covid19, complaint mechanism, GBV, PSEA in addition to camp management and cross-cutting issues through sessions, training and banners, which be available to all camp residents. Multi-sectoral gap analysis will be conducted and submitted regularly to CCCM cluster.
To ensure the safety of IDPs and enhance protection against fire incidents that might happen on large or small scale. The intervention will focus on two aspects in consistence with Fire Response TF guidance:
1. Increase the awareness of IDPs residing in those camps on safe practices against fire incidents from small to large scale, causes and harmful effects; the targeted age/sex groups will include children and women.
2. Set up fire units within targeted camps and equipping them with necessary tools (fire kits); in addition to selecting one of out of every 75 IDPs, (total 730 IDPs) voluntarily to be trained on firefighting of fire ranged from low to medium scale by a technical team from SCD (Syrian Civil Defense).
The project will also include the maintenance of basic infrastructure in camps such as clearing drainage channels, replacing/refiling fire extinguishers and light repair to latrines, lighting, water tanks, fence...etc.
The project will be handed over to existing camp management and/or existing committees upon project closure after ensuring that the site management is acquainted with accountability and humanitarian principles, site management, maintenance in IDP sites, safety & security and existing CCCM Syria cross-border cluster tools and guidelines and provide open channels with CCCM to keep regular reporting of needs, ISIMM, displacement figures and other key information requested by CCCM through the training provided by SDI.
Prioritization of activities: All activities are life-saving and crucial to the survival and dignity of IDPs residing in the IDP camps. In case of underfunding, SDI will be obliged to reduce the number of camps targeted within this intervention. However, these camps will continue to be at risk of fire and suffer from bad living conditions due to lack of infrastructure and facilities maintenance in addition to lack of governance/participatory management thus affecting the dignity and rights of most vulnerable individuals.

4 - Needs

According to HRP draft, after a decade of crisis in Syria, humanitarian needs remain extensive and multifaceted across the country. More than 1.5 million of the 2.7 million IDPs in NWS currently shelter in around 1,100 “last resort” sites. Many of these sites are overcrowded, and access to shelter, safe water, food, health, and psychosocial support is inadequate. According to CCCM cluster mapping of IDPs sites, Dana, Maarat Tamisrin and Azaz subdistricts host the largest numbers of IDPs in NW Syria. The population of camps and IDP settlements in these areas are 870,474 in Dana, 248,709 in Maarat Tamisrin and 125,063 in Azaz. Social Development International has conducted a needs assessment in 36 camps in Dana and Maarrat Tamsrin. The needs assessment showed that residents of camps are mostly women and children, 13% of surveyed family members were under 5 years old, 4% were above 60. 62% of families included vulnerable individuals (PWDs or pregnant and lactating women). All assessed camps were affected by floods recently, 50% of respondents said their tents were affected by floods, 80% said the roads in the camps were in bad or very bad situation. SDI's meal team also reached to KIIs to ask about the situation in these camps: All assessed camps have valid HLP documents, all camps need manitainence/rehabiliatation of the infrastructure, traveling and land-leveling of tents and roads. 34 camps need water drainage systems. 28 of assessed camps were subject to fire incidents and all camps are in need of fire fighting equipment and training.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented by SDI's experienced field staff through its offices in Syria, and administered through program coordinator in Turkey. The project will begin by ensuring coordination with stakeholders inside Syria (e.g. camp management and local authorities), partner organizations and clusters throughout the project to ensure a coordinated and complementary approach for comprehensive services, avoid duplication and ensure accountability to affected populations. HLP due diligence will be conducted to ensure both owners' and IDPs' rights. SDI will create IDP representative committees, if not existing, and ensure they have female and PWD members and provide them with awareness-raising on project activities, humanitarian principles, protection, safety and security topics to increase their participation and resilience. Men, women, boys, girls, adolescents and elderly will be involved in the designing, implementation and monitoring of activities through focus groups, surveys and complaint mechanism. The project will be handed over to committees in camps upon project closure in addition to prior coordination with CCCM cluster to ensure follow-up and enhance the sustainability of the project. Project activities will take into consideration the age and gender differences and the needs of disabled, elderly and PWDs in addition to protection concerns, like GBV, Covid19 and PSEA awareness, maintaining lighting, locks for privacy and ensuring sphere standards. Materials and services will be procured based on SDI's policies and procedures in a competitive and transparent manner to rule out fraud and ensure cost-effectiveness. PDM will be conducted at the end of the project to assess impact and beneficiary satisfaction.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

PSEA risks; likelihood: moderate; impact: high; mitigation: SDI staff, contractors’ staff, camp management, and all committees will receive awareness about GBV, PSEA, and complaint mechanism. The code of conduct, read and signed by all SDI and contractor staff, includes GBV, PSEA with zero-tolerance policy in place. ​All staff members will be well informed and committed to PSEA principles. Large PSEA banners will be visible to all beneficiaries in camps with complaint number of both SDI and UN. Also PSEA zero-tolerance banners/posters will be posted in visible places to all staff. Feedback mechanisms will include hotline, email and fixed complaint boxes in work locations besides info on social media. Mechanisms will be well-announced on banners/posters in all targeted locations and during training. Complaints will be received by the accountability officer in Turkey. A separate hotline&email (05523997418, WhatsApp&Telegram, psea@sdi.ngo) will be advertised for sensitive complaints, like, PSEA, and will be received by or referred to the protection coordinator/PSEA FP (female) in Istanbul office. All complaints will be dealt with anonymously and confidentially. All staff members will be well informed and committed to PSEA principles and aware of GBV referral pathway. Female staff will be present within the team in all positions in the field including, implementation team, MEAL team, social workers and within IDP representative committees.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: Access to all project locations is secured and safe; Risk1: Access restrictions or danger on staff and beneficiaries due to security or HLP issues. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high. Mitigation: Security assessment, HLP due diligence, coordination with local authorities, staff training, provision of safety tools and equipment, identification of appropriate services and medical facilities, evacuation plan, relocation (plan B).Assumption2: the borders will keep open for humanitarian response. Risk2: Closure of border crossing might affect bringing multicluster assistance to beneficiaries, especially tents and NFIs. Likelihood: High; Impact: Low. Mitigation: using locally available materials while advocating through advocacy channels.Assumption3: Needed material, workshops, and skilled and non-skilled workers are available. Risk3: Unavailability of material or workers might hinder the implementation of activities. Likelihood: very low; Impact: high. Mitigation: RNA and market assessment, procure items from a wider geographic range.Risk4: Children or residents might fall in pits or get injured during construction and maintenance works; Mitigation: Awareness raising, clear warning signs, safety tapes..etc. to keep civilians out of danger zones.Risk5: Fire leading to the destruction of tents and endanger the lives of residents; Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high. Mitigation: provision of awareness, training and equipment is one of the main components of this project.Risk6: low-quality implementation of works. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: moderate. Mitigation: Follow up construction and maintenance works and conduct regular spot checks and PDM.For PSEA and protection risks, please check relevant sections.
