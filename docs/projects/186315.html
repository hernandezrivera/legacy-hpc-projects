Project Summary

The proposal is aligned with the overall Food Security, Nutrition, and WASH sectors strategies as defined in the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021. The action is designed to run for 12 months
To deliver on the food security component of this muli-sectoral proposed interventions, Caritas Nigeira will provide wet-feeding to newly arrived refugee returnees and IDPs across border communities/camps in Banki, Bama and Gwoza; and provide energy energy for cooking to vulnerable households not yet reached across Banki, Bama, Gwoza and Konduga LGAs of Borno State.
The nutrition component will contribute to a reduction of morbidity and mortality caused by malnutrition amongst children under 5 years of age through treatment of SAM with medical complications at stabilisation centers in Hong and Gombi LGAs of Adamawa State: and the prevention of acute malnutrition through screening, identification, referral to the OTP centers, and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) across Kaga, Konduga, Bama LGAs in Borno State and Mubi North, Gombi and Hong LGAs in Adamawa State. It will also ensure promotion of infant and young child feeding practices amongst pregnant and lactating women (PLW) to prevent malnutrition in the target LGAs. We will form support groups (MTMSG and FTFSG) where they do not exist and strengthen already formed ones where they are weak. Caregivers will be reached with health talk and IYCF Counselling in addition to food demonstration sessions. The programme will also support caregivers of children with SAM who have medical complications to receive a one-off cash transfer for immediate food needs. We will ensure enhanced needs analysis of the nutrition situation and enhanced monitoring and coordination of response. The nutrition programme will also focus on capacity building for staff in government health facilities through on-the-job training, as well as through participation in formal training and courses. Our primary focus is to provide training on Integrated Management of Severe Acute Malnutrition, which promotes the identification and treatment of SAM in line with the Government of Nigeria ministry of health (MoH) guidelines and SPHERE standards.
The WASH component will target host communities, IDP camps, schools, and health facilities, in order to reach the most vulnerable populations in need of WASH across Mubi North, Mubi South, Girei LGAs in Adamawa State and Kaga, Konduga,and Gwoza LGAs in Borno State; through the provision of solar boreholes powered by climate smart energy sources, construction of new ventilated improved pit (VIP) latrine and renovation of damaged ones, construction of new bath showers, distribution of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and Dignity kits that are reusable to young girls and women of reproductive age, regular Hygiene promotion messaging, distribution of water treatment tablets (Aquatabs), distribution of Cholera Preparedness Kits, procurement of hand wash stations to be placed in strategic public places, including VIP latrines, and training of the beneficiaries on solid waste management. The intervention will complement other ongoing emergency/recovery programmes in the target locations using the SPHERE standards.
For sustainability, various groups within target communities will be trained on the use locally available food items to prepare nutritious meals as well as on nutrition sensitive agriculture (home gardening). These will help to stem future malnutrition among infants, young children; WASHCOMMS will be formed, trained and strengthened to handle minor repairs and linked to Local Area Mechanics (LAMs) and technicians of solar panels and boreholes. This will help them to know where to go to in case of breakdown that the WASHCOMM cannot handle.

1 - Needs

Caritas Nigeria in conjunction with its partners, CAFOD, Caritas Germany and Justice Development for Peace Commission/Caritas (JDPC) Maiduguriin the first quarter of 2021, conducted a multi-sectoral needs assessments for Food Security, Nutrition, WASH and Protection in Kaga, Magumeri, Gwoza, Biu, Hawul,Shani, and Bayo LGAs in Borno State; and Mubi North, Mubi South, Michika, and Madagali LGAs in Adamawa State.The results revealed huge unmet Food Security, Nutrition, WaSH and Protection gaps,requiring immediate scale up of humanitarian assistance within these LGAs of our ongoing multi-sectoral interventions. This result informed our ongoing multi-sectoral consortium program,Scaling-up Assistance in Hard-to-Reach Areas (SAHaRA) Program with support from ECHO, to reach 20,052 most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host community members with safe, accessible, dignified, inclusive and multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance on cash transfer programming for Food Security, WASH and Protectiion assistance in Kaga and Magumeri. It also informed the scale up of our ongoing multi-sectoral program addressing cash transfer programming for Food Security, Nutrition, WaSH and Protection with funding support from Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs across Madagali, Michika, Mubi North and Biu LGAs. A rapid needs assessment was also conducted by Caritas Nigeria in October 2021 on food security across some host communities and IDP camps in Pulka, Banki, and Bama revealed live threathening food security and nutrition gaps, especially for the new arrivals and IDPs in these locations. These findings corroborates the huge food security gap reported by CCCM/UNHCR on the movement of IDPs, refugees, and returnees, as they registered 5637 returnees, IDPs, and refugees between Spetmber and October 2021 across GSSS camps in Bama, Banki, and the transit camp in Pulka. These new arrivals were facing starvation with high cases of MAM and SAM among children under 5 years as well as pregnant and lactating women. The results of March 2021 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis indicated that the BAY states are classified in phase 3 – 4 of acute food and nutritional insecurity across the inaccessible areas. Findings suggest a famine-like food consumption pattern among a minority of the inaccessible population (10%). The findings suggest severe food consumption deficits, with widespread use of food-based ration control. The 2021 HNO has highlighted the current nutrition situation as worrying, with 1.5 million (women 42%, children 58%) children and women in need of immediate nutrition services, and states that global acute malnutrition (GAM) in children aged 6–59 months remains highly concerning. GAM rates increased from 6.7% in 2017 to 11% in 2019, exceeding the WHO threshold of 10% disaggregated as 367,000 children under five (179,830 boys and 187,170 girls) suffering from SAM and 727,000 (356,230 boys and 370,770 girls) with MAM. These increases were attributed to weak health infrastructures, poor infant and young child feeding practices, limited access to safe water and sanitation services, poor hygiene conditions, and food insecurity as two-thirds of health facilities in the BAY states have been damaged by the conflict – a clear indication of the impact of the crisis on the nutrition and health system

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Caritas Nigeria will mainstream protection activities through the following measures: Avoid exposing people to further harm and maintain the principle of "do no harm." Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance – in proportion to need and without discrimination. Protect people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion. Assist people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuse To achieve the objectives above, Caritas Nigeria will ensure that in all our activities, women, children, and persons with special health conditions are given priority as beneficiaries. To this end, children, pregnant/lactating women, and persons with special health conditions will be the primary targets for the acute malnutrition screening that will be done. On the wet feeding, food will be given to all the returnees, IDPs, and refugees at the quarantine centers, but we will prioritize distribution of food rations to children, persons living with disabilities/special needs, pregnant/lactating women, and the sick and elderly. This will be applicable to beneficiaries of WASH and Nutrition too who will be receiving NFIs. To mitigate sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), all staff will receive safeguarding/PSEA training/refresher courses at the beginning of the project. They will then step-down this training to the returnees, IDPs, and refugees, community leaders, and community mobilizers, who will then ensure that all beneficiaries are sensitized and educated on their protection rights, possible occasions of violation and how to report in a secure and effective way, using the community based feedback mechanism. Frontline mobilizers, returnees, IDPs, and refugee leaders will be trained on how to detect and report instances of SGBV within the target locations or help refer GVB survivors for immediate assistance. All partners, vendors, contractors, and casual workers that would be involved in this project will sign a commitment to adherence to standard safeguarding principles and a standard code of staff conduct. Informal safe spaces will be created for the mentoring of younger women/girls and children by the older returnees, IDPs, and refugee women. They will be encouraged to freely discuss challenges they have faced or are facing, especially with regards to SEA and GBV and child abuse, while in transit or at the quarantine centers. Complaints and Feedback Desks will be provided in the Quarantine centres which will receive complaints from the beneficiaries. Hotlines and other virtual reporting mechanisms will be made available to the beneficiaries during sensitizations and in the IEC materials placed in the quarantine centres. Gender Marker will be observed during program implementation by deliberating targeting and getting feedback from women, children, and persons with special health conditions during our nutrition screening exercise and routine monitoring visits. We will maintain and establish functional referral mechanisms at LGA and community levels for vulnerable children and caregivers. Caritas Nigeria will facilitate referrals of GBV and child abuse survivors to relevant actors for case management and legal assistance where applicable. Caritas Nigeria will fully participate in regular Protection Sector and Safeguarding sub-sector meetings, coordinate/collaborate with sector partners in our locations of intervention, share relevant updates.
