Project Summary

Concern will provide multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to disaster-affected populations to improve their food security by targeting 2,620 families. 1,620 families will receive multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) in three cycles while 1,000 families will receive one cycle of MPCA in case of emergencies (flooding and or conflicts). Based on the data analysis from the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JIMMI) report and the internally conducted market assessment, the Aweil North and Guit markets are functioning and have sufficient stock of key food commodities to serve increased demand in the event of a cash injection. Despite the currently favourable market conditions, Concern acknowledges (i) the overall volatility of the South Sudanese economy (i.e. ramping inflation, accompanied by steady devaluation of the South Sudanese Pound and consequent loss of purchasing power of the target population), as well as (ii) the challenging contextual operating conditions (i.e. access issues during the flood season, protection risks and taxation practices in some counties). In this respect, Concern will ensure that adequate market monitoring practices, decision-making tools, and triggers will be used to inform potential changes in the delivery modality.

1. Needs

South Sudan is grappling with multiple crises, including ongoing conflict, climate disasters such as floods and droughts, social and political instability, pervasive food insecurity, and rampant inflation. In 2024, approximately 9 million people (75% of the population) require humanitarian and protection assistance. Malnutrition rates remain alarmingly high, particularly among women and children, with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels surpassing the World Health Organization (WHO) threshold of 15% in 46 out of 79 counties The economic situation in South Sudan continues to deteriorate as the government’s primary revenue source—oil exports—remains blocked. Additionally, implementing high customs and border fees to increase revenue has exacerbated the decline in market supplies, leading to soaring food and fuel prices. Compounding these challenges, one year of conflict in Sudan has resulted in the influx of over 891,815 returnees and refugees into South Sudan, intensifying already poor food security conditions. These returnees and refugees arrive in areas with scarce resources, are heavily reliant on relatives and host communities for their food needs, and lack the assets necessary to restart their lives and livelihoods. South Sudan relies heavily on commercial imports of basic commodities, including cereals and pulses from neighbouring countries; the disruptions in imports due to conflicts in Sudan have further compounded the related surge in food prices. According to the latest IPC report, Aweil North and Guit counties are projected to have experienced IPC Level 4 food/nutrition insecurity from April to July 2025. Concern and its partners conducted SMART Surveys in Aweil North (March 2024) and Guit (May 2024). SMART surveys reveal GAM prevalence in Aweil North was 26%, SAM was 3.6%; in Guit, GAM was 24.1%, and SAM was 6.3%. All GAM rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%. The IPC analysis and Concern’s multi-sector needs assessment MSNA (MSNA-July 2024) identified several key drivers of malnutrition: insufficient and uneven food access, poor dietary intake, inadequate feeding practices, and high prevalence of childhood diseases, all compounded by limited access to maternal and child healthcare. In both locations, hazards include heavy rains causing flash floods and riverine flooding, high temperatures (heatwave in 2024), bushfire outbreaks, delayed onset of rains, and dry spells (mini drought). The primary impacts of climate-related shocks and stresses include shifting seasonal calendars; late and reduced but still heavy rainfall leading to flooding; related destruction of property and crops; loss of lives and property; and displacement. Women, young children, the chronically ill, the elderly, PWD, and returnees were identified as the most affected by these hazards. Concern’s assessment found that, given the depleted local coping capacities due to protracted conflict, increased food insecurity, and the devastating effects of recent flooding, it is imperative to prioritize multi-sector and cross-sectoral interventions to support life-saving interventions targeting the most vulnerable with equitable access to health and nutrition services, with a focus on MPCA to promote equitable access to basic needs, supported by building local capacities and resilience of target community to future shocks.

2. Project activities

Concern will provide multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to 1,620 conflicts and flood-affected food-insecure HHs (9,720 individuals) across the 2 targeted counties (Aweil North and Guit). Each HH will receive three rounds of cash for cash assistance covering their basic food needs. Transfer values will be set against the county-specific Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) derived from the JMMI and CWG guidelines. Concern will closely monitor changes in the market prices and projected impacts of inflation and inform potential reviews of the transfer values. The total MEB for Aweil North is EUR98 (USD105), and Guit is EUR129 (USD138) for September 2024, as per the JMMI dashboard. The MPC is calculated and budgeted at the September MEB rate plus 30% buffer information. At the time of distribution, Concern will monitor the JMMI dashboard and CWG for the MEB rate for the month. Concern plans to distribute 100% of the MEB since selected households would be newly displaced / new arrivals and conflict-affected; the assumption here is that most families would not be able to provide additional income or means to meet the MEB.Concern will coordinate closely with WFP and other implementing partners to ensure that there is no overlap in HHs receiving cash voucher assistance. Concern will target food-insecure households (HHs), quantified by poor (0-21 score) FCS and high CSI scores, that have been displaced by conflict or flood. Acknowledging the level of the needs in the selected counties, Concern will further prioritize HHs with pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW), malnourished children under the age of 5, PWD, and elderly people. Concern will conduct community meetings, ensuring participation of all genders and vulnerability groups, covering project purpose, project selection criteria, and value of transfer, timing and duration of the cash assistance. Community input will be sought to determine the appropriate timing and location of distribution, ensuring travel distance is not too far and the timing permits maximum participation without disrupting livelihoods or household responsibilities and ensuring the safety of those travelling to distribution locations. Cash will be distributed by Financial Service Providers (Opportunity Bank). MEAL staff will be present during distributions to receive feedback and complaints and to monitor distribution process. Concern will conduct post distribution monitoring (PDM) to evaluate feedback on the utilization and impact of the distributed resources, to assess whether the intended objectives are being met and make necessary adjustments to the activities design. A first round of PDM will be conducted after first round of cash distribution to allow for rapid adjustments of delivery mechanism whenever necessary, a second PDM will be conducted after third round within one month from the last distribution.The crisis modifier is included in this project for anticipatory climate shocks (floods etc.) communal conflicts, diseases outbreaks, spike in market prices for essential and lifesaving commodities and or the influx of returnees and refugees for speed recovery. The crisis modifier is adaptive to proactively anticipate to respond quickly and effectively at the appropriate scale. The MPCA (one-off cash transfers) as the preferred response mechanism to 1000 families will be adopted. The cash value will follow the cash working group guidelines and the MEB. Concern will use project-specific cash distribution SOPs. The households with PBW, malnourished children, health-risk individuals, protection/ GBV survivors, PWDs, and elderly will be prioritized.
