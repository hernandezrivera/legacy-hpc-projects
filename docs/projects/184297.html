Project Summary

According to REACH reports show increasing poor living conditions of IDPs in informal sites have exacerbated consequences for vulnerable populations. Consequently, humanitarian needs for women and girls are exacerbated in the context of Somalia IDP sites. On average, only 14% of IDP sites host latrines with access to lighting positing safety constraints that are acutely felt by women and girls (DSA, 2021).
There are over 2,400 recorded IDP sites across Somalia. An approximate, 85% of the sites are informal settlements on private land and about 74% of them are in urban areas according to the Detailed Site Assessment (DSA) of the CCCM Cluster. Existing coordination and management mechanisms are largely informal, with most sites being managed by landlords or gatekeepers. These mechanisms have limited accountability and little adherence to minimum standards.
This project aim is to focus on Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) activities undertaken in settlements Daynile, Kahda, Dharkenley districts in Mogadishu and alongside Afgooye corridor specifically at KM13, Siinkadheer IDPs and Elasha IDPs, Afgooye and Merka district. It is designed to mitigate the physical and protection consequences of drought on populations and communities directly affected alongside Afgooye corridor. This overarching objective will be achieved by enhancing the overall protective environment for drought-affected population, increasing access to basic services linked to different sectors, reducing the risk thereof, and availing specialized support assistance to beneficiaries to help them address other interconnected protection concerns. The action is also conceptualized to consolidate the ongoing humanitarian response to the flood, conflict and drought situation in the targeted locations. CCCM, SOYDA will establish multi-sectoral teams with clearly defined geographic responsibility for specific IDP populations (approx. 9,133 HHs; 54,800 persons in 40 IDP settlements ). Alongside Afgooye corridor KM13, Siinkadheer IDPs and Elasha IDPs, with necessary services through coordination mechanism set in place with relevant stakeholders and other partners.
This project is designed to meet the needs of 65% IDPs and 35% host communities of the total beneficiaries targeted. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis- segregated data. Settlement mapping exercises will be conducted to get the information of all targeted settlements for necessary services in place and gap analysis, the settlement mapping exercise data will be shared with CCCM cluster lead, which will be useful in creating a master list of the area. This master list will then be posted online for the ease of access to all humanitarian actors, which will be used by different humanitarian partners for their future planning and response.
SOYDA CCCM activities will also improve the coordination and monitoring of service provision at site level and intra-site to ensure efficiency in service delivery and avoid duplication or gaps in services through capacity building for the existing community mechanisms to ensure access to information and services and improve accountability to affected populations. Throughout the project, the needs of women, girls, men and boys of all ages will be addressed all throughout the planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Through regular site monitoring, needs and gaps will be reported and shared with partners and all stakeholders. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to content with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision of Camp management approach training and community participation. 300 (150 female & 150 male) CCCMs members (15 individuals in each camp); will be identified and be trained so as to strengthen the existing coordination mechanisms in sites where there no existing structures by establishing active structures that will govern in line with Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). The training will be carried for a period of 7 days in each camp, Using the cluster training package the following topics will be covered;1. Community participation 2. Conflict resolution 3. Responsibilities of Camp management committees 4. Feedback and compliance mechanism 5. Protection training 6. Fire safety and preventions in the camps 2. Provision of CASH FOR WORK (CFW) activities for 4 months. 500 beneficiaries from the 40 IDP camps will work for 3 days in a week equivalent to 12 days in a month. The CFW activities will include BUSH clearing in all 20 target IDP camps; Garbage collection will be carried on weekly basis from the site and taken into an identified dumping site. The other CFW activities that will be involved by the 500 beneficiaries will include site decongestion activities (shelter spacing, among others as prioritized by the community) During this period, each beneficiary will receive $60 on monthly basis. SOYDA will procure a tool kit with a set of 8 tools that will be used on Cash for Work but also other decongestion site activities in all the 40 IDP camps; 3. SOYDA will provide Camp decongestion activities, The entire site population which is 51,300 from 40 sites are expected to receive this support. SOYDA with the help of the community and service providers will create a plan to reconfigure the IDP site to provide increased space between shelters, larger roads and sanitation facilities (toilets). this will be community and service providers consultative process to determine specifics.4. Provide training on-site maintenance and site planning to camp committees 5. Implement emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation, re prevention). 6. SOYDA will actively coordinate with the CCCM and other National and Sub National Clusters, including HLP colleagues responsible for Areas of Operations and local authorities. For the site care and maintenance, SOYDA will provide tools, skilled training supervision and capacity building for site maintenance and site improvements through cash for work activities according to a plan developed jointly by Camp Management Teams and the communities. 7. Risk communication, community engagement activities and information dissemination (for prevention of covid-19) in the target 20 IDP camps; Each site will get 300 copies of IEC materials which will be fixed on school, latrines, mosques and any other open places where information can easily be disseminated for the public. 8. Ensure through CCCM coordination that relevant responders are mobilized towards providing sectorial assistance to those identified as very vulnerable 9. Ensure the relevant Protection and Shelter responders are aware and take action with regards to HLP issues in sites, specifically regarding evictions. 10. Establish/update service mapping of partners operating in sites11. Monitor service delivery at site level to ensure that there are no gaps or duplication of activities and that minimum standards are being met 12. Establish roving site management teams to ensure coordination with community governance structures 13. Identify existing governance structures and focal points for sites 14. Durable solutions Camps and camp like settings are just temporary solutions to displacement, therefore the project will link to durable solutions through coordination with existing durable solutions forum at consortium, at the local and national level 15. Harmonize feedback mechanisms relevant for sites across all clusters

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance: The community leaders and respective committees will be fully involved in project implementation, monitoring and representation. SOYDA will ensure that project committees to be inclusive and that segments of the communities are well represented at all levels such as age and gender diversity. The project will be implemented in collaboration with all stakeholders while involving the beneficiaries. During community mobilization sessions, the project staff will provide detailed and an in-depth information on vulnerability-based selection criteria. Special attention will be given to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services through consulting them during the designing of the interventions. 4.5 - Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area. SOYDA will seek to mitigate against this through integrating HLP and eviction mitigation activities into the project programming such as monthly eviction risk data collection, HLP/conflict resolution trainings for CMC members and referral mechanisms in case an eviction notice has been posted. SOYDA will also work with the cluster to seek to mitigate evictions and collaborate with protection partners, local authorities, landlords and communities to find alternative solutions
