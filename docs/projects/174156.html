Project Summary

According Humanitarian Needs Overview (EiEWG HNO, 2020), about 1.2 million children are in dire need of basic education services in the region and majority of these population are in Borno state and are internally displaced. JENA report highlights some of the challenges most of these children go through in order to access and the barriers to the education. In the report, the major factors militating against the right to education as promulgated by 1989 Convention on the Right of the child are early marriage among the female children, poverty (most of the families could not afford school fees/levies) and school closure.
These challenges were exacerbated by the global pandemic that struck at the beginning of the year that led to total closure of schools which contributed to massive school dropouts in almost all the countries in the world. UNICEF reported that due to the novel coronavirus, many children are still at home due to the virus as some of the schools are yet to re-open.
Gender Equality Peace and Development Centre (GEPaDC) seeks to address issues related to gap in education within the targeted Local governments. It aims to do that by creating and rehabilitating spaces for EIE learning in IDP camps and conflict-affected communities and Schools in order to ensure that children in the communities within the targeted LGAs are not left behind. The project will Support systemic strengthening by training formal and Non-formal school teachers, enhance the capacity of the community Education Committees (CECs), School Based Management Committees (SBMCs) to demand for equitable quality education and provide age appropriate and cultural/gender sensitive learning and teaching materials/aids.
The action with overall objective support improved Gender inclusive and Disability friendly formal and non-formal education access, learning outcomes and continuity in learning for conflict-affected children will target 22,475 beneficiaries in 2 LGAs (Jere and Ngala) of Borno state. 2,000 will be targeted for IQE, 4,000 out of school children with non-formal education basic - numeracy and literacy, 40 classroom safe learning centres will be constructed with WaSH facilities (gender sensitive & disability friendly latrine) and school gardens. 20 classroom blocks will be upgraded in formal schools and 40 latrines will be constructed. 300 formal school teachers and 100 community volunteer teachers will be trained to deliver quality education and PSS using teaching at the right level approach for 22,000 children from both formal and non-formal. The capacity of 75 SBMC members will be refreshed, retrained for advocacy, effective school management and accountability.
Child Protection Committees will be established and trained to ensure prevention of SGBV and PSEA within the target communities.

2 - Activities

Conflict-affected children have increased access to quality education and child protection services in safe and protective learning environment:The action will ensure safe and protective learning environment for both learners and teachers through improving the physical learning environment by renovation of classrooms and construction of gender separated and disability friendly latrines. The project will also work closely with government, community coalition and other actors to identify out of school children, and map out strategies to support the provision of non-formal learning opportunities such as basic literacy and numeracy, which will also be integrated into the Sangaya and Islamiya schools, with clear pathways to mainstream into formal education. Temporary learning spaces will be constructed especially for the IQE centers and camps to create a more conducive accessible and safe learning environment. Targeted children and teachers will be provided with teaching and learning kits, capacity of teachers for both formal and non-formal schools will be built in child development, PSS, pedagogy, life skills and SGBV to deliver quality education for learners using the teaching at the right level approach. Child protection will be mainstream into classroom activities in non-formal schools in ensuring well-being and safety of children accessing the educational services. Child help desk as a feedback and response mechanism among other mechanisms will be established for receiving feedbacks, counseling and provision of PSS services.This output will therefore target 22,000 children in both formal and non-formal school with learning kits and PSS, 4,000 will be target in non-formal education, 2,000 in IQE and 16,000 in formal school. 300 formal school teachers and 100 CVTs will be trained on PSS, life skills, pedagogy and SGBV. 20 classroom will be renovated in formal schools, 40 TLC will be constructed and 40 gender sensitive and disability friendly latrines.Communities have increased capacity to participate in school development and risk reduction planning and demand equitable access to conflict sensitive educationTo ensure sustainability and accountability to both beneficiaries and donor, the action will work closely with community structures, government agencies and other actors. This will enhance coordination and ensuring complementary approach to meeting the needs of beneficiaries. The action will train SBMC to ensure grassroots advocacy and improvement management of school activities for sustainability. The action will also ensure establishment of community education committee to champion identification and enrolment out of school children into school.To ensure systematic M&E, a framework will be developed based on global and regional EiE frameworks and standards. A detailed M&E plan will also be developed, based on the log-frame, together with the requisite monitoring tools in which staff will be trained to ensure the monitoring of programme deliverables and proposed targets
