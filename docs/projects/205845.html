Project Summary

Samaritan’s Purse South Sudan proposes to support Maiwut Primary Health Care Center (PHCC) in 2024 in a bid to enhance access to basic primary health care and nutrition services through treatment of common communicable diseases, promotion of safe motherhood and Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI), and promotion optimal maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN), in order to improve community resilience.
The project will be implemented in close collaboration and coordination with the State Ministry of Health (SMOH) and County Health Department (CHD). The project will support the PHCC in providing the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS), in order to prevent the already fragile health situation from deteriorating. The Project will support the County Health Department in paying incentives for the health workers, providing capacity building trainings and mentorship, stocking of essential medicines and medical supplies to aid the delivery of inpatient and outpatient consultations, maternal and newborn care, and prevention of malnutrition through regular growth monitoring promotion and micronutrient supplementation among the target population.
This project will also respond to the immediate humanitarian health and nutrition needs of communities in crisis within Samaritan’s Purse areas of operation in Unity and Upper Nile states. In the past years, Samaritan's Purse emergency response projects have mainly focused on distributing non-food items (NFIs) to address emergency shelter needs and provision of limited WaSH items, but the beneficiaries never receive support in managing health conditions as part of humanitarian support in crisis. This year’s Health Rapid Response Team (HRRT) strategy was informed by the SPSS teams’ learnings in running mobile health services, flood responses and mass vaccination campaigns conducted for ‘communities in crisis. The project will deploy a mobile rapid response team to address the immediate, basic humanitarian needs for health and nutrition services due to multiple crisis including extreme flooding, violence, poverty, food insecurity, displacement, and limited access to essential services, existing access constraints, operational interferences, public health challenges such as measles and cholera outbreaks, and climatic shocks resulting in extraordinary flooding and localized drought which are all exacerbated by the Sudan crisis, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire with only 26% of the Humanitarian Response Plan currently funded. The Country’s Health system cannot cope with, respond to, and provide essential life-saving health services in humanitarian emergencies as it relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance that continue to face constrained funding.
This project seeks to improve the health status and nutritional wellbeing of 59,106 people (28,492 males and 30,614 females) in Maiwut County (38,492 host community, 1,850 internally displaced persons (IDPs) and 18,760 are returnees), through the delivery of quality and comprehensive primary health care and nutrition services at Maiwut PHCC. The vulnerable population groups that will be reached by this project include children under five years old (U5), pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and other women of reproductive age, people with disability, returnees and internally displaced persons (IDPs).

1. Needs

South Sudan’s health system remains ill-equipped, heavily dependent on humanitarian aid and is characterized by compromised functionality of health facilities and poor coverage of basic primary health care services due to limited funding, which has continued to decline steadily amidst increasing needs. The influx of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia into Maiwut exacerbates the already exist pressure on the health facilities in Maiwut County. South Sudan faces a high disease burden with under-five (U5), infant and neonatal mortality rate of 40 per 1,000 live births while maternal mortality rate stands at 1,223 per 100,000 live births, making South Sudan one of the countries with the highest maternal mortality rates in the world. According to the Integrated Disease Surveillance Response (IDSR) Report from epidemiology weeks 1-22, 2023, the top causes of morbidity in South Sudan are Respiratory Tract Infections (RTIs), Malaria, diarrhea, and skin infections. There are 11 health facilities in Maiwut County (three PHCCs and eight Primary Health Care Units [PHCUs]). Ten of these facilities are supported with funding from World bank/UNICEF while Maiwut PHCC was run by International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) from 2015 to June 2023. However, due to the funding constraints, the ICRC phased out its presence in Maiwut. Consequently, in August 2023, at the request of the CHD, Samaritan’s Purse took over the PHCC following the withdrawal of the International Committee of Red Cross (ICRC). Closure of the Maiwut PHCC will leave Maiwut town/payam with a big health gap, given that the PHCC provides services to over 40,960 people in addition to being a referral destination to three PHCUs in the nearby Payams of Kigeli, Jotome, and Meth. The PHCC receives an average of 100 patients per day, and takes care of an average of five deliveries/postpartum cases per day. There is no alternative health care facility for the community of Maiwut town given that the nearest health facilities in Pagak and Wunkir payams are located more than 25 kilometers away while the closest referral hospital is across the border in Gambella, which is approximately over three hours’ drive in dry seasons or longer when the road condition get worst in rainy season.id dry . Moreover, there are no ambulatory services in Maiwut. According to round 27- Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) report, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate, timely initiation and exclusive breastfeeding rates in Upper Nile state are 16.4%, 48.8% and 48.4%, respectively. The GAM rate is above World Health Organization (WHO) standards for defining a nutrition emergency as GAM exceeding the emergency threshold of 15% of children with acute malnutrition (< -2 weight-for-height z-scores (WHZ) or with nutritional edema). emergency. The endline assessment conducted by Samaritan’s Purse in Maiwut in 2022 revealed a Minimum Dietary Diversity of only 20% in children 6-23 months old. The major contributing factors to sub optimal Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices in Maiwut County include cultural norms and practices, inadequate dietary intake, including compromised childcare and feeding practices. Therefore, in 2024, the Country Office intends to continue supporting the PHCC in providing the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS), in a bid to prevent the already fragile health situation from deteriorating. The Project will support the County Health Department in paying incentives for the health workers, providing capacity building trainings and mentorship, stocking of essential medicines and medical supplies to aid the delivery of inpatient and outpatient consultations, maternal and newborn care, and prevention of malnutrition through routine growth monitoring promotion (GMP). The project will also deliver four rapid response interventions for the whole year averaging one intervention every quarter beyond Maiwut PHCC and surrounding areas.

2. Project activities

The project will be implemented in line with national priorities and policies as set out in the National Health Policy 2016-2026, and will include; 1. Improving access to and utilization of essential lifesaving healthcare services by all people in need. 2. Developing essential health infrastructure to ensure quality and safety of health services delivery. 3. Scaling up production, and strengthening CHD management and development of the human resources.4. Ensuring that children U5 and pregnant women use more equitable and better quality essential maternal, newborn and child health services.Through this project, Samaritan’s Purse will continue to support quality service delivery of basic primary health care services in the facility through the CHD/ Ministry of Health (MOH) while building the capacity of the cadres to promote accountability and ownership of the service. The project will enhance the capacity of the PHCC staff to provide essential health care services through outpatient and inpatient services with complimentary health promotion strategies including disease prevention. The county health department (CHD) shall also be empowered to conduct adequate planning, implementation, monitoring and strengthening of Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert and Response (EWARS) systems. Key activities will include:• Provision of incentives to PHCC cadre: Samaritan’s Purse has hired an experienced Project Coordinator who will be supported by the Area Coordinator, the National Health Coordinator and the Health and Nutrition Technical Lead to oversee the operation of the PHCC. The health technical team will ensure quality improvement through capacity building while the day to day running of the Maiwut PHCC will be supervised and managed by the Project Coordinator through mentoring of PHCC staff on continuous quality improvement activities, infection prevention and control activities, proper waste management and general safety of staff and patient in the facility. • Maternal and new-born and child health services: Samaritan’s Purse will support the CHD with incentives for midwives, nurses and clinical officers to deliver maternal and new-born and child health Services such as maternal health education, antenatal care, skilled birth attendance, postnatal care of mothers and new-born, prevention of mother to child transmission (PMTCT) and refer complicated cases for secondary and tertiary health services not available in Maiwut county. • Pharmaceuticals and medical commodities prepositioning: Samaritan’s Purse will support the CHD in transporting medicines and medical supplies from Juba to Maiwut, as needed. Pharmaceuticals and medical commodities will be stored in lockable stores and managed through the use of a bin card system and a pharmaceutical excel tracker designed by the International Health Unit (IHU). • Capacity building of incentive workers: Incentive workers will receive capacity building and continuing medical education (CME) in order to enhance their capacity in delivering essential life-saving services and conducting health and nutrition education for patients and the community at large. • Basic health facility infrastructural improvement: Notwithstanding budgetary constraints, Samaritan’s Purse will try to improve the basic amenities at the PHCC such as supply of electricity and external lighting, generator maintenance, and other maintenance that will be needed. • Growth Monitoring Promotion (GMP): The project will also enhance the capacity of the PHCC incentive workers and CHWs to effectively promote and support monthly GMP The health rapid response team will provide the following activities: Outbreak prevention and health education such as mass vaccination of the targeted groups,Case management: General consultation for all < 5 and >5 yearsEPI and Nutrition
