Project Summary

Over 3,000 civilians and hundreds of security forces personnel have been killed in the Anglophone regions since 2016, when the crisis started. The NW/SW crisis has been aptly labelled a ‘protection crises by much of the humanitarian community. Focus groups discussions on several locations pointed to the disintegration of security not only from on-going conflict, but also the absence of rule of law. Focus group discussions took place in November 2020 in organised by The Danish Refugee Council (DRC) in 5 different affected communities with 92 participants, including 55 men and 37 women. The Anglophone crisis a major conflict that started in 2016 has evolved into a full war pitting the government forces and Non State Armed groups. The population is at crossroads and in constant fear as nothing is certain. Coupled to this is the steady increase in the prices of basic commodities (vegetable oil, rice, meat, fish, soap, sanitary pads, and local food stuff).
Project Need
Agriculture is the heartbeat of the economy in Northwest and Southwest Cameroon, and farming is relied upon by the majority of households in the region as a livelihood. Now with over seven years of conflict and displacement, agriculture and farming have been severely affected, and with this has come deterioration in food security. This urgently calls for actions in providing adequate food supply, capacity support to produce food for family and farm input supplies to support family food production.
 Displacement and reduction in labour for cultivation. Households reliant on farming as a livelihood have had to displace from conflict, missing cultivation and harvesting periods or abandoning farms altogether in favour of more secure areas.
 Restrictions on movement of food and food spoilage. Road insecurity has presented movement restrictions of both people and goods, contributing to increases in food costs and at times the spoilage of food when restrictions make transport and delivery to markers impossible.
 Loss of livelihoods and income. Widespread loss of income has meant that families struggle to have sufficient income to meet their families’ food needs.
 Cost of food. Decreased food production combined with road insecurity and extortion has significantly impacted the cost of food prices in the Northwest region.
Project objective
This project seeks to address food insecurity that exists in the conflict affected Northwest region of Cameroon caused mainly by displacement, reduction in labour for cultivation, reduction in purchasing power of households, increase in market prices of basic commodities, restrictions on movement of food and food spoilage. This project build will agricultural capacities in domains of poultry production and market gardening and set up farms for the target population of the region so as to support and maintain livelihood. The poultry farms with a capacity of 30 fowls.
Project Target
This projects targets 400 Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) who are resident in the North-West region of Cameroon Specifically, in Santa, Bambui, Bambili and Bafut (100 beneficiaries from each target area). The 100 beneficiaries will be made of 60 women and 40 men.
Project Activities
1) Community Sensitization and participant selection
2) Organizing Agro-Capacity Building Workshops
3) Setting up Farms for project beneficiaries
4) Monitoring and Evaluation
Project Output:
1) Built capacities of 400 IDPs on poultry farming and market gardening.
2) 280 established poultry and 120 market gardening farms.
3) Target communities sensitized on Capacity building workshops and food insecurity.
Expected Result:
Improved livelihood of 400 IDPs and their families and improvement in food security situation in the North-West region of Cameroon.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Agriculture is the heartbeat of the economy in Northwest and Southwest Cameroon, and farming is relied upon by the majority of households in the region as a livelihood. Inhabitants of these regions widely reported that the sector was experiencing growth and development prior to the crisis and that families were able to meet their basic needs and afford opportunities such as educating their children as a result. Now with over four years of conflict and displacement, agriculture and farming have been severely affected, and with this has come deterioration in food security. The following have been identified as major causes to food insecurity in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon: Displacement and reduction in labour for cultivation. Households reliant on farming as a livelihood have had to displace from conflict, missing cultivation and harvesting periods or abandoning farms altogether in favour of more secure areas. In addition, for those farms that have remained functional, labour to cultivate and harvest is not available which has affected production levels. Restrictions on movement of food and food spoilage. Road insecurity has presented movement restrictions of both people and goods, contributing to increases in food costs and at times the spoilage of food when restrictions make transport and delivery to markers impossible.  Loss of livelihoods and income. Widespread loss of income has meant that families struggle to have sufficient income to meet their families’ food needs. Overall loss in purchasing power has directly contributed to increases in food insecurity in affected populations.  Cost of food. Decreased food production combined with road insecurity and extortion has significantly impacted the cost of food prices in the Northwest region. This means that families have an overall reduction in income and purchasing power while prices for basic food staples have exponentially increased, making it incredibly challenging for families to meet food needs.
