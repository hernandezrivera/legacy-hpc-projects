Project Summary

The project aims to improve the quality, access, and availability of WASH facilities in EPC school and community, through borehole drilling and other infrastructure upgrades. The project will promote improved hygiene practices of students, teachers, and community members in Metuge district through Training of edTrainers (ToTs) and hygiene promotion campaigns.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IsraAID will provide access to safe water and sanitation services to school children, teachers and community members from EPC Impire. IsraAID will build 12 handwashing stations with an integrated water distribution system sourced from 2 new boreholes – currently there are none. The project will ensure equitable access to WASH facilities for girls, women and boys through accessible and visible gender separated latrines. The project will supplement the new WASH infrastructure, by developing and implementing hygiene promotion programs for youth, and the surrounding host community, based on a ToT model. The community WASH training curricula will be adapted to fit the needs of the community members, in a gender sensitive and inclusive way. Around 2,500 students (ages 6-15) and teachers use the EPC infrastructures daily; and the immediate surrounding community use it during after-school hours. Approximately 10,000 people are estimated to benefit from this service. This project is the WASH component of a broader, holistic program centered on Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) for both host and IDP children, where IsraAID supports and promotes the social inclusion and integration of internally displaced children into the community and school system, while providing psycho-social (PSS) support.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Geographical areas: Impire EPC school, reaching the surrounding community and IDP camp in Metuge. Activity 1: Infrastructure development to improve the WASH quality, access, and availability in the Impire EPC school, including: - 2 boreholes drills, 12 handwashing stations. The water supply system in school will be rehabilitated or upgraded.- The water quality will be tested.A volunteer committee will be established in collaboration with community leaders that will be trained on how to maintain the WASH facilities. Equitable access of sanitation facilities for girls, boys, women, and men will be ensured through visible and accessible gender separated latrines and distribution of appropriate hygiene materials. Activity 2: Gender responsive hygiene promotion to improve WASH practices of students, teachers, and community members in Metuge:- Identifying and engaging with focal teachers to design the community WASH training curricula.- Training of trainers (ToTs) programs on relevant topics including hygiene promotion and waterborne disease prevention, in a gender-responsive manner (i.e., addressing menstrual hygiene and specific needs for girls and women), conducted in the school community (with PTAs, teachers, youth groups). - Hygiene promotion programs targeting school students.- Hygiene promotion and awareness materials for community members will be developed and distributed by teachers to students. - WASH in Schools Operations and Maintenance program to ensure sustainability and usability of school toilets.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection mainstreaming is an important aspect of IsraAID’s interventions globally. IsraAID Mozambique minimizes the risk of causing harm by running risk assessments and working according to Protection Mainstreaming ethics and guidelines provided by the GPC and the IASC. IsraAID Mozambique’s approach to WASH-related promotion activities is community based, working in close collaboration with local actors and building on existing resources within the community. The project was designed following focus group discussions and 1-2-1 meetings with people with different community roles. To reinforce the community’s role in beneficiary selection, IsraAID actively consults with communities regarding all selection processes. IsraAID will consult with key community members; and run focus group discussions and key informant interviews with vulnerable and underserved people and groups. Groups of special consideration include women; children; unaccompanied or separated children; the elderly; survivors of GBV and those at risk; and people with disabilities or with serious medical conditions. Regular meetings with the school board and key community members will serve as an effective feedback mechanism on project implementation. Affected populations will also be informed in advance of selection criteria, date/time, and process. Reviews will be conducted on a regular basis to ensure inclusiveness, accountability, local ownership, and sustainability. All IsraAID Mozambique staff (including drivers, volunteers, schoolteachers), partners, and service providers will be trained on advanced Protection Principles, with additional emphasis on the needs of women and children in emergencies. IsraAID staff and partners will sign the IsraAID Code of Conduct, which includes the prevention of PSEA. IsraAID is committed to preventing and combating Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), which includes all situations where a person in a position of power, authority, or in control of resources. This includes situations where a person seeks or accepts Protection assistance in exchange for sexual acts or favors.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

IsraAID Mozambique’s methodologies include training and capacity building of local actors to promote empowerment, participation and adaptation to context and culture. Latrines will be built in areas that are safe and its locations will be decided following consultation with women of the community. Latrines will also be segregated for men and women to ensure dignity for women and girls. IsraAID Mozambique will use a multi-sectoral approach to training local service providers and community focal points on ethics and guidelines of intervention, CP risks, referral pathways and PSS. These trainings are aimed at strengthening the referral pathways and the prevention and support mechanisms to CP issues within the community. The training will emphasize concepts of ensuring access to support services, PSEA, and setting up complaint and feedback mechanisms in the community to ensure safety and security and prevent abuse and discrimination.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

All activities will be under-pinned by the principles of equity and inclusion. All WASH services will be inclusive and accessible to all community members. People with disabilities and older persons will be consulted during the design, implementation, and monitoring of the project. Consultations will be done with individuals living with disabilities and the elderly to identify specific adaptation needs for infrastructure and the infrastructure will be adapted for their needs. Water points and latrines will be accessible to individuals with mobility issues.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This project will consider environmental impacts and ensure that mitigation measures are applied. For example:- Educating IsraAID Mozambique staff, children, and community members on the importance of water conservation across all WASH practices.- Assessments will be conducted to ensure that water points are in appropriate sites where implementation causes minimal environmental harm (e.g., soil degradation, loss of biodiversity etc.).- Water quality testing will be conducted to ensure water is safe and appropriate mechanisms in place to maintain water quality. - Ensuring use of local materials that are durable and resistant. - Minimizing car use as much as possible.
