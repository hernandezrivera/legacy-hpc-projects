Project Summary

The proposed intervention is implemented jointly by Norwegian Church Aid (NCA) and its local partners-Ekklesiya Ya’uwa Nigeria (EYN), Federation of Muslim Women Association (FOMWAN) and Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF) in 4 conflict affected Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Borno state, Nigeria.
It focuses on provision of lifesaving GBV assistance to the most vulnerable populations (women, men, girls and boys) in conflict affected areas while taking into account the gender dimensions of the COVID-19 pandemic. Evidence from other parts of the world suggest that the pandemic creates heightened risk for women and girls due to increased burden of care, in addition to other GBV risks such as intimate partner violence. Similarly, economic hardships may expose women and girls to the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse as witnessed during the Ebola outbreak in 2003-2016.
The intervention will, therefore, enhance timely access to critical GBV services for vulnerable individuals, including GBV survivors through the provision of remote GBV case management and psychosocial support services to 86% of GBV survivors using hotlines on mobile phones using 16 phone booths in existing women and girls safe spaces in four communities, support referrals to lifesaving health services, remote counselling, follow-up sessions and mass dissemination of key GBV prevention messages integrated into prevention messages on COVID-19 delivered through targeted sensitizations, with specific sessions targeting boys and men. The project will provide access to critical risk mitigation items such as dignity kits and stockpiles of equipment and supplies for the continuation of income generation activities to mitigate negative coping mechanism during the peak of the outbreak. Adolescent girls will also be provided with delivery kits to be used in health facilities during the outbreak. The proposed intervention feeds into the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020-2021 (HRP) and is coordinated through respective GBV sub sector cluster with lead agencies as well as other partners in the target areas.
The affected populations (women, men, girls and boys) are in the center of the intervention and participatory methods are used throughout the project cycle management to ensure ownership and sustainability of project results. In addition to that, NCA GBV intervention focuses on strengthening the GBV response through capacity building, especially in specialized GBV programming that will allow NCA staff and partners to provide quality services to GBV survivors

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence occurs in every society worldwide and is exacerbated in emergencies. In Borno state, Nigeria, approximately 1,015,865 individuals are in need of GBV services. Among these 443,328 are IDPs, 201,949 are returnees, 370,588 are host communities. this is according to the GBV 2020 HRP/HNO. With the COVID-19 pandemic, prolonged quarantine and other measures taken to reduce the spread of the disease - including restriction of movement of service providers and beneficiaries- are putting girls and women at heightened risk of GBV and preventing them from accessing critical services and social networks. Normal service provision schedule have been disrupted greatly and service providers have started withdrawing frontline personnel from deep field locations due to fear of contracting the virus, leaving affected women and girls with limited or no option of accessing services. Pre-existing gender inequalities and harmful norms coupled with increased exposure to abusers at home creates the right environment for violence to thrive. In almost all the countries affected by the pandemic, there are unsettling amounts of information on the surge of GBV, with women and girls at the receiving end. Further, the economic fallout from small business closures and livelihood loss will lead to increased household stress which can increase the risk for IPV and lead to risky coping strategies including survival sex, early/forced marriage and increase the risk of SEA. In addition, evidence from previous epidemics also shows that during health crises, women typically take on additional physical and psychological burdens as caregivers and this may be overwhelming for many. In view of the foregoing, the main objective of this intervention is to enhance timely access to critical services including GBV case management, psychosocial supports, material support including dignity, MHM and delivery kits for GBV survivors, adolescent girls and women and other vulnerable individuals while also taking concrete actions to prevent the spread of COVID-19 through early information sharing in different local languages. NCA will implement directly and also engage closely and provide technical supports to three of its local partners in different locations for the response.

2 - Activities

GBV case management- The intervention will enhance timely access to critical multisectoral GBV services to vulnerable individuals, including GBV survivors through the provision of remote GBV case management and psychosocial support services using hotlines on mobile phones to support referrals to lifesaving services, remote mobile counselling and follow-up sessions. Existing safe spaces equipped with internet facilities including installation of solar energy at the spaces so that survivors can promptly access and call GBV caseworkers and receive support . This is in a bid to minimise the challenges of restriction of movement, lock downs thus, hindering provision of essential GBV services to women and girls. Locations chosen for this modality are current NCA project locations where there are no internet, mobile networks. Infection Prevention Control (IPC) protocols including social distancing policies, provision of thermometers and hand washing facilities will be strictly adhered to and monitored. only 4 individuals are permitted in the centre at any one time. four safe space volunteers recruited and trained within the community/among the women will be available to help with any technical issues and to ensure hygiene management of the mobile phones in the safe space. NCA will implement the GBVIMS+ to ensure quality data management. Dignity kits and other material assistance- NCA and its partners will provide context and age-appropriate dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls to promote their dignity and safety. NCA will preposition and distribute dignity kits, MHM and delivery kits for women and girls at designated safe spaces, isolation and health facilities. To improve access to income opportunities for vulnerable women and girls’ and mitigate negative coping mechanisms NCA will engage women and girls in the production of bar and liquid soap and reusable face masks which will be included in the kits. this will be done in close collaboration with NCAs WASH teams to ensure quality and NCA local partner FOMWAN, who will support women and girls in the production of these kits. Livelihood training kits will also be prepositioned in the safe space to be given to women and girls while they at home to continue producing materials and sell out as required to ensure an alternative means of livelihood. GBV and COVID-19 messaging: NCA and its partners will design communication materials, with key GBV and COVID-19 messages and disseminate within the affected communities. Hotlines will be used to share key messages on COVID-19. Radio sensitizations will be utilized to inform communities on COVID 19 prevention with GBV messaging incorporated. Capacity Building: Training on GBV remote service delivery will be provided to staff to ensure quality care to GBV survivors.
