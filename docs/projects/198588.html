Project Summary

YIPDI is proposing an 11-month intervention in MMC, Dikwa, Maffa, Jere and Madagali LGAs,Borno State to respond to child protection needs. The goal of the project is to reduce conflict-related risks and vulnerabilities affecting children, adolescents, and caregivers by strengthening access to a range of life-saving, child-sensitive, and youth-friendly protection services reducing and responding to children’s exposure to violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect. These services include 1) providing children with quality and tailored case management services, including support to ongoing caseloads and responding to newly identified cases 2) reinforcing the provision of psychosocial support to children, adolescents, and caregivers in need (including piloting of YIPDI’s remote PSS curriculum for children and adolescent in case of emergency or restriction of movement (12-18 years) to improve stress coping mechanism; 3) strengthening community capacities, through training/mentoring of Community-based CP focal points and volunteers, to protect children against abuse and exploitation as well as health-related risks, using an integrated approach with WASH and health teams.
YIPDI is recognized in the NE as a major CP actor with strong expertise and fieldwork experience in
Maiduguri, Dikwa, Mafa and Bama, and Damboa Through its work, YIPDI has developed excellent working
relationships with local authorities and is well accepted by community members. YIPDI will use this
to tailor this intervention and maximize its impact and beneficiary reach.

1 - Needs

According to UN OCHA in the BAY states, a total of 8.4 million people are in need of protection and humanitarian assistance. This includes 2.2 million IDPs, 1.5 million IDPs, 3.9 million host community members, and 700,000 individuals in areas not accessible to humanitarian actors. Of the total persons in need, 80% are women and children. The global percentage of persons with disabilities is 15% and is expected to be higher in locations with conflict. The closure of official camps in Maiduguri municipality by the Borno State Government has resulted in secondary and tertiary displacement and upwards of 150,000 IDPs in Maiduguri will be affected (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 Northeast Nigeria). Child Protection needs have increased in 2023 due to the sustained military offensive leading to the high influx of people from NSAG, inflation, high poverty rates, and relocations of IDPs, etc. Priority needs are case management services for victims of abuse, neglect, and violence, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative community care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), socio-economic reintegration of former CAAG and other vulnerable children, addressing child marriage, trafficking, child labor, low birth registration rates, social norms affecting children’s rights and critical support to children with disabilities. Children are constantly at risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect, and violence and children overall are more likely to be exploited as many are pressured to fend for themselves. The majority of CP mechanisms at the community level have been broken, family separation- Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) in targeted areas are particularly vulnerable to increasing violence and abuse, as family dignity and integrity has been severely impacted and family separation compromises children’s access to basic services. Approximately 2.1 million people need child protection services due to the absence of child protection systems and community-based safety nets, out of which 1.3 million children need child protection services, The AoR aims to reach 1.5m PiN (1,050,000 children, 53% girls, and 450,000caregivers, 53% women). The CP AoR prioritizes 13 LGAs in Adamawa, 21 in Borno and 14 in Yobe with the highest severity ranking of child protection needs as per the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023. To ensure that all children boys and girls are free from violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, trafficking, and harmful practices and are safe /thriving to their full potential, the AoR will focus on prevention and response with a systems-strengthening approach in consideration of the humanitarian and development nexus as well as the evolving situation in the Northeast (Child Protection Capacity Needs Assessment and Capacity Development Plan, Northeast Nigeria, 2022).

2 - Activities

Provide child protection case management (CM) services to children meeting YIPDI vulnerability criteria, implemented through a community-based approach, either remotely or face to face. Provide refresher training to Case Work Officers and volunteers on case management, safeguarding, preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, identifying children with psychological distress, and new vulnerabilities/risks in line with CPMS, Inter-Agency CM guidelines, and YIPDI protocols (on risks and mitigation measures). Mental health and psychosocial support services are provided to children, adolescents & caregivers through adapted service delivery methods and need relative to Conducting case management beneficiary satisfaction survey Rehabilitate existing CFS in MMC, Dikwa, Bama, Jere, Maffa, and Madagali to support adult PSS activities in small groups with protective measures in place. Train YIPDI staff and Volunteers to support the implementation of the YIPDI remote PSS curriculum (self-guided home-based PSS during pandemics or disease outbreaks) & Cholera prevention risks & control. provide structured PSS / Structured Emotional Support Group Sessions for male and female caregivers Support/ Facilitate adolescent peer learning through small group sessions to increase knowledge of community members on providing support to children with psychosocial distress. Community-based child protection and referral mechanisms are strengthened to support prevention and response to child abuse & exploitation Support CBPF (with IEC materials: posters and flyers) to deliver community awareness sessions (in small groups) on child protection - risks and mitigation to people in the community. (The sessions will be guided strictly by the risks and mitigation measures imposed by the state authorities) Support CBPF to take initiatives to mitigate violence against children in the community using risk/protection mapping and action to address the identified protection concerns/risks. Provide materials and technical support to CBFPs to develop and action work plans for the identification, reporting, and referral of cases (with support from the YIPDI MEAL team) Conduct monthly mentoring and coaching sessions for the 45 focal points and 3 YIPDI Community Development Officers on cases identification, documentation, and referrals Conduct refresher training for 45 CBFPs and 3 Community Development Officers on safe identification, reporting, and referrals of vulnerable and at-risk children (including children of caregivers) Train 45 Community Based Child Protection Focal Points already in place (CBFP) and 49 volunteers (PSS volunteers, casework volunteers, outreach volunteers) on risk mitigation and community engagement; and existing community services available, The casework volunteers assist the casework officer in identifying the children and refer them for case management. This strategy is justified as YIPDI's must anticipate changes in the context that could prevent the staff (among which the casework officer) to access the field.
