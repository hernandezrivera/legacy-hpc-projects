Project Summary

African Humanitarian Corps (AHC) proposes a multipronged intervention in the selected Counties of Maiwut, Nasir, Panyikang, Rubkona and Ulang linked to FSL Cluster objectives 1, 2 and 3 (CO1,CO2 &CO3). During the implementation period, AHC seeks to conduct both emergency livelihood and capacity-building activities that will contribute to longer-term development and peacebuilding goals while partnering with the communities in the selected counties.
For FSL CO1, AHC plans to support 10,000 IPC 4&5 crisis affected individuals, including women, and men with food assistance for assets (ACL) while offering nutrient-dense supplementary food. These planned activities are linked to WFP CSP 2022-2025 Activity 4: Engage targeted communities in resilience building activities including through community asset creation and climate adaptation using a conflict-sensitive and gender-transformative approach. The provision of nutritious food assistance – cereal (sorghum), pulses, oil, and salt – will address immediate hunger needs, especially during the lean season, and save lives amongst the increasing number of people requiring urgent humanitarian assistance within the counties.
For CO2, AHC seeks to support 20,000 IPC 4&3 crisis affected individuals, including women, girls, and men with emergency livelihood support for emergency food production by providing the capacity to grow food or harvest fish in a matter of weeks, cultivate crops to harvest and protect livelihoods by livestock vaccination and treatment (IPC3+ population) and small ruminants restocking. Linked with the areas seasonality, these activities will be implemented either during the rain (MSR) or the dry season (DSR) periods. This will be accomplished through the distribution of season and locality specific vegetable and crop seeds, farming tools and fishing gear including monofilaments, fishing hooks, twines, okra, sorghum, maize, watermelon and eggplants. AHC will also conduct livestock vaccination and treatment activities against common livestock diseases including Hemorrhagic Syndrome (HS), Black Quarter (BQ), Anthrax, Contagious Bovine Pleural Pneumonia (CBPP) in 200,000 cattle and Contagious Caprine Pleural Pneumonia (CCPP), Sheep and Goat pox, Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR) and rabies in 300,000 shoats.
For CO3, AHC will conduct capacity-building initiatives for 5000 women, men, persons living with disabilities to contribute to their longer-term development and peacebuilding goals through training of women, youth, persons living with disabilities on economic skills that are aligned to government longer term development plans and priorities. AHC will seek to build community resilience capacities by providing pest & drought resistant crops, implement DRR initiatives and the development of community-based Early Warning Systems.
The project will ensure that at least 50% of the beneficiaries are female. AHC will ensure that all child protection measures are taken from inception to exit, including ensuring that AHC processes do not expose children to harm. AHC will ensure PWDs and PWD headed households, are adequately mapped during community mapping, and prioritized during beneficiary selection and inputs distribution.
AHC will also seek to create synergies with other humanitarian sectors and actors including UNFAO, WFP, UNICEF, Education, shelter NFI, Protection, Nutrition, Health and others through sharing of beneficiaries lists, common logistic initiatives and platforms. This will not only avoid duplication of activities, but also ensure holistic assistance same beneficiaries so that they gradually become more self-sufficient in food security.

1. Needs

WFP/UNFAO CFSAM report indicates that 2022 net cereal production (after deduction of post-harvest losses and seed use) in the traditional sector is estimated at about 936 200 tonnes, 11.5 percent above the 2021 output and 15.8 percent above the average of the previous five years (2018-2021).The Sudan crisis in mid-April 2023 compounded with the suspension of food aid in Ethiopian refugee camps, culminated into the influx of South Sudan refugee returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia into Upper Nile State counties (FSLC May 2023). Currently, Maiwut, Longechuk and Luakpiny/Nasir continue to receive returnees and refugees through Burbyei border point of South Sudan-Ethiopian border and Renk border point of Sudan -South Sudan Border (Maiwut and Luakpiny/Nasir IRNA August 2023). On 15th August 2023 one refugee returnee destined for Ulang was killed in passive revenge attack leaving the remaining household members stranded in Nasir (AHC field report, 2023). This has created fear in many returnees due to revenge target because of negative historical information related to actions of their clan members.The displaced persons from Sudan reach the South Sudan border mostly exhausted and without enough money to proceed further to their final settlement (WFP August 2023). Most of them are very vulnerable (including women and under 5 children) in need of immediate assistance and support. The Sudan conflict has negatively impacted South Sudan’s economic outlook for 2023, particularly for Upper Nile and Unity States which are largely dependent on imports from Sudan and now seeing skyrocketing of the prices of basic commodities (WFP Sudan Emergency Response, May 2023). The Fuel cost in Upper Nile has shot up to 60% within two weeks and food prices are up by more than 30% in some areas, and they are likely to increase further. With border markets not functioning and the rainy season affecting logistical movements of goods by road, humanitarian operations will require the use of airlifting and potentially waterway transportation to assist refugees, returnees, and third country nationals at borders and transit facilities. The IRNA report in Maiwut (August 2023) indicates that the returnees in Luakpiny/Nasir have high food consumption gaps only affording a single meal a day. 88% of the new arrivals from Sudan Crisis are food insecure in comparison to the host community (FSNMS R29 August 2023). Luakpiny/Nasir is classified in Band A first level of needs in the recent Needs Analysis Working group (NAWG) (August-Sept 2023); Band A to E from highest level of needs to the lowest respectively. Below-average rainfall across much of South Sudan in July and August has eroded the cumulative rainfall performance across the country increasing portions of the country with deepened cereal deficits (FEWS NET, September 2023). The report also projects that the crop harvest in 2023 is likely to be similar to or slightly below last year and the five-year average.Cash transfers are a cost-efficient and effective way of accelerating progress towards the Sustainable Development Goal of Zero Hunger by 2030. Cash transfers are effective where food is available in the markets but beyond people’s financial reach, whereas physical food deliveries help respond better in emergency situations where basic market infrastructure is no longer functioning or cannot be revitalized using proactive market support tools (CALP Network, 2023).

2. Project activities

Overall Objective: Livelihood support and improving communities resilience to seasonal, climate shocks and prevention of negative coping strategies by IPC 3&4 populations in Maiwut, Panyikang, Longechuk, Nasir and Ulang Counties Output 1: 20,000 IPC 4&3 crisis affected individuals, including women, and men supported with food assistance for assets (ACL) while offering nutrient-dense supplementary food.Activity 1.1: Asset creation and Livelihood (ACL)Construction of community infrastructures including multipurpose ponds, rehabilitation of access roads and constructions of dykes. For ACL and cash for work activities, the assets for rehabilitation will be identified through community-based participatory planning methodology comprising of representatives of various socio-economic groups; men, women, youth, local leaders, people with disability, religious leaders. Activity 1.2: Distribution of food assistance to ACL participantsAHC will distribute cereal (sorghum), pulses, oil, and salt to ACL participants to address immediate hunger needs, especially during the lean season.Output 2: 20,000 IPC 4&3 crisis affected individuals, including women, girls, and men supported with emergency food production and capacity building initiatives Activity 2.1: Direct distribution of 15,000 crop, vegetable and fishing kits to vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in each cropping season for 2024-2025. AHC being UNFAO partner will benefit from FSL cluster pipeline for direct distribution of crop, fishing and vegetable kits. Also, to improve the effectiveness and efficiency of humanitarian action as detailed on "Grand Bargain", common logistic & coordination services in South Sudan will be used.Activity 2.2: Purchase of 5,000 crop & vegetable seeds from local farmers through Inputs Trade Fares (ITF) and distribute to vulnerable households. The purpose is to spur local seeds production capacity and markets where applicable in the target counties.Activity 2.3: Treatment and vaccination of 200,000 cattle, 300,000 shoats, 1000 poultry and 300 equineThe livestock will be vaccinated against endemic diseases in the target areas including Hemorrhagic Syndrome (HS), Black Quarter (BQ), Anthrax, Contagious Bovine Pleural Pneumonia (CBPP) and Contagious Caprine Pleural Pneumonia (CCPP), Sheep and Goat pox, Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR) and rabies.AHC will undertake consultation with communities, ministries, marginalized groups representatives and other relevant stakeholders at all levels of project implementation. Activity 2.4: Small ruminant restockingAHC will purchase and distribute 500 sheep and goats to IPC3&4 vulnerable households and women groups in the selected countiesOutput 3: Capacity-building initiatives for 2500 women, men, persons living with disabilities Activity 3.1: Train 2000 farmers and fisherfolks improved crop and fish production, preservation of harvest entrepreneurship, VSLAs and market accessActivity 3.2: Train 1000 farmers on climate-smart agriculture and support local NRM and DRR mechanisms to disseminate EWS data to farmers in a timely manner.Activity 3.3: Training and support of 200 CAHWs and livestock extension workers on animal diseases Early Warning system (EWS) disease outbreaks investigation and reportingActivity 3.4: establishment of 100 kitchen gardens in selected nutrition facilitiesAHC will establish kitchen gardens in selected areas benefiting 100 male children, 100 female children, 100 elderly men, 100 elderly women, 200 men and 225 womenThe proposed project interventions will prevent households adopting negative coping and help protect/restore livelihoods assets, build resilience, linking emergency response interventions to longer-term recovery-development programming for households and communities by addressing household/community shocks.
