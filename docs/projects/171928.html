Project Summary

Seven years of protracted conflict in Eastern Conflict Area (ECA) has put 3.4 million people in need of humanitarian aid, among which 119,314 require Shelter/Non-Food Items Assistance, including Winterization needs (OCHA, 2020). Civilians living close to the Line of Contact (LoC), where the majority of security incidents take place, remain exposed to significant risks and compounding vulnerabilities which have been further exacerbated following the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in March 2020.
With the incoming harsh Ukrainian winter season (2020-2021), people’s coping capacities are likely to be exhausted. Those who were in situations of impoverishment before, especially women, the elderly and those with disabilities, are expected to worsen due to the pandemic. In addition to already vulnerable populations whose needs will be compounded by the intersection of COVID-19 with conflict, new vulnerabilities profiles are likely to appear in this context; including in urban and rural areas further from the LoC where so far have tended to be observed the most severe needs.

In this context it is crucial to increase the coping capacity of the most vulnerable conflict-affected households both living close to the LoC and in urban centres, to ensure that they can meet their basic winterization needs (including heating, warm clothes, etc.) during the incoming winter, noting that HHs health expenditures will likely increase as a result of the pandemic.
ACTED thus proposes to support the coping capacity resilience of conflict-affected populations on locations within 20km of the LoC (GCA), through the distribution of winterization cash grants in GCA in the lead up to winter.
Cash transfers are suitable and appropriate to meet these needs: there is in ECA access to functional local markets that can absorb the increase in demand; support local traders and market systems; enable choice and help maintain dignity, providing the resources people need to prioritize their own specific needs depending on what is best for them and their family; and empower beneficiaries, giving them more control over how to prioritize their own needs when facing shocks and the protracted conflict. Through direct cash assistance, benchmarked at 8,000 UAH/household/season (covering 50% of seasonal needs as per Shelter Cluster Winterization Guidelines 2019-2020 – subject to revision for the next winter season, in which case ACTED would align the revised recommendation), beneficiaries will be able to cover costs related to heating and other winterization items such as winter clothing – which according to an ACCESS Consortium study 74% of older people (76% women) reported needing - essential survival during the harsh winter months. This aid will especially benefit women and the elderly (who make up 60 and 34% of the population in ECA, respectively) given women in ECA have fewer livelihood opportunities then men in the area and 99,46% of older women and men rely on a pension as their main source of income (ACCESS, 2020).
Implementation will benefit from ACTED's experience providing cash assistance (cash for food security, cash for winterization, multi-purpose cash) in all raions/cities bordering the LoC since 2018 and ACTED's expertise as a leading cash actor (incl. as co-lead of Ukraine's Cash Working Group).

1 - Needs

In GCA, rural and urban areas within 5km of the LoC have the highest proportion of HHs with unmet and overlapping needs (shelter: 24%, REACH 2019). In 2020, 22% of households within 20km of the LoC in GCA reported living in partially destroyed or damaged shelter due to conflict and HHs report spending on average 862 UAH/month on rent (REACH, 2020).Data collected on shelter needs has mainly focused on direct conflict impact such as physical damage to housing due to security incidents, however the price of utilities and fuel is also a driving factor. According to recent Joint Market Monitoring (JMM), average price for firewood in Aug 2019 was UAH793 but increased to UAH886 in Aug 2020 (in line with the annual upward trend observed over several years). The price per ton of coal also increased from UAH3786 in August 2019 to UAH4306 in August 2020. In more rural areas which do not have ready access to main/central heating and must rely on solid fuel (coal, firewood) HHs face continued challenges to afford sufficient heating. Thus, 68% of households surveyed in the 0-20km area (GCA) by REACH in 2019 reported cost as the most frequent problem in accessing fuel, vs 9% reporting availability. A significant 39% and 37% of surveyed households also reported dissatisfaction with the quality of coal and wood, respectively, that are accessible on the market. With COVID-19 and its socio-economic impacts, conflict-affected households’ financial capacity to meet the costs of heating and other winterization items is likely to be even lower next year.Winterization needs vary across gender and age groups – stemming from different socio-economic vulnerability profiles. A previous ACTED winterization cash program targeted – on the basis of vulnerability – 65% women and 33% of elderly: among those aged 60 and above, 77% were women and 33% men. Looking at ACTED program data, single elderly women have the lowest ability to undertake shelter renovation (ACCESS 2018), and 60% of households of unemployed pre-pensioners who benefitted from multi-purpose cash spent at least part of it on shelter needs compared to 48% on average across other vulnerable categories (ACCESS 2019).Under a previous ACTED cash-for-winterization program (2018-2019), on average, beneficiaries spent 52% of the assistance on fuel, 24% on heating, 6% on winter clothes and shoes, and 4% on purchasing stoves and heaters. ACTED’s last ACTED’s MPC program, beneficiaries spent on average 29% of the assistance on shelter needs (2019).

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome: Critical winterization needs of 750 most vulnerable HHs in GCA are met Activity 1: Distribution of cash-for-winterization in GCA Building on current intervention, ACTED will distribute cash for winterization in GCA, following the Cluster Recommendation to prioritize monetized modalities where markets are functional. Beneficiary selection will be based on a compound vulnerability assessment carried out prior to distribution and ACTED will undertake a HH income and HH status/composition assessment, incl. age, gender, and disability status of HH members, to identify beneficiaries’ shelter and livelihood-related coping strategies at baseline. Project-specific selection and assessment methodologies and tools will be adjusted/designed as part of implementation, but will take in account geography eg. HH proximity to LoC, rural v urban settlements, income, vulnerability factors and drivers of humanitarian need incl. combinations of multiple vulnerabilities such as age, disability, isolation, size of household, IDP status, and presence of chronically ill members. Geography may be especially considered given in urban areas families with unemployed pre-pensioners (aged 50-59) will likely face greatest hardship, while in rural areas disability and older age, not necessarily income status, will be greater drivers of vulnerability due to distance/difficulties accessing from essential services. Beneficiary assessment is preceded by community mobilizations, to which representatives including local authorities, community leaders/volunteers, and local population are invited to present the project, type and amount of assistance to be provided, and selection criteria in a participative manner. In light of COVID-19, ACTED will ensure that this is done in a safe manner that protects beneficiaries and staff through social distancing and wearing of PPE. All gender, age and disability groups are invited and their inputs sought for/integrated into program design.ACTED will distribute amounts of cash for winterization depending on the type of heating and fuel used by selected HHs, meeting for all the minimum needs as per Cluster Recommendations. This will also respond to beneficiary preferences and mitigate the differences in price for fuels, noting for instance that firewood is on average cheaper but less convenient to transport, and less adapted to beneficiaries with limited mobility than coal. Average cost per HH is 273 USD. Through these interventions, ACTED aims to support the needs of the most vulnerable with the provision of cash to support winterization. Taking into careful consideration the specific needs of groups with compounded and high vulnerability, ACTED will conducted beneficiary selection through the lens of household compound vulnerability selection rather than taking a solely status-based approach (which merely considers individual status markers without considering the layers and complexities which drive humanitarian vulnerability and need). This will naturally lead activities to benefit the most vulnerable, among which are women and the elderly.Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be promoted during the activity, and beneficiaries will be encouraged to report on any concerns or risks associated with implementation.Finally, post-distribution monitoring will be conducted on a representative sample of beneficiaries (i.e. including disaggregations based on gender, age, disability status and others) to generate feedback from beneficiaries on the relevance, appropriateness and effectiveness of the intervention and satisfaction with the assistance received.
