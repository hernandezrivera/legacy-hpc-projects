Project Summary

According to HNO 2021, 6.4 M people need WASH services in Somalia, of these 4.1 M people are children and 1 M women as well as 965,000 people living with disability, with 1.6 M internally displaced people, the country has suffered a severe drought coupled with the global pandemic of COVID-19 since March 2020. Major needs persist with increases in displacement figures in the Lower Shabelle region there has been a build-up of pressure on existing resources. Heavy rainfalls affected more than 289,400people in August 2020. WASH infrastructures suffered from important damages in many parts of Galgadud and Mudug region target districts. Most of these infrastructures have not been repaired yet. Because of the floods and the chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, Somalia continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2020, and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots Galgadud and Mudug region Unreliable access to a temporary water source and/or unprotected water is still an important feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Nationwide, access to an improved water source remains below 40%, with large variations from one region to another. In drought-affected regions, water scarcity and poor quality are leading causes of population displacement and conflict, particularly in south-central regions, respectively 69 and 35% of households need more than 30 minutes to reach their main drinking water points. More than half of the population lack access to adequate sanitation. The situation is particularly critical in Lower Shabelle (below 25% each) and IDP settlements. Latrine safety index is worryingly high in all parts of Shabele region district in Somalia, resulting in all categories of users being at high risk of violence when using facilities. Handwashing with soap at the critical moment is practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability to a functional handwashing point is limited this couple with high needs of handwashing due to COVID-19 pandemic that has also put at risk to many population safety. This percentage drops to below 25% in Mudug and Galgadud region, despite that these two states were badly affected by drought and AWD/Cholera recently,
DEH, therefore, intends to provide enhanced water, sanitation, and hygiene promotion to these affected populations including people living with disabilities (PLWDs) in Galgadud and Mudug regions.
DEH will enhance access to appropriate WASH lifesaving and life-sustaining services through construction and rehabilitation of the water points and desludging of sanitation facilities in the community. The project is keen on the provision of safe and adequate water to all vulnerable populations regardless of their abilities. Also, the community will be sensitized on safe utilization of the sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion.
DEH upholds gender equality and inclusion principles thus will align these interventions with gender principles. The water points will be located in safe and accessible sites that would not predispose users to further harm.
The water points and sanitation facilities will be equipped with disability-friendly ramps and support and a pictorial label to distinguish different genders. The project will mainstream protection and provide dignified, equal opportunity, and non-discriminatory services to all irrespective of their abilities. To harness ownership and sustainability the intervention will establish a feedback mechanism for accountability.

1. - Projects activities

1.1Rehabilitation and upgrading of 10 strategic boreholes in 10 droughts, flood and AWD prone areas through replacement of electro-mechanical systems into installation of hybrid solar systems (PV+Generator), construction of elevated tanks, extension of distribution system, construction of new water kiosks hence reaching a total of 8000 vulnerable population in the target district 1.2 Construction and extension of 12 communal water points including water kiosks in IDPs in xaradheere,Hobyo,Dhusamareeb and Cadado and 18 rainwater harvesting systems with storage facilities at health/nutrition sites and schools reaching 8000 men,women,boys,girls and people with disability 1.3 Rehabilitate 20 community water points including roof water catchments in schools, CFS and health/nutrition facilities in both rural and IDPs. 1.4 Create/reactivate and strengthen the capacity of 140 water point management committees and 40 community based hygiene promotion committees in target communities including IDPs, with a focus on gender parity and disable population 2.1 Construction of 150 HH emergency shared latrines(10% for PLWD) in IDPs, AWD/Cholera hotspot areas and drought affected villages with portable hand washing facilities. 2.2 Rehabilitate/Desludge 100 flood affected latrines (10% for PLWD) in Dhusamareeb,Hobyo,Cadado and xaradheere district 2.3 Construction of 100 gender segregated latrine blocs with hand washing stations in health/nutrition facilities and public schools with attention to ensuring proper sanitary facilities for girls, in line with SPHERE standards 2.4. Construction of 30 medical incinerators at health centers for proper treatment of medical waste 3.1 Conduct community hygiene promotion sessions to promote good hygiene and sanitation practices in targeted communities, health/nutrition facilities and schools 3.2. Conduct community meetings to sensitize people on women inclusion in the management of community WASH committees 3.3. Conduct woman to woman sessions on hygiene promotion to focusing on menstrual hygiene management (MHM) and safe drinking water within the family.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project is meant to build community resilience by strengthening local water infrastructure and thus provide timely action, maintaining the momentum of the response, and expanding reach in underserved areas promoting equity. Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services, where priority will be given to the delivery of equitable access to basic Wash service services, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk to the vulnerable IDPs and host communities. To increase community uptake of provided services, community-level demand creation will be strengthened. DEH will strengthen the capacity of the WASH committee by training them on life-skill, Wash service, and technical project staff as well as community Wash service. Communities will be involved in project designing and will be part of the committees supporting. Working with other actors will ensure leverage amongst themselves and provide maximum support to target communities strengthening their capacities in various aspects, For sustainable and equitable access of the donor, funded project DEH will ensure community inclusion in the initial decision making of the project design and structure, in this case, the WASH component will prioritize the needs of the community and in such will ensure to capacitate the existing community wash committee as well as the staff that will ensure the carryover/sustainability of the project after the expiry of the donor funds. DEH will ensure to provide more responsive services to the affected population under strict supervision and coordination with government and other humanitarian actors within its area of operation. The WASH activities will consider the most vulnerable and disadvantaged groups hence building the community resilience to prevent a recurrent and protracted emergency. DEH will provide sufficient internal control through the dedicated monitoring and accountability officer who will ensure the project activities are implemented by set DEH internal monitoring criteria as well as include the donor and community level contribution hence this will minimize the project financial and quality risk while it also enhances the accountability to the affected population DEH will ensure to capacity build its staff and community focal point on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) organization policy as well as its code of conduct hence ensure proper understanding of the PSEA policies and procedure hence reduces the risk related with PSEA
