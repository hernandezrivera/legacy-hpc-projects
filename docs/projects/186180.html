Project Summary

Through the Project, unconditional food assistance in-kind and cash-based transfers will be provided to 106,000 the vulnerable CAR refugees to have access to adequate and nutritious food, thus prevent them from adopting negative coping strategies to sustain their food needs. Monthly food distributions will be organized for the targeted beneficiary households for 12 months. The full ration of the in-kind and cash distributions will provide 2,100 kcal energy per day per person for the beneficiaries who rely entirely on humanitarian food assistance. the food rations comprise 370g cereals 120g pulses 40g vegetable oil and 5g salt. The daily ration for cash-based transfers is set at 0.2 USD Dollars.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon has welcome over 300,000 CAR refugees that were uprooted from their homes due to the socio-political crisis in CAR since March 2003. These refugees are hosted in Cameroon in the regions along the border with CAR. About 800 km of Cameroon borders are shared with CAR and also the cross-border ethnic affinities between the two countries. According to UNHCR CAR refugees that entered Cameroon from different arrival waves (2003, 2008 and 2014) are settled in the East, Adamawa, and North regions, mainly in the rural areas. About 30% of these them are established in 7 refugee sites created by NHCR (Ngarissingo, Lolo, Mbile, Timangolo, Gado, Borgop and Ngam,) while the remaining 70% live in more than 100 villages across 23 councils in these three regions. This refugee population is largely made up of children, young people and women, from various ethnic groups including Mbororo, Gbaya, Haoussa, Kolé, Kako, Foulbé, Mbimo, Mvon-Mvon and Yanguéré. The demographic pressure resulting form the presence of the CAR refugees has disrupted the economic and social order of the three regions, and put additional pressure on already scarce resources, resulting in increased vulnerabilities, and humanitarian-peace-development challenges. The vulnerability targeting exercise conducted by UNHCR and WFP has identified 112,000 people and WFP is targeting the 106,000 most vulnerable and highly food insecure refugees in need for immediate food assistance. The moderately vulnerable refugees will be catered through early recovery and livelihood support through HDP Nexus approach. Despite this stability, the refugee households rely on unstable food sources, mainly food assistance, humanitarian cash transfers and donations from well-wishers. Consumption from own production is low-conversely, dependence on markets is high for both refugees and host communities, which raises questions on the purchasing power of these households and their capacity to access to food. Natural resources are highly impacted by the presence of CAR refugees so as basic social infrastructures that were already precarious. The cohabitation of refugees and host population raises the problem of social cohesion mainly resulting from the competition over scarce resources, food and fodder between farmers and pastoralists. The situation has led to an increase in food needs, as well as a deterioration in the provision of basic social services and severe environmental degradation. Due to lack of funding, the various type of assistance provided by humanitarian actors and the Government of Cameroon could not be optimal thus could not ensure sustainable food security and basic needs for this population.
