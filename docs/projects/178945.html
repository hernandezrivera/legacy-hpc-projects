Project Summary

WFP, as the lead agency for Logistics Sector, aims to oversee all the strategic objectives from the sector, namely: 1. provide logistics services (inclusive of surface transportation, transhipment, and warehousing) to humanitarian organisations responding to the Syria crisis; 2. maintain regional WoS interagency logistics coordination and information management in order to support the overall humanitarian response; 3. enhance the response capacity of humanitarian actors via dedicated logistics trainings and the procurement of necessary equipment; and 4. Continue the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) to provide safe, reliable and sustainable air access to beneficiaries for the humanitarian community, medical and security evacuations and facilitating the movement of light cargo where required.

4 - Needs

Based on feedback from regular consultations with partners, key needs identified include the provision of access to common logistics services that include air cargo and surface transportation, warehousing and cross-border transhipment services. These will be reinforced by a robust logistics coordination and information management platform with regular coordination meetings taking place to ensure a predictable and secure logistics supply chain for responding organisations. WFP also remains on standby to facilitate emergency access to fuel as a last resort, in case availability and humanitarian needs become critical. In 2021, WFP plans to provide capacity strengthening opportunities to organisations through diverse logistics trainings to augment logistics expertise. These trainings will be implemented in line with partners’ expressed needs. Due to COVID-19 restrictions, the initial focus will be on remote training solutions. Responding actors showed a need for domestic passenger and cargo flights operating from Damascus to Qamishli, Aleppo as well as other regions in Syria. The cluster as whole maintains a flexible approach that allows it to adapt its services, taking into account the situational needs, and augment or reduce capacities as required.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Logistics Cluster will maintain common logistics services by ensuring that WFP’s warehouse contracts for different locations are in place for 2021 in order to maintain sufficient storage capacity inside Syria, and coordinating inter-agency humanitarian deliveries to newly accessible areas by communicating with the different actors. The Logistics Cluster will also coordinate cross-border transhipment operations by maintain the contract with the hub owner and taking all the necessary security and COVID-19 measures required for ensuring the operations are not interrupted. In addition, the Logistics Cluster will continue to organise meetings in Damascus, Gaziantep, and other field locations to better respond to the different needs of organisations and provide partners with operational updates and logistics information on regular basis. While remaining focused on filling logistics gaps faced by the programmatic sectors in the Syria response, the Logistics Cluster will also bring subject matter experts to conduct trainings across Whole of Syria in order to enhance the capacities of humanitarian actors, enabling them to better implement sectoral response plans. UNHAS will continue passenger and light cargo air services for the humanitarian community using medium size jet aircraft CRJ200 in compliance with UNAVSTADS. UNHAS Steering Committee will provide overall governance on the smooth running of UNHAS and the User Group will promote interest of users in the movement of passengers and cargo in order to fulfil the humanitarian needs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

WFP ensures that the service providers contracted abide by labour rules and regulations in-country. WFP will maintain the already implemented reporting mechanism for harassment cases in Logistics Cluster warehouses, and furthermore, organisations will be encouraged to nominate female candidates to attend trainings organized. Gender sensitive and gender appropriate facilities will also be made available during a training. Finally, agency GBV focal points will be invited to meetings with contracted service providers and humanitarian organisations to give guidance on issues related to harassment, abuse and gender-based violence.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Logistics coordination and information management remain pivotal in ensuring needs continue to be met while simultaneously maintaining capacity and resources to address any emerging issues. The main risk the Logistics Cluster might face is loss of access. In order to mitigate that risk, the sector will continue to advocate for better access through both OCHA and SARC, explore different routes and assess the available options to reach different locations. In addition, WFP as provider of last resort has long term agreements and contracts with different transport companies which allow flexibility and adaptability in case of any sudden change in the situation on the ground. Safety and security risks that may affect implementation of UNHAS operations will be continuously monitored and relevant mitigation measures put in place to ensure implementation of safe operations. In addition, a lack of fuel availability will severely impact the running of partner logistics operations. WFP is in close discussion with the Ministry of Petroleum and SADCOP for a guaranteed minimum supply for humanitarian logistics operations. Finally, a lack of sufficient funds is also an important risk to take into consideration. Funds need to be made available to adequately respond to emergency developments, while also ensuring the continued implementation of other ongoing sector activities. In such a case, the Logistics Cluster will work in consultation with partners and HCT to ensure that the most critical logistics services are prioritized and remain uninterrupted to meet the most vital needs. In addition, regular monitoring of funding and reporting to donors will allow for best future planning and timely fund raising.
