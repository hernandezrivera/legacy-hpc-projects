Project Summary

The project is responding to the CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence
This proposed project has been designed in response to the needs of the most vulnerable groups, the program looks to focus on supporting the rehabilitation and reintegration of children (boys and girls) affected by military detention, house arrest, and Imprisonment trauma .Save the Children will work with national level actors to strengthen existing protection and education systems to support reintegration of ex-detainees girls and boys, while addressing specific policy and gender based barriers. Strengthen the community child protection mechanisms in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem employing the tested and validated case management in humanitarian action “Steps to Protect” common approach.
The proposed project aims to facilitate the reintegration of ex-detainee children into their communities providing tailor-made packages of psychosocial support for them and their families, enhancing the coping capacity of ex-detainee, and house arrested children and their families. It provides ex-detainee boys and girls and their families relief and strategies that are also preventative to build towards a hopeful future and more resilient capacities. The project adopts a holistic approach towards rehabilitation by providing gender sensitive psycho-social rehabilitation services to ex-detainee boys and girls through individual and group counselling services, educational and career guiding sessions, vocational assessment and support their enrolment in tailored Technical and Vocational Education and Training and employability skills programs, and supporting remedial education and catch up classes and facilitate their reintegration in the formal education system
Legal support for children detained and, Children identified as being at risk or subject to arrest would receive gender responsive case management and referral to appropriate services to provide for their safety. The services would include provision of legal counselling and representation and assistance for detainee children. At early stages, the provision of information, explaining technical terms and direction to available resources can be very important to support children during this traumatic process. Being able to access the appropriate level of support as soon as possible can be beneficial in helping children boys and girls have representation and avoid lengthy jail times.
The project will specifically support the successful reintegration of 300 ex-detainee Palestinian boys and girls within their families and local communities through protection services, remedial education support, mental health and psycho-social and legal support, the project will target 300 parent (males and females) with psychological and wellbeing support and awareness raising interventions, and also involve and benefit 150 Community-Based Organization and NGO staff members (males and females) through capacity-building activities.
SCI, being the leading organization that has historically worked with ex-detainees through national local partners, remains committed to responding to this need, especially that there are a limited number of organizations targeting them. Interventions will be targeting ex-detainee children, including Jerusalemite children in house-arrest in the selected locations in addition to their families, being their main and direct supporters and caregivers

1 - Needs

It is estimated that every year, Israel continues to prosecute between 500 and 700 Palestinian children in military courts. Children exposed to conflict-related violence including detention remain a most vulnerable group. By the end of 2020, 140 children were detained in the Israeli military prisons. The number of arrests reached 543 children. Moreover, in light of the mass arrests and detention that took place in the first half of 2021, a total of 854 children were detained and 225 were arrested of which 5% are girls and 95% are boys. A study conducted by Save the Children (SCI) on the Impact of Military Detention on Children Learning and Well Being surveyed more than 470 children across the West Bank, to hear about their experience of detention and to understand its resounding impact on their lives after release. Children reported they had endured a distressing or violent arrest or detention in most cases at night, a coercive interrogation environment, physical and emotional abuse in detention and a denial of essential services including an adequate education – all of which constitute a breach of their rights enshrined in international law.Many children reported they were denied a meaningful opportunity to defend themselves against allegations, and almost half (47%) were denied timely access to legal assistance, such as before they were interrogated. In every respect, the Israeli military detention system appears designed to render Palestinian children defenseless. Save the Children’s research also reveals the lasting impacts of detention on children’s lives. As a result of the children’s and their families’ willingness to share their experiences and challenges after release, it is clear from their testimony that military detention has profound and far-reaching repercussions on children’s lives. Some boys and girls feel that the damage that has been done to their lives is irrevocable, and nearly all of them bear emotional or physical scars from their time in detention. This manifests anxiety, depression, behavioural changes, eating and sleeping disorders, and physical symptoms including chest pains, exhaustion, and numbness. Detention and the subsequent impact on their health and wellbeing diminish children’s ability to return to their normal lives, maintain relationships with their families and friends, and feel secure in their communities. Children told us how they feel ostracized by their peers and unable to connect with their family and friends, leading to feelings of isolation. Other complex social dynamics hinder children’s ability to reintegrate back into the fabric of their old lives, such as the normalization of the experience due to the prevalence of detention.Even after release, ex-detainee children have little access to psychosocial support and often drop out of school, hence the impacts of their arrest affect them for years to come. The needs of target children are often complex (psychosocial, educational, etc.) and may not be effectively covered by one type of service. Ex-detainees and house arrest children continue to be among the identified vulnerable groups prioritized by the protection cluster that need holistic support to ensure their continued access to comprehensive and inclusive multi sectoral services. More than half of the children consulted for this report were denied access to support and comfort from their families, and in some instances they were made to believe that their families had abandoned them – threatening a key pillar of psychological resilience, jeopardizing their ability to recover. Approximately half of all detained children are arrested at night and report physical and psychological abuse during arrest, transfer, and interrogation (according to Military Court Watch). Approximately 50% of Palestinian child detainees are held in prisons in Israel in breach of Article 76 of the Fourth Geneva Convention. Violations against minors include late night arrests, being transferred to interrogation and detention center without their parents` knowledge, questioning for long periods of time without a lawyer, rest, or food and drink. Confessions are often extracted under threats and intimidation, usually written in Hebrew, a language they do not understand. Even after release, ex-detainee children have little access to psycho-social support and often drop out of school and early engagement of child labor and hazardous forms of child labor and risky behaviors; meaning the impacts of their arrest affect them for years to come. Close to 95% of the total population of ex-detainee children is boys, approximately 5% of the detained children are females who receive special attention and gender responsive services. girls however face additional challenges in their reintegration into their families and communities due to the stigma associated with detention (related to the family concerns on the exposure of their daughters to sexual harassment that will impact on the what they call the family honor and how they and their daughters perceived by the community. All of this leads to school drop out for girls, and increased risk of early marriage and associated risks of domestic violence. The relevant ministries of the Palestinian Authority in charge of the protection of ex-detainee children, among which the Commission of Detainees and ex-Detainees (CDED) and the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), are not yet able to provide effective, coherent, comprehensive multi sector and systematic support to child ex-detainees. This results from the still precarious effectiveness of the Child Protection Networks and the National Referral System integrating the children affected by the conflict violence and rights violations due to the limited financial resources and staff capacity.

Provide Case Management and Referral Pathways Services to Specialized and Multi -Sectoral Services according to assessed and Identified , needs , protection risks and vulnerabilities.- Using database and reports from Al Dameer, AL Haq, DCI and Commission of Detainee and Ex-Detainee, Prisoners club and HaMoked SC and partner will be utilizing their outreach programme and referral of cases from other actors including schools, to identify new cases of boys and girl’s detainee and ex-detainee children including boys and girls in need of specialized psychosocial and protection services. including case management and link them to the age and gender responsive services through the referral system. - Provision of structured age and gender sensitive PSS support sessions to the children (boys and girls) affected by the continuous military attacks on their schools.- Provision of individual and group psychosocial support sessions for ex-detainees and children victims of conflict violence and provide low intense PSS recreational activities.- Provision of legal counselling, representation and assistance for detainee children and their families. DCI female and male lawyers, and in coordination with Save the Children, Adameer, UNICEF, and other stakeholders will be working on providing the legal representation and assistance to the children and youth in military detention. This includes the design and implementation of the awareness raising program at the schools and community center on the rights of the children from the first moment of arrest, during interrogation, booking, trial, and sentencing. DCIP lawyers will provide free legal representation to Palestinian minors and their families in the Israeli military and civil courts. In addition, DCIP lawyers conduct monitoring visits for Palestinian minors in Israeli custody and collect affidavits using a gender lens from them, detailing their arrest, interrogation and detention, to monitor and document GBV, torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment. DCIP lawyers provide legal consultations for children and their families and use the sworn testimonies of Palestinian female and male minors to submit complaints and advocate on their behalf at the international level.In order to prevent and minimize child rights violations in Israeli detention, DCIP will continue to conduct gender sensitive awareness raising campaign called “Know your Rights”. The campaign aims at empowering and educating Palestinian children to secure their basic rights while detained in the Israeli military detention system and civil system in East Jerusalem.- Support children to return to school through catch-up classes and community-based education - Conduct skills aptitude testing of targeted female and male to match them to appropriate Technical Vocational Education and Training (TVET) Opportunities that meet labour market.- Provide gender sensitive vocational and employability and work readiness soft skills trainings to the most vulnerable female and male affected by military detention and violence due to conflict. - Assessment of the psychosocial and mental health condition of the families of ex-detainees and female and male children affected by conflict violence. - Provision of individual and group psychosocial support and awareness sessions for the families of ex-detainee children.- Conduct workshops for parents on child psychosocial well-being and gender sensitive children’s rights. - Develop and disseminate gender sensitive awareness raising media and communications materials on child protection risks and on the importance of the community-based child protection mechanisms.All activities will be implemented with disability inclusion lens, including vulnerability and risk assessment specifically for CWDs who are subject to military detention and home arrest. Moreover, all social workers, case workers, counsellors and any personnel in direct contact with the ex-detainee children will be trained on CPMS in Humanitarian action 2019, ISAC MHPSS Guidelines, PSEA, Child and Adult safeguarding policies as well as accountability to affected people and reporting and complaints mechanism; Including training on home visits protocols with the conflict-sensitive Do No Harm principle with risk analysis and mitigation measures. which will also be embedded and implemented across all project interventions. SC and partners will disseminate child friendly awareness communication materials on PSEA risks and how to safely report any concerns of cases of sexual exploitation and abuse and where to seek help for survivals and victims of SEA. Awareness sessions for adults and children and community members on PSEA and the reporting mechanisms and orient them on the existing accountability mechanism for affected people how to access it and share these mechanisms with other people in target communities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks. SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project will prioritize for example PwDs with means to enhance their access to project services. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to boys, girls, women and men according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create women only working groups to facilitate their participation. SCI, together with project partners, will check during the project visits the problems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse by the family members. Both Men and women will be included in sessions with different interests and fields related mainly to enhancing their protection and building positive resilience and coping strategies. The Gender Advisor will ensure gender mainstreaming in the interventions. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities in communities.
