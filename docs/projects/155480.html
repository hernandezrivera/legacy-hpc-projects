Project Summary

A malnutrition surge is expected due to the anticipated drought, amidst a rapid economic decline and disease outbreaks. The rural areas of Zimbabwe are usually seen as the epicentre of poverty, hunger and malnutrition. However, urban households are particularly vulnerable to food insecurity because of their heavy dependence on food purchases coupled with increases in other urban costs such as housing, water and electricity. The urban population have limited positive copying mechanisms to enhance food and nutrition security as compared to the rural counterparts. The above scenario is expected to deteriorate as the lean season approaches should there be no efforts to scale up response to the dire situation. The most affected areas are new high density suburbs that are not limited to but include; Hopely, Southlands, Stoneridge, Southlea park, Caledonia, Hatcliffe, Eastview /Caledonia (Mabvuku) and Glaudina and Whitecliffe. Interaction with nurses at some of the health facilities shows that there is a gap in terms of the nurse’s knowledge of the guidelines of how to manage malnutrition. The service does not always follow the set procedure of how a child arrives at the clinic, is screened for malnutrition and then referred for the appropriate treatment and receives it. This emanates from gaps in nutrition surveillance, active screening, referral and treatment of children with SAM; information management and reporting; community level support for stunting reduction through IYCF-e and hygiene promotion. There is need to:1. Support Community Health workers(health promoters and volunteers) to ensure effective management of SAM (case finding, referral, defaulter tracing). 2. Support Facility level health workers to effectively manage the IMAM program (prevention, identification, referral, treatment and discharge). 3.Improve care practices at household and community level through IYCF-e. 4. Support the Supply chain management to prevent stock out of nutrition commodities at health facility level 5. Support micronutrient supplementation for children and pregnant women.

Needs

Zimbabwe experienced a succession of climatic changes that led to droughts and floods in the past years over and above socio-economic and political crises in the recent past that led to a significant increase in poverty, food insecurity, and malnutrition. Chipinge, Chiredzi and Masvingo and Harare districts are among the 24 vulnerable districts in light of the lean season. The above scenario is expected to deteriorate as the lean season approaches should there be no efforts to scale up response to the dire situation. There are gaps in nutrition surveillance, active screening, referral and treatment of children with SAM; information management and reporting; community level support for stunting reduction through IYCF-e and hygiene promotion. Hence the need to strengthen service delivery in order to prevent nutrition related morbidities and mortalities during the lean season.
