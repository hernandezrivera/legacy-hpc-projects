Project Summary

The project aims at improving the resilience and the access of 470,000 of the most vulnerable and high risk groups of IDPs, returnees and the affected community in underserved areas to comprehensive package of PHC services. The project will effectively contribute to building the resilience of the community in the protracted and early recovery phase through rehabilitation of infrastructure, building the capacity of health professionals, NGO partners, and communities while ensuring community participation. The project is designed to contribute to the sector response strategy in addressing health needs of new born, children, adolescents, women in reproductive age and beyond, persons with disability, older person, and persons with chronic medical conditions, while mainstreaming GBV prevention and MHPSS in services and in the community through capacity building. The comparative advantage of UNHCR community centers programs will be used to ensure linkages with other relevant sectors and address other factors determinants of health. The project will be implemented in 11 governorates in Syria ( Damascus, rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Daraa, Qunaitra,Tartous, Aleppo, Hasakeh, Derezzor and Raqqa)by 11 NGO and 2 INGO partners. The project has three components (1) Support the delivery of comprehensive package of free of charge PHC services through 10 PHC facilities in 5 governorates to ensure the access of 145,000 IDPs and returnees to : Child and reproductive health, management of CD and NCDs including MHPSS, nutrition awareness, screening and referral, medical response to GBV survivors, health promotion and prevention activities and coordination for referral to advanced care (2) Support of public PHC revitalization in coordination with MoH and WHO by supporting rehabilitation, furnishing and equipping of 9 public health facilities in 7 governorates (Rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Latakia, Aleppo, Qunaitra, and Derezzor ) to ensure the access of some 135,000 PoCs to PHC services (3) support of 38 health points in community centers run by 11 NGOs in 10 governorates and supported by 9 mobile health units. The health points will be functioned by 80 community health workers providing and engaging the community in static, mobile and outreach disease prevention and health promotion activities accessing 190,000 PoCs while ensuring community participation in identifying health risks and supporting the implementation of 152 community led initiatives in health. A minimum of 38 community health teams representing the neighborhoods in the targeted villages will be formed and capacitated. The activities will be guided by local health needs identified by the health workers and the community and by national health priorities and strategies in child health, RH, CDs and NCDs while also including environmental health as an important health determinant factor. Capacity building is vital in the project and will be coordinated with UN agencies and MoH for best use of resources.

4 - Needs

The project will support several response strategy components adopted by the health sector. Based on sector assessment of needs, HeRAMS,EWARS, WHO health assessment at community level and UNHCR offices observations, the country need for revitalization of the health system will continue in 2020, especially in underserved areas which witnessed large scale destruction resulted in 22% of the PHC facilities being destroyed, while 48% are non-functional. Access of the most vulnerable groups in rural areas such as children under five (11.9% ), pregnant women (4%), older persons(2.8%), persons with disabilities(15%) or chronic health conditions to basic care becomes a challenge when no close PHC or transportation means. The coordinated efforts for the revitalization of over 50 public primary health care facilities in 11 governorates which constitute the entry point is vital to ensure sustainable free of charge basic health services for the affected population, meanwhile the temporary support to the provision of a package of PHC services should continue. Medicines is one of the main challenges in all locations, especially NCDs which constituted over 50% of the consumption and ranked as the second in need. The most common morbidities are epidemic prone diseases such as influenza like illness, acute diarrhoea, hepatitis, and NCDs such as ischemic heart disease, hypertension and diabetes, all of which the risk factors are amenable to change and would require joint community efforts and enhanced linkages with actors in WASH, shelter, nutrition and livelihood opportunities. Some areas need enhanced efforts to routine immunization including the soft component of awareness and outreach. Mothers are in need for outreach support to properly care for their new-born at home. The chronic shortage of health care workers especially in rural areas, remains a challenge, while preventive activities and community health promotion remain essential in all areas of Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNHCR will sign partnership agreements with 11 NGO and 2INGO partners. The agreements include the logical framework, unified salary scale, monitoring and reporting requirement. SOPs will guide implementation. Criteria for selection of PHC facilities in coordination with MoH and BoQs approved by MoH will guide the rehabilitation. Health points and PHC clinics will ensure the comprehensive approach through ensuring the CD, NCD care, MHPSS, nutrition package, child, RH and elderly health, GBV prevention and response and referral to advanced health care directly or by networking. Coordination and cooperation will be ensured through meetings and mapping at central and area levels. The delivery modality will be mixed of static, mobile and outreach through volunteers to enhance access. Workshops, short trainings, webinars, on job training and follow up meetings will be used for capacity building of partners and volunteers. Three layers of monitoring will be in place to support quality implementation starting with partners qualified focal point for weekly monitoring and on job training, UNHCR health staff in 6 field offices and multifunctional teams with spot checks field visits of minimum one monthly to different sites followed by visits’ reports for recommendation on follow up, and UNHCR health unit in country office for technical support and field visits, if MoFA approval is granted. Monitoring tools will be used by partners for systemization and unification. Partners will report weekly on the activities quantitively and qualitatively and will report monthly against the indicators the health component will be added to UNHCR information system of the community centers which will assist in accurate and timely reporting. 63,6% of the budget will be transferred to partners 26,9% to INGOs mainly for PHC rehabilitation and 73,1% to 11 local NGOs. Capacity building should cover professionals, workers and volunteers to build resilience and ensure quality delivery.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- The risk of Gender based violence and poor health outcomes among women and girls. Mitigation: health professional in each PHC and health point is equipped to medically manage GBV cases, comprehensive GBV services are available in the community centres including MHPSS, social, legal and referrals to other services. Female health staff are present in health points and in PHC. Updated referral pathways of GBV survivors is available. 2- Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse PSEA. Mitigation: Partners to adopt COC that include PSEA. capacity building plans of partner staff includes COC and SEA. Awareness sessions and materials provided to convey messages to the community. Reporting enhanced through focus group discussions, safe complaint system including hotline. Community participation embedded in the project activities will assist in identifying risks and vulnerabilities and providing with mitigations. Female nurse/physician assistant is always present with the male physician when patients are received and examined. Receptionists and some of the guards in the PHCs are female. The scope of free of charge services is published. Information regarding services are visible in health points and PHCs. 3- Lack of effective humanitarian response due to lack of community engagement and feedback. The project is adopting community based and rights-based approaches, yet partners capacity might impose this risks. Mitigation: Clear staffing requirement and continuous capacity building that includes methods and techniques used to ensure community participation such as structured discussions, focus group discussions, in addition to suggestion/complaint box, outreach services, monitoring and technical support. 4- Risk of inequity. Mitigation: reporting includes disaggregated data for age groups, the outreach component and the balanced structure of community health teams representing the neighbourhoods will improve equitable access to PoC.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Risk: Lack of access hampering needs and impact assessments and protection monitoring of the programs results in continuous relying on estimations and 3rd party reports, which may not accurately reflect needs and impact. Mitigation measures: Maintain and expand UNHCR field presence and monitoring capacity. Advocate with GoS on the need for systematic monitoring and continue the direct and indirect advocacy for access to safe areas. Return to rural areas will need additional efforts, cooperation and coordination to ensure the availability of health services. 2.Risk: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigation measures: Implementing risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote monitoring arrangements. Train/Support Partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and capacitate staff on fraud prevention. Accountability to PoC through “community health team” nominated by the neighborhoods, structured community discussions, monitoring system and performance evaluation that includes the achievement and PoC feedback will be in place. Duplication of assistance to be prevented through coordination groups meetings at hub and central level, in addition to mapping of services at the health point level will contribute to reducing this risk. 3. Risk: Limited national capacity of MoH in staffing and running health facilities might hinder the plans for the long term sustainable solution in ensuring health services for PoC in rural areas. Mitigation: Coordination, detection of the capacity and supporting the mobile option and community empowerment.4.Risk: High staff turn over rate might hinder the quality of work, especially with no knowledge of management system within the NGOs (partners) institutions. Mitigation measures: having backup staff, clear hand over policies, guidelines and SOPs in place.
