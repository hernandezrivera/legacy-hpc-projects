Project Summary

Assessment by WHO and IOM shows “limited capacity” of designated PoE having access to appropriate medical services including diagnostic facilities for the prompt assessment and care of ill travelers and with adequate staff, equipment and premises. The availability of portable water and solid and liquid waste management are absent. In few places like Ras Al Jadir and Wazan land cossing point, there was no vehicle for transportation of ill travelers. Similarly, it was observed that “no” national public health emergency contingency plan exists for responding to public health emergencies occurring at points of entry.
Libya shares a border with six countries and has a total of 24 PoE inclduding eight (8) airports, nine (9) seaports and eight (8) ground crossings points.
The COVID-19 outbreak, which has had unprecedented effects on global mobility patterns, has tested health screening practices and capacities at PoEs. Border staff are playing an increasingly critical role in the implementation of key COVID-19 and general public health measures, such as disease surveillance, screening and testing.
IOM, as a co-lead of the Migration Health-Sub working group and as a lead on PoE pillar of the COVID-19 strategic response, conducts regular PoEs’ assessments and plays a leading role in supporting PoEs to respond to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic. As part of its support to PoEs across Libya, IOM has already installed high resolution thermal cameras at five (5) Po Es (Ras Aljadir, Amsad and Wazen land crossing point; Misrata and Benina airports) as well as prefabs for temporary/transient isolation at two (2) PoEs (Ras Aljadir and Wazen and in progress at Mitiga, Benina and Amsad). Additionally, 99 PoE health staff from the above POEs have been trained so far.
This project aims to continue strengthening the health system’s capacity at Libyan PoEs through the provision of screening equipment (thermal cameras), Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), and capacity building of PoE staff on Infection Prevention and Control (IPC). IOM will target 8 PoEs in total, 4 airports and 4 ground/land crossing points. This project will also support the establishment of isolation spaces at PoEs including provision of human resources, establishing handwashing facilities and disposal of waste mechanism. Finally, the project will ensure the provision of lifesaving emergency services at 1 high- and 2 medium- active disembarkation points.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic in Libya has exacerbated the health-related challenges the government of Libya was facing prior to the pandemic. Years of fighting and instability weakened the health system in the country, with many public health facilities forced to close, an overall lack in medicines, supplies and equipment, and damage due to direct fighting. The facilities that remained open are often overburdened or not maintained. It is therefore critical for Libya to control the spreading of the pandemic even more, to avoid a total collapse of the fragile system. Reinforcing the skills of border authorities and health-care workers at PoE, as well as providing the relevant equipment to respond to COVID-19 is essential to contribute to slowing and eventually stopping the virus transmission and to reduce the burden on health-care facilities in the country. International Health Regulations stress the importance of taking measures at Points of Entry (PoE) to strengthen national capacities to prevent, prepare for, detect and respond to health emergencies. These above mentioned regulations also include the designation of certain airports and ports as PoEs with specific core capacities, for being particularly important in terms of public health reasons. The regulations encourage neighboring countries to conclude bilateral/multilateral agreements to jointly identify the key PoE, and to cooperate on infectious disease prevention and control. This includes jointly identify routine prevention and control measures, to be ready to report and respond to events that may constitute a public health emergency of international concern. Due to conflict, Libyan health system is fragmented and have weak capacity to detect and response to threats due to communicable diseases especially at the point of entries as being reflected in various WHO and IOM assessments. The flow of passengers at the airport and land crossing point is on constant rise i.e. from 700 in March to 54488 in November 2020. The government is planning to ease the restrictions on international travel while the preparedness level of many PoEs need to strengthened.IOM assessment in October 2020, showed a number of critical issues at several PoEs across the country. Specifically, the assessment showed there is a need for thermal cameras and isolation spaces at four (4) airports (Tobruk, Sebha, Alkufra and Al Abraq) and at four (4) land crossing points (Ghadamis, Ghat, Sara and AlToum). There is no public health emergency plan for PoEs in Libya. The hand washing stations or measures to ensure hand hygiene for travelers and front-line border staff is not available at any of the aforementioned PoEs. There is limited staff at the PoEs to conduct screening. The same assessment at disembarkation point shows that out of 13 disembarkation points, one (1) is highly active, two (2) have a medium level of activity, while six (6) are in range of very low to low activity and three (3) sites are inactive. Strengthening the core capacity of the PoE staff on communicable disease identification, notification and appropriate referral of ill travelers to be ready to respond to emergencies equally benefits all travelers and critical staff, including men and women, across a spectrum of ages. By increasing disease surveillance, case management and IPC capacities, this also supports broader national efforts across Libya to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and other infectious diseases, enhancing overall efforts to reduce vulnerabilities in the country. IOM will continue providing emergency assistance at the active sites which needs to be sustained and strengthened and aims at strengthening the other sites so that they can become functional.

2 - Activities

This project will indirectly support an estimated 6000 passengers per day at 4 airports and 4 land crossing points as well as directly support an estimated 60 individuals per day having suspected symptoms at the same locations. Additionally, IOM will support overall 1,080 individuals at three disembarkation points (Zwara, Abu Sitta and Tripoli main port) for a total budget of $ 1,715,638 through the following activities: Public Health Emergency Plan for Points of Entry and Disembarkation Points: will be developed through consultative workshops followed by intensive trainings. IOM, with International Health Regulations (IHR) focal point, will conduct regular assessments to ensure that the plan is implemented and all the PoEs are compliant to the international health regulations. An emergency plan for the provision of emergency health services at disembarkation points will be prepared. Strengthen/Establish eight (8) isolation spaces at PoEs (Tobruk, Sebha, Alkufra and Al Abraq airports and Ghadamis, Ghat, Sara and AlToum land crossing points) or in their close proximity: The isolation spaces will provide basic screening and medical services in line with the contingency plan which will be developed with support of NCDC. 8 thermal cameras will be installed. IOM will provide Medical staff (one physician, nurse and technician) to all functional airports and land crossing points. Hand washing stations will be placed at all functional PoEs and effective waste management mechanisms will be put in place. Capacity building of medical staff and other staff at PoE: PoEs’ staff will be trained on infection prevention and control measures and will be provided with PPE. In addition, non-medical staff working at PoEs will be trained on surveillance and infection prevention and control measures. A component on MHPSS will be includedand RCCE activities will be implemented, so that medical staff can disseminate prevention messages. The activities support men, women and all age group.
