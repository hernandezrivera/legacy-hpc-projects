Project Summary

According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with GAM levels of 13.6% in Yobe, 10.7% in Borno and 7.5% in Adamawa. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. Based on the NFSS IX, approximately 809,814 children under five will suffer from acute malnutrition including 295,288 with SAM and 514,526 with MAM.
Approximately only 54% of infants less than 6 months are exclusively breastfed in the BAY States. A total of 720,739 PLW women will require contextualized and skilled IYCF counselling both at facility and community levels with the aim of improving the exclusive breastfeeding rates.
To reduce morbidity and mortality associated with global acute malnutrition, the nutrition sector will provide lifesaving treatment to both SAM and MAM cases.
The nutrition sector urgently requires increasing its capacity to treat SAM cases with medical complications by establishing additional inpatient care facilities in Yobe and Adamawa States and in LGAs near to inaccessible areas in Borno State. The sector also needs to scale-up the identification, referral and treatment of children less than 6 months with breastfeeding difficulties or acutely malnourished. The sector also requires to urgently establish nutrition treatment services in the newly liberated or return locations including Ajiri in Mafa, and other areas planned for the returns.

1 - Needs

The northeast conflict continues to have a direct impact on affected population’s nutrition status especially the most vulnerable and those in inaccessible locations. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months is on the increase across the BAY states when compared year-on-year, with Yobe and Borno state rates above the emergency threshold at 13.6 per cent and 10.7 per cent respectively as at September 2020. This includes about 300,000 children currently suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) requiring urgent treatment and over half a million children moderately malnourished. Without adequate treatment, one in every five of these children could die. In MMC,JERE,Central borno(Magumeri,Monguno) and south Borno(Askira Uba) where IRC propose for intervention-Round 9 NFSS reveals a situation significantly worse compared to Round 8 NFSS conducted in November 2019 . This is attributed to the worsening food security and WASH situation in the area amidst access and security constraints. The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors, including: weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity. This worrisome trend is likely to place a heavy strain on already overstretched capacities and resources on the ground, including WASH services, due to the vicious cycle of malnutrition and diarrheal diseases, exacerbated by access constraints and insecurity.Women and children are the most affected and those arriving from inaccessible areas into the reception centers are extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed nutrition services prior to their arrival. These groups continue to bear the brunt of the nutrition crisis due to increased micronutrient deficiencies. If untreated, acute malnutrition could have debilitating consequences on the growth and development of infants and children, further exacerbating the already existing burden of malnutrition in the northeast.

2 - Activities

IRC will Maintain 5 stabilization centre (2 in MMC and Jere, 1 in Askira Uba,1 in BAMA and 1 in Gujba, this Facilities will provide 24 hours treatment services to SAM children with medical complication, currently the 5 SC's have bed capacity of 124 with bed occupancy above 70% throughout-. The IRC will create additional space to admit and triage children suspected of covid 19 transmission.IRC will procure essential drugs, provide medical equipment's and recruit clinicians to ensure adequate coverage and provide patients clinical management and monitoring of feedings 3 hourly. Following a thorough needs assessment of the site, the IRC will supply the stabilization center with the required supplies and medical consumables, including basic furniture, equipment and anthropometric tools (scales, measuring board, and MUAC tapes), as well as medicine for the systematic treatment of malnourished children. The nutritional products (therapeutic milk and RUTF) will be supplied by UNICEF as part of the IRC’s Program Cooperation Agreement (PCA) and IRC will ensure delivery to the stabilization center. In addition IRC will provide meals to caregivers looking after their child with SAM for the duration of admission in the SC. Children who have recovered from medical complications will be transferred to the OTP. Engaging community leaders and men in awareness raising in CMAM and e-IYCF in hard to reach areas and transit centers. Mothers rarely act alone in making decisions about IYCF. They are guided by influential family members and perceptions of social norms, among other factors. Mobilizing community leaders’ support can help to create a supportive environment for improved practices in the communities. Community leaders are custodians of culture in their domains. They maintain law and order in their communities and also support members of the communities in their day to day activities. Community leaders. Lessons from project IRC is currently implementing in MMC on engaging on community leader’s participation show positive result in reducing barriers to practicing optimal IYCF and accessing services at the mobile clinics. In communities where the top leader (Lawan) and key leader (religious leader, women leader, youth leader and Traditional Birth Attendant representative, and CV leader) were informed and about IYCF CMAM practices an increase of referral to OTP was noticed For this grant, the IRC plans to extend its support to community leaders with inclusion of the leaders of sub communities (Bulamas) as a focal point in sub communities to ensure coordination between all community leaders in creating more awareness in IYCF/CMAM. A total of 5 Bulamas from each of the 10 LGA supported will be trained at the beginning of the project by IRC E-IYCF officer. They will then be in charge of organizing mobilization through community dialogue sessions with men from their communities to encourage referral to nutrition services and improvement of IYCF practices at home. Regular monthly meeting organized by Lawan with all key actors including as well Bulamas, will be maintained to encourage participation, sharing experience and solution findings. The meeting will also be a forum to receive feedback from the community leaders and stakeholders on the CMAM and IYCF programme and recommendations from them on how to improve the programmes. Protection action groups members and WPE case workers will also be involved to mainstream protection into the nutrition activities. In addition IRC will continue to support 64 OTP clinics across the BAY states. In 2021 IRC will work with WFP on integrating targeted supplementary feeding program(TSFP) into SAM treatment.IRC will also strengthen micro-Nutrient powder supplementation and will work and support state primary health care development agency on provision of vitamin A and deworming of under five children in the BAY state. At the community and health facility-IRC will work with strengthen IYCF programming.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC is also a global leader in protection mainstreaming. The IRC’s Protection Mainstreaming Monitoring and Evaluation System and global protection cluster resources will guide the project’s protection mainstreaming interventions. The project will integrate four key elements of protection mainstreaming: (1) meaningful access to nutrition assistance and services; (2) prioritizing safety, dignity and doing no harm; (3) accountability and participation; and (4) empowerment of beneficiaries. The Protection and Rule of Law team together with Nutrition will ensure these elements will be promoted through updating the current protection mainstreaming strategy at project inception. The update will be informed by findings from beneficiary assessments, staff capacity assessments and findings from various project activities. Foundational capacity building activities for project staff will be conducted to strengthen and promote a shared understanding of protection mainstreaming, including child safe guarding principles, and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC will sensitize the community on IRC’s accountability for quality services and on the ways they can provide confidential feedback, drawing on the IRC’s in-depth guidelines on client responsiveness. Using feedback mechanisms and hotlines, the IRC will continue to gather feedback on services and other protection needs as well as conduct safety audits to inform risk mitigation strategies for vulnerable groups. The IRC employs a Protection Coordinator based in Maiduguri in order to ensure staff and partners are trained on how to ensure the sustainable incorporation of protection principles. The IRC will also work with local stakeholders in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states to inform the assistance they provide and help ensure that protection considerations are integrated into the full package of services for IDPs and host communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions specifically with women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums to collect beneficiary feedback on quality emergency response actions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, suggestion boxes and community engagement meetings/focus group discussions will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serve as reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews will be conducted to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allow the IRC to make rapid and informed adjustments to the project. Safety audits will also be conducted each month with women and girls in the IDP camp to understand the nutrition protection related risks. Advocacy strategies will be developed together with other partners, community structures, and female beneficiaries, to respond to identified needs.
