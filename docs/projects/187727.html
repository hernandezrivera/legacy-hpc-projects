Project Summary

The proposed action seeks to increase the access and quality of education and PSS services in target areas by implementing a combination of formal (non-accredited) and non-formal education activities that complement each other where possible. PIN takes an integrated and holistic approach to address the Education in Emergencies (EiE) and Child Protection needs. PIN will employ a dual approach to supporting education through establishing temporary learning centres (TLCs) for provision of PSS and non-formal education using the Self-Learning Programme (SLP) curriculum in areas with acute concentrations of OOSC – including in IDP camps and informal settlements; and assistance to non-accredited schools where education has been disrupted through providing construction and repair works, school furniture and equipment, teaching and learning materials, support for running costs, PSS sessions will be provided to all enrolled students and teachers as its been integrated to schools regular timetables. Additionally, provision of after-school catch-up and remedial classes to support pathways into education and reduce drop-out. Specific consideration will be given to vulnerable households with OOSC or children at risk of education disruption, who will be provided with psychosocial support and pathways for (re)integration into education. PIN takes an evidence-based approach to programming based on rigorous feedback loops to promote accountability and enhance impact. All activities will be implemented in line with relevant Education cluster tools, materials, and approaches, in addition to core INEE and Sphere standards, with a focus upon promoting safe learning spaces and essential WASH facilities and maximizing accessibility for those at risk of education disruption, including girls and children living with disabilities. Teaching and student kits will be provided to facilitate quality learning opportunities and minimize education barriers for children from low-income HH Geographical targeting will occur through further in-depth needs assessments in areas pre-identified in cooperation with the Education Cluster. These will determine schools and communities most in need of educational and child protection support. The process will ensure the participation of relevant stakeholders to consider local priorities while also ensuring that transparency is at the forefront and that PIN is accountable to the populations in its areas of intervention. Assessments will be conducted by PIN staff and engineers in collaboration with local authorities, where relevant. PIN will target communities where education services have been disrupted due to conflict, where there is no, or limited, access to education. Experience highlights that significant gaps also exist in such areas to meet teachers’ and students’ needs for safe and quality learning. There will be a strong focus on community outreach and engagement throughout the entire cycle, including caregivers. All activities have the final aim of reintegrating children into education through formal and non-formal accelerated learning programmes and the creation of child protection safety nets. The organization plays an active role within several clusters and working groups across Northern Syria, including the Cash Working Group, the Protection working group, and the Education cluster, of which PIN has played a vital role as a member of the Strategic Advisory Group in Gaziantep.
The locations were identified through needs assessment targeting the vulnerable communities with lack of education services, please check the needs assessment section

4 - Needs

PIN's needs assessment in NWS found an estimated 12,443 school-aged children live across 33 targeted IDP camps. An estimated 43.8% are vulnerable, and 36.1% are working instead of going to school. An estimated 46.1% of girls and 40.8% boys OOSC, often for more than a year.This assessment confirms the need for comprehensive educational support, especially the establishment of learning spaces and provision of learning materials, and training and incentives for qualified teachers. The latest assessments confirm the need for such support, as based on the HNO 2022 draft, the number of children attending school is the lowest for the IDP population in sites/camps. Data and assessments used for HNO preparation show that a quarter of HH with school-aged children indicated that children are not educated due to the lack of schools. In 1,017 assessed campsites in NWS, there are only 196 schools.In NES, A REACH assessment shows 53% of education services were accessible for assessed settlements in Ar-Raqqa compared to only 11% in Menbij. The assessments revealed 99% of informal settlements have education barriers; such as no education for specific ages and lack of learning spaces. COVID-19 and school closures decreased children's learning interests and increased child labor. Assements also show no education intervention in Aleppo Governorate while intervention in Hasakeh, Raqqa and Deir-Ezzor reached 2,064; 1,475; 499 beneficiaries; this shows underserving in Aleppo.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN proposes an integrated, holistic intervention to support the vulnerable conflict-affected children increasing pathways to education, PSS and protection services in light of the acute Education in Emergencies. Activities are designed by needs and protection assessments, relevant clusters, and targeting based on IDP camps and informal settlements with OOSC. Schools and Temporary Learning Centres (TLCs) will be supported and established, providing non-formal and formal education with integrated PSS activities. Trained school facilitators teach classes with support from PIN and identify cases with protection concerns and physical disabilities to be referred to external service provider. PIN will support teachers and PSS specialists with professional development training and enhance resilience and safe learning environments by training staff and children on emergency preparedness. The activities foreseen include setting up non-formal learning centers, construction work and light rehabilitation of schools and classrooms, monthly operational costs, and school supplies for formal and non-formal settings. PIN will contribute to the Education Cluster objectives by providing quality offline and online classes, distributing essential items for quality learning and ensure safe access to integrated PSS activities. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy carries out regular protection risk assessments. These protection risk assessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified:1/R: Specific vulnerable groups are excluded from accessing education;1/MM: accessibility for males and females and children with disabilities; CFRM mechanism, conducting consultations and FGDs to understand barriers related to access and safety (such as distance), organizing community and awareness-raising events, referral of children with mobility and non-mobility related disabilities to specialized actors; Conflict Sensitive teacher training (disability sensitive classroom for smooth integration of children with disabilities with their peers and reduction of the negative social effects of their injuries, such as stigma and bullying. 2/R: Additional threats (child labor, early marriage, poverty and economic situation) 2/MM: targeting communities with different types of assistance; to minimize education barriers; PIN aims to integrate Cash for work activity to eliminate some financial barriers. PIN’s referral system community awareness sessions. 3/R: Behavior of parents and education personnel threatening children’s wellbeing. 3/MM: signature of Code of Conduct; Child-friendly CFRM workshops are available in schools; training to all staff CP policy.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA considerations are integral part of PIN protection mainstreaming strategy. PSEA trainings are conducted to all new staff as part of the protection mainstreaming trainings. PSEA is also integral part of the Code of conducted signed with staff, local authorities, daily workers, and other stakeholders involved in PIN projects. Protection risks and mitigations matrix is developed for all the activities implemented by PIN in order to ensure that PIN has well-thought identification of possible gaps and standardize the precautionary and preventive measures required.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): Security situation in target areas remains conducive to implementation;(MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN's staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occur, PIN temporarily suspends activities; conduct internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with actors, inform counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of incidents; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN relocates resources to other areas. (A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitates safe humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with UNOCHA, NGO forums, Working Groups) to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; shift to Cash program.
