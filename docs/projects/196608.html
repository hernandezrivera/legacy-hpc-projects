Project Summary

latest HNO indicated that14.6 million people in need, 5.34 million are Internally Displaced People and over two million reside in 1,760 last resort sites such as informal settlements and camps, planned camps and collective centres, the vast majority in Idleb (69%) and Aleppo (22%) governorates, with three sub-districts – Dana, Maaret Tamsrin in Idleb, and Azaz in Aleppo - alone hosting more than two-thirds of all IDPs in last resort sites in the country, 70% of them facing food insecurity and 42% in need of winterization support.
Food insecurity fuelled by conflict and mass population displacement was exacerbated by the impacts of the financial crisis in Lebanon and the COVID-19 pandemic as well as continued economic deterioration. The purchasing power of Syrian households was further eroded as the Syrian pound continued to lose its value, while food prices increased.
Furthermore, projections indicate that significant improvements in food security are unlikely in the near-to-medium term. As food insecurity soared, vulnerable segments of the Syrian population, unable to access diversified diets, have become at an increased risk of malnutrition.
In 2022, SARD’s conducted a YNA (Yearly Needs Assessment) which observed the needs in various sectors including Food, Non-Food Items, Shelter, and Livelihood sectors. Findings showed that 25% of households reported that their food consumption decreased in the last month. Furthermore, the main challenge relating to food access pertained to the high price of food items in local markets, as reported by 78% of households, the low quality of available food (36%), and the lack of variety of goods in the local market (14%). Additionally, safety and security concerns in markets, particularly for women, were reported as an impediment to access.
Lack of job opportunities in the local market, lack of financial means to start a business along lack of technical qualifications are the main challenges faced by conflict-affected households. Cash remains the most preferred aid modality for livelihood support, as reported by 55% of households.
To address the wide range of multi-sectoral needs, SARD’s intervention is aiming for regular cash distribution for families in informal settlements and in substandard living conditions.
Through a protection lens, SARD’s intervention is designed to ensure that vulnerable families have access to dignified and timely assistance through a robust response mechanism. To ensure that marginalized groups such as female-headed households, the elderly, and persons with disability (PwD) have safe and secure access to assistance, specialized and gender-sensitive activities have formed an integral part of SARD’s response strategy.
The proposed intervention is in line with the objectives and strategic direction of the various Northwest Syria Cluster Response Plans. These activities have been informed by Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments conducted in Idleb and North Aleppo during November 2022 as well as lessons learned from previous projects, ongoing monitoring, feedback from beneficiaries and other assessments.
Through this action, SARD will address the multiple vulnerabilities that households are facing after ten years of conflict.
SARD has included two MPCA components under this submission; the first is the one-off emergency MPC with full SMEB value and the second is the regular MPC to cover the basic needs.
The proposed activities outlined below will target approximately 4000 HHs/20000 vulnerable displaced and resident individuals in North West Syria (in Idleb and North Aleppo). 400 HHS will benefitted from 3 rounds of emergency MPC ( 150 $) and 3600 HHs will benefit from 8 month of repetitive MPCA (100 $).

3 - Needs

In SARD’s recent needs assessment:- 78% of households interviewed reported the high price of food items in local markets as a significant impediment in their access to food. In order to cope with the lack of food / money to purchase food, households tend to resort to stress and crisis coping strategies such as decreasing spending on non-food essential items and on productive assets, purchasing food on credit, borrowing / sharing food, or selling assets. For example, 25% of households reported a decrease in food consumption in the last month. Additional barriers of access were reported for women or persons with disabilities were cited, as the security and safety in the markets remain a concern. Thus, food continues to rank as the main priority for affected households, followed by access to basic services (fuel, electricity, etc.), hygiene items, shelter, NFI/household items, health care, education, PPE (mask, gloves, etc.), and income-generating activities.- Top three shelter challenges are (i) 23% no protection from weather conditions (cold, heat, rain, wind, snow, etc.), (ii) 31% lack of heating, and (iii) 10% lack of ventilation inside the shelter.- It was evident from the need assessment and the monitoring findings that MPC came in the top priority needs and was requested by the affected population across SARD geographic coverage. - Cash remains the most preferred aid modality for shelter support, as reported by 80% of households.

4 - Implementation of activities

Coordinated MPCA will be implemented through cash transfer is the preferred modality where markets are accessible and functional and security conditions allow. a vulnerability and need assessment will be conducted, alongside monthly market monitoring to assess market functionality, capacities and gaps. 4000 households will be reached. The selection of the family will be based on a criteria that includes the following: • HHs displaced due to a defined shock (natural disaster/ conflict) • Displaced HHs haven’t received similar assistance based on the coordination with LCs, Clusters and NGOs • Verify affected people’s income level/status as much as possible. SARD will conduct Distribution Monitoring and Post Distribution Monitoring activities for each round, carried out by the MEAL team. This action provides 8 months of MPC assistance for selected beneficiaries. Vulnerability is identified and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism. Assessment of the security, locations, tenders and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women-headed HH, and PwD, may be excluded from accessing the service due to sociocultural barriers.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD increase visibility for self-referral to ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) SARD applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries in order to categorise them according to their vulnerabilities. Protection Risk: The high target of the project may lead in some cases to reduce the quality of the service.Likelihood: low, Impact: medium.Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEAL teams constantly monitors the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) SARD has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities, currently, due to Covid-19 prevention measures, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries can register any feedback/complaints via SARD’s WhatsApp number. Abuse of power by actors; Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: Mitigation Measures: a)Ensure all staff sign code of conduct, anti-fraud, whistleblowing, safeguarding and PSEA policies. b)Safeguarding Reporting Framework. c)At least 1 female in each verification team.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Beneficiaries of the service may be targeted for SEA by a staff member or associate. Likelihood: low, Impact: high Mitigation: Through the established accountability and complaint mechanism, SARD ensures a quick response to any incident. Staff are aware of the PSEA policies and undergo regular training. In 2023, strengthening external knowledge and compliance with safeguarding policy, including PSEA, is an institutional objective at SARD (the process started in 2022) through organising surveys and training of associated service providers. Risk: The socio-cultural context in the area may impact the relationship between SARD employees and beneficiaries, which may result in abuse of power or sexual harassment. Likelihood: low, Impact: High Mitigation: apart from taking the measures already mentioned in the above sections, awareness-raising conducted within the communities will help to challenge gender stereotypes and patriarchal norms and reinforce the idea of accountability towards the affected population.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: -Locations of project activities are secure and accessible. -Markets are functioning with the availability of food on the market -People of concern (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) are aware of selection and prioritization criteria implemented by SARD -No Covid-19 cases reported in the IDP sites/camps. -SARD staff and targeted communities are aware of Covid-19 threats, risk, and mitigation measures. Risks and Mitigation: -Insecurity conditions result in accessibility problems and exposure to risks to staff and beneficiaries: To prevent these risks, safety precautions will be taken such as alternate routes, changing the warehouse and office locations. -Lack of supplies. To prevent any shortages, SARD will identify alternative options in nearby areas. SARD's warehouse in Syria has 1-2 weeks food provision, and SARD has secured access for additional food supply in Turkey. -Implementation of gender and protection-sensitive measures during activities, including additional technical support when required. - Risk of tension due to selection criteria for the livelihood component. SARD will assess in advance and adopt mitigation measures aiming at adequate distribution of chances - Activities in the context of the Covid-19 pandemic: Standard operation procedures were developed and implemented by SARD’s teams.
