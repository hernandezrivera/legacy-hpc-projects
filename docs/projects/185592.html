Project Summary

While it is generally acknowledged that peace, security, stability and development would be challenging in the context of the Anglophone Crisis without disarmament, demobilization and reintegration (DDR), Green Partners Association on one side has found out (through field surveys and observations) that, adversely pro-youth livelihoods development activities can enhance disengagement and reintegration, which would go a long way to contribute to transition to security, peace and stability.
An integrated pro-youth livelihoods development activity is highly invaluable to enhance a platform of social cohesion, social protection, tolerance, security, stability, prevention of radicalization and violent extremism so as to enhance promotion of peace, early recovery, reconstruction and sustainable development.
Evidently within the past one year several youths have been disassociating themselves from the armed groups, and while few are still trapped in the villages too many of them have moved to the relatively secured urban areas where few are occupied in the ‘Ocada’ (bike riding) business while too many are yet with no means of survival. These youths that decided to take refuge in towns and suburbs could forms another dangerous group for the future of the conflict because persistent hardship can be a push factor to create new armed groups in these relatively secured suburbs/urban communities or armed robbery/gangs, both of which are not health for any peaceful society especially the relatively peaceful urban areas.
This project is aligned with UNDP definition of human rights-based approach to development that, “A person for whom a number of human rights remain unfulfilled; ...the right to food, health, education, information, participation...is a poor person.” Poverty is thus more than lack of resources – it is the manifestation of exclusion and powerlessness. This has been the basis of this on-going persistent crisis (involuntary exclusion) and the bases for this pro-youth project (which is motivated by a survey on the demand and supply of pork and chicken in the NWR. The survey revealed that while at least 80% of youth (male/female) interested in the rearing of pigs, fowls and market gardening but are constrained by inadequate training, lack of finance to equip their farms and inability to manage outbreaks of livestock diseases, and thus the scarcity of pigs/chicken. This problem has resulted in high prices of pigs and broilers in all the markets in the NW hence leading to mass importation of chicken and eggs from the West region of Cameroon
This project therefore involves disengagement and reintegration of ex-fighters of NSAGs, IDPs, enhancement of social cohesion and protection, and the promotion of peace, security, stability, prevention of radicalization and violent extremism so as to enhance early recovery, and development assistance. It is designed to identify and recruit 400 youths in one batch, running 2 batches in one year which gives a total of 800 youths in 1 year and projected 3 years. Selected youths shall be trained, shall respect the rules of the game through active participation, attendance and evaluation, shall then be handed piglets/chicks, and materials to start their own projects under the regular supervision of GPA, which shall equally be available to facilitate marketing.

1 - Besoins / Needs

A new and common tendency that characterizes the Anglophone Crisis is a disengagement and disassociation of several youths (including females) from the arms groups – a trend that is almost been consistent for the past one year. However the separation of an individual from violent extremist group puts pressure on policy makers and communities to find an acceptable balance among competing interests among which may be the choice to return to the arms group and in worse case scenarios join armed robbery if there are no alternative livelihoods opportunities available. On one side, former associates who receive timely rehabilitation and reintegration support will be more likely to transition successfully into socio-economic spheres of civilian life. This gives reason for a resilient strategy to enhance the disengagement and reintegration of these former combatants and equally to target other youths of communities who have lost their livelihoods as a result of this war.GPA went out in a survey to find out that solution development of an Integrated Livestock/Market Gardening Value Addition and Marketing would enhance Disengagement and Reintegration of ex-combatant youth, IDPs in urban/semi-urban areas and sustenance to youths of these host communities including girls and youths with disabilities. Evidently at least 80% of youths in the rural areas are engaged in subsistence agriculture, market gardening and rearing of livestock especially pigs, fowls and goats. A project of this kind is therefore designed with standards of higher production and productivity and efficient marketing management so as to enhance improved livelihoods through higher and sustained income generation. This project will involve provision of inputs (production materials) and capacity skills enhancement on the production value chain and marketing management. Justification Piggery and poultry farming have scaled up in all parts of NW region as the demand for the fresh meat, readily-prepared pork and chicken at every road sides and bear parlous keeps increasing evidenced by high prices/scarcity as well as the demand for other by-products – the droppings (widely used as manure on market garden enterprises). Fowl/Pig farming/market gardening are relatively short cycle activities (3-6months respectively) with respect to other livestock. In spite of the short production cycle inherent to the livestock farming, farmers in the NW still face a lot of difficulties to rear them to maturity due to the rudimentary methods practiced and poor quality inputs. Among meat consumed in the NW in a year, chicken and pork stands one of the most consumed. This new appetite for the meat both for leisure, and for its protein intake allows for urgent need to step up and improve on production and marketing of fowl, pigs. Moreover, culturally pig and fowl are extremely on high demand in the NW – for traditional marriages, wedding ceremonies, funeral celebrations, and village traditional and cultural celebrations/activities. Besides, chicken (and most often pork - but for the Moslem community) stand among the healthiest recommended and highly consumed by every individual with no cultural restrictions.Evidently the demand for pig and fowl meat and other by-products in the NW is consistently far above supply making investments in these areas strategic and supportive to the youth population as it has often been a traditional means of livelihood.It is obvious that some youths including girls will be engaged in market gardening than livestock rearing. The project design equally allows for training and equipping of organic market gardeners with the necessary resources required to enhance production and productivity enhancement including marketing management.GPA has the technical capacity to support the implementation of this project with limited constraints and manageable risks. The project targets 400 youths it's design is a sustainability cycle to benefit at least 2400 youths in 3 years
