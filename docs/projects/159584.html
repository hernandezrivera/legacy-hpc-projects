Project Summary

Approximately 2.9 million people require urgent WASH support. IDP communities, host populations in rural areas at risk of drought and those living in districts prone to disasters and outbreaks of AWD/cholera are the most vulnerable. While most urban and sub-urban populations often have access to a sufficient quantity of water, it is often unsafe due to cross contamination between water and sanitation systems. Safety of people, particularly women and children to access latrines and water points remains a serious concern and a serious risk for exposure to violence (Somalia HNO 2019).
Unreliable access to a temporary and/or inadequate WASH facility remain an important feature of the humanitarian landscape in Somalia. More than half of Somalis lack of access to adequate sanitation, with critical gaps in Lower Shabelle, Lower Juba, Gedo and Bakool and in most IDP settlements, resulting in widespread open defecation. Hand washing with soap is practiced by very few because of the limited availability of soap and functional handwashing points. The heavy Gu rainfall observed in 2018 resulted in water supply infrastructure damage in more than 25 districts, particularly in southern and central Somalia. Most such infrastructure is yet to be repaired. As a consequence of recent disasters, population displacements and the chronic lack of investment in quality WASH infrastructure, Somalia continues to suffer from AWD/Cholera and risk factors for outbreaks persist in several hotspots, including main urban and sub-urban crowded settings.
A recent needs assessment conducted by HAPEN in Badhaadhe and Kismayo indicate that large number of households (62.5%) has no access to latrine and instead practice open defecation, unprotected shallow wells are the main source of water for households and schools, few households (33.5%) drink treated water either chlorinated or boiled, majority of households have had members who have suffered diarrhea in the past 2 weeks preceding the study, majority of people do not wash their hands after defecation and they use water only in washing their hands after defecation, 72% of households surveyed do not have access to soaps, large number of mothers (55%) do not know how to prepare Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) Poor access to safe drinking water and lack of adequate sanitation facilities coupled with poor hygienic practices are major threats for the survival and development of children in Somalia. Only 30 % of Somalis had access to safe drinking water while only 23% have access to safe means of excreta disposal. Open defecation stands at 83% for rural areas. Large portions of the people are at continuous risk of waterborne diseases like Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD)/Cholera and Polio (UNICEF / WHO, Joint Monitoring Programme for Water Supply and Sanitation update). Shallow wells are by far the most common sources of water. However, majority of these are impaired and the functional ones cannot meet the needs of the population because of impaired capacity. Existing few wells, despite being strategic, are not protected, which leaves them vulnerable to contamination and damage. Often, people are forced to use untreated unprotected shallow wells water which adds to health and nutrition crises. This project will mainstream gender and ensure that the needs of women, men, girls and boys are taken into account during the construction/rehabilitation of water and sanitation as well as provision of hygiene services. At the decision making level, men and women will be involved in selection of beneficiaries. Rehabilitation and construction of strategic WASH facilities and bringing them closer to the IDPs, vulnerable hosts and returnees, who will contribute to improved health and productivity, reduce malnutrition and human rights violations among IDPs and returnees; and ensure better school attendance especially for girls.

Project Activities

•Rehabilitate 4 strategic water sources and construct 4 new ones coupled with sustained community management systems to serve 7,000 IDPs and host communities (1,260 men, 1,540 women, 2,240 girls and 1,960 boys). •Provision of fuel and spare parts for 8 strategic shallow wells to serve 7,000 IDPs and host communities (1,260 men, 1,540 women, 2,240 girls and 1,960 boys). •Construct 100 gender sensitive VIP latrines and 10 institutional block sanitation facilities (5 schools and 5 health facilities) with hand washing facilities for 7,000 IDPs and host communities in emergency need comprised of (1,260 men, 1,540 women, 2,240 girls and 1,960 boys),including De sludging of latrines. Selection criteria will include chronically ill people, elders, female headed household to prioritize newly arrived IDPs and returnees as well as the most vulnerable from the host community. •HAPEN will support 30 community members’ de sludge excreta from filled latrines and institution’s septic tanks to sustain the use of the latrines over longer periods. De sludging will be conducted by vacuuming excreta using a small pump and discharging it into drums on donkey carts. Hygiene training will be also part of the training to mitigate unhygienic practices, including by providing appropriate equipment, such as masks and protective gloves. •Training of trainers (ToT) for selected 80 hygiene promoters (50 women and 30 men) from the community. Trained hygiene promoters will conduct monthly hygiene promotion sessions to increase community awareness on good hygiene practices. They will promote good hygiene practice through house to house visits, and campaigns for example on Global Hand washing Day. •Promotion of hand washing with soap will be highlighted in the activities as well as the cleaning of water containers (jerry cans) and safe waste disposal. Hygiene promotion will target schools and health facilities to maximize impact of good hygiene practice by children at schools and patients at health facilities.

Please provide further explanation

In the project design, HAPEN has factored protection mainstreamimg by ensuring close cordination and integration with CCCM and protection interventions. The CCCM component of the project is designed to provide both the beneficiaries and other stakeholders with relevant cross-sectoral data to allow for an evidence based response, as well as to ensure that displaced people have the information they need to access services safely. In this regard, HAPEN will adopt the following key actions: a) Ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. b) Ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimizes safety risk – be aware that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their kits home. c) Ensure the kitsize is appropriate for beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them d) Design and construction of shelters must preserve the safety and dignity of the affected populations: e) Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising due to crowded shelter conditions. f) Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups' g) Ensure that NFI packages include suitable items to cover the specific needs of women, girls, older persons(eg. sanitary pads, adult diapers) and provide additional clothing and blankets for the frail and weak. Strengthen participation and empowerment a) Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to shelter/NFI needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics. b) Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific shelter and NFI requirements and preferences for the location of distribution points and design of shelters. c) Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response d) Reinforce the capacity of the community - encourage traditional construction methods, particularly in areas where transportation of shelter material is restricted for logistical or security reasons; equip beneficiaries with knowledge of safe construction practices. e) Coordinate with civil society specializing in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases. The project is closely linked with Durable Solution through ensuring it takes into account it take into account the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. Advocate with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent. Community engagement is critical to inform reintegration analysis and programing to make solutions lasting, locally relevant and supportive of social cohesion and to adopt a ‘displacement affected communities’ approach- inclusive of returnees, IDPs and host communities.
