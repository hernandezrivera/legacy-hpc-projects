Project Summary

PAH will conduct short- and long-term interventions across the country, both static and rapid/mobile, especially in areas where cross-sectoral needs are dire. Shelter/NFI projects are critical to complementing other responses and ensuring a coordinated approach. PAH will prioritize synergies with its own projects and other partners’ programs, recognizing that effective partnerships are essential for maximizing impact. Using both in-kind assistance and CVA, PAH will respond to emergency shelter (ES) and essential NFI needs for newly displaced persons, returnees, and others living in high-vulnerability conditions, in coordination with the protection team. PAH will strengthen its CVA interventions in 2025-2026 to approximately 30% of the project, which will be implemented in areas of return with functional and accessible markets.
In-kind responses will be conducted through mobile and static teams in close collaboration with cluster partners. PAH will leverage established humanitarian hubs for logistic coordination, information centers, and organizing responses to reach remote locations. Where such a modality is not available, PAH will utilize its own procurement and transportation solutions, especially for last-mile transportation. PAH plans to implement both static and mobile responses.
Regarding the beneficiary breakdown, PAH will follow Clusters/Working Groups and OCHA population aggregation while planning responses. As an experienced organization with a broad portfolio of Shelter/NFI projects successfully implemented across the country, PAH will collaborate closely with protection partners to conduct in-depth HLP rights analysis using unified cluster tools and questionnaires in shelter construction areas. Access to land consultation meetings with diverse groups will be organized throughout the project cycle to ensure equitable access to land for shelter construction and provision of essential shelter kits. Beneficiaries will be supported, through HLP focal points and partners such as UNHCR, IOM, and NRC, to secure communal land tenure documents, mitigating the risk of forced evictions.
PAH will consider forming shelter committees in implementation areas. These committees, comprising representatives from local authorities, traditional chiefs, women, and youth, will ensure equal rights and representation for all demographic groups. They will monitor implementation progress and provide feedback and complaints throughout the project cycle. Before constructing any transitional shelter, PAH will require beneficiaries to provide proof of land ownership with legal documents, in consultation with authorities, to avoid land-related disputes. For collective centers, PAH will seek consent and agreements (MoUs) with relevant authorities and community leaders to guarantee land use and mitigate eviction risks.
PAH will maintain a follow-up strategy to address land ownership and HLP-related issues identified during project phases and refer them to the HLP focal point and other stakeholders. The organization plans to construct flood-resilient ES for both returnees and flood-affected populations. PAH will implement multisector responses to address the diverse needs of 35,000 individuals. For sustainability, PAH will form and train shelter construction committees in intervention areas, ensuring the inclusion of women, youth, persons with disabilities, and local builders. To promote environmental sustainability, PAH will encourage beneficiaries to use local materials and plant trees to replace those used for construction.

1. Needs

About 47% of the population of South Sudan, i.e., 4.2 million people, are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above ([source: IPC Technical Working Group, South Sudan, 2024]). At least 1.8 million South Sudanese are displaced, and 2.9 million are returnees ([source: UNHCR, South Sudan Displacement Report, 2024]). Continued conflict and instability, combined with recurrent flooding and climate shocks, are the main drivers of large-scale displacement within the country. The conflict in Sudan is generating an unprecedented wave of returns, with people crossing the border presenting critical immediate basic needs while stuck in congested transit locations. It may also generate tensions over land tenure issues and a wave of economically vulnerable individuals settling in urban centers. In 2025, approximately 4.2 million people in South Sudan are projected to struggle to meet their basic SNFI needs due to the continued destruction of houses and loss of essential goods resulting from ongoing floods and conflict ([source: Shelter Cluster South Sudan, 2024 Needs Overview]). The increase in affected populations is attributed to the current economic crisis, new displacements, and recurring floods and conflict, mainly in Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile, Unity, Western Bahr-El Ghazel, and Central/Western Equatoria States. Continued displacement and the movement of people, combined with sustained flooding and economic crisis, have further depleted natural resources, leading to competition for the limited available resources. This competition often generates conflict and localized violence, causing multiple displacements. Markets in these areas lack essential items or have become non-functional, making it difficult for the community to access the required goods for a safe and dignified life ([source: South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Report, 2024]). The lack of adequate shelter presents health and protection risks, negatively impacting the economic situation of affected households. Exposure to extreme weather conditions is linked to increased morbidity rates, mainly from malaria and airborne diseases due to congestion, and other risks associated with inadequate shelter. The lack of privacy and protection disproportionately affects women and girls, who face GBV risks while collecting increasingly scarce construction materials ([source: UN Women, Gender and Shelter Needs Assessment, South Sudan, 2024]).Displacement and relocation exacerbate conflict related to housing, land, and property ownership among displaced populations, returnees, and host communities. These compounding factors leave affected populations with limited coping strategies, often adopting harmful mechanisms such as exploitative work, commercial sex, or living under trees or in open shelters, exposing them to health and protection risks. Timely provision of SNFI support to the most vulnerable populations remains critical to enabling them to live a dignified life. Inadequate or absent shelter negatively affects physical and mental health, education, livelihoods, and food security, creating increased protection risks, particularly for women and children ([source: WHO South Sudan Health Impact Report, 2024]). People with inadequate shelter, particularly women, girls, and PWDs, experience higher levels of violence, exposing them to negative coping mechanisms. Essential SNFI are vital for people’s well-being, health, and dignity – to have a restful night’s sleep, meet hygiene needs, prepare and consume food, and provide thermal comfort and privacy. PAH plans to address vulnerabilities and support returnee reintegration through SNFI provision. Recent PAH assessments identified the lack of shelters as stemming from multiple factors, including natural and man-made disasters such as flooding, conflict, and cattle raiding ([source: PAH Needs Assessment Report, 2024]).

2. Project activities

1)PAH will provide emergency shelter kits and NFIs to newly displaced people in prioritized locations affected by sub-national violence, returns, and protracted displacement, focusing on those with disabilities. These distributions will address immediate needs, with items sourced from the core pipeline through cluster coordination and supplemented by local markets. To enhance accountability, PAH will hold initial community meetings to explain objectives, criteria, and timelines. 2)PAH will provide emergency shelter and critical NFI kits to populations newly displaced by floods or any type of shock. PAH designs separate emergency shelter and life-saving NFI kits targeting newly affected populations or displaced communities. The emergency kits will be distributed based on cluster recommendations. Cluster reporting templates will be used for all shock-related responses in close collaboration with cluster partners and SFPs.3)PAH will deliver in-kind shelter and NFIs to improve conditions for highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities, focusing on those unable to meet S/NFI needs. This activity emphasizes long-term support and integrates WASH, FSL, and Protection clusters. Feedback committees will involve beneficiaries early in decision-making. PAH will collaborate with CCCM partners for IDP sites and align integrated response plans for clarity. Targeting criteria will be shared with stakeholders, and distributions will address gender, age, vulnerability, and disability. PAH will provide transport for items where needed.4) PAH will provide Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) to respond to emergency shelter and essential NFI needs for the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees. PAH will conduct rapid market assessments and protection risk analyses to determine the delivery mechanism before the provision of CVA.(a) CVA interventions will be implemented in areas with functional and accessible markets.(b) JMMI reports published by REACH will be used for monthly price monitoring, supported by IA-CWG partners. PAH plans to increase the CVA response modality to 30%, including multi-purpose cash assistance that is both restricted and unrestricted. However, there remains uncertainty about the extent to which MPCA achieves specific shelter outcomes; therefore, a sectoral cash approach will be prioritized to better align assistance with shelter objectives. The CVA team will encourage the use of locally available materials for shelter construction and repair in traditional ways to enhance resilience and coping mechanisms. PAH is an active member of IA-CWG and JMMI South Sudan.5)PAH will construct flood-resilient emergency shelters for returnees and flood-affected populations, targeting vulnerable households identified through community consultations and assessments. Piloting will focus on return areas, engaging women, men, and people with disabilities to ensure community participation. Where flood-resilient responses are unviable, PAH will explore alternatives like temporary shelters, community-based mitigation, and structural reinforcements. PAH will advocate for flood-resilient responses to address displacement and vulnerabilities and will develop plans to mainstream DRR activities across shelter programs for long-term impact.6)PAH will adhere to HLP due diligence protocols and conduct area-specific conflict analyses to determine land ownership, mitigating risks of legitimizing illegal occupancy or marginalizing rightful landowners, which could exacerbate conflicts. Community sensitization and awareness on HLP issues will be supported by the Protection Cluster and HLP partners. This process will begin with community engagement to ensure shared understanding of land rights and responsibilities. A MEAL plan will be developed to track progress, focusing on data collection for accountability and adaptive management.
