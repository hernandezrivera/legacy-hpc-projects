Project Summary

Within the framework of the ERP 2021-2022 under the funding of LHF, Tdh in collaboration with its local partner “SAMA for development” started implementing the project “Provision of quality and tailored prevention and response services for 750 street connected children and their families, exposed to high child protection risks in 9 cadasters in Beirut district in Lebanon”. The project grants access to holistic and integrated child protection services to street connected boys and girls and their families. Tdh requests the extension of the project until end of the year, including a scale-up of the target from July 2022 onwards together with the second local partner "Nabad Association For Development". The project will directly target 1160 street connected children (Original project: 750 + 410 additional for the 3 months) in 9 cadasters of Beirut District. Tdh collaborates with local partners to implement activities in localities that show a lack of access to services and high needs and to avoid overlapping of service provision. Based on these assessments, Tdh focused the project to cover areas such as Bourj Hammoud, Karantina, Mar Mikhael, Gemayze, Cola, Sioufi and Achrafieh. In the ongoing project period, Tdh opened Drop-in centers in Cola, Ain Mrayse and Bourj Hammoud where children can access the safe space and participate in the activities of the project. In the case of high-risk cases being identified, Tdh, after coordinating with other actors, will be flexible in reaching out to other areas to cover the cases.
Tdh provides street connected children in need of immediate life-saving support with access to holistic integrated child protection response services and community-based prevention activities. Specifically, the extension of the proposed intervention responds to the identified needs as follows:
Direct support is provided through specialized services within the case management to enhance protection and safety of 500 children (300 and 200 additional target) to reduce the risks they are exposed to and support the development of a tailored care plan for dignified access to additional services and information. Tdh and SAMA established a strong outreach modality to ensure monitoring of the children and to provide activities on the streets in a safe environment. This is achieved through a street-work approach, implemented by mobile teams who work in shifts, covering both morning and afternoon but also weekends and operating a 24/7 Hotline.
Street connected children and their caregivers need to improve their mental and emotional wellbeing through mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS). Tdh and partners provide focused psychosocial support services (FPSS) to each 500 caregivers and street connected children (300+200 additional target), positive parenting (PP) to 835 caregivers (500+335 additional target) and community based psychosocial support services (CBPSS) to 835 children (500+335 additional target). These activities will improve their wellbeing and enhance their resilience, agency, and empowerment after exposure to prolonged crises and high protection risks, such as worst forms of child labor (WFCL) or different types of violence.
Capacity building and awareness raising among parents, children, community members and local authorities on child’s rights, child protection, law 422, PSEA are essential for better prevention of risks but also the social norms change. Those are achieved through structured capacity building activities organized by Tdh to local authorities but also through community-based activities such as social behavioral change Initiatives – Qudwa and child-led advocacy “Time2Talk, Dialogue Works”. 7 initiatives (5 in the original project and 2 in the extension) will be conducted. Those activities will ensure that children are not passive victims of harmful practices but can be agents of change in transforming their lives and the power dynamics in their communities.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

R: Street connected children and their caregivers are reluctant to trust child protection actors and are difficult to track, since they are on a constant move. M: Tdh will maximize its support and time to build relationships of trust with street connected children and their parents/caregivers. Outreach/mobile teams will be present and accessible during day and night shifts, and children and caregivers have 24h/7 access to a hotline.R:Children are recruited and manipulated by an organized network that prevents them to participate in the activities. M: Identification of the group leader and planning a strategy to approach and mediate with him in order to give the permission for the children to attend the activities. Consultation with the children about the best mediation way. Coordination with local authorities in case of children’s exploitation, trafficking or other risks. R: Local community and neighbors are negative towards activities. M: Development of a community engagement plan

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The 4 pillars of accountability are ensured: 1)Beneficiaries’ participation and empowerment in activities design, ensuring inclusion and representation of the different groups with G&D markers. 2) The Feedback Complaint Response Mechanism is managed by the accountability team, informing every stakeholder of its existence and functioning modalities. 3) Prevention and response to issues linked with Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), G&D, Child Safeguarding, fraud/corruption allegations and program quality through dedicated PSEA focal points. 4) Suggestion, Feedback and Complaints Boxes installed inside offices. 5) Hotlines and dedicated accountability email addresses for reporting any kind of breach. 6)Focus group discussions and satisfaction surveys are planned with the participants to the activities to collect feedback around the quality of service received and any faced challenges; that shall feed as well in the process of adapting the activities’ implementation and decision making.
