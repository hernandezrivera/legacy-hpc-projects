Project Summary

Since 2017, the conflict in the north of Cabo Delgado Province has caused more than 900,000 people to abandon their homes and belongings in search of a safer environment. Specific and recurrent shocks compound chronic poverty and public sector weaknesses. In line with MdM strategic axes in the country, the proposed project is based on a two-pronged strategy that intends to reinforce the provision of health assistance to population in underserved hard-to-reach areas in Nangade district with a short-term quick impact, while building resilience and paving the way towards development in Montepuez and Balama districts. The project will contribute to the following cluster objectives:
Obj.1. To provide accessible essential health services to targeted populations, focusing on main causes of morbidity and sexual and reproductive health.
Obj.2 To provide quality care for people with physical injuries, disabilities and mental health needs.
Obj.3 To prepare for, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project will be done, on one hand, by providing direct health, including gender-based violence and mental health and psychosocial support to people particularly exposed to violence and diseases, and on the other hand by strengthening the existing national health system to respond to future shocks. The project, that will directly benefit 32.055 people and indirectly the population as a whole, works at three different levels – namely at community, primary and secondary healthcare levels – building capacities, improving systems (set up of epidemiologic surveillance and information management systems) and provision of drugs and equipment supplies. The proposed project perfectly fits within the Government Health Sector Strategic Plan (supported also by WHO and the World Bank) that includes increasing access and utilization of health services; improving quality of service provision; and strengthening Mozambican Health system among others. MdM will closely coordinate also with development actors, who will be working to rebuild infrastructure and re-establish essential services impacted by the conflict. Gender and environment are also key components of the project, with both mainstreaming and specifically tailored activities.In a first stage it is key to collect data disaggregated by sex, age, and disability, as well as data relating to the capacities, the specific risks and barriers that each group may face. This includes information on physical, information and attitudinal barriers and how they intersect with gender, age and other discrimination factors. Based on needs analysis. Men and women from 18 to 59 would be seriously exposed to sexually transmitted diseases, also adolescents and young people adue to the high rate of early marriage and rate of sexual violence; due to the lack of follow-up, pregnant women suffer numerous spontaneous abortions and show signs of malnutrition and anemia. In the case of older people, skin diseases, vision and respiratory problems are the most reported.The interventions tend to make assistance responsive and fair, improving gender equality, by: - Describing the specific priorities, needs and dynamics that affect women and men, girls and boys in different age groups for emergency health services;- Designing activities to address the needs, roles and power dynamics at home and in the community that might deprive groups of equal access to health services;- Locating health services based on the needs expressed by girls, boys, men and women in different age groups, including adolescent girls and boys and older women and men; - Recording and comparing the different health results for women and men, girls and boys in comparable age groups.- Understanding the extent to which women, girls, boys and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds, including those with disabilities, participate in community management and decision-making, and who might be excluded and why might help to transform gender roles and/or relation. For example during the mobile brigades cofidential safe Spaces will be available for GBV survivors as well as to MHPSS patients and identifying and establishing contact with the leaders among the person of concern and in the host community will be key to benefit the wider community. While most of the leaders are men it is important to check if there are female leadership structures, such as midwives, females elders or traditional healers to promote the leadership and representation of women. For example, project recognizing the risks of childbirth attended by unskilled traditional birth attendants seeks to improve women’s knowledge, skills, and employment potential by providing training for local midwifes

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Multiple efforts will be undertaken to address the protection risks associated with the project. The affected population will participate in program design through anassessment and participatory discussions that include questions on their basic needsand preferred ways to address them, access to channels of information, as well as protection risks associated. The priority will be to offer a variety of channels to raise feedback or complaints, so that affected populations with differentneeds will be able to access and use the mechanism. Beneficiaries will be consultedon their preferred mechanisms to give feedback, ensuring that people with differentvulnerabilities and characteristics are part of this assessment. At the same time, clear SOPs and referral pathways will be developed, including managing critical or sensitive cases that will be registered on a separate database with limited access and will be answered within 24 hours. Other practical measures will be incorporated in the activity design to minimize risk of exposure to violence due to distances to medical services. The health workers will receive training on SGBV and mental health topics to better address protection needs identified in the communities. MdM will follow the protection guide on the management of data.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The key to ensuring meaningful participation is to identify and understand discriminatory barriers that may be present and that affect opportunities to contribute. These could include gender inequality as well as intersecting discriminatory inequalities related to poverty, social isolation, age, abuse, ethnic background, sexual orientation, social standing, religion, and disability status. There are some key actions wich are especially important to consider during the assesment/project design stage of the project cycle:- Ensure that the location of health facilities and routes to them are away from actual or potentital threats, especially the risk of GBV and are accesible to individual with disabilities and elderly person.- Ensure confidentiality and privacy is respected through examination rooms that are well separated from public spaces or the waiting area.- Employ also female health staff members with skills and experience working with women, children and elderly person. - Put in place guidelines and mechanisms for monitoring and reporting instances of abuse and exploitation.- Ensure that all beneficiaries receive information about the ir right to health care and services. - Identifying and establishing contact with the leaders among the person of concern and in the host community it is key to Benefit the wider community. While most of the leaders are men it is important to check if there are female leadership structures, such as midwives, females elders or traditional healers to promote the leadership and representation of women.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will be focused in: - Identification and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities and older persons in humanitarian contexts with considerations for intersectionality of age gender, socio-economic factors and other diversities. - Identification, address and monitor environmental, attitudinal and institutional barriers and enablers to participation for people with disabilities and older persons. - Involvement of people with disabilities, older persons and/or their representative organizations during the design and implementation & monitoring of the project.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

As determinants of health, the climate crisis and environmental pollution are among MdM’s main topics to address for protecting human health. MdM will apply the recommendations defined in its environmental policy and best practices aimed, on one hand to incorporate an environmental focus in all interventions, and on the other hand to contribute to environmental sustainability by reducing the impact (“zero footprint”) of its activities. An environmental pre-diagnosis was conducted by MdM in Mozambique in 2022 to identify proper actions and approaches to apply to the interventions in the country. Furthermore, a specific protocol entitled "COVID-19 and the environment" has been defined to complement the above-mentioned policy, providing specific guidance to ensure the environmental approach to COVID-19 related measures, services, and actions. It is noteworthy to mention that MdM also follows the recommendation of WHO "Operational Framework for building climate resilient health systems" to integrate the environmental dimension in the supported health facilities
