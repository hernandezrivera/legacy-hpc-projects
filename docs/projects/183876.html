Project Summary

The purpose of project is to contribute to the efforts of addressing the deteriorated Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) situation caused by the worsening economic crisis, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, decreased/insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation, and increased operation maintenance costs attributed to an increase in fuel prices in 2021. The project will be targeting crisis-affected populations, including IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents who are at significant risk of epidemics, food insecurity, malnutrition, COVID 19 pandemic, floods, and new resource-related conflict in South Kordofan (Kadogli and Habila localities) and Blue Nile (Baw, Kurmuk and Geisan localities). A total of 29,323 persons, including IDPs, Refugees, Returnees and Host Communities will be benefiting from the services provided by the project in the two targeted states. FPDO will be working in close consultation and coordination with the WASH Sector and other relevant state authorities to deliver the services to the people who are under the highest severity of needs to ensure their easy and safe access to drinking water from improved sources i.e. rehabilitated existing handpumps, water yards, hafirs, while communities who cannot afford to repair their water facilities will be supported with maintenance and operations. Furthermore, FPDO will be striving to ensure, with community support as much as possible, that the populations in the targeted locations will enjoy access to different types of improved latrines, including household, communal and institutional latrines. In addition, the project will provide the targeted population with hygiene promotion services with the aim of improving their practices through household visits, campaigns, and media coverage as well as the provision of the necessary materials/items. Throughout the project duration, women and girls who are part of the newly displaced population will receive reusable sanitary pads or culturally appropriate menstrual hygiene supplies. Environmental health interventions, such as solid waste management and vector control will be implemented in risk-prone areas.

1 - Needs

Twenty-seven percent of the Sudan population (around 11 million people) don't have access to basic domestic water. Multi-Sector Need Assessment (MSNA) 2020 showed that about 25 percent of the water sources are not functioning. Twenty-five percent of the population reports water quantity is not sufficient to meet their basic needs; 50 percent of the people reported that it takes more than 50 minutes to fetch water which also causes insecurity, especially for the girls and women. In addition, 54 percent of the schools and half of the health facilities do not have basic water services. Also 70% of the population (around 28 million people) don't have access to Basic Sanitation. Out of them, 33 percent defecate in the open, 30 percent use unimproved sanitation facilities, and seven percent have access to limited sanitation facilities. In addition, 49 percent of the existing schools do not have improved sanitation facilities. In line with the above, the MSNA 2021 found that only 14.26% of the households have access to a handwashing facility with soap and water. At the state and locality levels, the targeted project locations are inhabited by populations whose localities are with the high rates of severity ratings.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

FPDO has a good experience in the targeted ‎areas and totally familiar with the context of ‎‎refugee communities residing there. This project is using the (Do No Harm) approach and is designed to minimize harm by supporting local capacities through trainings and awareness sessions to build peace. protection of the targeted community is mainstreamed as a cross cutting theme‎.‎ The project team will share regular information with targeted community to ensure their knowledge about their basic rights and the available protection services in the area and how to access them. And FPDO will strive to avoid tensions between host communities and SSRs through its holistic approach to projects whereby both old and new SSRs caseload are targeted based on the prioritization of needs. And will make sure that over the project lifetime beneficiaries will not be exposed to any kind of physical or psychological risks. FPDO, will consider and apply neutrality and nondiscrimination based on age, gender, or group especially in the selection of promoters, construction labors, sessions, and trainings’ target individuals. And ensure all beneficiaries has equal and free access to services. The project activities are designed in a way that promotes protection and safety e.g. the construction of latrines will take into consideration protection aspects, dignity, and privacy Furthermore, FPDO will work closely with community and local authorities to avail awareness and improve their knowledge to GBV issues through sessions. Open defecation also causes loss of dignity, especially for women and girls, loss of privacy and brings with it protection concerns when women and girls go out to defecate in the open. Partner efforts should be directed towards eliminating open defecation and ensuring daily cleaning of latrines. proper use and cleaning of latrines, will also help women and girls to safely manage their menstrual periods, safe their dignity, privacy, and mitigate the protection concerns women may face as they constitute about 57% of the overall population.
