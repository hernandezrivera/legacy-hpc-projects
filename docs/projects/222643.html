Project Summary

Enhancing health service delivery in South Sudan is a critical objective, particularly in light of the immediate and long-term needs of the population, including their health, well-being, economic development, and the pursuit of lasting peace. The Community Action Organization (CAO) is proposing a comprehensive health response to address the urgent needs of flood- and crisis-affected individuals in Ayod and Fangak counties. This initiative aligns with the strategic objectives of the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan, which aims to save lives, mitigate health risks, and facilitate recovery while promoting collaboration with partners to transition from emergency aid to sustainable, routine healthcare services.
The proposed interventions aim to provide essential life-saving health care based on the South Sudan basic essential health care package, targeting a total of 20,000 beneficiaries—12,000 in Ayod and 8,000 in Fangak. This includes a focus on vulnerable groups such as children under five and people with disabilities. To achieve these goals, CAO plans to support Primary Health Care Centers and Units and mobile clinics through various activities.
Key components of the project include rehabilitating existing healthcare infrastructure and building new semi-permanent structures, ensuring 24/7 availability of safe motherhood services, and training health facility staff in essential medical protocols. Community Health Volunteers will be engaged to provide health education, while integrated community case management for common health issues will be implemented. Additional efforts will focus on mental health support, managing cases of sexual and gender-based violence, and providing routine immunization services.
CAO is committed to sustainability and community ownership. It involves local leaders and ensures equal representation in health committees, thus fostering equitable access to healthcare. CAO will collaborate with the MoH, SMoH, and CHDs within the targeted counties. The project will also collaborate with partners in the WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Food Security, and Livelihoods sectors to ensure comprehensive health service delivery. Ultimately, the goal is to empower community members to actively seek health services while building a resilient healthcare system in South Sudan.

1. Needs

The health systems in South Sudan, particularly in Jonglei State, are severely compromised, leading to significant challenges in delivering healthcare interventions. The World Health Organization's six building parameters highlight the prevailing gaps, with maternal mortality in the country reaching alarming levels of over 1,223 deaths per 100,000 live births, one of the highest rates globally. An ISNA survey conducted in 2022 reported that around 112 under-five children die per 1,000 live births annually, and many households have experienced the loss of a relative due to health complications. Teenage pregnancy is prevalent, with estimates indicating that 30% of girls aged 15-19 are affected, while the contraceptive prevalence rate is a mere 6%. Access to healthcare services is further hindered by multiple barriers, including a lack of essential medicines, healthcare staff, and equipment, alongside challenging conditions such as insecurity, floods, and high transportation costs. Only about 49% of the population can access health facilities within an hour, exacerbating the situation. Inadequate health infrastructure and limited services result in untreated Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) and widespread morbidity, with malaria alone accounting for 63% of reported illnesses and 69% of deaths in 2023. Hepatitis E cases have sharply risen, raising further health concerns. The situation is critical in regions like Fangak, where health infrastructure is either submerged or in ruins. An outbreak of Hepatitis E in September 2023 highlighted these vulnerabilities. Moreover, a gender safety audit conducted by the Community Action Organization (CAO) revealed significant disparities in access to healthcare, particularly affecting women and increasing exposure to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). A recent Integrated Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) in Ayod County noted that 26,700 people were displaced and urgently required humanitarian assistance due to flooding. The assessment also pointed out rising cases of malaria and diarrheal diseases and challenges faced by young children and pregnant women in accessing essential maternal and child health services. To address these pressing needs, a comprehensive health response program is required to improve health infrastructure, enhance laboratory capabilities, provide mental health services, and ensure mobile clinical support reaches underserved communities. Additionally, there is a critical need to preposition medical supplies, enhance disaster response mechanisms, improve referral systems, and prioritize support for SGBV clinical cases. CAO is committed to advocating for increased funding and collaboration with health clusters to continue addressing these urgent health challenges.

2. Project activities

The Community Action Organization (CAO) plans to carry out its proposed health response activities in alignment with the health cluster objectives and the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) strategy. The Cluster Objectives 1,2&3 where the CAO will: Our goal is to enhance equitable access to essential quality healthcare services that save lives, focusing on maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment for common illnesses, disability support, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and gender-based violence (GBV) health services for populations affected by crises. To create a supportive environment, CAO will rehabilitate existing infrastructure and construct new semi-permanent and permanent facilities in Ayod and Fangak Counties. We aim to strengthen the safe motherhood package by ensuring that antenatal care (ANC), labor and delivery, and postnatal care (PNC) services are available 24/7 in all Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCCs) with skilled professionals. Primary healthcare services will be delivered through Primary Healthcare Units (PHCUs) and PHCCs, including outpatient consultations, reproductive health services (ANC, normal deliveries, PNC), expanded programs on immunization (EPI) for Measles and Penta 3, and health education. We will conduct training for health facility staff on Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI), Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC), Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM), and Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and IDSR. Community Health Volunteers will be mobilized to provide health education in local communities and refer individuals needing medical treatment. Health promotion messages will be developed based on weekly disease epidemiological trends from health facilities, incorporating information on cholera, measles, Mpox, and COVID-19. We will implement integrated community case management for malaria, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), and pneumonia in Ayod and Fangak County. Complicated cases will be referred to the nearest secondary level of care, and routine EPI services will be provided through both static health facilities and mobile outreach sites. We are committed to procuring supplementary drugs, obtaining essential health kits (EHK) and reproductive health (RH) kits from our core pipeline, and maintaining a consistent pharmaceutical supply chain. Furthermore, we will identify and refer patients with mental health issues for appropriate treatment and psychosocial support. Clinical management of rape cases will be addressed, along with the referral of patients with disabilities to the nearest service points. Community mobilization and awareness of SGBV will be ensured by training staff in infection prevention, control, and medical waste management. We will install hand-washing stations in consultation rooms, maternity areas, and waiting zones within health facilities and run an awareness campaign on family planning for community leaders and stakeholders. To reduce excess morbidity and mortality, we will focus on the timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases. This will include: Training mobile outreach teams and health and nutrition staff on epidemic-prone diseases and outbreak surveillance. Providing case definitions for common communicable diseases to all healthcare workers to facilitate early identification and treatment. We will compile and submit data into the Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS) and the District Health Information Software 2 (DHIS 2) through the Health Cluster for analysis and informed decision-making. Health promotion efforts will help identify and manage epidemic threats at local levels. CAO will coordinate outbreak responses with stakeholders, partners, and the Health Cluster to ensure a unified approach. We will establish a waste management area and implement standard infection prevention and control proced.
