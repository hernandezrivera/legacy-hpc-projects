Project Summary

The proposed project will directly benefit 55,662 IDPs, returnees and crisis affected host community members in Rural Damascus, Eastern Ghouta, and Daraa, Syria. It will provide remedial classes for 11,700 ninth grade and baccalaureate students, whose education has been disrupted by the crisis, and for drop-out students, who have missed years of school for the same reason. The courses will be conducted in the established centres and other five schools. The remedial class teachers will be provided teacher training through a Training of Trainers (ToT) scheme. 400 teachers will be trained. Access to safe learning spaces will be facilitated for 12,500 students through rehabilitation of 25 school buildings. In order to address the economic strain of crisis-affected students, 10,000 students will be offered school supplies and uniforms that will allow them to enroll back to formal education and continue their disturbed education. To provide an integrated response to enable children's access to formal education, teaching materials will be distributed to all classroom teachers in 25 schools to be rehabilitated. The materials are necessary for learning, as they support teachers and are considered an essential part of any sufficient classroom. The integration of protection, conflict sensitivity and gender equality aspects, as well as the inclusion of PwD will increase the quality and safe access to education for the most vulnerable groups, girls and children with disabilities. Offering equal opportunities to the crisis affected girls and boys from host and IDP communities to access to safe, protective and inclusive learning environment will promote psychosocial wellbeing of children and enable them to be prepared to enroll back to school.

Needs

According to the NRC report which was published on 26. Apr 2018 “Since the beginning of the crisis in Syria, over half of the Syrian population has been displaced. One in four schools has been damaged, destroyed or are being used as temporary shelters, leaving an estimated 2.08 million children and youth in Syria out of school”[1]. According to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) [2] for Syrian Arab Republic displacement patterns across Syria are extremely fluid. Many people have been displaced multiple times, moving from one location to another as front lines shift and hostilities draw closer. Since the beginning of the crisis Rural Damascus has received the biggest number of IDPs. HNO quotes the number of IDPs residing there as 1,3 million, but the figure is commonly believed to be higher. The IPDs originate especially from the areas of Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor and Idleb. These locations have been under siege from one to two years forcing big numbers of students to drop out of school, as many parents stopped enroll their children in school due to hardships and dangers. The situation in Daraa is of no much difference. During the crisis, some schools were used as fortification causing total or partial destruction to these facilities. When the areas changed control the children in Daraa came back to their affected school which lost a lot of their qualified staff. With the limited number of teachers due to displacement, migration, involvement in the fighting, and death, teachers find themselves confronted with huge number of students per class. This fact caused teachers low quality in teaching. As a result of the crisis and the high prices, the children's parents suffer from the inability to afford the stationary, back bags and uniforms. Both learners and school personnel have been undermined by the poor state of physical school infrastructure. The devastation of schools forced large numbers of education personnel to leave and consequently the number of teachers in the formal education system has decreased to 0.30 million in 2018. Moreover, the shortage of functional classrooms and a lack of adequate schools left students in many areas out of reach of schools, making it impossible for them to enroll back to school. Especially children with disabilities have been affected by this. Yet at the same time, the number of children with disabilities has increased due war related injuries and lack of functional heath services. In addition, poverty and income deficit is obstructing parents from purchasing stationary and uniforms for the school return. Overall, the education system is badly harmed and fragmented. Additionally, the education sector is still not prioritized in humanitarian response leaving a full generation of Syria children left behind.According to the HNO 2018, 85% of sub-districts report early marriage as a major concern caused by attempts to relieve the economic burden on families, or as a perverse ‘protection strategy’ to prevent girls being forcibly taken by fighters. In addition, two thirds of schools have been damaged or destroyed. Implementation of activities

Result 1: Provide children and youth with non-formal education programsActivity 1.1: Improved learning outcomes of children by facilitating remedial classes for 8,775 dropped out students and 9th and 12th grades students in 4 public schools and 5 non-formal education centers. This result will be achieved by developing selection criteria of beneficiary students, generating list of target beneficiaries and enrolling students to remedial classes. The 9th and 12th graders will be provided 5 months remedial classes. The baccalaureate students will include both scientific and literary baccalaureates.Dropout students will benefit from 3 months remedial courses, where the formal curriculum of the Ministry of Education and the self-learning curriculum.The indicators for this result are the number of students enrolled in remedial classes, number of remedial class teachers, number of education institutions who signed agreements with EPDC and the percentage of students who reported academic and confidence improvement during the project’s lifetime, and the percentage of dropout students who enroll back to school.Activity 1.2: Improved learning outcomes by facilitating SLP classes for 2,925 in 5 non-formal education centers.The Self-Learning Program (SLP) has been developed for out-of-school children and those who are at risk of dropping out in Syria.Activity 1.3: Provision of PSS support to children enrolled in non-formal education.All children enrolled in non-formal education and SLP program will receive PSS support.Activity 1.4: Parents/caregivers (men and women) well-being and competencies are strengthened for enhanced child protection within family environmentResult 2: Establish, expand and rehabilitate new classrooms (temporary or permanent)Activity 2.1: Improve access to formal education through rehabilitation of 25 partially damaged schools in target locations. The partially damaged schools in need of repairs are rehabilitated to provide suitable space for learn. The schools were nominated upon specific selection criteria, they are both located in large communities, which serve a big number of students around them. The school rehabilitation is expected to benefit 10 000 students.The project will also rehabilitate 5 non-formal education center in Dar'a and Rural Damascus, 10 classrooms in total.Result 3: Rehabilitate, improve or construct gender-sensitive WASH facilitiesActivity 3.1: the project will include the rehabilitation of WASH facilities for 5 non-formal education centers and 25 public schools in 2 governorates. Rehabilitation of centers will include separate and accessible latrines and washrooms, separated by and suitable for each gender. The project activities are comprehensively inclusive for the children and their caregivers in the areas of operationsResult 4: Provide students with learning materials (stationary, pens, notebooks), school bags and school in a Carton kit in formal and non-formal settingsActivity 4.1: Improved access to quality formal education by providing 10 000 financially challenged children with uniforms and stationary kits. This will be obtained by conducting a need assessment to the potential beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will be selected among displaced and conflict-affected children of primary and secondary school age, who are unable to attend school due to displacement or lack of financial resources. The support will be delivered through a provision of uniforms and stationary.Result 5: Provide professional development to teaching staffActivity 5.1: Improved quality education through building capacity of teachers, by provision of teachers’ professional development training for 200 teachers. Twenty teachers, who teach the remedial classes are enrolled in Teachers’ Professional Development (TPD). EPDC has developed a teacher training package.
