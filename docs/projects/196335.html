Project Summary

Save the Children International (SCI) will implement integrated preventive nutrition services through 7 health posts and 1 Primary Health Care Center in 8 camps (as assigned to SCI by Health sector rationalization exercise). SCI is providing preventive nutrition services integrated with health service points since 2019 following Minimum Essential Health Services strategy developed by Health Sector. Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) global framework is also consulted during designing the integrated approach. The service will have synergistic advantage rather duplication with Integrated Nutrition Facility services. Same nutrition technical modules will be followed during counseling and take-way documents will be designed carefully to avoid any confusion at the beneficiary level with INF services. Integrating preventive nutrition services with health post gives beneficiaries some advantages like- Pregnant woman’s primary service point is Health service facilities, postnatal women seek services from health facilities prior to nutrition centers, early detection of nutrition status change identified at health facilities for sick children , increase contact frequency for Social Behavioral Change and comprehensive service provision suitable for beneficiaries to seek support at any time in a month as caregivers prioritize their Nutrition facility visits during distribution dates. Nutrition program will target pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and under five children who attend the health service points for clinical needs. Nutritional assessment, age specific negotiated counseling and management of non-breastfed (NBF) children will be one of the priorities with appropriate logistics support. SCI will maintain referral linkage with Integrated Nutrition Facilities (INF) for identified malnourished cases, Stabilization center for feeding supports, health facilities for mental and psychosocial and clinical services and child protection and gender-based violence sub sector actors for protection and gender issues. As there is no distribution of supplementary products distribution plan, the project will only serve those who attend the health post for clinical consultation. Increase frequency of SBC contacts and at the sensitive time is always beneficial and technical recommended. Project will not hand over any take-way materials that will confuse project beneficiaries with any materials distributed from INFs. The project has ECCD component as guided by Nutrition sector. Transformative gender role will be focused during household negotiated counseling and group awareness sessions with the objective of shifting the role of male members of the family more supportive to pregnancy and child care acknowledging the importance. Health post services including nutrition corner are designed for all groups of population male, female, person with disabilities (PWD) and all gender orientations (LGBTI). Waste management is part of facility management. SCI has dedicated site management and WASH team to handle environmental issues. As the project will not distributed any products, so disposal of nutrition product waste is minimum. SCI will continue its technical assistance to implementing partners in managing NBF children and required lifesaving logistics will be distributed to specific cases following all the regulations and technical protocols. SCI will continue leading the IYCF-E technical group and provide strategic guidance to nutrition sector for evidence-based decision making and partner’s capacity building on IYCF-E and CMAMI.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The levels of acute malnutrition among Rohingya refugees living in Cox’s Bazar remain high. Rohingya refugee children under the age of 5, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), and adolescent girls represent particularly vulnerable groups, with high levels of malnutrition. Despite of continuous nutrition curative and preventive services, the trend of malnutrition declining slowly in recent years. The reasons for this are multifold and include a limited scope of diversified diet provision, prevailing unhealthy social support and cultural norms and reluctance in compiling referral suggestions. Emphasizing preventive approaches delivered at every contact point, boosting the messages during sickness episodes when changes of nutrition status faltering are high, and integrated service provision of clinical, mental, and nutrition assessment with counseling avoiding referral time laps will give an edge to accelerate the gain. Addressing these gaps requires service modality beyond a single nutrition service point, at least for preventive components, reaching every possible contact facility available for multiple exposers to counseling and follow-up of compliance.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In this response, a merger of health and nutrition services is in discussion at the sectoral level. The health sector’s minimum emergency health service package has the preventive nutrition service embedded in the health facility service package. Integrated and multisectoral preventive nutrition service provision is encouraged in the Nutrition sector implementation strategy paper for the broader outcome. The preventive nutrition service will be integrated with SCI-run health facilities that are allocated after vigorous Health sector rationalization exercise. Cost-efficient integrated service modalities for comprehensive outcomes will be more appreciated by the donor to support.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

SCI is an active actor in the protection sector in this response. SCI mainstreamed the protection theme from the need assessment to the project designing phase where pregnant and lactating women, adolescents, PWD, and diverse populations are identified as vulnerable groups with the identification of potential risk, and community strengths. Protection principals will be mainstreamed in responding during the COVID-19 pandemic and when designing and implementing emergency plans in case of any natural disasters. Mechanisms to protect refugees from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation by humanitarian workers will be in place. Sector Specific Tip Sheets with a measure to establish Protection Mainstreaming Action Plan will be part of the staff orientation, monitoring, and reporting.

3.2 - Gender

An integrated nutrition program spontaneously adheres to the population irrespective of gender orientation. Gender segregated Infra-structure and WASH facilities are also engineered considering the vulnerability and to ensure pleasant and comfortable acceptance of facilities. Community activities are designed to create a supportive environment for women to practice and access services. Significant male engagement is also prioritized at both community and facility levels to transform their role into one that is more supportive and acceptable, ensuring household equitable distribution of food and creating a support system for practicing optimal pregnancy and child care including breastfeeding. Transforming male and other influential groups' mindsets to be more supportive and acceptable of women's engagement in decision-making is the higher objective of the program to achieve.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

SCI has a comprehensive safe guarding policy and conducts risk assessment of every project at designing stage. The mitigation plan is part of the program and budegetd accordingly. Safeguarding orientation is part of staff induction. Awareness materials and reporting chennels are well circulated and displayed in facilities and participants are informed about the process. There is a dedicated safeguarding team in regional and country office to gatekeeping the policy. Any report or flagging is addressed with highest priorities. A safeguarding report is generated quarterly to be shared among staff, concern stakeholders and donor.

3.4 - Diversity

Health post services including nutrition cornet is designed for all groups of population male, female, person with disabilities (PWD), and all gender orientations (LGBTI). Nutrition service will be the same. The inclusion of PWD is one of the priorities and health posts including nutrition corners will be made structurally suitable for their access and comfort for service seeking. Children with special mental and physical needs will be referred to appropriate service providers for feeding technique support. Community workers will ensure that PWDs are practicing optimal nutrition practices and berries can be minimized through regular household visits.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

SCI has a comprehensive safeguarding policy and conducts risk assessment of every project at design stage. The risk mitigation plan is part of the program and budgeted accordingly. Safeguarding orientation is part of staff induction. Awareness materials and reporting channels are well circulated and displayed in facilities and participants are informed about the process. There is a dedicated safeguarding team in regional and country office to gatekeep the policy. Any report or flagging is addressed with the highest priorities. A safeguarding report is generated quarterly to be shared among staff, concerned stakeholders and donors.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

SCI DRR team has a comprehensive work plan based on risk assessment done in the camps and is actively engaged in mitigating any damage to the facility structures due to natural calamities and also to support in the service continuity in case of any emergency by establishing temporary/mobile service point and fast recovery work to regain the regular services.
