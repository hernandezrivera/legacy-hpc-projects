Project Summary

"The IRC Bangladesh is one of the important health partners of Health Sector in Cox’s Bazar in Rohingya response and will be providing Maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health (MNCAH), Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH), Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) including MHPSS and Communicable Diseases (CD) health services from 04 Primary Health Care Centers (camp 6, 11, 23 and 25), 02 BEmONC Centers (1 with camp 2E PHCC; another one with camp 11 PHCC), 15 Referral Hubs, 42 Women Friend Space (WFS) (32 are active and rest 10 will be active by December 2021), 03 Women and Girl’s Safe Spaces (WGSS) and one (1) 20 beds SARI Isolation and Treatment Center at the camp level (Camp 23). Barriers faced by women and girls in seeking care, especially BEmONC and SRH services, will be addressed through outreach activities. The services include the ANC, PNC, FP (Both long- and short-term methods), NVD, and emergency referral to Secondary Health Care Centers. IRC Community Outreach officers will work with Community Health Volunteers to increase their capacity to both increase knowledge related to SRH and how gender norms influence health seeking behavior. The Referral Hub (RH) project of IRC is to ensure patients with emergency obstetric conditions and other medical emergency cases will be able to access to PHCC/BEmONC facilities through rapid referral services. New modality of referral/transportation support project (community to nearby PHCC and field hospital)with support from UNFPA will be implemented. Besides, IRC is working in Host Community to support Upazila level health facilities at Teknaf and Ukhia upazila of Cox’s Bazar through logistic and Human Resources. The support is dedicated to support ITCs in these hospitals (30 beds Ukhia and 30 beds in Teknaf; total 60 ITC beds). ""
Male engagement activities in MNCH, family planning (FP) and SRH will be periodized. Additionally, needs of children, adolescents, youth and old in all their diversity (gender, age, disability, ethnicity) will also be addressed. IRC will focus on Sex, Age and Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) for program quality improvement."

1 - Identified Needs

UNHCR estimates around 884,041 Rohingya refugees are living in the Cox’s Bazar district in the 34 extremely congested refugee camps across Teknaf and Ukhia sub-districts and the total people in need is 1,437,900. Among the Rohingyas, 11% of them (10% male and 13% female) had a health problem and were needing to access health care (J-MSAN 2021). The major health problems prevailing among Rohingya refugees is acute respiratory infection followed by unexplained fever and diarrhoea. A total of 835,672 OPD consultations were reported between January – March 2021. Beside CD, the need for NCD is increasing in Rohingya camps. In a recent IRC research, hypertension (11.2%), diabetes (5.2%), and respiratory disease (8%) were the most frequent chronic disorders among Rohingyas. Conversely, host communities had greater rates of these diseases that indicates the need of treatment support from non-communicable diseases. Given the patriarchal society with sociocultural norms that restrict women’s mobility, access to resources for compounded with a difficult geographical terrain and COVID-19 movement restrictions that have further isolated women and pushed many households into poverty, women and adolescent girls are unable to access health facilities resorting to home deliveries. There is an urgent concern about increased maternal and new-born deaths, currently there is a high rate of maternal death due to preventable causes. The 2020 maternal and Perinatal Mortality Surveillance and Response Annual report showed that the majority of maternal fatalities in the camps (47%) were related to obstetric haemorrhage and hypertension in pregnancy (eclampsia and preeclampsia). This high fatality rate can be attributed to the three delay in seeking medical attention. From January-March 2021, a total of 2,124 obstetric complications, 15 maternal deaths, and 56 neonatal deaths, 76 still birth were reported in Cox Bazar. Generally, communities are unaware about the STI (Sexually Transmitted Infection) and their effect in reproductive health. Though the LARC uptake has been improved among the Rohingya women, the rate remained low at only 2% between April to June 2021. The Health Sector Bulletin reported increased uptake of short-acting contraceptives (oral pills and injectable) over long-acting and reversible contraceptives (LARC) and 64,143 women of reproductive age (WRA) received family planning contraceptive methods. The preferred family planning method was the oral contraceptive pill (56%) followed by injectable contraceptives (33.6%) and condoms (8.7%). A safety audit conducted by IRC in February 2021 has found that 76% of key interviewees shared that women and girls fear being identified as survivors of SGBV when seeking services. Through safety audits, GBVIMS data and focus groups discussions show that rape including marital rape is exacerbating the increase in unwanted pregnancies and unsafe abortions resulting in disastrous emotional and physical consequences on survivors such as health complications, including unwanted pregnancy, unsafe abortion, and sexually transmitted infections (STIs); and mental health issues such as acute stress, anxiety, or depression. Various barriers prevent women and girls from accessing basic health services; sociocultural norms dictate that women lack decision making power and depend on the permission and resource of husbands/male household members to access facilities. According to a report by Population Council, husbands and mothers-in-law are the key gatekeepers and decision makers determining if women and girls may use contraceptives and women must be accompanied by a male relative or spouse to obtain medical care. This gap and lack of access for women in the decision-making process effects negatively towards family planning service. As a result, the woman is not willing to use contraceptive methods to avoid the complexity in term of decision-making process.
