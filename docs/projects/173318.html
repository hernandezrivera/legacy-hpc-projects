Project Summary

The project aims to enhance protection and empowerment resilience of Rohingya women and adolescent girls in the refugee camps, as well as promote social cohesion between host communities and refugee communities. It will also strengthen capacities of humanitarian actors, government, law enforcement, media and women's and LGBTIQ rights organisations/groups on gender equality and addressing GBV, essentially towards ensuring: i) women and adolescent girls, including LGBTIQ persons and women with disabilities, both Rohingya and host community have access to key life-saving and protection services, are resilient leaders, and are able to address and build social cohesion through constructive joint participation and dialogue ii) technical assistance is provided to Camps-in-Charge through Gender field Officers so they are better equipped to address women’s protection and empowerment needs and ensure linkages with elected and self-mobilised women leaders, iii) potential women leaders are capacitated with the leadership skills to contribute to community representation, while they are supported by an enabling environment promoted through social norms change and community engagement initiatives, iv) Community Security and gender-responsive policing is enhanced through capacity building ( gender and GBV ToT, mentoring, monitoring) of Armed Police Battalions and Bangladesh Police, setting up of women and children help desks , and supporting the Bangladesh Women Police Network, and v) Capacity of journalists are improved to promote gender-responsive journalism for improving positive narratives in the media promoting social cohesion and women’s involvement in humanitarian response. All partnership agreements signed between UN Women and Implementing partners include commitment to PSEA. UN Women is an active member of the inter-agency PSEA network and all UN Women staff have been trained on PSEA including Gender field Officers who serve as camp level PSEA reference points and who have further trained all UN Women Rohingya volunteers on PSEA and inter-agency code of conduct.

1 - Identified Needs

Pre-existing gender inequalities, gender-based violence) and discrimination have been reinforced, perpetuated and exacerbated during this crisis disproportionately affecting women, girls and the most vulnerable and marginalized Rohingya refugee population groups. Gendered isolation of women and girls according to traditional gender norms and practices of Rohingya such as purdah as well as perceptions around women's honour are limiting their access to life-saving assistance, services and information as well as their participation in decision-making \ (IOM and UN Women 2020). Yet, there are Rohingya women leaders, including survivors of conflict-related sexual and gender-based violence, that are self-organizing and forming their own groups in the camps to advocate for their rights and call for justice. Enhanced efforts are needed to promote women's leadership skills and opportunities in decision making structures, as well as creating an enabling environment for them through social norms change and community engagement initiatives (UNHCR, Care and AAB (2020)). As highlighted in the Rapid Gender Analysis on the Impact of COVID-19 in Cox's Bazar (Gender Hub, UN Women, OXFAM and Care 2020), there has been a clear gendered impact of this pandemic. Activities by women perceived as ""dishonourable"" are being cited by women and men in Rohingya camps as a reason for the COVID-19. These gendered perceptions and the increased social stigma against women has led to increased policing of and violence and discrimination against women. There has been a reported increase in the number of GBV cases, particularly intimate partner violence, child marriage as well as polygamy and transphobic violence. Prolonged restrictive COVDI-19 prevention measures are likely to result in negative coping mechanisms, increased vulnerability to, and heightened risks of GBV, including SEA and trafficking. Women, who are mostly engaged in the informal economy, and vulnerable population dependent on daily work, such as transgender persons and female sex workers, are hit the hardest. There has been a reported steady growth in violent confrontation in the camps since January 2020 (UNDP CARU), with particular escalations in violence between armed gangs and conservative forces since July 2020, especially in the northern camps, as well as in Teknaf camps, particularly targeting women and girls, including women leaders and volunteers who are being blamed and scrutinized for not being ""good Muslim women"" (UNDP and UN Women 2020). The Oxfam 2020 study ""Himot"" (courage), as well as series of consultations by UN Women throughout 2020, identified several barriers, opportunities and entry points for promoting Rohingya women's leadership, which led to the development of a joint action plan on women's leadership under the women's leadership technical working group under the GiHA WG. Women, girls, and other vulnerable and marginalized groups among the Bangladesh host population critically face gender barriers to access already scarce and overstretched social services, livelihoods options, and social relations.Furthermore, mis-information and negative rhetoric about the Rohingya, negative portrayal of Bangladeshi women humanitarian staff and volunteers working for the refugee response in the media and gender norms are adding to the overall hostility of the host community towards Rohingya, and stigmatization, harassment and security threats against them women humanitarian workers. These indicate the need to work with the media and journalists for gender-responsive journalism and promote an accurate understanding of the media of the role of women humanitarian workers and Rohingya refugees. For improving the conditions for women and adolescent girls in both the host communities and Rohingya camps, it is vital that the local civil society organizations, especially those that work for the advancement of women's rights, women's leadership and empowerment, are strengthened.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Running of 8 multi-purpose women’s centers in camps 3, 4, 4e, 5 and 18, as well as in host communties Teknaf, Ukhiya and Cox's Bazar Sadar, to provide life-saving and protection and empowerment services to 25000 Rohingya women and 15000 adolescent girls, as well as targeting 9000 host community women and 6000 host community adolescent girls through a beneficiary selection process. Activity 2: Development of advanced Women's leadership training modules, based on research conducted by Oxfam (Himot study 2020) and UN Women/IOM (Honour in Transition 2020), and roll-out with women leaders across blocks in all camps and across villages in host community. The modules will be used during training sessions and include communication, mediation and negotiation skills development, public speaking, governance, network building, mobilisation, preparation of women to play an active role in social cohesion and mediation in their communities. Activity 3: Establishment of three new women & children’s police helpdesks, renovation of existing two women and children police help desks, and training (ToT and on the job-mentoring) to police and armed police battalions on GBV response including by engaging former and current Presidents and DIG of Bangladesh Women Police Network, as well as International Police Expert and President of International Women Police Association. Activity 4: Placement of 6 gender field officers and their 46 community outreach volunteers to support CICs and site management to effectively address women’s needs and concerns through technical support, capacity development, community engagement, monitoring, advocacy, referal support including as PSEA reference points at camp level. Activity 5: Capacity building of women's rights organisations, networks and CSOs in host and Rohingya community to address GBV, gendered impact of COVID-19 and promote social cohesion. Activity 6: Dialogues with Community Policing Forums and host community, dialogues with Rohingyas, etc. on gendered protection and security issues Activity 7: Capacity building of journalists on gender-responsive journalism for improving positive narratives in the media promoting social cohesion and women’s involvement in humanitarian response Activity 8: Roll-out pilot of new improved Community and Feedback Mechanism for UN Women interventions in camps including use of both complaint boxes, hotline, group and one-to-one consultations including by engaging Rohingya volunteers and CSO leaders in consultations with Rohingya refugees benefitting from UN Women services and activities.
