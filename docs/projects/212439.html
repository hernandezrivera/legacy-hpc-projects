Project Summary

As per HNO 2024, the sector PIN indicates a 4% increase from the previous year for children and teachers in need of education assistance. Access to education remains a challenge for many children, as the numbers of Out of School Children remain high. The main barriers are related to the deteriorating economic situation and the ongoing protracted crisis. Even for children in school, learning spaces continue to be unconducive with overcrowded classrooms, limited number of teachers and limited availability of teaching and learning materials.
UNICEF proposes to provide 3,182,074 children and teachers with education services in areas of severity 3-5. This includes children from preprimary, primary and secondary school-aged girls and boys to enable them to develop foundational numeracy and literacy skills, as well as life and citizenship skills, in order to strengthen child, family and community resilience. Additionally, support to enhance the physical learning spaces as well as provision of psychosocial support for children and teachers.
UNICEF will contribute to:
A. Providing equitable access to education
B. Supporting quality education in protective environment
C. Strengthening education systems
Access will be promoted through Back-to-Learning Campaigns, Vouchers-for-Education, provision of non-formal education programs, school textbooks and school supplies, support to national exams to enhance access to accredited education as part of a multi-sector approach, rehabilitation of schools (formal and nonformal) and provision of temporary classrooms. Rehabilitation is also needed in lower severity areas, where displaced children and returnees as well as large numbers of host community children reside, to contribute to resilience and social cohesion.
For school rehabilitation UNICEF coordinates with relevant authorities to identify schools in need. UNICEF conducts an assessment to confirm needs and works through vetted independent contractors to commence rehabilitation, including the improvement of WASH facilities, enhancing accessibility for children with disabilities. This is complemented by the provision of school furniture and supplies.
UNICEF will support the Self-learning program to ensure that OOSC have an opportunity to learn, this includes provision of materials, establishment of learning spaces, provision of supplies and support to teacher training. To improve the quality of education services, UNICEF will ensure schools are child-friendly, with teachers trained in child-centered pedagogy, and where children develop academic, cognitive, life and psychosocial skills that empower them to engage, relate, cope and contribute to community and family life. Due to the large number of children at different learning levels within grades, children will be provided with remedial learning support. To support system resilience, UNICEF will establish Parent Teacher Associations that cooperate with Headmasters, and train education staff on monitoring schools, staff performance and children’s learning. UNICEF monitors ongoing activities through staff and implementing partner visits, Third Partner Monitoring and through established feedback mechanisms. In NWS, TPMs provide on-site verification and beneficiary interviews.
