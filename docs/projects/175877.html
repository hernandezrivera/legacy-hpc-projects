Project Summary

The Emergency and recovery health project intends to address the gaps in health service delivery in South Sudan resulting from years of conflict, sub national violence and impact of climate change that left 8.3 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance. 7.3 million are food insecure and are in need of urgent assistance (HNO 2021). 5.2 million People are projected in need of health care services and 5.9 in need of WASH by 2021(HNO 2021). GAM rate is 15% compared to 16.2% in 2020, still surpassing the global threshold by WHO definition. Yet, expected to deteriorate further during the onset of lean season in 2021. Under five mortality remain at 90.7/1000 live birth one of the highest in the world (HNO 2021). Main drivers are lack of food, scarce safe water and sanitation, shelter, displacement, protection risk, loss of livelihoods and eroded coping mechanism impacted by conflict and flooding. Western Bahr El Ghazal state –Wau is one of the Counties severely affected by the impact of conflict hence, hosting a large number of IDPs, returnees and host communities and are living under poor conditions both in urban and remote settings. In rural areas, women and children still walk for more than 30-40 kilometres to access health services. The cluster projected health need in 2021 for Wau is 62467 (HC PiN Feb 2021). Additionally, 46734 returnees are in general need of assistance further stressing the scarce resources available (IOM DTM Oct 2020). Tonj North is in IPC 4, the largest County in Warrap and has severe health gaps more especially in the rural areas; off 24 health facilities only 14 are functional with very limited basic service packages. 16480 people were affected by flooding in Sept 2020 spreading to 7 of the 9 Payam of the County. (IOM DTM Sept 2020). The cluster projected health need for 2021 is 81614 people. Main cause of sufferings are attributed to chronic inter-communal violence, yearly flooding, and rivalry over natural resources resulting to displacement. Kapoeta East is IPC level 4 and the largest county in Eastern Equatoria but severely underserved. The County has poor health indicators and a hot spot for epidemic prone diseases such as Malaria, cholera and Measles. The major drivers of shocks are inter-communal fighting over natural resources, cattle rustling, and drought and disease outbreaks due to poor living conditions. 165,518 people in need (HC PiN data Feb 2021) of which 21,762 are IRW targets. IRW intends to scale up response to provide immediate and longer term solutions for the affected population through recovery and build resilience against all forms of disasters. Geographical priorities are Wau, Kapoeta East and Tonj North Counties. Hence, 30100 (10% of the PiN for the vulnerable) men, women boys, girls, elderly people and people with disability of all age groups from the 3 targeted Counties will receive equitable, quality lifesaving and dignified health services through clinical consultation and treatment for common ailments. Reproductive health services with components of antenatal care, deliveries of pregnant women by skilled health workers, postnatal care and TT immunization shall be provided. Equitable access to DPT/PENTA 3 vaccination for children below 1 year shall be provided. IRW will participate in cluster activities including cluster coordination, joint need assessment, joint supportive monitoring and supervision, rapid response mission and health surveys. Malaria incident rate expected to reduce to ≤ 2.65 through Mosquito nets distribution to the most vulnerable people and health education on preventive measures. Children 6 months to 15 years will receive measles vaccination to improve coverage and immunization outcome. IRW will recruit and train project staffs on IDSR and EWARS to improve timely reporting and response to warnings and alerts. SGBV survivors will be provided clinical treatment for CMR and psycho-social support referral pathways will be strengthened.

1 - Needs

Effects of conflict and natural disasters continued to hit South Sudan hard with no state and County spared. As such, 8.3 million people are in dare need of humanitarian assistance (HNO 2021). Of 78 counties, 72 are in severe need while 5 are in extreme need. Moreover, over 1 million people are affected by flooding in 48 Counties (HNO 2021) 5.2 million People are projected in need of health services across the Country (HNO 2021). Out of 2300 health facilities, 1300 are non- functional and of the functioning health facilities, 57% are supported by humanitarian and development partners and many remain inaccessible by communities in remote areas within a distance of 5 km (HNO 2021). Under five mortality remain at 90.7/1000 live birth one of the highest in world while patient doctor ration is 1:65574 a bad indicator of human resource gaps for health (HNO 2021). Women, boys, girls, elderly people and people with disability are the most affected in either of the status i.e. IDPs, returnees and host communities. In western Bahr el Ghazal-Wau, most populations are displaced to very remote locations not easily accessible and where services have never existed before. The cluster projection of health need in 2021 is 62467, in addition, 46734 returnees are in general need of assistance (IOM DTM Oct 2020) yet, HPF program is none existent in greater Bagari due to the nature of the context thus, the need for none HPF partners to intervene and save lives. Poor Maternal and child health indicators attributed to long distance travel 30-40 KMs to access services and lack of ambulance for referral as well as diagnostic equipment. (IRW Mobile clinic report Sept-2020). In Tonj North, effects of climate change, inter-communal fighting over natural resources and cattle raiding are common cause of disasters resulting to displacement. The County is in IPC 4 and has severe health gaps more especially in the rural setting; off 24 health facilities only 14 are functional with very limited or basic services including Marial Lou hospital. 16480 people were affected by flooding in Sept 2020 spreading to 7 of the 9 Payams of the County. (IOM DTM Sept 2020). The cluster projected health need for 2021 is 81614 people (HC PiN data Feb 2021). Kapoeta East is IPC level 4 and the largest county in Eastern Equatoria but severely underserved. The County has poor health indicators and a hot spot for epidemic prone diseases such as Malaria, cholera and Measles. An estimate of 10000 people are noted to have moved from Kapoeta North to East due to hunger, adding stress on the already scarce resources. (UNOCHA humanitarian bulletin Oct 2020). 165518 people in need. IRW is present in the planned locations and intends to use the knowledge of the areas, beneficiary feedback and lessons learnt to scale up interventions using context friendly strategies and approaches to provide appropriate solutions to the immediate and longer term needs of the target population in the 3 Counties resulting to reduced suffering, enhanced recovery and built resilience against negative health determinants and aftermath of unpredictable shocks. In line with HRP strategic objective 1 and cluster objectives 1,2 3 and 4, IRW will prioritize sectorial integration including WASH, Nutrition, FSL, Education, GBV and protection in to health to provide a comprehensive services to address the multiple needs through linking of health beneficiaries to the existing services therefore, providing a lasting solution to chronic needs of the affected population in the proposed priority locations of IPC 3,4 and 5. The cluster objectives for 2021 will address emergency needs and enhance early recovery, improve copping mechanism and resilience through community empowerment by full participation in need identification, proposal of appropriate approaches to solutions, monitoring and evaluation to enhance ownership and sustainability.

2 - Project activities

The Revitalized peace agreement in effect reduced insecurity and increased humanitarian space, access and collaboration to provide assistance to the population in need. IRW plan to undertake emergency and longer-term health assistance to 21762 women, men, boys, girls, elderly people and people with disability in Wau, Kapoeta East and Tonj North Counties. In general, 8 static PHCUs and 5 outreach sites will be supported in the named Counties. Gedi, Bagari, Biringi, Athieng-Poul, Rorkou, Kuanyiel, Lopua and Nakabongole PHCUs while Gaitan, Taban, Achiirchok, Pachaweng and Kokoro are Mobile clinics. IRW will procure essential health commodities and equipment to support operations as per the provisions in the Basic package of health and nutrition services. In line with cluster objective 1: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities among vulnerable women, men, boys and girls in the priority locations. Specifically, the project will contribute to the reduction of epidemic prone diseases such as Malaria, Acute watery diarrhea, Acute respiratory tract infections and vaccine preventable diseases including measles. 25 health workers will be trained on EPI and 1314 children from 6 months to 15 years will receive measles vaccination through routine and supplementary immunization activities to increase coverage and achieve better outcome. Project staffs and CHD health workers shall be trained on EWARS and IDRS to enhance timely, quality and complete reporting and response within 48 hours of incidence. 3211 vulnerable people will benefit from mosquito net distribution for malaria prevention. 3251 men and Women will benefit from health education on Malaria, diarrhea COVID 19 prevention measures. Hand washing shall be promoted by installation of additional facilities to improve hygiene for disease prevention including COVID-19.Objective 2: Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems. IRW will hire competent health workers as per the national Ministry of health guideline BPHNS and the health clusters clinical package for mobile clinic. The project will contribute to improve access to quality and dignified health care services through clinical consultations and treatment of common ailments among 9070 vulnerable men, women and children. 2000 children under5 will receive immunization for all vaccine antigens while 1500 children under 1 year will receive DPT3/PENTA3. IRW will access reproductive health supplies and drugs from UNFPA hence, 1202 pregnant women will receive Antenatal care services, TT immunization and safe deliveries by qualified midwives at all facilities. In consultation with stakeholders, 1 facility will be upgrade and equipped to provide BEmONC services to increase access and reduce maternal and neonatal mortality. Basic mental health and Psycho-social support services will be provided to 112 vulnerable people and subsequent referral for secondary treatment. PPE and infection prevention items shall be secured through the WHO pipeline or internal procurement. IRW will participate in cluster activities including sector coordination, health surveys, rapid response missions, campaigns and joint supportive monitoring and supervision. Accountability to affected population will be strengthened through complaint and feedback mechanism establishment. Objective 3: Improved access to services for survivors of SGBV, disable people and mental disorders. IRW will ensure staff capacity are strengthened on case identification, referral, and counselling. 102 GBV survivors shall have access to clinical services such as CMR and psycho-social support. 30 project staffs will be trained on CMR and GBV referral pathways. People with disability will be provided special care including provision of dignified user-friendly sanitary facilities

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)? All IRW staffs are trained on PSEA and have signed to the policy. Additionally, all field locations have PSEA focal persons to monitor and observe staff behaviors and advise accordingly. The complaint and feedback response committees are trained on PSEA and the beneficiaries will be given awareness to enhance knowledge, coordination and establishment of reporting using the complaint and feedback channels.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The complaint and feedback committees are representative of all categories of people and ethnic groups from the affected population. Feedback will be received directly from project staffs or committee members using local language that is easily understood by the affected people. Physical and language barriers to access information will be addressed using various community structured platforms including complaint and feedback response committees, Health facility management committees, community owned resource persons such as community nutrition volunteers, community health workers, maternal and child health workers and project staffs to collect and address or forward feedbacks.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IRW has formed complaint and feedback response committees at all the locations of intervention to link projects with the community and the entire beneficiaries. The committees are trained on their roles to coordinate complaints and feedback between the community and the organization and are given leverage space to participate in all the project cycles. The organization will use this platform and directly interact with affected population to transparently provide information on the project right at the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages. IRW has a well-coordinated complaint and feedback system established at all levels of offices and intervention locations to receive and address complaints using various tools including email addresses, phone calls, complaint box and direct interaction where other tools are not applicable while maintaining confidentiality/anonymity to sensitive information.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IRW staffs will work with the committee platform to engage with the beneficiaries in designing modalities appropriate for delivering assistance to the affected population. People of all categories will participate in the design to make it more inclusive and appropriate to all age groups, gender and ability challenged people.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the two Collective Outcomes, i.e. Food insecurity and Gender-Based Violence?

Yes

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This project will contribute to GBV response through provision of basic clinical care to GBV survivors and people with mental health disorders and referrals for further treatment. Dignified access to counselling services will be provided to people of all age groups, ability status and gender.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This project will contribute to decreasing IPC 3,4 and 5 in the priority locations through increase of equitable access to health care services. Food insecurity will lead to malnutrition and subsequently poor health and death. A well nourished child and adult will have a robust health and reduce incidences of falling sick, reducing medical expenditures, increase savings resulting to a stable household economy and a sustained development. Therefore IRW will integrate FSL, WASH, Education and nutrition into health to address multiple needs and achieve a comprehensive and lasting solution.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Equitable access to integrated Health services will be ensured to address the vertical challenges of the affected determinants such as WASH, FSL. GBV and Nutrition. People are affected differently and have varied priority needs along the line of gender, age, physical and mental ability challenged persons. This project will consider involvement of the people affected in need identification, in order to provide an appropriate assistance suitable to all categories of beneficiaries. Health services will be linked to other sectors such as provision of safe water and sanitation facilities to communities and institutions within priority locations. Seed distributions, kitchen gardening activities, distribution of farming inputs, fishing kits and WASH NFIs are some of the services health beneficiaries will be linked to. Nutrition screening and referral will be integrated into health and growth monitoring to rule out stunting. This will empower communities to improve with coping mechanism and stabilize sources of livelihoods to attain resilience against impacts of affected health determinants.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Health is a key determinant factor of nutrition and nutrition is also a key determinant of health hence, a well-nourished child will be healthy and 6 times less likely to die before the 5th birthday than a poorly nourished child. Therefore, health activities shall be integrated with nutrition as per the provisions in the basic package of health and nutrition services. As such the following activities will be integrated; Children and pregnant and lactating women will be screened for malnutrition, regular growth monitoring to rule out stunting, ensuring complete immunization for all children under 5 and women of child bearing age, nutrition education on good nutritional practices, optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding shall be emphasized. Regular treatment of ailments and health education on malaria and diarrheal disease prevention. Regular antenatal care, safe delivery and good nourishment of pregnant mothers will result to the birth of healthy baby with a robust health.These key activities will contribute to reducing GAM prevalence and the IPC severity as good health promotes good nutrition and verse versa therefore, with this in place, children will build a robust health in the 1st 1000 days of life and have better academic performance and become productive adult.
