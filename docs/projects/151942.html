Project Summary

This project seeks to provide enhanced access to child protection prevention and response services to children and their caregivers in the conflict affected states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe, in line with minimum standards for child protection and GBV in humanitarian actions.
This will be achieved by providing responsive case management services and cross-sectoral specialized services, building resilience by providing community based mental health and psychosocial support, reintegration of children who have suffered grave child rights violations including survivors of GBV and strengthening of community based mechanisms to prevent and respond to child protection incidences. Continued advocacy and programming on the situation of grave child rights violations in northeast Nigeria will be supported with focus on the engagement of duty-bearers, children and community members.
UNICEF will provide financial and technical support to assess the capacity gaps within the Child Protection Sub-Sector and to implement prioritized capacity development actions to strengthen institutional, technical and coordination capacities for delivery of child protection services.

Needs

The protection and well-being of 2.6 million children and adolescents (1.4 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the north-east remain at risk mainly due to the complex and protracted conflict. Children constitute 61% of the displaced, returnee and other affected populations; more than half of the children affected are girls (55%). Family separation, abduction, sexual violence, recruitment by non-state armed groups and other grave child rights violations have directly affected at least 49,500 girls and boys. More than two million girls, boys and caregivers need psychosocial support services due to the severe distress, hardship and displacement. Children and caregivers remain at risk of injury and loss of life from explosive remnants of war. Secondary displacement, loss of property and livelihoods and limited access to basic services have undermined the capacity of caregivers to cater for children under their care; the resilience of communities for protection of children has been weakened resulting in negative coping mechanisms. Violence against girls, boys and women, including sexual violence, and other forms of GBV, is widespread but underreported. According to the GBVIMS, despite being underreported, sexual violence and gender-based violence (GBV) prevalence has increased by 7.7% since the start of the conflict. As of July 2018, 22% of reported GBV incidents reported were perpetrated against children. Of GBV incidents reported by child survivors, 73% were cases of sexual abuse. There is a need to continue conducting GBV safety audits. In 2018, the UN Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting of the six-grave child right violations (UN CTFMR) conducted joint verification exercises to verify the level of association with the Civilian Joint Task Force (CJTF) of documented 3,704 children (3,414 boys and 294 girls) provided by the CJTF in Borno State and the Borno State Ministry of Justice. Since the signing of the Action Plan in September 2017, the UN CTFMR has verified a total of 1,777 children (as at 25 October 2018), leaving another 1,931 children who still need to be verified. Protection risks for vulnerable children and their caregivers are assessed to be greatest in conflict-affected LGAs, majority of which are in Borno State and LGAs in Adamawa and Yobe States. Analysis of protection and multi-sectoral assessments and inferences from the response activities in 2018 revealed that there is need for continued provision of child protection services in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. These include: integrated case management services for children in need of care and protection including unaccompanied and separated children in 62 LGAs (95% of total LGAs), socio-economic assistance and reintegration services for children formerly associated with armed groups or children who were abducted, missing, recruited or deprived of their liberty due to their alleged association with armed groups in 62 LGAs (95% of total LGAs); psychosocial support services in 49 LGAs (75% of total LGAs) and mine risk education due to exposure to explosive remnants of war in 39 LGAs (60% of total LGAs) . The institutional and resource capacities of some child protection agencies including national organizations continue to hamper the coverage and quality of services and there is a strong need for capacity development. Objectives (1)Increased access to quality child protection and specialized services for conflict-affected girls, boys and caregivers in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States,(2)Enhanced community resilience and capacities for prevention and response to child protection risks (3)Strengthened monitoring, reporting and response mechanisms for grave child rights violations and (4)Improved institutional, technical and coordination capacities of child protection actors to deliver services in an accountable and responsive manner

Activities

Child protection interventions/activities As one of the main child protection actors in northeast Nigeria, UNICEF will implement the following activities in close collaboration with the state governments, international and national non-governmental partners as well as traditional, religious and community based networks: Provision of quality and integrated case management services, including family tracing and reunification, alternative care and ensuring access to specialized services for 17,400 unaccompanied and separated children and children with other protection risks; Provision of age and gender appropriate psychosocial and mental health support services. These will be integrated with livelihood support for vulnerable caregivers, reaching a total of 237,500 girls, boys and caregivers; Socio-economic support for the reintegration of 5,000 girls and boys formerly associated with armed groups and other children who have suffered from grave child rights violations; Mine-risk education for 287,5000 children, adolescents and caregivers to minimize loss of life and injuries as a result of explosive remnants of war; Provision of information on child care and child protection to 125,000 community members and caregivers of conflict-affected children to enhance family and community capacities to provide improved care and protection for their children; Strengthening of the monitoring, reporting and response mechanisms on the grave child rights violations; Provision of information on grave child rights violations to 90,000 community members and caregivers of conflict-affected children and engagement of duty-bearers for the prevention of grave child rights violations; Roll-out of CPIMS+ and provision of technical support to child protection partners on use of the inter-agency database to improve case management at field level and inform advocacy and programming; Strengthening the institutional and technical capacities of governmental and non-governmental child protection actors in northeast Nigeria through coaching, mentoring, training and localization of coordination and referral mechanisms
