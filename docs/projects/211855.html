Project Summary

Based on the October 2023 Protection Cluster report, significant gaps persist, notably in legal support, unaccompanied and separated children, children in conflict with the law, undocumented individuals, abuse, sexual exploitation, and gender-based violence (GBV). The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 emphasizes the ongoing impact of human rights law, exposing civilians to hostilities, torture, arbitrary arrest, detention, and forced recruitment.
Violations against children, endemic GBV, and explosive ordnance contamination characterize the violence. Economic decline, poverty, loss of livelihoods, drought, and disease outbreaks worsen protection needs. Lack of civil documentation hampers movement, property transactions, and aid access. Prolonged inability to secure documentation risks statelessness for internally displaced children.
GBV continues to rise, affecting women, girls, and children. Explosive ordnance compounds the crisis, causing casualties and hindering access to support. Widespread damage to housing and forced evictions add to the challenges, increasing strain on individuals and communities.
Therefore, over the next two years, SAMS will continue providing specialized and tailored protection services in Sarmin, Atareb, Batbu, Afrin, Hazzano, Kafr Daryan, Bab Al Hawa, Maarret Msrin, Qah, Idleb, Korin, and Raju. These services, encompassing GP, CP, and GBV, will be integrated into SAMS health facilities. The offerings include individual and group PSS services for GB, CP, and GBV cases, GBV case management for survivors, identification of legal issues, and referrals to legal consultations. Additionally, SAMS will provide CP case management for children with protection risks, conduct awareness-raising activities about GP, CP, GBV, and legal issues for the community and humanitarian workers, and facilitate referrals to internal and external services, including health and protection.
As part of this project, SAMS will focus on capacity building for humanitarian workers and service providers to enhance their ability to detect and respond to GP, CP, and GBV concerns in their daily work. Furthermore, SAMS will provide Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) as in-kind support for vulnerable groups in targeted sub-districts.
To ensure the quality of services, SAMS will implement technical supervision of service providers, safeguard the security of beneficiaries' data, and prioritize their safety during service provision and referrals. Monthly routine visits to supported facilities will be conducted, monitoring performance through the GBV M&E toolkit, including individual interviews, spot checks, and observations. The MEAL team will execute a community-based KAP survey to gauge the impact of provided protection services on beneficiaries and targeted communities. Technical monitoring of the project in Gaziantep, led by the GBV specialist, MHPSS, and Physical Rehabilitation technical staff, will also take place.
SAMS is committed to the inclusion of people with disabilities, conducting direct visits assessing beneficiaries' needs, and tailoring responses on a case-by-case basis. Special attention will be given to providing transportation services for vulnerable groups, specifically women and adolescent girls, by mapping communities to identify targeted beneficiaries. Additionally, SAMS will address the needs of people with mobility disabilities by making alterations and adjustments to healthcare facilities' buildings, such as establishing ramps, and handicapped toilets, and securing clear floor space at entry points. Important equipment, including wheelchairs and support rails, will be made available to enhance accessibility. Monthly routine visits and technical monitoring will ensure ongoing assessment and adjustment of services to meet the evolving needs of beneficiaries.
