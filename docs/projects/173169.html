Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable HHs and community-based assets. Individual capacities will be built across three sectors to improve people knowledge and skills to engage in livelihoods and social service activities. HH capacity will be strengthened through improvement of livelihoods opportunities and access to different services (WASH/Protection). IDPs, returnees, resident farmers, pastoralists, and fisher folk in conflict-affected areas are impacted by underdevelopment - they are still using primitive production methods/tools which are not climate sensitive. They are vulnerable to malnutrition resulting from production gaps and limited access to income earning opportunities. They also face risks of exposure to water-borne diseases and vector-borne diseases due to limited access to safe and clean water resources and poor environmental sanitation and hygiene. Conflict is common over natural resources (water, pasture) especially between farmers and pastoralists. Pastoralists also experience livestock losses annually due to outbreaks, and veterinary coverage is inadequate. Lack of or weakness of community-based governance and management structures contributes to recurring malfunctioning of viable water resources. Existing veterinary facilities are non-functional and need rehabilitation. All of the already-vulnerable people in target areas are affected by implications of political instability and economic deterioration, threatening to further exacerbate the humanitarian and security situation. To increase resilience to economic, environmental, social, and political shocks, this project intends to foster capacity building, self-help initiatives, and technical assistance for individuals and HHs to adopt resilient livelihood approaches; to build the capacity of local institutions including line ministries, DRR/NRM committees, CBRMSs, etc. to contribute to the transition from relief to development. The project will conduct gender and risk analysis to understand roles, and gender-based challenges, including GBV, and devise strategies to prevent and manage risks. The project will conduct conflict risk analysis at the planning stage and will reinforce its conflict sensitive approach throughout; meaning that the activities/results will not be conflict triggers, but rather will act as unifies to promote peace and social cohesion.
The project will follow a gender sensitive approach that enhances the participation of women/girls in socioeconomic activities (50%) and governance systems including their representation (35%) in committees. Gender will be mainstreamed throughout project planning, implementation, and monitoring. Data collected from the field will be dis-aggregated by gender and age. The result framework have gender markers.
VSF will implement GBV prevention and response through construction of 4 permanent women centers in Rashad and Abugubeiha in SK and Baw and Gissan in BN, the center will be a place for GBV awareness promotion, information sharing, and linking protection activities with capacity building and economic empowerment of women. The center will be equipped with small solar unit, furniture, training materials, audiovisual equipment and food processing equipment. Through the center 300 women and girls will be trained on response to GBV; VSFG Gender and Protection Specialist will be responsible for coordinating and facilitating the trainings with Social Development Units, will be involved in selection of trainees, preparing training manuals and materials. Training topics will include: female genital mutilation, early marriage, domestic violence, sexual gender based violence, sexual harassment, the role of men in combating violence against women, HIV/AIDS, child abuse and women rights. 5 GBV/Protection awareness- Rehab of 2 women Centers-Training of 80 women and girls supported with start-up capital on different IGAs- Support community based protection networks and Support referral mechanisms.

1 - Needs

This project proposal is based on different needs assessments conducted by the VSF G including a needs assessment mission carried out in Jan 2020 covering Abukarshola, Rashad, Abugubeiha, Dilling and Tolodi localities in SK and Baw, El Tadamon, Gessan, Roseries and Kormuk in BN. HNO 2021 stated that Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high inflation rates. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population. The assessments conducted have indicated that the humanitarian situation particularly food and nutrition security has worsened due to soaring of food- and also other commodities- prices which affect poor households food consumption and nutrition. Water supply infrastructure is inadequate especially, people are forced to use unsafe sources, several water points have been identified as being malfunctioning and in need of rehabilitation /maintenance. Open defecation is practiced and other indicators of poor hygiene and sanitation knowledge were observed in the assessed areas. This is in addition to the severe shortage of health and nutrition services in all of the targeted localities. The protracted displacement in South Kordofan and Blue Nile state creates a conducive environment for the occurrence of varying forms of gender-based violence, poverty, food insecurity, inadequate shelter and WASH service and low level of education, all of these factors contribute to the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV, in addition to that the cultural attitudes towards GBV in the state are worrying. Though the accessibility to reliable data and information of reported incidence on gender-based violence is very limited due to cultural hindrance, gender-based violence in such contexts can intuitively be perceived and expected, especially during the farming season. The proposed activities in this project directly correspond with the identified needs across the five sectors in the four targeted localities for IDPs, returnees and affected host communities. According to the assessments’ key findings and recommendations in addition to covering identified gaps and needs in the above mentioned sectors, GBV and child protection components are much advisable to be integrated with the project activities as well as components of natural resources management focusing on integrated water resource management program (IWRM), implemented closely with livelihood programs in addition to peace building and cohabitation. Further, IGAs programs targeting girls and women were also recommended.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF Germany will apply different standards and best practices in emergency response including SPHERE standards, LEGS, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Natural Resource Management (NRM) and "Do No Harm'' approaches in the implementation of this project activities. It will be taken into consideration that the transfer of resources (project inputs and services including agricultural commodities and cash) into a resource-scarce environment can trigger conflict in the communities. Careful assessment of the local context, examining how humanitarian assistance would interact with the current situation in the target areas, and the creation of options for project revision to improve its quality will be carried out. this assessment will include analysis of: (i) dividers and sources of tensions between groups: systems and institutions; attitudes and actions; values and interests; experiences; symbols and occasions; etc.; (ii) connectors across sub-groups and the local capacities for peace; (iii) the project in terms of why; where; what; when; with whom; by whom and how and its impact on dividers/tensions and connectors/local capacities for peace. The partners will work together to ensure that the project is implemented in a way that can strengthen local capacities for peace, build on connectors that bring communities together, and reduce the divisions and sources of tensions that can lead to destructive conflict. To ensure that no negative impact should result from this project the following will be undertaken: just distribution of resources, creating or strengthening networks of relationships across divisions, strengthening good governance, the use of participatory processes for decision making, supporting traditional or indigenous mechanisms for conflict resolution and reconciliation, inclusion of diversity of ethnic or religious groups, gender, and youth in project activities and leadership structures. Selection of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their ethnicity, religion or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns or complaints. Selection criteria for all project activities will focus on female-headed HHs, child-headed HHs, orphans, elders and persons with disabilities. The population of the targeted localities; Nertiti, Golo and Rokero, and Kereinik, Sirba and Habila localities includes a high proportion of vulnerable individuals; targeted HHs might have lost a family members during the conflict, mainly men, which explains the considerable number of women-headed HHs among the targeted communities; those women have to act as the breadwinners for their children and often their elders on their own. Enabling them safe and dignified access to basic services is part of the protection mainstreaming in this program.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

VSF Germany will adopt Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. IASC commitments with regard to AAP are incorporated in this plan including governance and integration of feedback and accountability mechanisms into program design, maintaining transparency and dialogue between the partners and the affected population, participation and involvement of the target population in design, monitoring and evaluation of this project, establishment of. Community participation will be promoted throughout all the stages of the program; communities’ feedback was the most significant element for the needs assessments conducted before proposing this action. Community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness about their participatory roles and responsibilities. The community committees will be created to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. Representatives from a cross-section (concerning age, gender, and diversity) will be ensured to participate as community committee members and in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. The consortium partners have intensive experience working with the target communities and established relationships and inclusive community structures which will facilitate the establishment of effective feedback mechanisms. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Good internal procedures and confidential reporting systems will be implemented to ensure the confidentiality and safety of all community members. The communities’ feedback will be acquired and verified through different approaches including regular monitoring activities and feedback, complaints and response mechanisms including focus group discussions, semi-structured interviews, key informant interviews, community meetings, establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes in project sites and communicating the partners’ contacts and addresses with community members and committees.
