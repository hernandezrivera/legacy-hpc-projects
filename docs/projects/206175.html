Project Summary

The project will target 30,000 individuals to provide GBV support and services. Ideally, this will include the establishment and maintenance of Women and Girl Friendly Spaces (WGFS) as well as the distribution of WGFS kits. Titi Foundation will support case management for GBV survivors, awareness-raising, establishment and strengthening of referral pathways, provide Mental Health and psychosocial support (individual and group), Legal education, safety and security education, as well as livelihoods training relating to fuel sources. Additionally, Titi Foundation will engage in GBV monitoring, assessment and information sharing in each of the locations targeted in this project. This project will help both the targeted GBV Survivors and other highly exposed vulnerable members of the communities at risk of being exposed to gender-based violence (GBV) regain and maintain their dignity in society through Sustainable livelihood programs like Fuel Energy saving stoves (FESS) and briquettes. PSEA and Gender mainstreaming continue to be the fundamental approaches that have been well integrated within the program with each beneficiary engaged accordingly in all programs aspect that strengthens gender-sensitive and inclusive mechanisms, Titi Foundation will seek to ensure that all of its activities under this project are accessible and accommodating for all community members through an integrated Community awareness campaigns on GBV, EMAP, SASA models and Legal Aid, safe spaces for GBV survivors, PVC, IPV, Stakeholders forum, GBV support groups, Survivor network and linkages. In conclusion, TITI Foundation GBV minimum package will be structured and SOP developed to define the critical basic support for the rescue and repatriation of the
survivors. Furthermore, to ensure that other vulnerable groups, such as people with disabilities, are included in the distribution of key items, Titi Foundation will work closely with Local Monitoring Groups, as well as community leaders and civil society groups, to ensure that these community members are identified prior to implementation and the necessary accommodations are made.

1. Needs

According to the 2023 HNO, women and girls are differently affected, and their vulnerability is exacerbated by various forms of GBV, including rape, physical assault, forced marriage and SEA. Similarly, men and boys are also exposed to killings, abductions and forced recruitment, especially in conflict settings. An estimated 2.8 million people will face GBV risks in 2023 a 9 per cent increase from the 2022 estimates. They will need GBV prevention, risk mitigation and scale-up of specialized GBV response services. Climate shocks, severedrought, massive flooding, COVID 19 and conflicts have contributed to the increase in GBV risks such as rape, physical assault, sexual abuse, forced and early marriage in south Sudan. The FSNMS+ findings indicate that 38% of HHs reported that women and girls avoid more than one area in their communities such as water points, latrines, distribution areas (food and non-food items), markets and/or firewood collection sites, among others, because they feel unsafe. Findings further show that 32% of HHs also face an increased risk of GBV as they travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water. The FSMNS and HNO survey reveals that only 6% of the community members are aware of GBV response services available in their neighborhoods. TITI foundation will strengthen its response plan as per the HNO 2023 needs analysis on the plight of the South Sudanese women and girls who continue to face extreme levels of gender-based violence (GBV), much of which goes underreported, with limited availability of lifesaving GBV response services to survivors. Ninety-seven per cent of the reported GBV incident survivors were female. Left without access to the protective environment of school by the pandemic, the risk of children being neglected, abused or exploited increases significantly. The COVID-19 pandemic poses enormous risks to children, women, girls, families and communities including mental health and psychosocial, distress, intimate partner violence, family separations, violence against children, physical and emotional maltreatment, and risk of exploitation including child labor among others. The increased risk of GBV and gaps in response services, highlight an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and scaling up of specialized integrated GBV response services. Titi Foundation will conduct GBV prevention programs, such as Engaging Men and Boys through Accountable Practice and Community Care Program, will address harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality as part of the primary GBV prevention intervention approaches. Community engagement and awareness-raising activities will focus on the risk factors for GBV, available response services and timely seeking of services and reporting of GBV incidents. The GBV risk mitigation interventions will prioritize the integration of GBV actions and survivor support across the sectoral interventions along with dignity kit distribution, distribution of fuel-efficient stoves, conducting joint safety audits, ensuring the implementation of the recommendations and providing livelihood and economic support. Similarly, the GBV response interventions will include the provision of psychosocial support, GBV case management, establishment and strengthening of GBV referral systems and implementation of Women and Girls Friendly Space programming.
