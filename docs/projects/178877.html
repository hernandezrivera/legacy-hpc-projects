Project Summary

According to the HNO 2021 draft issued by the Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), there are 13.4 million people in Syria in need of humanitarian assistance - an increase of 21% compared to 2020 - with needs increasingly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy. . Statistics indicate an increased exposure to ongoing hostilities, particularly in northwest and northeast Syria. Limited access to services; Displacement and gender-based violence. Cases of torture, detention, kidnapping, sexual violence, denial of humanitarian assistance and lack of access to it due to lack of civil documentation, among other factors, incidents of early marriage of girls and boys and withdrawal from school to work, often in dangerous conditions, as noted. Conducted by AHF the report indicates that there are weaknesses in gender-based violence. The project aims to reduce the from GBV in northwestern Syria by expanding the services provided at the AHF Center in Syria. Azaz through increasing the interventions of protection activities from gender-based violence according to the following points: 1. Educating women, men and children. Regarding GBV cases through mobile teams, GBV social awareness will be conducted according to GBV group. . 2. Implement case management services that address the vulnerabilities of survivors of violence in accordance with the GBV standard operating procedures in relation to referrals. The project will be implemented in the Azaz region and the surrounding region. Strategies will be strengthened to empower women and girls, with a special focus on groups most at risk (former adolescent girls, divorced and widowed women and girls, women and girls with disabilities and older women) to prevent gender-based violence and norms. Harmful social development will work according to directives and inter-agency tools to benefit from capacity building in accordance with inter-agency capacity development initiatives. The project will target host communities, IDPs, returnees, community centers and camps. All activities are aligned with the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan as well as the strategic objectives of the Protection Cluster. Services will be provided for one year. The project will coordinate with affected populations and stakeholders and is designed to promote gender balance. The project aims to include people with special needs through their participation in community consultation sessions, identification, project design and employment processes, and through the services provided to them without exposing them to harm accordingly with the principle of no harm, and all activities will be implemented according to the recommendations of the Protection Cluster and the World Health Organization in order to Dealing with COVID-19 and the necessary preventive measures to protect beneficiaries and humanitarian workers. Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation is an active member within the gender-based violence sub-cluster, which enhances the coordination mechanism and contributes to effective response interventions. Coordination will also be made with the contact points within the project area to ensure more interventions. The project will be monitored by the monitoring and evaluation team, in order to monitor the progress of the project and make sure of Implement activities according to the agreed action plan and indicators in line with inter-agency directives
The monitoring plan will ensure that:
1. Monitoring the quality of the implemented activities and comparing the action plan using the tools to monitor and evaluate the case management and report any problem Monitoring case management (registration - evaluation - intervention plan - case file - closing and ensuring the access of the service) while maintaining the confidentiality and privacy of the case and that the names are coded in accordance with interagency procedures
