Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the resilience of the most vulnerable people in the communes of Ouinerden, Gossi and Hombori facing protracted crisis. The intervention seeks to address the vulnerabilities of the conflict affected communities along the humanitarian-development-peace nexus, in order to break the cycle of food insecurity and deepening vulnerabilities in the face of climate change, environmental degradation and conflict risk. This will be done through meeting the immediate humanitarian needs while addressing their long term needs by investing in diversified and resilient livelihood systems
The project will help to improve the living conditions of agro-pastoralist households displaced by the conflict by providing them with humanitarian assistance in the form of temporary shelters, non-food items and food of their choice through cash vouchers. There will be increased access to productive assets and use of diversified and resilient livelihood systems for agro-pastoralist households including both hosts and IDPs by building and rehabilitating productive assets (such as village irrigated perimeters, market gardening perimeters, micro water retention dams for agriculture and livestock, pasture regeneration, capacity building of agro-pastoralists. The project also aims to increase access and utilisation of social and financial inclusion services for women and youth groups through mobilization of local savings by women's and youth VSLAs and the financing of their development initiatives, including non-agricultural IGAs by women and youth trained.
WASH is also another project component, with plans to rehabilitate and build new water points, and to set up, train and equip community hygiene promotion and management committees to ensure a sustainable supply of drinking water, guaranteeing personal health and culturally appropriate hygiene in the host and IDP communities. A community mechanism for early warning, prevention and disaster risk management will be set up, as well as utilising crisis modifiers. Establishing this flexible funding mechanism will enable rapid adjustments of activities during crisis in the target areas to protect resilience gains on the ground.
In addition, community capacities will be strengthened to prevent and manage recurring social tensions linked to the exploitation of resources that are insufficient to meet pressing needs, as well as aspects of protection and GBV.

1 - Contexte

Over the past decade, Mali, a Sahelian country with a population of 22,395,489 inhabitants in 2023 (RGPH 5) has faced a complex crisis that derives from a volatile security situation exacerbated by structural vulnerability factors, socio-economic challenges as well as climate change. The gender distribution of this population is 50.3 per cent for men and 48.7 per cent for women. 49.4% of this population is under 18 years old. The average life expectancy at birth (for both sexes) is 52.6 years, which is below the global average of 71 years. Mali is ranked 186th out of 191 countries on the basis of the UNDP Human Development Index (HDI) 2021. The poverty line was estimated in 2020 at 179,327 XOF and the poverty index at the national level was 45.5% in 2022 according to the HRP 2023.Climate change and increased climate variability are already contributing to desertification and the degradation of ecosystems on which communities depend for their food security, food security and sources of drinking and agricultural water. According to HRP 2023, the temperatures of Mali have risen over the past last 40 years: where the average temperature was 28.5°C between 1950 and 1980, it was predicted to rise to 29.5°C between 2000 and 2021. The year 2021 was the warmest on record at 29.75°C. The effects of climate change have led to a deterioration in the incomes of agro-pastoralists in these communes in the tri-border area (Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali). In fact, the producers don't have easy access to market and lack of financial resources to purchase improved adapted seed. As a result, their production is low because their seeds are not adapted to the temperature conditions. In addition, flash flood is causing a lot damages to the productions as the communities are not well prepared to set up mitigation measures to prevent their farm from flood. This situation has contributed to the forced displacement of the population as well as the conflict, thereby aggravating the humanitarian situation. As a result, we are witnessing early agricultural and pastoral welding in the area.Following the withdrawal of MINUSMA, the FAMAs intensified their attacks against armed terrorist groups through air strikes and ground patrols.This situation has led to reprisals, in some places taking the form of blockades by armed terrorist groups on communities that they accuse of reporting their positions to the Malian army.Despite the lifting of these blockades, the socio-economic activities of the communities have suffered a major blow, making them even more vulnerable in the two cercles (Gourma Rharous and Douentza). The escalation in the security situation has also affected the tri-border area (Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali), forcing these countries to unite their forces within an organisation called the Alliance of Sahel States (AES). This situation has plunged Mali in isolation at sub-regional and international level. The trade bewteen Mali neighboring has been affected. The has lead to soaring prices for basic necessities, with a negative impact on the livelihoods of vulnerable households notably whose living from the products coming from sub region countries.In addition to the effects of climate change, which have led to a deterioration in the incomes of agro-pastoralists in these communes in the tri-border area (Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali), insecurity continues to cause large-scale displacement of people in need of immediate food assistance. According to the December 2023 DTM report, 354,739 IDPs (57% women) were registered in 2023, representing 88,313 households. 58% of these were children under the age of 18. People over the age of 60 account for 4% of IDPs. The Multisectoral Needs Assessment Report (MSNA) for 2023 revealed that IDPs have suffered substantial losses of income as a result of displacement, as many have no means of subsistence and no funds to meet their basic needs. The assessment confirmed the vital needs of IDPs in terms of protection, food, shelter, access to drinking water, hygiene and sanitation, health and schooling for children.The regions hosting the largest number of displaced people are Mopti (23%), Timbuktu (16%), Bandiagara (14%), Ménaka (12%), Gao (9%), Ségou (9%) and Kidal (8%), with high needs for shelter and non-food items. According to HRP 2024, around 14% of IDPs are displaced by natural disasters, including flooding, particularly in Mopti, Bamako, Ménaka, Kayes and Sikasso, and by the deteriorating security situation in the Kidal and Timbuktu regions.According to the data in the March 2024 Harmonised Framework report, the circles of Gourma Rharous and Douentza are among the 11 Zones in crisis phase (Douentza, Koro, Gourma Rharous, Gao, Ansongo, Bourem, Kidal, Abeïbara, Tessalit, Tin Essako and Taoudénni) for the projected situation from June to August 2024 and specified that this emergency situation is mainly linked to insecurity, inter-community conflicts, unusual population displacements, disruption of socio-economic activities and deterioration in livelihoods. The number of people in a disaster situation is estimated at 2,575, or 0.01%; 120,923 people in an emergency situation, or 0.53%; and 1,246,750 people in a crisis situation, or 5.44%. The number of people who could find themselves in the pressure phase is 4,051,701, or 17.69%.Also, according to the SMART 2023 nutrition survey, the IPC Acute Malnutrition (June 2023) (IPC AMN) analysis classifies most of Mali's cercles as being in a nutrition emergency situation for the year 2024. The combination of major aggravating factors such as the resurgence of armed conflict, internal displacement, restrictions on humanitarian access, climate change, epidemics, low water availability, low food consumption and recurrent food insecurity has left 3.3 million people in need of an emergency nutritional response (including 2.4 million children under the age of 5 and 908,865 pregnant and breastfeeding women).Despite this, the 2023 - 2024 agricultural season has been judged very poor in the Gourma Rharous and Douentza circles.According to the HRP 2024, the proportion of people living below the poverty line is 45.5%, and displaced and non-displaced people could fall into more severe levels of food insecurity, which could contribute to a deterioration in the country's nutritional status.
