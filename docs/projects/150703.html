Project Summary

The project will enable small farmers affected by the conflict in the Gaza strip to restore their effective production. This will be achieved through rehabilitation of small, open-land farms (including leveling, provision of essential agricultural inputs and irrigation system installation) as well as equipping of five agricultural wells with solar energy units. This will both help decrease levels of poverty and food insecurity as well as improve livelihood opportunities for poor labourers.

Needs

The three recent military offensives, the ongoing electricity crisis and the more than ten-year old Israeli blockade continues to prevent access to livelihood opportunities and limits the free movement of people, goods, and services. They have also decimated the local economy, public infrastructure, eroded self-reliance and households’ living standards. The high level of unemployment (54% FSS HNO 2018) is generating widespread food insecurity. An estimated 40% of HHs are severely or moderately food insecure. Youth and women are the most affected, as they have fewer opportunities in the labor market. In addition, the poor level of public services increases the financial burden on the poor families especially for providing clean water. Agricultural livelihoods are crucial to improving the food security of vulnerable communities. However, the increased costs and poor productivity pose obstacles to this. Repeated military offensives into North Gaza, Eastern Rafah and eastern Khan Younis have depleted small-scale farmers` lands and assets. The resources for repair and improvements are scarce. Land quality has deteriorated, and water depletion and salinity levels are affecting production. Farmers lack sufficient knowledge of modern agricultural techniques and environmental risks connected to excessive use of chemical pesticides and fertilizers. Practices are negatively affecting farmers` health, soil, and water. As the cost of agriculture inputs has risen, farmers’ income has dropped. Limitations of water for irrigation are concerning for vulnerable farmers near and within ARAs. The chronic energy deficit has placed additional pressure on farmers who already experience an increase in agricultural input costs. The electricity outage leads to shortage of water for irrigation and irregular irrigation which negatively affects the agriculture production. At the same time, the escalation of the public sector salary crisis contributes to undermining food security and leading to the weakening of the purchasing power and the recession in the local markets. This has indirectly affected small-business people including farmers. Through the current livelihood agriculture project, SC conducted socio-economic assessments in eastern Khanyunis villages and north Gaza. The results showed continued deterioration of households` income: the average income for the surveyed 1,277 farmers HH have dropped to 620 NIS/month in eastern Khanyunis and 884 NIS/month for farmers in North Gaza. Continued decline in farmers` income will result in stopping cultivation and deepen food insecurity in Gaza. Agricultural livelihood needs differ between men and women in the workforce. Women face challenges in accessing livelihoods: the labor market is influenced by the fact that women’s economic empowerment is limited by their share of agricultural holdings, and their restricted access to services and resources. In December 2017, SC has conducted a `Do no harm` (DNH) assessment in eastern Khanyounis and North Gaza. The results showed that there are socio- cultural traditions and dividers in some localities which prevent and undermine women and children’s roles and empowerment. Female youth feels excluded from benefitting from humanitarian assistance in the area creating tensions as they face the same vulnerabilities and poverty as others living nearby.

Activities

The project will directly enhance income generation and food security for 5,712 people in eight most vulnerable agricultural communities through rehabilitation of agriculture assets, improving access to irrigation, and improving agriculture knowledge and practices for small farmers in the target areas. The proposed project will complement current SC agriculture interventions which are being implemented with the same partners in eastern Khan Younis and north Gaza. The project partners will coordinate with MOA and FSS to ensure the quality of work and the integration with other actors to increase the project impact and sustainability. Key activities will include:1) 500 farmers (including 80 female farmers) will benefit from the rehabilitation of 960 dunums of open agricultural land. The work will include leveling, soil rehabilitation and enrichment (mixing sandy soil with clay to reduce water infiltration and improve the productivity), provision of essential assets and organic fertilizer, seeds and seedlings and installation of new irrigation system. The implementing partners will coordinate with MoA and other stakeholder to provide extension services for the target farmers to ensure good agricultural practices and sustainability. 2) Five agricultural wells will be equipped with solar energy unit to improve access to irrigation for 240 farmers. Implementing partners will hire qualified consultation firm to design and supervise the implementation of solar units 3) 400 poor households will gain access to temporary employment generation through cash for work program. Beneficiaries will be selected based on SC`s social data and poverty assessment. Household outreach visits will take place to identify beneficiaries for participation. Group consultations will ensure community participation in beneficiary selection and identification of needs. Female-headed households and farmers as well as farms with female workers will be prioritized. Those who are unable to conduct manual labour themselves will benefit through support to a household member. SC has developed this intervention in collaboration with the target intervention to ensure meeting local needs and priorities among most vulnerable households in the Gaza strip. SC and its partners will introduce the project to target communities and will conduct further focus group discussions to facilitate information-sharing, feedback mechanisms and identify potential risks and challenges during implementation. SC`s partners have been selected based on previous joint project implementation experience with these partners successfully implementing various donor-funded projects of a similar budget value (as proposed here). They are current operational partners of SC with well-established systems and adherence to SC`s partnership framework and principles including having passed a comprehensive partner assessment and various donor audits. The intervention will be integrated with SC`s current livelihood projects. The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities. Field monitors and supervisors will ensure gender parity. Community Protection Committees will also have 50% female participation.
