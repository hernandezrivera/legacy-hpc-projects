Project Summary

This project aims to support the SNFI response to the immediate needs of the most vulnerable displaced populations in northwest Syria through the (1) distribution of essential non–food items (NFIs) and (2) improving the basic infrastructure and shelter conditions in and out of camps.
The distribution of NFI activities include:
I. Provision of essential NFIs to 51,000 households - 510,000 individuals (estimated 255,000 in 2022, 255,000 in 2023) in northwest Syria through IOM’s Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) in coordination with the SNFI and CCCM clusters (activity 1.1.1.1) through both in-kind and cash modalities.
II. Provision of tents to 48,000 households - 240,000 individuals (120,000 in 2022, 120,000 in 2023) in northwest Syria through IOM’s RRM in coordination with the SNFI and CCCM clusters (activity 1.2.1.2).
III. Provision of procurement, storage and logistics support to humanitarian partners in Syria under the Single Source Procurement (SSP) pipeline, reaching 1,100,000 individuals (550,000 in 2022, 550,000 in 2023) with 110,000 kits per year (activity 1.1.1.1).
IV. Provision of 80,000 winter cash payments for seasonal NFIs, reaching 400,000 individuals (200,000 in 2022, and 200,000 in 2023) (activity 1.1.1.2).
The basic infrastructure and shelter activities include:
I. Installation and/ or construction of 7,000 temporary transitional shelters reaching 45,000 individuals (5000 shelters in 2022 reaching 25,000 individuals, 2,000 in 2023 reaching 10,000 individuals). (Activity 1.2.2.1)
II. Implementation of site-level improvements/ infrastructure (Infrastructure in IDP sites) targeting 100 informal sites and reaching a total of 200,000 individuals (50 sites in 2022 reaching 100,000 individuals, 50 sites in 2023 reaching 100,000 individuals). (Activity 1.2.3.2)
III. Rehabilitation of 1,800 damaged or unfinished housing, reaching 9000 individuals (800 units in 2022 reaching 4,000 individuals, 1000 units in 2023 reaching 5,000 individuals) (Activity 2.1.1.1)
Overall, the project intends to reach 1,254,000 individuals in 2022 and an estimated 1,240,000 individuals in 2023 targeting vulnerable men, women, boys and girls according to SNFI Cluster vulnerability criteria, and prioritizing those areas with high uncovered needs, as well as emergencies.

4 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, 5.9 million people are in need of Shelter and 4.9 million in need of NFIs across Syria. 44% of those in need of shelter and 31% in need of NFIs live in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, where 83% and 93% of PIN respectively are in severe or extreme need (HNO 2022). Half of IDPs listed shelter, NFIs and winterization as among their top three needs, while in these two governorates, more than half of the population lack income to meet their basic needs, and 60% reported an increased inability to access to NFIs. Together these governorates account for almost half of all IDPs in Syria (3.15 million), 40% of whom, are squatting or living in last resort sites. In NWS, 43% of IDP HHs reside in inadequate shelters, 13% live in damaged shelters, and 46% are unable to afford shelter repairs (HNAP report on Shelter Conditions Across Northwest Syria). A growing number of people have settled in camps or informal settlements, living in particularly inadequate, temporary shelters, such as tents and unfinished buildings, which provide limited protection against the elements and require frequent replacement and/or repair. Poor shelter conditions, overcrowding, and limited access to basic necessities in informal settlements and camps also increase protection risks such as forced marriages, especially for women and girls, and public health risks such as increased community transmission of COVID-19 (2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview).

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM will implement activities 1.1.1.2 and 1.2.1.1 through the SNFI Cluster Rapid Response Mechanism, which uses a pool of preselected IPs with proven SNFI capacity to respond to urgent SNFI needs with NFIs, tents and cash. Activity 1.1.1.1 will be implemented in part through the RRM, with other stocks being shipped to partners through the Single Source Procurement Pipeline. IOM will coordinate with the S/NFI Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure needs are addressed and there is no duplication. IPs will select locations and beneficiaries in coordination with clusters on the basis of emerging needs and vulnerability assessments and will be verified by TPMs. For cash, IOM will request market assessments prior to approval. Community and technical assessments will be conducted for shelter repair and rehab, and rent-free lease agreements will be signed for one year. For HHs with PwD, designs will be adapted based on the needs. HLP due diligence will be conducted for all relevant activities. Camp infrastructure upgrades will be done in sites identified as flood-affected by the Cluster and designs will be created depending on the needs. For dignified shelters, IOM will explore the best transitional modality and will target planned camps in need of shelter upgrade. For all activities, IOM will conduct beneficiary selection based on vulnerability criteria. IOM and its IPs will identify suitable land and develop technical designs in line with SPHERE and the S/NFI’s guidelines.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

In NFI/tent/cash distributions, as well as for the construction of the transitional shelter units, tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees is a risk. IOM will mitigate this by involving all stakeholders, communicating the selection criteria, distributing according to verified needs, and establishing a complaint mechanism. Given the unstablesecurity situation in NWS, there is the risk that conflict may impede humanitarian access to certain locations. IOM continuously monitors the security situation and has contingency plans in place, and IOM and IPs maintain communication with local authorities. Additionally, some of the access risks associated with transshipments could be mitigated through the use of cash modalities. For camp infrastructure upgrades, provision of transitional shelters and rehabilitation and repair of buildings, there is a risk of eviction of IDPs from sites due to HLP issues. This risk can be mitigated by only starting works once all HLP due diligence has been conducted and written ownership has been received and verified. Additionally, for rehab works, a tenancy agreement clause should be included in any agreements or MoUs signed with the owner of the building.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All IP staff are required to have undertaken training on PSEA and signed a PSEA Code of Conduct. Details of IOM’s AAP mechanism are communicated to participants, as well as the Interagency PSEA hotline where appropriate. Gender segregated lines and gender-mixed distribution teams are always used by IPs to further mitigate SEA risks. As IOM is the leading agency for PSEA, all programmes are required to follow the network’s recommendations and incorporate them into each project. For shelter and infrastructure, IOM makes sure all IP field teams consist of male and female members, and each beneficiary will receive a poster with the PSEA hotline number to report any misconduct by the project staff.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks include access restrictions due to the shifting of frontlines, aid diversion by armed groups, loss of assets, non-renewal of the UNSC resolution, inflation of market prices and COVID-19. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will be in regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents and conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations. IOM also has a dedicated access unit that monitors and facilitates principled access for partners within Syria, and IOM will continue to participate in OCHA’s access working group. On aid diversion and loss of assets, IOM will ensure warehouses are securely guarded and give awareness sessions to staff on how to handle attempted aid diversion, while also ensuring that IPs provide timely incident reports to its access unit and share the information with relevant partners. For a potential closure of Bab Al-Hawa, IOM can preposition items inside Syria and will work with the broader humanitarian community to further mitigate the risk of non-renewal. To mitigate the impact of inflation on cash recipients, IOM will conduct strong and constant market monitoring and will distribute USD to prevent currency devaluation. Additionally, there is the risk that beneficiaries will sell in-kind items or use cash for unintended purposes which IOM will mitigate by tailoring cash to SMEB needs and distributing items to those with verified need of S/NFIs but whose basic needs are already met.
