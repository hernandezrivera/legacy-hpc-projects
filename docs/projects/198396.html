Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the morbidity and mortality of vulnerable children and women, and improve their health status by increasing access to basic health care through (early identification, management, treatment and referral) of malaria, cholera and other waterborne diseases in IDP Camps and Host Communities in Damboa and Gwoza Local Government Areas.
The project will enhance a continuum of care for beneficiaries by strengthening the capacity of human resources for health to provide prevention, diagnostics and treatment of malaria, cholera and other waterborne diseases including referral pathways for sick children and women. Thus, Integrated Community Case Management of malaria, cholera and water-borne diseases will be strengthened.
The project will also improve the environmental and hygiene sanitation understanding of people living in IDP camps and host communities by increasing awareness and adherence on positive health and hygiene practices through community-based healthcare workers and volunteers.
In addition, the project will ensure last-mile distribution of essential lifesaving drugs and medical supplies for all internally displaced persons (IDPs'), including persons with disabilities, with critical needs in line with the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan.

1 - Needs

Globally, Nigeria is one of the malaria high burden countries and accounts for 27 % of malaria cases. In Borno State, Malaria is the top health risk for populations as the 2022Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Epidemiological statistics showed that malaria (including suspected malaria) is by far the leading cause of morbidity and death, accounting for 18%of mortality. The unacceptable health situation is compounded by poor and limited access to health services resulting in avoidable deaths of many children from preventable diseases like malaria, acute watery diarrhea, measles and cholera. The Week 32 report of the Borno State Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) showed thatDamboa and Gwoza LGAs account for over 1,000 and 500 malaria-reported cases respectively. This situation will be worsened by the forecasted heavy rain across Borno State that will dangerously increase the risk of disease outbreaks, particularly malaria, cholera and other water-borne diseases. Furthermore, the poor hygiene and sanitation conditions in the IDP Camps and Host Communities continue to increase the risk of disease outbreaks such as cholera, AWD, measles, etc among Host communities, Internally Displaced Persons as well as returnees from the currently relocated IDPs due to the closure of camps in Maiduguri. Whilst there have been awareness messages around the prevention of malaria and other waterborne diseases to IDPs in camps, the lack of positive health-focused behavioural change, calls for continuous, intensive, multilingual,multi-channel hygiene promotion programmes to foster sustainable awareness of safe hygiene practices. Thus, the project will enhance access to health actions for the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of malaria, cholera, acute watery diarrhea and other waterborne diseases.

2 - Activities

The specific interventions are as follows: 1. Build the capacity of 40 Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs) and Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) on early detection, treatment and prevention of malaria, cholera and other waterborne diseases. Through integrated Community Case Management (ICCM), the CHEWs and CHVs will diagnose and treat uncomplicated cases of malaria with antimalarial drugs, and diarrhea with oral rehydration salts and zinc, and provide referrals to public health facilities. 2. Establish Mobile Health Teams (MHTs) to strengthen healthcare delivery through case identification, and provision of essential medical care for malaria and acute watery diarrhea. The trained CHEWs and CHVs will conduct outreaches in the IDPs and host communities to enhance service delivery for women and children, which includes intermittent preventive treatment in pregnancy (IPTp), Intermittent Preventive TreatmentDuring Infancy (IPTi), and using ASAQ for children under 5. The CHEWs and CHVs will follow up and record progress through monthly visits to ensure that pregnant women are getting their IPTp. The health volunteers will complement and support healthcare service providers to reach beneficiaries with a complete package of care and referrals.3. Support the provision and distribution of essential medicines and supplies to health facilities to support healthcare delivery. Also, LLITN: Long Lasting Insecticide Treated Net will be procured and distributed to vulnerable women in the IDP Camps and Host Communities 4. Facilitate health promotion and education to the communities on water-borne diseases, hygiene, and malaria. The CHEWs and CHVs will organize educational awareness-raising sessions in the IDP camps and Host Communities on malaria, cholera and AWD prevention. AHI will develop/adapt risk communication and Social Behavior and communication materials on diarrhea, malaria prevention and basic hygiene that will be used in awareness-raising sessions. This will also help to increase access to services and treatments; improve the quality of services; and increased informed demand among women for appropriate treatment‐seeking behaviour.5. Enhance Accountability through meaningful Community Participation and beneficiary feedback mechanisms such as quarterly Community Stakeholders Meetings to share project updates and get feedback to improve project implementation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

AHI will actively promote adherence to Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) that describe the roles, responsibilities, guiding principles, and procedures for prevention of and response to any form of gender-based violence affecting the community. All the healthworkers will be sensitized about PSEA and Do No Harm, and sign a Do No Harm Pledge. AHI will ensure that the health workers apply culturally sensitive approaches as well as understand the structural and cultural factors that contribute to women’s and girls’ vulnerability to GBV and SRH risks. Thus, the application of the humanitarian guiding principles of “do no harm”, confidentiality, safety, and nondiscrimination, in every implementation phase. The service providers will include both male and female health workers so that it is more acceptable for women to benefit from services. The project is targeting the most vulnerable populations and its main beneficiaries are adolescent girls, women of child-bearing age and persons living with disabilities. To maximise the positive impacts of this malaria, cholera/ acute water diarrhea, and other waterborne diseases prevention and treatment program on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoyment their rights, we will : 1. Recognize the position of men as key decision makers, and thus ensure that men receive accurate malaria-related information (e.g. on transmission at the household level and their role in preventing it, the economic risk of malaria and the dangers of delayed health seeking) through effective and relevant channels, such as community dialogues. Exposing them to activities that foster joint decision-making at the household level and encouraging trust in women’s ability to make decisions will be key in this project, and2. Acknowledge that pregnant adolescents often experience discrimination at the hands of health workers and this affects/ delays health seeking, therefore hindering their access to antenatal care (ANC) and malaria in pregnancy (MiP) services. Thus, we will support the improvement of interpersonal communication skills among health workers to equip them to accept, counsel and support pregnant adolescents. Similarly, we will provide older household members and husbands with information on how to support younger women through pregnancy for better health outcomes, and on the dangers of malaria to mothers and unborn babies, in order to improve healthcare access among pregnant youths. Comment boxes will be placed in strategic locations where beneficiaries can put in their anonymous complaints, questions, or suggestions. These comments will be reviewed in a systematic way and beneficiaries will also be oriented by project staff on how they can share their feedback.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability mechanisms usually help to guarantee the quality of project implementation, and strengthen the relationship with and acceptance by beneficiaries and the communities. Thus, AHI will work with project beneficiaries, local authorities, community leaders, sector lead and health personnel throughout the project implementation regarding decisions to commence, adapt or complete programmes. Key informant interviews and focus group discussions will be conducted to identify and ensure beneficiaries preferred Feedback Mechanism. We will ensure that the feedback and complaints mechanisms are appropriate and robust enough to communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from with a view to improve programming, understanding community perceptions, promoting beneficiary empowerment and assisting in detecting misconduct.In addition, the quarterly stakeholders' meetings will ensure accountability within the project target areas and create trust between AHI, the beneficiaries and the communities.
