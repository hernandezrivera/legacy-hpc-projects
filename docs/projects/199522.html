Project Summary

Under the proposed project, DRC, through its technical unit, will implement humanitarian mine action activities in Northeast Nigeria aiming to improve the protection environment of conflict affected communities in the area. This intervention will focus on Explosive Ordinance Risk Education (EORE) and Non-Technical Survey (NTS) activities that aim to reduce and prevent harm from the impact of EO’s. DRC’s proposed activities will contribute to meet the protection needs of vulnerable populations (specifically women and children) while mitigating the risks of unexploded ordinances. The project will reach30,000 girls, boys, women and men as direct beneficiaries through EORE in Borno State, where there are suspected or perceived presence of Explosive Ordnance (EO)

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, approximately 1.2 million refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities across North-east Nigeria are impacted by the widespread and indiscriminate use of explosive ordnance (EO), resulting in deaths and injury, impeded access to basic services and socio-economic activities, mainly agriculture, and hampers safe resettlement and population movements. According to HNO, the number of explosive incidents increased in 2021, with 293 explosive incidents recorded from January to the first week of November 2021 as compared to 295 for the whole of 2020, a trend that is likely to continue with more people on the move and higher needs for agricultural activities. Indeed, IOM recorded only in July, 15,795 new arrivals to Borno and Adamawa states with Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza and Ngala, the targeted LGAs of this call, among the most affected LGAs in Borno increasing the risk of exposure to EO as new arrivals look for areas to settle. Data from the an end-line assessment implemented by DRC in May 2022 and through BHA-funded NTS collected between April2021 and April 2022 shows that 45% of the surveyed communities demonstrated EO contamination. The highest rate of contamination was found in Bama, Gwoza, NgalaLGAs, Borno state. The reported explosive items included high-explosive fragmentation submunitions and other types of EO such as mortar, hand grenades, projectiles, rocket, bomb and improvised explosive ordnance. The extent of contamination could likely be higher when the technical specifications of the submunitions are considered as, for example, a dispenser can contains many submunition depending on the type.

2 - Activities

Conduct Non-Technical Surveys (NTS) NTS activity will focus on EO-affected communities. DRC’s Non-technical survey not only addresses immediate questions about the nature and extent of the hazardous areas but provides information to make all subsequent stages of the land release process more efficient and reliable. For this intervention, NTS activity will target 15 communities for survey in Bama, Banki, Dikwa, Pulka and Ngala LGAs. DRC will use the proposed NTS activity to collect information from the members of the communities; information to be collected relates to the impact of contamination through the identification of local activities and land use to categorize the level of risks and provide recommendations to mitigate them. Furthermore, information on the impact to local, state and national authorities or NGOs within the mine action sub-sector will be assessed. Conduct EO Risk Education (EORE) Sessions EORE intervention aims at ensuring preventive measures by sensitizing and informing vulnerable people on the potential threat posed by EO and provides them with information and knowledge on safe behavior as well as who to report to in case of finding EO in their communities. DRC proposes to provide direct EORE sessions to 30,000 beneficiaries in IDP camps and Host communities in Bama 7000, Dikwa 5000, Pulka 6000, Ngala 7000 and Banki 5000.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

As one of the leading protection actors in Nigeria, DRC mainstreams protection throughout its programming, putting the “Do No Harm” principle at the center of its interventions. DRC has a robust protection mainstreaming package which it adopts for all projects, including training for all staff on protection principles and protection mainstreaming and the implementation of sector-specific checklists that support staff to monitor the extent to which the elements of protection mainstreaming are considered in each stage of the project cycle. EORE will ensure that women, girls, boys and men are mobilized separately to ensure sufficient absorption of information being relayed. Women and men will also be separated in sessions to ensure active participation while ensuring participants are comfortable sharing among members of their own gender. DRC in line with cultural sensitivity will hold separate gender meetings to strengthen acceptance by communities. DRC conducted a number of assessments over 2021 and first half of 2022 taking into consideration gender differences and has used findings to inform programming. An example of information used was the timings to hold sessions with women who prefer mid-morning sessions as in the early morning they have family care duties which would interfere with morning sessions if organized. DRC will carry out sessions in the community to ensure everyone can access and use community volunteers to carry out door to door mobilization to ensure all community members have access to information.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The Risk and Compliance (RC) department, an independent unit at DRC, manages the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) with an interface with protection department for complaints related to DRC programs. During field activities, the RC team creates awareness and sensitization about CRM, beneficiaries are informed how to access, what type of complaints to be raised and the various platforms to use which include the following; - Complaint's desk set up at distribution points. - Toll free lines are available in three different networks including Airtel, MTN and Glo. - Feedback days/ community meetings. - Feedback forms filled by Protection staff who encounter communities. - Complaints boxes are set up in strategic locations at DRC Offices Examples of complaints to be reported using the above CRM entry points include but are not limited to: 1. Sexual Exploitation, Harassment and Abuse 2. Take money or favours for assistance 3. Favouritism of family and friends 4. Exertion of pressure on beneficiaries or staff 5. Corruption 6. Theft 7. Falsification of records 8. Disregard of health and safety standards 9. Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information, including about an investigation or its parties 10. Retaliation against people for reporting suspected misconduct or witnessing in investigations thereof The RC department maintains a database where all records of all complaints are registered, categorised, and escalated to the various stakeholders for quick resolution.
