Project Summary

This project intends to provide timely equitable access to safe, water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/ host communities and returnees. The project will build on existing WASH services and support rapid response mechanisms, which provide lifesaving WASH services to conflict-affected populations in hard-to-access areas.
IOM will provide integrated WASH services in Bentiu, Malakal and Wau PoC sites and collective sites, targeting 274,119 beneficiaries. IOM will ensure that WASH activities are maintained at a good standard and able to scale up in the case of population influxes. Activities will include: operation and maintenance of water treatment and supply systems; care and maintenance of sanitation facilities; and solid and liquid waste management. IOM will sustain and improve the promotion of hygiene and public health, focusing on community ownership methods, including menstrual hygiene management for displaced women and adolescent girls. IOM plans to expand WASH services through the construction and/or improvement of key infrastructure and hygiene promotion to assist IPDs outside of PoC sites and host communities.
IOM’s WASH Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPnR) teams will expand access to WASH services to underserved and vulnerable populations in remote and hard-to-reach locations lacking access to basic services, as identified in coordination with WASH Cluster coordination mechanisms, targeting 274,119 beneficiaries. IOM will ensure provision of safe water through borehole rehabilitation, manual drilling of boreholes, rehabilitation of surface water treatment systems, distribution water treatment supplies and water trucking. IOM will ensure the sustainability of water supply through the formation and training of water management committees and the provision of spare parts for ongoing care and maintenance of boreholes. IOM will additionally promote good hygiene and sanitation practices through hygiene promotion activities and distribution of basic household hygiene items.
The project will additionally contribute to mitigating WASH-related GBV. Gender equality and prevention of GBV will be mainstreamed in all WASH activities such as building women’s leadership to manage water resources and participatory risk-reduction activities. IOM will facilitate the construction/ rehabilitation and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities will adhere to the GBV WASH facilities check list developed by the GBV Sub-cluster and supported by the WASH Cluster. IOM will ensure the active participation of the population in all components, in order to create or reinforce technical skills of community members and to promote actions to dignify the population. All WASH projects incorporate cross-sectoral linkages with health and nutrition to create integrate interventions and scale-up impact.

Needs

Despite renewed efforts to create a countrywide enduring and sustainable peace, the protracted conflict in South Sudan has continued throughout 2018 compounding the WASH and wider humanitarian needs of an already vulnerable population. Despite sustained efforts from humanitarian partners, WASH needs continue to be widespread in South Sudan. The widespread lack of safe drinking water, adequate sanitation facilities and prevalence of poor hygiene practices have left a large proportion of the population at risk of preventable diseases. Lack of access to WASH services further increases protection risks such as GBV and intensifies inter-communal conflict. The conflict in South Sudan has significantly compounded the WASH needs of the already vulnerable population. It is estimated that only 41% of the whole population have access to safe water. As the conflict spread to new areas, boreholes were damaged or made dysfunctional due to lack of repairs. Without access to basic sanitation facilities, the majority of the population, especially in rural areas, practices open defecation. In locations where hand pumps are not functioning, people collect water from ponds, open wells or rivers. The risk of water-borne and water-related diseases remains high. Worsening public health crisis leading to yearly cholera outbreaks. The acute malnutrition, mortality and morbidity, particularly among children, is linked to poor access to safe water and sanitation. The ongoing conflict has led to displacement of vulnerable populations, especially women and children, with acute need of WASH assistance The number of individuals displaced due to the conflict has reached record levels, with approximately four million people uprooted at the end of October 2018, including more than 1.7 million IDPs. There is a lack of adequate water, sanitation and hygiene since IDPs are hiding in in remote areas where there is lack of sanitation facilities, soap, and limited safe water sources. Lack of water compounds risks for women and girls, who are often exposed to violence when reaching remote water sources. In urban areas, people struggle to access safe water due to ever-rising prices. Women and girls of reproductive age have reported using old, torn clothes as sanitary pads for menstrual hygiene.

Activities

- Contribute to the WASH Cluster’s overall coordination of WASH activities through a dedicated WASH State Focal Point for Upper Nile State. The SFP coordinates with all WASH partners on the ground to ensure that WASH needs in the state are identified, overlaps are avoided, and needs on ground effectively addressed. - Provide integrated WASH services in Bentiu, Malakal, and Wau PoCs, ensuring that WASH activities are maintained at a good standard and scale up or revise activities if there are population influxes into the PoC, e.g. increase the provision of safe water as a result of rapid influx of populations, increase hygiene promotion to prevent disease outbreaks. - Sustain and when needs exist, scale up operation and maintenance of the water treatment and supply systems in Bentiu, Malakal, Melut and Wau PoCs. - Implement regular consistent water quality monitoring to ensure IDPs, host communities and other vulnerable populations are supplied with safe water to avoid the transmission of water borne diseases. - Expand access to water, sanitation, WASH NFI and hygiene promotion services to the underserved and vulnerable population in remote and hard to reach locations through Emergency Preparedness Response (EPnR) missions. - Facilitate the construction/rehabilitation and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities in adherence to the GBV WASH facilities check list developed by the GBV Sub-cluster and supported by the WASH Cluster. - Continue and strengthen activities that mitigate GBV cases in WASH structures; mainstream gender balance in soft and hardware activities. - Sustain and improve the promotion of hygiene and public health, conduct refresher trainings for established networks of hygiene/health promoters, realization of mass awareness campaigns, provision of appropriate IEC materials and monitoring. - Conduct hygiene promotion focusing on menstrual hygiene management, targeting displaced women and adolescent girls of menstrual age. IOM will emphasize the importance of hand washing in every hygiene promotion activity conducted. In the PoCs and beyond, IOM will work very closely with the local community existing structures in order to help with transitioning of these activities to the community.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

All relevant information concerning the project are disseminated to beneficiaries all throughout the project cycle through information sessions with community leaderships, youth and women’s groups, and other groups. Information is also disseminated through megaphones, radio, posters, and other visual and audio communication means that is appropriate for the context. In IOM managed PoC sites regular meetings are conducted and information boards updated by Camp Management. Information and complaint desks are accessible and the community is increasingly making use of this mechanism to obtain information.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context-specific and tailored for the targeted audience respecting age, gender, and diversity. Response team develop messages based on the information collected during assessments including household interviews, focus group discussion and key informant interviews to understand community needs. Message are translated into local language keeping in mind cultural context and target beneficiaries. IOM communication team are experienced in developing context specific messages and coordinate with other cluster for specific sectoral messages such as messaging on malaria, Ebola and HIV/AIDs, Protection and GBV.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IOM WASH teams are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse in line with the IOM Code of Conduct. WASH teams also work closely with IOM’s Protection Officer and IOM’s GVB Specialist to ensure that protection and PSEA is mainstreamed in all activities and that safety and security of targeted population is safeguarded.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

IOM conducts regular KAP surveys to gage gaps on information and services in PoC sites and collective sites. Consultations are also conducted prior to EP&R interventions, with representatives of varied groups within the community, such as people with special needs (PSN) and women to ensure that beneficiaries are identified without bias. When possible and appropriate, IOM holds separate discussions with different groups.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Prior to interventions, IOM invites the local community and local authorities to participate in selecting beneficiaries. A complaints desk is made available for beneficiaries during EP&R interventions and a standard complaints-feedback template is available. IOM announces the availability of feedback and complaint mechanism and encourages beneficiaries to raise complaints. In IOM-managed PoC sites, IOM maintains a regular complaint and feedback mechanism where individuals can reach out to camp management who will then channel the complaint to the responsible sector.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

WASH will empower community members to be able to monitor implementation for the project. IOM conducts monitoring of interventions and collects community feedback through surveys, semi-structured Interviews, focus group discussions, community meetings and verbal reporting. IOM ensures that all groups in the targeted community have opportunities to voice their opinions on assessing the results the intervention. The information are analysed and shared with response team to inform intervention.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IOM is committed to ensuring the specific needs of women, girls, boys and men of all ages, abilities and ethnicities are identified, considered and addressed. Gender considerations are integrated in planning, assessments, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of all projects. Gender and age disaggregated data is collected to assist service providers and protection partners to provide targeted and responsive programming. All WASH interventions are based on a needs-assessment, baseline, context and conflict-analysis, stakeholder analysis, and risk analysis considering specific needs of the targeted community. Further, all activities are coordinated with Cluster and State Focal Points that provide crucial background information and analysis on protection risks, as well as specific needs of different population groups. WASH team also works closely with IOM’s Protection Officer and GBV Specialist for all interventions. This enables the team to have a comprehensive understanding of protection risks considering specific needs of different groups, and upon which ways forward are identified and carried out.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The WASH team will conduct regular risk assessments and develop risk mitigation and reduction measures to ensure that access to water and sanitation facilities is meaningful, safe and dignified. The team will regularly consult women and girls on their use of facilities set up and maintained in the POCs and the feedback received shall be used to enhance program implementation so as to reduce risks that women and girls will face assessing these facilities. Communities are consulted on locations to set up water points and handwashing facilities and satisfaction surveys shall be undertaken to determine whether there are any access challenges faced by men, women, girls and boys in utilizing these facilities. IOM’s assessment tools for EP&R missions are designed to collect data on beneficiary access. Questions related to security and access ensure that all interventions can be accessed by beneficiaries in safe locations and in an accountable manner.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All WASH staff are trained on protection, psychological first aid, PSEA, and gender mainstreaming. This means that staff is equipped throughout project cycle in identifying protection threats and inequalities, and know how to mitigate or handle identified situations during response cycle. Further, prior to any intervention, needs-assessment, baseline, context and conflict-analysis, stakeholder analysis, and desk reviews enable the team to identify any protection threats or inequalities and design projects accordingly. IOM ensures the involvement of women in local water committees, which can positively impact on gender power relations and address inquealities. IOM WASH programming provides a strategic entry point for working towards women’s empowerment and towards community acceptance of and support for women’s leadership in this area.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

IOM’s monitoring tools are designed to collect data on beneficiary access through questions related to security and access. Data is collected through baseline and endline surveys as well as Post-distribution monitoring surveys (PDMs). The assessment tools use the Fulcrum data package to capture WASH infrastructure facilities surveys, Health and Education Facilities surveys, as well as a knowledge attitude and practices (KAP) survey. This information is complemented with qualitative information from focus group discussions (FGDs), which are conducted with women, girls, men and boys, and are inclusive of vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities and the elderly.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

IOM regularly conduct safety audits to provide space for users to discuss the design and siting options with IOM staff for implementation occurs. These are intended to foster ownership, improve technical designs, and mitigate against risks of GBV. IOM teams facilitate mentoring on the use of safety audits for water supply infrastructure creation, water point siting, borehole rehabilitations, and institutional latrine construction or rehabilitation. Beyond siting and design options, the safety audits also highlight issues of sexual violence along the way and at water points or latrines, and intimate partner violence if women get delayed at the water point. In many locations, men do not see GBV as a significant issue and are often not aware of the different experiences of women and girls. Therefore, community solutions are often for women to walk in groups, putting the onus of their protection on women and girls only.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

PDMs, KAP surveys, FGDs and end line tools are designed to measure whether the response was conducted in ways that respect dignity of beneficiaries. IOM is committed to engaging with affected communities at all phases of the program cycle and activities take into consideration specific needs of beneficiaries. Activities are implemented following assessments that include participation and involvement with different groups, including women and girls where possible. Populations with specific vulnerabilities are considered and targeted with assistance to meet specific needs, using methodologies that ensure access. The role of women and men in activities is monitored to ensure Do No Harm principles. The unique vulnerabilities for all groups are identified and analyzed to adequately address each set of needs. The procurement of menstrual hygiene kits is in line with the WASH Cluster goal to enable girls and women, including enabling displaced women and adolescent girls to practice safe, sanitary, and hygienic menstrual hygiene management. Gender disaggregation is critical in IOM’s standard operating procedures for collection and analysis of WASH related data.
