Project Summary

The project target for curative and preventive health intervention is composed of U5 (at least 75% of the beneficiaries, boys and girls equally targeted) and P&LW women (at least 25% of the beneficiaries) from host, IDP and returnees’ communities of Duk county targeting entire population in the county since JDF is the long health partner in Duk County implementing RRHP and ICCM project with UNICEF. However, the project only covers 5 health facilities out of 12 in Duk County. This project will allow JDF to expand services in Duk and fill health gaps not supported by UNICEF.
The overall objective is to maintain and sustain the existing safety net of basic essential emergency health services with improved emergency referral services for communities in Duk County with a focus on women, children & IDPs/returnees. This project will provide support to host communities in underserved areas affected by the recent violence in Duk County. The intervention will also target new arrivals fleeing recent insecurity in the neighboring Counties of Uror and Ayod. The goal of the project is to contribute to the reduction in health related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high quality Lifesaving emergency health interventions for the most vulnerable populations notably U5 children and pregnant and lactating women.
The project will support 1 PHCC, 1 PHCUs, EPI outreaches and community health education outreach activities. Program approaches will include inpatient and outpatient consultation, community health education, active case detection and treatment of SAM, deworming and Vitamin A, campaigns and EPI program both static and mobile out reaches targeting IDPs and host communities. The activities of this project will focus on ensuring that PHC and RH services are available in all the area; integrated capacity building refreshers (WASH, Health and Nutrition) to prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks, guarantee an adequate availability of drugs such as anti-Malarial, anti-diarrheal, cholera medications, sensitize the communities promoting the adoption of good health and hygiene practices; ensure measles and OPV vaccinations for under 5 in addition to routine vaccination at the Health facilities.
The project will support 2 activities that include PHCC and PHCU with all the clinical packages. The project plans to achieve the following targets:

The project will strengthen provision of emergency primary health care services with particular focus on maternal and child health care both preventive and curative services to the most vulnerable group of the rural communities supported by pre positioning of essential drugs, medical equipment & supplies to all the targeted health facilities as well as strengthening health facilities and capacity of health staffs to respond to quality emergency health services in Duk County.

Needs

The health situation in Jonglei is fragile with extremely low health care coverage, high infant mortality and under five mortality and morbidity rates. The high poverty and long history of conflict which has left an already very basic health care system extremely weakened, with severe shortages of health workers and vandalized health facilities leaving vulnerable communities with limited access to health services in addition to the huge influx of returnees. The county primary health care system has many challenges, such as insufficient qualified staff, poor infrastructure, inadequate health facilities, lack of drugs and medical supplies despite of significant efforts made by the MoH and NGOs. Most women, adolescent girls and children seeking health services spend two days travelling to the health centers thus the need for mobile clinical services in very remote areas. Environmental awareness on open defecation is low. Very few households have a latrine and standard of personal hygiene is very low. In the proposed project area, men take a more dominant role in family planning, birth spacing, marriage decisions and the choice of using contraceptives. Poor health indicators are mainly due to lack of awareness among children, women and men, on diseases such as malnutrition, diahorrea, HIV/AIDS and reproductive health. Other factors affecting the health indicators include continuing insecurity and vulnerability to natural disasters. Family planning attendance is below 15 %( DHIS, 2018). In addition, the antenatal visits and skilled delivery remains a major challenge with only 15% of pregnant women attending 4 visits. Antenatal is often reported not to be provided as comprehensive package and therefore complicated pregnancies are rarely identified and timely referred. Skilled delivery is less than 6%, since few health units are permanently staffed with skilled birth attendants. Sexually transmitted infection level (14% of annual OPD consultations) is worrying which require targeted health education activities. Child Health indicators are alarming, the immunization coverage is very low 49% of pregnant women received TT2 while 20% of U1 completed DPT3; therefore, children under the age of 5 will be at risk of measles outbreaks (DHIS 2018). Malnutrition is high, GAM at 25.3 %, MAM at 23.4% and SAM at 6 %which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention (ACF SS report 2018). Most households with pregnant and lactating women are food insecure which leads to adverse birth outcomes due to malnutrition. The County has high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (82%), diarrhoea (15%) and pneumonia (3%) in under 5 years which contributes to 70% of outpatient consultations and calls for high sensitization campaigns for timely health seeking behaviour, good hygiene practices and public health initiatives (DHIS Report 2018). The presence of multiple risk factors over a prolonged period contributed to increased incidence of Malnutrition, malaria, RTI and AWD, widespread SGBV and a high number of trauma wounds including gunshots. These statistics (from the monthly data reports) show the need to step up efforts in ensuring adequate availability and accessibility of health services to the communities in Duk county. The project aims to bring lifesaving care to the entire population of Duk county so that children under five, Pregnant and lactating women and women of reproductive age groups are able to access health facilities. This will contribute to the reduction in health related mortality and morbidity and improve access to high quality multi­sectoral life­saving primary health care interventions for the most vulnerable populations in places with very limited access to a health facility.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The accountability to the affected people is essential ingredients for protection analysis for responsible use of power, combined with effective and quality programming that recognizes the community’s dignity, capacity, and ability for self-determination as well as strengthening accountability, investments in direct and sustained engagement with communities play a key role in securing protection outcomes. This in turn requires will a transparent and continuous two-way communication that captures records and relays the expressed risks, needs, capacities and aspirations of communities. Actions and approaches will enable communities to participate meaningfully in key decisions throughout the programme cycle: assessment and analysis, planning and design, resource mobilization, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. AAP will be streamlined in all staff work plans (without restricting AAP outputs to a specific job title or individual) and that senior management exercise AAP oversight. The project will taking into the account of a broad spectrum of the community that must be able to participate in decision-making in a way that is inclusive, non-discriminatory and has real influence in the humanitarian response; for communities to engage meaningfully and to make informed decisions, the project will establish two-way communication. Dialogue, furthermore, cannot be limited to the simple exchange of information but needs to contribute to programme design, management and delivery, pursued regularly and aligned with the different phases of the programme cycle; a broad spectrum of the community will have the opportunity to assess and, where feasible, alter or sanction the activities and decisions of humanitarian actors. Being accountable thus involves direct inquiry with communities on the quality of humanitarian action, its fairness and effectiveness; tailoring protection outcomes in accordance with the context, thereby avoiding ready-made approaches and generalizations; Empowering communities, as rights-holders rather than as beneficiaries of aid, to exercise their rights and comply with their duties; Recognizing and enabling marginalized community members to be represented and partake in meaningful participation in programme design and implementation; Assuring equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes. The project will conduct continuous assessments which will investigate gender specific differences and needs in the prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition. Information collected from assessments and performance reports will be dis aggregated by gender, so as to identify any unique needs to the gender. This information will be shared to beneficiaries through community meetings, Health outreach activities and mother to mother support group sessions as well as through counseling sessions. The project consider gender mainstreaming to ensure there is gender equity, that boys, girls, men and women are fully involved in the implementation. Data collection will be segregated to allow analysis by gender to help inform better the trends, of malnutrition of different gender, hence been able to provide specific intervention to the more affected gender group.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The protection analysis emerging from a community- and rights-based approach that is underpinned by a comprehensive system of accountability will inform and shape the overall strategy, enable the design of appropriate preventive, responsive and environment-building interventions by protection actors. The project will ensure an equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes. The project will employ participatory assessments at key stages, including as part of the Joint Needs Assessment, incorporating systematic engagement to identify the diverse perspectives, risks and capacities of groups within crisis-affected communities, including men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority groups, and persons of diverse sexual orientation or gender identity complemented by Do no Harm and conflict analysis. The project will ensure that information dissemination includes, as minimum, accessible and timely information on cluster strategies, targeting criteria, geographical focus, services provided and entitlements, programmatic changes, and community feedback and complaints mechanisms; The project will translate messages into local languages and deliver them through context-appropriate, protection sensitive methods and channels such as, face-to-face meetings, notice boards, radio, mobile phones, and public fora, community outreach workers, religious leaders, training and awareness-raising sessions, social groups and community centres; Ensure that visible and accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, tailored to the operational context and the preferences of communities, which can appropriately handle complaints like violations of the law (such as sexual exploitation and abuse),institutional policies / codes of conduct; and quality and appropriateness of humanitarian programmes
