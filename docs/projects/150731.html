Project Summary

This proposal is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country HRP strategy under objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.

Further, this project is also aligned to the WASH Sector HRP objectives as follows: - Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank. - Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks

The specific objectives include: a) - Provision of service operational interventions for service providers to maintain existing levels of essential WASH services in the Gaza Strip. b) Provision of capacity building interventions for service providers to maintain existing levels of essential WASH services.

Needs

Since 2006, Gaza has experienced a deteriorated economy, lack of basic services and destruction of infrastructure due to the Israeli Occupation and three escalations. Poor water quality and the deteriorating infrastructure and services, continue to negatively impact quality of life. With an increasing population (2.1 million by 2020), the level of water demand has also increased, leading to water scarcity and a critical lack of safe drinking water (PWA Report, 2015). Gaza has been affected by a chronic electricity deficit for over a decade, due to the low capacity of the Gaza power plant, access constrains to needed fuel to run the plant. According to the Palestinian Energy and Natural Resources Authority (PENRA), electricity needs in Gaza is 450-500 megawatts (MWs) of which 120-140 MWs is available. In response to the fuel crisis, humanitarian actors are providing emergency donor-funded fuel to maintain of the minimum level of critical services, including ones provided by water and sanitation facilities. However, since 30 March 2018, emergency fuel consumption reached levels significantly higher than expected, predicting exhaustion by September 2018. These needs are highlighted in the WASH Cluster Ad Hoc Situation Report (July 2018), the community vulnerability mapping study conducted by Islamic Relief (20172018) . The main challenge facing the population and the water supply service providers, is the provision of safe domestic water and drinking water supplies specifically in Beit Hanoon and Beit Lahia. In response, the WASH Cluster adopted renewable energy (solar energy) for the operation of WASH facilities recommending that different organizations invest in solar energy sources to support humanitarian WASH facilities to enhance sustainability, operation, and access to services. In coordination with the WASH cluster, Oxfam established a sub-cluster technical Solar Energy Coordination Task Force (SECTF), chaired by PWA and main WASH service providers: CMWU/ Municipalities, UN agencies, INGOs/NGOs working in solar energy. The overall objective of the SECTF is to establish a solar energy technical lead in the WASH cluster and mainstream solar energy solutions in WASH. One of the main tasks of SECTF is to supervise the implementation of the ongoing comprehensive study lead by Oxfam to assess and prioritize efficient and feasible technology for WASH facilities. Preliminary findings indicate targeting desalination plants, domestic reservoirs, and booster’s facilities by installing solar systems to reduce and overcome electricity and fuel shortages. In September 2018, a technical field assessment was conducted by CMWU and Oxfam to nominated facilities in different communities to assess the feasibility of installing solar systems per facility and verify community vulnerability as per IR’s study. Based on the technical assessment, Al-Nada Booster Pumping Station and four wells in Beit Hanoon and Beit Lahia were selected for this intervention. Oxfam's local partner, MA’AN established community engagement activities (with key stakeholders and two FDGs per area) to receive feedback and ownership on the intervention. The need to promote women’s participation and representation was highlighted to influence the attitude of predominantly male participants. In a “rapid care assessment” conducted by Oxfam in May, 2018, women FGD participants proposed solar/alternative energy as a solution to address electricity dependability which would improve care responsibilities and house chores. Oxfam’s gender analysis (March 2018), highlights that women and children in rural areas bear the brunt of securing water in their HHs. To address this, women should participate in planning process of water supply and sanitation schemes to ensure that intra-household division of labor are seriously considered.

Activities

1- Developed PV Solar Power Generation System provides 180 kWp for Al-Nada booster pumping station in Bait Hanoon. Al-Nada booster pumping station capacity of 5,000 cubic meters. Attached to the reservoirs, a pumping facility operates with a total pumping capacity of 700 cubic meters per hour with a daily operation of 5 hours to pump around 3,500 cubic meters daily. The water is distributed through the municipal water network in different localities among north governate. CMWU estimates that 30,000 individuals benefit from this booster and 200 KW is needed to operate the booster pumping station. 2- Developed PV Solar Power Generation System provides 200 kWp for four wells in Beit Hanoon and Beit LahiaThe pumped water from the four wells equals 2400 cubic meters per day which is distributed through the municipal water network in Beit Hanoon and Beit Lahia. Ameen Al Shawa well in Beit Hanoon provides water to 12,000 individuals in southern Beit Hanoon while Fatma Al Ali, Al Sayeifa and Besan wells in Beit Lahia supply water to 18,000 individuals in Besan, Al Saiafa and Mashrou Beit Lahia. 30,000 individuals currently benefit from these wells. CMWU estimates that 200 KW is needed for the four wells. Both actions 1 and 2 will positively impact the operation of targeted facilities by maintaining steady operation and water supply, decreasing the reliance on the unstable electricity utility grid and fuel supply for the power generator. Savings of 50,000 USD per year in emergency fuel costs are expected. Oxfam’s partner MA’AN will lead on community mobilization, announce tenders for the installation of the WASH facilities solar system to be undertaken by experienced private contractors in coordination and support from Oxfam. Oxfam and MA’AN will coordinate with WASH cluster, CMWU, PWA and relevant municipalities to ensure all required procedures and quality standards are in place.
