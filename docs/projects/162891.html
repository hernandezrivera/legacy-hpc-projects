Project Summary

Prolonged conflict and instability in Darfur and the surrounding regions has caused mass displacement and necessitated the establishment of Internally Displace People (IDP) camps. The proposed project aims to intervene in education sector with focus to strengthening the existing education and increase access to basic education, responding to the immediate needs of children at school promoting to improve the school environment in three project targeted localities (Darasalam, Elfasher and Kutum. Plan International Sudan have considerable experience on standalone education programing in both emergency and development which focus on fighting to ensure girls and children have access to be bias inclusive quality education that would contributes in development of their cognitive and learning skills. In North Darfur access to safe learning environment and quality education where the coverage is not reached the minimum education standard MES as per the latest INEE book of 2018.

1 - Needs

- In North Darfur, approximately 32851 (16754 female, 16097 male) children at school age in the three targeted localities ((Darasalam, Elfasher and Kutum) in 55 schools, experiencing difficulties and lack of access to quality basic education as per the minimum INEE standards. Nearly 41 schools out of the 55 targeted school lack the minimum INEE standards in term of teaching and learning materials, access to gender sensitives latrines, access to blackboards, recreational materials, toppled with teachers lack trainings on basic syllabus with most school don’t provide textbook where each 4 to 5 students share one book in Kutum and 2 to 3 students in one book in Dar Al salam. Meanwhile many schools don’t have access to adequate drinking water - In the three mentioned localities most of the basic schools are either overcrowded with students for instance there’s (1 classroom for 70 students in Kutum locality 1 classroom for 75 children in Dar Alslalm locality schools, and 1: 67 individuals in Elfasher rural schools), and/or some children have no access to quality education due to inadequate classrooms and lack of minimum teaching materials. Meanwhile this compounded with poor school environment, lack of basic essential education and learning materials, while most of the 50% of the teachers within these mentioned localities either newly recruited or untrained, meanwhile PTAs lack knowledge on protection and GBV issues and mother are unaware of GBV and protection issues too, particular mothers within rural areas coupled with most schools lack sufficient water as per the last meeting Plan international conducted with North Darfur Ministry of Education and Guidance in Mid Jan 2019. All these issues are critical factors impacts the quality of children education in the targeted localities

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

- This project will take a gender-integrated approach across the board by: a) ensuring that the voices of women and girls boys and men, people with special needs..etc are equitably heard throughout the project cycle with youth involvement and consultation of project targeted communities and stakeholders by specifically organizing consultations with women and girls in gender-sensitive ways, and by promoting women’s decision-making. b) Including a gender lens across project outputs including the market mapping to assess, not only the emerging social economic problems and protection issues but also gender related challenges, GBV, children access to education, access to clean drinking water, and barriers obstructing young women and children to participate to their full potential within their respective community in such project. Therefore, they can be aware and acquainted with their rights and process of learn to lead, and decide, as well thriving with dignity and respect within their own community. c) Skills training to ensure no gender stereotypes are reinforced that the targeted communities are empowered with an understanding of their rights and gender equality. And the trainings are offered in gender-responsive ways to ensure women, children, farmers, PTAs and local authorities understand gender issues and challenges faces women to prosper in their life, and their role in preventing GBV or exploitation.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Above can only select one but will have multiple channels including face-to-face with community leaders, billboards, complaints box, and help desk during distributions. For Community Feedback & response Mechanism (CFRM) Plan International Sudan will ensure that target communities are fully oriented and consulted on establishing the CFRM; how they want to raise feedback and complaints, to interact with the organization, and to be acquainted with the purpose of this system. CFRM will ensure multiple layers or channels are available for affected populations to report, share their feedback, suggestions for improvement and report for any type of violations or abuse of resources. This will include Focus Group Discussions, high visibility of Plan staff e.g. community meetings, sharing of appropriate contact details with community members (hotlines and national hotline for PSEA), billboards and suggestion boxes. During activities banners/signposts with PIS details will be visible, especially during distributions. This will help the organization to avoid risks of serious harm of the organizations’ image and profile as well sustaining good relationship with the people it is trying to serve and impacting their lives. Plan International’s Global Policy on Safeguarding Children and Young People puts procedures in place to prevent and deal with the actions/behavior of our staff, associates, visitors or us as an organization that result in violence against a child or young person and/or places them at risk of the same. Plan staff are obligated to ensure children and young people we work with are aware of our responsibilities to prevent and respond to any harm against them arising from actions and behaviors of our staff, associates and visitors, and the routes for reporting such incidents. In addition to Plan’s global safeguarding policies, Plan International Sudan has committed to the Joint Framework for Action on PSEA under leadership OCHA and in partnership with other humanitarian actors.
