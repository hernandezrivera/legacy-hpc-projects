Project Summary

This project will target a total of (8333) beneficiaries (1250 Men, 2250 Women, 2083boys and 2750 girls) will be address directly with lifesaving Health Services. Health interventions will focus on Curative consultations, Immunization, ANC, and referral patients with complicated health condition to high level health facility. These essential health services will be provided through 1 PHCC, 5 PHCUs and two mobile clinics. CRADA will screen children with severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-five children and PLW and will send them to Nutrition centers.
A total of 16 Community Health Volunteers will be train on Protection and Promotion. A total of 45 health personnel will be trained on Health emergencies including; IDSR, and Infectious diseases.
This project will have three objectives; namely, (1) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities among target population (2) Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations in targeted Counties. (3) Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. CRADA aims is to strengthen the existing surveillance system to pick the first case and timely raise the alert. Response modalities will be static, fixed and mobile response in an integrated approach. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and disability tracking tools inform the determination of the feasibility of the response. Rapid response mechanism and mobile response would constitute to 40% of the response
CRADA has a good understanding of the geographical area and movement of host communities in the trend of crises such as drought, conflicts and sporadic fights.

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian response plan monitoring report for the period of January – March 2019 highlighted that Akobo, Pochalla, Koch, Mayom, and Awiel east Counties under severe problem classification. Pibor, Uror and Rubakona Counties under critical problem classification. This monitoring report has classified these Counties acute and immediate need of humanitarian assistance. Moreover, IPC report May 2019 had classified Akoba, Uror, Awiel East and Koch under emergency (IPC phase 4). Pibor, Pochalla, Mayom and Rubkona classified in crisis ( IPC phase 3). Currently, flood affected Akoba, Uror, Pibor, Mayom and Awiel East in which health services been affected and some health facilities occupied by flood. This led to displacement of households and increase morbidity among vulnerable population (children, pregnant women and disables). In last five months Pibor and Awiel experienced measles outbreak in 2019.A REACH, SMART survey conducted in Pibor and Koch Counties among the host population in March 2018 highlighted the detrimental impact of conflict displacement on children <5yrs and pregnant and lactating women ; Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates among children aged 6-59 months (WHZ) 20.7% ; Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) 4.9% in Pibor county .A Post- harvest Tearfund nutritional survey revealed; Global Acute Malnutrition(GAM) rates of 16% ;Severe Acute Malnutrition(SAM) rate of 1.9%, with the prevalence of reported illness 63.8%, measles coverage 7.6%, Vitamin A 9% and de-worming 2.4%. The barriers to child and maternal health outcomes include: delays in seeking care due distance to health facilities.CRADA carried out a needs assessment this year, May and August 2019 and found out that there are serious challenges in accessing health facilities in those locations. WASH services are non-existence as health services in the area. Some of the existing health facilities lack basic health care services and this was a major blow to the communities who were seeking health services. Some facilities have been constructed by the community, but there are no medical supplies; this further aggravated the already dire situation. Disease burden is very high with malaria the major cause of morbidity in these counties, this has also lead to increased cases of water borne diseases like cholera and acute watery diarrhea. Measles cases keeping rising despite reactive measles campaign conducted by CRADA, SMOH and IPS in Pibor County. During this crisis, a lot of violence against women, boys and girls have occurred. Several women and girls have been raped and went without noticing. Services that could be rendered to such victims are not available. No health protection including CMR services there was no established referral health Centre in place for SGBV cases. There is lack of infection Prevention Control, waste management, WASH in health facility, this cause high risk to the community and health facilities staff.

2 - Activities

This project will target (8333) IDPs, Returnees and host community in 8 Counties. This project will provide Basic Package Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS) through five Primary Health Care Unit (PHCUs), one PHCC and two mobile clinics. The activities will include; A) PHCUs; PHCUs will carry out below activities. 1. Screening/detection: 'Nutrition: Screening of under 5 for malnutrition with MUAC and Screening of pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition Health: Rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women - Screening for Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy - Screening for vaccination needs to all under 5 - Screening for TB 2. Essential Health Care Package: Nutrition: - Management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity through OPT, SFP... Health: - Outpatient consultation for common diseases - MCH (ante natal care, uncomplicated/normal delivery) - Routine EPI and Mass vaccination - Outpatient for TB - Application of the Minimum Initial response for HIV - Basic dressing for war-wounded - Provision of psycho-social support - Basic Management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment STI and wound) - ORS Corner 3. WASH: - Water quality control at health facility - Environmental health (vector control and medical waste management) - Management of common illnesses/Treatment 4.* Obstetric Care: Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC), manual vacuum aspiration (MVA), post abortion care (PAC) , PMTCT . Referral: Nutrition: - Referral of SAM cases to PHCC with Stabilization Centre or County Hospital for SAM with medical complications Health: - Referral for complicated delivery to skilled health care worker at PHCC - Referral of SGBV survivors - Referral of complicated medical cases 6. Health promotion/risk communication Nutrition: -Education/ Promotion of breastfeeding Health: - Health seeking behavior - Malaria: avoiding stagnant water pool, Use of bed nets - Use of clean water and sanitary facilities - Awareness raising of the availability of services for SGBV survivors, TB and people living with HIV - Mobilization for vaccination campaigns 7. Surveillance/EWARN reporting: - Active case finding (epidemic-prone diseases, Tb and HIV) - Surveillance/EWARN - Basic Laboratory diagnostics (malaria, TB, HIV/AIDS, and other local relevant communicable diseases (e.g. Kala-Azar) - Sentinel site of early warning system of epidemic prone diseases, outbreak response (EWARS) - Cholera Treatment Unit (CTU) 8- Health workforce: 2 Nurse/CHW- consultation, 1 MCHW, 1 Mid-wife, 1 Clinical officer/doctor, 2 Vaccinators/CHW, 1 Screen Nutrition health worker/CHW B) Mobile clinics: 1.Screening/detection: 'Nutrition: - screening all under 5 for malnutrition with MUAC Health: - Rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women - Screening for Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy - Screening for vaccination needs - Screening for TB 2.Essentail Health care package "Nutrition: - Management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity through OPT, SFP, ... Health: - Outpatient consultation for common diseases - Vaccination against Measles & Polio for all under 5 - Vaccination against Tetanus for pregnant women (if not received) - Mass vaccination and case management for epidemic-prone diseases (Meningitis, Measles and OCV) - Provision of psycho-social first aid - Basic management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care) - Ante- and postnatal care services /check-ups - First aid - mass casualties’ management - Basic management of war-wounded - ORS Corner" 3. Referral: Referral of complicated cases to PHCC, Upgraded PHCC or Country Hospital - Rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women 4. Prevention: Nutrition: - Folic Acid distribution for pregnant women- Vit A supplementation for under 5 every 6 months Health: - Malaria RDT and prophylaxis for pregnant women - De-worming treatment for all under 5 every 6m. Expanded Management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Through the environmental and social management plan for CRADA, the accountability to affected population using Primary health Care approach Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations, which means that CRADA will meaningfully engage affected people at every key stage of their work (assessment, design, implementation and monitoring).

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project specifically targets the most vulnerable members of the community who have been displaced; including IDPs, returnees, those affected by natural disasters like floods. The aim is to enable them recover from the damages caused to them by the prolonged conflict. The target of beneficiaries will focus on the most vulnerable groups among the IDPs, returnees and host communities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project implementation involves community participatory approach in which all stakeholders including CHD, SMOH, community leaders, community networks, youth groups and women groups, state authorities like RRC and other relevant authorities/chiefs are involved from project inception, implementation, monitoring and reporting. The project will ensure that there is increased access of primary health care services in project implementation sites for a period of one year. The project will ensure that there are measures put in place to quickly respond to possible outbreak and timely do surveillance to curve 5 epidemic prone diseases in the county. The project will provide routine immunization, EPI outreach and good cold chain system to ensure viability of vaccines and keep the vaccine’s potency as per EPI policy. CRADA will build the capacity for the CHD staff and health workers to improve their knowledge on prevention of diseases, control of outbreak, diseases surveillance and risk communication in this county. There will be fast and efficient outbreak investigation which will enable proper case management and control measures in the health facilities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

In the course of the implementation plan, CRADA ensure that the programs are well implemented by ensuring that it adheres to the BHPNS and work plan. The organization will work with the CHDs, Health cluster, government and the core pipeline partners to ensure that medical supplies are delivered in time to ensure that health services reach the vulnerable population. CRADA will have other external contractors to rehabilitate the PHCUs and will be sourced through open bidding to ensure that the contractor with the best price for money is given the works and also ensure transparency and accountability in implementing the projects in Pibor, Koch .Rubkona, Uror and Aweil East Counties. All the purchases for these projects will be done in accordance with the approved procurement procedures in the organization. There will be support supervision in the health facilities to ensure that the services delivered are meeting the required regulations. All the project activities will be reported to the relevant government agencies and all other relevant stake holders like the health cluster. Financial reports will be given with the necessary supporting documents to ensure proper accountability. CRADA will also report to financial tracking system to ensure that the money received from the donors is well accounted for. CRADA ensure that there are no duplication of activities but complimentary services are offered to the beneficiaries for them to benefit more. CRADA will also build capacity of the government staff to ensure that they are capable of offering the health services even after the project ends. This will be done in consultation to the community, government and other stake holders in areas.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The general security situation is gradually being stable as the inflow of returnees is being witnessed across the counties. The conflicts often occur when one ethnic group enters territories of other groups, competing over scarce resources such as land and water necessary for cattle grazing. The project is tailored in a manner that encourages the participation of CHD, SMOH and have ownership for health care services in the counties. CRADA will work strongly with other humanitarian actors who are implementing protection activities to ensure that the rights of the beneficiaries are well protected. CRADA will be ensure that, there will be clear referral pathway for GBV survivors to access assistance and this will be well communication through consultation with stakeholders and addressed by a female staff

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Sustainability is hoped to be retained through continued learning and sharing with grass root stakeholders who will remain in their locations after the project life span ends. At the end of the project CRADA will organize close out meeting that will share best practices and lessons learnt during project implementation. Together with the community CRADA will draw out exit plan that will be shared with the county authority, community leaders and other partners. The idea is to draw the attention of other actors who might take up and sustain the project. Throughout the live of the project CRADA will maximally engage the community at all levels such that they will be able to facilitate and sustain the Primary Health Care activities in the community when the project closes out. The formation of community mobilization group and their work become very crucial and as the project phases out they will remain in the community and act as community monitors, continue with their activities especially among the Payams and Boma’s who have existing institutions and jurisdictions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Land conflicts: The static and mobile clinics need land and yet the project has no provisions for provision of land. Therefore through the processes to get land for the health facilities, conflicts may come up. This risk will be addressed through signing of MoUs with SMOH involving land owners, local authorities and CRADA. 2. Ethnic tensions and violence: South Sudan has many ethnic groups with history of ethnic violence and therefore any provocations can cause tensions and violence especially in cases of exclusion of minority groups. This risk will be addressed through reliance on the legally existing administrative boundaries where chiefs and local government will be involved in identifying beneficiaries based on administrative boundaries. 3. Sexual abuse; This may happen during interactions of the beneficiaries, the project and communities where people of the opposite sex may physically, mentally or psychologically act in a manner that sexually embarrasses the other. This risk will be addressed through sensitization, grievance redress mechanism and norms
