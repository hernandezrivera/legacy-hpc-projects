Project Summary

The project will provide lifesaving GBV services and prevention activities in Aweil East, Rumbek Central, and Panyijiar counties targeting 38,850 (19,425W, 9,712G, 5,828M, 3,885B) IDPs and host community. IRC will promote access to lifesaving services through the provision of comprehensive case management, referrals, individual and group psychosocial support services at the women's and girls’ friendly spaces (WGFS). The intervention will emphasize on the provision of and access to holistic survivor-centered services and collaboration with service providers to ensure that the host community and IDP women and girls receive appropriate care in response to their needs, and in line with global best practices and minimum standards. The IRC will continue to use the existing WGFS in Nyal and Kol payams (Panyijiar) and through the UNICEF guidelines on WGFS establish more centers in Rumbek central (2) and South (2). The centers will host age-appropriate group psychosocial and skills-building activities; weekly information sessions; and recreational activities to promote healing, strengthen social cohesion, and support networks for women and girls from both the host community and IDPs. These different sessions will provide women with opportunities to share their experiences and build support networks as well as provide a safe place to openly talk about issues of concern for women and girls, which cannot easily be spoken about at home. Additional PSS activities will be integrated into these sessions and will include modules on life skills, self-esteem, communication and identifying coping mechanisms. Specialized PSS group sessions through the IRC Women Rise curriculum, with a focus on healing from trauma, will be conducted with women and girls who have experienced or face threats of violence. The IRC will continue to provide age-appropriate services to adolescent girls including information sessions on reproductive health and life skills using the Girl Shine curriculum which will be contextualized to address the prevailing needs of the girls in each project site. The sessions are meant to mitigate their vulnerability to multiple forms of GBV and offer support systems and access to information. This will be pivotal in reducing women's and girls’ isolation and boosting their self-confidence. Caregivers will also be engaged to introduce the adolescent girls (AG) programming (for new girls) and to help them understand key aspects that will help them support the girls. To promote resilience and enhance economic well-being, women will be consulted and supported with, livelihood interventions through the utilization of IRC Economic and Social Empowerment Model (EA$E) to improve access to food and basic needs including hygiene and sanitary items and reduce vulnerability for female and child-headed households, elderly, women with disability and those that who are chronically ill. The economic empowerment through livelihood interventions will enhance household purchasing power and economic capacities of beneficiaries to access food commodities of their choice and basic needs from the local market. The IRC will work to mitigate and respond to the needs of women, girls, men, and boys affected by conflict by reducing GBV risks through frequent joint community-based safety audit assessments, address risks identified, and strengthen community-based support mechanisms, creation of safe and meaningful access to relevant services, and advocacy with other humanitarian actors on GBV mainstreaming. The IRC will also provide material support such as dignity kits and survival kits to the most vulnerable women and girls in the targeted project sites and also ensure that within the women.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2019 indicates that 98% of the survivors were women and girls, whereby among the GBV incidents reported, physical assault perpetrated by an intimate partner accounted for 37%, sexual violence 18%, and emotional abuse 25%. Women and girls continue to face the brunt of the devastating famine and ongoing conflict, gender roles, and community-based practices assign them the duty of ensuring family sustenance with worsened food security affecting many communities hence an increased likelihood of sexual violence while traveling long distances in search of food. A multi-sectoral flood assessment report conducted by the South Sudan Red Cross in June 2019 for the population affected by floods in indicated that 7,780 households in greater Aweil were affected by the flooding which led to displacement and this increase the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV, in addition to protection needs among children and the people living with disabilities. According to the GBVIMS report for Aweil East One-stop Center in Malualkon, there was a 43% increase in the number of GBV incidents reported between January-June 2019 compared to August-December 2018. This coupled with harmful traditional practices and limited GBV response services remains a major gaps to the women and girls in need of care and support. While in Panyijiar County findings from an inter-agency safety audit assessment (June 2019) conducted in Nyal and Kol Payams show that multiple displacements have continually exposed women and girls to violence and other forms of vulnerabilities which remains risky especially when going to collect food, water, and firewood in the surrounding areas. Currently IRC and IMC are providing the GBV services but however due to the vastness of the county some of the Payams are not yet reached with the services. In the Lakes State, persistent insecurity and armed conflict especially due to cattle raiding have disrupted livelihood activities, affected market functionality which has impacted the community's economic well-being. A Rapid Protection Assessment conducted by DRC in Cueibet Rumbek Center (April 2019) indicated that incidents of rape and sexual assault were happening in the assessed locations. The collection of firewood from the surrounding areas continued to cause great risk to the women whereby in several FGDs conducted rape and assaults were reported when going to the bush for firewood, wild fruits and anything else they could find available. Informational gaps and limited engagement with the communities to promote positive social norms amongst men and boys to enhance a protective environment for women and girls remain a challenge towards GBV mitigation. Across South Sudan, violence against women and girls has been pervasive owing to the decades of political instability as a result of prolonged conflict. The increasing trend in sexual violence, the recent scale of intimate partner violence and the important gaps identified to justify the prioritization of sectoral support for GBV prevention and response in the HRP allocation. IRC will scale up its current GBV programming in the proposed areas especially in Aweil East and Rumbek Center beyond the One Stop Centers, through incorporation of the community engagement activities to strengthen the community based mechanisms in prevention and response to GBV, while in Panyijiar County expand the coverage to additional Payams for the 2020 programming. Establishment of Women and Girls friendly spaces, livelihood support and trainings, capacity building, functional referral pathways and comprehensive response mechanism will be made available for the targeted populations. The areas identified in this proposal require robust scale of interventions to ensure violence prevention and services. In addition, the increased risks, coupled with gaps in GBV service provision, necessitates the urgent need for strengthened GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services.

2 - Activities

Under GBV response, IRC will utilize the survivor-centered approach to provide age-appropriate case management and referral services through a holistic and coordinated approach for all GBV survivors both male and female (adults and children) who seek help. Also individual counseling and group psychosocial support activities will be provided using the IRC Women Rise PSS curriculum. 100% of all survivors who report will be provided with comprehensive care support and linked with other service providers for additional support. Psychosocial support sessions will be tailored to address the needs of each specified age groups with sessions for adolescent girls and women held differently. IRC guided by UNICEF recommendations for the establishment of WGSS, will establish additional Women and Girls safe spaces in Rumbek Center (2) and Aweil East (2) targeting 6 functional centers which will include the already existing women and girls safe spaces in Panyijiah (1 Nyal, 1 Kol). To enhance the case management services, weekly case management review meetings will be conducted and follow up bilateral meetings with service providers as well as establishment and frequent updating of a functional, safe and immediate referral pathways for GBV survivors based on changing contexts. Within the WGSS, context-based Skill building and recreational activities will be provided for women and girls and IRC will utilize the newly developed WGSS tool kit to guide its center-based activities across all the project sites.Community sensitization on the GBV available services in line with referral pathways to community members. Adolescent girls will be enrolled in the life skills program through the Girl Shine curriculum which incorporates sessions for their caregivers aimed at strengthening support for the girls and building their confidence as they transition from childhood to adulthood. Feedback mechanism on the provision of services will be established, including the distribution of risk mitigation materials and PSS services. This feedback will include client satisfaction surveys, quarterly listening sessions with women and girls, and quarterly community group discussions. Self-care and debriefing sessions for caseworkers and response teams will be prioritized to enhance their psychosocial well-being. On GBV prevention and risk mitigation, IRC will conduct focus group discussions, safety audits and community mapping assessments with women and adolescent girls to identify GBV risk prevalence in the community. Bi-weekly and monthly coordination/advocacy meetings with humanitarian actors will be held to ensure needs for women and girls identified are prioritized by other sectors. Targeted vulnerable women and girls including those with disabilities will be provided with risk mitigation material support to address basic needs .Training targeting caseworkers, outreach workers, humanitarian service providers and community structures including women groups will be conducted to enhance their knowledge and skills to respond to and prevent GBV. IRC will conduct mass community sensitization and training sessions on gender and GBV related topics targeting religious leaders, women’s leaders, and parties to the conflict will be incorporated in the project to strengthen the community’s capacity to address GBV and protection concerns. Boys and girls will be engaged in gender discussion sessions at the respective schools to enhance their understanding of gender dynamics and power in the endeavor to mitigate GBV. Through the Engaging Men through Accountable Practice curriculum (EMAP), 200(100 M, 100 F) community members targeting men with the voices from women will be enrolled in the curriculum across all the project sites to ensure accountability and enhance the protection environment. Livelihood opportunities for the targeted vulnerable women will be provided through the IRC Economic and Social empowerment (EA$E) model.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

IRC will ensure that through out the project implementation the protection principles will be adhered to whereby all the services provided and the activities tailored for respective community groups i.e Women Girls, Men and Boys do not cause any harm but rather are of impact and contribute to the overall protection environment of the people of concern. Women and girls safe spaces will be constructed upon consultation with the women and girls in addition to the community leaders so as to ensure that they are accessible and located in a safe area. Community will be sensitized of PSEA and the available safe reporting mechanisms especially on the need for reporting.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

IRC will work closely with the community structures especially the women groups where their capacity will be built and mentored during the project period so that they will take over after the project ends. IRC will ensure continuous capacity development and mentorship of the local organizations’, women groups and community key stakeholders on GBV prevention and response. This will ensure that the community is fully equipped with knowledge and skills to prevent and respond to GBV and support safe access to the available services even after IRC’s exit. IRC will ensure that the women and girls are involved in designing the WGFS activities which will enhance the ownership and assure continuous access and utilization of the centers. During the project women and girls will be involved in the development of the community based referral pathways and ensure their participation in community consultations on the exit strategy so as to identify the steps and support needed after the project period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

IRC will strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project period across the three proposed locations. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members are aware of what PSEA entails, the available reporting mechanisms and the importance of reporting. IRC will build the capacity of PSEA networks or focal point(s) who will be included in the community sensitization activities to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms. IEC materials with PSEA messages will be developed, translated into the local language, and distributed to the community. The humanitarian workers through the various coordination forums will be reminded of the protection principles to ensure that they do not take advantage of, or their activities are not causing more harm to the beneficiaries. The IRC will work closely with other GBV partners at the community level to ensure that there is no duplication of activities, and also enhance synergies with other service providers such as health, livelihoods, WASH, shelter and NFI partners. IRC will scale-up the community sensitization activities to ensure communities are well versed with the available GBV services, and in addition, enhance their participation in developing the community referral pathways from the onset of the project. Advocacy on the inclusion of women in decision making at the community level will be done continuously with the existing community structures to ensure that their voice and participation is incorporated in the decisions made to facilitate a protective environment for women and girls. Advocacy and sensitization for protection mainstreaming across all the sectors will be conducted to ensure that both men and women, boys and girls have equal access to the available services and opportunities including the inclusion of women in the cash for work activities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IRC has established multi-sector Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms( CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project location through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the IRC. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the IRC to handle any compliant report and feedback. The reporting procedures will be displayed in a visible place in the project location and complains and feedback boxes will be placed at WGSS and project site offices for those who are literate to use. Views and feedback will be collected from women, girls and community beneficiaries including those living with different disabilities and the minority, authorities from the start of the project to the intervention and monthly thereafter. Feedback and complaints will generally be collected using FGD’s with groups of men, women, boys, and girls; during community sensitization and awareness sessions; and through the use of suggestion boxes. IRC’s community feedback mechanism allows for a range of complaints to be received and managed, including a mechanism for confidentially fast-tracking concerns of a serious nature such as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Through this discussion, the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries of the IRC will explain its procedures, decisions and allow women and girls to hold IRC accountable. Awareness raising on existing PSEA Networks or Focal Point(s) will be included to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms.
