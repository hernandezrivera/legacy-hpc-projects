Project Name

Project Summary

This project will contribute to reductions of higher morbidity mortality rate by timely detections, reporting, coordination and response to epidermic prone disease in the targeted areas, it will also. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.
Will coordinate with other actors the like of health cluster at state and national level, WASH and Nutrition lusters, GBV and FSLC clusters, SMoH and NMoH for supplies provision, referral pathway to integrated services amongst others.
As shocks and humanitarian needs keeps on increasing, 9.06m people are in dire need of aid assistant, while people with health needs are 5.9m people across the country as per HNO glace indicate. As segregated, 22% are men representing 2.0m, 2.2% are women stand at 2.2m, 28% are boys representing 28%, girls represent 26% at 2.4 across the country.
Maternal mortality in South Sudan is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births. Only 39 percent women are availing recommended four Antenatal Care Services and 14.7 percent skilled birth attendance. As per ISNA survey indicate, approximately 112 under-five children per 1000 live births are dying every year and at least 18.5% households reported losing a relative. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6 percent with modern methods at 5 percent, with the unmet need for family planning estimated at 29.7 percent for 2020 (FP 2020). Gender inequality and gender-based violence are widespread and perpetuated by several factors including socio-cultural norms and armed groups presence in communities.
This project aims to address the problems of high morbidity and mortality rate and common diseases: Malaria, Diarrhea, Pneumonia, COVID-19, Measles and cholera, a situation brought about by limited number of functional health facilities, scarcity of supplies and flooding seasons. The project takes into consideration issues of gender disparity and try to improve accessibility to services for the most disadvantaged groups in the communities, in Ayod, Duk, Akobo, Panyijar, Gogrial East counties as disaggregated as follows; men 1500, women 2500, boys 2700 and girls 3300, people living with disabilities 15% and elderly people age 60 years above 5%)The beneficiaries will be provided with essential primary healthcare services in static, outreaches and mobile health facilities using qualified, Clinical officers, Midwives, Nurses and other health care providers. They will also be provided with mosquito nets, blankets and water chlorination tabs that will increase drinking safe and clean water which is favorable for human use.
The project will have supplies from Core pipe lines WHO UNFPA and MoH respectively.
This project will positively contribute to health cluster objectives 1. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations
EDA will monitor and evaluate the project and will report to health cluster, CHD and state MoH through 5Ws, IDSR, EWARS and DHIS2. This report will be collected and analyze by our M & E focal person and data will be shared in weekly and monthly basis. EDA will coordinate with state health cluster and involve in health needs assessment. We will coordinate with other partners from different clusters (nutrition, FSL,WASH protection so that there will be strong effort and synergy to improve the livelihood of affected population in Ayod. EDA will coordinate and maintain AAP strategy to keep local community and beneficiaries in the implementation process.

1 - Needs

Since Independent, South Sudan heavily depend on humanitarian aids with increase in 2013 due to conflict and flooding, this has worsen health indicators, cause food insecurity, malnutrition for under-five, lactating women and pregnant mothers, There is lack of infrastructures of health facilities and the main health system is compromised greatly by flooding and continuous conflicts. This cause health critical indicators at lower rate according to DHIS-2, IDSR and EWARS, Penta 3 coverage is (49% as per DHIS-2 rating Jan-October 2022) measles stand 69%, Skill birth at 51% and ANC fourth visit 48% according to DHIS-2 rating analysis. As flooding and conflicts occurring, Malaria keeps increasing during rainy seasons that cause flash flooding and keeps displacing the people and their live-stocks, the recent conflict in Tonga, Atar and Diel also displace some people to North part of Ayod and their situation is dire. This left a lot of gaps unclose due to insufficient resources cause by the huge needs across the world since the influx of refugees and IDPs is a global concerns in almost all the world continents, health remained the great concerns, With at least health cluster PiN indicate, 6,116,687 are seeking health services across the country, as glace HNO report indicate 9.09%m are in dire need of aid assistant, while people with health needs are 5.9m people across the country, below is the segregation by percentage, 22% are men representing 2.0m, 2.2% are women stand at 2.2m, 28% are boys representing 28%, girls represent 26% at 2.4 across the country, Therefore provisions of quality essential healthcare services shall be the top priority of this project, in doing so, qualify health professional shall be recruited and deployed across the healthcare units and center to reduce maternal mortality, this will be achieve through professional midwives to deliver mothers, EPI services to be intensify to help reduce childhood diseases, Episcopal Development Aid -EDA, There shall also be intensive awareness on infection preventions and diseases control, health educations sessions at the health facilities, coordinate with other actors to strengthen the referral pathway with GBV victims for further management, nutrition providers for nutrition cases management, we will integrate our existing health project with SSHF project and coordinate with other actors in the areas. In the targeted locations, EDA will implement and offer the healthcare services according to South Sudan basic package of health and nutrition services guide, in static, outreaches and mobile in the targeted locations, EDA will work closely with other actors on group to coordinate services. As South Sudan has continued to heavily depend on humanitarian aid, the following are required to be addr the deteriorating situation in the targeted counties.• Climatic shocks - Floods and droughts, led to displacements, destructions of health, food insecurity and educational facilities, destruction of homestead, loss of crops and live stocks and increased incidences of human and livestock diseases.• Sub-national and inter - communal conflicts, coupled with widespread insecurity, affected livelihoods and loss of productive assets as well as reduction in ownership of livestock. • Macroeconomic crisis, causing continuous depreciation of the South Sudan Pound and corresponding skyrocketing of prices and thereby eroding the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets to purchase food and other basic needs.• The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted and continues to disrupt supply chains. IRNA and IPC reports, indicate that Akobo, Fangak, Canal/Pig/Pibor, Leer and Mayendit are likely to undergo catastrophic IPC-5 between Oct-Nov 2022, 61,000 persons will be affected, period Dec 2022-Mar 2023, 32,000 persons are likely to suffer and between Apr-Jul 2023, 44,000 are likely to increase in 2023 if much effort is not exerted to mitigate the worsening humanitarian.

2 - Project activities

1. Recruitment and deployment of healthcare workers i.e. Clinical Officers, Nurses, Midwives and field highly technical team to supervise the junior clinicians. 2. Early detection of epidemic diseases, carry out intensive surveillance, IPC practices, investigation and timely reporting to the next level of care. 3. Intensifying expanded activities on immunization by administering all the childhood diseases antigens, procurement and prepositioning emergency items to areas of interventions. 4. Prepositioning of life-saving healthcare services on the target of managing communicable and non-communicable diseases throughout the lifespan of the project in either static, mobile and outreaches at (Hospitals, PHCCs, and PHCUs) supplying other significant medical equipment that may include, BeMONC equipment, midwifery kits amongst other. 5. Enhance humanitarian-peace-development by building truth between aid actors, communities and the governing authorities, this will help the aids providers to operates smoothly without fear. 6. Ensuring quality provision of health services throughout the lifespan of the project, this will be achieved through quality supportive supervision with clear objectives with special focus on specific areas in the hospitals, PHCC/Us. 7. Defaulters tracing through community health-based services, the like of Boma health initiative, this will be achieved through coordinating with other actors and existing of our health project funded by the world bank in Ayod, this will help to identify and report the children who missed their interval doses to receive full coverage. 8. Requesting medicines and other medical supplies, the like of vaccines from; WHO, mosquito nets from PSI, UNICEF, MoH, UNFPA, Health Clusters as well as finding transportation through Logistics cluster WFP for emergency transport that will support the project. 9. Health education and promotion through hygiene and keys health messages. 10. Training of healthcare providers through on job and special training sessions on CMR, HIV, COVID-19, IPC, and on how to report suspected outbreaks and how they can response to.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will follow the guidelines on accountability systems developed by cluster. It will involve all community groups throughout the implementation period, with special attention to the needs of women, children and physical impairment persons and people with special needs. It will also seek collaboration with other project relevant authorities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will provide training to the beneficiaries on how best to protect oneself and reporting sexual exploitation and abuse. In addition, the project will use various community engagement and communication modalities to advocate to stop practice.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

It will use the feedback to take corrective measures in the implementation of the project.
