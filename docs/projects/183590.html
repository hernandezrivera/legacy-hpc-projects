Project Summary

This action will contribute to improved access to agriculture and farming for vulnerable Palestinians in West Bank (WB) and enhance the potential and sustainability of livelihoods. It aims at addressing the priority needs identified by the FSS cluster, committing to the FSS Objectives and is also in line with the SDG 2 Zero Hunger. Within this framework, WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC) and the Applied Research Institute Jerusalem (ARIJ) designed a complementary action that capitalizes on each partner expertise and resources. In close coordination with local authorities, Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and FSS, the action will contribute to local plans in mitigating the risks faced by communities and support resilience building towards protracted threats and vulnerabilities. Safe access to resources will be sponsored to sustain income generation for households (HHs) with disrupted livelihoods due to intensification of demolition and confiscation of agricultural assets, climate change and Covid19. Specifically, the intervention will increase the access to natural resources through agricultural land/road/water resources rehabilitation and by providing inputs and services. Lastly, marginalized communities’ contracting power on rights and needs in front of duty-bearers will be sustained through advocacy awareness sessions. Priority will be given for
females, youth and people with special needs. Hence, the action unfolds on 3 pillars:
1. Restoration of access to natural resources : 90 dunum agricultural land /8km road rehabilitation in 6 communities; 2 wells, 2 cistern, 3 springs and 6 agricultural pools rehabilitation and 15 dunum irrigation systems installation in 15 communities; inputs for dryland farming for 500 dunum in 5 communities; 50 home gardens in 5 communities for 75 people.
2. Support to livelihood sustainability: 15 sheep and goat barn rehabilitation in 2
communities; 7 automatic milk feeding machine for newborn in 4 communities; 10 hybrid feeders in 1 community; 70 sheep feeders with milking barriers in 5 communities; 45 food processing assets for livestock-breeders in 4 communities; 118 dairy products toolkits in 5 communities; CB in livestock farm management, newborn sheep and goats’ feeding, dairy processing practices through 13 trainings (205 people,> 50% women)
3. Multi-scale (community, local/national authorities, international actors) advocacy: 1 group advocacy trainings in 15 communities; elaboration/dissemination of 5 food security and livelihood factsheets at governorate level;1 advocacy/dissemination event with relevant stakeholders; including FSS, advocacy FT, HCT advocacy WG. The action will benefit 922 HHs in 39 communities mostly in Area C for a total of 5126 direct beneficiaries (1493 women, 1585 men, 968 girls, 972 boys, 108 elders of which 56
PWD) in Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, Jerusalem, Jordan Valley (Jericho), Salfit and Tulkarem. The action serves beyond mere material needs by addressing broader issues of safety and dignity in order to promote gender and age responsive protection, equitable participation and access to services while preventing potential harm on different population groups including protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. The partnership will capitalize on the partners’ experience, WW-GVC is a WASH-expert and is the focal point within the WB Protection Consortium for 169 communities in Area C, while ARIJ has an expertise in promoting socioeconomic development through enhancing access to natural resources by promoting innovative, sustainable action, ARIJ will be targeting beneficiaries in the Southern Part of the West Bank mainly, and will cooperate with WWGVC in an integrated approach and exchanging experience to leverage the impact of the project, and will support with its experience in innovative interventions and advocacy techniques.

1 - Needs

In 2021, around 2 million Palestinians (36.7% of the population) experience moderate to severe food insecurity (HNO, 2022). Moreover, according to the latest Multi-Sectorial Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by REACH, 36% of Palestinian HHs in the WB have lost their job while 68% report that their monthly income has decreased due to the Covid19 pandemic, with severe impacts on food security and livelihood. More specifically, 38% of Palestinian HHs in the WB spend more than 60% of their income in food consumption while 4% of them contract debt to meet basic food consumption needs. Also, food consumption rates showcase a gender dimension in the WB, with 8.4% of female single-headed HHs presenting a borderline or poor food consumption vis-à-vis the 4.7% of men single-headed HHs. According to the FSS Humanitarian Needs, 238,622 people had their livelihood disrupted due to the consequences of Covid19 while around 50,662 are in need of basic food in the WB. Unemployment rates in the WB are on the rise with 43% unemployment among women, 20% among men and 36% among youth (UN Women). The Bedouin and herding communities in Area C are among the most vulnerable in the WB. These communities are facing constant threats of forcible transfer and increasing challenges to their vulnerabilities by the presence of Israeli occupation forces issuing military orders, settlement outposts expansion, increasing land confiscation, and volatile settler violence. Furthermore, the movement restrictions linked to Covid19 have substantially impacted their livelihoods. Hence, the increase of poverty and unemployment exposes Palestinian families to a greater risk of food insecurity and poverty than before. Women represent a particularly vulnerable group, given the patriarchal culture that stresses women’s role in the domestic sphere. Women specifically suffer from abuse and they are more vulnerable to poverty and hunger; the same applies to children, elders and PWDs. Moreover, WW-GVC elaborates and updates on a regular basis the Protection Response Plans, a set of sustainable multi-sectorial/stakeholder/scale actions designed with community members to address the specific coercive environment. This tool is built on the Community Protection Approach (CPA) - a participatory quantitative/qualitative data-collection process implemented to inform a contextual and multi-sectorial protection analysis to identify the protection risks experienced at community and Age Gender and Diversity (AGD) level and design sustainable solutions - and was used to identify priority activities. ARIJ instead has developed community vulnerability spatial and statistical information system at community level, which assists in identifying the most vulnerable communities based on socioeconomic, demographic, geopolitical and vulnerability data. The assessments conducted within the course of 2021 by WW-GVC and ARIJ highlighted the persistence of a coercive environment, increasing protection threats and restricted access to basic natural resources, livelihood inputs and services. Also, the targeted communities were found vulnerable to climate change as well as drought conditions and suffering from limited access to water or affected by the decrease of annual precipitation and bad distribution of rainfalls, as well as the degradation in the biomass of grazing areas. Hence, there is urgent demand for immediate response via agricultural/land rehabilitation, access to water for agriculture, provision of inputs for production, livestock shelter and management tools, irrigation networks, dairy processing tools, and access to more sources of water and markets in order to enable farmers to access quality products and essential resources to sustain their livelihoods. Moreover, an in-depth field assessment update, jointly conducted by WW-GVC and ARIJ in September 2021, further identified urgent needs of food security protection and livelihood sustainability at HH and community level. This assessment, along with instrumental coordination with the MoA and its directorates in the target Governorates, confirmed the gap in food security and safe access to necessary resources to sustain agricultural livelihoods in, at least, 39 priority communities, 86.2% of which are located in Area C, across 7 Governorates: Bethlehem (10 communities), Hebron (7 communities), Jerusalem (11 communities), Jericho (3 communities), Ramallah (5 communities), Salfit (1 community), Tulkarem (2 communities). Among these communities, 922 vulnerable farmer HHs consisting of 4728 individuals (1443 men, 1359 women, 884 boys, 880 girls, 106 Elderly of which 56 PWD) were found in need of immediate assistance to increase and sustain their food security and livelihood. Furthermore, the selected communities and interventions matching the Directorate’s and MoA’s developmental and response plans were discussed and approved by the agricultural directorates within each targeted governorate. The aim is to enhance the resilience of Palestinian farmers and developing the agriculture production of the selected communities. The proposed activities reflect the geographical priorities identified within each Objective of the FSS and strategic developmental plans of the MoA. Hence, WW-GVC and ARIJ identified 3 main priority needs: 1. Access to natural resources disrupted by the coercive environment and movement restrictions resulting from the military occupation of the WB; 2. Support to agricultural/farming disrupted livelihoods due to the protracted crisis, intensification of demolitions and confiscation of agricultural structures and resources, climate change and Covid19; 3. Need of coordination with local and national institutions and duty-bearers to better respond to the food security and livelihood needs of vulnerable Palestinians as right-holders.

Also, the local authorities and MoA will be involved in witnessing the signed agreements. Also, the beneficiaries will sign on a commitment agreement to keep, protect and use the provided inputs and improved resources.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

All feedback and complaints coming from beneficiaries will be given high consideration. Beneficiaries are informed that the complaints are to be kept confidential specifically for sensitive issues (such as GBV and SEA), and they will be dealt with accordingly. ARIJ and WW-GVC will jointly scrutiny the content of the complaints and/or feedback and if necessary will further investigate the matter to assess whether an adjustment to the programming or disciplinary actions are needed. Also, the two partners commit to address the complaints and feedback within a reasonable timeframe.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

b - Project hotline,c - Complaint box,d - Satisfaction survey,e - Field visit,f - Other

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

ARIJ and WW-GVC will arrange a dedicated hotline for complaints coming from beneficiaries and their communities throughout all the duration and up to 6 months after the end of the project (as such mechanism is being implemented by both organizations as part of the responsibilities of their M&E departments). This will complement WW-GVC’s Complains Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability and commitment to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneficiaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions and inquiries in a secured way, and receive a response in a timely manner. All complaints/feedback are reported to the WW-GVC’s and ARIJ’s Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff, project managers and/or focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to it sensitivity. If the complaint requires follow up, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call by WW-GVC’s and ARIJ Monitoring and Coordination manager. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the Admin officer analyses complaints and classifies each of them according to the internal classification system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by WW-GVC and ARIJ team and/or CRM team.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Every member of the field staff is obliged to inform and explain all direct and indirect beneficiaries of the presence of a complaint-response mechanism (CRM) and the referral pathway for their complaints, as well as provide the contact information/hotline for the CRM focal points depending on the type of the complaint (female focal point for GBV and PSEA; a male focal point for other types of complaints). Also, the beneficiary agreements will contain special part about the complaint mechanisms including the dedicated number for this subject. The complaint mechanism will be both in-person, at any time during the field visits of the staff of the two organizations, or remotely through the dedicated hotline. The latter will ensure an anonymous and discrete means to file in a complaint, ensuring that all population groups may access the mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Response is carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risk. Through the Community Protection Approach all community members will be involved in all the project phases. A thorough assessment will be conducted with each community to further investigate and understand the needs, risks and vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the rehabilitation works are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PWD. Additionally, livelihood support and land/road rehabilitation intervention will be designed to comply with protection principles, such as meaningful access and accountability towards beneficiaries, taking into account the diverse needs, roles and dynamics of women and men, age and disability status, in each community as well as PSEA SoPs. Furthermore, when possible, household members will work in the rehabilitation work, so the sense of dignity and ownership will also be improved. The field team participated in the protection cluster GBV training for improving their capacities in detecting and referring cases. Moreover, the field team also participated in a training with War Child to better detect and refer children violence. WWGVC is now mainstreaming the AGD and PWD toolkits in the community based protection methodology. During COVID-19 outbreaks protection measures (wearing face masks, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WWGVC and ARIJ teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Community Based Protection Approach (CPA) is a participatory methodology based on WW-GVC operational interpretation of the IASC definition of protection. An environment fully conducive to protection is obtained when multi-sector humanitarian and development needs of individuals and communities are met in a dignified and safe manner. As such, multi-sector needs are identified and ranked on the basis of the contextual protection risks of communities and individuals. A right-based analysis is used as entry point to elaborate local protection strategies to be then used as a programming framework for the definition of needs-based responses. The CPA was developed according to IHL, Humanitarian, Do No Harm and the following fundamental principles: 1. the safety of affected population (both of groups and individuals); 2. Restore the dignity of affected population putting a stop to emerging or established patterns of violations (and/or alleviating their immediate effects); 3. place the empowerment of communities at risk at the core of any action to enable self-protection and realize their rights. The use of focus groups, random interviews and transect walks, ensures the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, a specific situation, a particular household. Throughout the assessment and planning phases, the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed food security and livelihood interventions address the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each member of the community, and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, preserves the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowerment. The distribution of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) cards in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedbacks and complaints about the implemented project.
