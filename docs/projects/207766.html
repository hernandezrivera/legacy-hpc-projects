Project Summary

This project targets vulnerable displacement affected households in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon. Faced with urgent shelter-NFI and WASH related needs. SIRDEP will target women, girls, boys, persons with disability and the elderly as the main beneficiaries of this project. We will reach these target groups through focus groups discussions. The Focus group discussions will be organised based on gender and age. Community engagement meetings will be held with the various target groups to discuss the project objectives, deliverables, roles and responsibilities and selection criteria. Protection measures will be taken into consideration.
The ultimate outcome is to reduce suffering, increase human dignity and save lives in communities experiencing humanitarian crisis and communities affected by natural disasters. This will be achieved through access to adequate shelter and basic household items.
The twelve-month project will provide emergency relief, and recovery assistance to affected persons (IDPs, Returnees, Hosts) with a focus on girls, boys, women and persons with disability. protection will be mainstreamed throughout the implementation of the project activities.
Proposed Response:
The proposed response will contribute to improving the living conditions, dignity and protection of vulnerable crisis affected persons in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon with shelter- NFI assistance. Implementation of the activities will be done through a mixed modality of cash and in-kind distributions. SIRDEP will engage in core lifesaving activities – emergency and transitional shelter, distribution of non-food items, to address needs resulting from conflict and natural disasters (new and protracted) Shelter assistance can help reduce this risk by increasing available shelter and housing options for those who are vulnerable. Where people reside in a congested setting, the SIRDEP will expand its transitional shelter support by providing cash for rent assistance, light repairs, medium repairs to the houses to improve on the living conditions. Transitional shelter options are a good stepping stone for people’s journey towards self-reliance. The recipients of light and medium shelter repairs will be trained on building skills and build back safer options. This is particularly important because the shelter cluster highlighted the need for labour force in the rural communities since most of the builders had left to bigger towns due to the conflict. Shelter committees will be set up and equipped with materials from where such trainings can be initiated. During delivery of all Shelter assistance, cluster partners will ensure environmental mainstreaming.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The humanitarian situation in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon continuous to deteriorate, with increased displacement of civilians, burning of houses and destruction of property, recurrent floods and landslides as was the case of big Babanki (OCHA displacement incident report 10-18/06/2023), Egbekaw (ETT 217 SW 2023) and the floods in Buea and Limbe. This has rendered many families homeless and has forced many to flee their homes to find safety in other neighbouring communities, bushes, farmhouses considered to be relatively safer. According to the September 2023 MSNA, 486828 persons need shelter and NFI assistance in the Northwest and Southwest regions. According to the September 2023 MSNA, the Divisions of Donga-Mantung, Menchum, Bui and Ndian are the most affected, with a severity level 4 of inadequate severities. The MSNA reveals harsh living conditions for those displaced population, with 19% of the population living in timber houses, 56% in mudbrick houses and a smaller number living in makeshift shelters. 25% of the displaced population are living in damaged shelters due to the conflict with a major damages done to the roof (42%) and walls (28%).19% of the displaced population lack adequate space (less than 3.5 meter square per person) this is particularly true in urban centres where IDP families rent houses with overcrowded rooms. Some other important NFI needs includes access to cooking utensils and sleeping materials. The number of returnees is increasing as the crisis has grown more protracted, and the IDPs are finding it difficult meeting up with the high cost of living in urban canters. People are returning to the rural areas where they have engaged in doing some repairs however often they lack the financial and material resources and, while waiting for their homes to be repaired, they stay with host families, mostly in overcrowded conditions.The Shelter Cluster has identified Donga Mantung, Bui, Menchum in the North-West and Ndian, Lebialem in the South-West as the divisions with severe shelter inadequacies (severity level 4), where some IDPs are living in makeshift shelters in bush settlements, seeking protection from the threats of hostilities and conscription by armed groups. Not being able to find materials for shelters, these IDPs also settle in agricultural infrastructures or cocoa ovens to have a simple roof over their heads. In host families, and rented shelters, overcrowding and lack of privacy increase the risk of gender-based violence. Furthermore, 20% of the people sleep on the ground at nights and some family members are forced to sleep outside at night, exposing themselves to the risk of malaria and other diseases. In terms of common tenure types 22 per cent of households experiencing severe inadequacies are renting with a formal agreement. Over 47 per cent of these households pay over 40 per cent of their income to rent their current shelter. About 22per cent of the households found to live severe inadequacies are hosted for free by a host family. These families are lacking essential household items and must rely on alternative sources of energy for lighting and cooking. 47% per cent of households in severity 4 is cooking inside their homes using wood as they lack the space to cook outside or do not feel safe enough there. This will eventually pose a health challenge with the risk of upper respiratory tract complications due to the smoke.In the North-West and Southwest regions, 19 per cent of displaced households and 14 percent of the returnee population interviewed lack adequate living space (3.5 meters square per person). The shortage of essential household items is also a critical driver of inadequacy. In addition to overcrowding, Shelter Cluster partners have found that larger families are sometimes forced to share mosquito nets and mattresses and often there are not enough sleeping places.
