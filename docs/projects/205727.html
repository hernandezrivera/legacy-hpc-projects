Project Summary

Through the proposed response, SCI will provide integrated lifesaving nutrition service to children and women in Akobo East &West, Bor south, Kapoeta North Torit Counties, and Abyei administrative area. The aim of this is to reduce suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable children and women, SCI's proposed response will focus on both treatment and preventative approaches, through CMAM) and MIYCN approaches. SCI Emergency nutrition programs will include. I) Community mobilization to increase access and coverage of CMAM services, ii) Treatment of children and PLW with acute malnutrition through inpatient and Outpatient therapeutic programs. iii) Blanket Supplementary feeding program. iv) Institutional feeding program (IFP), v) Maternal dietary supplementation, vi) Promotion of optimal IYCF through mother-to-mother support groups. vii) Improving Dietary Diversity through household kitchen gardening and chicken rearing piloted in select locations, viii) Strengthen nutrition information systems and operations research to ensure an evidence-based response, ix) Building capacity for frontline nutrition workers and institutions at all levels; in all nutrition sites the SMOH will deploy Nutrition promoters who will be incentivized by SCI and as well some nutrition sites in select counties will be fully run by SMOH with support from SCI in effort to champion the transition agenda. SCI will work through communities to promote, protect and support recommended IYCF practices through one-on-one counselling and group message and counselling. Besides the CMAM and behavior change interventions. SCI will support 75 static OTP/TSFPs, 8 BSFP and 6 stabilization centers integrated within health facilities. The service will be delivered through static and outreach/mobile facilities for remote and hard-to-reach areas in the targeted project locations. SCI will work with MOH, UNICEF and WFP to secure timely provision of Nutrition supplies necessary for Nutrition Service Delivery. SCI targets a total of 258, 453 under 5 and PLWs with SAM, MAM, SC, MIYCN and VASD services. SCI will make deliberate efforts to enhance coordination and integration with the Health, WASH, FSL, Protection and Education clusters to address the drivers of malnutrition through a multi-sectoral approach that aims to reduce vulnerabilities, prevent malnutrition and build resilience among the vulnerable people. Specific multi-sectoral activities are to increase access to safe water and sanitation, promote critical WASH messages, and integrate early childhood development strategies in nutrition treatment sites. Kitchen gardening, chicken rearing pilot, screening and treating of malaria, managing diarrhea, and mainstreaming child protection and gender-based violence risk mitigation will also be promoted at the nutrition sites. SCI will also support hygiene interventions to improve nutrition outcomes and reduce risk of hygiene-related morbidities. SCI will monitor the nutrition situation and the needs of the affected people through KAP and SMART surveys, monthly nutrition program data collection and analysis, and joint monitoring visits. Spot checks, supportive supervision and monitoring visits will enhance service delivery and engage with the community and other stakeholders to gauge evolving needs. CNVs will be supported to undertake household visits, conduct regular screening of malnourished children and women to ensure timely detection and treatment, and report on a monthly basis. Data collection tools and analysis will be strengthened to enhance monitoring of integrated services. Accountability to affected populations will be enhanced via community awareness mechanisms, enhancing community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by establishing a well-attended private complain and feedback desk within the TSFP / OTP sites as well as conducting community focus group discussions with members of the community.

1. Needs

Children and women in South Sudan continue to suffer from unacceptably high levels of malnutrition. According to the preliminary 2023 FSNMS and SMART surveys' findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.5 million children and women at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024 and will need urgent treatment. The key drivers of malnutrition include high food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions, high child morbidity rates related to diarrhea, malaria and upper respiratory infections, gender-based violence concerns, and poor child feeding and caring practices with only 4% children getting the minimum acceptable diet for them to grow and develop well. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 percent was reported in 46 out of 79 counties; this included all the 3 states where SCI is currently implementing nutrition interventions (Jonglei, Warrap, and Eastern Equatoria) and planning for 2024 HRP. In terms of high presence of aggravating factors for malnutrition, the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster County priority map 2024 rates Abyei as priority 1, Bor South, Kapoeta North and Akobo as priority 2 while Torit as priority 3. According to the most recent published IPC (2022 – 2023) results, a Very High concern (>15%) of malnutrition rate continued to be reported in SCI project locations. Although Abyei was not included in the 2022 FSNMS+, Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screenings conducted by Save the Children (SC) in March 2022, indicated an elevated GAM and SAM prevalence of 18.6% and 2.4% respectively, with an observed deterioration of the nutrition situation during the subsequent months, including sub-optimal Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) care practices. In Eastern Equatoria, both Kapoeta North and Torit Counties have been drastically affected by long drought which negatively impacted most of the produces and resulted in serious Hunger crisis. This impact is anticipated to worsen in 2024 as the counties are projected to face IPC 3+ levels of food insecurity. From December 2022 to July 2023, Kapoeta North is in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) levels. Mostly, children, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in both counties are at high risk of acute malnutrition, with Torit being in IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 3 (Serious), WFP piloted BSFP as a preventive strategy to address the high prevalence of malnutrition. Jonglei state, which includes Akobo East and West, and Bor South Counties, is among the worst counties that are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity as classified by 2023 IPC report. Jonglei takes 68% of the national malnutrition burden. Akobo County (both eastern and western parts) is classified as Phase 4 (Critical) for Acute Malnutrition and Phase 4 (Emergency) for Food Insecurity, whereas Bor South continues to remain classified as Phase 3 for both food and nutrition insecurity. Findings of the most recent (2023) SMART survey in Akobo East County indicated GAM prevalence of 27.9% (23.5-32.8 95% CI) which is considered very high whereas SAM prevalence was 5.6% (3.7- 8.3 95% CI). Despite the improved quality of nutrition care and robust coverage of CMAM services, a very high concern of nutritional situation is attributed to food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions, and hygiene practices, hence the need to focus on context-based multi-sectoral nutrition programming and response is paramount to have quicker and more significant improvements on nutrition outcomes and impact.

2. Project activities

Through the proposed response, SCI will implement the below emergency nutrition activities covering nutrition specific interventions, nutrition-sensitive interventions and strengthening the enabling environment for nutrition in line with the cluster objectives. Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women. SCI will conduct the following activities: -I. Provide training for M2MSGs on IYCF promotion, hygiene and Child Protection II. Provide refresher training for Save the Children, health facility workers and volunteers on IYCF-E III. Training of MIYCN-counselors on counseling skills and behavior change communication (BCC) approaches.IV. Establish and Strengthen mother support groups to deliver key MIYCN messages at nutrition centers and community level. V. conduct weekly education sessions on MIYCN in the community through M2MSGsVI. Provide MIYCN group counselling at OTP/TSFP site level VII. Strengthen/Establish mother to child baby friendly spaces and breastfeeding corners in all OTP/TSFP sites. VIII. Train M2MSG on mother led MUAC approach and food cooking demonstrations. Objective 2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW, SCI will conduct the below activities: - I. Conduct regular and systematic community based MUAC/edema screening. ii. Establish two-way referral mechanisms between community and OTPs, OTPs and stabilization centers, OTPs and TSFPs in collaboration with TSFP partners and CHD. iii. Equip volunteers with screening and referral materials (MUAC tapes, referral sheets, forms, etc.) iv. Provide on-going support to OTP sites, stabilization center site and mobile teams, including supportive supervision, on-the-job training, technical advice. v. provide treatment to boys and girls aged 6-59 months, and pregnant and lactating women with moderate acute malnutrition without medical complication in TSFP sites.vi. Provide treatment to boys and girls aged 6-59 months, with severe acute malnutrition without medical complication in OTP sites. vii. Provide therapeutic and medical treatment to boys and girls aged 6-59 months with SAM with medical complications in the stabilization centers. viii. Support early pre-positioning of specialized nutrition products for the treatment of SAM and MAM to project sites,ix. Screen all children identified with severe acute malnutrition for malaria and treat those found positive with malaria. x. Provide refresher CMAM training to SCI nutrition staff and community nutrition volunteers using the national guidelineObjective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information, SCI will conduct the following activities: -I. Conduct anthropometric and mortality SMART surveys (lean season & post- harvest survey):ii. Support in the data collection of Food security and Nutrition monitoring system (FSNMS)Objective 4. To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations, SCI will implement the following activities: -I. Establish hand-washing facilities, latrines and clean drinking water for appetite test at Nutrition sites. ii. Disseminate integrated WASH/Health/Nutrition key messages at nutrition site and community level. iii. Establish kitchen gardening at nutrition facility and community contact points to improve the dietary diversity to diversify and improve their feeding habits. iv. Conduct safety audits for all nutrition sites. v. Establish community complain and feedback mechanisms in all Nutrition facilities by setting up private complain desks.
