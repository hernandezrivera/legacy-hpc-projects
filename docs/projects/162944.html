1 - Needs

The Jebel Marra massive straddles Sudan’s North, Central and South Darfur states. As such this area is still subject to significant civilian displacement, not only as a result of the fighting between the SAF and armed group of SLA AW, but also due to environmental factors. Despite the overall reduction of violence in Darfur and SK, there are still major concerns about intercommunal violence and general insecurity; as the situation remains volatile, more displacement is certain. This disproportionately affects women and children and restricts their access to basic services right to participation, development and their ability to realize their potential. In conflict contexts, women and girls bear the brunt of the violence and brutality as rape, sexual abuse, exploitation and other gender-based violence is common. The forms of violence and abuse encountered by minors include child marriage, child labor, child recruitment by armed groups, unaccompanied and separated children, female genital mutilation, lack of birth certificates and child trafficking. Jabel Marra have only became accessible to humanitarian workers as of 2017, and its population are therefore severely underserved. According to UPO assessment in (March 2019) in the localities of Jebel Marra area, ND, SK and South Darfur, there are significant numbers (PSN) in need of protection services. The assessment revealed that very little aid has been directed to the IDPs, most of the support coming from the hosting communities. The needs include provision of shelter, PSS support for both adults and children, health services, especially to pregnant women, mitigating measures to reduce prevailing threats to women and adolescent girls they likely to encounter on their way to water collection points, fire wood collection and local markets. There is no active CBPN and the existing traditional community platforms are weak. There is an urgent need to respond to these identified needs as well as to continue the registration and family reunification of unaccompanied/separated (UASC) and missing children, and to reinforce and establish CFS, to prevent violence, exploitation, abuse and other protection risks. GBV continues to be a life threatening concern for the newly displaced Jebel Marra and SK, women and girls continue to face risk of different types of the GBV, insecurity, economic challenges and lack of livelihood opportunities, as well as a lack of community awareness on women rights . GBV intervention is hampered by lack of accessibility to conflict affected areas, as well as the cultural acceptability of the issue, which affects the ability to provide a timely response to the needs of vulnerable women and survivors. There is also lack of access to social services and legal redress in the worst impacted areas, which means that the most vulnerable often do not receive the necessary support services due to cash liquidity and funding issues.

2 - Activities / Outputs

1. Conduct TOT for 3 Protection Officers, 3 Child Protection Officers and humanitarian social workers (one per locality) at a state level on coordination, protection and gender mainstreaming, PSS standards, referral and case management including how to conduct follow-up/beneficiary satisfaction feedback; 2. Provide refresher training for 300 CBPN/CBCPN facilitators to identify protection risks/incidents, provide adequate response and develop prevention strategy;3. Support 15 Community-Based Protection Networks to perform referral functions and provide psychosocial support to vulnerable groups or individuals; 4. Support 9 Community Based Protection Networks to identify Persons with Specific Need (PSN) and track protection concerns faced by IDPs in the target localities aiming to reach 7,000 vulnerable individuals; including 3,000 women, 1,000 men 1,000 boys and 2,000 girls;5. Support 6 Community Based Child Protection Networks through training, building their capacity (local activists, community leaders, PTAs) aiming to reach 4000 most vulnerable children; 6. Develop and coordinate referral pathways at village level and conduct beneficiary satisfaction feedback on the use of the referral systems; 7. Provide 2300 girls, 2200 boys, 4050 women and 2700 men individual or collective protection services and counseling;8. Conduct monthly home visits of identified PSN targeting 600 beneficiaries; 9. Support 600 identified extremely vulnerable and persons with special needs with the livelihood starting kits; 10. Establish 6 community centres including (3 youth and 3 woman and child) to provide collective and individual PSS, emergency kits and conduct bi-monthly IGA workshops; 11. Train health workers to provide psychosocial support services to malnourished children at risk and their caregivers; 12. Train 10 health workers on identification, referral and provision of PSS to malnourished children at risk and their caregivers; 13. Conduct protection monitoring in coordination with concerned agencies, share findings and recommendations.
