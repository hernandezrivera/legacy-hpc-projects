Project Summary

The objective of the project is to increase resilience and reduce vulnerability of the conflict-affected boys, girls and their families, caregivers, males and females, through lifesaving direct support/assistance, MHPSS activities, informal education and recreational activities by strengthening protective environment and improving access to child protection services. Save the Children has several years of filed work experience and has good presence and acceptance on the ground. Most staff of the case management unit are also engaged with the state social services in Druzhkivka. Their capacity is built by SCI in line with international best practice on case management and therefore this knowledge will remain in the state social service. The unit in Druzhkivka is the municipal centre of social services. Save the Children also makes effort to involve state social workers in all relevant trainings and working closely with the CM unit.
Special attention will be given to protective measures against COVID 19, namely sharing all the relevant and life-saving information on the disease, transmission modes, and how to mitigate other respective risks, providing printing materials, supporting with personal protective equipment, disinfecting materials, etc.

1 - Needs

Protection needs throughout 2020 remained serious and were exacerbated by the Covid-19 pandemic and accompanying quarantine restrictions. More than 500,000 boys and girls living along the 0-20 km-long contact line continue to be affected by conflict, with direct threats to their physical integrity and mental wellbeing. The majority of those children are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection (REACH and SCI assessments show that children are facing protection risks such as shelling, mines/ERW, all forms of violence (including physical, emotional, sexual), neglect, psychological distress, lack of documentation, absence of safe places to play, de-sensitization to violence). In addition, the repeated shelling of critical infrastructure, including schools is threatening access to education and other social services. Meanwhile, weakened family and community support mechanisms, daily stressors and loss of hope for the future continue to cause distress for children and their caregivers. According to the reports by child protection sub-cluster partners, caregivers highly affected by stress and hardships often lack positive parenting skills, thus, failing to ensure the nurturing and caring environment for their children. Furthermore, ongoing hostilities and economic hardships led to increased family – and GBV and neglect of children. While protection of children’s rights, responding to GBV and family violence are parts of the national policies and strategies, currently limited services exist for child witnesses or survivors of violence, including in the conflict-affected areas. Besides, existing community services are often understaffed and cannot effectively respond to the problems of children and their families.Therefore, affected children and families urgently require ongoing psychosocial support and strengthening of the protection and social services. SCI’s participatory gender assessment (2020) shows that gender norms and inequality exacerbate different risks for girls and boys. The exposure to violence and military presence is also reflected in boys’ behavior and they are at greater risk of injury from explosive remnants of war (ERW), whereas girls are at greater risk of some forms of gender-based violence, specifically sexual harassment, assault and exploitation. However, in contexts with limited income opportunities the perceived wealth of military personnel can be triggering. One older adolescent boy mentioned helping the military in his spare time to earn money and it was reported by organisations working in the NGCA that sometimes girls go to military bases to cook and clean. Both caregivers and staff from humanitarian agencies reported that older girls sometimes engaged in transactional sex with military personnel. In both GCA and NGCA, the increase in patriotic education and militarization of extracurricular activities was considered to be a factor that may increase children’s interest in joining the armed forces.” Existing gender norms in GCA and NGCA also influence children’s ability to seek support, and the extent to which they benefit from the help on offer. For example, boys are reluctant to show vulnerability, fear or to cry as they are seen as feminine behaviors; this may make it harder for them to access appropriate psychosocial support. UNICEF’s report shows that conflict-affected adolescent girls are more likely to internalize problems related to the conflict (anxiety, depression, self-harm, suicidality) than adolescent boys who will externalize problems (delinquency, substance abuse, bullying, risky sexual behaviors, aggression). CP systems, including referral mechanisms and case management, need further strengthening, as very limited numbers of social workers are available to manage child protection cases. Information gaps between referral services hamper adequate response. SCI will continue advocating against VAC in the home, in the community, online, etc. on the national and local level.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Output 1. Delivering immediate, life-saving humanitarian assistance along the contact line of GCA and NGCA Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. Boys and girls of different age groups, are provided with psychosocial services through structured activities at community centres and through mobile units in education institutions. 1) Strengthen existing community centres with mobile units along the contact line. Identify locations and establish new community centres. 2) Strengthen existing Case Management (CMU) units of oblast level and establish new CMUs of regional level to provide individual support for the most vulnerable categories of children. 3) Continue with a comprehensive program of psychosocial support including SCI signature programs HEART, Life Skills and Resiliency for children aged 4-18 y.o. 4) Conduct child protection and gender sensitivity public awareness campaigns. 5) Reinforcing the existing referral and case management systems at the local level through capacity building of duty bearers and protection actors with strong linkages to social services in GCA and NGCA. 6) Provide mine risk education sessions in schools and kindergartens along the contact line (dedicated Unexploaded Ordnance Education sessions). Output 2. Parents and caregivers, both males and females of conflict affected vulnerable boys and girls, are better equipped to care for, protect children in distress, need of psychosocial support. 1) Implementation of Parenting without Violence Common Approach to facilitate the positive parenting skills and improve the protection of children from violence in the home and community to. 2) Implementation of SCI signature programs like Life Skills and Resilience and Healing and Education Through the Arts (HEART) for children, parents and caregivers. The activities are planned to meet the urgent need of psycho-social support for youth and their families. 3) Hold community meetings and events to mobilize local communities and resources, make families more united and supportive; inform about existing local mechanisms, available services or referral pathways. Output 3. Local professionals, staff of centres of social services, pre/ school institutions, public libraries, culture institutions, volunteers, local authorities’ representatives, local NGOs representatives both male and female are trained on how to provide better support to boys and girls including identifying signs of distress and providing support personally or through referrals, case management approach, strengthening referral system and coordination at all levels, regular updating of service mapping. 1) Hold learning events on Humanitarian principles, CPMS and CPiE, MHPSS recommendations, PFA, Case Management approach, Child Participation, Parenting without Violence, Gender and Inclusion, GBV prevention, referrals, and availability of life-saving services 2) Implement Life skills and Resiliency for children and youth HEART programs through community centres and mobile units to meet PSS needs of children and their parents/ caregivers. Output 4. Awareness raising on prevention and risks of COVID-19 outbreak, as well as support to children and parents/caregivers during quarantine. 1) Awareness raising sessions for children and parents/caregivers who participate in activities in community centres and mobile locations to equip them with all the relevant and life-saving information on the disease, transmission modes, and how to mitigate other respective risks. 2) Providing support to parents and caregivers in how to parent positively under pressure of the quarantine.
