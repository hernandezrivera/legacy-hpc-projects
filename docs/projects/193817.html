Project Summary

This intervention aims to support 400 households (HH) living in severely or partially damaged/substandard/overcrowded shelters and HHs at risk of exposure to harsh weather and protection concerns across the Middle Area, Rafah and Khan Yunis governorates of Gaza in order to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity. It aims at alleviating the impact of the August 2022 and May 2021 escalations, and the overall protracted conflict affecting the Shelter sector in Gaza. Through unconditional restricted cash assistance and technical assistance/monitoring, ACTED and its implementing partners Unlimited Friends Association (UFA) and Fares Al-Arab For Development (FAFD) will support the self-repair and rehabilitation of the selected HHs’ shelters, the purchasing of NFIs, to ensure compliance with SPHERE and Shelter Cluster minimum standards to alleviate unmet needs that expose the 400 HH to protection risks.
The project is informed by assessments ACTED undertook under projects in 2021 and 2022, 2022 MSNA, consultations with stakeholders, and recommendations from the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), the Ministry of Public Work and Housing (MoPWH), the Shelter & NFI Cluster and Cash Working Group (CWG) in Gaza. This intervention is fully aligned with 2023 HNO priorities and supports the achievement of 2023 HRP for the Shelter Cluster, by addressing the priority humanitarian needs, namely: “Vulnerable households living in substandard shelter exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”. ACTED and its local partners UFA and FAFD will implement a multi-pronged approach to increase access of vulnerable HHs to secure shelters through the following activities:
-Conducting assessments and verifications of HHs nominated as vulnerable by MoPHW and MoSD and through ACTED CRM’s referred and waiting lists, defined based on the same criteria, in targeted governorates to select 400 vulnerable HHs as final BNFs.
-Facilitating shelter repair and adaptation works through unconditional restricted cash assistance and technical assistance/monitoring to ensure compliance with SPHERE and Shelter Cluster minimum standards and to alleviate unmet needs that expose 400 HH in severely or partially damaged/sub-standard shelters to protection risks.
-Strengthening 3 existing Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) through the provision of trainings, plus on the job coaching to mainstream protection principles within their communities and ensure community engagement.
Targeted communities will indirectly benefit from the intervention by the enhanced protection delivered by the CBPCs. The intervention will directly benefit 400 BNF HH (2,240 individuals) through the cash assistance to support shelter self-repair/rehabilitation works and purchase of needed NFIs. Additionally, 3 CBPCs (18 members) will benefit from trainings (refresher training, new topics) to support their communities and ensure mainstreaming of protection principles including key PSEA messages. Therefore, 2,258 direct BNFS in total will benefit from this project. The project is supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with a specific focus on most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, PwDs and older people. As sometimes PwD are prevented from expressing their needs, the project team will conduct HH-level visits to assess their specific needs and explain the purpose of the project. PwDs and their caregivers will be consulted throughout the project to better understand their safety and dignity concerns. Gender principles will also be mainstreamed and CBPCs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV. Finally, Accountability to Affected People will be core to the implementation of the proposed action.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip’s economy is at breaking point. Subject to recurrent economic shocks due to the protracted conflict, the August 2022 escalation, the May 2021 escalation, the limited ability of international actors to provide assistance, and the blockade on land, air and sea since 2007, economic activity has greatly diminished, leading to impoverishment and de-development of the population. Gaza is also one of the most densely-populated areas in the world: 2.11 million Palestinians are cramped in 365 km2 (Gaza’s total land area), where population density is 5,800/km2 (OCHA). It suffers from a chronic shortage in housing that was expected to exceed 120,000 units in 2022, with an annual average of 14,000 additional housing units needed to meet natural growth. Based on the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), conducted by REACH and OCHA in 2022, 59.8% of HHs reported existing shelter damage, defects, or issues in their shelters; 21,500 existing housing units across Gaza which are classified as substandard and do not meet the minimum requirements of living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities, and privacy, require different degrees of rehabilitation. It must be noted that the housing crisis in Gaza is also driven by over 15 years of Israeli restrictions on the import of building materials and equipment, the deteriorating socio-economic situation, and the recurring waves of hostilities According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 663,000 persons across oPt are in need of humanitarian assistance to access adequate shelter (including basic households supplies), indispensable to providing people with a minimum level of privacy, protection and dignity.According to the Shelter cluster updates, in the recent escalation of August 2022, 1,500 housing units were damaged or destroyed, of which 97 housing units had moderate to severe damages and 26 housing units were destroyed, and rendered uninhabitable, leaving 600 people displaced. 1,034 out of the partially damaged housing units have not received any repair assistance yet, leaving thousands of HHs unprotected and living in critical shelter conditions. The 2022 MSNA particularly emphasized the need to target HHs with a member with disability, as 6% of PwD do not have safe access to the housing unit facilities. In fact, HHs assessed to have a member with a disability were more likely to report difficulties to meeting their basic needs and having damaged, defects, or issues to their shelter. Based on these assessed needs, ACTED, UFA and FAFD will prioritise HHs with a member with disability in their beneficiary selection as these HHs have reported higher needs in shelter rehabilitation. In addition, 72% of the female-headed HHs are experiencing stress, severe or extreme need of assistance in the Gaza strip, including 452 individuals in extreme need of assistance. As such, this project will also prioritise female-headed HHs in the beneficiary selection. In addition, ACTED conducted three assessments between March and October 2022 in the Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah governorates of the Gaza Strip, which have informed the design of this project. The assessments targeted 425 HHs living in substandard shelters, escalation affected shelters, and shelters in need of winterization. All HHs visited were not complying with at least one of the minimum shelter standards. 53% were living in asbestos/corrugated metal shelters, or had a roof of either, and 33 % did not have access to at least one room insulated against heat and cold. Vulnerable groups (including children, PwDs and older people) which made up more than 53% of the visited individuals, were particularly affected by the impact of the recent hostilities and harsh winter conditions. Lack of adequate shelter protection increases their vulnerability, and health risks in particular for children and older people household’s members who are more vulnerable to harsh weather conditions. 81% of assessed HHs had window units that were not all sealed from rain, water or wind. Additionally, 72% had a housing unit roof that leaked rainwater into their house. Some vulnerable groups feel that they are more vulnerable to protection concerns when living in overcrowded shelter and need specific intervention to respect their dignity and safety. For instance, 52% of assessed HHs had no solid and lockable door/s in their housing unit. Therefore, 64% of women and 57% of children felt that their house was not securing their safety and dignity. Regarding NFIs, 78% of assessed HHs indicated that the NFIs they possess are inadequate or in poor conditions. FAFD had also conducted an assessment with 31 HHs (about 265 individuals), which revealed that 98% of the vulnerable families surveyed lived in very fragile conditions and could not face harsh weather conditions and climate fluctuations during the summer and winter seasons. These HHs should be supported through NFIs distribution to meet their needs for winterization/summarization assistance. Finally, the HNO 2023 also emphasized that for an effective, efficient and timely response to unexpected and frequent escalations as well as unforeseen emergencies, it is necessary to increase the partners’ capacities to respond to the emergent needs, notably through community capacity building. The proposed action thus aims at addressing part of the Shelter priorities for the HRP 2023. It must also be noted that all project staff are trained and will participate to refresher on PSEA and aware of PSEA focal points in each organisation along with reporting channels. All organisations are either members of the PSEA network or planning to join it.

2 - Activities

A1: Assessment and verification of HHs nominated. ACTED, UFA and FAFD will conduct assessments and verification of 500 HH nominated as vulnerable by MoPHW and MoSD in the targeted governorates, to select 400 BNF HH. During this exercise, ACTED will raise awareness on its Complaint Response Mechanisms (CRM) and key PSEA messages, through the dissemination of PSEA leaflets. Using obtained data, ACTED, UFA, FAFD and CBPCs will score assessed HHs according to vulnerability, with particular emphasis on female-headed HH, large-size HH, PwDs, and older people, and factors like basic NFI needs and exposure to harsh weather. This will allow ACTED to update its waiting list of vulnerable HHs identified through conducted assessments. ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process. Following BNF list validation by MoSD, MoPHW, and other key actors to avoid duplication, UFA and FAFD engineers will develop tailored Bills of Quantities (BoQs) and workplan for each HH. At least 1 male and 1 female per HH will be consulted by the project team, to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are considered, plus all marginalized persons, to reflect their unique vulnerabilities. This approach will ensure BNF involvement in all decisions regarding the rehabilitation, complemented with technical support. A2: Cash for shelter repair and purchase of NFIs for 400 HH living in damaged and substandard shelters. ACTED will provide each HH with cash assistance of 8,070 ILS, based on the needs to repair severely or partially damaged houses/ shelters, thus, upgrade shelters to consider vulnerable groups’ specific needs, and respond to protection concerns. This will allow the upgrade of overcrowded shelters, address protection concerns, in order to improve HH living conditions, ensure safety and privacy and meet shelter minimum standards. While the precise nature of the works will be determined based on the BoQs and workplan defined under A1, the focus and priority will be on the following: unsealed window unit, external walls with cracks or holes or not closed with cement material, housing unit roof which are not sealed or leaks. Each HH will sign an agreement, including the tailored BoQs. ACTED will provide cash assistance through 3 instalments (40%, 40%, 20%), that will be released through Palpay, a money transfer agent, with which ACTED has signed a framework agreement. During project implementation, ACTED could reduce the number of installments, depending on HHs specific needs. Particular attention will be given to PwD and female-headed HHs with young children with special needs. To ensure aid accessibility, the project team will have the possibility to opt for a cash-in-hand modality to address certain HHs’ specific needs. Moreover, ACTED will distribute a technical manual to each HH to enhance their autonomy during the implementation of repair works and to ensure safety. The GA will include a leaflet on environmental awareness designed by ACTED, in collaboration with UFA. Both instalments will be followed by monitoring field visits to ensure compliance with SPHERE and Shelter Cluster minimum standards and BNF’s satisfaction, considering the possibility to modify the BoQ to comply with HH’s evolving needs, within the cash ceiling. Based on follow-up, ACTED will decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. Through close follow-up, ACTED will identify technical issues and solve them through complementary training and guidance. BNF will advance 20% of rehabilitation costs and the 3rd instalment will be a reimbursement granted if all works are completed. In the event BNF would not be able to advance 20%, the project team could decide to provide all the cash in only 2 instalments (40%, 60%). Committees will support HHs unable to perform repair works themselves by nominating community-members to perform works and purchase required materials for them. HHs with specific needs will also have the possibility to request support from a contractor hired by ACTED. Additionally, based on assessed needs, BNF will have to purchase with part of the instalment, NFIs including dignity and repair kits. Finally, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct Post-Rehabilitation and Post-Monitoring Activities. ACTED will lead this activity by providing cash assistance through PalPay, with technical support of UFA, FAFD and CBPCs, which will supervise repair and rehabilitation works. A3: CBPCs strengthening through refresher training including on PSEA. Through this activity, 3 pre-existing CBPCs with which ACTED is already working under other HF-funded projects, will be trained on new topics to be decided upon in consultation with their members. ACTED will closely monitor CBPCs throughout the project and provide on-the-job coaching to ensure they reinforce communities’ resilience, report protection issues, and ensure community engagement. The roles and responsibilities will be based on a pre-existing methodology and ToRs, which have proven successful under previously implemented projects. Through this intervention, ACTED will ensure that 40% of the CBPC members remain female. ACTED will also integrate PSEA activities, in line with the PSEA network recommendations and according to IASC principles and guidance. Coordination activities will include ACTED’s direct engagement with the HCT oPt PSEA network and collaboration with operational partners to establish or strengthen inter- agency and community-based CRM accessible to targeted populations. ACTED will ensure that a standard clause on PSEA is included in all contracts in accordance with the IASC (Inter-Agency Standing Committee) principles and guidelines. Finally, any outstanding needs detected all along the different phases of the project, especially protection-related ones (PSEA, MHPSS, GBV), will be referred to sectoral partners.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. Specifically, ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) is independent from the Program Department and will be in charge of reinforcing the projects’ accountability throughout the project cycle management by regularly assessing project implementation. AMEU is an essential part of ACTED’s project management structure, providing a mechanism to crosscheck and give recommendations on the work of the project team and partners. AMEU leads assessment of baseline situations of beneficiaries through data collection, monitors ongoing implementation through regular field visits and consultations with beneficiaries, and evaluates the outcome of the activities. This methodology ensures the AMEU uses an outcome monitoring approach to analyse the effect of the action on target groups. ACTED will measure the following indicators:# of HHs have repaired or upgraded to the shelter minimum standards (400 HHs);# of people/HHs exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported with shelter assistance (2,240 individuals); # of individuals protected and have improved access to adequate shelter. (2240 individuals);% of female members interviewed during the validation of the vulnerability and technical assessment to guarantee that gender-specific needs are taken into consideration (50%); # of households have received emergency NFIs assistance (400 HHs);% of female members are participating actively in the community-based protection committees (40%);# of Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPCs) reinforced through trainings to scale-up their ability to provide services to their communities (3);# of community members have improved capacity for emergency response (disaggregated by governorate) )18 members) % of CBPC members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the trainings (80%);% of households who report satisfaction with the intervention (80%);% of females, older people, people with disabilities who report improved safety and dignity after the intervention (80%);% of complaints have been managed in a timely manner(15 working days based on ACTED CRM SOPs) (90%);ACTED’s project management team will use the Project Management Framework (PMF) tool to plan and monitor implementation to assess progress and highlight blocking points. The action will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to provide feedback and suggestions directly to ACTED staff through a phone line, WhatsApp, post, email, and complaint boxes in relevant locations, as well as directly to partner’s staff members. The CRM should ensure that beneficiaries can easily and without fear of repercussion communicate cases of abuse of power by financial institutions, community leaders and/or other key actors involved in the cash-transfer program and/or aspects of program and/or transfer modality that inadvertently have negative implications. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Particular efforts will be dedicated to ensure that BNF are aware of the functioning and use of this tool since the last MSNA (July 2022) revealed that only 18,4% of HHs had access/knowledge of complaint mechanisms in Gaza. Financial monitoring will be undertaken by all project partners and then consolidated by ACTED, with external audits of the action taking place on an annual basis. ACTED staff will request from UFA and FAFD monthly reporting on activities on top of contractual obligations to ensure all indicators are on track and register the beneficiaries’ satisfaction and any difficulties encountered. This monthly reporting will minimise the risk of delays in the activities’ implementation, that any change in the implementation of the project is notified and approved by oPt HF and finally that all indicators are on track and if needed corrective measures are taken to reach the target. Finally, this will enable ACTED to provide comprehensive and clear interim and final reports to oPt HF.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

As noted in the 2022 MSNA findings, cash assistance is beneficiaries’ preferred method (70.1%) in Gaza, especially for women and PwD, as it provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost-efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will contribute towards restoring the Palestinian economy. Finally, cash assistance ensures multisectoral coverage, while keeping beneficiaries involved all along the activities, as they can choose the more adapted items to cover their multiple needs. Service provider solution will also be an alternative available to beneficiaries for these activities, notably the rehabilitation work, which require specific technical skills. In this way ACTED/UFA/FAFD will ensure the quality and effectiveness of the results and ensure a do-no-harm approach for beneficiaries. For this methodology, ACTED/UFA/FAFD will identify experienced and professional contractors who will be selected through a transparent procurement process thereby ensuring the completion of activities on time whilst fulfilling all technical requirements.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co- applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that ACTED teams learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address, complaints boxes and directly through ACTED staff during field visits. This plurality of means aims to ensure CRM accessibility to all (women, girls, boys, and men), including vulnerable groups with specific needs. During the assessment phase, field officers will provide a CRM card to each BNF, including all communication channels mentioned above, and explanations on the CRM, to ensure that all BNF feel comfortable and in measure to reach it if needed. As PwD are sometimes deliberately prevented from expressing their needs, the project team will conduct household-level visits to assess their specific needs and explain the purpose of the project. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED, UFA, and FAFD will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do-No-Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do-No-Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’s Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; awareness amongst beneficiaries of what is unacceptable behavior and how to report it; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. ACTED will put particular emphasis on informing beneficiaries on the functioning and use of the CRM in the Gaza Strip, since the last MSNA conducted in June 2022, revealed that only 18.4% of HHs which benefitted from humanitarian aid, reported awareness of how to access a complaint or reporting mechanism. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per HH will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are considered, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, older people and other marginalized persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. ACTED will consider engaging seasoned gender expert(s) throughout the project cycle to support the project management unit in designing and implementing a project gender mainstreaming strategy & building and sustaining gender responsive M&E system. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. For instance, women will be highly encouraged to participate in the CBPCs to ensure that membership remains at 40% female at a minimum. Similar efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBPCs will conduct HH visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including older people, PwDs) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
