Project Summary

CEDS proposes to provide emergency WASH intervention to 181,806 crisis affected individuals in lakes, NBEG, and Warrap States of South Sudan that will enable the targeted beneficiaries to have increased access to clean and safe drinking water, secure sanitation facilities and good hygiene practices messages thus improving their living standards.

1 - Needs

The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview indicates that an estimated 6.1 million people are in dire need of WASH assistance in South Sudan with the WASH conditions in Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states being classified as most severe. The percentage of households presently accessing latrines in Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states is consistently less than 10% with open defecation as a norm. Inadequate, poorly functioning or non-functioning community water points cause women and girls to travel long distances to access safe water. Households unable to access water points rely on surface water. the surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this along with widespread open defecation practices are a health risk more so during flooding when latrines sip into the water pathways. The WASH situation is likely to worsen during the rainy season. WASH and sanitation infrastructures in locations experiencing a surge in numbers of people often get easily damaged by the floods or due to strenuous/over use as a result in a rise in WASH demands. Lack of WASH awareness coupled with cultural beliefs and taboos surrounding use of latrines has hindered uptake of WASH services by the community members. Soap utilization is still low owing to poor purchasing power and/ competitive needs such as food items. This project targets to provide adequate access to clean safe water, improved-climate resilient sanitation facilities, distribute hygiene and sanitation kits and as well pass on hygiene promotion messages to the vulnerable flood affected populations.

2 - Project activities

- Train Water User Committees on borehole operations and maintenance- Assess functionality of community water points- Rehabilitate community boreholes- Elevate aprons of community boreholes- Construct climate resilient emergency temporary community latrines - Train community hygiene promoters on hygiene promotion - Convey key hygiene promotion messages integrated with Covid-19 preventive messages to households through house-to-house visits and public mass awareness hygiene promotion sessions- Secure and distribute MHM kits to vulnerable flood affected women and adolescent girls - Secure and distribute WASH NFIs to vulnerable flood and crisis affected households including water purifying tabs and soap.- Conduct one onsite and post site distribution monitoring- Carry out safety audits at water points that are to be rehabilitated and latrines that are to be constructed or rehabilitated.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CEDS will ensure that the beneficiaries views are incorporated into the project implementation so that the project is implemented to the satisfaction of the targeted beneficiaries and to achieve the intended objective. Before rehabilitation of any boreholes CEDS will meetings with the communities that are using the targeted borehole to get their views on how the boreholes can be upgraded to be flood resilient with suggestions from the beneficiaries taken into consideration. CEDS will also consult the beneficiaries on the appropriate locations and timings when the WASH NFIs should be distributed to ensure that the beneficiaries are not inconvenienced during such exercises. CEDS will also consult the targeted communities on beneficiaries selection criteria particularly for beneficiaries of WASH NFIs with suggestions of the community taken into considerations. CEDS will also used the collected feedback and learning from this project to shape future projects.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEDS will ensure that all the different groups in the community are given opportunity to give their feedback on the project implementation and that their feedback are used to shape the project implementation to their benefit. Since CEDS will set up village feedback committees, during the setting of of these committees CEDS will ensure that each committee has at least one person living with disability to represent their interest and also ensure that the number of women in each committee is equal to that of men or even more where possible. To meet the needs of those who are not able to read and write, CEDS will use other feedback channels that are friendly to these group of people. To achieve this, CEDS will carry out community feedback meetings that will give an opportunity to those who are not able to read and write a forum to share their feedback on project implementation. Additionally CEDS will carry out community information sharing meetings that will provide those who are not able to read and write an opportunity to get to know about the project and its deliverable thus equipping them to give informed feedback on the project implementation processes. CEDS will also have feedback volunteers in each targeted village that will be able to receive feedback from those who are not able to write and relay them to AAP officer. Community feedback meetings will be organized that target only the disadvantaged people in the community for them to freely give their feedback such will include separate meetings with those people living with disability and that of women separately.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Since CEDS will be carrying out information sharing sessions through community meetings, during these meetings CEDS will inform the targeted beneficiaries about their protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. The targeted beneficiaries will be made to understand that the services they will be receiving will be their right and not meant for exchange of any sexual favours. Additionally, CEDS will let the targeted beneficiaries know that they are free to report any suspected cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. The beneficiaries will also be made aware of the various reporting channels that they can use to report any suspected cases of sexual exploitation and abuse by either staff or suppliers involved in the project. CEDS will also make sure that staff and suppliers involved in this project will be made aware of the organization's protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policy and will be made to commit through signing their commitment to this policy.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CEDS appreciates that beneficiary feedback is key in project implementations and helps in achieving the set objectives of the project. In order to achieve this CEDS will be holding beneficiary feedback meetings during which information about the project will be shared with the targeted beneficiaries as well as their views on the project collected. CEDS will also set up beneficiary feedback committees at the village level that will be mandated with receiving views/feedback from the targeted beneficiaries that they will then share with the AAP officer for action by the project implementing team. The committee will be provided with feedback record books in which the committee secretary will be recording feedback from the targeted beneficiaries. additionally, CEDS will also set up suggestion boxes in public places in the project implementation area where the literate people will be able to share their feedback. The information will be collected on a weekly basis, analysed and feedback shared with the targeted beneficiaries. CEDS will ensure that the project management team hold meetings every two weeks during which feedback that require decision of the project management team will be discussed and feedback shared with the targeted beneficiaries. These feedback that will be received will be used to shape the implementation of the project thus ensuring that the implemented project fits the needs of the targeted beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CEDS will put up measures that will try to mitigate the risks that are associated to the implementation of this project. To mitigate the risk of beneficiaries being exposed to violence during project activities such as registration and distributions CEDS will ensure that the beneficiary selection process is fair and inclusive thus not triggering disputes in the targeted communities. CEDS will also ensure activities such as registration and distributions are done in places that are deemed safe from violence. To mitigate the risk of targeted beneficiaries being exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse including child safeguarding issues, CEDS will ensure that the targeted beneficiaries are enlightened that the humanitarian support they will be receiving is free and not meant for any sexual favours from them to the implementing personnel. The beneficiaries will also be informed that they have the right to report any suspected cases of sexual exploitation and abuse. The staff and suppliers that will have direct contact with the beneficiaries will also be made to commit and sign CEDS PSEA policy ensuring that targeted beneficiaries are protected.To mitigate the risk of the beneficiaries lacking information on protection and referral assistance CEDS will ensure that these information will be shared during beneficiaries information sharing sessions and that those who need such services are supported. CEDS will equally work with protection partners in the targeted areas to be able to support beneficiaries that will need such services. To mitigate the risk of marginalised groups not being consulted during the project implementation CEDS will ensure that feedback channels used are tailor made to suit the marginalised groups. CEDS will hold community information sharing and feedback meetings targeting only the marginalised groups giving them opportunity to fully be consulted and participate in the project. To mitigate the risk of beneficiaries not accessing the set up feedback channels CEDS will ensure that the feedback channels will be set up will be as suggested by the targeted beneficiaries. CEDS will mostly use community meetings, feedback committees and feedback volunteers that are village based and accessible to all.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The proposed project activities will ensure access of clean and safe water for drinking, farming and livestock and domestic use to vulnerable flood affected households. The waiting time used by women and young girls to fetch water will be reduced upon rehabilitations thus this time can be utilized in other productive activities such as caring for the infants and young children and farming activities by the women and school work for the young girls. The newly constructed latrines will provide access to dignified and safe sanitation facilities for the targeted flood households advertently contributing to reduced open defecation practices in the community. Vulnerable households and women and adolescent girls will gain access to standard acceptable WASH NFIs and Menstrual Hygiene Management kits respectively. The money set aside for obtaining these hygiene and sanitation materials can be spent on essential food and other non-food items. All these efforts combined with access and uptake of hygiene promotion messages by the vulnerable flood affected households will ultimately result in reduced transmission of water-borne and sanitation related diseases and costs attributed to their treatment. Overall, these determined attempts will improve the WASH and health situation of the targeted vulnerable flood affected households therefore improving their living standards.
