Project Summary

The primary goal of this project is to reduce avoidable morbidity, mortality and disability as a result of chronic diseases and mental health disorders through supporting existing health facilities in the geographical targets. The project establishes an improved access of vulnerable people including migrants, displaced families, returnees and people with special needs to quality health service that will include treatment of chronic diseases.
In this project, WHO will strengthen the capacities of the health facilities with the provision of basic and supplementary medical equipment for the treatment of chronic diseases.

Needs

According to Global Health estimates, the burden of chronic diseases is responsible for the 77.7% of all deaths in Libya. Cardiovascular disease account for 43.2%, cancer 13.6% and diabetes mellitus 5.3% of all deaths. As a result, 18% of adults in Libya between the age of 30 and 70 years are expected to die from the main four chronic diseases. Furthermore, the non-communicable disease country profile documented 35.7% of adults to have died as a result of blood pressure. Mental health and disability are chronically neglected fields in Libya. There are insufficient trained staff in the field of mental disorders and disabilities. The conflict increased the proportion of the population in need of acute psychosocial support. There are different types of disabilities in Libya; according to the Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia report (2017) physical disability account for 28%, visual 11.8%, and cognitive 22.5&, hearing 16.3% and socializing with people 48.3%. The need for rehabilitation services in the country has increased due to the continuous armed conflicts, ongoing violence and the breakdown of the community support systems as well as massive migration and displacement with increasing number of disabilities and mental health disorders. Resources are scarce and Ministry of Health capacity is limited to cope with the current protracted crises. Presently, only seven of the 11 essential medicines required for treatment of chronic diseases are available in the public sector.

Activities

The project will support the following activities1. Capacity building a. Deploy five qualified psychiatrists to five mental health hospitals and clinicsb. Train five psychiatrists and 10 psychiatric nurses on mental health case management and provision of specialized services2. Procurement of life-saving drugsa. Procure and distribute 10 basic and five supplementary Non-communicable kits to secondary health facilitiesb. Procure two consignments of mental health drugs and distribute to mental health hospitals in close coordination with Medical Service Office (MSO).c. Procure three consignments of oncological medicines and distribute to three cancer treatment centers. 3. Procurement of rehabilitation materialsa. Procure and distribute three shipments of rehabilitation materials of different types in partnership with Handicap International.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

During the community consultation and needs assessment activities, the project identified the gender implications and gendered health determinants influencing health inequities between men, women, boys and girls. Vulnerabilities and strengths of men, women, girls and boys; older persons; persons with disabilities; persons belonging to minority groups and persons with mental disorders were also identified through joint assessments. The project will improve access of affected people to services by making services readily available round the clock. Gender equality in health service provision will be taken into consideration in order to warrant equity in health access and promote the Universal Health Coverage.WHO will organize a full orientation session on the accountability to affected people and protection for the service providers. A complaint mechanism will be established to receive and address concerns of beneficiaries.
