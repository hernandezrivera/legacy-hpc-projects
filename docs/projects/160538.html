Project Summary

The Project’s ultimate goal is to ensure that the displaced and host communities affected by the recent hostilities and subsequent displacement and trauma in Urban Juba, Lainya, Yei, Kajo Keji, Maridi, and Morobo including survivors of GBV, 1) have access to, and receive comprehensive, gender-sensitive, survivor-centered, life-saving psychosocial, case management and referral services; 2) rebuild social cohesion and community-based support mechanisms; and 3) are prevented from further harm. To reach this ultimate goal, the project will seek the following outcome: Enhanced life-saving and survivor-centered prevention and response protection services of quality available to/and visible to vulnerable communities affected by the recent conflict, with a particular focus on GBV survivors in line with the Protection Clusters first objective the project will deploy an integrated approach to ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services.
In addition, the project will work with communities to (re) build community-based prevention mechanisms and ensure a safer environment for all, especially girls, boys, women, the elderly, and people with disability – who were particularly affected by the shocks of conflict, displacement and crisis. Thus, in line with the Protection Cluster’s second objective- the project will ensure that communities are capable and prepared to address threats and vulnerabilities, while maximizing existing positive coping mechanisms, and through enhanced preparedness and resilience of
One key aspect of IsraAID’s mandate is to accompany national/local actors in their efforts to design, implement and evaluate their own programs to ensure local ownership and sustainability. IsraAID identifies local actors to design and implement Protection/GBV programs. IsraAID’s role is to provide continuing technical support and on-going training and mentoring to ensure that service providers in each of its partner institutions have the skills and tools needed to deliver services of quality. In order to ensure that IsraAID’s programs have a long-term impact, the organization involves and trains local partners from the need assessment to the evaluation phase (including national CBOs and service providers). Throughout its programs, IsraAID places a particular emphasis on IASC Accountability for Affected Populations Principles (AAP). It continuously trains its own staff as well as national partners on those principles and how to incorporate them into activities.
In 2020 IsraAID will engage continue to work and support our national partners; Facilitating Action for Community Empowerment (FACE) in Maridi, TITI Foundation in Juba, Sustainable Children Aid (SCA) in Kajo Keji, South Sudan Health Association (SSUHA) in Lainya and Voice for Change (VfC) in Yei.

1 - Needs

The ongoing instability in South Sudan, as a result of the 2013 and 2016 fighting has continued throughout 2019. During the year there were multiple altercations between government, IO and a multitude of armed actors. The general security situation in the country remains tense, and lower intensity fighting continued to take place in the greater Equatorias, affecting and displacing populations which have been subject to continuous violence and displacement since the 2013 and the 2016 crisis, further exacerbated vulnerability factors. While hope remains for the revitalised peace agreement, there are many delays in the implementation, and tensions remain high, especially with non-signatories to the agreement. In Kajo Keji, IsraAID is operating in Khorijo, supporting both IDP and host communities. Due to conflict between IO and NAS in early 2019 most of the population fled to Khorijo from Kerwa, Ajio and Logo. IsraAID together with partners are addressing a number of protection concerns including; domestic violence which is worsened by the conditions of the IDP’s most of whom lack basic needs, cases of rape and GBV are common. Additionally there is no proper school in Khorijo and most children are out of school. Children are showing signs of trauma and distress and girls are at risk of early and forced marriage. In Maridi, while the security situation remains calm, the ongoing tensions and instability throughout the country continues to affect the psychological wellbeing of IDP’s and returnees in the area. IsraAID and FACE have been responding to several GBV and CP concerns in Maridi since the start of 2019; GBV cases including intimate partner violence, domestic violence, harassment and additional forms of abuse. Additionally, the issue of forced and early marriage is common in Maridi. There is a need to extend GBV and CP services to additional areas of Maridi, especially rural areas. In 2020 IsraAID will seek to extend services and to establish additional WGFS and CFS. In Yei and Laniya the situation remains tense due to the presence of several armed actors including cattle raiders in Lainya. Through the year, there have been skirmishes and ambushes in the area. Vulnerability factors for women and girls are exacerbated by the presence of armed elements in civilian areas which severely limits the movement of people, access to farm lands, food and services. The community faces uncontrolled levels of violence, repeated displacement, very limited access to basic services, substantial levels of emotional distress, mental health problems and fear among women and girls who are being targeted. In March 2019 IsraAID together with partner Voice for Change conducted a safety audit and found a number of urgent protection concerns, including rampant GBV and rape. Women and girls reported high rates of abuse, and even abduction and killing of women by armed actors while collecting firewood and fetching water, going to the market, on walking on the roads. Girls reported they are dropping out of school out of fear of GBV attacks. Morobo is completely destroyed by the wake of the July 2016 conflict, and conditions are further exacerbated by the ongoing hostilities in late 2018-2019. The crisis in Morobo has displaced thousands of people, recently insecurity has further displacement of civilian population to nearby villages (outskirt of Central Morobo) with women and children heavily affected by the armed conflict. IsraAID field teams in Morobo report that the population of Morobo is facing life threatening protection concerns, due to the large presence of armed actors. Women reported a multitude of cases of GBV, rape, physical torture, destroyed homes, loss of personal & family properties like clothes, goats, saucepans, etc., early and forced marriages of girls, harassment by armed soldiers in the area, and lack of access to information and basic services for women and children.

2 - Activities

The project intends to save lives and alleviate suffering through safe access to services with dignity, and fully reflect the objectives of the protection cluster to ensure the availability of, and safe and free access to quality protection response services. In addition, the project will work with communities to (re)build community-based prevention mechanisms and ensure a safer environment for all, especially women, children, the elderly, pregnant women, lactating mothers, and people with disability – who were particularly affected by the shocks of conflict and displacement. Thus, the project will ensure that resilient communities are capable and prepared to address threats and vulnerabilities, while maximizing existing positive coping mechanisms, in accordance with the Protection Cluster’s strategic objectives. IsraAID and partners are running a number of WFGS and CFS in the in Urban Juba, Lainya, Yei, Kajo Keji, Maridi and Morobo. Project activities are defined in consultation with national partners, local leaders, communities, and beneficiaries. Activities include: • Comprehensive case management for child survivors- To provide case management (including referral pathway) services and psychosocial support (including Psychological First Aid and Post-Trauma Healing) that respect minimum standards of care to GBV survivors and their communities in cooperation with health, Rule of Law and other Protection actors; • PSS, PFA, and individual counselling to children and care givers;• Safe referrals, including information dissemination on available services;• Child Friendly Spaces and Women & Girl Friendly Spaces programming and activities; • Creation of groups (women, men, adolescent boys and girls) to reduce violence and mitigate risk factors associated with SGBV, empower women, girl, men and boys to become agents of change, to provide basic life skills to adolescent girls and boys, and female single-headed households to ensure self-sufficiency. IsraAID currently runs number of group interventions including; livelihood and skills training, trauma and stress relief groups, discussion and support groups, tea talks, and literacy and numeracy classes. • Community-based protection initiatives, through mobilization and training of community-based focal points identify conflict dynamics, generate early-response mechanisms (including CPie, GBV referral pathways in cooperation with health, Rule of Law and other protection actors, and establishment of Emergency Community Councils) • Awareness raising campaigns, using the community care approach;• Multi-sectoral training of police, health and legal actors to ensure survivors receive minimal standards of care. IsraAID and partners have been active and have strong ties with communities, all above-mentioned activities are implemented in accordance with a thorough needs assessment in consultation with community leaders and representatives.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

IsraAID works with vulnerable communities affected by conflict and displacement, who find themselves in situations where they can be exploited and/or abused. IsraAID, as an International Non-Governmental Organization working in South Sudan, is committed to prevent and combat Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). All IsraAID staff, partners, and consultant have signed the IsraAID Code of conduct on sexual exploitation and abuse.Throughout its programs, IsraAID has placed a particular emphasis on IASC AAP principles. It continuously trained its own staff as well as national partners on those principles and how to incorporate them into activities to prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm. IsraAID makes sure to include community members, service providers and other beneficiaries throughout the project cycle, from needs assessment to evaluation, by organizing regular FGD and consultation meetings in the communities (on a bi-weekly basis) and briefings with service providers and partners. Those discussions and briefings provide a platform of dialogue for feedback mechanisms from the beneficiaries. They offer a unique opportunity to discuss challenges, gaps and explore solutions in a participatory manner. For sensitive requests and/or complaints, beneficiaries are also informed that they can directly contact (by phone, email or at the office) a specifically identified staff, not associated with the project. IsraAID has placed a particular emphasis on accountability to affected individuals with disability, ensuring their full participation in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation frameworks of the project. IsraAID has a series of practical and user-friendly tools and checklists that all staff are required to use both at management and at the field/community level to ensure sustainability, local ownership, do-no-harm and effective implementation of program. Tools are all available to all in hard and soft copies.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Throughout its programs, IsraAID has placed a particular emphasis on IASC’s Accountability for Affected Populations (AAP) principles. The organization continuously trains its own staff as well as national partners on those principles and how to incorporate them into activities. All above-mentioned activities are implemented in accordance with thorough needs assessment and needs review that are conducted by IsraAID on a regular basis in consultation with community leaders and members. Consultations happen at various levels, from community leaders, beneficiaries, in women’s groups and youth groups. Focus group discussions (FDGs) are organized at the beginning of the project to further understand baseline, at mid-term to monitor the progress of the project, and at the end of the project to evaluate impact. FGD outcomes will be documented into IsraAID's FGD tools and shared with stakeholders. IsraAID has developed and tested a series of monitoring and evaluation tools that were endorsed by UNICEF and that enable us to assess the knowledge and perceptions of communities, as well as knowledge and practice of service providers and focal points. All IsraAID's programmatic staffs as well as partners have been comprehensively trained on the utilization and analysis of such tools. The tools are available in hard and soft copies to all stakeholders. IsraAID fully embraces and implements an inclusive approach with equity and meaningful access to all. All staff and partners have committed to provide services based on needs alone, without discrimination, to Respect and promote the fundamental rights of all, without discrimination of any kind, irrespective of race, religion, gender, ethnicity, sexual orientation, political beliefs, social status or disability; and to Treat all beneficiaries and other persons fairly and with respect and dignity, in accordance with the laws of South Sudan, international law and local customs.
