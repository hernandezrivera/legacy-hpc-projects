Project Summary

This project is an addendum to the earlier submission of food security and nutrition project that was submitted targeting three districts in Mashonaland East, namely Murehwa, Mutoko and Mudzi. The planned interventions are aimed at improving the food and nutrition status of 24,000 vulnerable households (120, 000 individuals) dis-aggregated as follows by district; Mudzi 8,000 H/H (40,000 people), Mutoko 9,000 H/H (45,000 people) and Murehwa 7,000 H/H; (35,000 people) in Mashonaland East Province.
Zimbabwe has been going through economic challenges for over two decades, ranging from unemployment, poor economic performance, financial crisis and the liquidity crunch, which exacerbates the vulnerable situation thus reducing its capacity to invest in food security and nutrition security, water and sanitation infrastructure. The COVID-19 pandemic, has further put a strain on the ailing economy making the population in both urban and rural areas more vulnerable the disease. People living in poverty with or without access to food, water, sanitation and healthcare services face the greatest risk from the COVID-19 pandemic as they may not be able to observe prevention guidelines due to the limited resources and their need to continue with livelihood activities for survival despite the imminent threat. Zimbabwe still faces a number of challenges including providing food and nutrition security, clean potable water and sanitation services for the citizens and promoting good public health. The food and nutrition security, water supply situation remains critical in both urban centers and rural areas due to the recurrent droughts that the country has experienced, resulting in heavy reliance on underground water thereby lowering of water tables, loss of livelihoods for communities. Promoting handwashing and general hygiene are critical routes to minimize the spread of COVID-19. Non availability of handwashing facilities in public places, agricultural farmer markets, over-crowding in the public spaces, all increase the risk of spreading of the virus among the citizens. This is exacerbated by a rapid spike in prices of Personal Protective Equipment, hygiene enhancing accessories and disinfectants in most formal outlets across the country. However, as part of home grown efforts the Zimbabwe is promoting the use of re-usable homemade face masks to curb the further spread of the disease. The project would like to support the target communities with PPEs, handwashing in public spaces and markets.
The target districts are at risky given that Mudzi hosts the Nyamapanda Boarder, which links Zimbabwe with Mozambique and Malawi and the project will help to create awareness among the populations of three districts which are the corridor that link Zimbabwe with its neighbours. The proposed activities will help to complement other livelihoods activities being implemented by Mercy Corps in Mudzie and Murehwa.
