Project Summary

Somalia faces multiple challenges such as conflict, drought, social economic impact of COVID19 pandemic, flooding, inadequate and inequitable social services and massive population displacement but persistently high levels of malnutrition are underlying the survival, growth and development of the population. According to FSNAU latest post-Gu seasonal assessment released, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. Sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance and government support are currently preventing more severe outcomes in many areas. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly. These findings reflect the outcome of the 2020 post-Gu seasonal food security and nutrition assessment conducted across Somalia during July and August 2020. In response to the situation and needs explained above, WARDI proposes a 12-month quality lifesaving curative nutrition care services aimed at supporting communities in Banadir, Hiraan and lower Shabelle Region of south -central Somalia. The project aims to cover 14 nutrition centers and two mobile teams. The proposed project intends to target 465,740 vulnerable population who are in need of humanitarian assistance, of this 65,204 ((33254 girls and 31,950 boys)) are children under the age of five years and 16,767 are pregnant and lactating women. The targeted nutrition centers will provide facility and community-based screening for severe acute malnutrition for children aged 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and provide treatment of acute malnourished 2794 (51%girsl) children below five years and pregnant and lactating women (16,767) at the outpatient therapeutic center. Further, micronutrient supplementation will be given to PLW mothers as well as children 6-59 months for treatment and prevention of malnutrition, Routine individual IYCF counselling services will be conducted for PLW mother attending nutrition centers; children aged 6-59 months will be supplemented with vitamin A, capacity building for nutrition facility and community workers(Boys,girls,men and women ) on appropriate IYCF counselling services to mothers attending the facilities, conduct training for the facility workers as well as community nutrition workers(50 male and 50 female ) on COVID19 awareness and protection to curb the spread of the infectious pandemic diseases. WARDI will closely work with other agencies in the region to better coordinate humanitarian interventions and prevent duplication, moreover services will be integrated with the existing primary health care services as well as TSFP/MCHN interventions supported by WFP in the targeted regions, further the project will adequately address and capture the rights of disabilities as well as protection measures by collecting gender-disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to nutrition activities implemented. WARDI will also ensure that all segments of the affected population including women and people with disabilities have equal access to nutrition/health care assistance to support and advance gender equality based on a gender analysis. The nutrition program will meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men to generate positive and sustainable outcomes.

Mitigation Measures

WARDI will ensure all minority and majority as well as well as disable groups are involved in the implementation of the project and be able to transparent in terms of employment. Community nutrition workers selection and training will also consider gender sensitivity including men and women. All this will ensure gender and equity issues are addressed among the community members. WARDI will collect gender-disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to Nutrition activities to ensure they have equal access to quality nutrition care services.WARDI will ensure that all segments of the affected population including pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months have equal access to Nutrition assistance. The minority and marginalized communities who are at risk of being neglected/ excluded are also given quality and equal nutrition care services to prevent malnutrition. WARDI will also advocate community participation in the implementation of the program. This will help in getting complaints and feedback responses that enhances effective decision making and promote accountability to the affected populationWARDI proposed project is designed in way that prioritizes the rights, safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. The target communities are fully involved and aware of the roles of each stakeholder where each sees the other as partners and equals. The proposed project will not discriminate against any beneficiary and will enable equal and impartial assistance and services for all. Targeting of vulnerable children and adults will be done on basis of their specific needs. The staff will be inducted on their duties and responsibilities and particularly the rights of the beneficiaries to ensure there are no any form of patient exploitation at the facilities and WARDI offices. Watertight mechanisms will be put in place to ensure served populations voices are heard and can file their complaints without being victimized.
