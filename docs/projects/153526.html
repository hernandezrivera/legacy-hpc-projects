Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to increase enrolment and retention in selected schools through reduced hunger and malnutrition in Greater Pibor Administrative Area GPAA, and facilitate the return to school for children & youth who dropped out because of hunger, forced child labor or recruitment into armed groups. Since 2014, FCA has implemented standard EiE activities such as construction of semi-permanent and temporary learning spaces, teacher capacity building through training, awareness raising activities, PTA/SMS formation & strengthening. In addition, FCA integrates protection and WASH in EiE programs and supports ALP and ECD centres. The proposed school feeding project will integrate nutrition education, WASH in schools and disease prevention.
Objective 1: Reduced hunger and malnutrition for 2,895 children in eight (8) schools in GPAA.
The proposed objective is reached through provision of on-site school meals, construction of basic school kitchens/stores, and hygiene training for safe handling, preparation, cooking and storage of food. This objective will increase school attendance by lowering the opportunity cost of attending school, and lead to more time spent in school, and towards learning. Schools targeted Pibor, Lekuangole and Gumuruk include Lurnyang, Manyiran, Tangajon, Kavachoch, St. Clement, Gei and Agoy primary school.
Objective 2: Enhanced awareness on nutrition education and WASH for school governing bodies (SGB), teachers and children.
The proposed objective is reached through establishment of nutrition clubs to popularize availability, diversity and accessibility & benefits of food values; and capacity support to SMCs/PTAs and youth in nutrition and food security advocacy at community level. FCA will establish school hygiene clubs & WASH facilities to support messaging on safe use of latrines. This objective will lead to better health and resistance to infectious diseases & illnesses that would keep children from attending school. Better nutrition indirectly improves educational achievement by increasing school attendance.

Needs

According to 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), by end of 2017, about 1.7 million children aged 3-17 years (58% boys and 42% girls) were in need of education services in South Sudan as conflict continued to affect the already fragile education system. Before the conflict began in 2013, about 73% of men and 84% of women above 15 years of age were illiterate and three out of five school-age children not enrolled in primary school . Although a few indicators have improved, the national assessment conducted by the Education Cluster in 2017 showed that 11% of primary schools open in the beginning of 2017 were no longer functional in October-November, and that 30% of primary schools had suffered at least one attack since December 2013. In addition, some 158,000-refugee children in South Sudan in need of pre-school, primary and secondary education assistance in 2018 through to 2019.Since 2013, Pibor County has seen multiple shocks, including severe insecurity, disease, insufficient rains and a reduction in available natural resources. These shocks have affected livelihoods through diminishing herd sizes and reduced crop yields, as well as impacted access to quality education. Cattle raids, which have links to food insecurity, are more frequent and violent in the area. In addition, inter-community and age-set violence remains a major concern, causing frequent disruption to learning. FCA has gained experience in implementation of humanitarian responses in Jonglei, Lakes, GPAA and Central Equatoria State, particularly in EiE and TVET projects with funding from European Union, MFA (Ministry for Foreign affairs Finland), ECHO and partnership with UNICEF. Currently FCA is running EiE projects in Mingkaman, Pibor, Gumuruk, Lekuangole, New Fangak, Old Fangak, Pulita, Barboi, Manajang and Paguer; including a TVET project in Juba, Bor, Old Fangak and Pibor. Since 2014, FCA constructed over 60 learning spaces in Lakes and Jonglei state, including five (5) blocks of permanent classrooms in Pibor and Gumuruk. In addition, FCA is in the process of establishing semi-permanent classrooms for secondary school education in Mingkaman and Old Fangak funded by CGA (Charlie Goldsmith Associates). The proposed intervention will add value to education in emergencies programming through increased enrolment and retention rates in schools supported. FCA has established full operational capacity & amicable relations with other sector partners and gained acceptance among local communities & authorities in areas of operation. Most importantly, FCA has ongoing EiE and TVET projects until 2020 in these areas. FCA has previously implemented school meal programs in Central African Republic (CAR) and Haiti; technical support from HO based experts is available. Vital experiences FCA gathered in recent years have showed that enrolment and retention rate in schools are very difficult to maintain. Usually when children are old enough, parents force them into child labor to contribute to family food requirements. In addition, children are involved in shifting agriculture and cattle raiding practices, which keeps them out of school. This intervention aims to increase children’s educational achievement to improve their future productivity through provision of meals on site. In addition, increase school attendance by lowering the opportunity cost of attending school, & provide additional incentives to engage in formal education. Children spend more time in school & towards learning as a result. School meals also alleviate short-term hunger; improve children’s cognitive functioning & attention span. On site meals, improve nutritional status of children that leads to better health and resistance to illness that would keep them from attending school. This intervention will complement other trainings such as life-saving messages in peace education, child protection, girls’ education, GBV, early marriage, HIV/AIDS in school.

Activities

1) Provision of on-site school meals: FCA will provide basic food items for preparation of school meals for eight selected Schools in GPAA. Food items include dried beans, vegetable oil, iodized salt and maize meal to supplement the 2,100 Kcal requirements of schoolchildren. SGBs are responsible for food preparation; pupils will receive cooked meals randomly for three days in a week. Selection of schools based on recent IPC data for food insecure places in the area.2) Establishment of school stores and basic kitchens for meal preparation: Project will support establishment of temporary kitchens with local materials; provide required NFIs and food storage facilities in the eight schools. In addition, FCA will establish handwashing facilities in the selected schools. SGBs are responsible for supervision and maintenance of kitchen and WASH facilities. 3) Hygiene training for school governing bodies (PTAs and SMCs): FCA will train teachers and PTA/SMC members in safe food preparation, handling, cooking and storage. SGBs will also receive capacity support in nutrition and food security advocacy at community level to increase their knowledge in emergency school meal programming. 4) Establishment of nutrition clubs to popularize availability, diversity and accessibility of food, benefits of food values/balanced diet: FCA will create nutrition clubs in all the eight schools, the clubs will conduct peer discussions on appropriate feeding practices, and what it means to their communities and schoolchildren. A teacher will be assigned to each nutrition club as a focal person to guide and support the sessions.5) Create hygiene clubs to support messaging and provide awareness on cleaning and safe use of latrines: The project will provide systematic awareness on hygiene-related risks, safe handling and food storage. To support messaging, FCA will use existing school hygiene clubs and establish new ones where there is none. Awareness raising activities will also include community sensitization on the importance of parental participation in children’s education.FCA will coordinate implementation of activities with other actors on the ground, such as UNICEF, Plan International, Nile Hope and County Education Department. FCA is an active member of the Education Cluster and has 6 years of experience and expertise in Education in Emergencies work in the region. In addition, FCA is an active member of FSL cluster and draws relevant experience required for this intervention. The proposed objectives contribute to education cluster objective CO1 and CO3. • CO1- Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies • CO3- Strengthen the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth As per the primary indicators and cluster objectives, FCA’s proposed project will contribute as below: Objective 1: Reduced hunger and malnutrition in eight schools for 3,982 conflict-affected children in GPAA. 1) Ten (10) schools (Pibor-4, Lekuangole-2, Gumuruk-4) supported with on-site meals2) 3,982 children receive emergency meals in school three times a week3) Ten (10) kitchen stores established in target schools to support on-site meals4) 70% increase in enrollment and retention rates in the targeted schools5) 50% increase in children reporting uninterrupted learning in safe, inclusive and protected learning spacesObjective 2: Enhance awareness on nutrition education and WASH for SGBs, teachers and children. 1) 160 PTA & SMC members trained in nutrition education and WASH in school surveillance. 2) 160 PTA & SMC members receive awareness on nutrition and food security advocacy3) Ten (10) nutrition and hygiene clubs established in target schools4) 4,231 children, teachers and SGBs sensitized on nutrition education, food hygiene and WASH in School

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Key education messages for children of school age like " All Children And Youths Have The Right To Go to School" is used to encourage enrollment. Messages for parents and community to encourage them to send children to school are tailored differently, taking into consideration cultural practices and sensitivity.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Children with special needs are taken into consideration in school meal programs. School Management committees like PTAs and SMCs will be trained to support and handle with care children with pre-existing conditions. School kitchens will be constructed with doors wide enough to enable children with disabilities to have equal access.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Barriers like cultural/traditional practices, ideas and beliefs which may affect children's access will be addressed through consultation with community leaders, PTAs, teachers and county department of education. FCA will conduct awareness raising and sensitization to the mentioned groups with the objective to address issues or barriers to access.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project activities seek to provide equal access to girls and boys for on-site meals. Composition of school governing bodies like PTA and SMC members include at least 30% women representation. Through awareness raising and sensitization activities, FCA seeks to address any barriers to access through awareness raising and sensitization activities. Teachers and school governing bodies will receive basic training in management of on-site meals, equal opportunity and access for all children.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Establishment of kitchen facilities to support provision of on-site meals seeks to provide equal opportunity to all children. Awareness raising activities on hygiene, food handling, cooking and storage will target PTA and SMC members with the objective to address any inequalities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Project will be monitored regularly by project staff, during which focus group discussions with children, teachers, parents and key community members are done. Group observations will be conducted to check challenges associated with access. FCA will address barriers-to-access identified in collaboration with school governing bodies, teachers and parents. Lessons learnt from progressive monitoring activities will be documented with the objective to improve future programming

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Through group observation and discussion with children, teachers and selected parents, FCA will ensure construction of kitchen facilities meet minimum safety standards, and children feel safe during on-site meal programs.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

By ensuring that school children have been adequately fed, the project does indeed monitor the dignity of beneficiaries. In addition, FCA project team will conduct regular spot-check visits to ensure children receive on-site meals.
