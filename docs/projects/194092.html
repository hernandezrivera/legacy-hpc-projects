Project Summary

Islamic Relief Worldwide planning to contribute in provision basic services to communities in El Nuhud and El Khoway Localities of West Kordofan, those impacted by the current crises started on April 15th as a result of ongoing war between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in the country. The intervention targets El Nuhud and El Khoway, located on the highway road linking Darfur and Kordofan States to Khartoum which is the main route for the rebels entering the country. The proposed services align with the revised (HRP) 2023, strategic objectives 1 & 2. This intervention aims to contribute in improving access to Education, WASH, Health and Nutrition and protection services. It is designed to address basic needs of 52,041 individuals, (20,816 women, 18,214 men, 7,806 girls and 5,204 boys), classified as 15,612 IDPs (6,245 male, 9,367 female), 2,602 returnees (1,040 male, 1,562 female), 1,561 refugees (624 male, 937 female) and 32,266 resident vulnerable communities (12,906 male, 19360 female).
In Education, the project targets 7,348 students (4,408 girls and 2,939 boys) focusing on increasing the learning spaces to accommodate the displaced students from North Kordofan and Khartoum, as well as contribute in maintaining attractive schools environment, to both resident students and students fleeing from war areas in El Obeid and Khartoum. The proposed activities aims to increase enrolment of children in child-friendly learning spaces, provide recreational (kits), hygiene kits and establish hand washing stations, provision safe drinking water, rehabilitate latrines, rehabilitate learning spaces and deliver psychosocial support activities. Islamic Relief will implement the project in close coordination with Ministry of Education at State level through direct implementation approach.
In WASH, the project target 18,370 persons (7,348 women, 6,429 men, 2,755 girls and 1,837 boys), including displaced families from war areas to improve their access to WASH services including sanitation and hygiene promotion. WASH activities contain rehabilitation of existing water points to ensure delivery of sustainable water supply. Comprehensive awareness raising campaigns on hygiene and sanitation will be delivered to communities to improve village environment will provide the community with cleaning and waste management tools.
In Health, the project target 15,309 individuals (6,123 women, 5,350 m3n, 2,296 girls and 1,530 boys) to increase access to improved primary healthcare services, by rehabilitating health facilities, strengthening the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services as well as deliver antenatal care services.
In nutrition, the project target 7,500 individuals, comprised of malnourished girls, boys and pregnant and lactating women focusing on increasing access to improved nutrition services for communities, through management of moderate acute malnutrition for children 6-59 months, deliver Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) counselling programming, conduct home-based food fortification with micronutrient supplements to U5 children. The project focus include conduct counselling of pregnant women and caregivers of children 0-23 months as well as early detection of malnutrition among under 5 through MUAC mass screenings/ referral and regular at household level per locality.
For protection, the project targets 6,123 beneficiaries to improve protection conditions and provide support to access basic services with dignity and advocate for their rights to receive quality specialized protection services. The project will consider protection issues, particularly unaccompanied children fleeing from war areas and deliver psychological support to displaced families and focus on issues affecting women and girls in particular.

1 - Needs

Clashes between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) erupted on 15 April 2023 causing devastating consequences for civilians all over the country. The conflict started in Khartoum, but its effect spread in other parts of the country. According to Ministry of Health, 700 people have been killed and over 5,200 injured since the onset of the fighting. Reports from IOM and OCHA indicates 736,000 displaced inside Sudan since 15 April. Overall Fourteen out of Sudan’s 18 states have been affected by displacement, including West Kordofan. The fighting has its effect on all sectors causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical water source, health care facilities, schools and power infrastructure. Education sector has particularly affected by this crises in terms of forcing schools closure which impacting school children well-being, in addition to risk of schools to be occupied by the armed groups or risk of school infrastructure subjected to damage. The closing of schools increase risk of school drop-out. The crises forced displacement of many teachers and children. The key Education needs arise due to this crises are: Protecting children from physical threats, mental health and deliver psychosocial support for both children & teachers and increase access to safe and protective learning environment for the wellbeing of children.This crises has increased number of people in need across all sectors from 15.8 million, to 24.7 million, representing a 57 per cent increase, as of Humanitarian Needs Over view 2023, and Humanitarian Response Plan documents for 2023. Many affected areas by the crises are grappling with severe water shortages caused by blackouts, fuel scarcity and lack of supplies. This war situation also limiting access to urgent health and nutrition services. These challenges are compounded by soaring prices for food, fuel, and other essential goods, as well as recurring disease outbreaks, pre-existing malnutrition, and acute food insecurity. Widespread displacement has been reported across several states including West Kordofan, which has added extra burdens on limited vulnerable resources. Vulnerable population groups are increasingly exposed to protection risks as well as longer-term harm to their physical and mental wellbeing. This includes an increased number of women, girls and at-risk groups, who are exposed to risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). The deteriorating security situation is also limiting access to lifesaving GBV services, including GBV case management, clinical management of rape (CMR), psychosocial support and referrals. Safe spaces for women and girls, including to access life-saving services and obtain referrals, also limit options for support. The current violence has caused extensive damage to infrastructure, leading to water shortages, disruptions and incidents of looting especially on the highway roads from State to another. Humanitarian programmes have been severely impacted by rebels’ attacks and looting of personnel properties, which has included the killing and injury of humanitarian staff. The suspension of aid services has disrupted treatment programmes for children suffering severe acute malnutrition, putting their lives at risk in all States including WK. Food production, supply chain disruptions, fuel shortages, inflation and currency depreciation have led to shortages and price hikes, making essential goods unaffordable for many populations. Education has been severely affected, with schools and educational institutions remaining closed. Water authorities are unable to repair damaged infrastructure due to access and security obstacles. Islamic Relief will ensure working closely with Protection, Nutrition, and WASH clusters to promote an integrated approach to address the needs of targeted groups.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be a key feature within planned interventions. In all planned activities, woman and girls will be sensitized about the protection issues to reduce the risk of abuse, interaction and violence. Water facilities and water points will be constructed in safe locations that minimize risks of various types of GBV. Pitching water in most part of remote areas in Sudan is the responsibility of women and young girls. However, this project will ensure sparing women an ample time and give them the opportunity to carry out other activities such as food security and livelihood related activities and participate in the development process of their communities, state and country. Women and children will be the project focus to support through provision of direct support especially children and women of special needs. The project will ensure protecting communities particularly women while delivering FSL related activities and at the same time ensure the delivered activities will cause no harm to affected population or to the environment around them.
