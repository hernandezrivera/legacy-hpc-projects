Project Summary

The project is to ensure the availability of sufficient cold chain equipment CCE to store the COVAX vaccines that was allocated to Syria as per the agreement with GAVI with the COVAX facility, and this will be part of the countrywide cold chain system used for the routine immunization programme with additional support for the COVAX vaccines at central, governorate, and district levels. This is contributing to the resilience and health system strengthening through building strong infrastructure for routine vaccination programme, COVAX vaccines, and any future vaccines that may be needed to respond to public health emergencies.
The project will contribute to infrastructure of the health centers and will strengthen the cold chain system capacity, and will increase the outreach skills and capacity for the national immunization programme. It will also contribute to the improvement of the quality of vaccination services through real-time data collection for monitoring of cold chain and communication interventions.
The project will ensure effectiveness of all received COVAX doses until reaching the final beneficiaries focusing on health workers and elderly people especially those wo has co-morbidity or disability. Disabled women and men will be reached through the outreach mobile vaccination teams and they will have access to the registration and vaccination services. Disabled women and men are among the priority groups for COVAX vaccination. The project also ensures training of health workers on all aspect of COVAX vaccination, and full protection of health workers while performing their duties to vaccinate the targeted population through providing them with personal protective equipment PPE, and prevent and/or mitigate the negative effects of medical waste on human health and the environment. This must be managed in a safe manner to prevent the spread of infection and reduce the exposure of health workers, patients and the public to the risks from medical waste. The project also includes a series of capacity advocacy events (workshops, webinars, orientations) with professional associations, influencers, NGOs, Gov’t officials through partnership agreements with NGOs, professional associations and education institutions for capacity building, community engagement, and crisis communication. This will be done in coordination with RCCE task forces in all hub, including the NES RCCE task force. The local NGOs is playing a vital role in RCCE, and will be supporting all community-based awareness activities and demand generation interventions. The project is including partnership with media agencies to conduct evidence driven media campaigns, development of a communication strategy, evidence generation and behavioral insights to support the implementation of national roll-out action plan.
The project also includes operational activities to transport COVAX vaccine in North-West Syria, monitoring and evaluation for all interventions including for the outreach activities.

4 - Needs

Due to the ongoing 10-year crisis, movement and the corrosion of public services and infrastructure, there is increased risk of outbreaks. More than half the population now lives in poverty with around 8 million people becoming poor since the beginning of the war. As of Jan 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian assistance. Since the first reported case of COVID-19 in 22 March 2020, a total of 16,401 confirmed cases reported with 1,094 deaths as of 14 March 2021, and the transmission of SARS-CoV-2 infection is classified as community transmission, including the healthcare workers. In North-West Syria, a total of 21,175 cases of COVID-19 were reported out of 94,384 tests conducted, including health care workers (76 percent of cases were reported from Idlib, Harim, Afrin and Azaz districts); and 408 deaths reported.UNICEF is a key partner to MOH in the response to COVID-19 pandemic, so far providing technical support and guidance on Training of Trainers (ToTs) for service delivery, Communication for Development (C4D), immunization Supply Chain Management, Waste Management. Also, providing personal protective equipment to all frontline health workers and life-saving equipment to COVID-19 patients in public hospitals. UNICEF also supports COVID-19 related messages to all people in Syria. UNICEF is now stepping in to support procurement, upgrade cold chains, train frontline health workers, and encourage public demand for vaccination. UNICEF will also support in coordination with WHO and partners in NW Syria to deliver COVAX vaccine to health workers, people older than 55 years, and people from 20-60 years with chronic diseases. No specific assessment in health facilities in NES.

5 - Implementation of activities

Syria’s NDVP Plan defines coordination mechanisms, vaccination strategies, target populations, supply needs and supply chain management. Vaccination is planned in 3 stages to vaccinate around 4.5 million people.UNICEF will implement through health facilities of MOH, MOHE, and local NGOs.In coordination with GAVI, UNICEF will procure and deliver COVAX vaccine to the central MOH vaccine stores, while WHO will support distribution to governorates except NWS.Assigned health facilities and 150 mobile teams who will provide COVAX vaccination will be provided with needed cold chain equipment, PPEs, and equipment for safe waste management, in addition to shredding autoclaves in central levels.Communication strategy will be developed in coordination with WHO and local partners. Two workshops will be implemented with stakeholders for planning and validation.Through partnership with media agencies UNICEF will run 3 rounds of behavioral insights evidence generation exercise as a time series to monitor and track the shifts in attitudes and behaviors towards the vaccine, this will help to conduct evidence driven media campaigns for demand generation. Local NGOs will support in RCCE and demand generation activities. UNICEF will monitor cold-chain & supply delivery, and communication & demand generation to promote uptake of COVAX. Therefore, UNICEF monitoring will focus on these two specific areas, to ensure quality & timeliness of support. Also to ensure real-time availability of data, simple checklists, administered through KoBo Toolbox, will be used by UNICEF staff and third party facilitators to collect and analyze data. The C4D questionnaire will be run on three phases, to cover the lifetime of the campaign (6 months).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Exposure to COVID pandemic: healthcare workers affected by COVID, with 691 official cases, including 30 deaths allover Syria. Mitigation measures: Providing HW with PPEs and continuous adhering to physical distancing. Gender Based Violence: risk of GBV is still elevated Mitigation measures: Training for HW in HFs, Synergy with child protection section & UNFPA to ensure availability of the full complement of training, skills & services for HW. Promote recruitment of female health workers by IPs to improve access & utilization by vulnerable women.Sexual Exploitation & Abuse: risk of sexual exploitation & abuse of beneficiaries persists. Mitigation measures: Enforce PSEA policy for all IPs with zero tolerance, with a documented commitment from IPs. Establish community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. Awareness-raising on SEA among IP staff with continuous training & monitoring.Duty of care: It is obligatory to provide physical and mental safety, security and wellbeing to staff. Mitigating measures: Implementation of “duty of care” policies, measures to ensure regular & consistent delivery of salaries, support HW to re-connect with their agencies in case of displacement. Equitable Access to Health Services: UNICEF concentrate on high severity (3-6) areas, target women & children with core interventions. Mitigation measures: Using sector approved vulnerability assessments in planning. Deliver integrated preventive and curative care. Violence against Health Workers: Data on attacks against HW showed 28 attacks in 2020 resulted in 13 killed HW and 42 injured. Mitigation measures: Reporting on attacks through SSA & MRM, continuous advocacy with all parties to respect International Humanitarian Law.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Access, particularly crossline: UNICEF is advocating with high-level authorities to guarantee access to all communities, and assigning responsibilities to the different hubs to reach local agreements with all controlling parties to ensure access. Crossline missions are coordinated with UNOCHA and the RC office. 2. Low acceptance/high hesitancy: C4D and RCCE plans are in place with media campaign for demand generation and increase vaccine acceptance. Implementing partners are playing critical role in disseminating correct messages on the vaccine and stopping the spread of myths.3. Procurement delays for Cold Chain Equipment: CCE assessment conducted to identify additional needed equipment with deployment plan, UNICEF is following-up with supply division in Copenhagen to ensure timely delivery of needed CCEs to all assigned health facilities and mobile vaccination teams 4. Guard against fraud: UNICEF implements programmatic visits & financial spot checks regularly to all IPs. partners are classified for their "risk", high-risk IPs are asked to take corrective action & checked more regularly and reassessed later. 5. Humanitarian principles are upheld and services delivered equally through regular monitoring and orientation of IPs on those principles, continuous follow-up & feedback from beneficiaries. 6. Devaluation of local currency & price increase: This led to increased cost of all commodities including medicines & medical consumables, this will continue in 2021 due to the economic crisis and continuous sanction on Syria. Mitigating measures: UNICEF will increase the salaries for staff working with IPs in coordination with other UN agencies to ensure continuity of service.
