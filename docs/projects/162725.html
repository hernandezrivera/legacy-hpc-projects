Project Summary

Despite all the efforts done in the country, humanitarian needs persist for vulnerable people who have been living in protracted displacement for over a decade. With 16.3% global acute malnutrition (GAM), Sudan has the highest rate of malnutrition in the World Health Organization’s (WHO) Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region. Between 1987 and 2014, stunting and wasting increased from 20% to 33% and 32% to 38.2%, respectively. CRS is being provided the nutrition focused and nutrition sensitive services to address the high malnutrition rates in the country in States of West, Central, East Darfur and Red Sea. These interventions are supported by different donors but to complement these services to the wider vulnerable population in the targeted localities in the afore mentioned States CRS will use this funding to improve access of the community to the nutrition services by improving screening for active case findings, referrals of the identified malnourished children under 5 and PLWs to the treatment sides, enhancing the capacity of the MoH staff providing services at the health facilities and build the capacity of the nutrition volunteers including mothers and caregivers for wider CMAM coverage.

1 - Needs

It was identified that 36.8% of households in Central Darfur experience moderate or severe levels of household hunger and that women’s Dietary Diversity Score is 2 or less for 42% of the population. The high GAM and SAM rates are even higher in Central Darfur and Wester Darfur as compared to the national figures with 18.4% GAM and 13.7% SAM respectively. The situation is worse in some localities with 23% GAM, 17% moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 5.8% SAM respectively and continues to be higher with the continuous influx of the new returnees from Chad and return of IDPs. Only 48% of children are exclusively breast fed (EBF) and 0% of non-breastfeeding U5 children receive the minimal acceptable diet (MAD). On the other hand, under-five mortality rate (U5MR) rate in Central and West Darfur is 77 & 91.4 per 1000 live births, and which is high compared to the national average of 52 per 1000 live births. Antenatal care coverage is low as only 33.99% (CD), and 56% (WD) of pregnant women attended four times visit by a health service provider. There are significant malnutrition rates in East Darfur as well with overall 17.5% of GAM and 6.5% SAM respectively as per HNO 2019. CRS is implementing its nutrition sensitive interventions with a long-term DFID funded project “Taadoud” in West Darfur and further took over the responsibilities from consortium partner exiting from East Darfur. CRS intend to link the CMAM treatment services with its existing nutrition sensitive services for better access to address the malnutrition in East Darfur as well. CRS will continue its nutrition programming in Central, Western and East Darfur and is committed to contribute in addressing the malnutrition situation in Sudan. CRS has been providing the nutrition services in targeted localities in these three states of Darfur and will continue the support in the target locations for 2019-20. The Red Sea state is showing more alarming situation as per HNO 2018 with overall 23.4% GAM rate and 9.4% SAM with 36.5 MAM in PLWs, the situation is also alarming with inadequate nutritional intake during pregnancy, insufficient access to safe water, poor sanitation facilities, and poor hygiene practices contribute to high levels of stunting in children under 2 years of age. In June 2017, an assessment team from CRS conducted interviews with key informants in Red Sea state. In an interview with the head of nutrition department Ministry of Health (MoH), severe malnutrition in the state alarmingly higher as compared to neighboring states. CRS intends to continue this support in Red Sea along with its interventions in Central, West and East Darfur. In the Red Sea State, CRS has started its intervention in 2018 by providing FSL, WASH and nutrition integrated response. Therefore, CRS is requesting support to continue providing lifesaving nutrition services in its all current targeted localities to complement and continue nutrition services with improved access to health, nutrition and WASH with a holistic and integrated approach.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CRS always ensure that during the project design the gender equality and women empowerment in the project interventions shall always be addressed across all sectors including health & nutrition. The project will strengthen the capacities of partners and community-based structures for gender analysis, targeting, collection of sex and age disaggregated data, and promotion of access to basic services for vulnerable women, PLW, adolescent girls & boys, girls with disabilities and ethnic minorities, which will increase their participation and influence decision-making in all stages of the project. The project will work with the Volunteers, partner organization to identify and address any harmful traditional/cultural practices and norms (e.g. FGM/C, early/forced child marriages, etc.) that are barriers to gender equality and women empowerment.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CRS always ensure that all projects are aligned with its MEAL policies. Therefore, CRS utilizes a list of MEAL Policies and Procedures to ensure compliance with CRS polices regarding the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning core functions of implementing our programs. Among these procedures is the establishment of a community feedback and response mechanism. Communities are introduced to various channels through which they can provide complaints, feedback, suggestions, and positive acknowledgements to the project. The Project Officer is the mechanism focal point, and this position has been oriented on the different categories of complaints, as well as how to treat and respond to the low-sensitivity information. For high-sensitivity information, the communication is elevated directly to the senior management.
