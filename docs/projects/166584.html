Project Summary

GBV exacerbation is an indirect consequence of COVID19 infection prevention measures, including restricted movements, increased demand and limited access to public services and basic commodities. Self-quarantine/ isolation and the limited freedom of mobility that generated from the national framework for COVID19 IPC, including lockdown, impacts on women’s and girls’ ability to access basic family resources (e.g. fetching water, accessing food), generating an increase of tensions within the household, which leads to increased risks of exposure to IPV. Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) risks are also exacerbated in the current context, as limited access to resources (food, water, health supplies) due to the limited freedom of movement and high demand can easily generate negative coping mechanisms such as transactional sex. Women and girls with disabilities, women and girls in most drought affected rural areas, refugees, migrants including returnees and key populations are among those most at risk during the COVID-19 pandemic. The national GBV Hotline has recorded an overall increase of over 75 per cent since the beginning of the lockdown, as compared to the pre-lockdown trends. About 94 per cent of the cases are women. The most dominant forms are physical violence (38 per cent of total cases) and psychological violence (38 per cent), followed by economic violence (19 per cent) and sexual violence (5 per cent). About 90 per cent of cases are IPV cases.
Access to GBV services is constraint as GBV service facilities lack COVID19 prevention and infection control equipment. This poses high risks of infection for both Staff and clients. Furthermore, access to GBV services is constrained due to the limited freedom of mobility of the most vulnerable, including in refugee camps. Unavailability of public transport means, as well as reduced access to income sources –with most sectors operations suspended during the lockdown – contributes to de-prioritization of GBV services. The impact of COVID19 on the health system represents an added risk for GBV survivors' Sexual and reproductive health, as some SGBV facilities have seen their space shrink to be reconverted as COVID-19 response centres. The reduced availability/accessibility of GBV services jeopardizes immediate health and physical safety of survivors, and results in impunity for perpetrators if the health and justice sectors are unable to adequately respond. Strengthening the capacity of frontline responders during COVID-19 emergency phase to liaise and refer GBV survivors is also a critical need to ensure the continuous application of a survivor-centred approach and Do-no-harm principles.
UNFPA will respond to the COVID19 GBV indirect needs through 1. Ensuring accessibility of static and mobile GBV services through equipment of facilities with COVID-19 infection prevention and control measures; 2. Scaling up mobile and remote GBV service provision, in order to cater for hardest to reach and those unable to reach static facilities, especially during the lockdown phase; 3. Enhancing information dissemination for COVID-19 and GBV and surveillance, including on GBV risk mitigation, referral pathways, PSEA. 4. Sensitizing non specialized service providers/inter-cluster teams on liaising with GBV survivors and referrals in COVID19 response, in order to ensure do-no harm.
The project aims to reach 323.040 most vulnerable women and girls, men and boys in 26 districts within the 2020 humanitarian Response plan.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

1. Equip 52 mobile One Stop Centres (OSCs), 10 Shelters, and 52 Safe spaces for women and girls with COVID 19 Protective measures (e.g. Hygiene and disinfection products, staff PPE, thermometers, boreholes and solar systems to ensure availability of running water, isolation tents for GBV survivors who are identified as suspected cases, transport support (alternative to public transport) for GBV survivors referral to higher level of care, including for referrals to COVID19 response health facilities 2. Train 600 GBV service provision facilities staff (70% female, 40% male) on COVID19 prevention and control/GBV case management in COVID19 outbreak3. Procure and avail 20,000 COVID-19 customized Dignity kits to all women and girls at safe spaces and GBV service facilities4. Provide MHPSS to 600 GBV specialized personnel (including remote debrief sessions options)5. Scale up Mobile OCS service provision (including COVID19 infection prevention and control measures) (20 additional mobile OSCs set up and operational)6. Scale up GBV hotline capacity – increase of lines, HR and training (40 lines set up to expand existing national hotlines capacity)7. Conduct GBV surveillance, monitoring of COVID19 GBV impact and vulnerability (Safety audits) and referrals – including training of 520 Community volunteers on COVID-19 and GBV impact, PFA and equipping community cadres with airtime and data bundles for enhanced communication and referrals during IPC movement restriction phase.8. Adapt, Print and distribute COVID19 IPC and GBV impact/modified referral pathways/PSEA IEC materials (SMS campaigns/Radio sensitization, including disability friendly materials)9. Train 620 inter-cluster actors on COVID19 and GBV impact, PFA (including remote training on the use of GBV pocked guide to all humanitarian actors involved in COVID19 response)
