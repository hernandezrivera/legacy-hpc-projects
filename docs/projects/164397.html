Project Summary

The general objectives:
- Provision of blood products; (whole blood units, packed units, platelets, fresh frozen plasma)
The purpose:
Covering the need for blood products in secondary health centers, where blood transfusion is a life-saving intervention. This intervention is needed in various medical conditions like; severe bleeding, trauma, ob-gyn, etc. This has a significant impact on reducing mortality in the mentioned cases. Besides, chronic cases are targeted by this project such as thalassemia patients and especially the blood-transfusion dependent cases.
The results of the project envisage to:
- Keep on providing the diagnostic and therapeutic medical support for free to more than 20 hospitals in the targeted areas.
- Raise the medical efficiency of WATAN’s and partners’ staff.
- Fully equipped health centers with well-trained staff capable of providing healthcare services with the best quality.

4 - Needs

Blood bank;For survivors, explosive hazards leave physical and mental scars, but also impact household economies, reducing sources of household income and impacting other household members’ livelihood opportunities. Health needs related to trauma, burn care and rehabilitation remain significant in many parts of the country following years of crisis. 11.5 million people in Syria live at risk of explosive hazards, 23% of which are children. [HNO 2020] There is a significant shortage of blood banks and specialized burn units across the country. In Northern Syria, there were five blood banks, including WATAN 3, the only one specialized in blood diseases. WATAN 3 consists of three departments: a blood bank, a medical test laboratory and a department for treating thalassemia. Currently, there are only 3 banks in Idleb city with a gap of blood products is estimated to be 40% to cover the need of 9 hospitals. The share of each bank is as follows; (WATAN; 80%, Saraqib:15%, and Alatarib: 5%). [WATAN need assessment Dec 2019] On average, 540 units of blood are transfused per month, and more than 1,000 laboratory tests are performed, serving more than 900 beneficiaries. WATAN 3 is performing a critical role when it comes to the treatment of the 123 thalassemia patients by blood transfusion process, providing periodical examinations and tests needed to follow up cases with diseases or epidemics, in addition to offering them medicines.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Gender-Based Violence GBV: - Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, the affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - The pres of female staff in health facilities increases access of women and girls to health services. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: - A PSEA policy describes a standard of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community-based complaint mechanisms, including victim assistance.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Unforeseen changes in the context: Conflict is such that the organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. - Management and mitigation of this risk: -Continue to gather information from a range of sources. -Develop a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues. -Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. 2-Detention / Arrest of the staff: This has been one of the most reoccurring security incidents in Syria since 2011 in the regime, and opposition areas. Detentions can be for any reason or duration. All must be managed on a case-by-case basis. Detention can be both of individuals and of materials. The mistreatment of detainees is a real threat. - Management and mitigation of this risk: Staff tracking and daily check-ins. -Actor mapping and connections established in all areas. -Staff training on visibility and communication practices. 3-Loss of acceptance or registration by Turkish authorities in the destination area: Beginning in the fall of 2014, Turkish governmental relations are good and increasing, for the work in Turkey and in northwest Syria areas controlled by Turkey. Management and mitigation of this risk: -Continue generating options: local procurement, stock-piling inside certain areas in Syria and seeking alternate border possibilities.
