Project Summary

The COVID-19 pandemic has heightened existing psychological distress, child marriage, child labour, increased child trafficking, heightened risks for UASC and the use of negative coping mechanisms by adolescents. The complex protection and security environment and the monsoon affected safe access to services for all children (representing 51% of the total population, including 52% female and 48% male) particularly those with disabilities.
With established child protection agencies working in compliance with CPSS strategy and CPMS, the project will address multiple protection risks faced by refugee children through a comprehensive package of activities for preventive and remedial actions in the best interests of the child, including access to quality case management, specialised services for children at a higher risk considering Age, Gender and Diversity, psychosocial support, resilience and development of especially adolescents through multipurpose centers and community-based child protection structures. Focus will be placed on scaling up and strengthening community child protection mechanisms, with increased participation of community volunteers, children, influential community leaders, parents/ caregivers for ownership and sustainability of the programmes.
Appropriate child friendly feedback mechanisms will be utilised throughout the project cycle in line with AAP principles and mechanisms including consultations and surveys, provision of Psychosocial First Aid jointly with Health and MHPSS teams. Child Protection expertise will be integrated within the Protection Emergency Response Plan for disaster with extension to children of host community.
Some of the outcomes include, but not limited to:
1. Increase in number of adolescents participating in targeted programmes;
2. Reduction in physical violence or psychological aggression experienced by children in home and community;
3. Girls and boys protected against harmful practices;
4. Children accessing safely to community spaces for socialising, play and learning; and
5. Children have non-discriminatory access to national child protection and social services.

1 - Identified Needs

Children make up 51% of the total population, which is 52% female and 48% male. Boys have reported physical and sexual abuse in madrassas. MSNA surveyed refugee households reported increases in child protection issues during the second and third quarters of 2020, with 16% reporting an increase in children going missing, 16% reporting an increase in child labour, and 9% reporting an increase in child marriage. 2020 saw an increasingly complex, protection environment characterized by a deteriorating security environment and heightened protection risks for children due to COVID-19 containment measures, that also resulted as well in the closure of all recreational activities and education and learning centres. With limited access to education and/or livelihood opportunities adolescent boys and girls remain at a high risk with some of them exposed to the worst forms of child labour. With 14% of the population between the ages of 12-17, a lot more has to be done to deliver protection to adolescents and to positively engage them through participation, life-skills, social cohesion and resilience-building.As pointed out by various reports from child protection actors, the COVID-19 pandemic has heightened psychological distress, negatively impacted the incidence of child marriage, child labour, increased the exposure of children to trafficking, and led to the use of negative coping mechanisms by adolescents. Within this context, UASC were exposed to heightened risks of exploitation, girls remained vulnerable to sexual exploitation facilitated by some family members and corrupted community and religious leaders, early pregnancy, and neglect, while boys continued to have limited access to SGBV services and resorted to child labour as a result of the socio-economic impact of COVID-19. The complex protection environment, with deteriorating security environment due to clashes among refugees or between refugees and host communities, the COVID-19 pandemic and the monsoon affected safe access to services for all children, particularly those with disabilities. All these have increased the vulnerability of boys, girls, men and women and place additional strains on children and their families and caregivers. Violence and exploitation remain then concerns in the family as well as in the community. Refugees, especially women and girls, face threats both within the home and in the community. The physical design and infrastructure of camps increase exposure to risks while in public, yet violence also takes place within the home. Additionally, it was noted that participation of children with disabilities was very low, compounded by the fact that there were limited structured activities that integrated the needs of these children. Response to these children’s needs has mainly been through mobile services leading to further isolation of the children. In reflection of COVID-19 pandemic, changing circumstances in the humanitarian response to ensure access to protection services for children and mitigate their protection risks, remote case management modalities were adopted in combination with community-based responses to child protection risks. All activities are implemented within the framework of the RRRC directive and the limitations on access in line with health imperative. Advocacy with the Government to allow refugee children have access to national services, including justice for minor in accordance with the national child policy and applicable laws is ongoing; while the social cohesion between refugees and refugees and host communities had to be strengthened.

2 - Activities or outputs

SO2-Activity 1: Strengthen Child protection community-based approach and mechanisms to prevent and mitigate Child Protection risks through multi-layered, cross-sectoral psychosocial support outreach and parenting sessions; building ownership and sustainability; meaningful participation of the community in case management agency programming, feedback and accountability mechanism through consultations and surveys.SO2-Activity 2: Multipurpose Centres will enable children and adolescents to actively engage in age-appropriate recreational and psycho-social activities in a safe space facilitated by trained staff and volunteers, considering the COVID-19 context. Identified CP cases will be referred to specialized services and mobile services will reach children with disability.SO4- Activity 1: Specialised case management service considering age, gender and diversity, will be provided for girls and boys at risk, appropriate alternative family or community-based care for UASC scaled up, Best Interests Process/Determination (BIP-BID) for complex cases; quality services for children most at-risk including those with disabilities. Urgent child protection needs will be prevented and responded to through CBCPM, caseworkers and in multipurpose centers.SO4-Activity 2: Optimising Child Protection outcomes through meaningful integration with other sectors and various forms of support to caregivers and families of children facing protection risks, to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children. Engagement with the Education Sector and selected partners to ensure adolescents’ access to life-skills, peacebuilding initiatives, individual case management, Adolescents Sexual Reproductive Health, specialized MHPSS. SO4-Activity 3: Streamlining of child referral mechanisms across partners and strengthen Child Protection information management for regular Child Protection trends analysis and evidence-based responses through inter-agency collaboration and CPIMS + reports. SO6-Activity 1: Train, coach social workers and empower local NGOs, community leaders and governance structures, including law enforcement in line with CPMS, UNHCR Framework for the Protection of Children, CPSS and IASC guidelines. Continue to encourage partnerships between local and international NGOs. SO6-Activity 2: PSEA/ PFA- UNHCR has prioritized SEA prevention and response activities with internal SOP and code of conduct. PSEA training will be conducted for staff/partners, SEA assistance and tracking mechanisms strengthened. PSEA and child safeguarding focal points appointed and the Code of Conduct and referral mechanism for complaints displayed in the MPCs.Integrated child protection services in both refugee camps and targeted host community as part of social cohesion activities with focus on peace-building, life-skills and self-reliance trainings, psychosocial support, awareness on protection risks, EPR, mitigation measures and referral pathways for identified host community children at risk developed and strengthened. Disaster Risk Reduction and Environment- UNHCR will harness community participation during disasters, work to prevent family separations in emergencies and develop referral pathways for reunification in mass displacement. Evaluation of the safety of facilities and repair of those affected.Contingency in case of natural disaster: UNHCR will support strengthening the emergency response plans and complement the communities’ response during disasters by actively participating in the PERUs, Medical Response Teams and Emergency Response Teams to mitigate risk for children with CBCPCs participating in repairs of facilities, identification and referral of people in need. Mainstream COVID-19 prevention through community-based child protection structures in raising awareness. Ensure compliance with containment measures during all activities. Volunteers will be deployed to monitor and provide PSS if required for children in quarantine centres.
