Project Summary

Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) is a human right and, like all other human rights, applies to refugees, internally displaced persons, and others living in humanitarian settings. To realize this right, affected populations (Women, men, young boys and girls) must have access to comprehensive SRH information and services so they are free to make informed choices about their health and well-being. The Minimum Initial Services Package - MISP - for Sexual and Reproductive Health care will be delivered by UNFPA and partners as essential elements to reduce the risk of maternal/ neonatal mortality and morbidity among the affected population. The planned interventions will prioritize the ensuring of timely access to Antenatal and postnatal care, women nutritional services, Emergency Obstetric care and save delivery. In addition to support of the referral system, capacity building of healthcare providers on Sexual Reproductive health, availability and accessibility of Family Planning services, HIV and STIs prevention and response program, Clinical management of SGBV survivor’s, and demand creation through awareness raising and educational activities. The coordination of SRH programs in humanitarian is also prioritized at national and states level. The UNFPA project will consider the needs for youth and adolescent (boys & girls) through the Youth Peer program aiming to build the capacity of Y Peers to design and deliver high-quality peer-to-peer education on issues affecting young people in humanitarian, including adolescent youth Reproductive health and gender-based violence, empowerment skills and partnership between youth and adults. Maternal morbidity and disability such as Obstetric fistula in fragile states will be given an especial attention by UNFPA. The SRH service package will be operationalized through the procurement of Emergency Reproductive Health Kits, micro-nutrients, medical equipment and supplies. UNFPA will coordinate closely with WHO, UNICEF, (I)NGOs and Ministries of Health during the program implementation period. In addition Gender and protection mainstreaming will be considered as critical cross cutting programs. The natural impact on environment with mitigation and enhancement procedures is considered by UNFPA.

Needs

Out the 5.7 million persons in need of basic services - Sudan in HRP 2019, the estimated number of Women at Reproductive Age is 1,425,000 on need for timely Reproductive health services. Near to 228,000 pregnant women, need antenatal care, support for safe vaginal delivery, and postnatal care services. Out of the total number of pregnant women, 34,200 expected to develop obstetrical and gynecological complications, and need timely access to basic and comprehensive emergency obstetrical and neonatal services, including referral to higher-level health facilities and C Section. So far 1,593, 750 WRA on need to use modern contraceptives which to be considered as priority within the integrated SRH service delivery. From protection side around 28,500 women, girls and boys expected to be victims of sexual violence. The gap in the provision of Basic Obstetric and Neonatal Care services was reflected in the HeRAMS June 2018 report for Darfur region, which reported that only 28% of HFs in Darfur region provide BEONC, up to 72% of the HFs do not provide the service. The referral capacity: referral procedures, means of communication, transportation still a great challenge in East Darfur (28%), South Darfur (32%) and North Darfur (55%). The 2017 HERAMs report also showed the poor number of health facilities that provided Clinical management of rape survivors (including psychological support) that only (19%) North Darfur, (17%) South Darfur and (21%) East Darfur and (14%) Central Darfur provided the services. Anemia related conditions are one of the main indirect causes of maternal deaths in Sudan (14.55%) as reported by the 2017 Maternal Death Surveillance and Response FMOH, 2017.The national EMONC assessment that targeted 631 health facilities in Sudan - governmental, private, non-private (NGOs) - showed critical gaps in the maternal and neonatal health services that need timely and quality of interventions. According to the survey results, only 121 EMONC facilities were fully functioning (31%), leaving a gap of 275 EmONC facilities. The availability of EmONC facilities was severely fell short and unequally distributed against the recommended in fragile states with humanitarian nature, in East, South and North Darfur, Gedarif, North and South Kordofan, Kassala, Blue Nile, and Red Sea; all had below (21%) EmONC facilities functioning. The proportion of expected births in all facilities was (23%). Lowest proportion of births was observed in East Darfur (one percent), South Darfur and Blue Nile (2 percent each). Young people, especially young girls, are often left out although they are among the most vulnerable as well as resourceful populations. A KAP survey among youth population in Darfur conducted by UNFPA in 2017, highlighted major gaps in knowledge and practices related to SRH, FP, GBV and HIV/AIDS. According to the relevant conclusion and recommendations of this study, the proposed youth component will address the needs of youth SRH.

Activities

As women and girls have specific risks during pregnancy and delivery, making the provision of lifesaving reproductive health related services of the utmost importance in emergency situation, the planned activities designed to respond timely to women, men, young girls and boys, SRH needs. UNFPA SRH integrated interventions will be implemented jointly with qualified partners at states and national level. The planned activities include: 1. Support the SRH sub sector coordination mechanism at states and national level to ensure the provision of SRH information/ Data, raising strategic and technical issues and concerns.2. Strengthening national/ states contingency planning and preparedness focusing on Minimum Initial Service Package on RH in Crisis (MISP) and scaling up of MISP.3. Capacity building of health care providers through formal training, supportive supervision, on-the-job training and mentor ship on relevant SRH topics including EMONC, FP, STI management, HIV in humanitarian, Clinical Management of Rape survivors and psycho-social support, etc. 4. Support referral system through enhancement of locally initiated referral system, to all emergency obstetric cases. 5. Rehabilitation and equipping of health facilities including delivery rooms, maternity words, blood transfusion unit and OBS theaters.6. Support the establishment of Family Planning model centres within selected health facilities inside the camps. 7. Establishment of integrated SRH mobile teams at states with fragile context.8. Procurement and delivery of emergency reproductive health kits, micronutrients, equipment and supplies.9. Support Obstetric Fistula treatment campaigns in states with high humanitarian burden.10. Support demand creation and awareness raising on reproductive health seeking behavior.11. Address Youth/ adolescent RH needs focusing on their specific needs and vulnerabilities in humanitarian settings.12. Conduct evidence based researches and studies on SRH.

Impact on environment/natural resources

UNFPA’s work on sexual and reproductive health, education, and violence prevention helps promote resilience among vulnerable populations. Access to voluntary family planning, maternal health care and quality education are key ways to empower vulnerable women and young people, making them better equipped to prepare for and respond to environmental -related crises. UNFPA aims to make a positive contribution in the area of environmental impact mitigation. At the procurement, level the organization works with suppliers (International and national levels) to implement sound environmental impact provisions. Manufacturers working with UNFPA have active and documented waste-water programmes and have agreed to reduce raw material usage. UNFPA also produced and disseminates a guide for safe disposal and management of unused unwanted commodities.Throughout the project, planning and implementation process UNFPA will closely monitoring any mitigation measures that needed to avoid, reduce or compensate for environmental impacts to enhance the environmental and community benefits of the proposal.
