Project Summary

The emergency this project will address is the high incidence of water related disease in underserved payams in Fangak and in Nyirol. Those worst affected are children 6-59 months with SAM and PLW with MAM. The in-depth inter-sectoral severity of need analysis covering WASH, health, nutrition, FSL and GBV rates priority concern as very high in both Fangak and Nyirol (WASH Presentation, November 15, 2022, slide 5). Affected payams are Manajang, Greater Pulita, Phom and Mareang in Fangak. In Nyirol County worst flood affected payams are Chuil and Pading. The extreme needs in Chuil and Pading payams warrant inclusion in the 2022 WASH HRP. The population of the project catchment area is 123,055 - 112,024 in Fangak (59% of payam total) and 62,732 in Nyirol (38% of payam total).
The critical driver of this WASH emergency is flooding over the past 3 consecutive years. Flooding caused displacement, damaged facilities, service disruptions and access barriers. WASH infrastructure has been destroyed forcing community reliance on surface water and a serious public health risk has developed. Failing to respond to this emergency will result in water related disease transmission with increased morbidity of malnourished children and PLW, and poor outcome performance of nutrition interventions.
This project will scale up an integrated response focused on WASH in nutrition to reduce disease transmission at the household level. The project will provide WASH NFI, hygiene promotion and emergency sanitation and water supply to break disease transmission.
The project will deliver the 3 assistance packages as summarized in the South Sudan WASH – HRP 2022. The focus will be on children under 5 with SAM, their caretakers and
PLW. From 16 static facilities, the project will deliver:
1. The nutrition facility package to all facilities;
2. The community package reaching 44,000 men, women boys and girls, including the most vulnerable female headed households, isolated IDP communities, the disabled and elderly;
3. The cross-cutting package consisting of do-no-harm conflict sensitivity practices, AAP, food resilience; gender appropriate rehabilitation of facilities, water-points and latrines and measures to mitigate SGBV in relation to program delivery.
CMA will engage co-located partners to fill in sector gaps. Co-located humanitarian partners in Fangak are NPA, ADA and ICRC in FSL, CMA and HFO in Health, CMA in Nutrition and FCA in Protection. Co-located humanitarian partners in Nyirol are Oxfam in FSL, CMA in Health and Nutrition and NP in Protection.
Key target estimates are:
1.0 1,311 SAM children/caretakers and PLW provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion
2.0 42,824 people reached through household water treatment (temporary assistance)
3.0 29,574 people reached with safe water other than household water treatment
4.0 Five water points and 10 latrine stances rehabilitated with a food-resilient design;
5.052,910 people reached with soap AND hygiene promotion.
The project will conduct gender analysis to clarify how the WASH emergency affects women, girls, boys and men differently, and to effectively respond to the WASH needs of affected populations. The project will mainstream gender and protection by:
1.0 Involving women and adolescent girls in project planning, implementation and monitoring;
2.0 Conducting safety audits and implement strategies that increase safety and accessibility to all humanitarian services;
3.0 Implementing strategies to meet the protection needs of most-at-risk groups including disabled persons, adolescent girls and U5 children;
4.0 Ensuring an appropriate gender balance in recruitment of frontline staff;
5.0 Ensuring the SGBV referral pathway for victims is clear to all staff and beneficiaries; Raising awareness on SGBV and the need for protection to ensure the most-at-risk groups have unimpeded access to project services will be strengthened.

1 - Needs

The in-depth inter-sectoral severity of need analysis covering WASH, health, nutrition, FSL and GBV rates priority concern as very high in both Fangak and Nyirol (WASH Presentation, November 15, 2022, slide 5). This project will address the high incidence of water related disease. Populations worst affected are children 6-59 months with SAM and PLW with MAM. Flooding, insecurity and the economic crisis have negatively impacted WASH services. These shocks have caused increased severity and number of people in need of WASH services. Worst affected payams are Manajang, Greater Pulita, Phom and Mareang in Fangak. The WASH PiN is 133460 in Fangak (68% of total county population) and the U5 GAM rate 22.6% and PLW MAM rate of 53.3% (NC PiN Targets for 2023, 10 Nov 22) and CMA’s health data shows the AWD incidence rate for U5 children is 13% for boys and girls. In Nyirol County, the payams of Chuil and Pading are critical. Both these payams exhibit the critical factors that create WASH severity of need of very high concern - 3 consecutive years of flooding, a high risk of disease outbreak, U5 GAM rate ≥ 19%, PLW MAM rate at 71.9.3% (NC PiN Targets for 2023, 10 Nov 22) and CMA’s health data shows the AWD incidence rate for U5 children is 18% for boys and 20% for girls. The WASH PiN is 28449 in Nyirol (20% of total county population). Chuil and Pading payams represent a majority of this PiN. Flooding was the most severe shock driving the current WASH crisis. Flooding caused displacement, loss of food-crops and livestock, damaged health and nutrition facilities causing service disruptions and access barriers for children, PLW, the elderly and disabled. Water-points and latrines have been destroyed. Virtually every household relied on surface water during the foods. The displaced are most seriously affected in this way. Households with access to latrines have been shown to be less than 10% (South Sudan HNO 2022, pg 91). Lack of soap remains a critical barriers to preventing the spread of water related disease. Given the common practice of open defecation, a serious public health risk has developed because of the absence of soap for hygiene and lack inputs to treat the water before consumption. The locations of Juaibor in Manajang Payam, Keew in Pulita Payam and Nyadin in Mareang Payam have experienced severe flooding for 3 consecutive years. In Pading, high rainfall has been the main factor causing flooding and in the Sobat River with drainage coming off the Ethiopian highlands has also flooded the past 3 consecutive years. This has destroyed WASH infrastructure and the health and nutrition facilities and produced the same public health risk as in Fangak. The destruction of WASH infrastructure has impacted women and adolescent girls differently than other population groups. Adding to the morbidity that affects everyone, women and adolescent girls have experienced greater exposure to GBV. Given long distance to access safe drinking water, women and girls feel less safe and choose to avoid using safe water sources in favor of surface sources (FSNMS 2022). Distances to access safe drinking water and the absence of safe latrines has contributed to increased rates of SGBV. This analysis indicates the critical WASH needs are: 1) integration of WASH with nutrition to reduce recurrence of malnutrition and raise awareness of hand hygiene to prevent water related disease outbreaks; 2) increased access to safe water and sanitation services in health and nutrition facilities and communities; 3) strengthened presence of gender analysis to determine appropriate SGBV mitigations measures and sustainable community-based protection measures.

2 - Project activities

This project will directly contribute to the reduction of WASH related morbidity and mortality in the context of flood damaged WASH infrastructure with focus on selected sites that exhibit WSC of 4 and 5 of Fangak and the Chuil payam of Nyirol counties. This overall objective will be achieved through the activities summarized below. The details of the output indicator are proved in section on WASH Indicator Targets.Objective 1: Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition.Activities: 1.1 Distribution of standard WASH kits to SAM children and PLW/caretakers with hygiene promotion; 1.2 Rehabilitate facility-based water points to provide secure water safety for nutrition facilities and community users;1.3 Provide NFI and chlorination with aquatabs maintaining a residual chlorine concentration ≤ 0.5 mg/L;1.4 Rehabilitate facility based latrines for use of attendees for nutrition services; 1.5 Provide soap and hand washing buckets at the facility level;1.6 Train facility nutrition personnel on hand washing and train pump mechanics on the operation and maintenance of facility-based boreholes for sustainability. Objective 2: Contribute to reducing exposure to WASH related protection threats, and improve safe, gender-appropriate and dignified access to WASH services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action.Objective 2.1: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties.Activities: 2.1.1 Promote sanitation and hygiene behavior change with training of community members through gender-balanced teams of frontline health and nutrition personnel and community-based hygiene promoter;2.2.2 Distribution of soap and hand washing NFIs like bucket, Jerri can with tap etc. for community members; 2.1.3 Rehabilitation of water point and provide NFIs and aquatabs for chlorination to secure water safety for community users;2.1.4 Rehabilitation of latrines for community use with gender safety measures included and promote safe practices for human excrement disposal. Objective 2.2: WASH specific activities for GBV (or mitigating WASH GBV)Activities:2:2.1 Conduct safety audits of community water-points and latrines, develop the safety plan for construction/rehabilitation;2.2.2 Construct/rehabilitate gender-appropriate pit-latrines at community centers like schools, churches and market centers;2.2.3 Provide menstrual hygiene management kits for the most vulnerable women and girls of adolescence age and train them how to make menstrual hygiene management using the local material for sustainability. Objective 3: Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructureActivities 3.1 Apply elevated flood-resilient designs to rehabilitation of gender-appropriate institutional water-points to serve health and nutrition facilities, schools, churches and markets centers;3.2 Apply elevated flood-resilient design to rehabilitation of gender-appropriate institutional pit latrines to serve health and nutrition facilities, schools, churches and markets centers.

3 - Project indicators

Planning: The baseline data for WASH sector has been derived from three main sources: 1) the 2022 HNO, WASH Cluster guidance on populations in need, targets at national and county levels rated in Severity Levels 4 and 5; 2) the experience and data CMA has gained through ongoing nutrition and health programming with on-ground presence, regular consultations with targeted communities and affected populations, and 3) analysis of data captured in IRNA and other WASH sector technical reports. Combined, these sources provide the best information for needs-based planning for objectives, activities and estimating realistic output targets. CMA’s on-ground static presence in all locations targeted is critical in the planning process as this provides opportunity for consultations with targeted communities through regular community meetings, FGD and KII, and with local authorities and co-located humanitarian partners through meetings of county-based forums and with sector departments at the county, state and federal levels. The six key indicators for measurement of success of this project are: 1) Number of children admitted for SAM treatment discharged from OTP/SC locations with WASH/Hygiene kit; 2) Number of nutrition facilities with improved access to sanitation and access to safe water; 3) Number of people reached through household water treatment (temporary assistance for 3 months); 4) Number of people reached with safe water other than household water treatment and sanitation facilities; 5) Number of women and girls with improved access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit; 6) Number of water points and latrines stances rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design. Monitoring Implementation: CMA will use the following tools to monitor implementation of project activities: 1) Monthly reports from project sites providing data focused on output achievement comparing actual with planned achievement with all data always disaggregated on the basis of gender and age; 2) Quarterly activity reports from project sites providing data on activities including trainings, community meetings and other potential progress impeding contextual details not included in monthly reports; 3) Regular meetings (monthly/quarterly) and consultations with affected populations (women, men, girls and boys) to ensure their direct participation in monitoring project progress, community access to services, the effectiveness of services, the safety of service delivery and the utilization and effectiveness of the complaints mechanism; 4) Quarterly field monitoring reports delivered from CMA’s county and Juba-based monitoring and technical management team. CMA’s monitoring approach will endeavor to collect information for the measurement of success, but also as aiding in decision-making regarding needed adjustments and modifications in approach and delivery as the project progresses. CMA will ensure the project data system yields information that is unambiguous, verifiable and consistent with national cluster data systems. Evaluation: From the planning, implementation and monitoring, CMA will derive the main data for evaluating the success of this project and to ensure accountability to affected populations. This foundation of evaluative data will be complemented with information directly obtained from affected populations and the beneficiaries of the project. CMA will use participatory tools including FGD, KII and community meetings, and the input from local authorities and county-based sector department M&E personnel to complete the information for internal project evaluation. These qualitative evaluation tools will help CMA assess and learn the impacts of awareness and behavior change communication, issues related to GBV and protection, effectiveness of outreach and mobile approaches and the resiliency of project results when delivered in the integrated and cross-sector approaches.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Implementation of this project will be fully integrated with CMA’s Nutrition services. In addition to a cadre of hygiene promoters, CMA will utilize the Nutrition frontline workers especially CNVs to ensure that individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls have unimpeded access to the project’s feedback mechanisms. Through these channels, these vulnerable community members will be able to provide feedback on project interventions and submit complaints. Further, these same channels will enable the disabled and other vulnerable groups to actively participate in program decision-making for the purpose of tailoring interventions to the different needs of these most vulnerable population groups. CMA’s on-ground teams will ensure that at least 75% of affected populations are able to utilize the complaints mechanism by practicing the principles of transparency, communication and provision of information linked to the complaints mechanism. These frontline workers will engage the vulnerable target populations including the disabled to proactively seek out their feedback. In addition to the feedback that is gathered through ongoing implementation, the project will conduct monthly meetings with local leaders, women, adolescent girls and boys and disabled individuals to obtain their feedback. The Facility Senior Officer will conduct these consultations providing the most vulnerable of affected populations full awareness and opportunity to participate in monitoring the project, to learn their entitlements for accessing project services and render complaints. Noting that an estimated 15% of the population is disabled and in specific reference to serving persons with disabilities, the project will use the six questions that comprise the WGSS with focus on the core functional domains of seeing, hearing, walking, cognition, self-care and communication to determine the level of functionality on the four level difficulty response scale. This tool will be used to help determine how best to serve the disabled population. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated by gender, type of disability and level of difficulty experienced by the beneficiary to better target assistance and the most appropriate means of delivering this assistance. The Facility Senior Officer will ensure that the most vulnerable disabled, illiterate, women and children are included in feedback consultations, FGD and KII sessions. At all sites, the affected population groups that CMA consults in the planning and feedback processes are as follows: (1) local women and men leaders, focus groups and key informant women and men; and (2) social groups representing the most vulnerable including individuals living with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Needs assessment in new locations and ongoing thereafter ensures that project activities are tailored to the different needs of communities especially adolescent mothers, new IDPs, the elderly and disabled. Information is gender and age disaggregated so interventions can be suitability tailored to the different needs of population groups. The strategy will ensure the structures and mechanisms established enable affected populations to participate actively in planning, learning their rights and entitlement, receive information they need to make informed decisions on accessing services, provide feedback on interventions, submit complaints and participate in program decision-making. Monthly meetings with local leaders, women, adolescent girls and boys will be conducted to report on project outputs and obtain feedback. The CMAM Supervisors will conduct these consultations providing the most vulnerable of affected populations full awareness and opportunity to participate in planning and monitoring the project and to learn their entitlements for accessing project services and render complaints. CMA’s on-ground teams will ensure that at least 75% of affected populations are able to utilize the complaints mechanism by practicing the principles of transparency, communication and provision of information linked to the complaints mechanism, and engaging the target populations to proactively seek out their feedback

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CMA has completed the rollout of its PSEA policy completing training of management and facility based frontline personnel. Additional PSEA training is planned for new workers through on-the-job training for all project workers. Part of the training covers dissemination which will be conducted through meetings with community groups and local authorities. The dissemination will be led by the designated officer at the facility level. Further, the project team and facility-based health and nutrition workers will ensure the populations they serve are thoroughly informed about PSEA. Beneficiaries will also be advised on: (1) the content of the PSEA policy; (2) available channels for reporting abuse; (3) confidentiality rules in reporting abuse cases; and (4) the mechanisms and locations available for victims of abuse to access support and treatment

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The Facility Senior Officer will coordinate the engagement with communities and actively seek their feedback. The consultation activities will be recorded and reported monthly to CMA’s County Officers, the M&E Officers of the CHD and CMA project management team. These measures will ensure that an effective system of representation of affected populations is in place for the purpose of improving project results. Further, the Project Manager will conduct supervisory visits to the project (once per quarter) to conduct monitoring and evaluation so that regular assessment of beneficiary knowledge on progress and their satisfaction with performance is collected and new measures put in place as required based on feedback. Further, the Project Manager will conduct meetings with local leaders of host and IDP communities and local authorities to verity that effective implementation and robust monitoring of the complaints mechanism is in place.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1 To mitigate this High risk, CMA will employ mobile and outreach approaches to go to the locations where at-risk populations live in order to reach beneficiaries in a safe way. Further, the project will promote community-based strategies and practices to provide protection for the most vulnerable community members (children, PLW, IDPs and disabled). The project will engage men and women leaders to take responsibility for mobilizing protection for disadvantaged and vulnerable individuals, so that they have unimpeded access to services. To adhere to the principles of “Do-No-Harm”, the project will strive to deliver services in a balanced manner so that IDP and host community populations will have equal access to project services. To achieve this balance, CMA will implement a strong awareness campaign to engage both IDP and host community leaders.8.2 High Risk: To mitigate this risk CMA will conduct community assessments for delivery of services from static facilities, and apply the “do-no-harm” approach to reduce the risk sexual of exploitation and abuse. The project will conduct safety audits at all static facilities and water-points, and implement all recommended measures to improve safe access to services. The safety audits will be led by CMA Safety Audit Focal Person, and implemented by female staff members in respect of common protection protocols. These measures will ensure access and minimize exposer of women and girls to violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse as they access to project assistance.8.4 To mitigate this high risk, the project sites will be linked to health services where CRM service will be provided for victims of SGBV including psychological support for the trauma experienced by child survivors of conflict and of SGBV. Through promotion and awareness sessions, the wider community will be made aware of the appropriate referral pathways for the services that survivors need. Further, local authorities and community leaders will be made aware of available protection assistance and the GBV referral pathway for CMR and psychological support for survivors of SGBV.8.5 This High risk will be mitigated by adopting strategies to ensure communities: (1) have information they need to make informed decisions on accessing services, (2) can provide feedback on the project intervention and submit complaints. As women, girls, boys, the disabled and elderly are primary targets of this project, and to ensure they are all included in consultations and decision-making, CMA will organize specific forums (KIIs, FGDs) and meetings to engage these vulnerable groups. This will include engaging women’s groups, and women community leaders in consultation and decision making in the planning, throughout the implementation and at evaluation. To reach the disabled and elderly, CMA will conduct interviews and consultation with these groups to ensure their situational context, needs and limitations/barriers are understood and accounted for in project planning and implementation decision-making

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Current data shows the 2021 flood related displacement in Fangak was 115,000 people and in Nyirol it was 25,000. Most of the displaced moved because of the destruction of houses, health/nutrition and WASH infrastructure. This project will make a direct contributions to lowering the percentage of flood related displacements by establishing flood resilient facilities and WASH infrastructure, specifically:1. community water-points that are flood resilient serving 18,500 people. This projects contribution to the national target is about 2.5%

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

At present, Fangak is rated at severity of need level Extreme and Niyrol is rated at severity of need level Severe for GBV. Displacement caused by flooding and intercommunal conflict are the main causes of the high severity for GBV. In relation to provision of the minimum package of services and activities for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV to achieve safe and timely access to services for case management and psychosocial support including capacity to provide such services, this project will directly contribute to mitigation of GBV in the following way: 1. 20,489 women and girls will have improved access to water and/or sanitation after safety audits are completed and GBV awareness and protection methods instituted at the facility and community level. 14 static facilities will be the target for the measures set out above with a catchment population estimated at 123,055 which is about 1.4% of the national target.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

At present, both Fangak and Nyirol counties are classified in IPC 4 with Fangak projected to deteriorate to IPC 5 by May 2022. Toward reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero), this project will address water related disease transmission and improve the outcome of nutrition interventions by breaking the disease transmission cycle. The project will make 3 direct contributions:1. 1,311 SAM children/caretakers and PLW will be provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion;2. 42,824 people reached through household water treatment;3. 29,574 people reached with safe water other than household water treatment; These measures combined with nutrition services will help reduce the number of people in IPC 4 and IPC 5 which is about 2.6% of total national target.
