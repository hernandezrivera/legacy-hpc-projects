Project Summary

HDPO is planning to intervene in North and South Darfur States ( Gerieda, Katila, Kas, Elfasher ,Saraf Omra and Tawila) focusing mainly on addressing the most critical protection needs and gaps for IDPs , returnees, host communities, women, men, boys and girls, PWSNs, elderly and disabilities who are exposed to risks of violence, exploitations to enable them to track incidents and reduce effects of violence, exploitations, abuse through that have escalated following the outbreak of war in Sudan on 15th April,2023 through GBV, General protection and child protection activities.
The three project components are : Gender Based Violence (GBV), General Protection (GP) and Child Protection (CP). The GBV activities will include conducting mass public community awareness raising campaigns on GBV, Provision of specialized GBV services, establish women centers (semi-permanent) using local materials , provide support women and girls with startup capital for Income Generating Activities , provision of women dignity kits giving priorities to vulnerable women and girls in reproductive age. HDPO will provide capacity building on GBV response services focusing on comprehensive GBV prevention interventions and response services for survivors, conflict-related sexual violence and addressing multiple forms of GBV occurring amongst the population. HDPO will conduct a GBV assessment targeting women , girls, children and people with disabilities, and their caregivers about the risks of GBV in their communities, potential barriers to accessing response services and participating in programs and activities, and their suggestions for improving GBV programs . The second component is general protection which includes referral of people with specific needs to specialized services. In line with General protection, HDPO will establish and support General protection, Community-Based Structures and protection monitoring will be conducted. Furthermore, site management briefings for government and partners and capacity development including government officials and other duty bearers, community representatives and humanitarian staff, will be train in protection issues and safe and inclusive service delivery and conduct awareness raising and community outreach activities on general protection. The third component is child protection where girls and boys are provided with specialized child protection response services, including case management for child survivors of sexual violence, child labor, child recruitment, UASCs and children in need for alternative care. Address the root causes of child protection risks among the population to reduce the likelihood of abuse, neglect, exploitation, or violence against children, including child labor, child recruitment, separation, sexual violence, child marriage, FGM, are implemented and identify and address specific sources of threat and/or vulnerabilities of children as being at particularly high risk of abuse, neglect, exploitation, or violence. Additionally, provide vulnerable and at-risk children with gender & age appropriate (structured) psychosocial support and life skills including literacy and numeracy and support vulnerable caregivers and caregivers of vulnerable boys and girls with parenting skills, family strengthening programs and multi-sectoral support as well as mobilize communities to support children through activation of child protection networks and community volunteers. HDPO will also use social media, and radio to reach various segments of the community. It is expected to reach at least 19000 persons through this activity directly and indirectly. HDPO is planning to target in this project approximately29,400 persons (men/6000, women/7900, girls/ 8500 and boys/ 7000) including IDPs and host communities, newly affected persons, people with special needs, most vulnerable women, elderly persons and other conflicted affected people within the areas of our responsibility.

1 - Needs

In Sudan, general protection, child protection, and Gender Based Violence (GBV) are a key concern within the different communities affected by different emergencies and this has worsened after the war and tribal clashes after 15th April in Sudan . Women, girls and children amongst the IDP communities and host communities are especially at risk. Civilians' vulnerability, particularly women and children, to protection risks such as GBV, family separation, child recruitment, and psychosocial and physical harm have increased. Insecurity and fighting have led to more child abuse, neglect, exploitation while reports indicate that many are being recruited into armed groups while others are targeted because of their ethnicity by militias .The GBV risks among IDPs and the vulnerable host communities has increased with the outbreak of the conflict aggravated by inadequate lighting in camps and settlements, overcrowding, and limited access to energy and water supply gaps that require women and girls to travel long distances to collect water and firewood, exposing them to harassment and violence which has increased cases of rape, SEA and domestic violence . Survivors of GBV lack adequate access to quality specialized lifesaving services, reporting mechanisms, and legal/justice interventions with the majority of Darfur regions institutions having closed due to insecurity. services (such as clinical management of rape, specialized psychosocial support, and counselling, legal aid, and case management) have become scarce . This is also confirmed in the findings from “The Voices from Sudan” report. 19 per cent of respondents of this study perceived domestic violence and sexual violence as the most common GBV incident happening in their community. Displacements have also further contributed to the breakdown of the community self-protection systems putting women and girls at risk. The conflicts in South and North Darfur leaves serious protection issues, putting extra burden on the lives of the vulnerable people especially people with special needs across community . Hence this project aims to support those categories and provide the essential interventions. HDPO works in North and South Darfur with IDPs and host community. Services in the IDP camps are severely limited, particularly regarding knowledge and awareness. In regard to children, majority can be found wandering within the camp, leaving them more vulnerable and exposed to protection risks like exploitation and child recruitment. Per unpublished records from agencies in affected areas, children in 4 out of 5 families are subject to violence, abuse, and/or harmful cultural practices, such as child abandonment, neglect, child marriage, corporal punishment, and GBV. Effects for girls can be as serious as unwanted pregnancy and miscarriage, child mortality, STIs, and serious injuries. Children are also less likely to be sent to school when they must travel through highly insecure areas daily, coupled with transportation costs and the undervaluing of girls’ education. 58% of community members do not know the exact age to get a girl married. Moreover, the majority of Sudanese nationals do not have identification documents like birth certificates. Many men have left the camps for war and to look for money to support their families, resulting in women assuming households’ heads' responsibility. The number of female-headed households among IDPs is about 31%, much higher than that of host communities (22%) and settled communities. As a result of these pressures, many women find it difficult to function psychologically and socially. In cases of child-marriage, women, as well as the children, do not have decision-making powers. 70% of female respondents mentioned that the decision to marry depends completely on the father or other male family members, leaving out women, who are most likely to know the interests of the child in terms of needs, challenges, and priorities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Objective 1: Improved capacity and resilience of children to cope with negative effects of violence, war and conflict. –Tracking of vulnerable boys and girls using virtual and outreach methods and raising awareness about existence of child protection services and information on referrals provided. –child abuse, neglect, and exploitation threats and risks identified and children mostly vulnerable assisted. –communities mobilised and child protection networks established/ strengthened/activated to support child protection.-PSS provided to children to deal with trauma and mental health.- Boys and girls provided with enhanced protection response involving case management for survivors of sexual abuse, separated children, child labor,forced armed recruitment, exploitation.-Enhancement of resource capacity to deal with child protection issues by training volunteers and creating child friendly spaces, recreational facilities provided.Objective 2: Provide timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity and the main activities are -Procure and distribute dignity kits for emergency response - Provide structured and sustained family based psychosocial support services, community engagement and awareness -Capacity building activities, this includes mentoring and coaching, for government, HDPO staff and community-based Health care givers - Involvement of the peacebuilding elements in protection through ensuring the women and young leaders participate and benefit from the opportunities. Objective 3: Provide protection services to vulnerable crisis-affected people, particularly those with special needs this includes; -Assisting People with special needs, disabilities and vulnerable groups especially girls and boys; -Identification and referral of the persons with specific needs within South and North Darfur to appropriate services and assistance; -Conduct psycho-social support and awareness sessions targeting people affected by conflict ie IDPs and, host communities focusing on the elderly, people with special needs; -Mapping out specialized services provided by the humanitarian agencies through community centre’s; Case management of those exposed to risk with focus on women; -Targeted individual assistance to help those who can’t hear or blind to get access to services; Objective 4: Strengthen community-based protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to protection risks where feasible; -Identify and train women, adolescents, children and religious leaders’ committees in the targeted localities /villages. - Conduct training on Human Rights in general and group wise rights targeting local different community structures and CBOs in North and South Darfur -Arrange awareness session and social dialogue on various protection issue to promote community-based protection mechanism and improve social cohesion between IDPs and affected host communities. - Conduct monthly dialogue with the trained institutions to monitor their adherence to guidelines, procedures regarding the protection and Human rights standards to increase immediate response to protection concerns. Under GBV activities will include: 1- conduct community awareness sessions on GBV 2- Establish of Women friendly Centers (permanent/semi-permanent) 3- selected most vulnerable women including women with special needs, disabilities, separated women, widows to be trained / supported with start-up capital. 4- Number of specialized GBV service providers (women or men) trained on GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and/or response 5- HDPO will several conduct assessments and some people reached by specialized GBV services 6- Number of community members (women and men) trained provided with dignity kits 7- Mass Awareness campaigns conducted on open spaces (schools, markets, etc..) discussing several issues related to GBV, harmful traditions 8-GBV specialized services to women and girls

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The activities and implementation design considered the impact on the environment with a mitigation and enhancement process, the planned women centers; if supported - will be rehabilitated using to the extent possible the no environmentally harmful materials. The promotion of fuel-efficient stoves - if supported - will contribute to the improved livelihood of vulnerable households while reducing environmental impact as fewer trees are cut for cooking fuel. In addition, during the dignity kits distribution, women and girls will be sensitized to environmentally friendly ways of disposing of the used sanitary wear. Through the women and girls’ empowerment program, the GBV AoR partners will explore the production of reusable sanitary pads as a replacement for disposable ones.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is core in the HDPO response and will be strengthened through continuous consultation with the affected population on the response modalities as well as their satisfaction with services provided. In addition, HDPO is an active member of the national PSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team and together is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanisms are accessible to survivors of SEA. HDPO will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the SEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. HDPO’s partners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regards to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities. This is done by conducting focus group discussions with women and girls in women centers. The project will support the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improving beneficiaries’ daily lives through improved formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequality as the root cause of GBV.
