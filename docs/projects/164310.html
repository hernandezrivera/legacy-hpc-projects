Project Summary

This project aims to run and support a specialized hospital that provides comprehensive child and maternal health services, including women's surgery and hospitalization, as well as ambulances and a specialized department for infants and neonatal care.
Where the hospital consists of outpatient clinics that have a clinic for women and children functioning daily to provide diagnosis and treatment services for women and children and referral cases that need admission in the hospital.
Emergency: manages the emerging pediatric and gynecological cases and provide appropriate treatment and referral of cases that require admission to other departments.
Gynecological department: It consists of 15 beds for women, where women who are being prepared for surgery, in addition to the gynecological conditions that require admission to the hospital.
The Department of Childhood Illness: It consists of 10 beds allocated for children referred from the emergency dept and other facilities in the locality.
Pediatric and phototherapy: 6 incubators and 3 devices for 24-hour phototherapy. Cases are received from the emergency and outpatient departments in the same facility, in addition to referred cases from primary health care centers and other maternity and child health units. In addition to the neonates born in the hospital who need admission and neonatal care.
Labour and delivery: Receives cases of planned delivery from the outpatient department as well as emergency cases.
The Department of Women's Surgery: consists of two operating rooms that provide cesarean delivery services and women's emergency and elective surgery.
The previous sections are supported by the 24-hour detective department as well as the pharmacy

4 - Needs

HeRAMs Code: B0202021According to HNO 2019, Syrian women and girls constitute the most neglected and marginalized group against whom various forms of discrimination and violence are steadily being practiced. Women and young girls are disproportionately affected.Sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need across the country. 37 per cent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services.According to HeRAMs and 4Ws reports by Health Cluster/Turkey hub, the health situation in Al Bab area is collapsed due to the low availability of the services provision which is supposed to meet the emerging needs in the area. Regarding the availability of secondary health care capacity, Al Bab area has recorded a gap up to 77% compared to the Sphere Standards, not mentioning the inadequate number of human resources in pediatrics and gynecology, as reported by HeRAMs (4th Quarter 2019 WHO), the currently available health staff covers only 33% of the population compared to Sphere Standards. Al Bab district was enrolled in the severity 4 out of 5 in the severity scale regarding Ratio of HF per 100K (3-4 functioning facilities only).The planned project will be scaled up to address the need in healthcare & nutrition services to pregnant women, both high risk and general maternity care with a better more comprehensive prenatal and perinatal care from the first trimester of pregnancy till delivery. This project also addresses the need for an improved and upgraded neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) with expansion of capacity in the internal blood bank unit.

5 - Implementation of activities

Output: • Run emergency obstetric and neonatal care unit and services (B&CEmONC), and provide reproductive health care to women in the form of antenatal, prenatal, natal, and postnatal care for women. • Provide family planning, maternal, infant, young, child and adolescent nutrition counseling corner in the MCH. • Provide gynecologic, pediatric and nutrition inpatient and outpatient services for women and children. (about 20 beds/ 1st phase) • Provide essential mental health and psychosocial support services through the targeted MCH. Activities: • Hospital a well equipped and staffed Mother and Child Health (MCH) center in Al-Bab, including family planning, maternal, infant, young, child and adolescent nutrition counseling corner in the MCH. • Train MCH health staff on the use of clinical protocols and guidelines, including B&CEmONC, acute malnutrition, and patient data collection. • Train technical staff on triage and BLS (Basic Life Support) and APLS (advance Pediatrics Life Support) for emergency staff (Pediatrician, doctors, nurse, midwives, and Obs/Gyc specialist). • Support and supervise MCH management and administration through performance standards monitoring and coaching. • Coordinating with local actor through a referral network between lower and higher levels of care, the network will be initiated through coordination meeting and setting pathways and list of facilities in the area. The project will be able to receive referral to NICU. Local authorities will be informed and included in project design and response.Monitoring Plan: 1- Measuring the impact of the project through conducting a baseline and endline survey. 2-Conducting spot-check visits. 3- Tracking the project progress through the IPTT. 4- beneficiaries’ satisfaction surveys through direct interviews with beneficiaries.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In maternal and child activities the proposed maternal and child health and reproductive health training, as well as the related PHC strengthening activities, prioritize health issues affecting women and children. With most of the deliveries in Syria requiring medical intervention, the proposed training on emergency obstetric care (EMoNC) for midwives will work to improve health services needed exclusively by women. Furthermore, our organization's emphasis on training midwives will facilitate meeting the needs of the most vulnerable groups who have limited access to health facilities for multiple reasons such as disability, distance from health facilities and displacement. our organization adopted mitigation measures including, training materials and operational protocols emphasize the need to observe, record, and address differences in health status affecting women, men, boys, and girls. To the extent possible, our organization requires and/or advocates for the use of sex and age disaggregated health data to monitor trends in health access and status that disproportionately affect one or more gender or age groups. Our organization proposed training of district-level health on Health Information Systems will further contribute to this effort. Messages promoted through health staff, community outreach workers, and awareness-raising campaigns also address issues related to women and men’s access to services, different dietary requirements for pregnant versus non-pregnant women, and age-specific nutrition feeding practices for infants and young children.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Access to targeted locations and beneficiaries. - Continued funds based on an approved plan. - The security situation does not deteriorate. - Acceptance and cooperation by stakeholders. 1. Attacks (several types) against Health Facilities and Staff: From January through September 2019 documented 174 attacks across Syria, exceeding the incidents reported throughout the year 2018 (138 incidents) as reported through SSA surveillance system. 266 casualties (97 fatalities and 169 injuries) from medical staff and patients/beneficiaries resulted from these violent incidences. Mitigating measures: - Increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare to enable the role of higher levels of liaison and steering groups in advocacy and taking the appropriate measures addressing this issue. - Assessment of the safest and least vulnerable areas. Training all workers on first aid and evacuation of wounded. Ensure that the hospital is equipped with full safety supplies such as fire extinguishers, first aid kit, etc. 2-Limited availability of qualified personnel that limits the ability of the hospital to provide services. Severity: High. Mitigating measures are taken: To provide the program with a comprehensive assessment of the current availability of staff, doctors and staff in and around the hospital to ensure a realistic, feasible and appropriate management and financial proposal. 3. Gender-Based Violence: Health services are the first to seek help in for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries. Health providers must be equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services.
