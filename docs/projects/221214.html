Project Summary

This project aims to provide sustainable and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) for returnees, refugees from Sudan, other displaced persons, and vulnerable host families in Renk, Fashoda, and Panyikang counties. Its objective is to help these groups recover from the shocks caused by conflicts and natural disasters, such as severe flooding and the Sudan crisis. A particular focus will be placed on women and children, who make up the majority of returnees, refugees, displaced persons, and at-risk host families. The project will deliver developmental and emergency WASH services, integrated with other sectors, helping also to contribute toward achieving Sustainable Development Goal 6 by 2030.
Building on ongoing initiatives led by CAFOD and Trócaire in Partnership, alongside partners such as Caritas Diocese of Malakal (CDoM) and Africa Development Aid (ADA), the project will continue efforts in the targeted counties. Emergency WASH assistance will prioritize households with children affected by malnutrition and preventable communicable diseases due to inadequate access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation facilities. The proposed counties face significant challenges, including the ongoing Sudan conflict, severe flooding, and widespread inter-communal violence in Upper Nile State, particularly in Panyikang County.
The most affected populations include children, women, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities (PWD). Priority will be given to specific vulnerable groups, including female-headed households, households with at least 15% PWD, households with malnourished children, and households with pregnant and lactating mothers. The exact criteria will be established in collaboration with these affected communities. Programme participants will be identified based on humanitarian principles, complemented by CAFOD's protection and safeguarding framework. This framework is central to ensuring that program management and development processes are safe, accessible, dignified, and inclusive (SADI). A participatory and consultative approach will guide the development of targeting criteria, ensuring that the voices of the most vulnerable individuals and households are heard and influence the process. This will build on CAFOD's established targeting methodologies.
To reduce dependency on humanitarian partners, the project aims to provide sustainable WASH assistance to 20,000 individuals. This will include the establishment of solar-powered water supply systems in the targeted counties, supported by the training of water point operators and management committees to ensure long-term sustainability. Household latrine construction will follow the Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) approach, with the organization offering 10% support in the form of construction tools to facilitate the community’s transition to and adoption of the CLTS model. Emergency WASH services will include the distribution of household water treatment supplies and essential non-food items (NFIs) such as buckets, jerrycans, and soap. Additional measures include house-to-house hygiene promotion, the construction of emergency communal latrines using cluster standards for the Renk response, and institutional latrine construction targeting nutrition centres. Hygiene campaigns will be conducted in public spaces, such as markets, health facilities, village centres, and schools, to raise awareness about environmental and public hygiene. Cholera prevention messages and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures for Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) will be integrated into hygiene promotion throughout the project cycle, ensuring communities are informed and better prepared.

Implementing Partners

CAFOD will deliver this project through two local partners. CAFOD itself has a long experience of implementing SSHF proposals. This includes a successful $943,292.96 multisector project in 2022 in the greater Tonj areas that reached over 42,897 households, as well as a 12-month SSHF-funded capacity-strengthening project to enhance the capabilities of 100 local partners.
Our first partner is the Caritas Diocese of Malakal (CDoM), a registered Catholic organization based at St. Joseph Cathedral in Malakal, Upper Nile State. Its response covers the Greater Upper Nile region, including Upper Nile, Jonglei, Unity, and the administrative areas of Ruweng and Pibor. CDoM works to alleviate poverty and promote sustainable development and well-being among disadvantaged communities, and will continue to do so through this project.
Our second partner, Africa Development Aid (ADA), will work with CAFOD in Renk County to address the returnee crisis from Sudan. ADA will provide emergency WASH assistance, complementing the ongoing CAFOD project in the area. This collaboration ensures a coordinated response to the urgent needs of returnees while leveraging existing project frameworks and resources

1. Needs

The WASH Cluster estimates that by 2025, the number of people in need (PIN) of WASH services will reach 5.01 million. Of these, the Cluster projects that 2.25 million individuals will be targeted for critical WASH services, representing approximately 45% of the total people in need. The funding required to achieve this is estimated at $89.53 million. The targeted counties are classified as priority levels 1 and 2 according to the Cluster prioritisation and are ranked levels 4 and 5 based on the severity of needs. According to the latest IPC report, released on 18th November 2024, these counties are also classified under levels 3 and 4. Several factors exacerbate existing vulnerabilities and increase exposure to crises and emergencies, including the continued influx of returnees from Sudan, a worsening macroeconomic crisis, low preparedness capacity at both national and state levels, outbreaks of pandemics, and consecutive years of flooding. The primary source of household and drinking water in these counties is untreated water from the Nile River, contributing to the high prevalence of waterborne infections and diseases. In Upper Nile State, particularly in Renk, returnees, refugees, and host communities rely on water trucking, untreated water distributed by donkeys, and public motorised water yards, which serve less than 40% of the population in Renk town. This limited access to safe water exposes people to health risks associated with waterborne diseases and protection risks, particularly for women and girls. These risks include attacks by dangerous aquatic animals such as crocodiles and snakes, as well as accidental drownings when collecting water from surface sources like rivers and streams. Women and girls also face heightened risks of physical and sexual assault and harassment at water collection points, bathing areas, and latrines. Additionally, a lack of access to latrines forces communities to resort to open defecation, which increases the risk of waterborne diseases and diarrhoeal disease outbreaks, such as cholera. The IPC intersectoral prioritisation report highlights that Fashoda and Panyikang counties are among the 44 priority counties classified under Phase 4, while Renk County is one of 32 priority counties classified under Phase 3. This underscores the significant challenges in accessing WASH facilities for vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, and the elderly. The influx of returnees and refugees has significantly altered the population composition in the targeted counties, which are now predominantly occupied by returnees and displaced persons, with a smaller proportion of host communities. According to the IOM report (25th November 2024), a total of 877,240 individuals (220,552 households) were registered at entry points. Of these, 665,215 individuals were South Sudanese nationals. Many of these will require assistance.

2. Project activities

CAFOD and Trócaire in Partnership, along with its implementing partners, will provide emergency WASH assistance to returnees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and vulnerable host communities in the targeted counties. This intervention will align with various WASH Cluster objectives and address gaps identified in ongoing multisector emergency response and recovery projects. Specifically, the project will contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through integrated WASH-Nutrition programming. Proposed activities under this objective include the provision of WASH standard kits paired with hygiene promotion for caretakers of children with cases of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and the construction of sanitation facilities at four nutrition centres. The project will provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and in counties categorised as Wash Severity Classification 4 and 5. Key activities include the establishment of solarised water supply systems to increase access to safe water in Panyikang and Fashoda counties, the distribution of emergency WASH kits to the most vulnerable households, house-to-house hygiene promotion campaigns, and the construction of emergency latrines. A clear transition strategy will be implemented to move from emergency latrines to Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) latrines through the distribution of digging tools. WASH-specific activities will be conducted to mitigate gender-based violence (GBV), including the use of safety audits to address risks associated with WASH services. Additionally, the project will reduce community exposure to environmental hazards through the construction of disaster-resistant infrastructure. This will involve constructing water points with flood-resilient platforms and communal latrines with flood-resilient and disability-inclusive designs. To ensure sustainability, the project will train community hygiene promotion volunteers, water point operators, and management committees to maintain and operationalise water supply infrastructure. Training will also be provided to communities on sustainable household water treatment techniques, such as using Moringa seeds for sedimentation (if available), boiling water at high temperatures, and utilising solar disinfection (SODIS). Household latrine construction will follow the CLTS approach, with 10% support provided by the organisation in the form of construction tools to facilitate community-led adoption. Hygiene promotion messages will focus on good handwashing practices, personal hygiene, food hygiene, environmental sanitation, safe water chain management, and the proper disposal of solid and human waste. Hygiene campaigns will be conducted in public places such as markets, health facilities, village centres, and schools to enlighten communities on environmental and public hygiene. Throughout the project cycle, cholera prevention messages and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures for Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) will be integrated into hygiene promotion activities.
