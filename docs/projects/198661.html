Project Summary

This project is aimed at strengthening the integrated primary health care and sexual reproductive healthcare services in emergency through capacity building of health workers, facility referrals and treatment of conflict-affected persons using the already existed health facilities (sixteen) e.g. health clinics, health post, PHCCs within the selected LGAs as well as using ten (10) mobile health outreach teams two per LGA. This project will cover the provision of outpatient and in-patient services at the health facility and the provision of mobile health services in strategic locations that are hard-to-reach including influx of IDPs from the borders that covers Yola North, Fufore and Demsa looking at the number of IDPs in those locations without accessibility to integrated PHC services. The focus location will be in Girei and Yola South LGAs of Adamawa State. The project tends to build and extend on previous efforts of the state ministry of health and other partners in improving access to outpatient, inpatient and SRH services in the areas. However, special efforts undertaken to address the early recovery and development needs of the health system within the framework of the Humanitarian Development Nexus that will enhance its responsiveness to the needs of the communities as they transition to full recovery. A mix of quick gain interventions based on more resilient outreach services that will utilize outreach tents, specialized centers for adolescents and young people and medium to long-term investments in equipment, capacity building used in this project.
Looking at the current season which is characterized with heavy rain, flooding, farming, migration due to flooding, etc. malaria is the leading cause of mortality and morbidity among under 5 year’s children as well as pregnant women; typhoid and peptic ulcer is common diseases as well. There will be deployment of mobile hard-to-reach teams going to places that are under-served with the available functioning health facilities. In addition, there will be training of Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs) and Junior Community Health Extension Workers (JCHEWs) using the standard protocol of WHO and UNFPA on various diseases that are related to rainy season as well as Sexual Reproductive Health. Nurses and community health volunteers will also be training in reducing mortality of the affected communities.
Establishing strong referrals system and network between mobile outreach locations and contiguous health facilities for emergency healthcare services. Provision of Emergency Reproductive Health (ERH) kits for the health facilities will ensure Basic Emergency Obstetric care at the primary care centers. FSACI will work closely with the State Ministry of Health, Supporting the health sector and SRH sub sector to ensure no duplication of services. FSACI will work closely with partners on ground in these locations. The trained community volunteers, hygiene promoters, community-based workers and the collaboration between the FSACI and the State Ministry will help in sensitizing and awareness creation in all the communities and settlements within the selected LGAs on water-borne related disease, communicable diseases, etc.

1 - Needs

Since the start of the humanitarian conflict in 2009, more than 300,000 people have been killed in the BAY states, thousands of women and girls abducted and children used as so-called suicide bombers. Presently, over 2.2 million people are internally displaced in the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states of which over 70% are women and children. After Borno, Adamawa State hosts the second highest number of IDPs in the region. Yola South remained the LGA where the highest number of IDPs were recorded with 28,937 individuals (or 13% IDPs in Adamawa). Michika LGA with 23,570 IDPs (11%) and Numan LGA with 23,051 IDPs (10%) followed Yola South. Adamawa State counted 28 camps and camp-like settings where a total of 20,156 IDPs or 9 per cent of the IDP population in the state of Adamawa was recorded. The majority (200,730 IDPs or 91 per cent) of Adamawa’s IDP population was residing in host community settings. A total of 1,983,130 returnees or 323,277 returnee households were recorded during Round 41 of DTM assessments in northeast Nigeria. This signified an increase of 1 per cent or 22,572Individuals compared to Round 40 when 1,960,558 returnees were identified. Note that IOM only tracks return movements in the BAY states. The state of Adamawa continued to host the most significant number of returnees with 843,493 individuals or 43 per cent of the total returnee population in northeast Nigeria. Borno State hosted 788,807 returnees, or 39 per cent of the total number of returneesThe humanitarian crisis in Nigeria’s BAY states is expected to persist unabated in 2022/2023: the continuing conflict will still severely affect millions of people in 2022/2023, subjecting them to displacement, impoverishment and threat of violence. Increased demand for STI/HIV/AIDS services, inpatients, outpatients, mobile health services and comprehensive referral services is growing out of proportion especially during the rainy season. Increased support of coordination mechanisms in the LGA levels to support partner’s synergies efforts and avoid duplication of services when so many other communities are in dire need of life saving interventions. Experiences from ongoing medical outreach to new areas and LGAs reveal gaps in basic access to Maternal Health Service’s, communities have reported alarming rates of teenage pregnancies which results in ripple effects of unsafe abortions with its attendant complications, obstetric fistula, maternal and perinatal mortalities. Thus, the need for ongoing integrated medical outreaches, mobile clinics and strengthening of the PHC structures at LGA level.In June 2022, the state of Adamawa carried out the first Seasonal Malaria Chemoprevention (SMC) intervention cycle, which resulted in an average protection of 0.98 million people, with Girei covering 39,498 of the population of 181,269 and Yola South covering 91,850 of the population of 368,749. This suggests that there is more ground to cover with the help of the NHF budget. This will be in accordance with the national recommendation and the T3 approach of Test, Treat, and Track with regard to malaria case management.Currently, cholera, measles, meningitis, typhoid, peptic ulcer, mother/child mortality during birth and viral hemorrhagic fevers continue to pose a serious threat to the affected populations in northeast Nigeria. More than 3,000 people have been displaced across Jabilamba community and environs in Girei LGA, following severe flash flooding from heavy downpours between 18 and 19 August. Sources have reported the arrival of some of the displaced households in neighbouring Song and Yola LGAs as of 22 August, while many homes remain submerged across the affected areas and most residents unable to return to salvage property and valuables. Girei LGA is one of the major cholera hotspots in Adamawa State and the contamination of water sources by the floodwater has heightened the risks of waterborne diseases across the affected areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All groups including women, children and elderly are engaged in gathering data on their specific health needs. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored in to the program design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. The programs include estimated sex and age dis-aggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. Project will ensure women's participation while selecting the community health volunteers to ensure it is gender sensitive. In addition, timing of health promotion sessions and other committee meetings will be considered to ensure suitability. A close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by FSACI to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material and as far as possible key actors, working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation and interests. Before and after each session, group play activities will test participants’ understanding on specific issues, if needed, topics will be re discussed. After the training cycle is completed, FSACI staffers initially will facilitate regular meetings with the community members for the establishment of Child Protection Focal Point (CPFP) and the subsequent design of an action work plan and afterwards to follow up on the action plan and its results. Gender Base Violence (GBV) issues if of keen interest of FSACI and this will not be allowed or tolerated among staffs and beneficiaries.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

FSACI will ensure accountability to affected populations through various activities. Using the currently established system of an Accountability and Quality Improvement Committee (AQIC) in the ongoing project, which comprises of community leaders, religious leaders, representative of women, youth and other social groups and a representative of all ethnic group present in the community. The monthly meetings of the AQIC has served as means by which the affected people minds and thoughts are been heard and incorporated into the project implementation. This will be a continuous process in this project. Lapse identified in the pass will be handled better. During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to health were also aired and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners. However, information about the project will be shared using pictorial materials. The FSACI team is gender-sensitive and balanced and speaks the local languages to ensure the information about the project is disseminated through community leaders. By doing so the target population will receive clear, relevant and timely information regarding the project.The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback and complaint mechanism that can be received using various means, such as FGD, interviews and feedback boxes. Information on relevant services including referral pathway if available will be shared in coordination with the Protection team and WASH teams. There will be use of feedback/suggestion boxes at the different facilities of this project implementation and if not available, one will be constructed by FSACI and mount at the facility as part of the feedback mechanism apart from meetings and field visit.
