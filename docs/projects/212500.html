Project Summary

Through this project, Dorcas and its partners will be providing food vouchers, food parcels, and healthy meals to vulnerable populations in Aleppo, Rural Damascus (RD), and Hama.
The food vouchers will be provided to 150 households in Aleppo in 2024, and these families will receive vouchers for three months. Additionally, Dorcas, through its partner MSJM, will provide food parcels to 166 households in Hama in 2024.
Moreover, Dorcas will continue to provide daily healthy meals to more than 5,050 students attending private schools in Aleppo and participating in activities at the two community centers run by the partner in close collaboration with the Dorcas team.
Furthermore, Dorcas will extend its support by providing food parcels in Al Tal and Harassta for 1,000 households, each receiving assistance three times.
The activities were planned taking into consideration the results of the needs assessment that was done as part of the Syria joint response project with the partners, TDH, OXFAM, and ZOA. A comprehensive needs assessment was done in October 2023 to inform the design and implementation of the food assistance program. The results of the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) revealed significant food insecurity among vulnerable populations in Aleppo, Rural Damascus (RD), Hama, Al Tal, and Harassta.
According to the MSNA findings, households with acceptable food consumption accounted for only 2.60% (n=10) of the surveyed population. A further 38.18% (n=147) of households reported borderline food consumption, while the majority, 59.22% (n=228), indicated poor food consumption levels. The average score across all food categories was 27.85%, placing the majority of households within the poor food consumption category.
In addition, the needs assessment also highlighted concerning trends in food-related coping strategies among households in the assessed areas. Analysis revealed that a significant proportion of households were resorting to high levels of food-related coping strategies to address food insecurity.
Specifically, the analysis found that 76.88% (n=296) of households were adopting high food-related coping strategies, indicating a significant reliance on coping mechanisms directly related to food access and consumption. Furthermore, 17.66% (n=68) of households were identified as adopting medium food-related coping strategies, while only 5.45% (n=21) were utilizing low or non-food-related coping strategies.
The average use of negative coping strategies among households was calculated at 14.89, indicating a prevalence of high coping strategies overall. This underscores the severity of the food insecurity situation and the urgent need for targeted interventions to address the underlying causes and alleviate the burden on vulnerable households.
In response to these concerning findings, Dorcas has designed the food assistance program to not only provide immediate relief but also to address the root causes of food insecurity and reduce households' reliance on negative coping strategies.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In addition to providing essential food assistance to vulnerable populations in Aleppo, Rural Damascus (RD), Hama, Al Tal, and Harassta, Dorcas and its partners are committed to integrating gender mainstreaming, accountability to affected populations (AAP), and protection considerations throughout the project implementation.Gender mainstreaming efforts will ensure that the specific needs, vulnerabilities, and capacities of women, men, boys, and girls are systematically considered and addressed in all aspects of the food assistance program. This includes promoting gender equality and women's empowerment, as well as addressing gender-based violence (GBV) risks and barriers to access for marginalized groups.Through its accountability mechanisms, Dorcas will actively engage with affected communities, ensuring their meaningful participation in decision-making processes, feedback mechanisms, and monitoring of project activities. Furthermore, protection considerations will be integrated into all stages of the food assistance program to mitigate risks of harm, exploitation, and abuse. This includes measures to safeguard the dignity, safety, and well-being of beneficiaries, particularly those in vulnerable situations, such as internally displaced persons (IDPs), refugees, and women-headed households.
