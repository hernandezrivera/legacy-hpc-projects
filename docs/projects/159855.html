Project Summary

Results by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2019 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin A supplementation, and poor care practices. At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (13.8% in 2019 Gu, 12.6% in 2018 Deyr and 14.0% in 2018 Gu). A Critical (15-29.9%) prevalence of GAM was observed in 10 out of the 33 population groups surveyed, with more areas likely to deteriorate from Serious to Critical between August and October 2019. The Shabelle regions have been categorized among the areas of concern and represent hotspots in need of urgent nutrition and health interventions. The regions show a critical prevalence of acute malnutrition (≥15% GAM or ≥10.7% of children have a MUAC below the 125 mm threshold). Projections of the number of people affected by food insecurity in Lower Juba between February and June 2019 are: stressed (IPC 2) 123,000, crisis (IPC 3) 46,000 and emergency (IPC 4) 11,000. A recent joint assessment (Jan 2019) led by Medair in some local villages of rural Afgooye (Dameraale), Balcad (Galooley) and rural Cadale highlighted significantly higher malnutrition levels. Proxy GAM through MUAC for children under five was at 20%.
Response plan
This project will target children between 6-59months as well as pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with preventive, promotive and curative nutritional services both at health facility and community level. The goal with be to improve the nutritional status of infants (0-6 months), young children (6-24 months) and children under 5 years as well as improve the nutritional status of women before, during and after delivery.
Medair’s proposes an integrated multi-sectoral response to the above needs with health, nutrition, and WASH projects implemented at community and static facility levels. Medair proposes to implement an integrated program with prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition including severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), infant and young Child feeding practices (IYCF) in emergency settings, micronutrient deficiency prevention and control being implemented in the supported facilities and their catchment villages. The admission criteria for TSFP admission are: W/H <-2 z score, <12.5cm and for discharges that are cured W/H > -2 z score, MUAC> 12.5cm, and for 2 consecutive visits. Medair will work closely with stabilization centers (SC) for severe malnutrition with complications. Following the client discharge from the SC, Medair supported facilities will receive and admit these children into the program to complete the therapy. Medair has established a referral system in the communities which works very well. The nutrition program will be integrated with health and WASH interventions in the health facilities as well as in the villages in the catchment areas, this will promote effective nutrition to improve community resilience to current and future nutrition emergencies which focus on capacity building and equipping nutrition centres to ensure a stronger local response during emergencies. Medair has ensured that all supported health facilities have access to safe water. Infant and Young Child Feeding in emergencies practices will be implemented concurrently with an emphasis on creating awareness of the direct link between poor feeding practices and malnutrition; prevention and management of micronutrient deficiencies will be integrated into the CMAM interventions. This will ensure Vitamin A supplements are administered to children in line with the national protocol.

Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance -Medair is committed to ensuring that members of groups who are internally displaced OR minorities persons are routinely involved at all levels of project cycle from inception to closure. They will also be engaged individually at their households and community level to better understand their unique needs and how best to respond to them. Dialogue meetings with local leaders will be enhanced to promote local ownership of project activities. Health workers will be trained and support-supervised to ensure they practice humanitarian core principles. Nutrition services will be offered round the clock to all including host and displaced persons at no cost. Hot line number, which is toll free will, be provided to community members so they provide feedback on any form of exclusion or marginalization to services delivered. Community leaders will be encouraged to advocate for inclusion of minority in decision making roles Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance - Medair has zero tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse and has strict policies to prevent and protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).Medair will continue to provide staff training on physical sexual exploitation and abuse mainstreaming and ensure they sign code of conducts on physical sexual exploitation and abuse, all employees have signed a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards including the rights of beneficiaries to access humanitarian services without undue exploitation. Medair has recruited a safeguarding officer at all bases in Somalia to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow up on any allegation or even rumors about sexual exploitation and abuse to the beneficiaries. The Care group volunteers and health/nutrition workers are also trained to identify passive cases in the community and counsel them to seek professional care at supported facilities. Where these incidences have happened, trauma counselling and first aid will be provided to the survivors. A clear referral pathway will be established in coordination with other actors with capacity for long-term psychological and legal support for affected persons.Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms- Transparency will be done in sharing all the information related to the project to include Project period, where necessary targeted beneficiaries and their entitlement. Service charter in local language posters will be published, staffs will create awareness on the rights to safety, access, dignity, privacy and confidentiality Medair has an efficient and effective feedback mechanism in place for suggestions and complaint that is accessible, confidential and well understood to beneficiaries with a view to feedback to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Timely responses are provided to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. Ongoing awareness and sensitization sessions at the facility through the monitoring and evaluation officer/nutrition facility workers and the community through the care group volunteers and community health/nutrtion workers where they are informed on the mechanism in place and how it works. All Medair and the partners’ staffs/nutrition staffs, and community volunteers they will be trained on Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients.Exit interviews will be conducted on the targeted beneficiaries to understand their needs and concerns on the quality of services they received and address whenever possible.
