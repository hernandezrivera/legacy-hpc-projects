Project Summary

The current security and humanitarian situation in South Sudan affected 299,123 refugees in South Sudan as of October 2018. The majority of them originated from Sudan (277,183) followed by DRC (15,683), Ethiopia (4,163) and CAR (1,999). They reside in 21 camps and urban locations in Upper Nile, Unity, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria, and Jonglei regions. South Sudan maintains an open-door policy for refugees and grants those arriving from Sudan, DRC, and CAR prima facie refugee status. There are additionally 2,454 asylum-seekers from Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia, Burundi and other countries waiting for determination on their individual refugee claims. Daily border crossing continues with 17,317 new arrivals registered in 2018 (as of October 2018) from Blue Nile and South Kordofan States of Sudan.
Refugees reside in conflict-affected regions where security remains precarious with fragile relations with host communities. The continuous economic decline, weak rule of law, limited development support and lack of social services and infrastructure in refugee-hosting areas as well as the conditions in the refugees’ areas of origin mean that refugees will continue to rely on lifesaving humanitarian assistance and on social cohesion with and acceptance by the host community for protection.
Under the refugee response, all refugees and asylum seekers registered with UNHCR will benefit from various forms of protection interventions from UNHCR and its partners, including individual registration and documentation. Specific protection services, such as SGBV response and child protection will focus on the identified at-risk groups. While food, water, health and education services are available for all refugees living in camps, provision of other assistance will be undertaken in a targeted manner, focusing on persons with specific protection needs and vulnerabilities. This will primarily be achieved through participatory assessments, surveys as well as verification exercise to identify vulnerable refugees and their needs. Impact monitoring will be used to gauge the efficiency of projects implemented and for the purposes of course-correction in terms of better targeting of refugees in need of assistance. The needs of persons with disabilities and elderly will be further mainstreamed in various multi-sector response services. Vulnerable refugees in urban setting will also continue to benefit from targeted protection interventions and facilitation of their access to national services.
UNHCR and partners will continue delivery of protection and multi-sectoral assistance to refugees. UNHCR and partners will continue programs ensuring refugees’ access to healthcare services, education, WASH, livelihoods, food and NFI distribution. Access to reproductive health and HIV services will be ensured through complete ANC coverage, health facility deliveries and skilled health workers, as well as comprehensive HIV/AIDS programmes allowing refugees to have access to ant-retroviral treatments (ART) through ART centers in all camps and the two referral hospitals.
Initiatives to strengthen SGBV prevention and response mechanisms and child protection will be further promoted. While facilitating traditional justice mechanisms, such as conflict resolution committees and joint customary courts, UNHCR will support re-establishment of statutory courts in refugee sites.

Needs

By the end of Ocotber 2018 South Sudan was hosting around 299,123 refugees, most of whom came as a result of the on-going fighting in South Kordofan and Blue Nile States in Sudan and were settled predominantly in 21 refugee locations and the vast majority of refugees reside in the remote locations of Unity and Upper Nile States. A smaller number of refugees from the Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of Congo and Ethiopia have been displaced on a protracted basis, and are mostly settled in Central and Western Equatoria States. All refugees in in South Sudan are in need of international protection, multi-sectorial life-saving and basic assistance to enable them to live in safety and dignity. Ensuring safety and security of refugees in South Sudan remains a fundamental concern and a major protection challenge. In recent participatory assessments, among main protection challenges refugees indicated insecurity and violence, presence of armed elements, recruitment of children, SGBV, insufficient food and education facilities, limited socio-economic opportunities and skills training as major protection concerns. Maintaining the civilian character of camps will necessitate increased advocacy with government and other stakeholders. Refugees in camps rely heavily on multi-sectorial assistance and any scaling back of such basic assistance will immediately lead to the deterioration of the humanitarian situation of refugees to emergency levels. Results from the end of 2017 nutrition surveys showed an average Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence of 6.2 per cent and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence of 0.8 per cent among children 6-59 months for the refugee camps indicating poor nutrition status. Total anaemia prevalence was 48%, which is categorized as high according to WHO standards. Among non-pregnant women of reproductive age (15-49), the total anaemia prevalence was 32.9%. The prevalence of global stunting was 44% which is categorized as critical according to WHO standards.Only 48.2% of the refugees had an acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS) in 2018 indicating the rest were vulnerable to food shortages. Access to education remains a big challenge for refugee children and the learner-teacher ratio is still at 92:1. Children in urban and peri-urban Juba struggled to access education due to closure of public schools because of limited government funding. Monitoring reports indicate refugees and host community are confronted with precarious socio-economic situation with approximately 80% of host and refugee populations facing food scarcity, limited livelihood options, and gap in food supply. Competition for scarce resources have led to high tensions between refugee and host communities, further aggravating situation for refugees.

Activities

• Coordination of humanitarian response to address protection and assistance needs of refugees, including liaison with authorities, partners and other stakeholders. • Registration and documentation support to refugees and other persons of concern. • Facilitation of essential services in the area of health, nutrition, WASH, education and camp management to meet refugees' basic needs. NFI distribution will target new arrivals and most vulnerable households including female headed households. Shelter support will be limited to emergency shelter for new arrivals and provision of transitional shelters to the most vulnerable.• Maintenance and upgrade of service infrastructures, including schools, health facilities, water distribution, shelters as well as access roads and air strips. Use of renewable energy will be pursued for better sustainability of services and environment protection. • Acceleration of relocations from Yida to Pamir and expansion of Pamir camp in Unity to accommodate both new arrivals and relocations from Yida. This will include installation of expanded basic services, and insuring that all camp facilities adhere to minimum UNHCR standards. • Protection monitoring, analysis, training and advocacy, addressing various protection concerns related to men, boys, women and girls of different ages and backgrounds. Activities will also focus on bolstering community protection mechanisms.• Multi sectoral GBV prevention and response, including installation of solar lights, information management, awareness raising, psychosocial counselling and referral services.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Following Age, Gender and Diversity approach the participatory assessment provides basis for designing protection and assistance programs for Persons of Concern. Annual Participatory Assessment (PA) exercises and consultations with Persons of Concern (PoCs) were undertaken in the course of 2018 which informs protection programming. This will be continued in 2018 involving all actors.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Refugees were consulted through participatory assessment (PA) exercises conducted by UNHCR, its implementing and operational partners countrywide. The PA process aimed to gather information on specific protection risks faced by refugees, analyse risks jointly with them, understand their capacities, and to hear their proposed solutions through structured dialogue.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The AGD assessment indicated that women continue to face increased risks of SGBV due to lack of livelihood activities, presence of armed elements in camps and tensions with host community. Community safe-homes for survivors of SGBV are generally in place in refugee camps though lacking in urban settings. Women will be supported through livelihoods initiatives and trainings on loans and financial management. Involvement of community groups in service delivery will be facilitated through trainings and sensitization. However, poverty and low incentives affect in general the community work as the volunteers have tendency to look for income generating activities. UNHCR will continue enhancing of community structures on identification and reporting of the SEA cases.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The principles of age, gender and diversity, a rights-based, and community-based approach guided the process. PA ensured that refugees are put at the centre of the decision-making concerning their protection and welfare as well as ensured accountability to affected populations. Qualitative data collection was done through FGDs and KIIs and feedback sessions were held with communities to present findings and recommendations.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

UNHCR and partners maintain its offices in the refugee camps since they have greatly increased the interaction between refugees and UNHCR, and also provide effective complaint mechanisms to address persons of concern’s views. Camp interagency coordination meetings are held on a monthly basis. Community Outreach Volunteers (COV) network is also reinforced since they crucial to provide real time information to UNHCR on issues affecting their lives. In 2019, UNHCR will work with its partners to strengthen community-based complaint mechanisms and expand awareness on PSEA across key areas of operation. Building on the Cluster approach to ensure consistent messaging and centralization of complaint mechanism will strengthen prevention awareness and integrated response, when necessary. Working closely with the GBV Sub-Cluster and the multi-agency PSEA Taskforce will allow boosting the implementation of the guidelines for the Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBMC), amplifying therefore the efficiency of reporting mechanisms using the methodologies the communities prefer.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community self-management structures focusing on advocacy and socio-recreational activities will be supported that includes refugee leadership, community outreach volunteers (COV), community-based protection teams and sector specific structures for education, WASH, child protection and SGBV, as well as youth, women and disability associations and early marriage task forces. COVs have been instrumental in identifying and providing assistance to extremely vulnerable refugees that will be further expanded in 2019 with more gender sensitive composition.

Briefly describe how

A major political development in South Sudan has set in after the signing by parties concerned of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) on 12 September 2018. The R-ARCSS cannot alter overnight a logistically, challenging, costly operating environment requiring careful prioritization. The humanitarian situation and protection crisis in SSD continue to necessitate humanitarian assistance. UNHCR as a Protection agency is well positioned to continue rendering protection and life-saving assistance to populations of concern, subject to any fundamental change in the operational context. Addressing basic humanitarian needs, provision of life-saving assistance for refugees. The 2019 HRP objectives revolve around life-saving humanitarian assistance, ensuring centrality of protection, strengthening resilience of the population, fostering greater community involvement. The 2019-21 UN Cooperation Framework will be implemented by UN agencies. Its operationalization of the humanitarian-development nexus is key to early recovery and enabling conditions for solutions, in line with UNHCR’s protection strategy.
