Project Summary

Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) project is targeting acute food insecure most vulnerable population (IDPs, and Returnees and host communities) due to protracted crises emanated from natural disaster/flood and conflict (compounded by inter-clan, inter-tribal violence and cattle raiding. The project plans to reach 3600 HHs (21600 Individuals) and additional 1640 Individuals (total of 23,640 Individuals) from IDPs, and Returnees communities in Twic East, Duk and Bor South counties through food assistance with cash modality, enhancing emergency food production and building capacities of people and households’ level to build resilience to better respond to shocks. This project will contribute to Cluster 1, 2, and 3. The project intends to reduce the food insecurity and malnutrition through food assistance with cash , enhanced emergency food production via distribution of seeds, agricultural tools, fishing kits and poultry for the most vulnerable people but also to build resilience slowly reducing the dependency on food and agricultural inputs through training & capacity building on-farm production, with demonstration plots; & awareness on nutritious food consumption and food waste management, capacity building on saving and income generation.
The project will ensure the sustainability of its outcome through working together with customary institution, line departments, women groups, youth groups, and local administration from beginning of the project.
LWF will starve to achieve this HRP objectives through soliciting funding via SSHF, LWF strategic partners such as CLWR/CFGB, ALWS, ACT CoS, GNC and DKH.

1 - Needs

Jonglei State has been impacted by the conflict between warring parties since 2013. The conflict in Jonglei continues to have a negative effect on the humanitarian needs of the population in 2022. Clashes between armed groups and insecurity are pervasive, particularly in the central Jonglei. Secondly, inter-tribal conflict is a common phenomenon which results in livestock raids and the kidnapping of women and children. Such raids often lead to massive attacks on villages, with major causalities. In addition, Jonglei State is severely affected by the devastating flooding from 2019 until 2020 and 2021/2022. Twic East County is a located at Southern part of Jonglei State neighbouring Duk to the North and Bor County to the South. Twic East County consists of five payams (Lith, Kongor, Nyuak, Ajuong and Pakeer), with a total population of 130,069 individuals before the 2020 flood. Following the devastating floods throughout the county, presently there are 54,500,000 people (8384 HHs) living on 42 narrow congested islands, and about 81% of them are internally displaced persons (IDPs) who are highly vulnerable, and in need of life saving food assistance.Duk county is situated in the central part of Jonglei state, bordered by Twic East County in the western and Southern part. It has 115,793 inhabitants in its six countries (Ageer, Payuel, Panyang, Pagak, Dongchack and Padeit). Duk has its own IDPs that moved from Panyang payam to Ageer, from Pagaak and Dongchak payam to Padiet, from Ageer and Padiet payam internally. These IDPs moved from flooded area to high ground around Jonglei canal. Further more Duk county is hosting influx of IDPs from Neighbouring counties of Nyirol, Uror and Ayod. There are about 15,500 people displaced and living in neighbouring counties, for example with only in June 2022, 3067 IDPS settled in Place named Mareng in Duk country . These IDPs are moving to Duk due to cattle raiding and food insecurity in their home areas (IRNA MARENG AUGUST 2022). Bor County located at Southern most edge of Jonglei state and South of Twic East County. According to forecasted population based on old census of 2010, Bor South County is home to 239,320 people and additional IDPS from within county and Twic East and Duk counties. It is the centre for Bor town (which state capital), and it has six payams (Jalle, Baidit, Makuach, Anyidi, Kolnyang and). Among the payams, Jalle and Baidit payams have been affected by floods covering 50% of its land area from 2020 and 2021/22.This led to 98% of two payams population being displaced to neighbouring payams the county as IDPs. Remaining four payams were also affected by floods but no displacements from original areas occurred. According to the latest IPC snapshot October 2022, for period covering December 2022-March 2023 Jonglei has been identified as the country’s most food insecure state. Moreover, 68% of the population were acutely food insecure, among which Twic East will face IPC 4-emergency and Duk and Bor South County Crises, IPC 3. The severity of food security was predicted to increase during the lean period between April and July 2023 where by Twic East and Duk county will fall under emergency IPC 4 and Bor South under IPC 3-crises. In the country as a whole, 62.7% are reportedly facing acute food insecurity, and in particular within Jonglei 32% are in IPC 4 and 3 in IPC 5. During the same period, 20% of the population of Duk County, 20% of the population of Bor South County and 40% of the population of Twic East County are predicted to be in IPC 4. As per IPC, during lean period in three counties a projected 277, 00 population will be under crises (IPC#) and 141,000 will be under emergency (IPC4).

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

According to LWF’s initial market data collection and mapping of market routes in July and October 2022, markets in all three counties are functioning and getting commodities through water or land transportation. Twic East County markets in targeted payams are getting commodities from Bor town and Juba Market using water transport on permanent waterbodies. Duk markets (especially Padeit, and Poktap markets) are getting commodities from Malakal and Reng (connected to Sudan markets) using water transport via Jonglei canal filled with flood water and also from Bor and Juba markets via Twic East. The Bor market (state level market) receives commodities mainly by road from Juba. The Bor market is located 200KM North of Juba (South Sudan capital) and the road is accessible all year round. Additionally, access to water is also an option for the Bor markets. Commodity prices across the three counties differ slightly. Thus, the choice of Direct Cash Transfer modality is preferable because there are functioning markets, it is cheaper for operational costs, and it has a deeper reach for vulnerable households in a sense that it allows a wider choice of commodities for households.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Direct Cash Transfer has been chosen for this project mainly because of its flexibility and convenience. With cash, HHs can buy what they need, including local foods such as dry fish, vegetables, and fresh milk. These foods are not typically included in pre-packaged in-kind food programs or cash voucher programs. Furthermore, the direct cash transfer program promotes local business and strengthens the local economy. Additionally, the costs of transportation for an in-kind modality would be high due to the costs of hiring boats and aircraft for transport because many roads in Twic East and Duk Counties are flooded. There would also be significant logistical costs to store and distribute food.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most of people in targeted counties are illiterate, and among the most vulnerable selected beneficiaries several will be people living with disables, elderly and females whom are physically and culturally less prone in providing feedbacks. For this reason LWF intends to work in close collaboration with the community committees who can closely work with the most in need providing immediate support and orientation throughout the project phases.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Beneficiaries will get full information about project as the project planning and implementation will be done with their full involvement as right holder. The project will have complained response committees as each Payam and county level, so that the complains are addressed immediately

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will mainstream protection in the implementation to ensure that the right holders are protected and sexual exploitation and abuse is controlled with zero tolerance. LWF will inform beneficiaries through awareness on PSEAS using meeting , and pamphlets. In addition, it will inform beneficiaries on its own PSEA policy

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will consider the feedback from beneficiaries through reorienting or revising the operational plan and revising where possible the key project delivery mechanisms. Feedback will be gathered through the community’s committees and the project staff on the ground.
