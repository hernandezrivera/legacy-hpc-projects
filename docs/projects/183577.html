Project Summary

The Mobile Health Teams project seeks to improve accessibility to essential primary health services for remote vulnerable communities in area C and East Jerusalem facing access restrictions to health services due to Israel's restrictive planning system. In providing health services to thirteen selected communities in Area C, the project also aims to raise awareness among beneficiaries on COVID-19 prevention and mitigation measures, as well as on accessibility of SEA complaints mechanisms. Finally, as long as COVID-19 continues, the project will seek to strengthen the ability of targeted communities to respond to the virus.

1 - Needs

In 2021, the security situation in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem deteriorated dramatically. ISF violence against Palestinians was at record-high levels judging by most conflict metrics – such as injuries, fatalities and live ammunition use. According to the 2021 MSNA, 20.6% of refugee households in the West Bank reported access barriers when trying to access health services in the past three months.Under its core budget, UNRWA only provides health services through its network of 43 health centers and Qualqilia Hospital. In March 2020, in the beginning of the COVID-19 outbreak, UNRWA's Protection team conducted an assessment of 22 communities living in remote areas (Area C) and in the seam zone to assess their situation in terms of access to services and their primary needs. Eleven communities, with a total population of nearly 40,210 refugees, indicated access to health services as an increased priority and their most pressing need. These concerns were raised in light of additional restrictions imposed following the COVID-19 outbreak which has added new challenges to the access and movement difficulties. The increased tensions in West Bank and East Jerusalem, have significantly and further reduced regular and routine access of Palestine refugees to urban and village centers where health services are offered. Many Palestine refugees in Area C have ceased access to any health service. Another significant factor regards to public transportation concerning especially persons who live in remote areas, far away from urban or village centers.These populations are not able to reach health centers by foot or by private vehicles and rely mostly on public transportation. In 2021, UNRWA started offering services to six remote communities and soon increased provision of services to eleven communities. Through the mobile health clinics sub cluster, UNRWA has identified additional communities in need and plans to target thirteen remote communities in Area C with a total population estimated at 63,665 persons. The demographics of these communities also reflect the vulnerability categories identified in the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview of the oPt, namely Bedouin and Herders in Area C, refugees outside camps and people living below the poverty line. Provision of health services through mobile health teams will be prioritized based on additional categories of vulnerability within the target communities. These communities have been disproportionately affected by the economic impact of the pandemic, withlimited means to obtain health services outside the village. Recent studies have also found that Bedouin communities have reported comparatively lower levels of knowledge about COVID-19 (UNICEF KAP preliminary findings 2020). In addition to the identification of the basic health needs for Bedouins and herders in Area C, additional vulnerability factors of gender, age and disability intersect to create additional and distinct health needs for sub-groups within these refugee communities. For example, multiple studies conducted in the oPt have highlighted additional barriers for elderly, pregnant and lactating women, members of female headed households, and persons with disabilities to access health care. According to the 2021 MSNA, 14% of refugee households reported having at least one member with disability while 18% of refugee households reported distance as the primary reasons services are inaccessible to persons with disability. UNFPA has also highlighted additional barriers for women and girls to access basic health services, particularly in rural/remote areas, with elderly women and women and girls with disabilities facing higher levels of exposure to violence, discrimination and access to basic services (oPt Gender Alert Analysis, 2020; UNFPA, Women and Girls with Disabilities, 2019; EducAid Italy, 2020; Al Marsad, 2020). Researchalso indicates that elderly persons are disproportionately affected, with almost one-third of elderly Palestinians reporting more than one disability (BMC Public Health, 2019). The need for continued prenatal and postnatal health care during the COVID-19 pandemic has also been reported to the UNRWA Protection team as a priority for several of the target communities. Recent studies on the Palestinian population estimate that 12% of households reported having a pregnant person in their household (UNICEF KAP preliminary findings, 2020). Moreover, the intermittent imposition of restrictions on movement during lockdowns has significantly impacted the the capacity of women and girls experiencing GBV to receive essential services, including health services. As the provision of health care is often a primary entry point for GBV survivors, the offering of essential primary health care for these communities will offer at risk groupsand survivors a vital means to obtain basic health services, information on available UNRWA GBV hotlines and support, and obtain appropriate referral services. The vulnerability of the target population to protection risks following rights violations and the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees requires UNRWA to ensure that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries, are accessible and known to the target population, including feedback and complaints on goods and services and more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single headed households, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers and elderly women. Information will be obtained through surveys, interviews and focus group discussions UNRWA plans to implement across the West Bank in 2022.

2 - Activities

1. Provision of health services. UNRWA will introduce three mobile health teams (MHT) to ensure that remote refugee communities have access to primary health care services, targeting thirteen communities in Area C and Seam Zone with a total population of up to 63,665 beneficiaries, namely: Barta’a, Kufor, Qaddom and Habla. Barieh Rashaydeh, Al Rashaydeh, Um Al kheir, Dkeikeh, Um Al daraj, Beit Awwa, Al Eizariye , Khan alahmar, Silwad, An Nabi Samwil. These localities are located several kilometers from the nearest health facility. CARE and UNRWA will provide services to Arab al-rashaydeh complementing each others and in different locations. Al Ezarayie and Silwad while also part of Area B, both of them include certain parts under area C. Each MHT comprises the following team members: a medical doctor, an assistant pharmacist, a staff nurse, a midwife nurse, a psychosocial counselor, a clerk, a driver, and two cleaners. Cleaners will be identified from the communities served. MHT will provide primary health care such as medical consultations, examinations, drug dispensary in a suitable space identified and secured by the communities themselves. MHT will strive to ensure readiness to interfere in case of need; capacity building (of the team and the community) on how to deal in case of any emergency as first aid, life support, infection control; sufficient equipment, material and drug supply; stockpiling lab supplies, drugs and disposables, enhanced coordination and communication (with all related health providers and the local community) and provision of health care services. In response to the distinct needs of the groups identified above, the provision of mobile health services will prioritize persons with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, children andfemale headed households. These groups will be prioritized for health services in addition to the high-risk groups for COVID-19 (chronically ill and NCD patients) and SSNP status refugees who are routinely prioritized for health services (abject poor refugee families). 2. Awareness raising material on COVID-19 preventative measures, PPE and individual Hygiene kits will be distributed to all patients. The addition of psychosocial counselors and midwives will allow UNRWA to identify vulnerable individuals, make referrals for GBV survivors, and conduct the community engagements aspects of the project. Health services will be provided to remote communities in consultation with the communities in order to identify safe, confidential and accessible health services to all members of the community, including monitoring and responding to feedback. 3. Health staff training. All health staff working within the project will complete PSEA training and are obligated to report all suspected cases in accordance with UNRWA's PSEA framework.4. Coordination. To ensure an efficient health service provision, UNRWA will coordinate with actors in the field through the health cluster and directly to avoid overlapping. In addition, UNWRA will coordinate with the MoH for the organisation of an MoH vaccination campaign.5. Distribution of PPEs (including sanitizer, masks, soap) to beneficiaries as well as awareness raising materials in regards of limit the spread of Covid-19. 6. Provision of home distribution of medication and possible services to elderly and people with disability by visiting them in their houses to provide the medications if needed, and increase their accessibility to health services.7. Monitoring: UNRWA will conduct a satisfaction survey to evaluate the project implementation and ensure community engagement in the project. The survey will be filled directly by the beneficiaries. In cases of illiteracy, an UNRWA staff who is neutral from the project will assist in filling the survey in coordination with protection team. UNRWA’s protection team will also continue to monitor and communicate directly with community leaders and representatives regarding their satisfaction with the services and to support remote health teams to address the health needs of the most vulnerable groups within the community.8. Consultations will be held with target groups, including vulnerable populations (women and girls, including women with disabilities, single headed households and elderly women) to inform the feedback and complaint channels for SEA. This includes a survey, interviews and focus group discussions identifying reporting barriers and mitigation measures. Existing complaints channels, SOPs and PSEA strategy will be adapted to reflect feedback from consultations with beneficiaries. 9.This project has built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA in accordance with CBCM SOPs and internal SOPs on Case Management which support frontline staff to safely identify and refer protection cases within the agency. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA works closely with the PSEA Network , including compulsory reporting and will also strengthen existing processes and raise issues requiring technical support and/or external referral through this forum. 10.Age and gender-sensitive PSEA awareness raising materials, containing information on rights, safe complaint channels and how to access survivor-centered assistance, will be disseminated during all MHT visits 11. All MHT health staff , including those operating complaint channels and feedback reporting mechanisms, will complete at least one PSEA training during the project period, to strengthen SEA safeguards for reporting SEA and referrals. 12.MHT staff will indirectly engage in the HCT oPt PSEA Network, through the active participation of UNRWA PSEA focal points and senior management, including contribution to the development of interagency workplans and SOPs.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback and complaints relating to specific cases is followed up according to internal SOPs used by health staff to adapt services, interventions and referrals for support services to meet the needs of families and individuals. In addition, project staff collect and analyse feedback and recommendations from the affected populations to inform decision making about the scope and modality of service provision and overall design of the project. As part of the satisfaction survey, beneficiaries are provided with details about the feedback hotline as well as specific information about UNRWA’s zero tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse and how to safely report SEA cases and supportservices available. Feedback obtained through consultations on SEA complaints channels will also inform improvements to accessibility for vulnerable populations.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :
9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The MHT project adopts a variety of mechanisms to facilitate the meaningful participation, feedback and complaints from all population groups. This includes a dedicated free phone hotline, in-person satisfaction surveys, and phone hotlines for SEA and serious misconduct complaints. Recognizing the limitations of these mechanisms to persons with disabilities, children and the elderly, and Bedouin/herder communities, project staff also reach out to these populations through dedicated focus group discussions and interviews conducted by Health staff. Technical protection staff working thematically on SEA, child protection, gender and disability inclusion will also advise where additional mechanisms should be developed and existing mechanisms strengthened to improve access to these populations, including through training of project staff, awareness raising of feedback and complaints channels with community based organisations representing these groups, linking with community-based complaint mechanisms and improving cross-programmatic referrals within UNRWA departments (health, education, infrastructure and camp improvement and relief and social services). The UNRWA Protection Department has also taken the lead in enhancing the accountability to affected persons (AAP) in UNRWA's service delivery in the context of COVID-19, establishing online platforms for information sharing and feedback, hotlines, and conducted dedicated outreach. Finally, the COVID-19 emergency has increased demands on UNRWA and its services and presented challenges to establishing appropriate communication with communities, as well as to accountability mechanisms in the context of COVID-19 restrictions. The Protection Department has led the establishment of dedicated AAP lens to the COVID-19 response, including drafting key communications to the refugee community.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The MHT project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action and unintended negative consequences are minimised. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases are identified and referred to specialised support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultation hotline is available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. For the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritises the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. Thisincludes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychological consultation hotline available to all refugees. In addition, UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project.
