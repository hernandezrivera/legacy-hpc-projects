Project Summary

The proposed action falls under the 2018-2020 HRP strategic objective 2 “the basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”. Specifically, the project will support and enhance the coping capacity of vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns.
The action will provide emergency assistance to acutely vulnerable households living in substandard shelters in Middle Area governorate (Gaza Strip). The action ensures their access to adequate and safe shelters and strengthens their capacity to cope with future shocks through a community-based approach. In total, 700 households (3,920 beneficiaries at households’ level) will be assisted, through the urgent rehabilitation of 200 shelters and the provision of 500 emergency winter kits to beneficiaries for whom a full upgrade is not possible, thus contributing to the Country Strategic Objective 3 “the ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”.
The project design endorses all of the Shelter Cluster recommendations, in particular by providing a tailored combination of modalities to maximize impact on beneficiaries while fostering ownership and resilience through a demanding community based self-implementation approach.
Eventually, the project will be developed based on ACTED and MAAN (partner) experience in emergency shelter rehabilitation in Gaza since 2016. The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with a specific focus on women and vulnerable groups, and a strong Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) component. In particular, beneficiaries will be heavily involved in the action through community-based empowered committees, and a comprehensive Complaint Response Mechanism will be deployed. Gender principles will be mainstreamed during the action and Committees will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases with a particular focus given to women and children issues such as related to GBV. An ACTED protection officer will be a focal point of the committees in order to receive and follow-up on cases.

Needs

The shelter survey on substandard housing conditions in Gaza, conducted in March 2018 by the Shelter cluster, showed than 92% of the Gaza strip housing units possessed some kind of shelter vulnerability, including 6.2% which are unsuitable for upgrading due to reconstruction or relocation requirement. The inability of these HHs to cover their basic shelter needs reflects acute vulnerability factors, such as abject poverty, disability, chronic illness, killed or disabled breadwinner, and occurs at higher rate among female-, elderly- or children-headed HHs. The capacity of these HHs to cope with their shelter needs has been drastically decreased because of the current economic deterioration resulting from the blockade and exacerbated by the recent Palestinian Authority’s measures in Gaza.Living in substandard shelters, coupled with lack of safe and dignified living conditions, worsens these HHs’ latent vulnerabilities, undermines their capacity to face future shocks and heightened GBV risks. It exposes them to additional threats, in particular harsh weather, adverse health impact and domestic accidents. These risks affect disproportionately the most vulnerable groups such as young children, the elderly and pregnant women, who are more prone to health conditions. Additionally, hardship case HHs are compelled to resort to negative coping strategies, such as moving to host families in overcrowded shelters, putting women and children at increased risk of domestic violence and further decreasing their resilience. ACTED’s experience in emergency shelter rehabilitation reveal that there is a link between GBV risks and severe shelter conditions. Assessments conducted by ACTED and MA’AN in Wadi al Salqa in September 2018, show that 45% of households show moderate to severe substandard shelters’ conditions. Out of the 198 households assessed in the sample, 66% reported that women lack of privacy and safety in their house for the following reasons: overcrowded houses (presence of significant number of vulnerable people -children, PwDs, elders…), no separation to ensure privacy for sleeping, changing, etc., bad conditions of latrines and bathrooms. The assessment also highlighted that 19% of households are actually ran by female breadwinners. The project will target Wadi al Salqa community in Middle Area governorate, which have been severely impacted by the Operation Protective Edge and display the worse socio-economic and vulnerability indicators based on ACTED and MA’AN’s assessments (December 2017 and September 2018). The project will give a specific focus to the highlighted vulnerable groups of female head of households and overcrowded households. All proposed activities have been designed after consulting with the beneficiaries and the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), and coordinated with the Shelter Cluster taking into account the current and planned interventions to avoid duplication, to ensure GBV risks mitigation and focus on gender-specific needs and capacities.

Activities

Activity 1 – Vulnerability and technical assessments (identification and selection of most vulnerable households)Vulnerability and technical assessments will be conducted based on households pre-identified as vulnerable by recent technical and socioeconomic assessment, relevant local authorities and technical committees to evaluate current conditions, detailed needs, and vulnerabilities, with the beneficiaries to ensure they can express their concerns and most pressing needs. At least one male and one female per household will be interviewed to ensure gender specific needs and concerns are taken into account. Criteria of vulnerability will include: basic NFI needs, damage repairs/renovation needs, exposure to harsh weather, exposure to domestic violence/GBV cases, safety risk (fire and child safety), adequateness with special needs (i.e. PWD and the elderly). A detailed Bill of Quantities (BoQ) pricing all the necessary shelter interventions and a work plan for the self upgrade works will be established for beneficiary HHs. Based on these assessment phase, ACTED will identify and select beneficiaries for both activities of emergency rehabilitation and winterization of vulnerable households.Activity 2 – Upgrade of substandard 130 vulnerable households in Wadi al SalqaTechnical guidance will be provided to the 130 beneficiary households to build their technical capacity to conduct repair works in their own shelter. Households will receive conditional cash for repair based on BoQs developed in Activity 1. This cash assistance of 2,300$/HH (average cost/HH) will be conditional to the works implementation plan based on the household’s BoQs, which will be monitored through 4 visits conducted by MA’AN field engineers to each household. These engineers will review progress made by beneficiaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. Those visits will also be used to identify any technical issues beneficiaries could be facing and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance by ACTED and MAAN. Moreover, field engineers will also control that the self-implementation results in safe and quality shelter solutions, compliant with SPHERE and Shelter Cluster minimum standards. Tailored remedial action, complementary guidance and/or special assistance will be provided to any household who would fail to reach those standards. Households who would not be able to perform the repair themselves will be assisted through direct repairs rather than cash assistance.Activity 3 – Provision of winterization assistance300 households will be provided with winterization kits to ensure their safety during winter, reduce their exposure to protection risks, thus increasing preparedness to harsh weather conditions and resilience capacities. ACTED and MA’AN will prepare 300 kits to be distributed in the 5 communities (Wadi al Salqa, Zawadia, Deir Al Balah, Mughazai and Burij). These kits, prepared in line with Shelter and protection standards, will include –but not limited to- tarpaulins, plastic sheets, mattresses, blankets.Activity 4 – Promotion of community engagement to protection principles and activities, through the Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPC) 5 Community-Based Protection Committees (CBPC), trained under former projects in the targeted areas, will be strengthened through training on technical maintenance, SPHERE standards, risks of ERW/UXO and protection principles. These committees are involved throughout the whole project to participate to all activities, ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee a better compliance and respect of protection standards,, especially for the most vulnerable groups. Women were particularly encouraged to join the CBPC as active members. All beneficiaries will be referred, when needed, to the Association for Woman and Child Protection (AISHA) in order to address the individual’s and families’ needs

Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects and anticipate and mitigate any challenge. The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. In particular, the AMEU will conduct a baseline and endline assessment to measure progress against pre-identified indicators, as well as beneficiary satisfaction, and draw lessons learned from the project. Those activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and to measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups (vulnerable farmers, female-headed households). Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP standards and ACTED’s SoPs. The mechanism will feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBPC members, ACTED protection officer and independent monitoring staff from MA’AN, to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. During the project, the Committees will be responsible for monitoring the works and ensuring that protection standards are upheld and concerns are addressed or referred to the ACTED protection officer. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring, identifying and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to ACTED as well as the relevant local service and maintenance providers, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, ACTED protection officer, with the support of the Committees, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process. In particular, ACTED protection officer and the committees will regularly visit all beneficiaries to ensure that protection concerns are being respected and addressed through the works. Were any lack to be identified, the Protection officer will offer guidance and complementary training to beneficiaries. Finally, ACTED Project Development Department (PDD), Finance Department and Audit Department will closely follow-up project activities and ensure the action is implemented in conformity with the HPF guidelines, international humanitarian principles and standards, as well as locally defined shelter cluster recommendations and standards. Using ACTED’s global Project Cycle Management tools, ACTED and MA’AN Project Management teams will report monthly to the PDD, on project implementation, outputs and data to ensure that all indicators are on track, delays are prevented and any difficulties are identified and recorded. Similarly, the Finance and Audit department will ensure a constant follow up of all procurement, expenses and transactions. Those monitoring activities will ensure the highest level of compliance and transparency, as well as the production of comprehensive and detailed reports.
