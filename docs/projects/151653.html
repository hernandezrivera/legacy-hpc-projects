Project Summary

The health needs remain high after five years of conflict in Ukraine. The protracted nature of the hostilities continues to affect the health status and well-being of the population and further burdens the most vulnerable populations such as the women, children, and elderly. Currently, 5.2 million people are directly affected in eastern Ukraine, with around 1.3 million people within 20-km of the “contact line” in need of life-saving and essential health services. The conflict has isolated rural government controlled areas (GCA) health facilities from major health centers located in urban areas in non-government controlled areas (NGCA) due to insecurity, increased distances, travel times and impeded access to referral hospitals and pharmacies, including restrictions caused by landmine and explosive remnants of war (ERW) contamination. National health care system has limited capacities to address the health care needs of vulnerable populations in the areas close to the contact line. Mobile health care services are not well developed, and most of health care has been provided in cities or rayon centres, leaving remoted places without adequate health care coverage. Access to health care services in GCA and NGCA is impeded due to: 1. Conflict induced insecurity and limited access to health care facilities; 2. Lack of access to primary, secondary and tertiary health care services particularly affecting vulnerable populations such as women, children and elderly facing limited access to preventative, curative and maternal health care; 3. Lack of qualified medical staff and brain drain; 4. Lack of training of medical staff (physicians and nurses). 5. Lack of sustainable capacity building for health care workers; 6. Lack of access to standardized medical protocols; 7. Lack of essential medicines, medical supplies, laboratories and equipment; 8. Stretched health care financing resulting in increased out-of-pocket contributions in an economically disadvantaged context; 9. Disrupted health information system. The population remains at high risk of acute, communicable, non-communicable and water-borne diseases as well as trauma and landmines. Advanced medical equipment such as endoscopic, laparoscopic and radio-logical and other diagnostic tools are insufficient which affects health outcomes. Humanitarian organizations face difficulties when collecting data to promote evidence-based decision-making and in providing medicines, medical supplies, medical equipment as well as training to health care workers. Needs

MHPSSLack of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services is a key gap in conflict-affected areas, particularly near the contact line. Up to 220’000 people are directly affected by emergencies and exposed to high risks of severe mental health disorders. An estimated 1.1 million people suffer a mild or moderate form of mental disorders in Ukraine. While the need for MHPSS services remain high, the recent MHPSS assessment in the conflict-affected area highlighted that two-thirds of households do not know where to access psychosocial services, and 17% of the rural households report that there is no place to access services in their settlements. The most common person to whom respondents talked about their symptoms, in case of seeking care, is their general medical provider. However, general medical staff receives very limited training in mental health and are generally unable to adequately detect or treat mental health problems in adults and children . TraumaAccess to the quality medical services for war wounded and other trauma patients is limited in GCA and NGCA affecting the most vulnerable populations including women, children and elderlies. Health system capacities of the affected regions are stretched due to the prolonged nature of the crisis, scarcity of funds and lack of qualified health care staff. Lack of evidence-based health data, equipment, essential medicines, trained staff, standardized treatment protocols and insufficient infrastructure for trauma care and emergency medical services are especially critical in eastern Ukraine. Increased number of the internally displaces persons (IDPs) to the GCA further exacerbate the burden on the local health system. In addition, the lack of trauma care capacities across all levels of the health care system affects the blood banks and referral mechanisms and quality of health services provision. Early warning systemsSince 2010, Ukraine attempted to implement the health system reform repeatedly. However, a delayed and fragmented implementation of the reform has affected the diseases surveillance system, which led to lack of coordination between stakeholders involved in detection, investigation and response, insufficient epidemiological surveillance capacities at sub-national and lower level, outdated surveillance protocols and guidelines. Ultimately, it resulted in an overall low performance of surveillance including poor data quality. For the past year, the population in Ukraine is exposed to infectious diseases, including food-borne and vaccine-preventable diseases. Each year, more than hundred cases and tens of deaths are reported due to botulism. Low vaccination coverage led to poliomyelitis outbreak caused by vaccine-derived polio virus type 1 in 2015. In 2017-2018, a large measles outbreak with more than 40,000 cases involved, 70% hospitalization rate, and nineteen deaths reported, including among newborns, children, adolescents and adults. In 2018, Ukraine has also reported seventh cases of diphtheria including among both children and adults. Beside challenges reported at national level, there are specific obstacles observed in the conflict area in eastern Ukraine. Lack of capacities to detect and report diseases cases and poor quality of surveillance data, further exacerbated by the lack of supportive supervision and external monitoring further burden the system. Information management systems The main aim of the WHE dashboard is to gather, visualize and analyze data to support humanitarian health relief work in eastern Ukraine. The WHE dashboard will be an online application that will support multiple dashboards and multiple data sources. Geographical data and mapping are essential to data-driven action in humanitarian emergencies. The main dashboard gives an overview of the public health situation and available resources in the affected region in eastern Ukraine.
