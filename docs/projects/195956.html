Project Summary

In 2023, PIB will extend its support to enhance access to quality, gender-transformative equitable and inclusive learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment for Rohingya refugees and host community children, adolescents and youth (aged 3 to 24 years). PIB will continue education interventions in camps and host communities through direct implementation and local implementing partners. PIB will sustain its community-based learning facilities (CBLF) modality and establish and refurbish new integrated ECD, Myanmar Curriculum, Adolescent and Youth learning facilities. Project beneficiaries will be benefitted from improved WASH facilities through setting up hand washing stations, distribution of basic hygienic, MHM kits and COVID 19 preventing materials. Facilitators and learners for the project LF will be jointly identified with community members and financially supported through 2023. This project includes an evident disability inclusion focus, aiming to identify and assess disability status of learners attending learning facilities. In 2023, PIB will assure the quality of the project’s learning opportunities through updating and upgrading teaching and learning modules for our various education levels, ensuring inclusion, gender sensitiveness and SEL is mainstreamed across our learning facilities.The project aims to count the number of learners, facilitators, youth, and CEM/PTA who are involved in carrying out the endeavor (both male and female). It also focuses on community engagement to reduce gender inequality in to ensure quality education.
PIB will build the capacities of teachers/facilitators of SBK, MC ,Functional Literacy Numeracy which will includes pre-vocational skill training especially life skill and host primary and secondary school teachers and sector education partners on Integrated Sbk, gender responsive pedagogy, inclusive education, safeguarding and PSEA theme-based training, crisis sensitive education and planning to become better teachers and improve learning outcomes of refugee and host communities’ learners. PIB will also identify and build the capacities of Rohingya women to become Myanmar Curriculum teachers and create a pool of female facilitators. In 2023, PIB is planning to incorporate technology-based teaching and learning approaches teachers training approaches in a sample of learning facilities across the various educational levels to assess its impact and benefits on students' learning outcomesto maximize the best outcome of training. To ensure students learning progress is assessed and tracked, PIB will conduct regular learning assessment and an individualizes progress tracking system put in place. In this JRP 2023, PIB will continue to support the community engagement and capacity strengthening of Education Sector partners (ECDWG), parents and affected communities to enhance their participation in the education of children, adolescents and youth. Key community stakeholders, parents and members of the learning facilities committees will be trained on gender, girls education, disability inclusion, child protection and safeguarding. PIB will also work on capacity strengthening of Parents support group and community members on DRR to jointly design emergency preparedness and response plans to equip Learning facilities and communities with required skills to respond to potential emergencies. Furthermore, through different capacity building training on gender responsive pedagogy, SEL, inclusive education and crisis sensitive education planning and management etc. PIB education partners will also be benefitted on planning and implementation of education project throughout the project period. This project will work with both host community and camp through supporting key community leaders, including members of education support groups, to undertake community consultations, advocacy initiatives, dialogue and strengthen their capacity to promote social cohesion and co-existence.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Through this project PIB lines up to address the main challenges and needs of Rohingya refugees and host community children, especially girls and children with disability, to access equitable and gender transformative quality education. For the holistic development of children, ECD interventions need multi-sectoral support, including early learning, responsive caring, health, nutrition, education, and protection, which is not available in both camp and host community settings. In cox’s bazar response, there is a need of developing appropriate guidelines for supporting and enabling young children’s holistic development in camp and host communities following the context of Bangladesh and customizing Early Learning competency framework of education sector, Cox’s Bazar. Also, there is no such referral mechanism or established linkage with the sectors of health, nutrition, WASH, education and child protection to ensure screening and services for young children including children with disabilities. According to the J-MSNA report of 2021, Education enrolment, in particular among adolescents and especially adolescent girls, remained low. Educational attendance of adolescent girls remains extremely limited in both the camps and host communities. In the camps, among those aged 11 to 14, girls are less likely than boys to attend education facilities, with this trend increasing with age, resulting in the highest proportion of children not in school being Rohingya adolescent girls. It is estimated that 86.8 percent of Rohingya adolescents and youth, aged 15 to 24, are not attending any education or training activities. The limited range of educational and economic opportunities exposes them to exploitation, increased exposure to protection risks, and can lead to negative coping mechanisms. On the other hand, the Rohingya youth and adolescents had to face new challenges in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, which resulted in the closure of education facilities in the camps. Limited data exists on the number of Rohingya youth and adolescents are currently engaged with education opportunities including pre-vocational program especially life skill. In the Camps there has been some success at recruiting a high percentage of female teachers, with 56% of teachers being female. However, the majority of female teachers are from the host community and are Bangladeshi, with female Rohingya teachers only comprising 25% of the total. This presents a challenge in the roll out of the Myanmar curriculum, with greater numbers of female teachers with sufficient Myanmar language skills needing to be recruited and trained. Significant challenges also exist for children with disabilities, who attend learning centers at much lower rates than their peers across all age groups for both girls and boys. This is contributed to by insufficient skills and training of the educators, and poor awareness, negative attitudes and stigma related to children with disabilities attending the same learning centres as children without disabilities. Learning facilities also commonly lack ramps and accessible seating arrangements, toilets, and water facilities, combined with surrounding terrain that is often steep and rough and difficult to navigate, and there is poor availability of assistive devices. A lack of inclusive learning and teaching materials, including teaching materials suitable for children with learning difficulties, is an additional barrier.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project will follow the rationalization principles and procedures guided by sector. Following the rationalization framework developed by education sector, PIB scaled down the number of camps that it is operating in to strategically reduce the footprint and aims to cover this project in camps 1E, 3 , 6 & 7 in Ukhiya and 21, 22 & 24 in Teknaf to implement project activities where PIB has the maximum camp coverage and education intervention along with great reputation of work among both CiC and community level stakeholders. PIB will ensure the implementing partners consolidate their services in specific blocks and camps following the integrated approach. This project targets 3-24 years learners to implement Education intervention like Integrated ECD, Myanmar curriculum, Adolescent and youth programming focusing on Functional Literacy, Numeracy and Life skills including Capacity building of relevant project staff, teachers’ professional development, Community engagement, youth led activities etc. Furthermore, all the implementing partners participate in camp-level coordination platforms and also maintain a good relation with Government officials. The project is designed for multi years with intention to secure donor fund for multi-year programmes and to serve in the same Sector in the same camps recognizing that it takes time to build relations with the government agencies, the refugees, and other implementing partners in the camp. Project will also carry out all the education activities through quality implementation that will be finalized by education sector through rationalization process.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project incorporates protection principles into it's all services. It will ensure that the interventions and activities will be targeting the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. It will be followed at every stage of project cycle. The project will strongly keep the following FOUR PRINCIPLES in all of its activities. The project will strongly Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended adverse effects of its intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Access to assistance and services will be ensured in proportion to need and without discrimination, gender, disability or age-related barriers. It will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance.

3.2 - Gender

Being a girl’s rights and humanitarian-based organization, PIB will address social norms and gender related barriers that hinder girls’ opportunities to access education. This project will address these social norms through adopting awareness related interventions increasing the knowledge and changing attitudes about girl’s education among parents, adolescent girls, wider community people, female teachers etc to eliminate discriminatory practices and support girls and young women to learn, lead and thrive. In this project, to shift the gender norms and increase girls’ values in the host and camp community, the programme will adopt and facilitate sessions gender sensitive parenting modules developed by UNESCO. The MC programme component has a special focus on girls and female teachers under this project.This experience will identify, train and build capacities of young Rohingya refugee women in the camps to become female teachers.The project initiates to organizes community reflection.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Plan International is fully committed to the gender responsive safeguarding of all children and young people from all forms of violence. Plan takes very seriously responsibility and duty to safeguard children and young people by not -in any way- harm, abuse and commit in any other act of violence or place them at risk of the same. Plan promotes child and youth safe practices, approaches, interventions and environments which respects, recognises, and responds to the specific safeguarding needs and addresses the protection risks of the differing gender and other identifies. Plan’s Say YES! to keeping children and Young People safe and protected, is Plan’s global safeguarding policy that will guide all Plan’s staff, partner staff and all who represent Plan’s.Furthermore, Plan promotes the active involvement of children and young people in their own protection. Some of the key Protection and Safeguard approaches in place: Harassment, Sexual exploitation, abuse, Bullying and Discrimination.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will ensure accessibility of learning environments and facilities are equipped to meet the needs of children with disabilities and that assistive devices are provided to decrease physical barriers; Community based approaches minimize access barriers, especially for the most vulnerable children; and, blended/multi-modal learning modalities are implemented to provide layers of support for vulnerable children, critical for reinforcement and learning engagement, specifically for children with disability, where layering can ensure audio, visual, and traditional learning approaches are applied. zIn addition, training on disability inclusive pedagogy for facilitators and teachers in host community and camps will be conducted, to ensure they have the pedagogical tools required to differentiate instruction through understanding different learning capacities to improve classroom management techniques to engage all learners, will be critical to improving inclusion.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Through this project PIB will ensure accountability towards the affected beneficiaries and targeted communities and will ensure that their perspectives are systematically listened to and their feedback are used to make critical programmatic decisions in addition to communicating and explaining how their feedback is being used to inform actions. Specifically, Plan’s child-friendly accountability and feedback mechanism will be rolled out and through this mechanism, feedback will be collected and responded accordingly through different channels, including mobile phone calls, SMS, community consultation, and Post Distribution Monitoring. There will be dedicated persons with dedicated phone numbers to receive phone calls and SMS from the community. Bi-monthly community consultations will be organized at the community level to listen to the children, young people, and other community members.

Yes. This project involves the carrying out of few of potential to cause negative environmental effects. Through this project, we will provide DRR session to the learners, teachers will be capacitated on Disaster risk management and DRM kits will be provided to all learning facilities. In addition, different awareness and campasign program will be take as initatiatives to aware the communitiy people on disaster response and reduction. The project also outlines the mitigation, monitoring, and institutional measures to be taken during project implementation and operation to avoid or minimize potential adverse environmental effects while maximizing environmental benefits, and the actions needed to implement these measures.
