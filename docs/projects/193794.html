Project Summary

This intervention aims to contribute to PC objective #1 (Increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations) and PC objective #2 (Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and con ict related violence) through undertaking the following eligible activities:
(1) Monitoring and documentation of violations and related trends in the Gaza Strip, focused on IHL and IHRL violations and con ict-related violence, particularly in the ARA. (2) Evidence generation on the impact of violations on children, particularly grave violations.
(3) Advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns, prevent violations and enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL by all duty bearers.
(4) Provision of legal aid to support access to freedom of movement outside Gaza, particularly for referral patients, and retrieval of seized fishing boats.
(5) Support accountability on IHL/IHRL violations in Gaza through products of legal representation for individual victims of IHL/IHRL violations in Gaza.
(6) Awareness raising on accessing legal aid in context of IHL/IHRL violations, including freedom of movement in Gaza.
The project team will:
- Monitor and document over 1,000 cases of IHL/IHRL violations, including grave violation against children, in the Gaza Strip;
- Issue 1 report and 12 monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL violations.
- Issue 1 report and 12 monthly statistical updates on grave violations against children.
- Issue over 25 press releases addressing protection concerns, IHL/IHRL violations and con icted-related violence.
- Conduct 10 brie ng to members of the international delegations in Gaza.
- Answer over 50 request of sharing information from humanitarian community;
- Engage with UN HRC, SPs, and CoI ( including through submission of 2 written and 4 oral statements to HRC, 1 submission to treaty-based bodies, 2 urgent appeals/communications to SPs, and provision of evidence to CoI).
- Provide legal counselling to 600 victims of human rights violations, of which 500 patients (200 women, 200 men, and 100 children) are represented to secure access to hospitals outside Gaza as well as 5 fishers to retrieve seized fishing boats.
- Implement legal awareness sessions targeting 250 farmers, fishers, rural women and staff members of CBOs working in the ARA received legal awareness sessions on IHL/IHRL.
Through high-quality monitoring and documentation, legal counselling and interventions, and raising awareness of at-risk communities, the project strive to strengthen resilience of the most vulnerable people in need. Monitoring and documentation data as well as outcomes of legal interventions will inform advocacy messaging vis-à-vis international community with the aim of prompting high-level statements (by HC/UN/EU member states) voicing protection concerns brought to focus under this project.

1 - Needs

The context in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains a protracted protection and human rights crisis, characterized by more than 54 years of Israeli military occupation, lack of respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. Since 2007, Israel has imposed a strict closure on Gaza, isolating it from the West Bank and Israel by severely restricting the movement of goods and people in and out of Gaza. Palestinian residents of the Gaza Strip were generally not allowed to travel to the West Bank and Israel through Erez crossing —the only pedestrian crossing between Gaza and Israel— save some few categories of people. Israel left open a narrow window of opportunity for travel that is limited to a very small segment of the population and for urgent humanitarian needs, including for medical patients whose treatment is unavailable in Gaza and are thus referred by the Palestinian Ministry of Health to hospitals in the West Bank and Israel. However, around a third of referral patient are not granted an exit permit by Israeli authorities to travel via Erez crossing [source: PAU, August 2022]. Hampering child patients access to hospital constitutes a denial of humanitarian access for children, one of the six grave violations against children recorded by the UN Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism pursuant to UN Resolution 1612 in relation to children and armed conflicts. The report of the UN Secretary-General on Children and Armed Conflict confirmed that over 1,581 exit permit applications for child patients were denied or delayed in 2021. In 2020, the Secretary-General’s report confirmed the death of three Palestinian children awaiting Israel’s permission to access medical care outside Gaza. WHO monthly reports on access in the first half of 2022 indicate that Palestinians in the Gaza Strip submitted over 9,600 exit permit requests to Israeli authorities to access medical facilities outside the Gaza Strip, of which 3,590 applications were not approved (2,062 from male applicants and 1,533 from female applicants, including 982 on behalf of children). Al Mezan documented the deaths of five referral patients, including three children, while awaiting exit permit approval in the period from January to September 2022.Other forms of grave violations against children are rampant in the Gaza Strip, particularly killing and maiming of children. In the eleven-day round of hostilities in May 2021, Israeli military attacks killed 59 children (37 boys and 22 girls), maimed 630 children (386 boys and 244 girls), of whom 19 were children with disabilities and another 15 children whose injuries resulted in permeant disabilities, and damaged over 180 schools and 80 pre-schools [source: Al Mezan database]. In the following year, a brief military escalation in August 2022 left 17 children dead and over one hundred others injured. Outside periods of military escalations, children are still at risk of death and injury from firearms and explosive weapons linked to local armed groups in the context of the armed conflict, which killed one child and injured 31 others in 2021 and killed three children and injured 13 others in the first seven months of 2022 —outside periods of military escalations [source: Al Mezan database].Part and parcel of Israel’s closure and blockade in Gaza is the imposition of an access-restricted area (ARA) both in land and in sea. In land, the ARA includes a no-go zone that extends up to 300 meters deep into the Gaza Strip along the perimeter fence and a further 1.2 Km that are designated as high risk, where crops are razed in recurrent limited incursions by Israeli forces [source: PAU, August 2022]. In sea, the access varies depending on the location and timing from three, six, ten, twelve to fifteen nautical miles. Even when fishing within the designated fishing zones, Palestinian fishers are at-risk of military attacks [source: PAU, August 2022]. In the first half of 2022, Al Mezan documented 225 incidents of opening fire at Palestinian fishers, which led to wounding 16 fishers, including 3 children. In the same period, 41 fishers were arrested (including 12 children) and 12 fishing boats were seized [source: Al Mezan database]. Seizure of one fishing boat can impact the livelihoods of up to 20 Palestinian families [source: PAU, August 2022]. In the territorial ARA, Israeli forces opened fire in over 300 separate incidents, injuring two Palestinians in the first half of 2022. At least 22 limited incursions were reported in the same period, in which Israeli forces razed a total of 3.3 hectares of cultivated land [source: Al Mezan database].Israel has systematically failed to conduct serious criminal investigations in relation to the violations and crimes committed against the Palestinian people. During the course of the Great March of Return demonstrations, Al Mezan submitted over 80 criminal complaints to the Israeli Military Advocate General (MAG) to request criminal investigation into killing and injury of civilians (including killing of 19 children, and injury of 24 children and 3 women). At the time of writing, 53 cases remain pending (including cases pertaining to killing and maiming of women and children), with only four cases currently under investigation. Only one soldier had been convicted in the context of the Great March of Return demonstrations for the killing of the 14-year-old child, Othman Hilles. However, he was not convicted of the killing, but rather of abuse of authority in a manner that endangers human life in violation of Israel’s rules of engagement. He was given a 30-day prison sentence to be served through military-related labor.

2 - Activities

Activities under PC objectives #1: (1) Monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations and conflict related violence, including grave violations against children and against ARA communities, in the Gaza Strip. Fieldworkers will fully document (i.e. collected relevant data using questionnaire forms, as well as testimonies and supporting evidence) on all cases of fatalities, cases involving maiming of children and women, attacks against hospitals and schools, arrests and detentions, and damage to civilian properties. (2) Documentation data will feed into the database for analysis of violation trends and will be utilized to draft press releases, reports, factsheets, monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL violations and violations against children. The produced materials will be disseminated to over 700 professionals, media organizations, and NGOs through existing mailing list, and will also be shared via Al Mezan’s website and social media platforms. Data will also be used to answers requests for sharing information received on weekly basis from humanitarian actors working in oPt. (3) Field information on IHL/IHRL violations will also shape and feed into advocacy activities, including submissions and communications to UN mechanisms, particularly the Human Rights Council, the UN Special Procedures, and the UN HRC permanent Commission of Inquiry. Locally, representatives of Al Mezan will meet in Gaza with members of the international community to brief them on violation trends and protection concerns. Activities under PC objective #2: (4) Lawyers will continue to follow-up with the Israeli Military Advocate General (MAG) and provide any requested material concerning 53 pending complaints submitted on behalf of casualties of the Great March of Return requesting criminal investigation, and several appeals of closed cases to the Israeli Attorney General. (5) Lawyers at Al Mezan will provide legal counselling to 600 victims of human rights violations, including referral patients whose exit permit applications were not approved and Palestinian fishers whose fishing boats were confiscated by Israeli navy.(6) Legal representation before Israeli administrative bodies for patients delayed/denied exit permits to access treatment outside Gaza. In case of child patients, intervention will also aim to secure exit permits for the child’s companion without whom the child cannot travel to their designated hospital. PwDs in need of legal services will be visited at home by the lawyer to provide legal counseling and sign documents relevant to legal intervention. (7) Legal representation of Palestinian fishers before the Israeli Military Advocate General (MAG) to retrieve seized fishing boats.(8) Awareness raising sessions for ARA communities, particularly farmers and fishers, on protection of civilians in armed conflicts, legal mechanisms to seek protection and redress, and available legal aid and how to access it. Supplementary activities to ensure staff and beneficiaries are both aware of the Center’s policies on Child Safeguarding and PSEA:(9) Al Mezan’s policies on Child Safeguarding (CSG) and PSEA are re-shared with staff prior to the start of the project.(10) beneficiaries are made aware of PSEA reporting mechanisms through awareness-raising material posted regularly on the Center’s social media account, with a reach of over 10 thousand followers Potential risks and mitigation measures:Social Risk: low engagement from victims and/or their families with fieldwork team conducting after-the-fact documentation. Mitigation measures: Al Mezan has decades of experience in documentation violations and our fieldworkers/researcher are well known within their communities, rendering risk of low engagement minimal. We will mitigate the risk of low engagement from target communities by increase its reach out efforts and put more focus on discussing the aims and expected outcomes of our work, highlight its vital contribution to protection of civilian and fighting impunity.Risk to staff well-being: particularly those working directly with victims of human rights violations. Mitigation measures: Implementing partners took necessary steps to adopting measures to address staff well-being and psychological issues resulting from constant exposure to crises and trauma. This includes sessions delivered by mental health on stress management techniques. COVID-19 risk mitigation measures: Visible notice posted at the entrances of Al Mezan’s branches instructing all visiting beneficiaries to wear face masks and avoid physical contact with staff. In addition, hand sanitizers have been placed around the office and at the reception for visiting beneficiaries to use. Similarly, staff are instructed to abide by the same rules. When feasible, beneficiaries are encouraged to send supporting evidence needed for legal interventions via email or WhatApp instead of handing-in hard copies at our offices. Participants in awareness sessions will adhere to safe distances when attending and, when possible, will be held in open-air spaces. Sanitizers and face mask will be made available to all participants. HRP and FTS: Al Mezan will report on relevant indicators to HRP periodic reporting and will also disclose financial contribution towards the proposed intervention on the FTS.

Monitoring and Evaluation: The M&E plan will include the project outputs, the related indicators, data collection method and tools, roles and responsibilities, and frequency of data collection, analysis, and reporting. Progress and results-based monitoring will be carried out at different levels and by applying different monitoring tools as follows: M&E of monitoring and documentation of IHL/IHRL violations:Head of Fieldwork Units will be in constant contact with fieldworkers out in the field. An electronic database will serve as the primary tool to track progress of documentation of violations, and to ensure all relevant incidents are fully documented. Initially, monitored/reported incidents of violations are compiled on daily basis in an electronic record that contains preliminary data. Fieldworkers are deployed to verify and document the reported incidents using designated questionnaire forms; their documentation is fed into the human rights violations database. At the end of each week, the record of monitored/reported incidents is compared to the record of documented incidents, any identified gaps are reported by the data entry officer and database administrator to the fieldwork coordinator ahead of his/her weekly meeting with fieldworkers to devise a plan to remedy any issues hampering timely documentation of violations. They also plan their deployment plan for the coming days based on lesson learned and trends of violations identified over the course of the previous week. M&E of legal aid:In case of provision of assistance and interventions to referral patients and their companions, an electronic database is utilized to keep track of the beneficiaries, their due hospital appointments, and the result of the intervention. Each beneficiary is assigned a record that contains all the communications and measures taken vis-à-vis the duty bearers, including scanned copies of said communications. Records relevant to this action are clearly designated as such in the database to avoid overlap and duplication of service with other projects. M&E of information sharing, publication and dissemination:Requests of sharing information received from humanitarian community in the oPt are recorded and processed by data entry officer, with answered requests earmarked as such on daily basis. Publication are drafted by researcher and reviewed by coordinator/program director before publication. Efficiency of dissemination of publications is evaluated by information/database officer by reviewing stats relevant to views and interactions on social media, clicks on webpages, re-publication of statements and reports on media outlets’ websites, and articles quoting or citing project’s published material. M&E of advocacy activities:Evaluation of interaction with UN special procedure is gauged through regular monitoring of relevant SP websites for issued statements and reports that reflect concerns raised by implementing organizations through communications/urgent appeals to SPs. Additionally, SP communications to Israel on issues and concerns raised by implementing organizations as well as the State’s response are regularly recorded from the web-portal (https://spcommreports.ohchr.org/). Assessment of advocacy vis-a-vis HRC is gauged by reviewing adopted resolutions at the end of each regular session which are relevant to the oPt and adopts messaging and asks put forth by civil society active in the oPt, including Palestinian human rights organizations. M&E of community capacity building:Training activities utilize oral and written evaluations/feedback after training, and pre-training and post-training tests to precisely measures learning, whereas awareness-raising sessions will use satisfaction evaluation at of each session, and in cases of illiterate groups, a verbal evaluation of satisfaction will be conducted. Program Evaluation: Al Mezan organizes at least one evaluation session annually (in January) whereby a gender balanced group of beneficiaries is invited to participate in focus group discussions to elicit their feedback for Al Mezan’s annual evaluation process. Al Mezan’s training staff is responsible for collection of data with support from internal focal points, including the fundraising officer and project coordinator. Data is analyzed and used for learning, planning and reporting.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Given the fact that affected communities are the main source of data for Monitoring and Documentation, their involvement starts at an early stage when fieldworkers obtain testimonies from victims and eyewitnesses, and in the process, victims are provided with explanation of the work and its expected outcome. Further, victims of violations are referred to Al Mezan’s Legal Aid Unit to discuss their legal options. Legal services are implemented in a manner that ensures respect of protection mainstreaming principles, most importantly ‘do no harm’ principle. The recipients of legal services are informed of possible ramifications of the legal actions, and it will be made clear that the final decisions to initiate any legal action is theirs to take. Al Mezan's code of conduct, read and signed by all staff members, sets clear guidelines for service providers on dealing with beneficiaries, particularly children who in case of being the precipitant of any service must be accompanied by their legal guardian at all times.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Monitoring of IHL/IHRL violations will encompass all affected communities in the Gaza Strip (children, women, men) while documentation work will cover all cases of killings and priorities documenting cases of injuries for these groups in the following order: children; women; PwDs; medical personnel; and journalists (during documentation of injuries, cases with severe to moderate injuries will take precedence over cases with below-moderate injuries). During monitoring and documentation action, victims who are identified by fieldworkers as potential beneficiaries of legal intervention are refereed to lawyers. In circumstances were victim's injures or condition (PWD) hinders his/her ability to reach Al Mezan's offices, lawyers will arrange a home visit to provide legal aid, take affidavits, and sign power of attorney. Information, statistics and trend analyses based on monitoring and documentations work are shared with the public and stakeholders on regular basis through implementing partner's websites, social media accounts and mailing lists. Printed materials are disseminated free of charge and accessible for all those interested at implementing partners’ multiple offices in the Gaza Strip.
