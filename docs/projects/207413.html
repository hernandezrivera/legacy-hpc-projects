Project Summary

The project seeks to increase the capacity of conflict-affected populations in the North West, South West and Far North regions to prevent and resolve HLP disputes. This will be achieved through the provision of legal protection services to 20,000 individuals to improve knowledge on HLP rights and procedures and increase the ability to advocate and protect rights of displacement-affected individuals.
In the Far North, the project will target IDPs, returnees, out-of-camp refugees and vulnerable host community members whereas in the NWSW, same groups of people will be targeted except for refugees. In all activities, NRC will prioritize women and girls, elderly persons and persons living with disabilities as they are disproportionally facing protection risks linked to their HLP rights.
Information on HLP rights, responsibilities and remedies will be provided to all crisis-affected persons in the project locations irrespective of sex, religion, ethnicity, and displacement status. Persons having difficulty in accessing housing, land, and property, and especially those at risk of forced eviction will receive individual legal assistance to ensure they can exercise the HLP rights and secure their assets. Geographic location for information sessions will be defined based on data collected through multisectoral needs assessments, HNO, partners and local authorities.
Returnees will be accompanied in re-accessing land through close collaboration with administrative and local authorities. Local partners, community and traditional leaders will play a key role in contributing to social cohesion among displaced and host populations. Local authorities and traditional leaders with a special emphasis on women leaders will be at the forefront of conflict resolution using collaborative dispute resolution methods (e.g. mediation). The regional and divisional delegations of the Ministries of both State Property and Land Affairs, and Social Affairs, Ministry of Agriculture, will provide technical support to these structures and ensure that they respect existing laws and policies.
Workshops will be organized to enhance the capacities of local and traditional authorities, women and youth leaders, and humanitarian actors participating in existing conflict prevention/resolution mechanisms based on the Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR) approach and associated tools.
As co-lead of the HLP AoR, NRC will leverage this project to enhance the coordination of HLP interventions with partners and other clusters, including in the organization of monthly meetings, sharing of updates and joint advocacy with partners.
The ultimate outcome of the project is a greater enjoyment of HLP rights and increased security of tenure for the affected populations.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Cameroon is currently grappling with two major violent conflicts. The conflict in the NWSW that started in 2017 has created humanitarian needs among 1.7million people including 638,421 IDPs and 442,580 returnees with 87,402 others having fled to Nigeria as refugees . In the Far North region, the number of people in need following violence perpetrated by Boko Haram is 1.6 million. This includes 453,661 IDPs, 198 940 returnees and 46,785 out-of-camp refugees Whereas security of tenure and improved access to HLP rights are essential to the protection and dignity of people affected by displacement, forced displacements in NWSW and in the Far North regions have resulted in the violation of many HLP rights. According to the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by IOM in 2023, 75% of affected populations in the FN are engaged in agriculture as their principal source of livelihood while in the NWSW, 84% of the surveyed populations said farming was their main source of food . Unfortunately, access to arable land is still very challenging, especially for women and girls due to systemic, social and economic challenges such as discrimination, obnoxious customary laws and practices and poverty. Most customary laws attribute land ownership to men and boys while women and girls have only use rights. Moreover, inheritance is often reserved for men and boys to the detriment of women and girls who are themselves considered as part of a man's property. Generally, in most cultures, it is a taboo for a woman to own land. While access to land and adequate housing is a challenge for displaced people, women and girls are disproportionately affected. This is the same for people living with disability. The same assessment shows that 40% of people surveyed in the FN had their houses destroyed because of conflicts and natural disasters while 78% of persons affected need assistance to access adequate housing. The pursuit of decent accommodation has also exacerbated conflicts between displaced and host communities as host communities are reluctant to rent their houses to IDPs as they believe they won’t be able to pay rent. IDPs who find decent accommodation are facing threats of eviction, as many lack legal protection and/or the means to cover rent consistently. In the NWSW, only 14% of the surveyed households indicated that their occupancy was backed by a formal rental agreement. Apart from these, other sources of conflicts identified in the project locations are lack of knowledge of HLP rights and responsibilities, land-grabbing and deprivation of inheritance which mostly affects women and girls. • Provision of counselling on HLP rights, • Provision of legal assistance on HLP. • Capacity building and advocacyThe project will enhance disputes resolution mechanisms by providing them with material and equipment they need to conduct their activities (i.e. equipping the meeting room of trained traditional councils with seats and a tables, providing GPS device to MINDCAF to enable them trace disputed boundaries for proper demarcation, transport facilities and other needs identified.) By providing information, counselling, and legal assistance to target populations, the project will help to create an environment where there is increased respect for human rights. With improved knowledge on HLP rights and remedies including procedures and different legal options, affected populations will be empowered to take appropriate actions to defend and enjoy their HLP rights. Legal protection services will help to increase access to arable land and adequate housing for vulnerable individuals and reduce cases of forced eviction and other threats to the enjoyment of HLP rights.Ultimately, these interventions will help to increase the security of tenure and contribute to durable solutions.
