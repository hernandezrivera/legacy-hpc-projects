Project Summary

The intervention will address urgent food insecurity (IPC 3, 4 & 5) through multi-purpose, unconditional cash assistance and market support; livelihoods support aims to contribute to food security and improve dietary diversity and reduce the use of negative coping strategies for vulnerable host, IDP and returnee populations (Cluster Objective 1); it will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support (Cluster Objective 2) and reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (Cluster Objective 3). The project will also ensure protection (Gender-based Violence (GBV) AoR & Mine Action AoR) and age, gender and diversity inclusion principles are mainstreamed throughout all activities.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire. The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 identifies 8.9M out of the 12.4 M (72%) as the people in need of humanitarian assistance including refugees. The 2022 overview shows 8.54 million of them are South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 328,000 of them are refugees and asylum seekers. The document further states, two million people are internally displaced; of these 1.4 million require urgent support, and 1.8 million are returnees, of these 1.2 million have urgent needs. In addition, there are 6 million vulnerable host community/non-displaced people, of whom some are older persons or infirm, or persons with disabilities. The situation become worse when compared to the previous year data from the 2021 South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan, more than 65% of the South Sudanese population need some form of humanitarian assistance and protection. The people in need of support is showing an increasing trend for the years from 2017 to 2022. The signed Revitalized Peace Agreement on the Resolution of the 2016 has led to an increase in voluntary return of refugees from the neighbouring countries thus putting high pressure on the demand for access for food and on limited social services in the area which calls for attention High food insecurity levels are a major concern in the targeted areas. The identified needs will focus on prioritised vulnerable target beneficiary group. The target beneficiaries selection will focus on the most vulnerable households in the target areas and will be based on a set of predetermined selection criteria that include both social and economic vulnerability criteria, such as asset ownership, food security status, households headed by a member of a vulnerable and/or marginalized group (women, disabled persons, child and/or elderly), household with large numbers of dependent family members (non-productive members and must be linked to low income). Returnee households and those with recent experience of disaster and conflict or displaced persons will also be prioritized based on the context. According to the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (Oct 2020-Jul 2021), chronic vulnerabilities in the country were exacerbated by several shocks: floods, insecurity, the macro-economic crisis and the effects of COVID-19 preventive measures. These trends show the possibility of famine during the lean period between harvests. According to the Conflict Sensitivity Resource Facility, South Sudan has suffered from intermittent conflict attributed to multiple factors.

2 - Project activities

DCA will target most vulnerable HHs with multipurpose, conditional and unconditional cash assistance and abled-bodied vulnerable households endorsed with labour will be targeted with cash for work activities. DCA will provide production inputs, such as crop and vegetable seeds and tools, fishing kits to targeted HHs and trained them on post-harvest handling, storage, preservation, utilization, nutrition, marketing and value addition and linked to local traders to open opportunities to sell surplus produces. The project will provide critical support to selected markets so that markets recover and grow and will be more resilient to shocks in the future. DCA will establish and strengthen cash-based emergency response mechanism/CMDRR to support crisis/shock affected populations with life-saving support. Covid-19 preventive measures will be part of all activities. DCA will provide capacity building for community leaders (teachers, religious leaders, chiefs, and opinion leaders), on SGBV prevention. Women will be trained in vocation skills and provided with start-up kits.

3 - Project indicators

Monitoring will take place throughout the project, at baseline, mid-term and end of project. In addition, post distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted after each distribution. PDMs will be planned at a relevant distance from the distribution to enable the measurement of impact, e.g. ca. 3 weeks after a cash distribution, but allowing for 2-3 months for a PDM after the distribution of seeds and tools. Indicators: % of targeted HHs report acceptable food consumption score. % of targeted HHs report reduction in average coping strategy index score for the target population.% of targeted women reporting shared decision making on cash transfer use % of targeted HHs using livelihoods inputs to produce food for own consumption and sale% of the targeted HHs report improved resilience to external shocks.% of targeted market actors reporting increased number of vendors or goods for sale % of trained market actors reporting improved capacities in business as result of market support initiatives% Reduction in conflict related risks% of women with increased participation in decisions making at community level

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

DCA chooses a cash-based intervention because money is easier to move, therefore responding with cash where markets are functioning is faster and more efficient. At the same time, it gives people choice and dignity and supports the local markets. Voucher-based activities also support the local market but allow for less choice. For this reason, voucher approaches are secondary to direct cash.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Monthly market price assessments are conducted as an integral part of the project. The data also contributes to the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (REACH/VAM) for improved coordination at national level. Market assessments together with representatives of the local trade union are conducted in targeted locations to determine gaps and develop an action plan on what market strengthening activities are prioritized. In the past, the following have been implemented to strengthen market functionality: safe storage to allow for prepositioning before the rainy season, drainage improvement, solar lights, market stalls, especially for use by petty traders, transport allowances for mobile traders to deliver goods to displaced populations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DCA and partners hold quarterly review meetings, where community feedback and complaints are discussed and analyzed as part of the ongoing lessons learnt exercise. Community feedback is a major source of information for improvement and adjustments of projects and to inform the design of new proposals. The feedback and compliant systems on the ground will be used to avoid possible protection risks on the beneficiaries as a result of cash transfer.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DCA and its partners aim to address inequalities through inclusion and participation through careful consideration of protection risks and existing inequalities and in consultation with targeted populations, specifically with vulnerable and marginalized groups. As an integral part of the project planning and design phase, potential barriers will be identified and mitigation measures, such as additional support to and prioritization of certain groups (i.e. people with disability, elderly) will be considered and guided by the beneficiary selection criteria. In addition, the targeted communities will be sensitized regarding the importance of inclusiveness and the targeting rational. Through the establishment and close collaboration with community-led project committees, DCA and its partners will monitor access and inclusiveness on an ongoing basis. Early identification of changes in the ability of beneficiaries to access assistance is addressed and mitigated. An example of a mitigation measure is the provision of transport to physically challenged beneficiaries to allow them to access distribution sites and markets. The choice of distribution site also takes into consideration the distance beneficiaries have to travel.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DCA provides PSEA training to implementing partner staff on an annual basis. Targeted communities are informed about DCAs code of conduct for staff and partner staff, including PSEA. This ensures that beneficiaries know what to expect and can recognise misconduct. They also receive information about the feedback and complaints mechanism and related confidentiality regulations in place, especially for sensitive complaints. The feedback and complaint mechanisms are multi-layered, allowing to bypass any party if they are directly involved in the issue or are not fully trusted for any reason. Since DCA’s activities have higher focus on cash transfer, protection issues arising from conditional and unconditional cash transfers will also be discussed during the orientation to beneficiaries on PSEA for an enhanced beneficiary protection from possible sexual exploitation and abuse.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1. Timing and location of distributions/trainings are key to mitigate this risk. Timing is chosen to ensure that beneficiaries are able to reach the location and return home during daylight hours. Beneficiaries are advised to travel in groups. Locations are chosen to be as close as possible to the biggest number of vulnerable beneficiaries. Transport is arranged for the most vulnerable, including people with disabilities and older people. In addition, the FSP registers an alternate person of trust, chosen by the primary beneficiary themselves, to attend the distribution on their behalf in case of sickness or difficulty accessing the location e.g. during rainy season. Providing any sort of resources, conditional or unconditional cash transfer to the identified beneficiaries, in conflict environments is risky and one of the risks is that parties to the conflict may divert resources intended for humanitarian purposes and use them to fuel the conflict. DCA has extensive experience in conducting in-depth assessments to identify the right beneficiaries to be included in the support and the selection criteria to be used are very helpful in bringing onboard the right beneficiaries. The rapport DCA build with the community-based stakeholders will serve to reduce the risks associated in the CBT/CVA programming. 8.2. A code of conduct, including commitment to PSEA and safeguarding policies is signed by partner staff, DCA staff and FSP staff. All major distributions are monitored by DCA and in many cases the donor. All distributions have a feedback and complaints table set up for beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The code of conduct is discussed during community consultations to ensure the community members know what behavior is deemed unacceptable. 8.4. Lack of awareness and access to services has been identified as a major gap. DCA and partners develop a referral network together with other humanitarian actors, RRC/ROSS, community, community leadership and local authorities and share the information with targeted communities. Gaps are raised with the relevant clusters at state/national level. 8.10. In some of the targeted areas contamination with explosive hazards is still high. While DCA has a Mine Action department, activities focus largely on Magwi and Pageri in Eastern Equatoria. In Magwi, DCA is training one of the local partners to localize risk education knowledge. Beneficiaries can call DCA or reach out through UNMAS hotlines if any explosives are found. Either DCA or another MA partner will then be tasked with destruction of the explosive and clearance of the concerned area. In other locations, DCA shares the contact details of the UNMAS hotline to link communities to services.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will establish Community Protection Committees (CPCs) in the targeted payams through community meetings (general assemblies) who are as representative of the community in order to ensure accountability and credibility. The CPCs will be trained on protection principles, including GBV, child protection, conflict mitigation and rule of law, and they will identify the main threats they face in their communities through participatory mapping exercises, and mitigation measures, referral pathway mapping and early warning systems will be developed for communities through participatory community protection planning meetings. The action plans will guide the communities’ response to act and provide mechanisms to address the protection concerns in the targeted communities to protect displaced people, returnees and host communities from violence and abuse and ensure an enabling environment in which the most vulnerable women/girls and men/boys feel safe. DCA and Partners will support the closure of key gaps as identified by the CPCs through Cash-for-Work initiatives, focussing on the engagement of youth. This approach is expected to have the added benefit of engaging youth positively, keeping them from engaging in negative behavioural patterns, such as GBV, cattle raiding, engaging in inter-communal clashes or being recruited by armed groups. Awareness raising on a variety of GBV and other protection related issues, including sharing information on early warning and referral mechanisms, will be shared will be conducted through use of loudspeakers and local radio. This allows the avoidance of gathering large groups inside and will strictly take place in the open air, or, with regards to radio shows, in people’s homes. Awareness creation and advocacy on gender-based violence (GBV) related issues are also a priority and aims to prevent GBV through radio shows developed and conducted with members of the women groups. In addition, and as relevant to the specific context, women will be trained on GBV, conflict mitigation and basic psychological first aid, construction of fuel-efficient stoves, enabling multiplication and reducing protection risks for women and girls.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Building on already on-going market and cash-based programming combined with livelihood initiatives in the areas, as well as successfully implemented emergency response mechanism (ERM) since 2018, the project will establish a life-saving preparedness measure through provision of unconditional cash assistance to provide immediate assistance to populations affected by sudden shocks including floods. The ERM includes rapid assessments, pooling of existing expertise and a rapid roll-out of cash distributions, which will allow DCA and partners to provide timely and effective life-saving support to affected households in instances of sudden shocks, for example due to COVID-19, floods and other forms of natural or man-made disasters. DCA’s partners will work closely with other actors in the target areas to ensure synergies with other ongoing and planned actions. Cash is relatively easy and cost-efficient to get into remote areas compared to in-kind assistance. Communities also prefer to receive cash because it provides them with dignity and the choice of prioritising based on their household’s needs. Supporting market actors will also be part of the intervention to ensure making markets available and functioning to the communities.Additionally, to prevent flood damage as experienced in 2019, 2020 and 2021, flood task forces will be established and trained on disaster risk reduction and flood mitigation measures. With the lessons learnt from the past and the even more severe 2020 -2021 floods in mind, task forces will be in place and trained and mitigation measures implemented during the dry season in preparation for expected flooding. After participatory assessments, the task forces will be supported to develop action plans, one for each task force/targeted community to reinforce flood defences, such as dykes, and improve and rehabilitate other mitigation measures such as drainage to keep water away from markets and residential areas. To increase community ownership, this will be managed by the task forces and community members in targeted areas will be engaged through cash for work to complete the work before the rains start. It is also anticipated that this response will help reduce the amount of damage and the need for people to move from their homes to less affected areas.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project will centre on providing unconditional, unrestricted cash transfers to the most vulnerable households mainly female-headed households (60 %), as well as elderly persons and persons with disabilities during the lean season to meet immediate food and other basic needs according to their household needs and preferences. In this specific context, the use of unconditional cash allows DCA to optimize the use of funding and increase coverage in a cost-effective and timely manner. Cash and market-based responses strengthen fragile local structures and increase sustainability through local economic recovery. The purpose of the multi-purpose cash grants is to increase the purchasing power and resilience of the targeted beneficiaries in the light of the extreme food insecure situation (IPC 3 and 4) in the target payams and cover half of the food basket for two months.
