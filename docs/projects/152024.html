Project Summary

After over nine years of on-going conflict in North-East Nigeria, intense fighting between the Nigerian army and Boko Haram has resulted in the widespread contamination of the area by explosive hazards. The presence of those explosive hazards is affecting the population and humanitarian actors, restricting the freedom of movement, access to villages, key infrastructures and arable land. With the beginning of movement of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) returning to their areas of origin, it is critical to ensure they are provided with sufficient information and are able to recognize explosive hazards, adopt safe behaviour, and report them to the relevant authorities.
Open source data indicates that there have been 1251 casualties from explosive hazards in 2018 as a result of 211 incidents. It is believed that these figures represent only a portion of the actual number of incidents and victims, given that large areas of Borno State are inaccessible and there is no central data collection system yet.

Results from the multi-sectoral needs analysis conducted by the United Nations in North-east Nigeria between June and August 2018, indicated that the majority of people consulted, reported that the presence of explosive hazards was a barrier to access land for agricultural activities. The lack of access is exacerbating food insecurity and will continue to do so until mine action activities can be undertaken. Many IDPs in Borno State, and Nigerian refugees in Cameroon, indicated their willingness to return to their places of origin when essential conditions are in place. Minimal conditions include inter alia, that they must be protected from explosive hazards.
In response to these threats, UNMAS developed a project that aims at providing life-saving messages in several local languages through two different channels:
1) Locally recruited gender-balanced risk education teams delivering context-specific awareness sessions on the risks posed by explosive hazards to conflict-affected populations
2) Mass media tools such as Public Service Announcement (PSA) on radio broadcasts, billboards and Short Message Service (SMS) on mobile phones to deliver risk awareness messages.
The project will be conducted in close coordination with national authorities and the mine action and humanitarian operators in order to maximize efficiency, avoid overlapping with other actors and concentrate on priority areas, needs and targets.

Needs

North-east Nigeria is contaminated by a wide range of explosive hazards that did not explode or have been abandoned. Although the full scope and scale of contamination is unknown, reports indicate that as much as 20 percent of munitions that were fired did not explode on impact, resulting in high volumes of unexploded ordnance contaminating communities. In addition to the direct threat it poses to civilians, UXOs and abandoned ammunition provide a readily available source of explosives to construct Improvised Explosive Devices. IEDs have been used extensively by Boko Haram, severely affecting the population and posing a threat to the humanitarian community since they are as indiscriminating as landmines and will remain active over time, if not cleared. Many of these explosive hazards have been laid on roads, in fields, and also in villages. It is critical to develop, deliver and repeat key awareness messages to all the population affected by the contamination: IDPs, returnees, host communities and humanitarian workers. The current capacity for risk education is limited to two international NGOs. UNMAS aims to increase that capacity by identifying potential national Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) to be trained and mentored to deliver risk education projects to the affected population. The main target groups for this project will be IDPs, returnees, as well as host communities living in conflict-affected areas of Borno State as well as Yobe and Adamawa if the need is identified. UNMAS will also conduct explosive hazards awareness sessions for humanitarian and relief aid workers operating in North-east Nigeria.

Activities

UNMAS is building up its response, in coordination with the national authorities, the humanitarian, development and mine action operators and in line with existing key strategic documents at country level such as: The Centrality of Protection Strategy; the Strategy on Protection, Return and Recovery for Northeast Nigeria; the Borno State Return Strategy as well as the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus. The risk education activities will be conducted through different components: 1) Delivery of direct Explosive Hazard Risk Education: UNMAS will establish partnerships with several Nigerian Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) that will be trained to deliver context-specific awareness on the risks posed by explosive hazards to conflict-affected populations. A total of twelve community liaison teams will be trained, equipped, deployed and mentored. The teams will be gender-balanced and awareness material will be tailored to the specificities of the North-east Nigeria context. Dissemination of safety and awareness information to beneficiaries will be undertaken directly by the community liaison teams. UNMAS will monitor the quality of the work conducted in order to ensure the standardization of messaging. 2) Dissemination of awareness messages: UNMAS will design and deploy billboards on well traveled roads and in key areas such as IDP camps, host communities, health facilities, food distribution sites, cash or voucher distribution sites, local markets and water hand-pump sites. Due to the high illiteracy rate, self-explanatory billboards will be designed with a minimum of text. A company will be identified to install billboards, in coordination with local authorities, in areas inaccessible to humanitarian workers, but where contamination is suspected. The use of mobile phones for communication is rapidly growing in Nigeria and mobile phone technology is an effectively manner to deliver SMS with emergency risk awareness content. Sending out SMS will allow outreach to a wider range of community members, of which some will be in areas that are inaccessible. For those who had received Risk Education, an SMS is a useful reminder of the core safety messages. In Borno State, the two largest mobile phone providers are Airtel with 1,257,152 users and MTN with 785,234 users (data December 2017), which will allow the messages to be a cost-effective tool to reach a large amount of people. Short messages will be designed according to the needs, context and culture, translated in the five main local languages and communicated effectively to those in need. Finally, Public Service Announcements' awareness messages will be specifically designed in Hausa, Kanuri and Fulfulde, thus covering 75% of the population, and tailormade for different age groups, gender and occupations (i.e.: fetching firewood, collecting water, herding livestock, etc.). These messages will be broadcasted during several campaigns over the project period. According to a study of the Broadcasting Board of Governors, radio is the dominant news platform in Nigeria with two-third nationwide listeners daily. This project will promote a gender-sensitive approach to ensure that the activities implemented fully take into consideration the needs of the diverse groups in the areas targeted by the project. This will ensure that humanitarian mine action programming benefits all people affected equally and avoids putting some at greater risk. Women head a high proportion of displaced families. Specific messages targeting the most common activities conducted by women will be designed. The project will significantly contribute to gender equality by employing gender-balanced teams within the CSOs and ensure access to risk education and gathering information from both women, children and men, on the attitudes and behaviours of men, women, boys and girls and on their knowledge of explosive threats to determine how at-risk behaviour might be modified within these groups.
