Project Summary

The continuation of protracted crises in Somalia, now compounded by the effects of Covid-19, Floods and droughts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene. Approximately, 4.6 million Somalis are still in need due to insufficient access to portable water and inadequate sanitation services of the affected population in many districts, thus negatively affecting the country’s public health and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities. This spike in numbers compared to 2020 is mainly exacerbated by the situation developed as a result of Covid-19 and its rapid transmission. Lack of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD), leading to malnutrition in the country and contributing to increasing the existing stunting rate of 25.3% in children below 5 years of age. It is also a cause of relapse (ADD) when treated patients and children returning to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited, resulting in limited sustainability of health programs.
According to JMCNA, 53% of the IDPs face challenges in terms of access to WASH services in Somalia (53% as highest pertaining to access to soap and handwashing facilities). As Somalia’s vulnerability to climate change is projected to increase especially with the upcoming La-Nina where there is a likelihood of drought, the country might be further affected by water scarcity, which calls for a stronger collaboration between the humanitarian and development partners to work on collective outcomes. Furthermore, issues related to access to WASH facilities for vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities, elderly, women, boys and girls is an ongoing challenge. It requires availability of reliable data, extensive consultations and community engagement on planning and design while considering unique needs of each group.
The proposed project is designed to boost the ongoing lifesaving assistance and will target the most vulnerable displaced, floods, and drought-affected communities in Banadir, Galgadud, and Middle Shabelle regions of Somalia. At the moment, IDPs and urban poor and rural communities are among the most vulnerable groups in the society. Since the capacity of the local community to address their needs is very weak it is clear that the increasing number of IDPs and urban poor in crisis requires an urgent comprehensive action to support the target populations. The project responds to urgent humanitarian needs including (1) Deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable settlements and communities (2) Reduce the risk of violence against women, children, and vulnerable groups when accessing WASH services, and (3) Provide or restore sustainable access to safe water and adequate sanitation services in targeted communities and institutions.
Further, the target population will be involved in activities planning, implementation, and monitoring. Special attention will be given to women, children, and the elderly, and people with disabilities during the designing and construction WASH activities (i.e. 15% of all latrines will have disability-friendly features and will be constructed close to their shelters to ease access). Moreover, women will be part of WASH committees and will be represented in all community structures to empower them. Minority groups and female-headed households likely to face exclusion will be part of community-based committees to represent the voice of the minorities. Distribution sites will be pre-selected by target population and hygiene kits contents shared with beneficiaries allowing them to understand their rights and entitlements. Different distribution modalities will be used where protection concerns are mitigated and reported if they occur. The household-level distribution will be employed if people with disabilities and the elderly cannot access distribution sites.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Water trucking/water VouchersPAH will implement emergency water trucking activities for 2 months reaching a total of 18000 people with a breakdown of 3600, men, 4860 females, 4140 boys, 5400 girls, including 2700 persons with disability. Activity 2: Water point construction or rehabilitationAs part of the exit strategy for outcome-1 above, PAH will rehabilitate 6 shallow wells installed with a solar system to have a hybrid system (generator set and solar system), submersible pump and construction of water pipeline connected to elevated water storage tanks, and provision of operation and maintenance tool. Activity 3: Water point construction or rehabilitationTo provide reliable and sustained access to suﬃcient safe water to vulnerable communities affected by the floods, PAH will construct 4 water kiosks (connected in strategic shallow rehabilitated in activity 2.1.1) equipped with pipeline extension. To mitigate the risk of violence against women, children, and vulnerable groups when accessing WASH services, a few measures have been foreseen. First, water points will be located close to shelters – a maximum of 500 meters from a household where every beneficiary can access. It will not only allow to increase their functionally but also to mitigate the Gender-Based Violence and protection risks associated with women and girls walking a long distance to fetch water. Activity 4: Capacity building (water committees and WASH training)PAH will provide capacity building and training to community members in order to ensure the sustainability of community water management systems. PAH will select and train 24 water management and Operational and Maintenance personnel (O&M) (30% female), 30% male and 30% people with disability, and 10% from minority groups. Activity 5: Chlorination (standalone separate to O&M)To guarantee the safe consumption of rehabilitated water sources, PAH will ensure that all water sources are free from microbiological (pathogens) and chemical contamination. Testing will take place at the water source, water collection points but also from randomly selected households.Activity 6: Latrine construction or rehabilitationTo reduce the risk of AWD/Cholera outbreak triggered by open defecation and spillage of waste from the damaged latrines, PAH will construct 250 communal emergency latrines (50 in each region: Banadir, middle and Galgadud) to reach 12,500 beneficiaries based on WASH cluster sphere standards (50HH/latrine). 200 flood damaged latrines will also be rehabilitated.Activity 7: Community Hygiene promotionAs part of the complementarity of the water trucking, water source construction/rehabilitation sanitation, and hygiene kits distribution activities, PAH will implement Hygiene promotion activity in 30 villages (10 each) in Banadir, middle and Galgadud region. Community Hygiene promoters will be selected from all the targeted settlements under the project. Activity 8: Hygiene kit distribution (complete kits of hygiene items)Hygiene kits will be distributed among 2500 HHs. Those displaced and conflict-affected will be prioritized. The hygiene kits adhere to the WASH cluster standard of agreed packages including Soaps, Jerry can (Non-collapsible), Aqua tabs, Buckets, and sanitary towels). The hygiene kits have an important role in preventing disease outbreak and helping those affected by the floods and conflict to carry out their essential daily activities such as collecting and storing water and maintaining personal hygiene. Activity 9: Hand washing facilities constructionPAH will install 12 handwashing stations near the water sources constructed (Activity 2.1.1). PAH hygiene promoters and water management committees will monitor the hygiene aspect of the water facilities with beneficiaries required to WASH their hands when collecting water.
