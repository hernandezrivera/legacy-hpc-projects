Project Summary

Emergency food, and cash distributions are tailored for immediate support for the most vulnerable IDPs, their host communities, returnees and their communities of return, including in hard-to-reach areas, in line with SPHERE and Core Humanitarian Standards.
Based on market size and structure, the modality used will be either cash, vouchers or in-kind.
Oxfam team will identify needs, gaps, challenges and opportunities and provide immediate support through cash transfers. Cash is instrumental in supporting beneficiaries to access a variety of basic goods and services while it also strengthens local economies. Beneficiaries will use money to access basic goods from the local markets, simultaneously the cash works as a stimulus for boosting market economic activities. Oxfam will also consider introducing vouchers to provide food, it contributes to the fragile economy especially if there is a risk of inflation when the markets are recovering from shocks. Vouchers are also a useful modality where there are no banks or financial service providers.
Emergency life sustaining food assistance, As may be necessary, the team will carry out emergency responses for crisis affected vulnerable communities consisting of short term food assistance. As a first line response in emergency, and in cases of non-functional markets, especially in the first three months of a disaster, Oxfam will distribute food rations for 3 days, one week, one month or three months, based on how acute and/or chronic the conditions are. Should Oxfam observe cases of malnutrition it will make referrals to appropriate partners and agencies.
Oxfam will ensure that each stakeholder involved in the project can safely participate in its activities. Safe programming (or protection mainstreaming) is implemented as part of the project cycle. Oxfam’s staff, volunteers and implementing partners are trained in safe programming and risk assessments. This includes the identification of the risks, possible targets, and risk prevention, mitigation and management measures: this is then implemented and reviewed regularly. Particular attention will be paid to addressing risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse and aid diversion, and a conflict sensitivity approach will be adopted.

4 - Needs

The civilian population of Eastern Ghouta have withstood years of violence, displacement and besiegement the populations were living in acute conditions. Due to access restrictions, the situation of local population has deteriorated dramatically facing severe humanitarian challenges, including protection concerns, and shortages in basic food and non-food commodities. A report by REACH in February 2018 indicated people’s impaired ability to access basic services due to extensive damage to hospitals and schools; markets, including food and fuel; and enough amounts of water. Following the onset of the military action, over half the population left their homes mostly to IDP sites in Rural Damascus, where they have resided in overcrowded, unsanitary and unsafe conditions and since then significant number of people have been spontaneously returning to the area. In a recent rapid assessment performed by Tamayouz Association, it was indicated that women may be shouldering additional burdens not present before the conflict. When sewage and potable water systems were destroyed during the siege, it created a shortage of drinking water. Women are now often the ones tasked with walking long distances to carry water back to their households. Men also face unique challenges related to the post-crisis situation including their mandatory military service. Any humanitarian response in the area must consider in its design the amount of time and effort required. The targeted communities will be selected based on needs severity, population, risks, access factors and after close coordination with other humanitarian actors. Beneficiaries will be selected in close cooperation with the communities. Oxfam will rely on community committees to identify vulnerability characteristics, then Oxfam will identify the families that match them to the large extent.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Occurrence of gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse: Oxfam staff, volunteers and partners are gender and GBV aware; GBV is strongly mainstreamed in this project; Safeguarding accountability and reporting mechanisms are in place in Oxfam and partners.• The project could be perceived as taking a side in the conflict due to favoring specific communities or social groups: for every project Oxfam will perform conflict sensitivity analysis in order to make sure that the project is enhancing connecting factors and minimizing dividing factors in the targeted locations• beneficiaries cannot receive their due payment from the FSP as 1) they do not have a valid Syrian ID card and 2) without a valid ID card, they could be subject to detention during their movement to reach the FSP. Oxfam will transport the participants' due payments from the FSP office to the targeted community. Beneficiaries will be required to present available legal documents (at least two documents) including Family Book, -authorized Registration Statement (issued by Civil Registry Directorate), uthorized police statement proving the losses of the IDs and contains all the personal information, driving license, and/or SARC ID. All documents must be validated by the Mokhtar..• Unintentionally excluding women from taking part in projects (due to domestic obligations or not allowed to take part in certain types of work etc): When conducting the vulnerability and market analysis, the focus will be given to gender aspects including rapid care analysis.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Safe access is possible for Oxfam team, partners and contractors to asses needs and implement the planned activities;- Adherence to the humanitarian principles of independence, operational neutrality and neutrality- The selected most vulnerable farmers, especially women, are willing to restart their farming activities.- Needed approvals are obtained from Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor, Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform and the Ministry of Local Administration and Environment-Fund is received into the country- Impact on the budget due to the fluctuation in local currency is manageable Risks: - Security hampers access of staff, volunteers, service providers and supplies and services to project locations. Oxfam has a security management plan and security protocols which all staff are trained on and required to adhere to. Oxfam conducts continuous assessment of the situation; security protocols are frequently updated and adapted to ensure risks are minimized. In cases where prolonged security affects operations or the project as a whole, Oxfam will update OCHA and discuss scenarios for continued or adapted implementation. - Perception of breaching humanitarian principles: Carry out a monthly evaluation against Oxfam’s red lines with critical analysis of impartiality, voice and safety. Ensure implementation if needs based program serving the most vulnerable communities, conduct validation and triangulation of data - Beneficiaries data protection is compromised : Conduct awareness sessions to staff, volunteers and partners on the legal compliance with the GDPR regulations, require all staff to take the online GDPR training, extend awareness discussion to partners and other key stakeholders and finally seek beneficiaries consent before sharing their info
