Project Summary

The proposed project contributes to strengthening the resilience of the population of Aleppo, Idleb, and Hama as the measures will decrease their vulnerability and invest in strengthening structures that will help the population after project end to meet their needs. In addition, the activities have the potential to reinforce each other.
The intervention will cover the WASH needs as below:
Water:
- Water station rehabilitation.
- Water networks fixing.
- Concrete water tanks constructing
- Water stations operating.
- Water safety plan applying.
- Water trucking
- Water tanks distributing
- Water in health facilities and schools
Hygiene:
- Hygiene kits distribution
-Hygiene awareness
-Hygiene in schools and health facilities
The project will target men, women, boys, and girls from the three governorates, both host communities, and IDPs from within cities in those governorates as well as the surrounding areas or other parts of the city who live in the targeted neighbourhoods.

Needs

Since late 2018, following an escalation of violence in north-western and southern Syria, the region has witnessed the further arrival of a substantial number of IDPs. Large cross-line displacements from Eastern Ghouta, northern Homs and southern Hama, as well as Da'ra and Quneitra governorates, beginning in March 2018, have led to a significant increase of the IDP population in the region and to a growing strain on resources and services in the region. While humanitarian assistance is reaching both IDP and resident populations, major gaps remain. In order to address such gaps, REACH has conducted a needs assessment to inform humanitarian actors of IDP and resident populations' priority needs and to address critical information gaps on the humanitarian conditions of IDPs and residents living in communities in the region. This data set provides the findings from this assessment. Findings presented in this data set are based on data collected by REACH as part of a needs assessment focused on IDP and resident populations living in communities of Idleb governorate and surrounding areas. Data was collected in 362 communities across 30 sub-districts between 12 and 20 August 2018. Data were collected at the community level, with enumerators interviewing between 2 and 7 key informants (KIs) per community and with KIs selected based on their knowledge of IDP and resident populations in the community and sector-specific expertise. Communities were assessed based on available data on total populations, focusing on those reported to have large IDP and resident populations and those that have received a high number of IDP arrivals in the two months prior to the start of data collection. Collected primary data was further triangulated through available secondary sources. - 50% of public infrastructure in the covered locations have been found to be not operational, often because it has been destroyed as a result of hostilities. Lack of sustained access to electricity. Electricity production, transmission, and distribution have been heavily affected by ongoing hostilities and this lack of electricity has had a negative impact across Health and WASH sectors since all the water boosting stations are being operated relying 100% on electricity. Lack of sustained access to clean water, with the population in the covered areas, often depending on water from tanks (untreated) and other private sources that lead and cause to expand and spread the water born disease among the whole population. Also unavailability of the tanks for water storage, the population are still using Jerry cans for daily water storage method that is quite difficult in addition to the high possibility of contaminating the stored water in camps. In terms of monthly water cost/fees, all the population in the covered communities were observed paying for their daily needed water. Mostly, the payments are done for the tank owners while the others are done to the related LC- Most of the sanitation systems excited in the covered communities were recorded to be whether damaged, blocked, the sewer water opened directly to air or not connected to the main sewer line. • Being targeted by airstrikes and bombardment. Lack of maintenance and equipment. Increase in beneficiary numbers (newly displaced IDPs) lead to decrease the capacity of the main existing lines. Shortage of funds for fuel, truck hire, salaries and appropriate equipment limits collection and disposal of solid waste in Idleb, Hama and Aleppo governorates were major issues. The hygiene conditions in the covered locations were recorded to be in a very miserable situation, The reasons for the bad hygiene conditions were recorded to be: Overcrowding in the targeted villages, with people living in camps and the newly displaced people from Alghota and north Hama. Limited WASH infrastructure led to reducing the practice of appropriate hygiene behavior which is leading to water-related diseases occurrence, Low awareness

Implementation of activities

Overall objective: To contribute to the resilience of conflict-affected people in Aleppo, Idleb and Hama governorates through improved quality of life, and health status Sub-objective 1.1: Improved access to safe drinking water sources for community members through strengthening the Aleppo, Hama and Idleb Water Units in the operation, maintenance, and management of water systems in select neighbourhoods – HA will rehabilitate the water station, water networks fixing, water tanks constructing and distribution, clean water trucking, rehabilitation the water system in schools and health facilities, in addition to operating the water schemes. Sub-objective 1.2: Environmental hygiene strengthened through support to communal waste disposal services in the operation of waste collection vehicles and waste collection campaigns - waste campaigns, - waste collection -fixing the waste collection vehicles - landfill rehabilitation Sub-objective 1.4: Beneficiaries have increased awareness and adoption of good hygiene and sanitation practices through hygiene promotion measures. -Hygiene awareness -hygiene kits distribution this project is linked to the UH-funded sector “reconstruction/rehabilitation of social and productive infrastructure”. The proposed project supports the priorities of the Humanitarian Response Plan HRP 2018 as follows: •WASH Objective 1: Sustainable water and sanitation systems are maintained and/ or restored to improve public health, including through the repair/ rehabilitation/ augmentation of water systems, support to operation and maintenance, support to wastewater systems, and support to solid waste management •WASH Objective 2: Most vulnerable groups receive life-saving assistance to reduce WASH-related morbidity, including through WASH technical training and capacity building for local authorities

Protection Risk Analysis

HA staff will participate in protection working groups, and child protection and GBV sub-clusters to take lessons learned for mainstreaming of protection in also WASH projects. Measures to protect the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be contextualized to the political and humanitarian situation. In order to reduce risks of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence, considerations will be made with respect to the location and design of WASH facilities in order to make sure that there is no sexual exploitation occurs through the implementation process and this issue is going to be managed and monitored through the complaint mechanism and roles of the HA MEAL staff that will always be available over the targeted locations. Moreover, HA team will be coordinating closely with cluster in order to develop a special tool for monitoring the issue related to this topic. For example, latrines in schools, clinics, hospitals, and GBV centers will be lit and lockable. HA will focus on protection of beneficiaries especially women, and children from sexual abuse and harassment in schools, camps, and communities. In this regard monitoring and reporting tools will be developed and implemented and during the project cycle. Finally. HA will focus on enhanced participation of women in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities. All proposed activities are in full alignment (UNSCR) 1325. They have also been designed to be culturally appropriate, inclusive, and confidential. In this project, HA will ensure the safe access to services for all genders and age groups at all times. This measure will protect beneficiaries. Likewise, by installing complaint mechanisms in the targeted locations, which will be checked by HA staff during the time of the project, we will allow any protection issues related to this project to be known and seek immediate advice from the senior project staff in Turkey for a response.
