1 - Needs

The prolonged conflict and political instability in Libya have affected greatly the capacities of the government institutions to respond to crises. Ministry requested FAO to provide support to the Ministry during the current period to allow its institution to respond to needs. The project will address different aspects among them plant and animal diseases as well as technical services in the various multi-dispensary areas. Previous qualitative assessments have shown that the crisis has exacerbated pre-existing challenges associated with agricultural production in Libya, including water scarcity, animal and plant diseases, desertification and labour shortages. In addition to these longer-term challenges, the crisis has ruptured market linkages and disrupted access to water, electricity, inputs, and transportation. Therefore, as the conflict continues, economic crisis are contributing to rising food prices then food security. Although the average food consumption patterns of households remains relatively high, low levels of coping capacity suggest that the current situation will deteriorate. Agriculture will continue to play a vital role in contributing to food security as households face-increasing difficulties.Under the livestock component: Libya is an important net importer of live animals and animal products. According to FAOstat, (2017), animal population is estimated around 124,941 cattle, 7,400,487 sheep, 2,628,894 goats and 64,469 camels. The National Veterinary Services and systems suffer from severe constraints such as fragmented and inappropriate Veterinary Service structures, lack of adequate institutional and legal frameworks. Similarly, there are inadequate and limited capacities and skills of the national cadre, private vets, producer organizations and other stakeholders. The current situation has become worrying especially with the political instability and the armed conflicts hampering prevention or control management programs of major zoonosis and transboundary animal diseases. These are major threat to livestock especially in areas where livestock represents the major source of food and income.Under the Plant Pests Component: Date palm green pit-scale (Palmaspis phoenicis, DPGS) is becoming among the most dangerous pests of palms in the NENA region. The first report of the DPGS pest in Libya was in early 2017 in the Southern Region of the country. Visual inspection of the National Plant Protection Center (NPPC) has confirmed that the infestation has now spread to 200,000 palm trees affecting large rural areas and populations dependent on palm production in this region. Date palm cultivation in Libya has historical and socio-economic importance for communities of the oases due to the high nutritional and commercial value of the dates. The date palms represent the symbol of life for the rural communities in Arabian countries being one of the main food sources especially for the residents of the desert oases. It ensures the food security in case of hunger and secures the livelihood in times of crisis and wars. The total cultivated area with date palm trees in Libya is about 70,000 hectares, with a total annual date production of 174.040 tons. The estimated number of palm trees in Libya is about 8 million planted in different locations. The impact of the widespread infestation of both pests to the palm population and palm based farming systems in Libya.
