Project Summary

Management, coordination and monitoring of activities, and deployment of professional staff are essential for UNRWA to deliver timely and effective humanitarian assistance, to ensure efficient coordination with relevant stakeholders and to enhance advocacy on behalf of Palestine refugees.
While the vast majority of posts needed to deliver the mandate of the Agency in Syria will continue to be filled by Palestine refugees and Syrian nationals, UNRWA will maintain some international capacity, particularly specialist staff in the fields of humanitarian response, monitoring and evaluation, and grants management.
UNRWA will continue to conduct safety and security trainings for its staff across Syria, UNRWA has made significant investments in the safety and security of its staff, assets and facilities despite financial challenges.
Finally, under this project, UNRWA will also sustain the operational needs of support departments, including HR, Administration, Procurement and Logistics. UNRWA has also included a budget for the minor repair and maintenance of UNRWA facilities to ensure safe and functional delivery of services for both beneficiaries and staff.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk: Exposure of staff and Palestine Refugees to Explosive Ordnance, particularly in newly accessible areas. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: 1. Prioritized awareness-raising and training to staff, and community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to Explosive Ordnance (awareness sessions, materials and activities). 2. Coordinate with UNMAS to increase outreach to staff and beneﬁciaries on mine risk awareness including through UNMAS flyers. 3. Coordinate with UNMAS to deliver EORE facilitator and focal points training for UNRWA staff.Risk: Attacks on education and health facilities, which might include bombings /explosions/shelling, fighting/gunfire, arson, fire, looting/theft, kidnapping/arrests of staff or students, and military abuse. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA facilities (including schools and health clinics) are United Nations facilities and marked as such (signboard, UN flag and no-weapons signs). 2. UNRWA facilities are all included on the deconfliction list. 3. Regular engagement with the community to maintain a relationship of trust and a sense of ownership, reducing the likelihood of violence from within the community. 4. All facilities have up-to-date evacuation plans and conduct drills. PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN ClearCheck recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations.
