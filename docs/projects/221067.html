Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has worsened due to the outbreak of civil war in neighbouring Sudan in April 2023, resulting in large-scale displacement and devastation of livelihoods. Floods have decimated crops and cattle, causing serious damage to vital services. The situation worsens through the year, with several counties sliding into emergency in the lean season. Vulnerable people, including women, children, people with disabilities, and the elderly, face challenges such as limited water supply, shelter, sanitation services, and lack of food. The IPC for Acute Malnutrition predicts that several counties will be in emergency in April-June 2025 due to high levels of food insecurity, disease prevalence, and poor hygiene and health.
GBI is proposing an Emergency Food Security and Livelihood Response to Conflict and Disaster affected Households in the affected locations with the aims to address the unfolding humanitarian crisis, exacerbated by the civil war in neighbouring Sudan and natural disasters such as floods. The project will provide integrated services in food security, livelihoods, health, protection, and nutrition to the most vulnerable populations. It targets 12,000 people across several counties, with a focus on women and children. The project seeks $200,000 to implement these services in 2025. Key activities include food assistance, emergency food production, livestock intervention, capacity building, and cash/voucher assistance. The project also emphasizes gender, age, and disability inclusion, and has mechanisms for accountability and protection risk mitigation.

1. Needs

Humanitarian situation has further deteriorated in South Sudan following the outbreak of civil war in the neighbouring Sudan in April 2023 which has led to the influx of refugees and returnees. According to IOM/DTM, there was a large-scale displacement in Northern Bahr el Ghazal where 14,455 individuals were displaced to Aweil East, 11,756 to Aweil West and 9,030 to Aweil North, triggered by communal clashes, flooding, insecurity, violence, natural disasters, and cross-border movements. Flooding has caused large scale devastation of livelihoods with 42 out of 78 counties under water affecting more 893,000 individuals (IFRC Nov 2024). In addition to severely damaging homes, the floods have decimated crops and cattle. Serious damage has been done to vital services. According to infographic published by WHO in November 2024, the flood affected people by county includes Aweil East (91000), Aweil North (31,000), Twic East (55300), Bor South (3,000), Duk (17,000) and Ayod (26,700) respectively. Flood situation leads to spread of water borne diseases, poor WASH practices due to lack of safe water and submersed crops and livestock impact negatively of livelihoods (Reliefweb July 2024). According to IPC projection towards end of 2024 and first quarter of 2025, 6.1 million people are estimated to face crisis or worse food insecurity in 69 counties including Aweil East, Aweil North, Ayod and Twic East in emergency. The situation worsens through the year with several counties sliding into emergency in the lean season (April – July 2025). The vulnerable people comprise of women, children, people with disabilities, and the elderly are exposed to bad weather and protection challenges, including limited water supply, shelter, sanitation services (open defecation) and lack of food etc, exposing them to heightened risks of illness and mortality (WHO Nov 2024). The displaced people are sheltering with relatives or sometimes in public buildings such as schools, in churches and in the open along roadsides. While provision of critical services to the affected population is constrained by poor infrastructure, lack of roads, flooding and funding constraints to respond to the needs of both new arrivals and receiving communities. According to the IPC for Acute Malnutrition, Aweil East, Aweil North, Twic East, Bor South, Duk and Ayod will be in emergency in April – June 2025. High levels of food insecurity, elevated prevalence of diseases, and poor hygiene and health, are the main drivers of acute malnutrition (WHO November 2024).GBI has been providing humanitarian responses since 2019 in Aweil East, Aweil North, Twic East, and Duk among other areas and will; therefore, use its networks, experiences and establishment to provide assistance to the vulnerable communities affected by conflict and natural disaster through 2024/25. In line with the call for proposals and 2025 HNRP FSL Cluster objectives, GBI plans to scale up in greater Jonglei and Northern Bahr El Ghazal, where we have an operational presence. Activities integrating FSL, WASH, Health, Protection, and Nutrition programming at households, institutions, and community level while mainstreaming GBV, prioritizing the immediate provision of lifesaving livelihoods and nutrition services to the most food insecure populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. The project will prioritize the needs of vulnerable people such as men, women, boys, girls, PWDs, PLW, elderly, etc. and this information is communicated through community mobilization and inception meetings in presence of local authority. The project will consult all vulnerable people including those living with disability and collect disability disaggregated data which allows them to identify specific needs/vulnerabilities, risks, and barriers that prevent people with disability from accessing FSL services on an equal basis with other people.

2. Project activities

1. Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3).2. Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds and tool distribution; fishing, vegetable and crop kits.3. Improve livestock intervention; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses. Provide livestock veterinary services to curtail livestock losses, improved meat quality, and milk yield, thereby stabilizing and safeguarding rural and urban livelihoods through livestock vaccination and treatment on common diseases as well as surveillance of diseases. 4. Training and capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training, etc.5. Provide conditional assistance in the form of cash/ vouchers or food. Cash and voucher-based transfers – beneficiaries meet their immediate cash needs in locations with functional markets while ensuring that all households are receiving training in financial literacy to make informed and effective decisions relating to their financial resources, including on how to generate, invest, spend and save money. Training will be delivered jointly to male–and female–household members, as a means of promoting joint decision-making over household budgets. Indicators: 1. Number of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers, and vouchers. Number of Cash and voucher-based transfers beneficiaries – meeting their immediate cash needs in locations with functional markets while ensuring that all households are receiving training in financial literacy to make informed and effective decisions relating to their financial resources, including on how to generate, invest, spend and save money. 2. Number of beneficiaries receiving agricultural inputs (vegetable, crop & fishing kits). 3. Number of kitchen gardens set up at Nutrition facilities. 4. Number of colds chained procured and livestock vaccinated/treated. As a capacity-building activity in livestock, Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) will be provided with drugs and trained on disease investigation, vaccination and treatment, and best management practices to increase output and minimize livestock losses. 5. Number of people benefiting from capacity building & training, asset building, and market support.
