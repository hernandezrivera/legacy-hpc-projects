Project Summary

GGSI will work on improving access to basic Water, sanitation services through the delivery of water trucking, Solid Waste Management, Desludging and the rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure, and areas we anticipate significant inward displacement in Jere, Nganzai and Askira Uba in Borno State, GGSI will continue providing a full WASH package in IDPs camps and Host communities, which includes Water Quality monitoring and treatment and hygiene education. Hygiene education, using carefully tailored messages according to Sector standard, and the distribution of hygiene kits will complement the other WASH interventions. Through ongoing Coordination with WASH sector, working groups, local councils and other agencies, GGSI will be coordinate its response in relation to the gaps identified and associated needs. GGSI will work actively to identify and implement water trucking sustainable solutions and cost efficient sanitation management in the targeted IDP locations. Internal water supply networks, water tanks and toilets will complement more comprehensive HH shelter activities, providing safe and dignified accommodation standards for displaced and for those who have chosen to return.

1 - Needs

A rapid needs assessment were conducted by Global Grassroot Soccour Intiative staff in Jere, Nganzai and Askira Uba LGA in Borno state respectively and 2000 IDPs HH were assessed. HH assessment and IDPs profiling tools were developed and adopted during a assessment. Focus Group Discussions, Key Informant Interviews and observations were used as key methodologies during assessment and profiling. At the end of assessment, WASH conditions emerged as the highest need of IDPs, FOOD condition were also discovered to be poor.

2 - Activities

The proposed Action has been designed according to the needs identified in Nigeria by the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), multi-sector needs assessment, and through communication with OCHA, sector leads and donors. This section of the proposal will be divided into the objectives outlined above: objective One: Enhance displacement management in camps, areas of return and out of camp sites to ensure equitable and dignified access to services, assistance, and protection for IDPs through WASH responses. objective Two: Enhance resilience and improved ability of communities and local partners to cope with displacement and ensure local ownership and self-governance through inclusive participation, gender mainstreaming and engagement of displaced persons. The GGSI will extend urgent need for support to the vulnerable groups among other activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. GGSI will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the well being, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to Protection and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. All GGSI staff will uphold protection principles at all times, as signatories to GGSI’s Code of Conduct, including GGSI’s Policy on PSEA. Moreover, as an agency and Partner in Protection and HLP in all proposed target sites, GGSI will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer protection cases identified through the CRM. In order to ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across the project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles: 1) Prioritize safety, dignity, and do no harm: GGSI will continuously monitor the security context in areas of intervention. GGSI’s team will ensure that community meetings and sensitization campaigns are located in safe and accessible locations and at a reasonable distance from beneficiary homes. Gender-segregated queues and General Protection component, GGSI will integrate gender and protection mainstreaming, ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups in decision-making processes. Strong referral mechanisms will be enacted, enabling direct referral of any vulnerable IDPs to relevant service providers, including victims of assault or GBV. 2) Ensure meaningful and equal access: GGSI will take additional steps to ensure that PWDs can be reached, as well as other vulnerable groups, and ensure that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy. Promote accountability, participation, and empowerment of beneficiaries: process reliant on a strict equal opportunities basis and considerate of protection and gender aspects. Accountability will also stem strongly from the CRM, ensuring timely and appropriate response to complaints. Moreover, PDM exercises will assess beneficiary satisfaction and inform further programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GGSI’s will establish Complaints and Response Feedback Mechanism (CRM) and Mobile Protection Helpdesk (MPHD) mainstreamed throughout all interventions in Northeast Nigeria, facilitates safe, accessible and transparent feedback channels for affected populations. GGSI’s CRM and MPHD enables beneficiaries to voice concerns, complaints and suggestions, while informing GGSI’s partner coordination efforts at all the sites and thereby increasing overall accountability of service providers and the wider humanitarian response. Installed CRM mediums, including complaints boxes, complaints desks, a telephone hotline and email, allow for contactless communication methods granting anonymity for confidential reporting of protection concerns and feedback to GGSI as Protection agency. GGSI’s independent AME Unit will maintain responsibility for collecting and responding to all reports, addressing complaints through protection-centered case-management support and systematically investigating, referring and following up on beneficiary complaints with protection partners and other service agencies in a timely and transparent manner. GGSI’s trailed and tested CRM provides an effective two-way communication channel, ensuring direct links to target populations, and inclusion of beneficiary and broader community voices in program implementation. Further, information gained through the CRM is looped back into the design of program approaches, as GGSI continuously adjusts intervention activities according to feedback received. All GGSI staff will be strictly compliant with the code of conduct, and any breach of the code of conduct uncovered through the CRM will be responded to with serious measure.
