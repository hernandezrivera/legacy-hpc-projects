Project Summary

In 2025, UNDP proposes expanding Temporary Solid Waste Management Facilities (sanitary landfill) for host and refugee communities to safely manage residual waste through extension of sanitary landfill site at Camp 20 Ext. for Ukhiya camps, construction of a new temporary sanitary landfill site near the Teknaf camps, and development of a sustainable solid waste management system for Teknaf Municipality and Baharchara Union. UNDP's Solid Waste Management (SWM) initiatives will also assist in the construction, operation, and maintenance of various Material Recovery Facilities (MRF) to facilitate resource recovery and link to the recycling industry (scrap dealers).
Several compost plants will be built and operationalized to ensure that organic waste is safely managed and to help build market demand for compost fertilizer. UNDP will leverage the knowledge gained from the recently finished project to support the local government (municipality and unions) in developing business model for the Sustainable SWM system by engaging multiple stakeholders and forming a public private partnership.
The project will also strengthen local government capacity by developing plans for operation and maintenance of landfill sites, MRFs, and compost plants, as well as providing them with the necessary tools to run the SWM infrastructure and the system.
Adhering to the district development and growth priorities, the project will aim to develop comprehensive solid waste management strategy for Teknaf and Ukhiya upazilas. The project will build capacity and provide support to local governmnet and institutions in implementing the solid waste management strategy. Besides, the targeted communities will be aware of the SWM system, the importance of waste segregation at the source, and the 4Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, and Recover) approaches, and will contribute to a sustainable solution to SWM through awareness practices and affordable service fees.
A contingency plan for managing emergency waste from any on-set hazard like fire, landslide, debris runoff etc., will be developed for both host and refugee camps.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The activities designed for 2025 are based on the critical needs for improving access to water, sanitation, and hygiene services for refugees and host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf, targeting over 1.28 million individuals. Priority areas include constructing and maintaining WASH facilities, including sanitary landfill sites, to support the needs of diverse groups, such as women (306,486 adult women and 23,726 elderly women), men, boys, girls, and youth (255,043 aged 15–24). The activities aimed at addressing the specific challenges faced by vulnerable populations, including persons with disabilities (30,309 refugees and 19,449 host community members), while ensuring equitable access through tailored interventions. Hygiene education initiatives will focus on adolescent female students in the host community, along with the provision of WASH blocks equipped with MHM supplies and MHM waste management technology, aiming to promote health, mental well-being, leadership, and a conducive learning environment. By emphasizing equitable access and community involvement, the activities aim to uphold dignity, promote resilience, and meet the critical WASH needs of all population groups. At the same time, learning from the experience of the existing landfill site in camp 20 extension, it is necessary to carry out alternative management options to reduce the volume of waste entering the landfill, as availability of land for further landfill site construction/expansion is becoming increasingly challenging due to conflicting priorities for the available lands and overall land scarcity in the area. Thus, a feasibility study is planned to find out the most context-appropriate innovative technological intervention (incinerator/gasifier/other advanced technology) that can reduce the volume of waste requiring landfilling, thereby can greatly enhance the sustainability of the overall SWM system. To ensure the sustainability of the SWM infrastructure and systems established in the host community areas through the planned activity packages, capacity-building support for local government bodies is a key priority. This includes enhancing both their physical assets and technical capacities to effectively manage and maintain the SWM facilities. The support will focus on providing necessary equipment, training programs, and knowledge-sharing initiatives to strengthen institutional capabilities. Robust monitoring and feedback mechanisms will also be in place to ensure refinement of interventions through active engagement of the local government and the communities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The UNDP’s Solid Waste Management project has been designed in accordance with the four principles of protection mainstreaming. The project will undergo with Environmental and Social Impact Assessment (ESIA) for the proposed landfill site and will hold multiple consultation with communities and other stakeholders to prioritize safety and dignity and causing no harms to the communities and the environment. The project will ensure access to the SWM services for all without discrimination, gender, disability and age-related barriers. The project will conduct multiple assessments engaging communities and stakeholders from the designing of project interventions to ensure that everyone’s voice has considered and will establish system to receive feedback and complaints and addressed accordingly. UNDP will reinforce the zero tolerance principles for child labor and gender-based violance across the project initiatives. No one under the age of 18 will be eligible for cash-for-work at landfill site, MRF, compost facilities or any other placement. In addition to the above, the SWM project, with its strong livelihood content, has also to be informed by the concept of ‘decent work’ as articulated by ILO.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Persons with disabilities typically face stigma and exclusion from community participation or decision making due to negative perceptions and physical dependence with a lack of empowerment. People with disabilities and older persons also face challenges in ensuring access to SWM services, cash-for-work engagement, etc. Adhering to the SDG, UNDP works on disability inclusion following United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and are committed to ensure adherence to the Humanitarian inclusion standards for older people and people with disabilities and the IASC Guidelines, Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action, 2019 and to implement programs accordingly. Following that, the project will continuously advocate for the inclusion of youth, older persons, and people with disabilities in the SWM services and decision-making process and include them in the cash-for-work activities through providing enabling environment to work with. The project will also seek to include local government gathering people with disabilities as they can be critical in the assessment of SWM services and in designing SWM system tailored to specific needs. Likewise, older people can be social change agents for developing the SWM system as they have rich experience, knowledge and skills rooted in the local social and environmental context, including potential hazards.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project mainstreams the ‘accountability to affected populations (AAP)’ principle into the project cycle. The project will follow the Sector and ISCG AAP guidelines. The project will consider and take action to initiate ‘information, communication, and transparency;’ to ensure ‘participation, engagement, and inclusion;’ to place ‘complaint and feedback response mechanism'. Different group of people's needs/assistance for SWM services will be assessed through-Community feedback mechanism at ward and bazar committee level-Consultation sessions targeting equal male/female respondents -Consultation to specific groups through specialized NGOs Based on the findings, the service will be tailored to meet the needs of the different groups. The project will set up appropriate mechanisms to ensure the active participation and inclusion of affected populations throughout the project cycle and will place measures to address concerns and complaints.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNDP ensures that PSEA is mainstreamed across all programming and project activities following the UN-wide policy on ‘Harassment, Sexual Harassment, Discrimination, and Abuse of Authority.’ Awareness campaign will target school-going students and teachers to educate them about their rights and the project's commitment to PSEA. Additionally, the project will ensure orientation and encourage participation of project staff, local government representatives, WATSAN committee members, volunteers, implementing partners, and vendors in PSEA training program as required to enhance awareness and build capacity to prevent and address issues of sexual exploitation and abuse. The project already has a ‘Grievance Redress Mechanism’ for safe reporting of any misconduct, including PSEA cases in a confidential and accessible manner. PSEA compliance requirements are also incorporated into partnership agreements, with monitoring to ensure adherence. The project will ensure collaboration with Protection sector to strengthen PSEA implementation and advocacy at both field and program levels.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNDP’s Solid Waste Management project will contribute greatly to reducing climate change impact through reducing emissions from energy consumption, limiting methene emissions from landfill operations, and compost facilities. Prevention of open dumping will immediately reduce the fugitive methene emissions, watershed and land pollutions and promote to a healthy environment. Promoting circular economy through establishing MRFs will support addressing GHG emissions associated with plastic waste. The project will develop compost facilities that will divert the organic waste and will contribute to reducing the methene emissions from landfill. The project will also contribute to restoring the forest through waste prevention and recycling resulting in large amount of carbon-di-oxide absorption out of the atmosphere and store it in wood, in a process called carbon sequestration. Furthermore, the proposed initiative will also reduce the risk of landslide, flood and erosion of fragile ground through basic waste management services and cleaning campaigns.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The proposed project will closely coordinate with the emergency preparedness and response working group throughout the project cycle and contribute to the debris management contingency plan of the ISCG. The project also targets to organize mass cleaning campaign before flood or cyclone seasons/based on hazard forecast as a readiness activity to reduce the disaster impacts. The project will coordinate with WASH, SCCCM, EPR working group and relevant actors to minimize waste generation during the response and ensure robust emergency waste systems are in place. In addition, the project will have separate contingency fund allocation to facilitate prompt, adaptable responses to unforeseen crises, enabling the mobilization of resources and support without delay. These contingency planning measures ensure the resilience and sustainability of SWM system, safeguarding both the infrastructure and the well-being of vulnerable refugee and host populations during emergencies.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project plans for engagement with youth by supporting their involvement in sustainable waste management and entrepreneurship. Specifically, it plans to create a scrap dealers' association and empower individual waste collectors, including young people, to create small businesses. This approach not only provides livelihood opportunities for youth but also fosters skills development and promotes self-reliance, encouraging youth to contribute actively to a sustainable waste management ecosystem in their communities. Additionally, young people will be involved in awareness campaigns, spreading knowledge on environmental sustainability and CfW opportunities involving material recovery, recycling and composting practices.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project supports promotion of self-reliance and resilience for refugees by establishing SWM facilities and promoting livelihood opportunities, including waste collection, material recovery, recycling, composting and final disposal. Refugees involved in CfW activities and trained in waste management skills would gain valuable experience that contributes to self-reliance and sustainable repatriation.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The project aligns its host community activities with district development and growth priorities by establishing a sustainable SWM system that supports environmental health and resilience of communities in the face of emerging developmental and environmental challenges. The project also focuses on building the capacity of local governments, which strengthens municipal services and infrastructure in line with district development goals. In this context, Teknaf and Ukhiya upazila will be targeted accordingly. Upazila level WASH strategy will be prepared to align with the development priorities in Cox's Bazar district.
