Project Summary

The prevalent and series of security crises in the Benishangul-Gumuz Region, mainly in the Assosa Zone caused by Unidentified Armed Groups (UAVs) has recently caused the massive displacements of citizens into Sherkole, Bilidiglu, Sedal, and Bambasi Woredas. Most particularly, the recent conflict escalations that occurred in the Kamashi Zone of Benishangul-Gumuz Region and the West Wollega Zone of Oromia Region had led to the destruction of many villages, instilling a negative impact on the population resulting in safety and protection concerns and depriving them of all human rights. Many affected women and children are being exposed to violence and most of them have lost hope in pursuing their dreams and aspiration.
Over 8,000 IDP HHs (40,714 Individuals) have been internally displaced and are now seeking refuge in neighboring Woreda Administrations of the Benishangul-Gumuz Regions. Both Kamashi and West Wollega Zones are mainly the areas greatly hit by armed conflict triggering the largest displacement in the region. They have been continuing confrontations between the UAG fighters and government forces resulting from a significant number of casualties and destruction of livelihoods. During the conflict, most of the IDPs lost their properties including houses and different small businesses like shops as they have been burnt leaving occupants homeless and businesses crumbling. So many people living in this area have fled to neighboring villages to live with relatives, friends and some in bushes and camps. The majority of the displaced are women and children and many of them are encountering more vulnerability as the humanitarian assistance linked with the provision of nutrition, shelter, NFI, WASH, Health, and other basic needs are very much limited.
The majority of the displaced communities in Bilidiglu, Bambasi, and Sherkole left without basic humanitarian aid and many IDPs are living under trees, in poor-conditioned public facilities such as schools and health centers as well as in camp-like sites where the shelter is in sub-standard condition. It has been assessed that there are more than 8,000 IDP HHs still lack to have access to safe, adequate, and standard shelter and adequate household NFls; therefore, requiring an urgent response to meet their basic needs accordingly.
According to the recent regional SNFI Cluster meeting minute where ANE was participated, quite few partners are responding to the crisis and some of them also have planned responses where ANE is one of them. ANE assisted 700 HHs at Bambasi with partial kits and a national NGO called ASDEPO reported the distribution of 1,920 NFls at Bidigilu and Sherkole Woredas. The partner also reported a plan to reach an additional 1,186 IDP HHS at Bildigilu Woreda and 334 IDP HHS at Sherkole Woreda with ES/NFI distribution. However, these responses could not absorb the overall ESNFI needs in addressing all IDPs in both Bilidglu and Bamabsi Woredas of Assosa Zone Administrations. ANE will work in consultation and collaboration with all relevant actors and stakeholders at both national and sub-national levels in the design and implementation of this program.
