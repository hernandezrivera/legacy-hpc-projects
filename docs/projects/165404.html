Project Summary

Emergency food, and cash distributions are tailored for immediate support for the most vulnerable IDPs, their host communities, returnees and their communities of return, including in hard-to-reach areas, in line with SPHERE and Core Humanitarian Standards.
Based on market size and structure, the modality used will be either cash, vouchers or in-kind.
Oxfam team will identify needs, gaps, challenges and opportunities and provide immediate support through cash transfers. Cash is instrumental in supporting beneficiaries to access a variety of basic goods and services while it also strengthens local economies. Beneficiaries will use money to access basic goods from the local markets, simultaneously the cash works as a stimulus for boosting market economic activities. Oxfam will also consider introducing vouchers to provide food, it contributes to the fragile economy especially if there is a risk of inflation when the markets are recovering from shocks. Vouchers are also a useful modality where there are no banks or financial service providers.
Emergency life sustaining food assistance, As may be necessary, the team will carry out emergency responses for crisis affected vulnerable communities consisting of short term food assistance. As a first line response in emergency, and in cases of non-functional markets, especially in the first three months of a disaster, Oxfam will distribute food rations for 3 days, one week, one month or three months, based on how acute and/or chronic the conditions are. Should Oxfam observe cases of malnutrition it will make referrals to appropriate partners and agencies.
Oxfam will ensure that each stakeholder involved in the project can safely participate in its activities. Safe programming (or protection mainstreaming) is implemented as part of the project cycle. Oxfam’s staff, volunteers and implementing partners are trained in safe programming and risk assessments. This includes the identification of the risks, possible targets, and risk prevention, mitigation and management measures: this is then implemented and reviewed regularly. Particular attention will be paid to addressing risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse and aid diversion, and a conflict sensitivity approach will be adopted.

4 - Needs

The overall food security situation has improved in many parts of Syria, which has improved access to markets. However, there remain areas of serious concern, due to continued localized hostilities, new and protracted displacement, increased numbers of returnees and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience during the years of crisis. WFP’s mVAM data shows variations in vulnerability among governorates. With the exception of Damascus, the situation has improved compared to last year. Dara’a, Deir-ez-Zor and Raqqa witnessed a higher decrease in the share of vulnerable households compared to 2018. According to the joint Crop and Food Security Mission report by the FAO and WFP published in September 2019, food prices have drastically increased as a result of the conflict. The main causes of the price increase have been the limited access to land and inputs, reduced local production, damage and looting of production assets and processing plants, and the devaluation of the national currency. Households have limited purchasing power. Farmers continue raising concerns about high production and transportation costs and lack of quality inputs. Government initiatives have led to an increase in the availability of farm machinery, but overall availability remains insufficient. Some progress has been made on the rehabilitation of irrigation structures, although illegal drilling in recent years is likely to lower the underground water table in a year of average rains. Nationally, overall livestock numbers have stabilized. The area of harvested wheat in 2019 was almost double that of 2018, but is still 25% below the pre-crisis harvested area. In a recent rapid assessment performed by Tamayouz Association, it was indicated that women may be shouldering additional burdens not present before the conflict. When sewage and potable water systems were destroyed during the siege, it created a shortage of drinking water.

5 - Implementation of activities

Through direct or indirect implementation modalities Oxfam will respond to new emergencies and will serve the most vulnerable families by providing emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable people with short- term food assistance through appropriate modalities, and supplying flour or bread directly to households or to bakeries. Based on access conditions, market functionality, and people willingness, Oxfam will decide whether to adopt cash, in-kind or voucher modality. Oxfam in Syria has developed a Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism (FCRM) to allow for the lodging of complaints or make suggestions to the organization. The mechanism functions under the PMEAL Manager/team and has close coordination with the programmes/operational team to resolve complaints in a timely and consistent manner. The project's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) system will be in line with Oxfam's Common Approach to MEL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) which will be adapted to the project context according to the Alternative Operation Procedures (AoP) for Syria. The project will complement primary data where possible with the use of information from secondary sources (e.g. UN agencies, local authorities, SARC, etc.) to inform ongoing situation and needs analysis and to triangulate monitoring data. Despite the limitations, Oxfam in Syria will where possible, conduct regular monitoring according to a planned schedule (MEAL framework, plan and Indicator Performance Tracking Table [IPTTs]) developed while achievements towards indicators are reviewed monthly and updated in Oxfam’s internal monitoring and reporting System. This System also tracks scheduling, issues arising, and lessons learned with the support of the project team.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Occurrence of gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse: Oxfam staff, volunteers and partners are gender and GBV aware; GBV is strongly mainstreamed in this project; Safeguarding accountability and reporting mechanisms are in place in Oxfam and partners.• The project could perceived as taking a side in the conflict due to favoring specific communities or social groups: for every project Oxfam will perform conflict sensitivity analysis in order to make sure that the project is enhancing connecting factors and minimizing dividing factors in the targeted locations

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Security hampers access of staff, volunteers, service providers and supplies and services to project locations. Oxfam has a security management plan and security protocols which all staff are trained on and required to adhere to. Oxfam conducts continuous assessment of the situation; security protocols are frequently updated and adapted to ensure risks are minimized. In cases where prolonged security affects operations or the project as a whole, Oxfam will update OCHA and discuss scenarios for continued or adapted implementation. - Perception of breaching humanitarian principles: Carry out a monthly evaluation against Oxfam’s red lines with critical analysis of impartiality, voice and safety. Ensure implementation of needs based program serving the most vulnerable communities.- Beneficiaries data protection is compromised: Conduct awareness sessions to staff, volunteers and partners on the legal compliance with the GDPR regulations, seek beneficiaries consent before sharing their info- Government approvals: delays in project approvals leading to delays in implementation. Oxfam has designed its project workplans to allow for potential delays in approvals.- Tension between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. Oxfam uses a participatory community approach to develop the vulnerability and eligibility criteria and the selection of beneficiaries to ensure transparency, acceptance and accountability to different stakeholders.- Inability to receive funds in the country due to sanctions: Open accounts with different banks to diversify transfer routing and corresponding banks, Increase foreign cash reserve within the country and balance the pool of suppliers/ contractors to include local and international companies. Assumptions:- Safe access is possible for Oxfam team, partners and contractors to assess needs and implement activities- Farmers, especially women, are willing to restart their farming activities.- Needed approvals are obtained
