Project Summary

According to the recent Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis, 6.35 million people (54 per cent of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) in August. In the period of September to December 2019, seasonal harvests become available with an estimated 4.54 million people (39 per cent of the population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Projection of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate and an estimated 5.5 million people (47 per cent of the total population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Moreover, the overall nutrition situation during the lean season of 2019 is worse compared to same period in 2018. The prevalence of acute malnutrition has increased significantly from 13.3 per cent in 2018 to 16.2 per cent in 2019 which is above the 15 per cent emergency threshold.
UNICEF and implementing partners will support preventive and lifesaving nutrition interventions through provision of maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN), treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), capacity building and robust information systems nationwide. UNICEF will respond in collaboration with sister UN agencies and NGOs to avert a life-threatening nutrition situation in South Sudan in 2020 using a multisectoral approach with health, WASH, Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Education and Child Protection interventions. These cross-sectoral linkages will result in optimum synergetic effect to contribute not only in reduction of acute malnutrition but also chronic malnutrition – stunting and micronutrient deficiencies.
Given the current nutrition situation and in response to humanitarian needs in South Sudan, UNICEF and nutrition implementing partners have projected to reach 1,098,241 pregnant women and caregivers of children below the age of 24 months and 268,045 children with SAM. The estimated total cost of delivering the UNICEF nutrition project is estimated USD 57,484,671 for the year 2020.

1 - Needs

The IPC analysis conducted in August 2019 was released by the Government on 10th September 2019. The report highlights that an estimated 6.35 million people (54 per cent of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by an estimated 5 per cent. The overall nutrition situation during the lean season of 2019 is worse compared to the same period in 2018. The prevalence of acute malnutrition has increased significantly from 13.3 per cent in 2018 to 16.2 per cent in 2019, which is above the 15 per cent emergency threshold. Based on the IPC Acute malnutrition (AMN), 58 counties are classified as IPC AMN Phase 3 and above (GAM prevalence above 9.9 per cent). In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47 per cent of the total population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Forty-three counties were classified as Critical (GAM prevalence of 15.0 – 29.9 per cent, IPC AMN Phase 4) and 14 counties are classified as Serious (GAM prevalence of 10.0-14.9 per cent, IPC AMN Phase 3). Most counties in Unity, Upper Nile, Jonglei and Warrap and parts of Eastern Equatoria and Lakes are in critical levels of acute malnutrition. A total of 1.3 million are projected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2020 including 292,373 SAM children.Several factors including the ongoing economic crisis, lack of agricultural produce to sell at the peak of the lean season, poor harvest due to flooding, depleted household assets and limited employment opportunities continue to constrain availability of income for most households and thereby compromise a household’s ability to obtain adequate food. The burden of acute malnutrition in South Sudan in 2020 is very high and therefore requires concerted efforts to reach all affected children. UNICEF will sustain the scaled-up response, strengthen existing programming modalities and ensure a multi-sector response to reach 1,098,241 pregnant women and caregivers of children below the age of 24 months and over quarter of a million children with preventative and curative interventions with an estimated budget $57.5 million. These proposed approaches need to holistically address acute malnutrition, starting with prevention and treatment in a multi-sectoral approach. This will be primarily through (1) UNICEF in partnership with 39 Civil Society Organizations (CSO); (2) Scaling up of outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP) activities; (3) Strengthening the complementarity of programming modalities with other nutrition sensitive sectors – Health, WASH, FSL, Education and Child Protection interventions; (4) Timely prepositioning of nutrition supplies for prevention and treatment; (5) Prioritization of preventative strategies as first initiatives through scaling up and redefining and expanding the scope of community nutrition volunteers; and (6) Continued Surveillance to monitor progress of activities.

2 - Activities

Various interventions and activities will be implemented by UNICEF and partners to respond to the needs. The planned activities are:  Train health and nutrition workers on MIYCN at national, state, county and community level.  Provide individual targeted and group counselling on MIYCN to caregivers of children aged below 24 months, including men.  Support establishment of demonstration kitchen gardens at the facilities and the communities.  Promotion of dietary diversity, food demonstration of locally available food and cooking methods through group counselling.  Support Growth Monitoring and Promotion (GMP) at facility and community level.  Micronutrient supplementation specifically vitamin A and deworming through integration with National Immunization Days (NID) and/or with acute malnutrition screening. Malaria testing and treatment at the nutrition sites (OTP).  Distribution of WASH non-food items (NFIs) and hygiene promotion at the OTP sites through WASH Cluster.  Training of the Health/Nutrition staff on how to provide psychosocial support (PSS) to beneficiaries and distribution of early childhood development (ECD) kits at the nutrition sites (OTP).  Train health and nutrition workers on Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) to improve care for children aged 6-59 months with malnutrition.  Community mobilization and active screening for early case detection of children with SAM and referral. Provide integrated treatment to children with SAM both at static facilities and through community outreach.  Provide integrated care for children with SAM in OTP/Stabilization Centres (SC) in health facilities, including WASH services and ECD at nutrition sites.  Create awareness and provide gender-based violence (GBV) referral services at the OTP sites focusing on women’s empowerment in community meetings. Timely emergency preparedness and response. Monitor the progress and quality of nutrition interventions and services at the static nutrition facilities and outreach sites.  Provide integrated on-the-job coaching and mentorship on SAM treatment, supplies management and MIYCN. Routine management of nutrition programme data and information.  Conduct nutrition assessments throughout the country twice yearly Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Systems (FSNMS).

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Special focus was given to engage POC and refugee camps and they are consulted to reflect their needs in programme proposal. Efforts were oriented to consult them during establishment and provision of nutrition services. Thus, UNICEF encourages all partners to include them throughout the development of Partnership Cooperation Agreements. Both women and girls are consulted through their community leaders and lead mothers during establishment and provision of nutrition services. Particularly, the lead mothers are the bridge between the women/girls and nutrition service providers. Thus, UNICEF encourages all partners to include mothers as community nutrition volunteers throughout the development of Partnership Cooperation Agreements. UNICEF supports nutrition implementing partners to provide services to communities’ nearby nutrition sites where usually located within a walkable distance (one day’s return walk) for the beneficiaries. Partners also provide outreach services to mitigate the long distance walk of caregivers to static nutrition sites. UNICEF supports nutrition implementing partners to establish static nutrition sites in every homogenous community to alleviate tribal community conflicts. Furthermore, services through the Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism has been given in multiple sites in such a way to avoid unnecessary conflicts with a sense of exclusion or discrimination. UNICEF supports nutrition implementing partners to provide services to communities by establishing nutrition sites which are usually located within a walkable distance (one day’s return walk) for the beneficiaries. Partners also provide outreach services to mitigate the long distance walk of caregivers to static nutrition sites to avoid exposure to violence as a result of distance.
