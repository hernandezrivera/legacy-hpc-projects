Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Syria has been exacerbated by the widespread use of explosive ordnance (EO), posing significant risks and requiring different mitigation strategies to reduce its impact on the civilian population and the humanitarian sector delivering aid to those in need. While the full scale of contamination is not known, preliminary findings for the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimate that 1 in 2 people is at risk from EO, and a third of communities are potentially contaminated. EO contamination in essential civilian infrastructure, agricultural land, roads and private property is a lethal barrier to safe movement which deprives civilians from accessing essential basic services, hampers delivery of humanitarian assistance, endangers those seeking refuge from violence or returning to their areas, and precludes resumption of livelihood activities, thus amplifying the social and economic crisis aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic. A comprehensive humanitarian mine action response is a humanitarian necessity and vital precursor for delivering humanitarian aid and early recovery.
The overall aim of UNMAS project is to reduce the impact of EO on the civilian population and humanitarian workers through effective sector coordination and implementation of humanitarian mine action activities, in line with humanitarian priorities as identified in the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), and within the framework of agreements between the Syrian State and UN agencies, funds and programmes, as well as Security Council resolution 2504 (S/RES/2504, 2020).
Under this project, in accordance with the HRP, UNMAS will lead, manage and coordinate humanitarian mine action activities as defined by International Mine Action Standards (IMAS).
All components of humanitarian mine action (risk education, victim assistance, survey and clearance) remain critical, but in 2021, UNMAS will focus on expanding survey and clearance, as the only way to permanently remove the threat posed by EO and subject to access and funding.
The delivery of EO Risk Education (EORE), tailored to age, gender, localized threats and activity patterns of different at-risk groups, is a critical, life-saving activity for raising the awareness of civilians and humanitarian personnel about the dangers of EO, thus directly contributing to HRP strategic objective 1: “Save Lives” and strategic objective 2: “Enhance Protection.” As the Syrian crisis has been characterized by large-scale conflict and displacement, survey, data collection, and mapping of EO are critical to informing humanitarian programming, needs-based prioritization, and the expansion of clearance to remove the contamination threats, particularly in high-priority areas. UNMAS will support the expansion of victim assistance and provision of specialized services for persons with disabilities, including survivors of EO and their families, thus contributing to strategic objective 3: “Increase Resilience and Access to Services”.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, 1 in 2 people is at risk from EO, and a third of the communities are potentially contaminated. While all people in contaminated areas are vulnerable, certain groups are at greater risk in correlation with gender, age, responsibilities, and displacement status. Men and boys are considered to be the most exposed to the direct threat of EO, representing 85% of victims. Women and girls are indirectly affected, but also become at-risk when they carry out their livelihood activities in contaminated areas. EO accidents can result in serious injury or death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families, severely impacting their lives and livelihoods. Reportedly, about 34% of incidents take place in agricultural fields, 39% in residential areas, and 10% on roads and roadsides. The COVID-19 pandemic’s impact on livelihoods may force more people to adopt unsafe behaviours, such as cultivating contaminated land, removing rubble and collecting metal for trade to maintain their livelihood. Meanwhile the widespread EO contamination continues to hamper access to basic services and the delivery of humanitarian assistance. In this context, humanitarian mine action is a key activity to prevent downward vulnerability spirals caused by COVID-19. The presence of EO exacerbates vulnerabilities and increases pressure on other humanitarian sectors. Sustaining and scaling up humanitarian mine action is an essential protection measure that will have a direct positive impact on other humanitarian interventions in Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

Coordination-UNMAS leads the Mine Action AoR where it provides technical expertise, works on advancing technical humanitarian mine action (HMA) standards/frameworks, ensures HMA is integrated in the planning and programming of humanitarian response, & manages specialized Information Management System, adopting gender & diversity sensitive collection & analysis of data to enable needs-based prioritization & guide safer humanitarian relief.EO Risk Education (EORE) is the first critical step to prevent civilian casualties from EO & build people’s resilience. UNMAS will continue delivering EORE tailored to age, gender, nature of threat & activity patterns; enhancing its efficiency, effectiveness & impact. 2021 priority is to mainstream EORE into humanitarian partners’ service delivery through training & provision of materials. Survey and Clearance is the only way to fully remove EO threat to civilians, enable safe delivery of humanitarian aid & access to critical services, & provide the basis for early recovery efforts. UNMAS identifies target areas based on HRP priorities; & identifies IPs in accordance with humanitarian principles. UNMAS will deploy EO Assessment capacity to support humanitarian agencies’ work in infrastructure/areas where EO impedes lifesaving humanitarian activities. Victim assistance (VA) for persons with disabilities, including survivors of EO accidents, is underfunded & under implemented whilst population needs are growing. In coordination with relevant stakeholders, UNMAS will continue its VA initiatives, including provision of specialized services through referral pathways with specialized partners. Within Syria, UNMAS has set up a VA framework to mainstream VA into other humanitarian responses.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UNMAS refers to & promotes among the Mine Action Sub-Sector & Sub-Cluster the PSEA approach defined for Syria, under leadership of the WoS SSG/UNCT & guidance of PSEA Specialist designated by the UNCT. UNMAS has organisational Code of Conduct/Rules & Regulations that outlines acceptable & unacceptable behaviors in terms of interactions within the organization & with external stakeholders, including beneficiary communities & casual laborers. The CoC condemns any illegal or unethical activity (SEA, harassment, etc.) & includes disciplinary & financial measures in case of breach by UNMAS personnel. UNMAS SRP contract & grants has standard clauses for implementing partner’s (IP) requirement for PSEA & SH and incorporates UN Supplier CoC where the IP is required to put in place an Organizational CoC/Rules & Regulations that outlines acceptable/unacceptable behaviors in terms of interactions within the organization & with external stakeholders, including beneficiary communities & casual laborers. The CoC includes disciplinary & financial measures in case of breach by IP’s personnel. All UNMAS personnel receive briefing upon deployment & regular updates on how to report cases of SH, SEA, discrimination, other inappropriate behavior & issues of women’s safety & GBV. A channel to report concerns & raise gender & safety issues, also anonymously, is made available to all. Focal persons who receive complaints are diverse in gender & trained in creating respectful work environment.
