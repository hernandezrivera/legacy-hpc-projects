Project Summary

Since 2012, UOSSM France has been a UNICEF Nutrition Group partner and active member of technical working groups (IYCF, CMAM) in addition to contributing in coordination with nutrition cluster partners to report the gap in nutrition services in northwestern Syria. UOSSM France has implemented nutrition projects over the past years through a partnership with UNICEF and UOSSM INT in northwestern Syria.
Through the activities of the Nutrition Program, UOSSM France will provide services to the most affected groups (children under 5), pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), and caregivers, in accordance with UNICEF protocols and the nutrition group. This project will contribute to increasing access to coordinated, life-saving, and sustainable humanitarian nutrition services for those most in need, enhancing the ability of the nutrition sector to detect and respond in a timely manner to the acute malnutrition in NWS through improving access and ensuring the sustainability and quality of nutrition services provided through operating five health facilities (three primary healthcare centers and 2 mobile clinics) in northwestern Syria (find Step3: target locations).
Through the nutrition program, which will be supervised by a nutrition technician and supervised by a pediatrician in care centers and a general practitioner in the mobile clinic and in coordination with the PNC/ANC clinic in each health facility (5 nutrition technicians), each of the following services will be provided: raising community awareness in the field of nutrition skills for young children and infants IYCF: where the focus is on the messages provided to pregnant and lactating mothers and caregivers for children screening malnutrition: Malnutrition is screened for all children aged 6-59 months, pregnant women and lactating women who visit health centers by the MUAC scale; where malnutrition cases are accepted into the outpatient treatment program, while cases of severe acute malnutrition and medical complications reached with inpatient treatment will be referred to hospitals.
Distribution of supplements (preventive supplements and therapeutic supplements
Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) is aimed at treating malnutrition cases that are screened and accepted into the program among the target groups of the service: children 0-59 months and pregnant women and lactating women where cases are followed up during the treatment period by regular follow-up visits by the community health team until full recovery and referral of cases of severe malnutrition to specialized centers
In addition to (the two-person) community health workers' team (one team for each mobile clinic) and three teams in each primary care center (three teams per primary care center, where each team conducts 5-7 weekly family visits to raise awareness of nutrition services provided by health facilities and during visits: malnutrition is investigated for the most affected groups such as children, pregnant women, and lactating mothers by measuring MUAC for children 6-59 months, pregnant women and lactating mothers and referring them to receive nutrition services in CMAM facility and the ordering of the distribution of some types of preventive supplements to families benefiting from the program, in addition to spreading awareness messages within the community, which fall under the five main units including nutrition: infant and young child nutrition (IYCF) and will provide COVID-19 awareness messages and nutrition technicians will receive CMAM and IYCF nutrition program training.
Nutrition services will be integrated with the provided services of the target (3 PHCs) and Mobile Clinics (2 MCs) linked to the HRP strategic objective #1 of the health cluster: Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need.

4 - Needs

According to the CCCM Cluster, of the 4.2 M people in NWS, some 2.7 M are displaced, 2.8 million people rely on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs including shelter, health, food and waterThe situation has been worsened by the effects of the ongoing COVID 19 outbreak, volatile economic situation, insecurity and explosive risks. According to the Nutrition surveillance system report in July 2021, UNICEF partners screened 178,671 children under the age of five (89,426 girls) for malnutrition, including 60,185 children (29,707 girls) in northwestern Syria. Of these children, 1,444 (816 girls) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) were identified and enrolled for treatment in UNICEF-supported outpatient treatment programs, including 989 children (570 girls)According to earlier OCHA reports on the humanitarian situation, the poor nutritional situation in northwestern Syria is on the rise, in addition to an increase in the rate of acute and chronic malnutrition (stunting) among children 0-59 months in the previous period. 9 months increased from 19% to 33% 35% male,31% female, between May 2019 and September 2020 while the rate of acute malnutrition increased from 1% to 2% in the same time period.For pregnant and lactating mothers, the average acute malnutrition rate is about 11 percent in northwestern Syria, and the rate of acute malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months is 4 percent higher in Idlib than in Aleppo.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented throughout three phases: i. Preparation phase: Within the first week of the project, the first order for medicines will be prepared and submitted to be performed; to save time, efforts and maximize the value of money the UOSSM FRANCE will check with donor/ partner to explore the possibility of purchasing Nutrients / nutrition complements, taking into account the quality of items and reliability of available providers duly.A recruitment plan will be set up within the first week of the project and will be performed within one month. Additionally, a training plan will be set in the first month to ensure all medical and paramedical staff received the packages of trainings according to the re-visited Nutrition related training package based on EHSP, UNFPA, UNICEF and UOSSM France guidelines. ii. Implementation phase:Through this phase, the nutrition teams will continue functioning to provide the nutrition services to the beneficiaries, and distributing nutritional complements. iii. Exit phase: starting the last quarter of the project UOSSM FRANCE will communicate closely with health cluster, partners, and Idleb Health Directorate to explore the available opportunities to secure fund for nutrition services and/or set up a proper exit plan.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The following risks were identified based on UOSSM France:1. Risk: Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control. Mitigating actions: 1) Close monitoring and participation to info-sharing mechanisms and meetings 2) Security related SOPs in place; 3) Remote monitoring mechanisms in place; 3) Ensure accurate inventory of assets within the facilities and establish plans to relocate assets as necessary; 4) Ensure the maintenance of the humanitarian standards and the neutral positioning.2. Risk: Loss and/or damages to program equipment and supplies and/or staff injured or killed due to airstrikes or other security-related incidents. Mitigating actions: 1) Ensure adequate security/surveillance at the warehouses; 2) Security related SOPs in place; 3) Minimizing value/quantity of items at any given site while ensuring balance with programmatic needs and contingency;3. Risk: Risk of Aid diversion, corruption, fraud. Mitigating actions: 1) Appropriate mitigation measures within procurement process (including mandatory reviews from Compliance and other relevant departments); 2) Anti-fraud, anti-corruption, code of conduct, humanitarian principles, and other relevant training provided to all staff;4. Risk: COVID-19 spread among staff and beneficiaries. Mitigating action: 1) Provide adequate PPE supplies and enhance; 2) Vaccination is a condition to work with UOSSM France in the field at the facility level
