Project Summary

South Sudan's Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.5 million children and women at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024. Most counties have GAM rates above 15%. Key drivers of malnutrition include high food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions, high child morbidity rates, gender-based violence concerns, and poor child feeding and caring practices. Over 50% of the population lacks access to safe water or sanitation, and 62.5% suffer from diarrhea, malaria, or upper respiratory infections. 18 counties have very high levels of deprivation, exacerbating the already dire nutrition situation and requiring urgent multi-sectoral interventions.
The nutrition cluster aims to provide emergency nutrition support to 1.8 million children and women in Tonj North, Kapoeta East, North of Eastern Equatoria State, and Wau of WBeG State. It will collaborate with food and livelihood, health, protection, WASH, and Education clusters to enhance community resilience to shocks by increasing access to nutrition integrated with health, food security, livelihoods services, and WASH. The response will include preventive services for maternal, infant, and child nutrition and treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition.
The IRSS aims to target 114,266 SAM, MAM, and PLW cases across various needy populations. It will increase equitable access to quality preventative and curative nutrition services for vulnerable children and women in counties facing multiple deprivations. Nutrition sites will provide integrated services, collaborating with Food Security and Livelihood, WASH, Health, and Protection clusters to prevent malnutrition and benefit nutritionally vulnerable children and women.
IRSS is focusing on strengthening community capacities and government staff at the Counties and States level to support the transition of nutrition services from Implementing Partners. They will engage with the government and development actors through the Scaling Up Nutrition Movement & Health Development Partners Group to advocate for long-term investment to address malnutrition drivers and build community resilience. IRSS will also advocate for the strengthening of community platforms using community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) and MtMSGP for delivering multi-sectoral services at community and household levels. To ensure continuity of nutrition services against the backdrop of reducing humanitarian funding and increasing needs, IRSS will continue to implement efforts to reduce efficiencies; this includes working with County Health Department to rationalize the number of partners, type, and number of sites per county and staffing per nutrition sites through areas mapping. IRSS will integrate health and nutrition service delivery at the mobile, static sites and monitor the situation to identify insecure and inaccessible areas for rapid response mechanisms (RRM) to improve the timeliness of lifesaving services. IRSS will be supported by evidence from different sources, including the Nutrition Information System (NIS) and 5Ws, Needs Assessments, Based and End Line surveys, Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) nutrition surveys, and gender-based violence safety quality audits. For the sustainability of NIS, IRSS will continue to advocate for integration nutrition indicators in the District Health Information System- 2 (DHIS2) and in the national Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) proposed project will address acute malnutrition through Early detection and treatment to ensure nutrition governance at community level.

1. Needs

According to the 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey’s findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.5 million children and women at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024 and will need urgent treatment. 46 out of 79 counties, have GAM rates above 15%. Analysis includes high food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions, with over 50% of the population lacking access to safe water or sanitation; high child morbidity rates with 53.6% suffering from diarrhea, malaria or upper respiratory infections; gender-based violence concerns; and poor child feeding and caring practices with only 4% children getting the minimum acceptable diet for them to grow and develop well. The nutrition cluster will aim to reach 1.8 million children and women with emergency nutrition support and collaborate with food and livelihood, health, protection, WASH and Education clusters to provide multi-sectoral interventions.To address the suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable children and women, Islamic Relief South Sudan response project will aim to increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition services for children, adolescents and women at 7 static nutrition facilities and 7 outreach sites in Wau, Tonj North, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North counties experiencing multiple deprivations, including high acute malnutrition rates, food insecurity, morbidities, high GBV concerns and poor WASH conditions.In 2024, the IDPs, Returnees and Host communities of the proposed project area are vulnerable due to flood, drought and conflict as a result, an estimated 110,734 people, including 60,609 children under the age of 5, and 50,125 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment in the Kapoeta East and North of Eastern Equatorial States, Tonj North of Warrap State, and Wau of Western Bahr-Ghazal State out of the cluster identified People in Need. The leading cause of morbidity is malaria, followed by acute respiratory infections and acute watery. diarrhea. In 2024, morbidity rates are expected to increase proportionally for each of these causes as they did in 2023, Therefore, out of the cluster PIN, IRSS response project will reach to 114,266 under-five and PLW with various nutrition curative and preventive services in Wau, Tonj North, Kapoeta East and North Counties.To address the acute malnutrition of the people in need the Islamic Relief South Sudan willbe providing support to the food insecure malnourished children and PLWs through introducing nutrition sensitive kitchen garden and sensitizing the community people for hygiene practices and optimum maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN). The project will also focus on early childhood development through providing playing materials for the children. The project will help the community people for early childprotection and gender-based violence and risk mitigation at nutrition sites. The objectives of the proposed project: 1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 20242. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW in prioritized locations by the end of 20243. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 20244. To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2024.

2. Project activities

To provide emergency nutrition support to affected under-five children and women, Islamic Relief South Sudan will focus on implementing the following key activities at the IRSS supported health and nutrition facilities in the project targeted counties. 1) Conduct facility and community-based screening, admission, and treatment of 7,946 (3,178 boys and 4,768 girls) 6-59 months children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and referral of under-five children = 9,171 (3,669 boys and 5,502 girls), pregnant and lactating women = 12,186 identified suffering from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) to the nearby facility with the TSFP services. 2) Conduct 84 sessions of integrated health and nutrition mobile outreach clinics to provide community mass screening, curative consultation, and EPI vaccination to under-five children, PLW and vulnerable communities living in hard to reach areas. 3) Conduct quarterly community mass awareness and advocacy campaigns on causes of malnutrition, its prevention, MIYCN counselling, and other behavioural change reaching 65,000 caregivers of 0-23 months children. 4) Conduct 56 quarterly cooking demonstration sessions, Kitchen gardening and optimal MIYCN trainings to 14 groups of mother-to-mother support groups comprising of 280 members. 5) Conduct Vitamin A supplementation to 46,593 boys and girls aged 6-59 months and deworming campaigns with Albendazole tablets to 38,370 children aged 12-59 months in semester 1 & 2 respectively. 6) Recruit, train and provide incentives to community nutrition volunteers and social mobilizers to support active case finding, screening and community referral of SAM and MAM cases. 7) Procure and Provide preventive packages such as soaps and LLITN to caregivers of 5-59 months children and PLW admitted and treated for SAM and MAM for prevention of hygiene related diseases and malaria infection. Procure and preposition IEC and ECD materials to nutrition facilities for stimulation play of under-five children. 8) Link under-five children and PLW identified suffering from SAM and MAM with their caregivers to FSL for cash voucher assistance, health for medical, EPI, & antenatal care services, WASH for WASH kits, education and Protection services. 9) provide capacity building trainings to the staffs. 10) Conduct joint supportive supervision and monitoring visits to health and nutrition OTP facilities together with CHD and other implementing partners.
