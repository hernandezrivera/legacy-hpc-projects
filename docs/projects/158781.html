Project Summary

The sustained IDP and Host community Livelihoods project will be implemented in Jubaland state, Galmudug and Banadir Regions. It will target and benefit 23,000 individuals in need. This will be achieved through the provision of seasonally appropriate agricultural inputs, emergency livestock assistance, livelihood diversification for women and the youth, improved post-harvest practices, response to food chain threats, protection of rangeland and development of income-generating activities. To facilitate immediate access to food for vulnerable communities living in areas with severe acute food insecurity, 720 households (4320 people) in Kismayo, Mogadishu, Dollow and Dhusamareb will receive food vouchers All cash rates will be based on the rates as set by the Cash working group of Somalia. Conditional and unconditional cash transfers will increase the purchasing power for targeted households to access food commodities available in the local markets for at least 3 months as they recover their assets. The cash is a digniﬁed approach to supporting these beneﬁciaries to make choices of types and quantities of food to purchase. Conditional transfers to 840 households will ensure that agricultural canals and river dykes are rehabilitated. Post Distribution Surveys will be conducted to monitor the use of transferred and the benefit it has brought the families.
1,560 households( 9360 individuals) will be supported to develop or restore their agricultural livestock production through the provision of farm inputs (seeds tools and fertilizers to mention but a few) and provision of basic treatment of 28,800,000 livestock. 140 women will be supported to diversify their livestock production to take up poultry keeping for household consumption and commercial benefits to the households. 150 women and youth will also be supported to establish income-generating activities while 90 youth will be supported to take up technical courses that will broaden their opportunities for self-employment. In a bid to reduce the number of people living in acute food insecurity, 850 farmers and pastoralists will be facilitated will SMART agricultural and livestock production skills through demonstration farms established through the proposed project and provision of agricultural extension services. Protection of rangeland in Abudwak, Dhusamareb, Dollow, Belet Hawa and Kismayo will be conducted through trained community-based natural resource management committees. This project is will be implemented in a 12 month period.

Mitigation Measures

To mitigate against risks highlighted above, NAPAD will ensure that all cash transfers (CFW) will be conducted to beneficiaries electronically through local mobile service providers. This is after information verification during and after biometric registration of beneficiaries. These measures will ensure safety of beneficiaries. All beneficiary data will be safely stored by NAPAD for accountability. Distributions will be conducted from targeted villages to ensure that beneficiaries do not have to travel to other location to get their vouchers. All vouchers are redeemable at local stores ensuring that beneficiaries do not have to travel far and in insecure areas to access required commodities. All cash rates will be based on the rates as set by the Cash working group of Somalia. NAPAD will be keen to issue food vouchers to the most vulnerable and in particular female-headed households, child-headed households, pregnant and lactating women with under 5-year-olds and older persons and unaccompanied children in their own name. Both IDP and host community members will be targeted to receive the vouchers and CFW so as to avoid conflicts, tension or harassment over the food and resources. It has been a practice of NAPAD to consult community, clan and local authority leadership in identification of beneficiaries of food vouchers and the individuals involved in Cash For Work. This has ensured that there is a balanced division of resources, community ownership for better management of assets and provision of security for beneficiaries as well as assets. Distributions will be conducted regularly to ensure that beneficiaries will not run out of food commodities until the next distribution. NAPAD has ensured that its food security field officers are gender-balanced and include different local clans including the Bantus. This was effected to ensure that women and girls , minority tribes like the bantus and people with disability issues are attended to adequately especially during beneficiary registration and distributions. NAPAD’s balanced staffing profile has and will continue to enhance NAPAD’s reporting and feed- back mechanism. Communities have access to NAPAD staff numbers and NAPADs email address for any information sharing. NAPAD will also conduct pre and post distribution surveys to capture any complaints and recommendations from the community. NAPAD staff and contractors have signed a child protection policy and will also be expected to be formally committed to adhere to humanitarian code of conduct during all interactions with vulnerable populations. NAPAD will continue to conduct refresher child protection and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training to their staff so they continue to have capacities to identify and react effectively to protection issues. Continued engagement of school based clubs in child advocacy will ensure that children and especially girls will have skills and knowledge on their safety during voucher distributions. Additionally, project inception community mobilization meetings will be key in sharing protection information to leaders and beneficiaries on modalities of maintaining individual security during distributions activities. This will also be an opportunity to share with especially women and girl that do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance and that they should ensure their safety by walking in groups to their local distribution points.

Please provide further explanation

NAPAD is registered to operate in Galmudug, Banadir and Jubaland. The Local administration, relevant District Ministry, community leadership and local IDP and Host community are key in identification of project beneficiaries, sites and resources. This will enhance legality of the interventions, enhance ownership as well as mitigate against potential conflicts among the clans, IDPs, and host communities. On one end, an engaged local leadership will offer sufficient protective environment to beneficiaries and the investments of the project. On the other hand, the local leadership will ensure the sustainability of the project upon handing over of the project. After each project is commissioned, an MOU stipulating ownership, management and maintenance roles of each project will be signed between all local elders, local administration and community groups. The proposed project is linked to the food security and livelihoods objectives of Somali National Development Plan and Food security cluster targets with an emphasis on implementing the objectives of Food security cluster response and developing the resilience of the food security and livelihoods sector to benefit more displaced and non displaced households.
