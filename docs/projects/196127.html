Project Summary

The continuation of the deteriorating conditions over the past years has tremendously increased the number of socially, psychologically and physically affected persons along with their multiple diverse needs. This project is a leveraging of Al Nada Development NGO's vast experience in the humanitarian fields, especially after partnership with UNHCR, via its community centers in Damascus, Rural Damascus providing wide range of services (MHPSS, HRBP, GBV, GIKA MIKA, CLI & YLI, CP, and SSBP) and in cooperation with Relief and Development Centre that is specialized in manufacturing prosthetics, providing physiotherapy, already achieved great goals and targets. This project aims to provide all kinds of support and services which contribute to rehabilitation and development of the affected people in Sbeina and Beitsawa and their surroundings by:
Supply and maintenance of prosthetics. The remarkable increasing number of disable persons and victims of explosive ordinance, in addition to the incapability of the specialized centers to cover their urgent needs, not to mention these centers’ insufficient response, motivated the concept of this project.
Mental health and psychosocial wellbeing, psycho-social support such as recreational thoughtful activities which main aim is to shape up psychological well-being and mental health. In addition to group counselling to secure social and emotional support through focused group discussions, besides individual counselling. Raising awareness sessions, lectures, workshops to support establishing healthy relationships and family life to make sure both direct and indirect explosive ordnance victims, children and persons with disabilities have social inclusion and positive relationships. Referrals and home-based rehabilitation services includes parental skills, awareness raising sessions.
Child protection: all children have the right to live in safe and violence-free, abuse-free surroundings consolidating their skills to develop and progress. Children and adolescents in Sbeinh, Beitsawa are unable to attain minimum proficiency levels in reading, writing and fundamentals of learning. Besides the number of drop outs that is determinately increasing. Wide spread of poverty, economic fragility and the extremely deteriorated livelihood conditions forced children and adolescent to drop out of schools and get involved in labor to support their families. Activities will focus on Children/Children with disabilities and Caregivers including raising awareness sessions, case management for children and adolescents prone to explosive ordnance risk, abuse, violence and harassments taking into consideration required criteria. Group meaningful activities enhancing children’s social and educational inclusion. Education activities will focus on non-formal settings to enhance numeracy, proficiency in reading and writing. Besides, workshops to revive life skills, remedial classes in both English and Arabic, learning via playing. In addition to explosive ordnance risk education. Vocational training and education will be part of the project in cooperation with other organizations and associations in the surroundings to enable all beneficiaries securing and retaining sustainable employment to establish their own suitable business as well as projects, even progress in their current skills. The project tackles diversity of explosive ordnance victims either direct or non-direct and disabilities (children, adolescents, women, men).
All activities will be reported and monitored on weekly, quarterly and yearly basis plus regular 4Ws, PRMD, PFR.

3 - Needs

Sources: ORV team, volunteers live in Sbeina & Beitsawa. Key persons Mokhtars and Municipality, Relief & Development center’s sources. The project meets the needs of explosive ordnance victims direct/indirect, people with disabilities: children, adolescents, women, men. PoC under this project includes IDPs, returnees, affected host communities, refugees, female& male-heads. Needs: According to HNO ,92% of individuals older than 59 have disabilities. Children with disabilities are more likely not to attend or drop out of school. 22% and 47% of children with disabilities aged 6-11 and 15-17 are not in education. Both Beitsawa, Sbeina are in urgent need for several basic needs and have no community center yet. Shortage of schools, poor livelihood conditions, no access to basic services such as electricity, education, water, health services, GBV risks, and children labor are major problems in both areas. Persons with physical/mental impairment and injuries, including direct victims of EO incidents and their families, face urgent needs. Children Labor in Beitsawa is 70% while 40% in Sbeina. School dropout exposes children to protection threats such as child labor mainly for boys and early marriage mainly for girl. Negative coping mechanisms are on the rise such as child labor including its worst forms to generate income, in Beitsawa two children were working on melting metallic collections to extract copper but faced death and similar cases repeated.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project aims at launching the first community center in Beitsawa and Sbeinah. Activities will focus on providing prosthetics service via Relief and development center which will handle the cases from physiotherapy/maintaining prosthetics. This service is aligned by Al Nada MHPSS services via raising awareness sessions, lectures, workshops, parental skills, referrals and home-based rehabilitation for EO victims direct/indirect, people with disabilities: children, adolescents, women, men. Child protection activities: Raising awareness sessions for children/children with disabilities along with their caregivers, case management for children and adolescents prone to explosive ordnance risk, abuse, violence and harassments. Group meaningful activities enhancing children’s social and educational inclusion. Education activities: workshops to revive life skills, explosive ordnance risk education, remedial classes, developing numeracy and proficiency in English and Arabic, vocational rehabilitation, training, and guidance to enhance beneficiaries’ economic and social inclusion such as: hair dressing, sewing, embroidery, detergents, accessories industry and perfumes, ICDL training, web design, freelancing, Photoshop and language courses. The vocational training will be conducted in collaboration with other associations. Covid-19 preventive measures will be fully considered. Activities will be reported and monitored on weekly, quarterly and yearly basis plus regular 4Ws, PRMD, PFR.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Children and adolescents Risk: Children Labor. Ignorance. Children with disabilities facing bullying. Children safety. Distance children of disabilities. Mitigation Measures raising awareness sessions for caregivers. Case management. Individual or group discussions, meaningful activities/workshops illustrate bad effects of bullying. Remedial classes. location of the facility is away from threats. children walking in groups. child-friendly spaces, surrounding pose no safety risks, also establishing separate, secure, hygienic private washroom facilities. Trained staff members. Paying particular attention to children with disabilities. Infrastructure and services can be accessed by persons with disabilities, make access paths for wheelchair access. Risk: Bad electricity supply, lack of enough lighting. Mitigation: installing lights or distributing personal flashlights. Risk: Women could be prone to gender-bias due to cultural norms, women are more prone to violence/harassment being shy/afraid to report their accidents. Mitigation: Assuring women beneficiaries that confidentiality, privacy is respected in all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information. Well-separated rooms. Risk: Victims of explosive ordnance especially girls and women are more likely to face discrimination, and isolation due to their disabilities. Prosthetic referrals could face risk of distance and accommodation. Mitigation: considering transport and accommodation allowance.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Al Nada NGO believes anyone who has contact with the organization must be safeguarded from harm, abuse, sexual exploitation and harassment. Al Nada will apply systems and policies in place consist of a Code of Conduct, PSEA policy includes details about reporting any misconduct (direct reporting, complaints mailbox, hotline WhatsApp, and e-mail). In addition to PSEA mandatory training for all employees, they must abide by safeguarding standards and what actions will be taken if they do not. Engaging organization-specific strategies and mechanisms to respond to reported cases of exploitation, abuse and harassment. All allegations are reviewed and investigated by staff or consultants with appropriate expertise. Al Nada NGO responds to any proven breaches of policy with disciplinary action, dismissal and, where applicable, reporting to relevant authorities (unless this would expose survivors to further harm).Al Nada’s staff members were trained with main focus on teachers, ORVs, social workers, and data entry employees for them being the first line of contact with the beneficiaries in order to educate them to gain supervision over their acts in order to mitigate any misinterpretation of their good intuitions by the POCs. Zero Tolerance policy is declared against any type of SEA either by the staff members or contracted third parties affiliated with Al Nada.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Fluctuating rate exchange. Mitigation: Planning efficient well-studied procurement policy considering exchange rate. Financial risks, inflation, increased prices. Mitigation: considering a reasonable budget covered further increase. Risk: Weather circumstances (Heavy rain, snow, hot summer). Mitigation: Making sure the facility's location appropriate to all beneficiaries to reach, facility will include fireplace for warming up. Risk: lack of beneficiaries' motivations. Mitigation: Constant Emboldening from Al Nada staff, incentives, interactive appealing activities and workshops. Risk: Indigenous ignorance/refusal/misconceptions/pre-existing thoughts. Mitigation: Raising awareness sessions tackling literacy, significance of education. Risk: Difficulties in gaining required fuel for generator/ shortage of fuels, lack of electricity, water. Mitigation: supplying water reservoir, generator, exerting intensive efforts to secure sufficient amount of fuel. Risk: difficulties in finding well trained and qualified staff from the area of implementation. Mitigation: capacity building is essential in Al Nada/complete the staff from Damascus. Risk: Covid-19 related restrictions. Mitigation: avoiding proximity and applying preventive measures.
