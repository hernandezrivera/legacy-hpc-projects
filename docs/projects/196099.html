Project Summary

The proposed project “Self -Reliance Initiative and Skill Tailoring for Youth-SRISTY” will achieve the main outcome of economic empowerment and livelihood improvement of people of concern through the promotion of technical skills , access to livelihoods products and income opportunities, wage earning employment facilities and social cohesions initervention. The Livelihoods and skill Development for Rohingya Refugees project in Cox’s Bazar will demonstrates Educo's capacity to operate in camp settings and host communities, ensuring the delivery of quality, livelihoods interventions that target the most vulnerable and meet their objectives, particularly addressing the unique needs of people of concerns (15-24). The project will cover 2500 adolescent, youth and women in camp 4, 8E, 10, 14, 17, 21, 27 and adjacent host communities. There will be 2000 will be in camps and 500 in host community. Among these people of concern there will be 1500 female and 1000 male.
The project will provide demand based and contextualized vocational and technical training, as well as financial literacy training to 2000 participants from FDMN community targeting 50% women to enable sustainable livelihood opportunities for the targeted project participants with essential post training input support so that they can apply training knowledge in practice and engage them in IGA as and when necessary. Also 500 participants from the host community targeting 50% women will receive vocational training along with financial literacy training, and start-up capital to execute their business plan through linkage with local traders
The project also indicated that select 400 unskilled and semi-skilled camp volunteers from FDMN communities targeting 50% women who will be provided training on waste management, communication skill, facilitation skill, basic literacy, data collection and reporting skill to capacitate them further and improve their volunteering skill to enable wage earning employment opportunities for the targeted participants. Upazila level government training service providing institutions will be linked with training participants. Also, the participants will be linked with local traders so that they have access to local markets and sell their product to have better income earning opportunities. These volunteers will be engaged in production (solar light, handicrafts etc) and humanitarian response activities e.g., health, waste management, sanitation, site management, construction etc. These initiatives will make them self-reliant and improve their livelihood. Knowledge, information, and skills (both hard and soft skills) of the participants will be boost up for their improved livelihood and grow as active and skilled human resources.
Keeping in mind the green skills and eco-friendly production the project will skilled up and engage 2000 FDMN (70% women) on climate smart sack/ vertical homestead gardening, livestock rearing etc with essential input support to ensure their engagement in production and marketing that will enable their access to nutritious foods i.e., vegetables and fruits along with improved income earning opportunities.The project will provide training to 500 HC participants (70% women) on homestead gardening, seasonal crop cultivation, bio flock and pond-based fish cultivation, livestock rearing, micro entrepreneurship with start-up capital based on their business plan. Farmers' Field Day and World Food Day will be organized and celebrated in camps and host community.
The project activity will form Peace Ambassador Group (PAG) from the FDMN & Host Community. The project will initiate awareness, interaction meeting and local level advocacy, product fair, sports and cultural events for peaceful co-existence between local and Rohingya Community.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya people have faced decades of systematic discrimination, statelessness and targeted violence in Rakhine State, Myanmar. After more than 5 years on, the Rohingya refugee crisis remains a protection and solutions crisis at its core (JRP 2022)” . Joint Response Plan highlighted the context of capacity development of refugees/FDMNs through skills development and livelihoods activities. Faced with economic pressures that create a demand for income, camp residents pursue a range of strategies to meet the needs of their households. For those with relatives abroad, remittances are often an important source of support. Others accrue debt, sell rations, compete for limited work opportunities, or engage in riskier activities to supplement aid. With little access to market, women, especially those who head the households, find it difficult to live with dignity in the absence of reliable sources of income. The study of COAST Foundation in July 2022 found that only 7% Rohingya women are involved in economic activities inside the camps, when the figure is 57.46% for the overall Rohingya population. Almost half of the Rohingyas (42.54.%) are involved in different economic activities inside the camps. In economic pursuits, 19.48% are engaged in vegetable cultivation and 15.19% in running grocery stores, 43.09% have the opportunity to engage in skill labor like building shelters/ homes, repair of solar panels and maintenance work, and vegetable cultivation and 36.46% to run grocery stores and 46.41% wanted seeds, 56.35% wanted chickens and 40.33% sought poultry sheds. Almost all (95.58%) viewed that these products have great potentials for sale inside the camps. As for other livelihoods skills, 32.60% can make bamboo products, while 47.51% are in need for needles, yarn, and frames for designing tapestry on clothes. More than half (51.93%) of the respondents think setting up shops inside the camps is a good option to sell their products. A study conducted by NRC in 2022 reveals that refugee youth and adolescents remain firmly on the margins of humanitarian programming, and are largely excluded from decision making processes, with lack of inclusion contributing to child marriage, informal labour and negative coping practices.Stress and anxiety are omnipresent among the community, with an overwhelming majority of respondents reporting experiencing intrusive thoughts and resorting to negative coping mechanisms. Approximately 96 per cent of surveyed youth aged 18 to 24 are currently unemployed. 9 out of 10 respondents aged 18 to 24 are in debt, having borrowed money within the last six months. For women aged 18 to 24, unemployment rates border at 99 per cent.Based on the findings from technical assessments, the project will focus on five key pillars: i) skills and capacity building for Rohingya commensurate with portable skills and opportunities available in Myanmar to prepare for voluntary and sustainable repatriation and reintegration in Myanmar; ii) livelihoods opportunities, iii) food security at household level such as homestead and kitchen gardening,livestock rearing, fishing within the designated area, poultry, and animal husbandry; and iv) wage earning opportunity for FDMN v) Social cohesion for bridging host and FDMN community to reduce risk and conflict supposed to imvalance livelihood impacts on host community.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project activities will be carried out with a “protection lens”, and in particular for ensuring that their activities integrate protection principles. The project will incorporate protection objectives into the programming. Safety, safeguarding, inclusiveness of participants, access, accountability and participation of people of concern will be strongly addressed in the project. All the staffs will be oriented on protection issues. Without access to livelihood and resilience opportunities, protections risks faced refugees are multiplied. These risks include exploitation and variety of forms of sexual and gender-based violence, increased domestic violence, the worst forms of child labour and child abuse including early marriages. Projects have been designed and having the trained human resources who are the members of protection sector and ADWG (Age and Disability working Group. Educo will provide MHPSS support to the livelihood groups and their parent on protection and GBV issues.

3.2 - Gender

The project has some of cross-cutting issues were including among the issues Age, Gender and diversity mainstreaming one of them. Educo's partners are expected to mainstream an age, gender diversity inclusion throughout planning stage, implementation and incorporate in reporting systems.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Educo's strongly motivated partners to use ISCG's gender with age marker guideline. Educo is strongly following the PSEA and other child safeguarding and safety net policy. Child safeguarding policy it is mandatory to follow everywhere, and staff, partners and vendors all will be oriented on CSP and to ensure its effective impact SCC (suggestion complaint and commendation) box with dedicated number, email and focal person will be used and monitored regularly. Educo maintains comprehensive risk management and safeguarding approaches for its central offices, regional offices and especially for humanitarian crisis area, Cox’s Bazar. Staffs are trained on child safeguarding to be able to respond appropriately to potential abuse of children, staffs are made aware of child protection issues, including how to identify signs of different forms of abuse and respond to allegations and they are made aware on the equal treatment of children, focusing on those with disabilities.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will practice quality of including or involving people from a range of different social and ethnic backgrounds and of different genders, sexual orientations, etc. Adolescent girls out of education service, single mother, people with disability, LGBTQI etc will be included and engaged in project activity. I will will also concentrate on DRR and eco-friendly environment aspects. Educo concentrates on inclusion and diversity in its planning, implementation and evaluation process of any project. The project will collect data on people with disability in the intervention areas. The PWD will be provided skill development training, assistive devices and linked with other relevant actors for mainstreaming then in the society with their sustainable income. Staffs will be provided training on inclusion, diversity and rights of persons with disabilities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will focuses on guideline of ISCG that affected populations will have a clear understanding of their rights related to humanitarian assistance. They will be awared on raising their voices in their own language, through which they can provide feedback and/or complaints. Educo has SCC policy and will ensure SCC (Suggestion complaint and commendation) box with dedicated number, email, and focal person in all its centres with proper reporting mechanism. The project will ensure Accountability to Affected People(AAP) by taking their opinion and demand of required materials, devices, services. Their participation and decision will be valued in project planning, implementation and assessment process. It important to ensure that the refugee participants including women, men, boys, girls and of PWD of different age and background to achieve comprehensive community engagement.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Educo and partners should continue to undertake concrete and innovative protections sensitive preparedness intervention to mitigate risk of landslide, cyclone and floods, particularly in area where refugees unable to evacuate to safe areas. Contingency planning is essential in advance of seasonal events such as monsoon preparedness, cyclone and potential outbreak of diseases. Mainstreaming disaster risk reduction will be emphasized through information, awareness and capacity building initiatives through the project activity. All the project participants will receive GBV, PSEA, DRR related awareness and training. The community people will be sensitized especially on DRR issues through audio-visual interactive theatre show, multi-media projector etc. The project will undertake tree plantation activities in wards and blocks of the camps as per the demand in the project intervention areas under cash for work activity.
