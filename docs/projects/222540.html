Project Summary

The Integrated Resilience project is aimed at improving the overall food security and economic status of 91,387 vulnerable people, particularly children, internally displaced persons (IPDs) pregnant and lactating women (PLW), returnees, refugees, the elderly and people with disabilities (PWD) through sustainable livelihoods, food systems for crop and livestock production. The project will be implemented in three counties in Unity State, that is Mayom county with 3,960 targeted individuals (1,901 males and 2,059 females), Mayendit county will have 9,600 beneficiaries (4,608 males and 4,992 females), Pariang County with 31028 beneficiaries (16,153 males and 14,875 females) as well as in Upper Nile State’s Maban county where 46,799 people (22,653 males and 24,146 females) will be reached. The project will enhance beneficiaries’ food security and economic status through implementing interventions for agricultural production and livelihoods support. Of the total 91, 387 beneficiaries, 71,977 are primary school learners from 54 schools in Maban (34 schools) and Pariang (20 schools) where Samaritan’s Purse is supporting vegetable production in school gardens to complement the WFP’s School Feeding Program.
Under the crop production support, the project will promote vegetable and staple crop farming through training on improved farming practices, inputs provision and tractor assisted tillage for farmlands in Maban and Pariang refugee and host community farmlands. The project will support the production of strategic crops for enhancing food & nutrition security such as cassava, sweet potato, groundnuts, sesame in addition to growing of local varieties of maize and sorghum crops. This support will be complemented with seed multiplication to increase availability and sustainable access to local preferred staple crop seeds. In Pariang, vulnerable refugees and host community with little or no access to land for crop production will be provided with goats to enhance their income and nutritional status. Tapping into the abundant fish in Mayendit and Mayom counties, the program will support existing groups of fisherfolks by constructing fish market stalls and support with solar drying equipment. Livelihood interventions will target mainly the youth and vulnerable women to improve their income security, and these include support for fruit and timber sapling production, village savings and loan association groups for access to finance.

1. Needs

The FSL cluster notes that the acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks, primarily widespread flooding, conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile context. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal county (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. In the projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analyzed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Mayendit, Mayom, Pariang and Maban counties are among the counties that are anticipated to continually face at least crisis level (IPC 3 or worse) acute food insecurity during for projected period (September 2024 and July 2025) and these locations are listed among the priority 1,2 and 3 counties for the 2025 intersectoral severity and prioritization mapping. Between the harvest/post-harvest period (September-November 2024) and the lean season (April-July 2025), in Mayendit the proportion of people facing IPC 3 or worse levels will increase from 60% to 75%, in Mayom (from 40% to 50%), in Pariang (from 45% to 55%) and in Maban (from 56% to 61%). The project will respond to the following key drivers of food insecurity in the country: economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, climatic shocks and low agricultural production. The 2024 Inter Agency Rapid Needs Assessments findings in Mayendit, Mayom, and Pariang (Jamjang camp) place food support as top priority intervention area through food assistance and emergency crop production. In 2024, Unity and Upper Nile States where SP will implement the project recorded some of the highest Multi-Sectoral Minimum Expenditure basket (MSSMEB), with Mayendit recording the highest expenditure at South Sudanese Pounds (SSP) 692,230 in September 2024, which is a 39 % increase from that of August owing to the high inflationary trends and currency depreciation. As of November 2024, UN OCHA reported that 1.4 million people are affected by flooding, over 377,000 people have been displaced by floods, seeking shelter on higher ground. In Unity State, which also has the highest number of internally displaced persons at 464,000, overcrowding on higher ground in Mayom, Panyijiar and Koch counties resulted in conflict among flood-dis placed people. In October 2024 alone 40,753 people were displaced compared to 9,458 displaced in October,2024. The FAO/WFP 2023 Crop and Food Security assessment mission (CFSAM) for South Sudan indicates that the national average cereal gross yield for the 2023 main agriculture is 1.11 tonnes per hectare compared to that for Mayendit (0.70 t/ha), Mayom (0.60 t/ha) and Maban (0.80 t/ha). The three counties have produced deficit cereal tonnage against total requirement for 2024, a trend that has occurred for the past 5 seasons. This points to a need for increased productivity and production in the targeted counties through interventions that includes training farmers in improved crop production practices.

2. Project activities

The agriculture and food security sector’s objective is to enhance HH agricultural production through promotion of good agricultural practices including integrated pest management (IPM) and post-harvest loss reduction (PHL), small-scale irrigation, seed system strengthening, support for small ruminants production, support for fisheries value chain through provision of fish processing equipment and marketing infrastructure, as well as economic recovery and market systems strengthening through linkages to financial services using Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs) model. Complementary staple and vegetable seeds support, provision of small ruminants, and hand tools will contribute to increased production among most vulnerable households comprising largely the recipients of WFP lifesaving food assistance thus contributing to (CLFSCO2). Additional, in line with CLFSCO2, the project will facilitate the vaccination and treatment of goats distributed through the activity and will support 54 schools' garden vegetable production to complement the WFP School Feeding programs. To ensure sustainable access and availability of adaptable locally preferred staple crop varieties, the project will support seed multiplication and also facilitate farmers voucher-based seed fairs. It is expected that by improving HH agriculture production and enhancing knowledge on sustainable agriculture, the HHs will be more resilient. This aligns with cluster object CLFSCO3 by seeking to reduce HH dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb and adapt to shocks and contextual changes. In line with the CO3, the project will also train Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and Community Based Extension Service Providers (CBESPs), train farmers in post-harvest management techniques, pest management strategies and provide water pumps for increased horticultural production. To strengthen market development under the very objective, the program will facilitate the formation of and member training on village savings and loan associations (VSLAs), fish processing and preservation, and support for youth in tree seedlings production and distribution. A series of coordinated activities are planned which include stakeholder engagements, sensitization and planning meetings in all counties involving Payam level representatives, beneficiary registration, verification and selection; identification of community extension workforce, beneficiary accountability committees, establishment of Agricultural training groups (ATGs), School based Young Farmer Clubs (YFCs), Goat Rearing groups (GRGs), fisheries groups (FGs) VSLA groups all with an average membership of 25 people. To support beneficiary capacity, the project will provide inputs for production including seeds, hand tools and will facilitate intervention specific trainings focusing on improved and sustainable technologies. Overall, the project will reach 91,537 people (45,315 males and 46,072 females) towards improving their food security and resilience. This is achieved mainly through household staple and vegetable crop production support benefitting 14,250 people (6,840 males and 7,410 females), goat production support for 1,500 people (720 males, 780 females), fisheries support for 3,660 people (2,100 males and 1,560 females) and school gardens vegetable production support benefitting 71,977 learners (35,998 males and 35,979 females) from 54 schools targeted with WFP’ school feeding program. Of the total 14,250 people supported through crop production, 135 households will be selected to become seed multipliers of preferred local maize varieties and also 600 households comprising mainly youth and vulnerable young women will be selected to raise fruit and timber tree seedlings in nurseries. The project will support 500 households from the crop production beneficiaries to engage in VSLAs.
