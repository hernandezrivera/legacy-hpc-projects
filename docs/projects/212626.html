Project Summary

In Syria, almost one in three people (30%) aged 12 and over live with one or more disabilities. The disability rate within Syria is significantly higher than the global average (15%) and equates to 4.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Due to the lack of accessible specialized services, for therapies, inclusion pathways, education, architectural barriers, social stigma and the absence of personalized assistance, people with disabilities have very few means to satisfy their primaries needs. These problems increase the risk of exploitation and abuse for them - and even more so for minors with disabilities. Participation in community life is also widely denied, thus increasing the risk of isolation and exclusion.
Sex, age, displacement status, disability and marital status have been identified as factors that expose the population more to the risks of gender violence. Along with widows and divorcees, older women and displaced people are at greater risk, especially if associated with disabilities. These risks are compounded for women who are breadwinners and responsible for meeting the financial/material needs of their families. In the area of Damascus and Rural Damascus, home to the intervention proposed here, there are 3.5 million people in need, or almost 23% of the entire vulnerable Syrian population, making it the first area for the presence of people in a state of need.
In this framework, Armadilla developed a 360-degree approach twin track approach to respond the crisis, with projects and actions aim to:
a) Deliver immediate assistance to people with disability (therapies), and women (reproductive health services).
b) Develop and implement activities with the aim to increase the capacity of health workers about the disability topics.
Main activities
1. Therapies and services for children and people with disability, and women, in collaboration with 3 local associations and the Centre for Prosthesis and Rehabilitation at Ibn Al Nafis Hospital
2. Training cycles for health and social professionals on disability-related topics, including: public health workers, students of the Public University of Damascus, training for helth and social workers of Syrian NGOs.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Armadilla has been working in Syria within the health sector since the year 2005 with a specific focus on persons with disabilities. In Syria, 3.7 million or 27% of the total population (aged 12+) have one or more disabilities, confirming that prevalence and negative impacts of living with a disability are more pervasive in developing and crisis-affected countries. According to the “HNAP, Disability: Prevalence and Impact. A Nationwide Household Survey Using Washington Group Methodology”, 2019, 26% of resident population in Syria suffers one or more form of disability, while the percentage among IDPs raises up to 29% and around 25% among the returnees. Distribution of people with disability among the national territory is somehow consistent, having a peak in the Rural Damascus Area (32%) and the less prevalence in As-Sweida (19%)According to these needs, Armadilla increased its intervention in Syria focussed, both in providing health and social services to users, and in reinforcing the capacity of local actors in delivering such services, through training and technical support. In Syria the health care systems, especially trauma cares, suffers for lack of equipment and, above all, health workers that are insuﬃciently trained. Two huge gaps hinder the capacity of the Syrian actors in delivering quality services to final beneficiaries in the field of disability. 1) techniques taught to students of physical rehabilitation and prosthesis are old and do not fit with the present problems deriving (also) by the consequence of the war, and b) a chronic lack of equipment and consumables. As data collected by Armadilla in November 2023, all the actors working on the topic (health ministries, Syrian NGOs) there is a huge lack in equipment for therapy pathways, and, again, of specific expertise among the Syrian professionals. The project aims also to reinforce local associations in providing health and social services to people with disabilities and women. In fact, through the support of community centres beneficiaries may have a quicker and easier access to social and healthcare, without facing cost of transportation, long waiting for care, and be more comfortable and protected thank to the fact that associations and beneficiaries belong to the same community. In this way, local centres may directly deliver services, and/or provide fist screening and refer to more specialised services, such as hospitals and specialists, and even be a contact point between single beneficiaries and international/local organisation working on the topics. Project will aim to: 1) delivery health services, including motor/intellectual therapies and mental health, for people with disability and women; 2) provide support to community centres and health public service through the provision of equipment, material and professional training.Activities will be complemented with others related to the protection sectors (GBV and special education for children with special needs).
