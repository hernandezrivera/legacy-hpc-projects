Project Summary

This project will specifically identify and address a significance role in protection needs in 2022 such as monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation-and assessments including severe mental distress caused by cycle of war and violence in South Sudan to internally displaced persons, returnees and host community in the proposed project locations leaving children and women vulnerable to sexual abuses and psychological distress, livelihoods disrupted, separation of children, child recruitment and households looted. Despite recent positive developments on the implementation of the R-ARCSS, violence and insecurity remain a persistent reality, however, human right violations are still being perpetrated by the same parties and most of violation and abuses went unreported especially in the hard-to-reach areas. This project aims to prevent and mitigate protection risks through community engagement including protection monitoring in hard to reach areas targeting 18,600 beneficiaries, 12,000 for general protection programming (3,100 girls, 2,100 boys, 3,800 women, and 3,000 men), 6,600 beneficiaries for HLP activities (1,600 girls, 1,450 boys, 1,950 women and 1,600 men). Through UNCDR experiences in the protection field monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment including HLP related concerns will be in place. Over the last four years 80 GBV related incidence including the twenty nine underage girls were recorded in Ayod, Nyirol, Maiwut and Panyijiar alone. That indicates that the level of GBV is high amongst the community of South Sudan especially in hard-to-reach areas. Women and girls being exposed to rape forced marriages and other forms of GBV due to the fact that displacement makes women and girls particularly vulnerable to exploitation and abuse due to their social and economic status. Women have been reported exposure to the sexual assault in several locations across South Sudan including Ayod, Nyirol, Maiwut and Panyijiar counties when it comes to accessing services and assistance. They face similar risks when walking long distances to collect water and firewood. According to the report and individual/group focus discussion during UNCDR Assessment in January 2021 in Ayod as mentioned above, UNCDR wanted women and girls to have safe spaces for sharing their experiences and accessing support, link them with services providers and dissemination of life-saving messages through awareness raising/community mobilization. UNCDR has both static and mobile presence in Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar as protection partner dealing with protection monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment, we had conducted the report between 26-30 of Jan 2021 in Ayod and 18-22 of Feb 2021 in Maiwut including surrounding villages and interviewed a good number of boys, girls, and women who expressed their basic needs and protection as well. Based on the discussion and observation, It was clear stated that more efforts are needed to ensure that most vulnerable and persons with specific need must be reached wherever they are. People had to walk a long distances about 3-4 hours in order to access humanitarian assistance. In accessing the areas of intervention, UNCDR will consider the following three;
1. Physical access (air, road and water) according of the site and urgency of the response, areas most affected by the flooding/rainy season require careful attention and planning in order to not jeopardize the service delivery. 2. Negotiated access: UNCDR has traditionally operated in hard to reach locations and has thus endured humanitarian support reaches populations, in need upon the principles of neutrality, impartiality and non-discrimination. In the past years, UNCDR relations with local authorities and elders have been forged based on transparency communication, while being very clear on the principles of confidentiality as it concerns. 3. Staffing consideration: UNCDR will take into consideration age and sex balances in all the recruitments.

1 - Needs

Since then South Sudan evolved into multidimensional challenges, combining military, economic and natural hazards, resulting in increased displacement. According to South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (2022 SS-HNO) 5.6 million women, girls, men and boys will face protection risks and violations in 2022. Despite recent positive developments on the implementation of the R-ARCSS, violence and insecurity remain a persistent reality unless programs are scaled up through both static and mobile field based intervention in the targeted counties. Insecurity and violence have disproportionately affected vulnerable people including children, older persons, survivors of gender-based violence and conflict-related sexual violence, and people living with disabilities in Ayod, Nyirol, Maiwut and Panyijiar. According to the report of UN Mission in South Sudan Human Rights Division (HRD) of January-June 2021, men accounted for the majority of victims (76%/1,406 individuals), women (13%/254 individuals) and children (11%/199 individuals).The onset of the COVID-19 pandemic worldwide and in South Sudan in March 2020 occasioned significant changes to some aspects of the Protection Cluster approach. The prevention measures taken rise the human rights and protection concerns. Sub-national organized violence has increased during the first six months of 2021 as compared to the same period in 20192020. They continue to face protection risks including neglect, abuse, exploitation, child labour, forced recruitment, SGBV, and lack of access to services. Protection concerns are especially high for girls’ risks for early marriage, early pregnancies, and denial of education particularly for girls and children living with disabilities. Decades of conflict, poverty and poor access to services made PWD particularly fragile. PWD are often stigmatized, marginalized, excluded and left unable to access the support needed to live in dignity as equal members of society. Women, the elderly, the person living with disabilities, women, and child-headed households are the most affected/exposed to all sorts of protection risks of physical and psychological torture. To meet the growing needs of the conflict-affected population, there is a need to scale up protection activities through an integrated mobile response to support UNCDR static activities on the ground. UNCDR already had both static and mobile presence in Ayod, Maiwut and Panyijiar counties since 2018, but in June - 2021 UNCDR had expanded its operation to filling in uncovered general protection gaps by 2022. We established new field office in Canal/Pigi and Longochuk with a team of 7 staffs each who had already been deployed (6 females and 8 males’ staffs). As protection partner dealing with protection monitoring, risk prevention, mitigation and assessment, we had conducted an assessment between 26 - 30 of January 2021 in Ayod and 18 - 22 in Maiwut including surrounding villages and interviewed a good number of boys, girls, and women who expressed their basic needs and protection as well. Based on the discussion and observation, It was clear stated that more efforts are needed to ensure that most vulnerable and persons with specific need must be reached wherever they are. People had to walk a long distances about 3 - 4hours in order to access humanitarian assistance. There is also a need to strengthen the existing CBPN, local area land committee where the members can be trained on basic human rights, HLP rights, and peace-building for a peaceful coexistence of the IDPs, returnees, and host community. This project will intend to carry out HLP activities to promote and claim the rights of women and group at risk, also facilitate free legal assistance. However UNCDR will therefore arrange and encourage broad based community dialogue regarding HLP among women and men. Capacity building of the local authority aims to strengthen the local capacity to prevent, mitigate protection risk and resolve the land disputes.

2 - Project activities

Activities. 1. Protection monitoring and assessment through mobile response to deep field locations to identify the protection need of the conflict-affected IDPs, Returnees, and Host community. 2. Enhance the case management activities and facilitate the referral of the beneficiaries to access humanitarian assistance and provision of psychosocial support services to the conflict-affected women, men, boys, and girls. 3. Advocacy and awareness raising to sensitize the community to refrain from harmful cultural practices such as forced and early child marriage, gender-based/domestics violence, and this activity including dissemination of live-saving messaging and information. 4. Provision of targeted assistance (IPA) to the most vulnerable individuals to cover the protection-related needs which is an effort to prevent negative coping mechanism. 5. Training of CPBNs and local authorities on home-grown prevention and risk mitigation measures including how community leaders can peacefully address potentials conflict among the community. 6. Establish or Support the existing community-based mechanism for long-term protection outcome. HLP Activities: 1. Support the person with HLP related issues to access alternative dispute mechanisms including mediation, negotiation and arbitration. 2. Facilitate the referral of a person with HLP needs to NFIs and other humanitarian partners to access the needed assistance. 3. Provide support and Capacity building on land registration and policy development. 4. Conduct HLP training for the community members comprised of IDPs, returnees and host community on human rights, HLP rights, local and international legal frameworks including traditional customary laws. 5. Provide options with HLP issues on legal remedies particularly claims of lost documents, institutions, and land registration. 6. Provide support and capacity building on land registration and policy development. 7. Provide Legal counseling and psychosocial support including community sensitization and awareness-raising on HLP rights and alternative dispute resolutions. 8. UNCDR will use IEC materials including T-shirts and posters for social messages and awareness-raising purposes.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNCDR mobile protection team will use different methodologies of collecting feedback from beneficiaries. We pay more focus on the most vulnerable individuals’ women, men, boys, girls including elderly person and people living with disabilities, our routine protection monitoring and house-to-house visit has been an effective way of getting feedback from people who cannot easily access the complaint desk. UNCDR also has been using FGD and KII to get feedback from vulnerable individuals or groups and address their needs accordingly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community feedback mechanism will be in place and accessible to beneficiaries. In the previous projects, UNCDR had established four CBPNs centers in Ayod in Jonglei state, Panyijiar in Unity State as well as Maiwut in Upper Nile State where the centers were used as protection desk to attend to any complaint or other protection concern, the same approach will be used in this project to ensure that community feedback is collected and addressed amicably. At the beginning of the project, UNCDR will carry out rapid protection needs assessments, including KII, and FGD with beneficiaries and key stakeholders to have a better understanding of the context, and this will inform the basis of our intervention. UNCDR will introduce a monthly stakeholders meeting as part of our effort to ensure that feedback from the affected community is put into consideration for effective response.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNCDR will ensure clear communication with beneficiaries include the provision of the hotline for reporting the cases of sexual exploitation and abuse committed against beneficiaries and will put in place a high degree of confidentiality so that informants are protected against any retaliation from the perpetrators. Same will be made clear to UNCDR staff, volunteers and constructors that any attempt / sexual exploitation against beneficiaries including failure to report can attract administrative measures including dismissal when the investigators will have reasonable ground to believe that staff member has committed PSEA or was aware when another colleague members committed and he/she failed to report it. It will be communicated to the beneficiaries as well that any attempt or exchange of core relief items and money for sexual favor constitutes sexual exploitation and misuse of power, therefore, should be reported immediately for further investigation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

UNCDR will immediately look into the feedback from beneficiaries and will consider corrective measures accordingly. Beneficiaries' participation in all stages of the project cycles is a crucial part of project implementation including the design of the project. UNCDR will ensure the project assistance genuinely reflect the true needs of the beneficiaries and they will be involved in all stages of the project cycles including post-distribution monitoring and evaluations.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

UNCDR is aware of Do no harm principle, the organization will ensure that any assistance will be taken closer to the beneficiaries to avoid long-distance walk as such can expose the beneficiaries to further protection risk, looting of relief items, and exhaustion. If the project will evolve any distribution of the core relief items, UNCDR will ensure proper arrangements such as the deployment of crowd controllers during distribution and registration, and in all cases, the most vulnerable individuals and people with specific protection needs will be prioritized. UNCDR technical team will work with all stakeholders to ensure that communities equally get services and they are not discriminated at all levels. UNCDR will also ensure accountability to the affected population including their full participation in the project cycle and the routine protection monitoring and assessment has been a mechanism UNCDR to identify those marginalized within the community and advocate for their inclusion in any humanitarian services and target them with direct cash support. Based on the experience in the project location, youth need to be involved in the decision making the process and strong pillar who contribute positively to bringing peace among the community.
