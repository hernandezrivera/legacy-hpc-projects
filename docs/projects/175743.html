Project Summary

Although the formation of transitional government of unity in February 2020 resulted in improved security however the subsequent shocks including COVID-19, flooding, inter-communal violence and political conflict in part of the country continue to exacerbate the already fragile humanitarian context. According to the latest IPC Analysis (December 2020), over half of the population (52.6%) faced ‘Crisis’ or worse levels of acute food insecurity, with heavy flooding and the economic impacts of COVID-19 exacerbating the situation for many. According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO)-2020, 8.3 million people in South Sudan estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. The HNO estimate that around 1.4 million children under age 5 and another 483,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021. Out of 78 counties, 47 counties have a proxy prevalence of GAM above the emergency threshold of 15 percent.
Concern will continue to support preventative and curative nutrition services in Aweil West and Aweil North in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Rubkona (Bentiu PoC and Nhialdiu) and Guit in Unity and Juba IDP camp and Kajo-Koji in Central Equatoria. In addition to the treatment of acute malnutrition, Concern will emphasise prevention of acute malnutrition in this response through the focus on behaviour change communication to improve MIYCN practices. In Juba IDP camp (former PoC) Concern manages two OTP/TSFP sites while SAM with medical complications are referred to stabilisation centre managed by other partners. In Unity, Concern provides nutrition services in 18 sites including 8 mobile sites, while in Leer county in Unity, Concern supports local partner Nile Hope to provide nutrition services in 5 sites. In Kajo-Keji Concern provides nutrition services in 4 mobile and one static health facilities. Similarly in NBeG Concern provides nutrition services in 50 sites both in Aweil North and Aweil West counties. The curative services will be provided through CMAM approach where the community volunteers will be engaged to identify and support enrolment of children with acute malnutrition before they become seriously ill. The Mother to Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs) in each sites led by lead mothers and linked to Male Change Agents (MCAs) in the community to continue in cascading the nutrition messages, MYICN practices, identification and referral of nutrition cases to sites. The Caregivers provide treatment for the majority of children with severe acute malnutrition in the home using nutrition supplies and routine medical care. When necessary, severely malnourished children who have medical complications or lack an appetite are referred to in-patient facilities for more intensive treatment.
The level of acute malnutrition often attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, extremely poor diets and limited access to clean water, poor sanitation and hygiene. Therefore, the nutrition service provision will be integrated with provision of WASH, health and food security assistance where possible and appropriate.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in South Sudan has contributed to an extended humanitarian crisis compounded by high level of flooding and the COVID-19. The formation of transitional government of national unity (TGoNU) in February 2020 provided some hope in term of political stability and reduced conflict related incidents however, the overall progress on implementation of peace agreement remained slow with no progress on the critical area of security sector reform. The inter-communal violence has continued in many pockets of the country resulted in new displacement often many times and impeding humanitarian access. More than one million people have been affected by widespread of flooding during 2020 in 39 counties and about 480,000 people have been displaced, with women and children most affected. According to the latest IPC Analysis (December 2020), over half of the population (52.6%) faced ‘Crisis’ or worse levels of acute food insecurity, with heavy flooding and the economic impacts of COVID-19 exacerbating the situation for many. According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO)-2020, 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 including an estimated 1.9 million people (74% children and 26% PLW) are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021. As per latest IPC projection, 7.2 million people or 60% of the people analysed will likely face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) from April to July 2021. In proposed target areas of Concern interventions; IPC projections for April-July 2021 indicate emergency level of food insecurity in Rubkona County (Bentiu PoC) while Guit and Leer County projected for crisis level. In Central Equatoria the Kajo-Keji county is projected to face emergency level and Juba IDP camp in crisis level. Similarly both Aweil West and Aweil North in NBeG projected to face emergency level of food insecurity. The SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020, reflects continued needs for nutrition services to ensure that malnutrition did not escalate beyond the normal trends. Despite change in admission criteria and mass screening due to COVID-19, the admissions in all Concern nutrition sites exceeded the initial projected caseload for 2020. No latest SMART survey available (suspended due to COVID-19), however the SMART Survey (January 2020) conducted by Concern indicate a GAM prevalence of 17.4%, and 2.3% SAM prevalence in Bentiu PoC while in Guit, a GAM prevalence of 12.2% and SAM 3.1%. The SMART survey reflects increasing trends in Juba IDPs camp, where GAM is reported at 18.3%. In NBeG, the January 2020 SMART survey found GAM rates of 11.1%, and 13.8% in Aweil North and Aweil West respectively, and SAM rates of 2.7% and 2.2% in each of Aweil North and Aweil West, which are all considered serious levels based on WHO classification. In Kajo-Keji, specific data on prevalence of GAM is unknown, however latest IPC indicate emergency level food insecurity and recent assessment by Concern found limited access to WASH and health services. The high levels of malnutrition are attributed to chronic food insecurity, high levels of morbidity, poor diets and feeding practices, and poor sanitation and hygiene behaviours. The COVID-19 outbreak and related restrictions resulted in higher inflation and increase in food price exacerbation the already fragile context. There is higher need for continuation of integrated nutrition services to sustain and avoid escalated nutrition or food insecurity crisis.

2 - Project activities

CO1: Concern will emphasis prevention of MAM and SAM through focus on behaviour change communication to improve MIYCN practices through individual counselling and group sessions at nutrition sites and at community level. Moreover, supporting lead mothers to support MtMSGs to improve MIYCN and involving men as father support group/male change agents in order to improve their knowledge and support to their family in maternal infant and young child nutrition. Concern will comply with COVID-19 adapted guidelines. The counseling sessions will be based on identified gaps in MIYCN practices and poor outcome (reducing weight/MUAC or recording the same weight/MUAC with previous follow up) during the follow up. In Aweil North, WFP support Concern for BSFP to prevent acute malnutrition under 5 years with special focus on children under two years. Vulnerable Household selected by county leaders and validated with children under 5 and GFD beneficiaries to effectively target those at increased risk of acute malnutrition.CO2: Concern will continue the management of acute malnutrition through the CMAM approach for treatment of both SAM and MAM as per MoH guidelines. In Unity, Concern will provide nutrition services in 18 sites including 8 mobile sites, while in Leer county, the local partner Nile Hope to provide nutrition services in 5 sites. In Juba, Concern will provide nutrition services in two sites and in Kajo-Keji five sites. Similarly in NBeG Concern will provide nutrition services in 50 sites both in Aweil North and Aweil West. SAM without medical complications will be managed through the weekly OTP. UNICEF provides RUTF and routine medicine. OTP beneficiaries attend weekly follow-up until they recover; follow up visits will consider COVID-19 guidelines. Children discharged from OTP will be admitted to TSFP. SAM with medical complications will be referred to a Stabilization Centre (SC) for inpatient care and return to OTP to complete treatment. Infants less than 6 months of age with SAM will be automatically admitted to SC until the complication is resolved and mothers will be counselled on exclusive breastfeeding. In Juba IDP camp (former PoC) and Bentiu PoCs, cases are referred to the nearby health facilities. Concern operates one SC in Unity, and two SCs in NBeG. Children and PLW with MAM will be treated in TSFP. Only in Juba IDP camp (former PoC) elders targeted for TSFP as pilot programme with WFP. Children with MAM will be treated by RUSF while PLW and elderly will receive Corn Soya Blend (CSB)++. Due to COVID-19, follow-up visits are changed from fortnightly to monthly but will follow cluster guidelines. WFP provides supplies for TSFP. To maximize the coverage, Concern uses CNVs and lead mothers to conduct screening, follow-up and behavior change education. Concern will continue to train mothers on MUAC to reduce physical contact with beneficiaries and to prevent the transmission of COVID-19.CO3: Concern will contribute to the national database by providing monthly NIS data to the nutrition cluster. The nutrition cluster analyses the admission trend and exit performance indicators and shares with partners during the bi-weekly coordination meeting. Concern also has a CMAM database to monitor the admission trend and exit performance indicators on a monthly basis. In order to estimate the prevalence of acute malnutrition, to evaluate the impact of nutrition intervention and to contribute to IPC analysis, Concern conducts annual SMART surveys in all project locations. However, SMART surveys have been temporarily suspended because of COVID-19CO4: The nutrition sites are integrated with health facilities, all SAM and MAM cases with malaria symptoms will be tested and treated for malaria. To prevent malaria, beneficiaries will receive LLITNs. Concern will liaise with WASH cluster for WASH kits for OTP beneficiaries. Concern will promote vegetable production and protection issues will be referred to the protection partners.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitors are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern will inform them about right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. Concern will refer complaint to the camp manager and protection concerns to the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff and trainings will ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect information on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through lead mothers and CNVs hired from local community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the start of project implementation, Concern will introduce the project to community leaders such as block and sector leaders; the Camp High Committee (CHC); boma and village chiefs and payam administrators; local authorities such as County Commissioner and the RRC; the SMoH; and the CHD to share information on the project implementation, entitlements to assistance, targeting for TSFP/OTP and selection of volunteers including CNVs, lead mothers and lead fathers. Caregivers will be informed of admissions criteria during TSFP/OTP days which will also be displayed at nutrition sites.The banners and IEC materials displayed at nutrition sites will have information about beneficiary entitlements and feedback mechanism. In IDP and POC sites, the Camp Management Committee (CMC) and block/zone leaders will be informed about the feedback mechanism. During the OTP/TSFP days and in counselling sessions, the staff will regularly orient the caregivers and mothers about the admission criteria, entitlements, feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. The MtMSGs and MCA will be sensitized about feedback mechanism and to cascade the message to their target members. In IDP/ PoC sites, the camp manager implements the CRM but Concern will have a Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in all sites where beneficiaries can register suggestions, feedback or complaints. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During project implementation Concern will closely collaborate with government health department, community leaders and communities. The Camp Management Committee (CMC), sector and block leaders in PoC/IDP sites and boma and village chiefs in other locations will support supervising and nominating Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNV) lead mothers and lead fathers based on agreed selection criteria. CNVs and lead mothers will continue to screen for malnutrition, defaulter tracing, early case detection and referral of malnourished children 6-59 months. The regularly monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and will inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern will consult with the beneficiaries about the site selection and distributions location/timing and plan according to the feedback from the community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with CCCM and Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Distribution sites will be located in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe locations. Prior to distributions, Concern will conduct sensitisations on how beneficiaries can reduce protection risks such as travelling in mixed groups and during daylight hours.
