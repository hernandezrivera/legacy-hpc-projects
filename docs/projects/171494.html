Project Summary

Access challenges differ greatly across the oPt, primarily due to the range of political and administrative measures implemented by Israeli and Palestinian authorities. Access and movement restrictions are severe, widespread, costly and difficult to overcome. They increase the impoverishment and vulnerability of Palestinian women, girls, boys, and men within the oPt, and directly impede humanitarian and development operations. They make reaching vulnerable communities difficult and compromise the quality of programming and impact the effectiveness of humanitarian and development interventions. These restrictions which have profound gendered and protection impacts on different vulnerable groups, have been accentuated by the measures imposed by relevant authorities since March 2020 to contain the spread of C19.
The Access Coordination Unit (ACU) provides a more systematic approach to these challenges and improves access of humanitarian personnel and goods in Israel and oPt including Gaza and the WB. Overall, 56% of ACU's UN staff beneficiaries are female (3350 from 6000). Working over all access points, the ACU provides direct engagement and advocacy with relevant authorities as well as direct and timely support, information and advice to international organizations and their personnel. The ACU also facilitates the movement of Palestinian staff serving at duty stations around the world, supporting family reunification and career development of humanitarian personnel.
The ACU formulates common policies with a coordinated approach to alleviate constraints to access and to negotiate changes to access policies and procedures with the relevant authorities including Israel, the PA, Gaza authorities, Egypt and Jordan.
The ACU currently supports the work of 24 UN agencies, over 200 NGOs, and 65 diplomatic missions whose access to populations in need is impeded by a series of political, physical and bureaucratic restrictions on access and movement imposed by authorities. In addition, an estimated 2.45 M Palestinians in need of assistance throughout the WB and Gaza will indirectly benefit through the more efficient and timely delivery of humanitarian assistance.
One of the primary services of the ACU is a 24/7 access hotline where staff can call the ACU to conduct real-time interventions with relevant authorities to resolve access incidents at checkpoints and crossings for both personnel and goods. The ACU also manages the ACULive mobile app which has 255 active users from 80 organizations, and which enables humanitarian and development personnel and agencies in the oPt to obtain timely and effective access information, guidance and support.The ACU also provides information and advice on procedures for obtaining visas, permits, project approvals and materials coordination as well as conditions and accessibility at checkpoints and crossings via the hotline and beneficiary-specific mailing list which includes 631 individuals working in UN, NGOs, and Diplomatic delegations.
During emergencies and contingency situations, the ACU deploys to key field and coordination centres to conduct access and civil-military coordination with the relevant authorities. During the July-August 2014 escalation, the ACU facilitated evacuation of over 1,300 foreign and dual nationals from Gaza to Jordan as well as coordinating other humanitarian activities. ACU coordinated the exit of dual nationals from Gaza during the C19 restrictions, as well as the entry and exit of humanitarian personnel. The humanitarian locations notification system developed by the ACU was launched in 2019 and contains over 500 locations that are regularly shared with the Israeli authorities.
Finally, information, analysis and messaging on restrictions to humanitarian space are made publicly available to support advocacy efforts undertaken by the humanitarian community and other stakeholders with regards to access of the civilian population to basic service provision.

1 - Needs

Unhindered humanitarian access is the cornerstone to ensuring humanitarians - and others - have access to vulnerable populations including women, girls, boys, and men in need. This is required to implement activities and programmes which meet needs and reduce vulnerability.The 2021 HRP for oPt has identified 2.45 million people across the oPt as being in need of assistance (PIN) of which 64% are in Gaza and 36% in the WB. This includes 44,000 females aged 65+; 36,000 males 65+; 560,000 females 18-65; 580,000 males 18-65; 600,000 females under 18; and 631,000 males under 18. Of the 2.45 million people in need, 1.4 million or 60% are severely impacted: 77% in Gaza and 23% in the West Bank. As underlined in the 2021 HRP, the nature of these needs can be grouped into three categories: Those related to lack of protection and displacement (85% or 2.1 M); limited access to essential services (60% or 1.5 M); and erosion of resilience and preparedness (82% or 2 M). Most of these needs require multi-sectorial and gender and age responsive approaches and most of the people in need have multiple needs across the different clusters which means that the total number of people in need by cluster will be higher than the total PIN for the HRP as a whole.The following are the total number of people requiring assistance under each Cluster:- Protection: 2.1 M; 60% Gaza and 40% WB- Health and Nutrition: 1.45 M; 67% Gaza and 33% WB- Food Security: 2 M; 72% Gaza and 28% WB- Education: 0.52 M; 60% Gaza and 40% WB- WASH: 1.6 M- Shelter and NFI: 0.43 M; 83% Gaza and 27% WBThe Access Coordination Unit will be supporting 24 UN agencies, over 200 international and national NGOs, and 65 diplomatic missions in accessing the 2.45 million people in need in both Gaza and the West Bank in order for them to implement projects across all six clusters listed above and to meet their needs as listed in the HRP. This will reflect positively on the ability of those organizations to respond to affected population’s needs and address vulnerabilities, including gender specific vulnerabilities, with multisectoral approaches. The needs of these organisations vis-a-vis access are related to three main areas:1. Direct support to access specific areas2. Information, data, and strategies to advocate for access3. Training and capacity building to improve access negotiation skillsIn an environment already highly restrictive due to a range of political and administrative measures implemented by Israeli and Palestinian authorities, the COVID-19 pandemic has accentuated access needs in many ways. These measures have restricted access into the oPt and between Gaza and the West Bank, in addition to movement restrictions inside Gaza and the West Bank, particularly between East Jerusalem and the rest of the West Bank. The ACU negotiated and facilitated access for humanitarian agencies and personnel during lockdowns and other restrictions. It is expected that COVID-19 access restrictions will continue into 2021, particularly affecting the 1.5 million people in need with limited access to essential services, as well as the humanitarian partners seeking to reach them and provide essential services. The ACU will continue to support the response the access of partners and ensure a coordinated response to the restrictions. Since March 2020 Israel restricted the entry of foreign citizens into Israel, including the transit of international humanitarian staff through Israel to the oPt, as well as of the movement ofPalestinians between Gaza and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, including national humanitarian staff. In response, the ACU negotiated and facilitated access of humanitarian partners and their staff. Ongoing support and intervention is needed to ensure these restrictions are minimised and continuous humanitarian access is secured.The Gaza de facto authorities also restricted access into the Strip since March and imposed a lockdown in August following a surge in positive coronaviruses cases, curtailing movement in Gaza. The ACU’s coordination with the Gaza de facto authorities for critical movement has proven to be essential for humanitarians working for NGOs and the UN.In the West Bank restrictions imposed by the PA since the start of the pandemic also made humanitarian access more difficult. Coordination by the ACU and OCHA in this environment is also needed to ensure humanitarian needs in the West Bank continue to be addressed by all humanitarian partners. Humanitarian access has also been affected by the decision taken by the Palestinian Authority (PA) to suspend coordination with Israel in response to its plans to annex parts of the occupied West Bank. The UN has engaged with Israel and the PA to enable the coordination of humanitarian deliveries with UN support, and brokered arrangements which have enabled patient transfers from Gaza with the support of WHO. Agreements facilitated by the UN have also enabled much-needed humanitarian – and other – goods to be sent to Gaza. The ACU will continue to support the coordination of humanitarian personnel and goods as required, through its membership and contribution to the COVID-19 taskforce led by the Humanitarian Coordinator, the HCT, and the logistics cluster, as well as direct support to humanitarian partners, including coordinating the exit of humanitarian personnel from Gaza in the absence of PA-Israel coordination at the crossing. The ACU will continue playing an important role in advocating for the right to humanitarian assistance and to access to health with the relevant authorities. The activities listed below will meet these needs and ultimately the overall objective of having access to PIN across the oPt in order to meet their humanitarian needs.Please note that in addition to these humanitarian and recovery needs, there are similar access requirements for development actors which are supported outside of this HRP project.

2 - Activities

- Management of a 24/7 access hotline where staff can call the ACU to conduct real-time interventions with relevant authorities to resolve access incidents at checkpoints and terminals for both personnel and goods. The ACU has a gender balanced team and makes sure that ACU female personnel can be assigned to provide assistance to female personnel when the need arises. - Provision of general information, advice, and support on the movement of personnel and goods. Information is collected in online and off-line databases and is provided through reports, the ACU website, ACU Twitter feed and a new ACULive mobile which enables humanitarian and development personnel and agencies in the oPt to obtain timely and effective access information, guidance and support. - Provision of information and advice via the hotline and beneficiary-specific mailing lists. This information includes procedures for obtaining visas, permits, project approvals and materials coordination as well as conditions and accessibility at checkpoints and terminals.- Provision of direct support to the UN, international and Palestinian NGOs, as well as diplomatic missions, to obtain visas, permits, special coordination and project approvals. - Deployment to key field and coordination centres to conduct access and civil-military coordination with the relevant authorities. In preparation for such events the ACU directly trains COGAT staff and IDF humanitarian affairs officers and conducts preparatory exercises in civil-military coordination activities during emergencies. - The running of a humanitarian locations notification system containing over 500 humanitarian locations that are regularly shared with the Israeli authorities to ensure they are not targeted during escalations.- Provision of a range of access briefings and trainings to UN and INGO staff and to other interlocutors including personnel manning checkpoints controlled by Gaza de facto authorities to familiarize participants to the variety of access-related issues, humanitarian principles and UN operations. - Provision of trainings in humanitarian negotiation to support partner colleagues involved in negotiations with duty bearers.- Support to the HC/RC, UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) with a mechanism for common policies and coordinated approaches for negotiating access with the related interlocutors, including during the COVID-19 response. This coordination role includes producing or assisting in the production of concept notes, leading or participating in negotiations and coordinating amongst the relevant stakeholders.- Regular tracking of access conditions to identify the extent and impact of access constraints. The ACU then works with the HC/RC and affected agencies either bilaterally or through coordination forums such as the UNCT, HCT and AIDA (Association of International Development Agencies), as well as COVID-19 coordination bodies such as the HC/RC led task team and coordination groups, to determine priority issues for and ways of engagement. - Development of common approaches to policies and practices introduced by the relevant authorities to counter COVID-19. The UN and humanitarian partners are respecting measures while seeking to maintain critical humanitarian movement related to COVID-19 and other emergency response. ACU is well-placed to support a common approach that assists in ensuring that humanitarian movement is facilitated and opened-up as much and as soon as it is safe to do so, and the temporary restrictions do not become permanent. Challenges relate especially to movement withing Gaza, between East Jerusalem and the rest of the West Bank, and in and out of Gaza. - Provision of access information and analysis for public reporting, internal working documents, policy papers, advocacy documents and talking points. Regular contributions are made to public UN reports, including the Ad Hoc Liaison Committee report, monthly Security Council briefings and the UNSCO socio-economic report. Contributions are made to OCHA reports, including the monthly Humanitarian Bulletin, regular Special Focus Reports, and COVID-19 sitreps, as well as humanitarian updates and other external reports produced by UN agencies, INGOs and diplomatic missions. This would include access briefings and field visits to diplomatic missions in addition to coordinating their access. - Provision of regular updates on access trends, developments and ongoing engagements with authorities in regular reports to the HC/RC, including in relation to COVID-19 related restrictions. In addition to reports, provision of regular verbal briefings at different forums, including UNCT, SMT, HCT, OMT (Operations Management Team), AIDA, Security Cell and donor meetings in Jerusalem and their equivalents in Gaza, including the COVID-19 task team, general coordination meetings and donor briefings.

3 - Indicators

The success of each activity and of the overall project will be measured through a series of performance indicators as follows:- Number of UN and NGO staff members (disaggregated by sex) receiving technical advice and support during access incidents via 24/7 hotline. Target: 150 per month- Number of UN and NGO staff (disaggregated by sex) receiving support in obtaining visas, permits, approvals and/or prior coordination, including COVID-19 related movement. Target: 250 per month- Number of personnel (disaggregated by sex) receiving access briefs as part of their training. Target: 120 per quarter- Number of updates to UN and NGO personnel with information and analysis of access conditions, including those related to COVID-19, through advisories, twitter feeds and email alerts, and ACULive mobile app notifications. Target: 20 per month- Number of negotiations on improving humanitarian access initiated and maintained, including the facilitation of critical movement during COVID-19 restrictions. Target: 30 per quarter.- Number of internal policy reports including information on issues affecting humanitarian space, brought to the attention of HC/RC in a monthly report with recommendations for action, including in relation to COVID-19 related movement restrictions and its gendered impacts to enable to COVID-19 response and other emergency response. Target: 12 per annum.- Number of access related contribution to policy level public reports. Target: 12 per annum

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Regular feedback received through the 24/7 hotline and by email is acted upon immediately; for example if an NGO staff member coordinated through the ACU to undertake a critical mission in Gaza is stopped and not allowed to proceed by the authorities, he/she can call the hotline immediately and action will be taken to contact the authorities and rectify the problem. If s/he does not make contact immediately but only after the incident occurs, an ACU official will still contact the authorities in Gaza to explain what happened, understand why it happened and ensure it does not happen again. The ACU would then call back the NGO staff member to explain what happened.If the feedback is received through a survey or an evaluation - usually conducted once a year - the these would be analysed and corrective action taken as required. The action would then be communicated back to all the direct beneficiaries usually by email.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project is designed to assist all humanitarian actors regardless of their nationality, ethnic group, religion, social status, gender, sexual orientation or race in delivering humanitarian assistance to people in need in the oPt. The modality of ensuring that the direct beneficiaries - the PIN - are assisted in a safe and dignified manner in adherence to the Do-No-Harm approach and the Centrality of Protection principles is decided by the implementing agency. Being part of the cluster system and having a seat in the ICCG, the HCT, the C-19 Task Force and other important fora ensures proper monitoring of the activities of all the implementing partners and corrective action by the ACU if needed.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed to assist all humanitarian actors regardless of their nationality, ethnic group, religion, social status, gender, sexual orientation or race in delivering humanitarian assistance to people in need in the oPt. The modality of ensuring that the direct beneficiaries - the PIN - have equal and impartial access to assistance and services and vulnerable groups, including women and girls and PWDs are specifically targeted is decided by the implementing agency. Being part of the cluster system and having a seat in the ICCG, the HCT, the C-19 Task Force and other important fora ensures proper monitoring of the activities of all the implementing partners and corrective action by the ACU if needed.
