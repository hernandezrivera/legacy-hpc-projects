Project Summary

Through the proposed two-year program outlined herein, UN-Habitat aims to boost sustainable access to housing, through restoration of community infrastructure, mitigation of safety risks, and support returning and affected families with housing-related services for later rehabilitation phase in Rural Damascus, Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Homs, Hama and Aleppo.
The Project will revive neighborhoods by rehabilitating and improving essential infrastructure and housing-related services of conflict-affected, vulnerable communities where those interventions are identified as a priority under the area-based recovery plan of the target municipalities.
The activities outlined in this program respond to the needs and priorities foreseen in 2022, and although these needs are anticipated to continue in 2023, but in the event of developments in situation that force a change in the program, UN-Habitat will inform OCAH and Shelter Sector to make appropriate adjustments.
Project Components:
Proceeding from UN-Habitat’s program of area-based local recovery planning, which is a participatory process includes representatives from local community, local and national authorities, line ministries and, where applicable, the international organizations, the program aims to facilitate access to housing and basic services through a combination of integrated activities including:
- Debris removal and management in streets and public spaces (Target: 2022: 98,000 –2023: 100,000).
- Light rehabilitation and maintenance of sewage pipes, potable water, and house connections in target communities (Target: 2022: 29,000 –2023: 30,000).
- Light rehabilitation of roads and sidewalks (Target: 2022: 98,000 –2023: 100,000).
- Where imposing high risks, fill in underground tunnels that were dug during military operations beneath residential buildings to protect buildings from collapsing (Target: 2022: 10,000 –2023: 10,000).
- Removal of dangerous, hanging and falling parts of damaged buildings (Target: 2022: 35,000 –2023: 35,000).
- Rehabilitation of common spaces in damaged buildings including the entrance, stairs, staircase, etc. to secure accessibility to apartments (Target: 2022: 35,000 –2023: 35,000).
- Installation of solar street lighting (Target: 2022: 98,000 –2023: 100,000).
- Support to local relevant cadastral entities (Municipalities and Cadastral Departments) to restore its functions and to provide services in regards of ownership and tenure documentation to all affected families when needs are presented, including advocacy, technical support, and necessary trainings (Target: 2022: 98,000 –2023: 100,000).
Specific activities per each target location will be prioritized and designed upon consultation with local communities and other stakeholders.
The selection of locations was based on a combination of criteria including
• Shelter Sector strategic priorities and severity scale in HNO 2021 and 2022
• Large population presenting shelter needs, returnees, host community and high number of people in need
• Complementarity with other ongoing/planned interventions by UN-Habitat, and other programs in WASH and ERL sectors
• Level of damage and needs identified by UN-Habitat municipal recovery plans in target locations.
Based on the above, the project targets the following sub-districts:
Rural Damascus: Babella, Duma, Harasta, Nashabiyeh, Ghizlaniyyeh.
Homs: Homs, Ar-Rastan, Talbiseh.
Hama: Suran, As-Suqaylabiyah, Muhardah.
Aleppo: Jebel Samaan.
Daraa: Daraa.
DEZ: Deir-Ez-Zor.

4 - Needs

With the crisis approaches its 11th year, 14M people remain in need of humanitarian assistance, and 5.92M will require shelter support as a result of unprecedented economic downturn, limited availability of adequate shelter, limited longer-term solutions and some of the new challenges presented by COVID-19. Needs related to basic service delivery, livelihoods and income generation have grown consistently across Syria, these needs require a more comprehensive longer-term response and will remain inadequate and hampered by the lack of infrastructure, investment, human resources and, increasingly an unreliable electricity supply (HNO 2022).In this tow-year plan, UN-Habitat is proposing shelter support to the most vulnerable communities as follows:Rural Damascus: Babella, Duma, Harasta, Nashabiyeh, Ghizlaniyyeh.
