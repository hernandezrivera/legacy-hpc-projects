Project Summary

UNFPA as the coordinating agency of the sexual and reproductive health working group (SRH WG) will strengthen the coordination of over 42 SRH partners with a focus on strengthening strategies and interventions aimed to improve the access to and utilization of quality SRH and maternal health services. UNFPA will coordinate the maternal and perinatal mortality surveillance and response (MPMSR) and the SRH information management to identify and address the preventable causes of maternal and perinatal deaths. The operationalization and use of the MCH card as well as the availability and use of SRH related proformas, protocols and guidelines for all health sector partners will be ensured. The project will support the availability of adequate reproductive health commodities and supplies including a method mix of modern contraceptives which are properly forecasted, quantified and stored. Efficient stock management will be ensured through use of the e-stock software. Implementation of the Cox’s Bazar Family Planning (FP) strategy through support to facility and community-based provision of voluntary family planning methods as well as evidence generation will be a major focus. The training, mentorship and skilling of the midwives and other health service providers in the management of emergency obstetric and newborn emergencies, family planning, respectful maternity care, quality ANC including on detection and management of high-risk pregnancy, value clarification and attitude transformation (VCAT) to strengthen the quality of care will continue. UNFPA will support the management of pregnant women with infectious diseases such as Hepatitis C, B, HIV and Dengue as well as the management of maternal nutrition needs such as anaemia. Support to effective ambulance referral of emergency obstetric and newborn care services from community to health facility (BEmONC) and between health facilities offering higher levels of emergency care (CEmONC) while ensuring that the referred mothers are transported back to their camps after management will be ensured. Support for the provision of emergency Obstetric and newborn care services at 21 health facilities including one CEmONC HF, 6 PHCCs, 1 HP, 6 FWCs, 2 HCs, 3 SCs, facility for the management of obstetric complications and emergencies will be continued as will midwifery support to Sadar hospital, MCWC and the provision of voluntary family planning counselling and services in 40 health facilities. The project will provide integrated SRH/GBV/ASRH services through a caseworker at health facilities and a midwife within women friendly and women multipurpose spaces. UNFPA will improve access to SRH information and services targeting adolescents and youths as well other minority groups like the persons with disabilities through a variety of ways like the alapon helpline and integrating SRH information in life skills activities at community level. UNFPA will work with community health workers for community awareness-raising, male engagement, pregnancy mapping, high-risk pregnant mothers follow up and referral, community distribution of family planning interventions. The project will use traditional birth attendants (TBAs) to increase facility-based deliveries through incentivization to refer and escort pregnant mothers and provide non-clinical labour support as trusted community members to pregnant mothers while at the health facility. The project will work with Islamic Foundation to address the religious barriers to access of SRH services. UNFPA will enhance its preparedness to ensure that during natural disasters, the minimum initial services package (MISP) for reproductive health will be integrated within the health response plans and emergency medical response teams (MMTs) trained on MISP. UNFPA will preposition emergency reproductive health kits including MAMA kits to support the implementation of MISP in all acute emergencies like cyclones, floods and fires.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the ISCG population estimates, there are 369,445 women of reproductive age of whom 239,339 are Rohingya refugees and 130,106 are Bangladeshi who are in need of sexual reproductive health services. Maternal health was identified as one of the major health needs during the health and nutrition survey. Despite the presence of adequate emergency obstetric and newborn care health facilities, at least one or two mothers die every week due to preventable obstetric causes and over 60 perinatal mortalities per month which is unacceptably high. The facility-based delivery remains low at 49% and according to the social autopsies conducted, the community still use and prefer traditional birth attendants which leads to delayed referrals from community to health facilities and therefore untimely interventions by health workers. In addition, lack of adequate ambulance transportation, back referrals and insecurity especially in the night also cause a lot of delays or failure to access health facilities for facility delivery even for blood donors. There is poor identification and management of high-risk pregnancies during antenatal care as well as their follow up by the community health workers at community level. At the same time only 10% of the community knew the pregnancy and newborn danger signs while 13% knew about birth preparedness according to the UNFPA SRH study. There is increased antenatal care coverage but over 70% of the mothers that die have received at least four or more antenatal care visits which implies poor quality of antenatal care that is provided. The joint monitoring assessment report on access, availability and quality of SRH services conducted by the SRH WG in April-July 2024 indicated that SRH services are readily available and accessible at health facilities but there is a need to continue strengthening the capacity of health facilities to provide quality emergency obstetric care, equipping the health facilities to provide specialized services such as laboratory services and blood banks and capacity strengthening of health service providers in different aspects of comprehensive sexual reproductive health as well as the use of standard protocols and guidelines so as to improve the quality of SRH services provided. Even though there is improved uptake of voluntary modern family planning methods with a contraceptive prevalence rate of 55% from 33.7% and the uptake of long-term methods at 7% from 1.9% as well as high knowledge (98%) on family planning, the socio-cultural- religious barriers to sexual and reproductive health care services still hinder access to voluntary family planning and other SRH services especially for adolescents and persons with disabilities. The Youth ISNA reported that 35% of adolescents and youth reported are not receiving any sexual and reproductive health services while the nutrition ISNA reported 24% of women have anaemia which may pose a significant risk to maternal and fetal health. There is a need to provide alternate and integrated approaches to access SRH information and services. Efforts to create and improve community sensitization, awareness, male/religious/community leaders’ engagement, mobilization, and timely referral from community to health facilities for SRH services remains critical. Women and adolescent girls including the PWDs are at an increased risk of sexual violence and its health consequences such as teenage pregnancy are a major risk factor to poor obstetric complications and outcomes for both mother and baby. Efforts to address child marriage and poor health seeking behaviours as well as poor access to SRH services by adolescents at community level are vital . Cox’s Bazar is generally prone to acute natural disasters like fire, floods, cyclones and monsoons and sexual reproductive health needs for women and girls are always forgotten and there is a need to ensure that integration of the MISPinto the emergency response plans and teams is done.

UNFPA, as the coordinating agency for the GBV sub sector and a lead agency working on GBV prevention and response, continuously analyzes the different needs, roles, relationships, and experiences of women, girls, men and boys in the assessment, planning, implementation, and review of the assistance (including protection). UNFPA mainstreams GBV in all its programmes of which SRH is one and fosters GBV/SRH integration. UNFPA believes that the promotion of gender equality in programming not only increases the effectiveness of humanitarian action but also upholds our moral and legal obligations to protect the dignity and rights of all people including persons with disabilities to ensure dignified impartial access by operating according to the principle of non-discrimination and bolstering our mandates to serve the most vulnerable individuals who are at risk of being left behind

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNFPA will ensure its commitment of reaching the most vulnerable population by enhancing the safety and dignity of the targeted populations including children and adolescent girls and boys as well as persons living with disabilities and older persons. UNFPA as a SRHR agency works to protect all persons, including adolescents and youth, from all forms of violence, abuse, and exploitation including gender based violence and harmful practices like child marriage. Along with providing key information and awareness sessions, the programmes will also support the beneficiaries to understand and identify the referral services related to SRHR, AYSRH, GBV and MHPSS available in the community. As a result, this will enable them to seek services when needed. In accordance with that, UNFPA will ensure the inclusion of all age and gender groups to make the programme inclusive and non-biased. UNFPA services target all persons in need without discrimination including women of reproductive age, adolescent girls and boys, adult men and women as well as key populations such as sex workers and survivors of gender based violence. UNFPA will continue reinforcing partnerships with the organizations working with the Gender Diverse Populations (GDP) and capacity development on SRH/GBV, AYSRH and PSEA. The ethnic, key populations and religious minorities will continue to be a special focus and be involved in the SRH services delivery and access.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNFPA along with its implementing partners (IP) ensure a people centered approach across its programming to achieve better health outcomes and embrace our collective commitment toward the accountability to affected populations (AAP) framework as it ensures the Rohingya, and host communities provide invaluable influence, meaningful participation and decision making in the design, implementation and monitoring of program and services. UNFPA will ensure that the people's voices are listened to especially through the regular community engagement strategies including conducting courtyard sessions targeting different members of the community like men, adolescent boys, adolescent girls and women. Client Satisfaction Surveys and suggestion boxes will be in place to solicit, hear, and act upon the voices and priorities of affected people and establish the level of client satisfaction with the quality of services being delivered at UNFPA supported facilities including health facilities and WFS.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNFPA has zero tolerance for sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA) and sexual harassment (SH) and actively advocates for this principle across the organization, IPs, vendors, service providers & beneficiaries. The organization is committed to preventing SEA and providing immediate support to victims. UNFPA works with other organizations to create a workplace free from SEA. Since 2017, UNFPA has been a member of ISCG PSEA network in Cox's Bazar and appointed PSEA FocalPoints for the organization.Training on PSEA is a top priority for UNFPA staff when they join the organization, including on staff obligations to report any wrongdoing and survivor-centered support. UNFPA's Office of Audit & Investigation Services (OAIS) ensures that staff can easily report misconduct through an online platform/hotline. PSEA focal points are available to receive complaints & report them back to the OAIS. All personnel involved with UNFPA, including volunteers, contractors, and vendors, receive training on PSEA. The organization has a retaliation policy in place to protect survivors, victims, and whistleblowers from any form of retaliation. Under this project, UNFPA aims to continue building the capacity of their staff and related personnel on SEA and related issues. UNFPA will also strengthen PSEA Champions to prevent SEA and raise awareness on reporting mechanisms among the Rohingya population and host communities. UNFPA will conduct community-based awareness modules and use audiovisual & pictorial resources in awareness sessions to ensure communities are aware of SEA & reporting mechanisms.The organization will work to strengthen reporting channels and ensure conducive reporting mechanisms are available in each UNFPA-supported facility. Whenever needed, UNFPA will conduct capacity assessments and support the implementation of capacity strengthening plans to ensure IPs are maintaining the required PSEA and safeguarding mechanism in coordination with ISCG PSEA Network

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Climate-induced hazards like floods, cyclones, and storms can hinder access to healthcare facilities, making it harder for pregnant women to receive timely prenatal and emergency care. In addition, climate hazards can affect the nutrition status of women and girls which increases risks like anemia, preterm birth, and low birth weight. Adaptation measures like resilient healthcare infrastructure and accessible transportation routes improve reliable access to maternal health services. Addressing the adverse impact of climate change on maternal and newborn health , SRHWG will advocate and provide strategic guidance for ensuring holistic approaches/targeted interventions that mitigate environmental risks of mothers and newborns that are contextually fitted and evidence-based to reduce climate-related mortality and morbidity in humanitarian settings.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

UNFPA aligns to the health sector emergency and response plan with collective efforts to enhance the preparedness and response readiness actions to minimize the direct and indirect impacts of natural hazards. Through this project, UNFPA will enhance its preparedness to ensure that during natural disasters and disease outbreaks, the minimum initial services package (MISP) for reproductive health is integrated within the health response plans and members of the emergency medical response teams (MMTs) will be trained on MISP to improve their capacity to provide quality MISP implementation during emergencies. UNFPA will ensure the preposition of emergency reproductive health kits to support the implementation of MISP in all acute emergencies. UNFPA/SRH also has contingency plans for all scenarios of natural disasters. UNFPA will have a contingency budget as well as resource mobilise through CERF and other emergency funds to ensure that life saving SRH services are provided during any emergency

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. Adolescents and youth is a core mandate of UNFPA. UNFPA is the co-chair of the youth working group and so will coordinate the youth activities across all sectors and ensure that all sectors are adolescent and youth responsive. As this age group is vulnerable to sexual violence, poor access to sexual reproductive health and voluntary family planning especially due to social cultural barriers, are faced with child and forced marriage and well as teenage pregnancies, this project will provide alternative SRH-GBV approaches to access of information and services. In addition, UNFPA will provide adolescent and youth responsive services at all UNFPA supported service points and through the SRH WG will coordinate the ASRH technical committee to ensure that all health services are adolescent and youth responsive

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project ensures access to maternal health care and thus lays a foundation for long-term health, empowerment, and recovery by addressing critical healthcare needs, empowering women, and building healthier communities. The project empowers women with knowledge and skills through health education and counselling which helps them to care for themselves and their families by equipping women to make informed decisions about their health and well-being including on the importance of good nutrition, spacing pregnancies for positive health outcomes for mother and baby, the danger signs in pregnancy and the importance of seeking care including for ante natal care and for their birth and delivery. When refugee women receive reliable maternal health care, they are healthier and better able to support their families and contribute to their communities.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Cox's Bazar (CXB) has poorer Health, Nutrition, and Population (HNP) indicators relative to national averages. For example, the estimated total fertility rate of 3.2 children per woman (2016) in the district contrasts with the national rate of 2.1 as such it is a UNFPA priority district for the development programme focussing on host communities within the UNFPA country programme which is contributing to the Bangladesh development plan. This project will also support the Government health facilities which are linked to the district development plan
