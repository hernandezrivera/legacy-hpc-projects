Project Summary

In 2023, Shafak organization plan to respond to the increased GBV concerns of the crises affected women and girls by providing prevention, response, and mitigation GBV activities through 6 static facilities as follows:
Two WGSSs in Marret Tamsrin, one WGSS in Azaz, one WGSS in Qurqenea, one WGSS in Armanaz, and one WGSS in Idleb city. Moreover, 9 outreach teams will conduct GBV activities in four main Districts Idleb, Harim, Jebel Saman, and Azaz.
GBV prevention, response, and mitigation activities will be delivered to 34405 individuals from IDPs and host communities, focusing on the most vulnerable, including survivors, adolescent girls, elderly women, and PWDs.
The proposed project will offer gender and age-appropriate prevention and response GBV services, including GBV case management (including case fund) by using GBVIMS+ system, recreational and structured PSS activities, Income Generating Activities (IGA) through the provision of microfinance training, small business management training, and provide small-grants for women to be able to start new small projects/businesses to empower women and achieve economic independence, provision of one-off unconditional Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) (in-kind and cash and voucher modality), AMAL initiative includes three components (Young Mothers' Clubs (YMCs), community-centered components, and provider-centered components), GBV awareness-raising sessions and campaign targeting women, men, boys, and girls in both IPDs and host community sites, life skills, safety audits, as well as GBV maintaining training for GBV and non-GBV actors including humanitarian actors (workers in other sectors like WASH, Shelter/NFI, and Education). Shafak will support women-led organizations in the targeted locations that are implementing GBV and women empowerment projects by responding closely to their needs and delivering technical GBV capacity-building training.
Moreover, Shafak will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on GBV topics such as GBV core concepts, safe identification and referral, and communication skills to enable them to identify and refer people in need to the case management team. Moreover, the community committees will play a significant role in raising GBV issues that are faced by the community and introducing the project activities.
Furthermore, as part of response activities, the outreach teams will conduct Psychological First Aid (PFA) within camps, reception centers, and other IDP collective places. Shafak will engage men and boys in GBV prevention activities through mobile GBV activities, which aim to mitigate GBV risks and make the community more supportive of GBV issues.

3 - Needs

More than ten years of crisis have inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population, who have been subject to massive and systematic violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. More recently, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have increasingly become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. HNO 2022According to the GBVIMS+ quarter three report of 2022, 100% of female survivors reported that GBV disproportionately affects women and girls. PWDs still have significant challenges accessing GBV services. 63% of reported incidents occurred at the survivors' homes, and 95% of alleged perpetrators are people who belong to survivors' first circle of relation. IDPs continue to be the population most accessing GBV services. The most common forms of GBV experienced by survivors are physical Assault of reported incidents, followed by psychological/emotional Abuse and Denial of Resources, Opportunities, or Services.Furthermore, women typically do not engage in income-generating activities and household or community-level decision-making, and for women with disabilities, the opportunity to participate in decision-making is even further reduced. Disproportionate stigma against divorced women and girls pervades in northern Syria, and most conversations lumped divorced and widowed women and girls together when discussing their vulnerability “Voices 2022”

4 - Implementation of activities

GBV project activities will be delivered in 5 WGSSs, Shafak RH facilities as integration with health/SRH, and by 9 mobile outreach teams. The prevention, mitigation, and response GBV activities for 34405 individuals, including PwDs, will be as follows:1.Response services to 2500 GBV survivors by using GBVIMS+.2.AMAL initiative, including three components for 300 G.3.IPA, and CVA for 600 w and g. CVA SOPs will be used to ensure providing quality services.4.Structured and non-structured PSS /recreational for 9800(6800W and 3000G). 5.IGA and vocational training for 875 (575W and 300G) using IGA SOPs. 6.Structured (GBV awareness raising toolkit) and non-structured awareness sessions for 18830 W, M, B, and G.7.Life skills sessions for 1100 W and G using “My Safety My wellbeing.” 8.Safety audits in 6 IDP camps.9.GBV mainstreaming training for 320 humanitarian workers and building capacity of 80 GBV staff on GBV topics. Collaboration between the WGSSs and Shafak health facility to refer GBV cases between the WGSSs and RH services. The project activities ensure PwDs' and elderly women's inclusion in the activities. Building the project staff’s capacity and providing transportation will increase PwDs access to WGSS centers. The MEAL activities will be conducted by using the GBV M&E toolkit.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Rosk1: Privacy concerns and confidentiality and security may prevent women and girls from accessing GBV response services. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation 1:1. GBV case management service providers ensure that confidentiality principles align with global standards and GBV SOPs. 1:2. Ensure that data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. 1:3. Use of the GBV IMS system, which enables GBV case managers to effectively and safely collect, store, analyze and share data related to the reported incidents of GBV. Risk 2: Resistance or backlash from community sections and community leaders to GBV messages and lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access services. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation. 2:1. Community involvement from the project's inception, the evaluation stage, and the entire project cycle. 2:2 Ensuring the support of male family members by involving them in awareness-raising activities. 2:3 Adapt GBV messages. Risk 3: Livelihood opportunities may increase the risk of GBV, and income expectations may be unrealistic and create dependency. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation 3:1 Consult women, men, boys, and girls in program design. 3:2. Ensure service beneficiaries know where to report it in the event of violence. 3:3. Raise awareness about PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report it.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk Identified1: the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by the project personnel on project BNFs and women staff. Mitigation: 1- provide awareness raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA 2- train the new staff on PSEA, CSG and to have refresher trainings to the old staff. 3- Distribute banners, PSEA reporting mechanism including the suggestion box, WhatsApp number...etc. Risk 2: No reporting from SEA survivors: Mitigation: 1- Raise awareness between project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms. 2- Apply various reporting channels that are accessible to all BNFs. 3- Emphasize on the principle of confidentiality in dealing with SEA cases. 4- Assign gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage women and girls to report


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due to increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures:1.1 Close monitoring and participation in info-sharing mechanisms and meetings.1.2 Security-related SOPs in place. 1.3 Remote monitoring mechanisms in place.1.4 Maintain strong lines of communication with Shafak HQ security in order to preempt any potential increase in hostilities, takeover, or areas through agreements, and be prepared to react accordingly. Risk 2: Disputes between host communities and IDPs; Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 2.1:Targeting host community, 2.2: ensure Complaint mechanism is accessible by all people. 2.3 integrity and clear verification process conducted according to announced criteria. 2.4 Prior coordination with local actors and stakeholders in the community to ensure the process.Risk: Outbreak of COVID 19; Likelihood: High; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 3.1:Daily check of staff to ensure their safety is a major symptom of the Coronavirus.3.2:Emphasis during work on the need to leave a distance of at least 1 between workers.3.3:The necessity not to exchange equipment and tools before sterilization. 3.4:Ensure the presence of awareness brochures within the work area and in a clear place.3.4:In case of imposed curfew. Suspend the activities and change the modality to be through service providers and distribute the dues of the staff
