Project Summary

The WATER project will provide approximately 450,000 vulnerable individuals with WASH assistance including 126,000 men, 126,000 women, 99,000 boys and 99,000 girls in areas of high WASH severity across the governorates of Homs, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Idlib, and Deir-ez-Zor. The project aims to provide both life-sustaining and life-saving WASH interventions for the most critically in need including IDPs, newly-returned returnees, and vulnerable conflict-affected hosting communities. Communities will be selected based on a detailed needs assessment and those with the highest need and underserved by the humanitarian community will be prioritized for assistance. Life-sustaining activities will include water/sewerage network rehabilitation in conflict-affected areas where access to household-level water is limited or where the sewerage system is damaged and poses a significant risk to the local population. Reverse Osmosis units will be provided to communities in East Ghouta with high levels of nitrate while debris removal will be conducted in areas where it poses a significant health and protection risk to the local population, with a focus on Deir-ez-Zor, Homs and Aleppo governorates.
Life-saving activities will also be implemented to ensure immediate relief for vulnerable IDPs and returnees and those affected by the spread of COVID-19 including water tank distribution, and the provision of COVID-19 kits and cleaning kits for vulnerable families.
The project will use a participatory and integrated approach ensuring that the needs of men, women, boys and girls are includeding from project design stage through to implementation and post-implementation monitoring of the works. Children, adolescents, young adults, middle-age adults and older adults will be solicited during the initial needs assessment for the project and post-implementation. Each age and gender group will have an opportunity to provide timely feedback to ADRA on the intervention through regular contact with ADRA monitoring staff.
The needs of young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-age adults and older adults will also be taken into consideration as children, adolescents, middle-age and older adults may require a higher amount of water for cleaning, cooking and for children who play outside and require more frequent hygiene practices.

4 - Needs

The WASH needs remain critical across Syria with approximately 12.2 million people in need of WASH assistance (HNO 2021), an increase since 2020. It is estimated that 63% of the Syrian population remain at WASH severity levels 4-5, 36% of the population rely on informal sources of drinking water, 70% of sewerage remains untreated while half of sewerage systems are not functioning and 29% of garbage inadequately disposed (HNO 2021) posing significant health and protection risks to men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and people with disabilities (PwDs).Based on ADRA’s needs assessments, ongoing presence in the area and current interventions, priority WASH needs were identified in Rural Damascus where communities rely on water trucking every 15 days, or private boreholes with unverified water quality (Mneen, Maliha, Hosh Al Dawahra, Isal Al Ward, Rabwa) due to a limited power supply or damages to the network. In Deir-ez-Zor governorate, low water pressure at household level or network damages in Jbeilieh, Deir-ez-Zor (DeZ) city, Dablan and Abo Kamal causes many already impoverished families to purchase water with unverified quality or resort to negative coping mechanisms. Idlib governorate is in high need with At Teh and Khan Sheikhoun returneees lacking water at household level while in Halbeh, families collect rainwater for household use. Aleppo governorate also remains in high need of WASH intervention with low water pressure in Qadi Asker, Aleppo city, causing families to purchase water for their basic needs while the accumulation of debris in Al Liramoon poses protection risks to families and the lack of adequate sewerage in Zabadin causes health risks to local families.

5 - Implementation of activities

ADRA will implement life-sustaining and life-saving WASH activities in close coordination with multiple stakeholders including the WASH sector, SARC, other humanitarian partners and the Ministry of Water Resources (MoWR), the Ministry of Local Administration and Environment (MoLAE) and local authorities such as the mayor, moktar, and the Head of the local water unit. ADRA’s social and technical staff will conduct needs assessments in the targeted areas including interaction with men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and PwDs to ensure the participation of beneficiaries and will select activities based on identified needs for the vulnerable communities. Activities will include life-sustaining works such as the rehabilitation/expansion of water and sewerage networks, the distribution of garbage bins and solid waste management in addition to life-saving interventions with the distribution of water tanks and COVID-19 WASH kits. Water and sewerage network rehabilitation will include works such as the provision and installation of RO units with solar panels to remove nitrate from contaminated water, the spot repair or replacement of damaged pipelines in water networks, the provision of equipment or rehabilitation of boreholes with solar panels/generator, replacing or repairing damages sewage lines, desludging sewage lines, and the provision of manhole covers to avoid protection risks. ADRA will also distribute garbage bins and conduct solid waste management in areas at high risk of health and/or protection risks. ADRA will conduct a competitive procurement process, monitor all activities with gender-balanced staff, and complete a handover once works are complete. Baseline and endline data will be collected for all activities

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Target areas are perceived to prioritize and empower one group over another or unfair selection of communities, causing community tension and increasing the risk of (S)GBV.2. Vulnerable community members, particularly women and children are asked to provide services in return for humanitarian assistance.3. There is uinequal access to WASH works due to their location raising (S)GBV risks for women and children who travel further to reach assistance.4. Exposure to UXOs causes injuries during the implementation of solid waste management posing protection risks to men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and PwDs.5. Child labour used on project sites increases the risk of school drop out and protection risks such as physical injury in addition to GBV.6. Exposure to pollution, bacteria, rodents and other health hazards during solid waste management poses health risks to men, women, boys and girls. Mitigation: 1. Conduct detailed assessments at local level to ensure assistance takes into account community dynamics, explain selection criteria clearly to Ministries and ensure communities selected are based on needs assessments alone.2. Train staff in PSEA and closely monitor all staff and contractors working in target areas. Ensure complaints’ feedback mechanism availability.3. Ensure detailed needs assessments are conducted in coordination with local stakeholders and works are located in areas promoting equal access to assistance.4. Conduct hazard awareness training with staff; ensure areas are cleared pre-intervention; contractors take necessary safety precautions.5. Explain ADRA Syria’s policy on child labor to contractor prior to implementation; monitoring.6. Ensure adequate safety measures are followed.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks/Assumptions:1. COVID-19 spreads significantly impeding implementation and posing health risks to staff, contractors and beneficiaries.2. Cash funds transfers into Syria remain challenging, impacting the timeliness of intervention.3. The devaluation of the Syrian Pound continues, causing an increase in costs and activities cannot be implemented within the budgeted amount.4. The security situation in the targeted areas deteriorates, impeding access to the area for intervention.5. Delays in receiving permission impact the timeliness of the intervention or reduce the program period thereby impacting program quality. 6. Qualified staff are not able to be found to implement the planned activities.7. There is the potential for corruption or fraud particularly during the procurement process.8. Pressure is placed on ADRA to select certain areas for implementation, impacting fair selection. Mitigation:1. Ensure staff and contractor use of PPE during implementation and compliance with all WHO recommendations.2. Continue to try alternative transfer routes into Syria; maintain close coordination with banks and NGO coordination bodies to facilitate transfers.3. Monitor the exchange rate closely; use open and competitive procurement processes; adjust project targets if necessary.4. Close monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. Temporary pause activities when necessary.5. Close follow up with ministries throughout the project. Where delays are extended, consider relocation of assistance.6. Recruit qualified staff early in the project; conduct regular capacity building7. Adhere to ADRA's Procurement and Finance Policies8. Explain selection criteria early; verify need
