Project Summary

The organization seeks to alleviate and mitigate the accidents related to the presence of explosive ordnances (EOs) in contaminated areas and increase the protection of at-risk persons through direct and indirect risk education activities, and through the identification, marking and disposal of EOs spots. The risk education (RE)component includes three key activities as follows:
- RE Sessions’ which aim that the at-risks persons, living in contaminated areas, have the necessary information to be able to adopt safe behaviours and reduce the threats posed by the presence of explosive hazards. Based on context, access and needs analysis, this is conducted either through direct RE sessions at households’ level or through Training of Trainers to strengthen the skills of key local actors and ensure the proper dissemination of the messages through a sustainable approach. Noting that the sessions include the COVID-19 key messages, and the provision of the personal protection equipment while maintaining the preventative measures to mitigate the risk of virus transmission.
- Contamination Impact Surveys which aim to identify locations that have experienced a certain level of bombing, shelling and fighting.
- Accident and Victim surveys which aim to identify the areas in which the accident occurred, and the type of weapon that is suspected to be the cause of the accident. An accident could result in one or more victims or even no victims at all.
During the provision of RE activities, people who need multi-disciplinary rehabilitation services are internally referred to supported health centres, where they can have access to a variety of services depending on the outcomes of their needs assessment process. In addition, safety briefings are provided to humanitarian actors (local and international NGOs) to raise their awareness within the humanitarian community about risks and the consequences of explosive hazards they could face when in the field to minimize the risk of accidents among aid workers.
In Parallel, Non-Technical Survey and clearance teams identify, mark and dispose EOs. It starts in the collection of surveys to obtain accurate information on the presence, type & level of contamination in the areas. Prioritization is dependent on the presence of IDPs in communities where there are visible EOs in their area or the presence of any community in areas where the accidents that have occurred within the past 6 months. Currently, the organization implements clearance activities in Raqqa, while maintaining the possibility of deploying Clearance teams to Deir Ezzor if the context allows.
Finally, The organisation’s advocacy team continues to lobby for the ban of the use of Explosive Weapons in Populated Areas and produce then disseminate evidence-based advocacy factsheets to mobilize and influence local, national, regional and International stakeholders by raising key humanitarian and protection issues for populations and humanitarian actors in Syria.

4 - Needs

10.3M people live in EOs contaminated areas who are in need of Mine action (MA) assistance. Escalations in hostilities, in areas around Idleb, added additional layers of contamination. Following hostilities in late 2019, areas in NES, notably in RAQ, DEZ and HSK governorates remain difficult to access due to contamination and recontamination of areas previously cleared. Contamination by EO is a major protection risk that poses threats to the safety of civilians, exacerbates vulnerabilities and hampers safe access to humanitarian support, and prevent safe returns. Since 2018, 12,000 incidents have been reported by MA working group informing that there were injured or killed persons while playing in residential areas. Out of 854 Contamination Impact Survey conducted by the INGO in 2020, 4% of surveyed villages are contaminated.According to the INGO MA internal data, 530 incidents have been reported in 2020, with an average of 1.5 incident per day. 630 victims have been affected in the last 12 months, almost 2 per day. Most affected governorates are Dier Ezzor with 30% of incidents reported, Raqqa and Idleb with 22%, followed by Aleppo with 12%.53%. Men and boys represent 85% of all recorded victims affected by EO accidents (50% men and 35% boys). Of the total number of direct victims, 41% are children.Low level of knowledge on risk is also confirmed by MSNA 2020: 100% of interviewed people in HSK reported to have not participated to EORE awareness in the previous 3 months, 92% in RAQ , 89% in Aleppo and 61% in DEZ. Number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased the burden on health sector, in particular, on the provision of specialized services including trauma care, physical rehabilitation, and PSS

5 - Implementation of activities

Contamination Impact Survey (CIS) constitute the first step of RE intervention through collecting information on evidence of contamination. Field teams are deployed at community level to gather relevant information through questionnaires on EOs related accidents, victims, and presence of contamination using accident and victim reports. The simultaneous implementation of CIS and RE sessions will permit to gather necessary and relevant information on accidents and victims. The field teams will be able to identify record and refer victims of EOs and IEDs to appropriate services. The NTS will be conducted to gather and analyze data on suspected and confirmed hazardous areas and EOs to support prioritization for clearance. The information is fed back to Multi Task Teams (MTT) for clearance. RE sessions, a composite intervention methodology implemented, to remain flexible to changes in context and needs. Thus, based on context, access and needs analysis, RE is conducted either through direct sessions or through TOTs. All implemented activities are provided in an inclusive approach to DGA. PWD will participate through conducting activities, or participation of identification of activities linked to specific needs or barriers accessing information. All Material used will be inclusive, taking into account gender and culture sensitivity, age groups, and types of impairments difficulties. The INGO will conduct analysis on its internal and secondary data, share relevant data with other advocacy actors (in a confidential and secure way), produce and disseminate two evidence-based advocacy factsheets highlighting protection concerns on use of explosive weapons to the national, regional and international community.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Protection of Field staff and partners: keeping low visibility •To avoid risk to partners, staff and beneficiaries, the INGO does not conduct MA activities in GoS areas. •The INGO adopts conflict sensitivity approach. The INGO will conduct prioritization assessment of land/property or areas to be cleared based on: local priority, land/property ownership, contamination evidence and density.•Facilities safe closure and protecting data. Field staff trained on safe withdrawal/evacuation including ways to protect and/or destroy data.•Applying PSEA policy for staff, partner staff and suppliers. •Presence of explosive hazards limiting access and posing a threat to beneficiaries, mitigated by delivery of risk education training to humanitarian personnel to increase awareness of the explosive threat; include first aid component in risk education sessions and TOT level.•Creation of wrong expectations among beneficiaries, who could wrongly attribute to risk education teams the ability of removing explosive ordnance mitigated by clear explanation of intervention and objectives to beneficiaries.•RE not reaching all vulnerable groups because of lack of understanding of social norms, work patterns and age and gender considerations; mitigated by involvement of community focal points in the planning and delivery inclusive DGA of RE sessions•beneficiaries become overconfident and engage in risky behaviors, mitigated by Involvement of survivors of explosive hazards as role model for behavior change; training of trainers (ToT) of local liaison team.•Lack of contextual information on accessibility to services or on the condition of service facilities mitigated by collection of information from health sector and stakeholders.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Loss of access to intervention areas can be mitigate as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted to the context. The INGO also continuously monitors the security situation in its intervention areas and adapts its access strategy to the regular conflict analysis and how it affects its operations.• Lack of local community acceptance is mitigated through positive relations with the community. The INGO and its partners are well-perceived on the ground and always make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in its programs; setting in place community satisfaction surveys to measure the satisfaction of the community with its services. • Loss of funding is mitigated by the organization’s close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding. • The eventual spread-out of COVD-19 pandemic and its implications on the operational capacities, as per prior experience in 2020. To mitigate this risk, the organization and partners will ensure the following: i) continue exploring alternatives remote modalities of intervention ii) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available in targeted locations; iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging in the overall package of activities; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster.• The available budget reduction due to exchange rate loss is managed by the organization continuous use ofUSD value for money transfer and payment as well as, close monitoring the local market flow.
