Project Summary

IDP movements still continued across all geographic areas, with Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa making up the top seven most-affected governorates in 2020 alongside.
While an overall lessening in hostilities has contributed to reduced population movement since March 2020, there is some evidence to suggest that the increasingly precarious financial position households find themselves in, combined with the often-minimal basic services available in areas of return, has prompted some pre-emptive movement towards camps and sites where the promise of humanitarian assistance exists.
At least 3,000 households are currently on waitlists for camps in the north-east. Reports have also recently been received of IDPs departing Al Hol camp for areas of origin or secondary destination in Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Deir-Ez-Zor Governorates, only to immediately approach other camps, such as Areesha and Mahmoudli, for admission.
needs for mine risk education remain paramount for prevention but are currently not met at scale, with more than 70 per cent of key informants reporting they had not attended mine risk education activities recently
The project will ensure equal access to mine risk education for women and girls in school and
community based initiatives.
Activity: The RE part of the project provides two main activities. The first RE activity is delivery of lectures and recreational programs to groups of teachers and students; these will form a curriculum for mines and ERW awareness in schools. The curriculum contains
lessons and planned activities and games which will raise children's awareness of ERW and their ability to identify them and act safely around them. in AL Raqq and TABQA is mostly contaminated with ERW rather than landmines; in this environment the curriculum
will prioritize and concentrate its focus on ERW, cluster bombs, unexploded barrel bombs
and other relevant hazardous items. Teams will provide the targeted schools with training
and booklets containing this curriculum, and thereby try to integrate education about ERWs and living safely with ERWs into the school program so that children will internalize the information rather than just recall it. White Hats organizations feel that ERWs are a
long-term problem, and that as such, long impact approaches for children must be sustained. The second RE activity is distribution of leaflets and posters with basic instructions about the avoidance of ERW, including information about Improvised
explosive devices (IEDs) and precautions to be taken, to parents and to communities in coordination with local councils.
In addition to the RE component, White Hats proposes that the teams begin gathering data on contamination and causalities with the use of Kobo. The information collected will be recorded into a database which will be
designed using White Hats experience of survivor interviews and ERW mapping, and
white hats experience within Syria of data collection and remote monitoring; through it
information will be made available to share with other humanitarian actors. Data
collected will then be used as a tool to plan ERW clearance in the future.
M&E department at WHOSD is headed by a qualified M&E Officer, based in Erbil, who remotely leads a team of field staff that are hired locally from the area of intervention. Currently, WHOSD also dispose of a M&E field team staff based in Ar Raqqa working under other projects. This project will hire a dedicated M&E staff member who will monitor the project at the output and activity level. Where necessary, interviews or gender segregated FGDs will be implemented.
The monitoring plan is structured on pre- and post-intervention monitoring, as well as activity-monitoring, by conducting weekly and monthly visits by White Hats Field MEAL personnel.

4 - Needs

3.6 million girls and boys are estimated to be living in areas affected by abandoned explosive ordinance and unexploded ordnance (UXO) in Syria Presence of explosive hazards, including landmines, explosive remnants of war, and improvised explosive devices, continue to threaten the lives of girls and boys and families, livelihoods of affected communities and endanger humanitarian actors. According to the 2021 HNO assessed communities reported the presence of explosive hazards and assessed communities reported known cases of injury or death as a result of explosive hazard contamination. Explosive hazards are often buried, even when above the ground they can be hard to recognize posing a significant danger to civilians. Key infrastructure such as schools, roads, housing, and water/sanitation systems have been contaminated as a result of conflict Secretary General report on Children and Armed Conflict verified grave violations against children , which included incidences of killing and maiming. Out of these incidences, injuries resulted mostly from IED, victim activated improvised explosive devices and UXOs. 2021 HNO data found that children are exposed to threats while playing and by mistaking explosive weapons for toys Heavy use of explosive weapons and high level of contamination in some inhabited areas, or areas to which children and their families want to return, can prevent girls and boys from accessing basic services such as healthcare and education. Data described movement restrictions they have placed on their children to limit their risk of being killed or injured by unexploded bombs and landmines. Coping strategy limits children’s other opportunities for education and socialization with others.

5 - Implementation of activities

Deliver ERW-RE sessions to groups of approximately 20 children, and provide training inERW-RE to teachers to promote ongoing ERW- RE. Risk Education Risk education (RE) isan essential, lifesaving component of the humanitarian response in Syria; it is the firststep to preventing civilian casualties from explosive hazards. Raising awareness helpsmitigate the threat by providing people with the necessary knowledge to stay out ofharm’s way. Tailored RE messaging (age, gender, and localized threats) ensurescommunities are aware of the risks from explosive hazards and are encouraged tobehave in ways that reduce the risk to people, property and the environment. White HatsResponse will undertake delivery of audience specific RE for vulnerable groups identifiedas being most at risk, such as IDPs and settled communities affected by the conflict, aswell as those exposed to explosive hazards on a regular basis, such as children, farmers,rubble removers, and humanitarian actors. The messages will also empower both womenand men to make safer decisions. Explosive incidents expose 8.2 million men, women,and children to severe, debilitating psychological trauma, physical injury, and death on adaily basis. In addition to socio economic impact and the erosion of security, explosiveincidents have a psychologically detrimental effect on survivors and their families,increasing hopelessness, fear, and fatigue. Victim assistance is currently underfundedand an identified need in Syria. White Hats Response will implement victim assistanceinitiatives, provision psychosocial support and case management for most acute needs.
