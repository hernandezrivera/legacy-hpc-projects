Project Summary

Through the project WFP will be providing unconditional food support to 250,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the North-west and south-west (NWSW) regions. In line with WFP Cameroon Country Strategic Plan (CSP 2018-2020 extended to 2021), life-saving assistance will be provided IDPs in NWSW. The transfer modality of food assistance to the beneficiaries will be in-kind food through unconditional monthly food distributions to support refugees’ households’ access to adequate and nutritious food, therefore prevent them from adopting negative coping practices. The full ration of the in-kind distributions will provide 2,100 kcal energy per day per person for the beneficiaries who entirely rely on humanitarian food assistance The daily food rations will comprise 350g cereals, 100g pulses, 35g vegetable, and 5g salt

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since November 2017, the socio-political crisis in these regions has progressively translated into insecurity and armed violence. The escalation of tension and upsurge in hostilities between non-state armed groups (NSAG) and government defense and security forces have triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, marked by signiﬁcant internal displacement. Assessments carried out by IOM reported that more than 542,158 people have been displaced in the North West and South West regions as of October 2019. The crisis continues to have a negative impact on livelihoods, trade, markets and access to land for the affected population. Farmers, small-scale ﬁshermen and their families ﬁnd themselves in an extremely vulnerable position due to the disruption of their activities (Cadre Harmonise, Dec 2019). IDPs have had to resort to negative food-related coping strategies, these include; reducing the number of meals (to one meal a day) and /or the quantity of food consumed by adults in order to feed the children. According to a recent Cadre Harmonise (October 2019) and Emergency Food Security Assessment conducted by WFP in October 2019, more than half of the households have inadequate food consumption in these two regions. Worrying trends include: • Up to 1.5 million people are food insecure of which 900,000 in North-West and 600,000 in South-West region (EFSA, January 2019); • Up to 57.5% in NW and 53.7% of households in SW have insuﬃcient food consumption (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2019); • Over 51.0% in NW and 70.2% in SW households apply potentially irreversible “crisis” or “emergency” coping strategies to cope with food insecurity and other basic needs (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2019); • Global acute malnutrition in SW and NW deteriorated to 7.4% and 5.1% respectively (Cadre Harmonise, EFSA, October 2019) compared to last results of January (EFSA 2019). The most vulnerable people in these regions highly need emergency humanitarian food assistance to save lives. The NWSW crisis that led to the displacement of thousands of people has create food insecurity as it is unsafe for people income generating activities such as farming and open shops or move for business. The food insecurity risks have impacted on those in desperate need for food thus leading to negative coping mechanisms. Assessments by humanitarian actors revealed that the high rate of displacement is due to to insecurity; armed clashes between NSAG and Government military forces. Also, there is high risks of enrolment in armed groups as a source of livelihood; increased kidnappings, killing, mutilations arbitrary arrest and disappearances. As far gender and protection is concerned, the assessments indicated that sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), has been on the rise among other protection risks. Rape is being used as a weapon of war; younger girls are forced to engage in survival sex, (including with armed groups) leading to unwanted pregnancies and sexual and reproductive health problems, which need to be treated but are left unattended owing to ﬁnancial constraints. There are families separated and relatives killed, schools and school children are threatened, and civilians living in a „climate of terror‟ (HRP 2019). Freedom of movement is greatly restricted and becomes riskier by the day, and people who have ﬂed to the forests or bushes live precariously. Persons living with disabilities are subjected to targeted abusive treatment, torture and killings by the conﬂicting parties in addition to the disproportionate impact of instability, sudden movements and violence places on them (Protection Cluster Strategy, August 2019). There is a distinct overlap between groups of people who are most vulnerable to food insecurity and those who face the greatest protection risks, and these include households headed by a child, by a single mother, by an elderly person, by a person with a disability (WFP EFSA).
