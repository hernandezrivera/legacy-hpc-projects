Project Summary

Provision of health care services was greatly affected by the crisis in north east and posed a huge burden on Women and children. Most of the health facilities in the rural areas were damaged and are non-functional. Women and children are the most affected in a crisis specifically those arriving from the inaccessible areas are extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed humanitarian services prior to their arrival at the reception sites. The leading causes of morbidity and mortality linked to malnutrition has been heavily linked with diseases outbreaks such as the acute watery diarrhoea and malaria. Female headed households as well as separated and unaccompanied children face an uphill task of accessing adequate services and are exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse as well as gender based violence. Communities that have settled in the host with limited restoration of livelihood as they cannot access their fields for cultivations and are not actively engaged with economic activities, their children face the risks of repeated episodes of acute malnutrition.
Nutrition intervention targets 4M women and children in BAY states, of which 3M are children below the age of 5 and 1M are women of reproductive age with infants below 2 years. Majority of those in need are in the host communities 2.6M while approximately 0.6M are IDPS and 0.5M returnees while 0.2M still not accessed by humanitarian workers. The prevalence of acute malnutrition in the north east is further heightened by inadequate health services coverage, poor child care practices, inadequate hygiene and sanitation conditions and food insecurity.
Population arriving from the inaccessible areas have particularity high prevalence of acute malnutrition, a SMART survey conducted in Bama in August 2018 indicated a very high prevalence of GAM among the new arrival compared to those that have been in the camp GAM by WHZ – 37.5% (reception site) while 7.8% (rest of camp) . Similar situation is observed in all the 9 reception sites in Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Kalabarge, Kukawa, Mobbar and Ngala, through the screening of new arrivals. Population in host communities continue to grapple with serious nutrition situation, routine monitoring of the prevalence of acute malnutrition though the nutrition and food security surveillance Findings indicate that Borno State has a GAM rate of 9.1 per cent and a SAM rate of 0.8 percent; Adamawa State has a GAM rate of 8.2 per cent and a SAM rate of 0.4 per cent, Yobe State has a GAM rate of 12.1 per cent and a SAM rate of 2.1 per cent. North Adamawa has reported increase in GAM prevalence from 9.2 per cent reported in November to 10.1 per cent in April 2018. Yobe reported improved GAM rate in South and Northern parts while central Borno reported increased GAM from 11.6 per cent in Nov 2017 to 14.1 per cent in April (NBS, UNICEF Nutrition and Food security surveillance round 5 April 2018) .The health system is still contending with extensive damage and inadequate human resources as well as insufficient medical supplies to cater for the needs of the displaced population as well as those in the host communities to date the health system functionality is below 50%
Population movement within the northeast has been a key phenomenal with the majority arriving from the inaccessible areas has increased the burden on the existing nutrition services as this movements are associated with extreme needs as those IDPS arriving from these areas have not accessed humanitarian services. Movement have also been realized for those intending to move back to their LGA of origin to explore livelihood activities have also been reported. This movements have been sporadic and have continued to put pressure on existing humanitarian services mostly affected including the nutrition sector as services providing SAM with medical complication have been inadequate.
Acute malnutrition is often an outcome of poor food security, Health and poor child feeding and caring practices as well as other humanitarian services with chronic malnutrition thus the importance of involving multiple sectors in addressing malnutrition. The causes of malnutrition are complex and interconnected hence requiring intersectoral collaboration and integration with health agriculture social protection among others. An IRC Semi-Quantitative Evaluation of Access and Coverage survey (SQUEC) survey conducted by IRC between 5th to 23rd of March 2018 in 10 communities in MMC and Jere estimated single coverage at 47.7 percent of existing needs (95 percent confidence interval between 38.7 and 56.9 percent coverage). This coverage is below the 70 percent Sphere minimum standard for urban settings
IRC has been present in North east as health and Nutrition actor since 2012 and an active member and co-lead in both sectors in the 3 north east states, As for nutrition programming IRC is currently supporting 22 OTP clinics and 1 stabilization centre in Adamawa state,22 mobile clinics in Borno state comprising of :12 mobile clinics in MMC and Jere,4 mobile clinics in Konduga, 4mobile clinics in Monguno,10 health facility OTP in Askira Uba and also run the largest in-patient stabilization centres in Borno state(64 bed stabilization centres in MMC and Jere,10 bed capacity in Askira Uba. In addition IRC also Run 22 bed capacity stabilization centre in Gujba LGA and 12 OTP clinics in Yobe state. Each of the facilities are run by dedicated MOH and IRC staffs. IRC has expert medical Doctor who oversees all the SC’s and train SC staffs on clinical care for SAM children. The IRC expert Doctor also conducts and support WHO/UNICEF and other partners in training of staffs.In addition IRC has technical health and nutrition advisers who oversee the Quality of the CMAM and health programs. Nutrition Technical adviser visit the country after every 6 months and conduct Quality assessment and reviews the performance of the CMAM and IYCF activities

Activities

Establish and Maintain 5 stabilization centre (2 in MMC and Jere, 1 in Askira Uba, 1 in Gujba and 1 in Mubi and Increase bed capacity from 10 to 20 bed Stabilization Center at Uba General Hospital. The IRC is currently running 5 (24 hour stabilization centres) in the BAY states (3 in Borno.1 in Adamawa and 1 in Gujba with a total bed capacity of 104-Uba General Hospital is currently operating 10 bed capacity stabilization centre supported by GAC funds. The IRC will therefore increase the center’s capacity to admit more children and main the other 4 stabilization centres. The current SC’s have a bed occupancy above 120% and most of the time eligible patients are turn away due to lack of space. With NHS funding IRC will continue to provide 24 hour critical care services in the 5 SC’s currently managed. Additional Qualified health personnel will be recruited to support the MOH staffs operate the center 24 hours a day, providing patients clinical management and monitoring of feedings 3 hourly. Following a thorough needs assessment of the site, the IRC will supply the stabilization center with the required supplies and medical consumables, including basic furniture, equipment and anthropometric tools (scales, measuring board, and MUAC tapes), as well as medicine for the systematic treatment of malnourished children. The nutritional products (therapeutic milk and RUTF) will be supplied by UNICEF as part of the IRC’s Program Cooperation Agreement (PCA) and IRC will ensure delivery to the stabilization center. In addition IRC will provide meals to caregivers looking after their child with SAM for the duration of admission in the SC. Children who have recovered from medical complications will be transferred to the OTP. Engaging community leaders and men in awareness raising in CMAM and e-IYCF in hard to reach areas and transit centres.Mothers rarely act alone in making decisions about IYCF. They are guided by influential family members and perceptions of social norms, among other factors. Mobilizing community leaders’ support can help to create a supportive environment for improved practices in the communities. Community leaders are custodians of culture in their domains. They maintain law and order in their communities and also support members of the communities in their day to day activities.Community leaders. Lessons from project IRC is currently implementing in MMC on engaging on community leader’s participation show positive result in reducing barriers to practicing optimal IYCF and accessing services at the mobile clinics. In communities where the top leader (Lawan) and key leader (religious leader, women leader, youth leader and Traditional Birth Attendant representative, and CV leader) were informed and about IYCF CMAM practices an increase of referral to OTP was noticed For this grant, the IRC plans to extend its support to community leaders with inclusion of the leaders of sub communities (Bulamas) as a focal point in sub communities to ensure coordination between all community leaders in creating more awareness in IYCF/CMAM. A total of 5 Bulamas from each of the 10 LGA supported will be trained at the beginning of the project by IRC E-IYCF officer. They will then be in charge of organising mobilisation through community dialogue sessions with men from their communities to encourage referral to nutrition services and improvement of IYCF practices at home. Regular monthly meeting organised by Lawan with all key actors including as well Bulamas, will be maintained to encourage participation, sharing experience and solution findings. The meeting will also be a forum to receive feedback from the community leaders and stakeholders on the CMAM and IYCF programme and recommendations from them on how to improve the programmes. Protection action groups members and WPE case workers will also be involved to mainstream protection into the nutrition activities.
