Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring vulnerable children, especially girls, live in resilient communities and have free and safe access to life-saving services during and after natural disasters and conflict crisis, this will be achieved through provision of Child Protection and gender based violence humanitarian response services and activities targeting 400,000 people (94,080 men, 101,920 women, 97,920 boys and 106,080 girls) including IDPs, returnees &host communities in Khartoum,South Kordofan, White Nile, Aj Jazirah, Kassala and North Kordofan.
Awareness will be provided to girls and boys on child rights. Children with special needs will be prioritized. Children and parents will be oriented on how to access information on child protection referrals, provision of inclusive complaints and response facilities. Child Protection will be incorporated across all sector interventions to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity. The approach will be based on Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS) including avoiding causing harm by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects. Child Friendly spaces will be provided 2 in each of the 3 targeted localities. Some 2000 (1000 boys and 1000 girls) adolescent boys and girls will be trained on life skills and supported to form social clubs.
Gender based violence interventions will provide among others provision of mental health Provision of mental health and Psychosocial support services including provision of Psychological first aid, clinical counselling and recreational, social and life skills interventions for GBV survivors targeting 2000 SGBV survivors. Dignity kits will also be provided to 2000 GBV survivors in addition to cash/voucher assistance will also be provided targeting 2000 SGBV survivors among other services.

1 - Needs

Plan International with its vast experience and knowledge of working in Child Protection and sexual gender-based violence responses will seek to address the gaps and needs identified in various assessed needs that have been reported by a number of research papers. The current war crisis in Sudan has exposed Children, women and other vulnerable groups to various forms of abuse and harm that include among others family separation due to inter-communal conflicts and wars, recruitment into armed groups especially boys, forced child marriages particularly girls due to negative cultural practices and the current bad economic situation, child labor, human trafficking, child neglect, denied access to birth records and more other such child rights violations and rape. The Sudan HNO 2022 reports that, up to 60 per cent of the population in need of protection services in Sudan are children who continue to be exposed to protection threats of recruitment, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence. The preliminary report v6 Sudan HNO 2023 established that in 2023, 361,000 children are in extreme need (10 per cent), about 2,061,920 in severe need (58 per cent), and about 939,650 are in stress need (27 per cent) and require humanitarian child protection assistance. Children with disabilities are among the most vulnerable. The paper also established that, about 25 per cent of children aged 5-17 years are involved in child labour, while the ratio of those working in hazardous conditions is higher among the 15-17 years age group (28.5 per cent), with clear differentials in the population of children working under hazardous conditions who live in urban areas (8.2 per cent) compared to those living in rural areas (21.8 per cent). The Child protection needs are higher in Rural areas that include in the Darfur regions, South Kordofan, Blue Nile and North Kordofan. Gender based violence equally experiences huge gaps and needs in Sudan. The Voices from Sudan 2021 carried out a nationwide GBV assessment and established that Sexual violence is more pervasive in rural and conflict-affected communities including refugee camps. Intimate Partner Violence is not considered a crime in Sudan, and laws such as the Personal Status Law (1991) mean that incidents that occur in the home are viewed as private issues, thus women tend not to seek legal redress. The Sudan HNO 2023 preliminary findings indicate that Access to services remain particularly weak in conflict-affected and refugee hosting areas of South Darfur, North Darfur, West Kordofan, White Nile, Blue Nile, as well as states in eastern Sudan. Women in the said states and environment are subjected to SGBV.Among girls aged 20-24 years, 60.2 per cent were first married or in a union before the age of 18, and 87 per cent of women aged 15–49 years have been subjected to FGM. The project therefore seeks to address these needs through the Child Protection and SGBV activities in South Kordofan during the period January through to December 2023 period. The assessments in the current war in Sudan have also revealed that there are gross sexual gender based violence according to the Statement by the Humanitarian Coordinator in Sudan Abdou Dieng.
