Project Summary

The project aims to support vulnerable mobile populations, including internally displaced persons (IDPs) and migrants as well as their host communities to address food insecurity and enhance socioeconomic stability through improving access to livelihood opportunities. This will be achieved through support to agricultural and livestock-related income generating activities for approximately 33,800 individuals (7,936 men, 8,950 women, 8, 224 boys and 8,690 girls) and at least 15 per cent of the target group will be persons with disabilities. The target population will also consist of IDPs, vulnerable migrants, and host community members, all targeted based on their needs. IOM will apply a people-centered approach with community-based planning to improve conditions in target areas and ensure that interventions are based on needs and the rights of the vulnerable migrants and communities to access food and improved livelihood opportunities. The project will focus on communities across Blue Nile, Aj Jazirah, White Nile, River Nile, Northern State, Gedaref, Red Sea, Kassala, South Kordofan, and West Kordofan; when possible, IOM will also seek to provide assistance to vulnerable conflict-affected communities in Khartoum and the Darfur states, if security conditions allow access to target areas. IDPs, returnees, migrants, and local communities in these areas were already vulnerable to food insecurity and limited livelihood opportunities prior to the ongoing military conflict that began in April 2023 between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF), due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high staple food prices. Now, in light of recent events, existing vulnerabilities have drastically increased, and many people have found themselves newly or secondarily displaced, lacking liquidity and without livelihood opportunities. According to HRP 2024, the selected localities have been classified as Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) phase 3 'Crisis' and/or phase 4 'Emergency' further highlighting the need for timely and adequate livelihood interventions to address food insecurity, protect lives, and move towards economic self-sufficiency. The project is in line with HRP 2024 FSL Cluster Objectives to support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income opportunities to save and sustain lives. The planned interventions will include provision of provision of in-kind or cash assistance for agricultural support including seeds to vulnerable farmers or other individuals for subsistence; provision of small livestock and veterinary services; and inputs and small business support tailored to IDPs, youth, and women in agriculture and in income generating skills such as food processing, brick making, welding activities. IOM has previously implemented activities in the target localities through ongoing projects, and seeks to continue and expand implementing life-sustaining and early recovery initiatives in 2024. The planned interventions will be delivered in a way to complement past and ongoing activities and targeting will be based on needs as well as conflict sensitivity assessments and analysis including ensuring participation of underrepresented groups and supporting both newly displaced persons and host communities to reduce potential tension and conflict over access to services.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IDPs and returnees in vulnerable situations including youth and women and disabled persons will be prioritized to ensure their protection and adequate access to basic services and livelihoods opportunities. All members of the community including people with additional vulnerabilities, men and women will be separately consulted and encouraged to be fully involved in consultations, activities, and post activity processes to address their specific needs and encourage open discussions. The project will empower these groups by involving them in the decision-making processes through a rights-based, people-centered approach using community-based planning during project design and implementation, to ensure that the activities respond to the actual needs and market opportunities for target beneficiaries and promote community ownership. A broad range of mobility issues will be discussed during the community-based planning process, ranging from protection issues faced by IDPs, returnees, women, and youth and the various drivers of their displacement, additional vulnerabilities, and economic disparities to ensure that needs and gaps are addressed and challenges of different segments of the target population are considered. IOM will adhere to its gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations (AAP) guidelines, as they are aligned with the “do no harm” principle and IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises. The needs of individuals in extremely vulnerable situations will be particularly reflected in the programme design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, IOM will ensure the following elements are considered: 1) safety, security, dignity; 2) equitable access; 3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected throughout the implementation period. Staff and implementing partners will be trained on protection topics including how to treat GBV disclosures ethically and appropriately, as well as using the referral pathways to provide essential support to community members to access these pathways. IOM will work with staff and suppliers to strengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness raising and in line with IOM PSEA guidelines. Another group that will be prioritized is vulnerable members of the host community in dire need of food security and livelihoods. This will include identifying locations for interventions according to conflict analysis, feedback from community committees on activities, and identifying appropriate risk mitigation measures that ensure conflict sensitivity and adherence to “do no harm” including providing equitable access to assistance to avoid increases in tensions or inter-communal conflicts due to influx of IDPs and returnees in host community areas, increasing the needs, leading to a reduction of resources, and perception by the community that they are not receiving comparable support.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will be guided by key principles ensuring accountability to affected populations (AAP), including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase immediate assessment of critical humanitarian needs of affected migrants including IDPs and returnees to the implementation of the interventions. The proposed project will be delivered with a people-centred and participatory approach following IOM’s Community-Based Planning tool guidance to ensure that communities themselves are enablers and drivers of their own recovery. AAP will also be promoted through capacity building activities for relevant stakeholders including local authorities and community leaders such as leaders from women and youth groups, etc. As part of capacity building, the project will establish or reactivate community management committees with the key responsibility to coordinate and monitor overall project implementation as well as obtain feedback from beneficiaries. IOM will support these committees to establish a complaints and feedback mechanism that is diverse and contextually appropriate as possible to enable people to provide feedback on programmes and submit complaints, that activate appropriate processes and procedures to provide timely response including: 1) Establishing an appropriate, safe, and accessible process. 2) Clear referral pathways with designated tasks and responsibilities for complaints or feedback. 3) Ensure protection of all personal data handled through the mechanisms, in terms of consent and confidentiality in line with IOM data protection principles. 4)Establish timelines for response by community, partners, IOM, or other agencies as needed. 5) Evaluate effectiveness, lessons learnt, and best practices of the response.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IOM is also part of inter-agency coordination approaches and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms as agreed by the inter-agency groups. Women’s representation and participation will be strongly encouraged within the committees to address their general lack of access to decision-making processes and to ensure that female complainants receive adequate support. All instances of harassment, sexual exploitation, and abuse reported by beneficiaries, committees, and other relevant stakeholders will be referred to IOM’s PSEA focal point. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy towards SEA and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and a reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA cases. IOM will ensure to inform its beneficiaries of the available avenues of feedback and complaint mechanisms including PSEA reporting. If needed, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling, and medical assistance to survivors, and this will be done in coordination with other qualified protection partners and service providers.
