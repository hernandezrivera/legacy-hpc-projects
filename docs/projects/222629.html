Project Summary

Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI), proposes actions that will target 24,000 beneficiaries (4000 men, 4000 women, 8000 girls, 8000 boys), who have been grappling with flooding (which has affected 1.4 million people in 43 counties) and cyclical waves of cattle raiding, inter/ intra communal skirmishes and subsequent multiple displacements, compounded by multiple needs, unprecedented food insecurity levels, high malnutrition levels, protection concerns including gender based violence and abuse, inadequate health and water, sanitation and hygiene and public health with recent cholera outbreak further impacting health systems (IPC November 2024; OCHA Flood Updates 2024; Ministry of Health Sitreps 2024).
Further knock-on effects of this insecurity often lead to protection risks, such as an increase in GBV cases, in relation to WASH needs in addition to vulnerability access constraints among people with disabilities. This is likely due to declining food security and access to WASH services as majority of women and girls are reported to be collecting water far away from their homes. This contributes to higher rates of malnutrition, increased WASH related diseases leading to high public health diseases prevalence, increased infant morbidity and mortality rates, and MAM and SAM incidents linked to high malnutrition the targeted counties (IPC November 2024; Protection trends
2024).
Proposed actions will integrate the WASH response with nutrition and health. With an aim of reducing the burden of WASH-related diseases as aggravating factors for malnutrition in locations presenting an extreme WASH vulnerability and high prevalence of malnutrition with key actions aimed at breaking the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhea), thus improving food intake of malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition objectives. This will be accompanied by actions that address live-saving needs in disaster contexts in restoring immediately access to basic WASH services either temporarily or permanently while mainstreaming protection and mitigating WASH Gender Based Violence and finally support resilience building in locations that were affected by flooding in the past years through community engagement in finding solutions and maintenance of facilities.
SPOCI recently accomplished various emergency WASH actions in the proposed states/ counties and will scale up activities that will support SAM children/caretakers and PLW who will be provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion, support to nutrition facilities with improved access to sanitation services and improved access to safe water, support water treatment and good hygiene practices, support access to sanitation facilities and support women and girls with improved access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit in addition to rehabilitation/ construction of water points and latrines with flood-resilient designs in flood prone areas.

1. Needs

Flooding extremes across 43 counties have affected at least, 1.4 million people with over 379,000 displaced. This is coupled with a surging cholera outbreak, mainly impacting flood affected zones including people displaced by Sudan crisis, further complicated by unprecedented food insecurity and malnutrition levels where at least 6.3 million people are facing crisis level or worse food insecurity (IPC November 2024). Despite progress in the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement, which has been followed by the ongoing armed forces re-unification; there have been an increase in numbers of returnees which have been compounded by additional influx due to the Sudan crisis (IOM DTM Updates, 2024) in addition to continued waves of subnational violence in Pibor, Akobo, Nasir among other counties. Flooding extremes impacts in Akobo, Nasir and Pibor, with increasing water levels of Pibor river and its tributaries have persisted to date compounded with consequent destruction and submerging of WASH infrastructure and a surge in WASH related diseases including cholera (MOH Updates, 2024). This is in addition to protection concerns which include destruction and looting of property, bureaucratic impediments, operational interference and access constraints and increased cases of GBV along major roads; including mines presence and increased cases of ambushes and attacks on humanitarian convoys and other commercial vehicles, further impacting delivery of critical supplies/ aid (MAG Updates, 2024; NGO Forum and REACH Updates 2024). Furthermore, flooding and insecurity has limited humanitarian access, and therefore, more isolated areas have received significantly less services, including WASH services, and their resilience and ability to sustain coping mechanisms. Such reduced ability to cope has led to famine-like conditions in counties like flood prone counties like Pibor and Nasir which put heavy strain on water, nutrition and health facilities. These water resources are already limited due to being damaged in the floods, therefore repairing boreholes will aid in lessening potential strains on water resources and the effects of famine-like conditions (UNICEF Country Reports, 2024). These contribute negatively to the wellbeing/health and subsequent reduced livelihoods and incomes at household level. This creates a cyclical effect, and furthering the need for scaling up WASH activities to address unprecedented food insecurity levels. directly attributed to poor hygiene practices, lack of access to safe water and the rampant open defecation cases witnessed in Pibor, Akobo and Nasir; almost 70% of population in these counties have no access to WASH services (South Sudan Flooding Situation Reports, 2024). Further knock on effects of this insecurity often lead to protection risks, such as an increase in GBV cases, in relation to WASH needs in addition to vulnerability access constraints among people with disabilities.

2. Project activities

• Conduct safety audits, sensitization and mobilization of beneficiaries and key stakeholders including mapping of OTP/CS/ nutrition/ Centres in consultation with nutrition partners • Provide targeted children admitted for SAM treatment with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion. • Map, assess dysnfunctional water points and rehabilitate water points at key strategic locations neighboring OTP/CS/ nutrition Centres• Mobilize and train hygiene promoters/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on trained on core WASH and Nutrition competencies and WASH minimum package of activities • Map, assess and rehabilitate latrine facilities including provision of lockable doors/ locks insecure locations agreed after a GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staff • Procure and support target beneficiaries with household water treatment kits • Support water point and latrine rehabilitationtargeting women and girls to improve their access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit • Carry out rehabilitation /construction of water points with a flood-resilient design in flood zones • Carry out rehabilitation/ construction of latrines stances with aflood-resilient design in flood zones
