Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address the critical needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations living in sites, settlements, and surrounding areas in Syria of Northern Syria, through camp and coordination support. Specifically, ACTED will implement mobile CCCM responses to coordinate service provision and build local capacity for self-management. ACTED’s proposed assistance, through its CCCM mobile teams, will include key information management activities such as service mapping, risks and demographic assessments, running of coordination structures through regular engagement and advocacy and the strengthening of self-management structures through trainings. ACTED will focus on the reinforcement of accountability systems (e.g. CRMs). In addition to providing CCCM core activities, ACTED CCCM mobile team will conduct rapid needs assessments throughout Northern Syria which will primarily use to inform the programmatic decision to deploy. In NES, ACTED will also continue to provide static CCCM support through maintaining the Camp Management function in Areesheh; Serekaniye; Washokani camps (Al-Hasakeh governorate) and Abu Khashab Camp (Deir-ez-Zor governorate), as well as mobile responses to sites and settlements across NES. In NWS, ACTED will target camps located in the Azaz district (Northern Aleppo governorate) through shelter and settlement assistance.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 HNO, nearly 125,000 individuals are residing in camps in NES. The Sites and Settlements Working Group (SSWG) for NES reported 154 collective centers and 192 informal settlement hosting a total of 24,476 households as of February 2021. 39% of IDPs are living in informal settlements, 33% of IDPs are living in planned camps, 16% in informal camps and 12% in collective centers. Due to lack of access to income, camp residents are highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs, particularly with regards to non-food items (NFI) and winterization, as limited purchasing powers means households must prioritize food and water at the expenses of NFI and shelter. In Northwest Syria, IDPs in camp settings in NWS to endured an exceptionally harsh 2020-21 winter, in the midst of a worsening Covid-19 outbreak, with inadequate tents, a lack of heating, warm bedding and winter clothes for children. According to a WFP 2021 Report, the fuel shortage crisis worsened in early 2021, thereby limiting Syria’s access to fuel and reducing fuel supply in markets, amidst a major currency crisis. This resulted in negative coping strategies, such as reliance on burning unsafe materials for heating. Recurrent heavy rain in January 2021 resulted in widespread flooding throughout NWS, affecting an estimated 122,953 IDPs. More than 300 camps and sites across Idleb and Aleppo were affected by the floods with 8,400 shelters destroyed and another 13,800 damaged.

5 - Implementation of activities

Through this intervention, ACTED will focus on offering comprehensive responses in new and protracted underserved displacement sites as well as surrounding areas. ACTED will implement mobile CCCM responses that advocate for and coordinate service provision as well as build local capacity for self-management. Relying on CCCM mobile teams, ACTED’s proposed assistance will include key information management activities such as service mapping, risks and demographic assessments, running of coordination structures through regular engagement and advocacy and the strengthening of self-management structures through trainings. ACTED will focus on the reinforcement of accountability systems (e.g. CRMs). In addition to providing CCCM core activities, ACTED will also ensure gap filling emergency services such as key WASH repairs and emergency sanitation. The CCCM mobile team has in-built capacity to conduct rapid needs assessments throughout Northern Syria which will primarily inform the decision to deploy. In NES, ACTED will also continue to provide static CCCM support through maintaining the Camp Management function in Areesheh, Washokani and Serekaniye camps (Al-Hasakeh governorate), and Abu Khashab camp (Deir-ez-Zor governorate) as well as mobile responses to sites and settlements across NES. In NWS, ACTED will target 17 formal camps located in the Azaz district (Northern Aleppo governorate). In each of these sites, the implementation of key essential services falls under the responsibility of the one camp manager who is the focal point for all in-camp coordination, as well as the locally recruited Camp Management Unit (CMU) in place.

During the community sensitisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents. Information on the CRM will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents, especially those who may be directly impacted by the road rehabilitation. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.
