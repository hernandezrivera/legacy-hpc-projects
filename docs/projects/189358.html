Project Summary

According the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2022, and the Health Cluster Response Plan for 2022, 5.47 million South Sudanese are in need of lifesaving healthcare services, with 1.01 among them being internally displaced, and 750,000 returnees. The government is only able to meet 41.9% of the healthcare budget. Insecurity, long distances, limited healthcare supplies, inaccessibility of healthcare facilities and inadequate healthcare workers are some of the barriers to accessing healthcare. Flooding in large areas of Unity and Jonglei states has further compounded the healthcare needs of huge populations.
Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 789 in every 100,000 live births ending in maternal death. Only 39% of pregnant women avail themselves for the mandatory four antenatal visits. Institutional delivery is a paltry 12.3%, while skilled birth attendants only assist 14.7% of deliveries. Teenage pregnancy in the country remains high, with 30% of deliveries occurring among girls aged 15-19 or younger.
Only 8% of healthcare facilities are able to offer the full package of the basic package of health and nutrition services. The rates of immunization are extremely low, malaria cases remain high, while acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea are endemic in multiple counties. Thousands of hepatitis cases have been reported due to a dire WASH situation, while infectious diseases such as COVID-19 continue spreading, with significant portions of the population depending on traditional healers as a coping mechanism.
The proposed project shall extend primary health care and reproductive health services to vulnerable persons in former IDPs, returnees and host population in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Pochalla. This project is in line with the health cluster objectives and 2022 Humanitarian response plan which seek to:
1. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support and GBV health services to crisis affected populations
2. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases
3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
The project will target 30,000 beneficiaries and support them with lifesaving emergency health care and reproductive healthcare services through the 6 Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs) that CH has been supporting in the targeted counties. CH will build the capacity of the community health workers, County Health Directorate, Bomas health initiative volunteers to report outbreaks timely and provide life-saving emergency, carry out vaccination campaigns such as EPI, and provide case management, rapid response and risk communication in the outreach locations earmarked for the project activities in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Pochalla.
Special consideration will be given to people with disability, women and girls, to ensure inclusion. CH will ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. women girls people with disability and the elderly will be consulted during project design, implementation and monitoring in order for their needs to be captured. Project monitoring will be separated by gender and by disability. this project will target 5,000 Men, 5,000 women, 10,000 boys, 10,000 girls and 15% people with disability

2 - Project activities

CH will offer complementary services in the target locations through:1. Building the capacity of community health workers to report outbreaks timely and provide life-saving emergency services2. Carrying out vaccination campaigns such as EPI3. Providing case management for endemic diseases such as malaria, acute watery and acute bloody diarrhea4. Carrying out rapid response and risk communication in the outreach locations earmarked for the project activities in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Pochalla.5. Providing antenatal care (ANC) services at the health facilities6. Providing children with nutritional services by conducting screening, deworming and referrals7. Conducting community-based outreach services in the PHCU’s and Mobile Clinic’s8. Creating awareness on key health messages through trained community Health Workers9. Providing Integrated disease surveillance response service (IDSR)10. Promoting family planning through distribution of pills and condoms11. Establishing village health committees to support the health facilities and staff in outreach activities12. Strengthening Health information system (HIS) at implementation sites13. Providing weekly continuous medical education at select health facilities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from community will inform the Health risks, most needy Payams, Bomas, and this will inform prioritization at community level. Feedback on needs beyond this project will be referred to other partners in the county who have capacity to handle cases beyond this project. Further feedback will inform change in strategy, and future proposals.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

There is a risk of external stakeholder interference, Coalition for Humanity will conduct a stakeholder engagement meeting at the start of the project to define objectives of the project, their role and responsibility. There is a risk of minority, people with disability, women, girls and boys not participating effectively in the project. Coalition for Humanity will conduct focus group discussions targeting people with disability, boys, girls, women separately to make sure their needs and concerns are captured. Coalition for Humanity has a PSEA code of conduct, all field staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, community leaders are also trained on PSEA to raise awareness in the community so that beneficiaries can know they have a right to humanitarian aid without their dignity being compromised.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for humanity has a PSEA code of conduct signed by all project staff including support staffs, all those who shall be directly involved with the project implementation including humanitarian staff, community leaders, health workers shall be taken through 1 day training on PSEA and the community members sensitized on their right to access the humanitarian assistance without being exploited.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Coalition for Humanity shall engage with the communities through baseline survey, focus group discussions, community consultations before and during the development of this Project. Moving forward, Coalition for Humanity will continue engaging the communities through risks assessment, Health related risk discussions, community engagement meetings/Consultations (CEC)involving the affected community before and during the project implementation. The community needs will be verified through focus group discussions and Key Informant Interviews with leaders. A project inception meeting will be held with local authorities involving women, men, boys and girls to ensure participation and feedback. Village Health Committees shall be setup. Coalition for Humanity will adapt participatory monitoring and evaluation.Coalition for Humanity will set up complaint desk/box, and an AAP committee, the committee will receive feedback from the community and forward to CH team. CH has a complain email for literate people to to send email in case they have something to report. CH will also provide mobile numbers for beneficiaries to call
