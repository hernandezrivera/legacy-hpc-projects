Needs

With refugees living in precarious settlements highly vulnerable to flooding and landslide, and both refugee and host community populations living in areas highly exposed to cyclones, the need to mitigate the risk of natural hazards, in close coordination with the Humanitarian Community, has become critical in the response to the humanitarian crisis. Most refugees live in makeshift shelters in camps highly vulnerable to floods and landslides, while host communities along the coast of the Bay of Bengal are exposed to cyclone winds and tidal surge. Further, the combined pressure stemming from the magnitude of the population displacement and exposure to natural hazards is also likely to put a significant strain on the ability of local authorities' ability to manage rapid and complex information flows in preperation for or in the event of a disaster. In order to mitigate these risks, the humanitarian community and authorities need accurate, reliable and rapid information to inform effective disaster risk reduction and emergency response programming. There is also a critical information gap on the needs of the mixed populations of vulnerable host communities living outside of camps but directly or indirectly affected by the presence of refugees. To date, the majority of data collection and information management among the humanitarian community has focused overwhelmingly on the needs of the camp-based refugee population. This means that limited information exists on the needs and vulnerabilities of population outside of camps. Further, little data exists on the varying impacts of the current refugee crisis on host communities, as well as on the dynamics and interactions between the two communities. These information gaps are hindering the identification of priority areas for intervention, as well as the adoption of appropriate modalities or entry points for host/mixed community interventions. As the crisis becomes more prolonged, there is an increasing likelihood of cyclones or other large-scale natural hazards that will affect both refugee and host community populations. In addition, it can be expected that the humanitarian community will begin to place increased emphasis on host community programming from a conflict sensitivity perspective. It is thus vital that information gaps concerning these vulnerable but under-served populations are closed as quickly as possible.
