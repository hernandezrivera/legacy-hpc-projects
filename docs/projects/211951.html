Project Summary

Over May 2023 - January 2024, INTERSOS conducted Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments and health-specific analyses in multiple sub-districts in Hama, Idleb, and Rural Damascus. While a general lack of functioning health facilities is noted, challenges persist even within functioning HFs (e.g. shortages of medical staff, medications, and consumables/equipment). The worsening socio-economic situation has further impacted individual health conditions, especially in rural communities due to financial inability to afford transportation to the closest HF and pay user fees. Specific reported barriers to accessing SRH care were the lack of female medical staff alongside the lack of local health services and financial constraints preventing transportation to distant facilities. The main medical concerns reported are acute respiratory infections, hypertension, diarrheal diseases, leishmaniasis, trauma/wounds, intestinal inflammation, hepatitis and reproductive health problems. In 2024-2025, INTERSOS will continue integrating its health programme with protection (HSYR24-PRO-212371-1), education (HSYR24-EDU-212411-1), and livelihoods (HSYR24-ERY-212363-1) to ensure a holistic response to the increasingly complex needs of local populations. Targeting Hama, Idleb and RD - possibly expanding to Deir-ez-Zor, INTERSOS will maintain its primary focus on providing primary healthcare via mobile approaches, complementing this with community-based health awareness and capacity building for health workers. Particular attention is paid to awareness raising on SRH, using Menstrual Hygiene Management (with distribution of MHM kits) as entry point. Upon signature of the sub-MoU with MoH, INTERSOS will also work on defining operational modalities to support static HFs with rehabilitation works (including solar-powered systems) and provision of medical supplies and equipment. Discrimination in service provision (Likelihood: L; Impact: H) will be mitigated through regular field monitoring visits, satisfaction surveys, and CFRM in place. PwD’s exclusion (L: M; I: H) will be prevented by building capacities of INTERSOS and SARC staff/volunteers on disability inclusion and safe identification and referral, alongside home visits by CHVs to follow up on those who cannot reach the clinics. Tensions between population groups due to perceived unequal assistance (L: L; I: H) will be mitigated through conflict sensitivity analyses and community consultations. INTERSOS has global PSEA/CP policies applying to all personnel and suppliers. A country-specific strategy ensures SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP declaration and attend PSEA/CP induction/refreshers (PSEA/CP trainings are also provided to partners). In-country PSEA Referent and 6 PSEA FPs are appointed to receive reports about alleged SEA acts and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Reports can be made in person, to a dedicated email address, by phone or through INTERSOS CFM boxes. Complaints are then referred to the PSEA Referent and investigated. Programme risks are mitigated through robust monitoring and reporting mechanisms, capacity building for staff and partners, and clear engagement protocols with SARC/line ministries. Safety and access risks are mitigated via security assessments, capacity building on relevant protocols, and close coordination with key actors (OCHA, SARC, line ministries, other partners, community networks). Close supervision of the compliance with internal SOPs (including those on the purchase of pharmaceuticals and medical commodities), rigorous monitoring of financial expenditure and financial planning, frequent due diligence and pre-qualification processes, regular market research and procurement in stocks reduce financial and procurement risks. INTERSOS Contingency Planning Process details risks and mitigation measures for each target governorate.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Promoting integration between Health and Protection, Education and Livelihood, in 2024-2025, INTERSOS will continue providing essential primary healthcare services to vulnerable communities in Rural Damascus, Hama and Idleb - while possibly expanding to Deir-ez-Zor. INTERSOS will continue focusing on the provision of PHC through mobile approaches (in partnership with SARC) - targeting unserved rural and hard-to-reach areas, while expanding support to static HFs (under the sub-MoU with MoH). INTERSOS will thus support the provision of the minimum package of quality and free care based on national and international standard and comprehensive of: general consultation (CDs including IMCI and NCDs, trauma); sexual and reproductive health (ANC, PNC); referrals to guarantee the continuum of care; community-based health and hygiene promotion; provision of pharmaceuticals and medical commodities; health data management and support to the EWARS; technical capacity building for health workers (IPC, Cholera, IYCF, malnutrition surveillance, IMCI, RH, MhGAP, disability inclusion, health awareness - including on MHM) and volunteers (IPC, C-19 and Cholera awareness, RCCE); screening and referral of children in need of vaccination to MoH’s vaccination centers/mobile teams. Following an integrated approach, HWs and CHVs will be trained on safe identification and referral, CP principles and GBV risk mitigation. INTERSOS will ensure referral pathways and provision of medical in-kind assistance to vulnerable cases identified during protection case management. INTERSOS is reinforcing integration with EiE by training school personnel and teachers on health and hygiene related topics and conducting health and hygiene education - including on menstrual hygiene management (complemented by the distribution of MHM kits). In 2024-2025, INTERSOS plans to reach 81,482 individuals with 1,881,450 USD budget requirement, including 44,106 individuals (920,915.00 USD) in 2024 and 37,376 individuals (960,535.00 USD) in 2025.
