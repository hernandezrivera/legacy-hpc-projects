Project Summary

Mafa, according to assessments conducted by NJR partners and REACH , have high number of populations engaging in negative coping mechanisms in insuring food in the household. The assessments revealed that, compared to other areas, Mafa has less humanitarian partners providing services. The high negative coping mechanisms practiced by the population plus the security contraints in accessing land for livelihood leads to a situation of food insecurity for the target populations. ZOA aims to provide vulnerable households among both IDPs and host population with per capita MPC of NGN 4,010 for a period of 5 months. The distribution period will be from June to October to insure coverage during the lean season with project project inception happening in April and closing in November. The per capita calculation takes into account coverage for 70% of the Multisectoral Minimum expenditure basket for expenditure in health, education and others. Total of 1000 individuals are being targeted for the unrestricted multi=purpose cash grants.

1 - Needs

REACH (September 2019) shows that 70% of households in Mafa demonstrated high use of negative coping strategies and that Mafa is among LGAs having less access to services and humanitarian actor presence. The same assessment identified Mafa as among LGAs where access to land for productive activities is a challenge. 26% of households in Mafa named explosive hazards as a major barrier to accessing land. The combination of constrained access to livelihood activities and high use of negative coping mechanisms are factors that lead to food insecurity particularly during the lean season. A September 2019 Multisectoral Needs assessment conducted by partners that compose the Nigeria Joint Response revealed that target groups in Mafa identify other major expenditures as education (17.7%) , water (35.8%) and health (14.20%). These expenses put pressure on the fragile and constrained resources of the household.and force households into more negative coping.As such, the provision of multipurpose cash grants ( MPGs) covering 70% of the MEB aims to support households reduce negative coping strategies.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Beneficiary targeting, selection and registration exercises - ZOA utilizes a criteria that allows of targeting and selection of the most in need or vulnerable, The vulnerability criteria includes variables such as female and child headed households, vulnerable members such as pregnant and lactating women, elderly, PLW and chronically ill, large household members and dependency and in cases such as food security, access to land and productive assets. A socio-economic score is determined and a cut-off to determine the number. This is followed by biometric registration using fingerprint scanners and tablet phones. ZOA is part of the Nigeria joint response funded by the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs through the Dutch Relief Alliance. As such, NJR members coordinate targeting through regular planning and operational meetings, joint assessments and the establishment of communities of practice according to sectors for harmonization of targeting and selection. ZOA is also an active participant of the Food Security Sector Cluster and Cash working groups and regularly submits 5Ws and refers to the dashboard of partner presence. Activity 2: Community entry meetings and monthly feedback meetings. These meetings explain to the community the project and develops champions from within the community. This also concretizes ZOAs accountability to affected population by providing a venue for transparency and face to face feedback.Activity 3: Cash distribution events-5 monthly cash distribution with beneficiaries separated in smaller groups. ZOA distribution system through the use of color coded cards and different distribution dates by batch of beneficiaries to avoid drawing crowds and reduce security risks as part of ZOAs commitment in protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safety of beneficiaries at distribution sites. The integrity lay-out of distribution sites also ensures appropriate sections and priorities for vulnerable groups such as elderly and pregnant and lactating women. Activity 4: Sensitization on Protection issues and gender based GBV . These are sessions where women and girls join focused groups discussions that identify risks , threats, vulnerabilities and align these with community capacities to mitigate or external sources for reporting. ZOA has already conducted gender analysis training for staff and has a gender analysis framework ready for implementation for its year 2020 project including a GBV safety audit. This is also how ZOA intends to integrate PSEA issues and prevention in its project. The gender analysis, coupled with sessions on identifying community mechanisms of prevention PSEA. ZOA has also established presence in the PSEA network task force to establish connections with the referral mechanisms where present.Activity 5: IYCF/HDD sessions- women beneficiaries will be selected and grouped. Bi-monthly sessions will be conducted in groups to enhance the nutritious preparation and feeding of food bought from multi-[purpose grants. Community nutrition mobilizers and lead mothers together with ZOA staff come together for regular sessions designed to increase the knowledge of mother and child pairs in proper feeding practices and preparation of various foods that diversify the household diet. ZOA does not do nutrition and considers this activity as a form of labelling and compliment to strengthen the use of cash for food. Activity 6: Post distribution monitoring , community feedback sessions and operationalizing CRM- The ZOA CRM system is operationalized on field through help desks during distribution, face to face conversations, suggestion boxes and hot lines

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ZOAs commitment to the IASC accountability to affected population can be demonstrated by its developed policies and guidelines that ensure feedback and accountability. Community feedback meetings and FGDs provide a venue for transparency as this allows the communities to know exactly what the project is all about and provide a venue for feedback and complaints.The policies and guidelines are translated on field into the presence of a help desk, a hotline, face to face receiving of complaints and suggestion boxes. These are all stored in a CRM data base that monitors appropriate response times and if all recorded complaints have been attended to. The CRM data base is reported quarterly during directors meetings at HQ and country offices are held accountable for delays or inaction. For this specific project, ZOA will conduct gender analysis to further inform of the power dynamics and insure actionable learning particularly on issues arising from control of cash resources that ZOA puts more in the hands of women.
