Project Summary

CARE is planning to implement this project in partnership with Abdel Shafi Community Health Association (ACHA) and Palestinian Medical relief Society (PMRS). The project aims firstly, to provide health care and prevention services to women including: detection and primary care of gynecological problems; ultrasound examination; counseling and treatment for menopausal women; testing during pregnancy; ante and postnatal; care providing family planning services to women and adolescents including information and counselling regarding all possible contraceptive methods in order to facilitate an optimal and responsible free choice. Meetings and raising awareness sessions on reproduction and family planning will be organized for various target groups with special focus on male and female adolescents, men and women. Pre-delivery courses will be offered to women in the last quarter of pregnancy consisting of suitable physical exercises, relaxation and breathing techniques and health education sessions regarding pregnancy, delivery, breast-feeding and family planning. Post-delivery counselling and services will be offered for women who have recently given birth and who will receive information on breast-feeding, family planning, baby care and conduct physical exercises and enhancing their access for essential health services including: primary health care “PHC”, Maternal and Child Health “MCH”, Sexual Reproductive health “SRH”, Nutrition and None communicable Disease “NCD”; identification and referral of GBV cases in accordance with the national referral mechanism. Secondly, the project aims to build the capacity of frontlines staff from the health specialized actors on self-care, safety, provision of PPEs and hygiene materials to the medical teams as well as training on prevention and control of Covid-19, health counselling, continue planning, GBV identifications and referral , PSEA measures , complain and response mechanism training for 130 male and female staff/volunteers. the project will provide necessary medical supplies to implement the project’s activities.
; Thirdly, Communication; Accountability and Advocacy using broadcast/ social media platforms;
However, the project will address the main problem of “Protection and respect the right to health for most vulnerable children, women people with disabilities (PWD) in Gaza Strip.” It responds to time critical needs identified in recent HNO 2022 and specific needs assessment conducted on 15-19/9/2021 using adapted/remote modalities.
The project targets directly 8190 beneficiaries (6585 women, 225 men, 780 girls , boys 600), out of them 522 (PWD) identified based on vulnerability criteria: women/ men 18-59 and elderly>59 years and girls/ boys<18 who are NCD patients (cancer,), women in reproductive age/ pregnant/ lactating, <5 children, persons with disabilities, internally displaced, protection risks and needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from vulnerable area particularly Gaza North (Jabalia, Beit Hanoun / Ezbet Beit Hanoun , Beit Lahia / El Siafa, UM al-Nasser and El Salatin ) and Gaza (Gaza City, El Shaaf – El Zaitoun - Shati' Camp), Rafah(Rafah, Shokat as Sufi and Khanyounis ( Klab village Abasan al Kabira, Al Fukhkhari, Mawasi Khan Yunis, and Qarara). CARE/partners. will use their PHC health centers to provide services and will coordinate with health/women CBOs to facilitate targeting beneficiaries.
The project activities go in line with the HNC priorities to improve Access to essential Health Services including SRH
CARE partners will coordinate with all relevant actors to ensure the complementarity and avoid duplication. At the same time implementing a clear feedback and Accountability mechanism, MEAL, security and full access to project services based on humanitarian principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence & humanity).
All project's activities will be delivered FREE of charge.

1 - Needs

Over a year of responding to the COVID-19 pandemic has had significant impact on the delivery of other essential healthcare services which have been intermittently disrupted whenever there have been surges in COVID-19 cases. According to MoH 2020 Annual Report, there are about 130,000 adolescent girls,90,000 vulnerable women and 225,000 children, including 30,000 neonates in need of the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, gender-based violence, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services. About 110,000 people with non-communicable diseases and some of these in need ofpalliative care remains with very limited available support, affecting especially those with cancer, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. The July 2021 MSNA found that on average 47% of surveyed households in Gaza have at least one member with chronic disease and 23%of su rveyed HHs reported facing access barriers like lack of services, high cost, physical access, etc. As of 14 October 2021, 178,452of COVID 19 cases have been reported in Gaza including 1,487 deaths and active cases 11,945. Rapid increase of people with the virus and the number of deaths is of grave concern especially in a context like Gaza noting that around 36% of the targeted population for vaccination received the vaccine (at least one shot, 24% received full 2 shots). As all assessments including the HNO 2022 reveled; Pregnant and lactating women and children < 5 are considered the most vulnerable and they may not be able to receive essential healthcare in response to the crisis because of the re-prioritization of health services as a result of the COVID 19 crisis. Gendered inequalities in the oPt further limit freedom of movement, decision- making power, and access to health, education, and basic services and resources differently for men, women, boys, and girls. Also, socially and economically disadvantaged women, male/female youth, and marginalized populations (i.e. PWDs) are more disproportionately affected. When overlapped with existing gendered disparities, the response to both the protracted crisis and C-19 becomes increasingly complex. Global trends indicate that women and girls are bearing speciﬁc burdens of the C-19 crisis such as: increased household responsibilities and family care, restricted mobility, and rising levels of GBV.CARE’s assessment (April 2020 and repeated May-June 2021) noted that signiﬁcantly fewer female respondents (58%) than males (86%) have safe access to health facilities inside and outside of their community, which they attributed to a lack of cash and inability to travel. The prevalence of male medical and security staff also creates barriers for women accessing health care, mental health support, basic hygiene, as well as the right to privacy and comfort, given the taboos and norms related to gender mixing. As of 14-19 September, 2021 CARE/Partners conducted (2) FGDs and (15) phone interviews with partners’ staff and volunteers (women and men 24-54 years); (2) meetings targeting health institutions and health CBOs/NGOs; WhatsApp group targeting 50 women with breast cancer, WhatsApp group targeting 50 women in reproductive age, pregnant women, lactating women and mothers of children <5 years; needs expressed by beneficiaries via helpline; and (25) phone interviews with most vulnerable women/ men 18-59 and>59 years and girls/ boys<18 years, persons with disabilities (with reduced mobility including with physical disabilities, elderly and bed-ridden as well as with non-mobility related disabilities including blind, hard of hearing, intellectually), breast cancer survivors, SGBV survivors, refugees, IDPs and FHHs from Gaza North (Jabalia, Beit Hanoun, Beit Lahia, Siafa) and Gaza (Gaza City, Shati' Camp, Mughraga, Juhr Dik) and ARAs. CARE/partners concluded the participants’ differentiated needs in alignment with 2022 HNO, to be addressed by the project. The assessment ﬁndings reveal critical needs for essential health services including SRH with its detailed list of services like ant-natal and post-natal care, family planning, gynecological problems, counseling, provision of supplies etc. this comes in in alignment with 2022 HNO and all participants expressed their fear and grave concern about the rise in transmission of COVID-19 in local communities and amongst healthcare workers in Gaza Strip especially cancer cases. Partners staff and volunteers reflected shortage of PPEs and hygiene and sterilization materials; frontline staff of health specialized actors lack knowledge on maintaining self-care and wellbeing viewed; women in reproductive age and pregnant/ lactating women lack quality maternal and nutrition services in governmental health facilities; SGBV cases lack integrated case management services including prevention and referral services.The frontline health staff are in need of self-care capacity building training for frontline staff in health and nutrition, and also noted the need for PSEAH, complaints/response mechanisms, and remote health counselling. Women of reproductive age and pregnant/ lactating women noted the lack of quality maternal and nutrition services in governmental health facilities, primarily access to SRH, family planning, prevention and referral of SGBV, screening, diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation, medical lab tests; individual and group PSS, couple therapy, specialized mental health treatment, social/legal counselling; home visits, dignity/hygiene kits and community awareness raising. . Women and girls with physical disabilities noted the need for assistive mobility devices (mobile chairs, walkers), while those with hearing disabilities lack assistive audio devices. SGBV cases lack integrated case management services, and prevention/ referral services. Finally, a broad advocacy is needed to raise the voices of women and girls about their critical health gaps/needs

2 - Activities

The project aims to improve and enhance the sexual and reproductive healthcare of pregnant and lactating women including those with disabilities, breast cancer survivors, GBV survivors, and FHHs taking into account the local impact of the current COVID-19 pandemic. The planned activities were guided by CARE/partners assessments and HNO 2022 which indicated that redirection of resources from the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services affected.The proposed activities are closely coordinated with HNC, health providers, local CBOs, while being aware of what is offered in the targeted locations. The activities will be implemented in partnership with Abdel Shafi Community Health Association (ACHA) and Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) who will be responsible of the technical implementation at the ﬁeld level (provision of health services). CARE will be responsible for the overall quality management, technical support in certain areas like gender and protection, GBV, PSEA, M&E, reporting, as well as managing for the feedback and complaint mechanism, and will act as the main contact for donors/actors. The project activities will be as follows: #1: Providefamily planning services to 2100 women including information and counselling regarding all possible contraceptive commodities; #2: Provide emergency sexual and reproductive health care services for 975 women and (225) girls include ante and postnatal care Pre-natal counselling services including ultrasound examinations, will be offered to women. Pre-delivery courses will be offered to women in the last quarter of pregnancy consisting of suitable physical exercises, and medication by using mobile clinics to reach most marginalized area; #3 Provide Psychosocial support to 200 women and girls (GBV survivors, Cancer patients, post-natal depressions cases) who received SRH services; #4: Provide health services and micronutrients supplements for 600 children below 5 years of women who received SRH services; #5: Conduct awareness raising sessions on child common health problems and malnutrition disorders for 600 women and 60 early married girls; #6: Conduct 80 awareness raising sessions on productive and family planning , GBV, SRH and young people needs (540 women, 180 girls and 180 male); #7: Provide remote health counselling via help line about SRH issues and malnutrition (Include health promoters fees- by (ACHA)); #7: Screening of 600 GBV survivors and breast cancer cases (mammogram and U/S) and diagnosis, treatment of 120 GBV and breast cancer cases (FNAP, True cut, immunity tests, others) ; #8 Conducting capacity building for 40 frontlines staff from the health specialized actors on self- care and safety, SRH topics and capacity building training for 40 health providers on SRH topics (Pre-contraceptive Care “PCC”, Family planning “FP” on how to deal with gynecological problems, counseling skills), provision of PPEs to the medical teams, GBV identiﬁcation, follow and referral, PSEA measures, complaint and response mechanism; #9 Communication, accountability and advocacy: Conduct 3 radio spots and 1 press releases about the project, Conduct 3 remote/ face to face community meetings to explain the project, conduct documentation and sensitized brief joint report for project activities and organize advocacy campaign on health and nutrition humanitarian needs for women in reproductive age/pregnant and lactating women as well as adolescent girls and cancer cases. this will include one community dialogue forums; one accountability hearing panels; SMS messages to 30000 women/girls, men/boys; one tweeting event using appropriate Hashtag on social media; broadcast media (2 radio spots, 2 radio sessions); production of 1 short film uploaded on social media pages of CARE/ ACHA/PMRS and #10: Procurement and distribution of PPEs and hygiene / dignity kits At the beginning of the project, at the preparatory phase; CARE and the partner will reach out to the targeted communities to explain the project's objectives and activities (services). During this phase, meetings with different actors (MoH, local CBOs, community representatives/leaders, women groups) to share the information about the project. At the same time description about the project will be posted on the main locations in the communities like Mosques, CBOs, municipalities, etc.). more over there will be announcements about the project on the social media, etc. CARE has zero tolerance policy towards (1) sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse of program participants and community members and (2) sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse within the organization and has put in place measures for its prevention and reporting. Concretely, CARE’s global policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection explicitly outlines unacceptable behavior, and what we will do to investigate allegations, support people who have experienced sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and discipline perpetrators, including referring them to the relevant authorities. Internally as well, CARE has the Safeguarding Code of Conduct to which all employees and related personnel must adhere. CARE oPt’s FAM, which will be implemented during the life of the project, ensures that such reports are identified and community members’ complaints addressed and that steps are taken toward redressing any power imbalances. Additionally, CARE has a “Tell Us” whistle-blower policy and has mechanisms in place to report abuse or harassment of any kind, including anonymously to a third party via the internet or phone call

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

CARE is committed to assuring accountability to stakeholders in all aspects and phases of the project. At the inception stage, community members helped inform the design of project activities, composition of the teams, frequency and type of the services, urgent health issues, etc. CARE/partner also took into consideration the different health needs of the different groups residing in the targeted communities (sex and age). Monitoring has been carried out through the on sight monitoring of distributions and activities, and it always includes segregation of data by sex and age (SADD). CARE’s Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) builds on our previous experience with humanitarian accountability, and it is in-formed by standards and guidance to ensure quality and accountability to our partners and stakeholders, and to support existing organizational and technical standards, with which to bring about sustainable change to communities in need. It was designed and piloted in other projects within CARE allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE for any feedback or complaint about the project activities, criteria, services, behavior by any of the project’s staff CARE will continue to raise community awareness regarding FAM, regularly reporting on feedback and complaints as part of CARE’s continuous learning agenda. CARE has started the distribution of the FAM cards in the targeted communities and started to receive feedback/complaints. Feedback/complaints are documented in a log sheet by the Focal Point / Accountability Officer responsible for monitoring the progress of each input until the feedback loop is closed. The data will be reported and used for management decisions and analyzing trends. CARE also is planning to conduct a community-based survey that will further assess accessibility of the mechanism, as well as preferred channels of communication; a patient satisfaction survey will be conducted to see the feedback to enable CARE to respond accordingly

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

To create an effective and easily accessible mechanism for all stakeholders, CARE Palestine WBG considered the existing challenges and constraints that participants may face in the local context, including the continuous restrictions on movement, security risks and sudden changes to security, etc., as well as the social mores and gender norms. Before fully launching the mechanism, CARE piloted the FAM to gain deeper insight into beneficiary / stakeholders’ satisfaction levels about the quality of and access to services, as well as about the extent to which the most vulnerable community members were being reached and their needs were being met, to promote community empowerment, participation, and ownership of the feedback mechanism, and to help improve monitoring and evaluation activities by incorporating beneficiaries’ perspectives into reporting practices through real time analysis of CARE’s work on the ground. CARE continues to evolve FAM by soliciting stakeholders’ opinions about preferences with respect to access, methods / channels through which to provide feedback and complaints. With respect to the clinics where the mobile teams work, Arabic instructions (cards) for lodging complaints are posted in clear view of visiting patients, and contact cards are distributed to participants via home visits, through local CBOs, local councils, focal points in the communities, etc. Where the mobile teams work without a physical clinic, CARE will distribute the FAM cards in the communities including the beneficiaries so they are informed about the mechanism.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

CARE’s ‘Do No Harm’ principles are embedded in all approaches and interventions by CARE, from the ideation process of a project, throughout its duration and after the project is finished. CARE has identified risks, their potential impact and mitigation strategies, and all activities proposed are designed based on numerous discussions with community members, partners and other stakeholders, as well as on needs analyses (i.e. rapid / gender needs assessment) that are sensitive to the complexities of the local context to ensure that people are not exposed to additional risks through our action. CARE is committed to protection and ensuring safety and security of participants, dignity and accessibility of services; CARE’s activities are inclusive, and designed with the community members’ feedback, and the selection criteria for activities are transparent; the data collection processes are sensitive to the context, power dynamics in communities, and also the management of sensitive information is conducted with caution and care.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PWD/women and other vulnerable groups are considered as main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project, as they were identiﬁed by as vulnerable groups who needs special consideration, for that the project team will work with the communities’ representatives and actors in the different communities to ensure equal access of PWD/women/others to all projects’ activities and will ensure the full inclusion of PWD/women/other in all project activities. PWD will have special attention when they come to the clinics, and those who are not able to access the clinic will be visited at home. Elderly and the bed ridden patients also have special needs as a target group. Home visits will also be planned for those who can’t access the clinic. The services will be provided free of charge.
