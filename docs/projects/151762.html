Project Summary

The project will provide immediate access to safe drinking water to 15,000 vulnerable population boys,girls,women and men affected by the drought and other natural disaster, HIDIG intends to rehabilitates 15 shallow wells benefiting 15,000 men, women and children to enable enhanced the community access to safe drinking water. The project will also provide the target beneficiaries with hygiene kits as well as provide hygiene promotion to ensure community is aware of the water born risk as well as sanitation and other vector born disease. Through this HIDIG will ensure to establish 19 water management committee at IDPs and rehabilitate 19 water sources to enable ease the water related protection risk among the community in the target location.
HIDIG will construct both IDPs latrines as well as health facilities and schools to ensure proper sanitation in the target areas. Through this project HIDIG will ensure the project is coordinated across other sector as well as regional leadership to enable have better and well managed program

Needs

According Somalia humanitarian needs overview (WASH cluster) a total of 2.9 M people are in needs need urgent WASH assistance in Somalia in 2019,if these 1.5 M are in IDPS WHILE 1.4M are in non IDPs and host communities.2.1m women,children boys and girls are in need of protection at water point Unreliable access to a temporary water source and/or unprotected water is still an important feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Nationwide, access to an improved water source remains below 40%, with large variations from one region to another, however, Bay and Bakol region below 25% each. In most urban and sub-urban hubs and IDP settlements, access to an adequate quantity of water is often granted but water is unsafe and often microbiologically contaminated due to cross-contamination by poorly design water supply and sanitation systems. More than half of the population lack of access to adequate sanitation.The situation is particularly critical in Bakool region below 25% and in IDP settlements in Bay and Hiran region, Hand washing with soap at critical moment is practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability to a functional hand washing point is limited. This percentage drops to below 25% in Hirshabelle, despite that these two states were badly affected by AWD/Cholera recently. Heavy rain falls affected more than 700,000 people in 2018 affecting WASH infrastructures in more than 25 districts. Most of these infrastructures have not been repaired yet. Because of the floods and the chronical lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, Somalia continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2018 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots Hiraan and Bay region included.HRDO will continue to focus our activities on to most vulnerable populations in hard to reach locations; IDPs, people at risk of AWD /cholera, acutely malnourished and people in drought-affected areas in Bay,Bakol andHiran region

Activities

1.1: Provide safe and clean water to 2,000 drought/floods affected households (15,000 persons) through voucher system. Every household will receive 45 liter/day as SPHERE standard for 60 days. The selection of the beneficiaries will be done with the consultation of community leader 1.2: Rehabilitate 15 shallow wells (10 with hand pump and 5 with animal trough) benefiting around 15000 men, women and children. The community leaders including women groups will assign the design and the location of the shallow wells.1.3: Rehabilitate 4 water pans benefiting 4,000 men, women and children including the limited saved animals from the drought. The action will include: de-silting, increasing the volume of the water pan and protecting its areas from animals and human, in order to avoid the contamination of the water pan. 1.4: Provide water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing programme, in the 19 water points (systematic) and household level 1.5: Establish and train: (a) 19 water management committees (WMCs) at community and IDP level from the rehabilitated 19 water sources, composed 133 members shared equally by men and women (76 men and 57 women) on water source management and water source maintenance to keep the sustainability of the water source. (b) 38 well operators shared equally men and women (19 men and 19 women) on operation and maintenance (O&M), chlorination of water sources and collection of revenues. (c) 100 community members from community leaders, community elders, women groups, and youth shared equally by men and women (50 men and 50 women) on Disaster and Risk Reduction mechanisms (DRR) and conflict resolution mechanisms..1: Rehabilitate/Construct 200 gender-sensitive and flood proof IDP latrines. The action includes: desludging of the filled septic tank, repairing of broken floors, replacing the damaged walls and doors. 2.2: construct 150 gender-sensitive, desludging and flood proof IDP latrines with hand washing facilities.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is meant to build community resilience by strengthening local infrastructure and thus provide timely action, maintaining momentum of the response, and expanding reach in underserved areas promoting equity. Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services, where priority will be given to the delivery of equitable access to basic Wash service services, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of school attacks, poor enrollment and retention rate. To increase community uptake of provided services, community level demand creation will be strengthened. HIDIG will strengthen capacity of teachers by training them on life-skill, Wash service and livelihood technical project staff as well as community Wash serviceand livelihood workers. Communities will be involved in project designation and will be part of the committees supporting district level education committees. Working with other actors will ensure leverage amongst themselves and providing maximum support to target communities strengthening their capacities in various aspects, For sustainable and equitable access of the donor funded project HIDIG will ensure community inclusion in the initial decision making of the project design and structure in this case the WASH component will be prioritize the needs of community and in such will ensure to capacitate the existing community wash committee as well as the staff that will ensure the carryover/sustainability of the project after the expiry of the donor funds.HIDIG will ensure to provide more responsive services to the affected population under the strict supervision and coordination with government and other humanitarian actors within its area of operation. The WASH activities will consider the most vulnerable and disadvantage groups hence build the community resilience to prevent recurrent and protracted emergency.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Baladweyne and pastoralist in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing Wash service and livelihood center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care.Baidoa and Baladweyne exhibit high number of IDPs an unaccompanied and separated children however, in this proposed program HIDIG will ensure these unaccompanied or separated children were provided with adequate dignity kits as well as material assistance that will ensure to deter any form of abuse.HIDIG will ensure to provide hotline/call centers within the community WASH committees who will in turn report to the project team for quick feedback and attention these vulnerable children and mothers.HIDIG will ensure awareness creation to reduce the occurrence of domestic violence by conducting joint community and other stakeholders meeting within the project areaConflict a rising from water point.HIDIG will mitigate this menace through building the capacity of WASH committee as well as improving proper coordination with government line ministry for sustainable water services. The WASH committee will be selected among the communities with the project location as well as provide them with infrastructural equipment that will enable them to perform the minor breakdown within the water sources. Based on consultations with the community and elders within the target IDPs, the need for the project to continue, to be expanded and integrated became evident. HIDIG shall be able to take into consideration the needs at the ground and the support required so that the project responds to their needs and priorities and that the project activities do not go against

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneficiaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health and Wash service, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, HIDIG will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identification and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the education sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, mobile/outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Building community structures including local temporary learning space that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate Wash service without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence.
