Project Summary

Water and access to sanitation continues to be a major priority for 2021. Interruptions in water supply in villages along the contact line prevent communities, households, and schools from having access to safe water and sanitation facilities affecting over 1.1. million people on both sides of the contact line increasing health risks. Water scarcity is particularly acute in communities within 5 km of the contact line as some communities have lost complete access. Prior to the conflict, communities were able to drill wells as an alternative to access potable water for drinking, hygiene and irrigation but climate change and conflict has rendered that option impossible.
Risks associated with lack of access to reliable water sources and sanitation facilities across the contact line have been exacerbated by the Covid-19 pandemic. Communities were already lacking adequate access to healthcare as many health facilities had been damaged by the conflict. However as demand for health services due to the Covid-19 pandemic went up, the lack of adequate sanitation facilities at health care facilities is now a potential source of infection for the population; only 84.2% of Health Care Facilities have access to water points, of which 75% requires some forms of repair and only half of HCFs had enough handwashing stations. With health services and emergency medical care in conflict-affected Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts already underfunded pre-Covid and with regional and local authorities in these areas focusing on Ukraine’s second wave of infection which is expected to peak in the winter of 2020, it is critical HCFs catering to highly vulnerable individuals including the elderly are resourced. Of particular concern are EECPs where the risk of infection among the elderly is high due to overcrowding.
In response the Norwegian Refugee Council Proposes the project “Providing immediate WASH assistance to health facilities and conflict-affected populations in Ukraine.” The project aligns with the WASH cluster’s strategic Objective 1. “Provide emergency and time-critical assistance and ensure access to basic essential services to 2 million conflict-affected people with humanitarian needs.” Through a three-pronged geographic approach (communities along the contact line, Health Care Facilities & Social Service Institutions, and EECPs) the project will “Increase the resilience of communities affected populations by strengthening the humanitarian WASH response to provide adequate access to water and sanitation in communities along the contact line.” The specific objectives of the project are:
• Access to safe water through decentralized systems in communities along the contact line is improved
• Communities along the contact line adopt best water management practices.
• Health Care Facilities have improved WASH infrastructure to respond more effectively to Covid-19
• Risks of infectious disease transmission including Covid19 at Health Care Facilities are mitigated.
• Risks of Infectious Disease Transmission including Covid19 at three EECPs in Luhansk are mitigated through handwashing stations and improved WASH facilities.
The project will benefit 70,000 individuals in communities located within 5 & 20 kms of the contact line in Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts. It will also address immediate humanitarian WASH needs in communities beyond 20 kms to prevent the spread of Covid-19.

1 - Needs

Water and access to sanitation continues to be a major priority for 2021. According to the 2020 HNO, frequent disruptions of water supply caused by security incidents in the First Lift Pumping station of the South Donbas Water Way threatened several times the water supply of over 1.1. million people on both sides of the contact line. These interruptions in supply prevented communities and schools from having access to safe water and sanitation facilities increasing health risks. The need for WASH assistance is greater for rural households living within 20 km of the “contact-line,” particularly among vulnerable groups. The HNO specifies that assessments carried out in 2020 found that “more than one in four families on both sides of the contact line reported that the water situation (…) deteriorated [further] over the past five years [with] water stoppages, unavailability, inability to treat water, and a lack of money to pay for water, sanitation and hygiene products are among the commonly identified critical problems.” A NRC assessment confirms this data. Findings from focus group discussions carried out in communities within 5 km of the contact line in GCA confirm that potable water is scarce, and, in some locations, there is no access. It is reported that before the conflict communities were able to drill wells for personal or public use as alternatives to access to potable water for drinking, hygiene and irrigation to cope with shortages. However, climate change is now reducing water levels in most underground aquifers across conflict affected communities in both Luhansk and Donetsk exacerbating already existing water problems. An assessment carried out by REACH in November 2018 can illustrate the depth of the problem. More than a third of KIs (32%) reported experiencing water shortages of more than 3 hours per day in education facilities. The shortages lasted 3-6 hours (25%), 6-12 hours (19%), 12-24 hours (13%), 1-3 days (19%) or more than 3 days (25%). In sum, lack of access to reliable water sources threatens not only communities’ access to basic sanitation, but it also impacts the ability of children to access schools and the livelihoods of rural communities who depend on agriculture. Risks associated with lack of access to reliable water sources and sanitation facilities across the contact line have been exacerbated by the Covid-19 pandemic. Communities were already lacking adequate access to healthcare as many health facilities had been damaged by the conflict. An assessment carried out by REACH in 2018 mentions that facilities in Petropavlivka, Peredilske, Teple, Plotyna and Nyzhnia Vilkhova were heavily affected” with sanitation facilities and toilets being particularly in need of being built and/or repaired. A more detailed assessment carried out by MDM in 2020 mentions that only 84.2% of Health Care Facilities have access to water points, of which 75% requires some forms of repair and only half of HCFs had enough handwashing stations. With health services and emergency medical care in conflict-affected Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts already underfunded pre-COVID and with regional and local authorities in these areas focusing on Ukraine’s second wave of infection which is expected to peak in the winter of 2020, it is critical HCFs catering to highly vulnerable individuals including the elderly are resourced and that the rigorous application of prevention measures including the rehabilitation of basic WASH facilities are introduce to decrease the risk of forming clusters of infection that puts both staff and vulnerable individuals at risk. Of particular concern are EECPs where the risk of infection among the elderly is high due to overcrowding. An assessment carried out by NRC in June 2020 shows that WASH infrastructure and increased handwashing facilities must be strengthened as a mitigation measure.
