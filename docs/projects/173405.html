Project Summary

HI inclusive education projects expertise in working across three distinct levels (community and family level, school and services level and national and policy level). Maintaining standards based on the lessons learned and experiences from its vast array of inclusive education projects, HI develops and standardizes various components of inclusive education services. Ensuring that no children is left behind, HI targets children with a range of different disabilities to support their access to education by applying a multi-sectorial approach and strong links with other sectors such as rehabilitation, psychosocial support, child protection and many others. Support for both formal enrolment and home based learning sessions with Individual Education Plans (IEP) are provided to the beneficiaries in collaboration with their caregivers, teachers and volunteers. In addition, assistive devices, therapeutic materials, learning and recreational materials are distributed to the children based on their individual specialized needs identified via technical assessments. That is why in 2021, HI will keep on implementing its Inclusive Education projects through the provision of Individualized Education Action Plans to 300 children with disabilities meanwhile working on diminishing the social (stigma), communication and environmental barriers through awareness sessions and accessibility works at school and community levels, along with the provision of assistive and mobility devices. Also, in 2021 HI plans to train on Inclusive education to 30 mid to senior level IP staffs to become master trainer. These master trainers will cascade knowledge to facilitator/ teachers to improve and promote inclusive teaching learning environment in LC/CFs in their program, more awareness and sensitization to accept and support inclusive education, enrolling children with disabilities by partners in their own learning centers to create more ownership and responsibility among teachers and staffs.
A joint action plan with the IPs of Education sector should be developed and regularly be followed up with support of education sector The partnership will apply a twin-track approach of implementation modalities of inclusive education and inclusive mainstreaming. That is, focus on the specific needs of children with disabilities as well as a focus on mainstreaming services for them will be applied, so they are fully inclusive. The approach will ensure that existing structural gaps and individual needs of children with disabilities are addressed in programming, while at the same time community, environmental and socio-economic barriers to inclusive education are identified and considered.

1 - Identified Needs

To date 860,494 Rohingya refugees have settled in the refugee camps of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas of the Cox’s Bazar district in Bangladesh fleeing violence in Myanmar’s Rakhine State. Around 52% of the total refugee population are children with 1% of the refugee households having a person with disability in the family. The 2019 REACH Disability Prevalence Report noted that 10% of children of age 6-14 years have a functional difficulty in at least one domain. The population continues to live in overcrowded camps vulnerable to landslides and flash-flooding with heavy dependence on humanitarian assistance to cover their basic needs three-years after the mass influx began in August 2017. This dependency on humanitarian assistance is particularly higher for person with disabilities as they are more vulnerable to exploitation, abuse and violence than persons without disabilities and much less likely to access any facility-based services. Particularly for educational facilities, children with disabilities are less likely to attend learning centers than children without disabilities (for both age groups 3-5 and 6-14). Learning centers in the camps have a number of barriers which are preventing children with disabilities from attending. Physical barriers exist due to the inaccessible terrain and a lack of ramps or accessible toilets. They also face attitudinal barriers from staff, parents or other children who may not believe children with disabilities should attend mainstream education. This was evident from the 2019 Reach Education Needs Assessment which reported participants experiencing substantial physical access constraints for children with disabilities (only 14 per cent of assessed centers were wheelchair accessible), as well as worrying levels of verbal and physical abuse from peers at learning centers. In addition, the needs assessment also reports that only 26 per cent of surveyed staff received training in supporting children with disabilities. This further confirms that environmental barriers such as the learning environment are also prevalent since teachers are not trained in inclusive education pedagogy while learning centers are rarely equipped with accessible and inclusive learning materials. This gap in low uptake in enrollment for children with disabilities and the need to equip all educations facilities in the camps to become as inclusive and accessible require substantial and immediate efforts. In order to address these gaps, HI will train master trainers on inclusive education and support them to cascade the training to an enhanced capacity building programme for education partner staffs on applying skills of inclusive education and specialized (tailor-made) individual education support for children with disabilities through partnership between Education Sector and HI. This capacity building of Education sector partners will enable them to effectively integrate inclusion of children with disability in their programme planning, design, implementation and monitoring. The programme will apply a twin-track approach of implementation modalities of inclusive education and inclusive mainstreaming. That is, focus on the specific needs of children with disabilities as well as a focus on mainstreaming services for them will be applied, so they are fully inclusive. The approach will ensure that existing structural gaps and individual needs of children with disabilities are addressed in programming, while at the same time community, environmental and socio-economic barriers to inclusive education are identified and considered. The partnership will target physical accessibility of children with disability in learning centers by training staffs from local partners on programme modules that promote respect, diversity and inclusive teaching methodologies.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Assessments of Inclusion barriers and facilitators of learning facilities operated by partners and formulating action plan in collaboration with 5 partners.Activity 2: Module development, training and coaching (cascading training of first two batches will be co-facilitated) of Master Trainers of Education sector partners and refresher training will also be arranged. Total facilitator reached through co-facilitated trainings 30*28= 840. If each facilitator is supporting 70 children then total children with functional difficulties benefiting is around 4099 (according REACH 2019 assessment, 10% of children of age 6-14 years have a functional difficulty, and of these children with functional difficulty only 61% attends learning facilities).Activity 3: Evaluation of teachers/facilitators capacity on IE skills developed from trainings by Master Trainers.Activity 4: Developing age appropriate Accessible Learning Materials (AEM) set and piloting.Activity 5: Capacity building training and workshop on inclusive education of Project staff.Activity 6: Recruiting and Training of community volunteers.Activity 7: Conducting technical assessment of children with disabilities using Child Functioning Module and other Technical assessment forms.Activity 8: Conducting technical assessment of children with disabilities using Child Functioning Module and other Technical assessment forms.Activity 9: Development, implementation (HBL) of individual education and physical/functional rehabilitation plan in collaboration with caregivers and final evaluation.Activity 10: Procurement and distribution/provision of adapted age appropriate learning materials, assistive device for children with disabilities.Activity 11: Maintenance for HI operated Activity and Learning Center (Preschool) for children with severe disabilities.Activity 12: Inclusive community awareness sessions for parents and caregivers education meetings maintaining health and safety precaution.Activity 13: Inclusive communication messages development and distribute to 5 partners.
