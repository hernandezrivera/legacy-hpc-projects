Project Summary

Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) will provide emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced groups or populations with new vulnerabilities. Key focus will be put on increased number of returnees, internally displaced persons and most vulnerable host communities through coordinated and timely joint responses in Central Equatoria State, Jonglei State, former Unity State, Upper Nile State, in Western Equatoria State and in Lakes State. PAH will use both in-kind (26,000 Ind ) response modality and CBI modality (14,000) in the areas of returns with functional markets. PAH will construct 200 transitional shelter to reduce protection risks and exposure to diseases attributed with poor shelter conditions and lack of household non-food items. The project will support the sustainable existing coping mechanisms and community initiatives for local resource mobilization through a component of multi-purpose cash-based interventions in locations where PAH currently implements FSL, Education and WASH projects. Training in leadership, HLP concepts combined with specific skills training with community leaders and beneficiaries will be conducted in order to build resilience. PAH will also build the capacity of the new cluster partners on response methodology and CBI modality in order to ensure high quality and coordinated response. Mobile data collection tools and bio-metric system will be used throughout the project cycle.
PAH will provide equitable and dignified access to multi-sectorial basic needs through integrated, multi-sectorial approach and these include; emergency shelter/ non-food items, WASH NFIs, Health, nutritional items and multi-purpose cash assistance to the most affected populations with new vulnerabilities in order to meet household basic needs and alleviate human suffering in a safe and dignified manner.
PAH will focus on timely, joint and coordinated missions within the humanitarian hubs, with logistic cluster and CCCM cluster in targeting female headed households, unaccompanied elderly, people with no links to the community, separated children, women at risk (pregnant and lactating mothers), child-headed households, people living with disability and returnees whose properties were damaged and looted during the conflict. The mobile response teams will comprise of experienced ES/NFI oﬃcers (50 percent women and 50 percent men). The protection team will participate in 80 percent of interventions with close coordination with the protection Cluster and static partners. The team will integrate the interventions jointly with partners, more specifically nutrition, WASH, FSL and Protection actors and in close coordination with the respective clusters through SFP.
PAH will conduct timely, joint and coordinated in-depth multi-sectorial needs analysis, rapid market assessment with focus on protection risk analysis with participation of the community-led committees. The needs analysis teams will conduct interviews at household level with consultative community meetings, key informant interviews, observations, focus group discussions with men, women, boys and girls .
Peer Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted 1 month after the intervention in the mobile response and in CBI modality by M&E team. This will measure the quality of the response, impact of the project, HLP, conflict sensitive issues and will determine if the assistance reached the most vulnerable groups. Complaint/feedback mechanism will be in place throughout the project cycle. PAH will use the PDM report for better programming since we will take into account the lessons learnt, complaints raised and recommendations raised by the beneficiaries. Security and logistical constraints have made it diﬃcult in the recent past to conduct PDMs therefore close coordination with clusters partners and joint planning will be emphasized.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, 2,442,716 individuals are estimated to require Shelter and Non-Food Items (S/NFI) assistance in 2020. This figure includes newly internally displaced person (IDPs), returnees and the Host Communities (HC). The South Sudan emergency shelter and non-food item context is differenced into three phases: inside of formal and informal sites, including PoCs, collective sites and other spontaneous sites; outside of formal/informal sites; and relatively stable locations where people are returning to their former habitual residence. Based on most recent IPC reports (June 2019) 25 counties has been classified be under emergency phase. These include; Kapoeta, Akobo, Bor south, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol, Twic East, Uror, Cueibet, Yirol East, Yirol West, Nasir, Kajo-keji and Panyijiar. Emergency needs in these areas are the result of ongoing conflict, localized smaller conflicts, and current floods that continue displacing populations and causing major humanitarian needs. Inside PoCs and collective sites where the displaced populations are sheltered for safety and security, PAH target is 30 percent in informal and collective sites, outside PAH estimates 70 percent of the displaced population are in need of ES/NFI assistance. In hard to reach areas of displacement, targeted caseloads lack access to essential lifesaving NFI items and often are unable to support themselves through communal connections or coping mechanisms through other resources. In cases of returnees, where the areas of returns are stable, returnees are assumed to stay, rebuild their lives and able to cope with the existing/available resources. PAH will look into alternative support modalities, such as multi-purpose cash vouchers, training, and other engagement with communities. Major challenges for the displaced population remains to be access to naturally grown shelter materials, thus necessitating regular provision of shelter material in PoCs and collective sites. On the contrary, IDPs outside of displacement sites may have better access to shelter materials but due to the absence of functional and accessible markets, they lack essential household items, in particular, IDPs that have been subject to multiple displacement. Amongst the IDPs, women and children remain especially vulnerable both due to their roles in society and the protection risks related to being without shelter or social support networks. A small number of host community members (5% of most vulnerable HCs) will also require assistance as they use their already limited resources to support and assist the displaced population. Returnees need more support in understanding their rights for Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) in cases where their land has been given away or reused. Furthermore, current laws and policies do not guarantee security of tenure to marginalized groups particularly women. As returns continue to happen in many parts of the country, several cases of multiple claims to land and property are being recorded by HLP partners. These challenges may fuel tensions among affected populations and exacerbate conflict. PAH will work within the framework of the HLP WG to mitigate these risks within its projects.There are existing coping mechanisms in the community, however they need to be strengthened. These include: leadership structure for massaging and reporting, local resource mobilization initiatives e.g. use of local materials for shelter framing, waving sleeping mats and cooking items, borrowing live-saving NFIs from the host community, participating in income generating activities where markets are functional and sharing of scarce basic household items. Considering the high level of protection risks as well as environmental issues related to firewood and shelter materials collection, appropriate programming needs to be designed to mitigate those risks.

2 - Activities

1.PAH will use both In-kind and CBI modalities to respond to emergency shelter and essential NFI needs for the most vulnerable returnees and internally displaced persons across South Sudan in coordination with protection team: a) CBI interventions (35%) will be implemented in areas of returns with functional and accessible markets b) In-kind response will be conducted through mobile teams in close coordination with cluster partners in conducting responses across South Sudan. PAH will use the established humanitarian hubs for logistic coordination, information center, organize response to reach remote locations.2. Conducting timely, joint and coordinated in-depth needs analysis (focusing on integrated responses in S/NFI, WASH, FSL, Protection, Education, and Nutrition) among the displaced population, returnees and vulnerable host communities. PAH, mobile team will carry out twelve coordinated multi-sector needs analysis for in-kind response and four coordinated needs analysis for CBI focusing more on the protection risk, gender, and disability. In CBI interventions there will be community sensitization and awareness on Housing, Land and Property (HLP) supported by protection cluster and HLP partners.3. Conducting joint beneficiaries' identification and registration through biometric registration system. PAH will use SCOPE and DTM database for registration in locations that are covered by WFP and DTM. In locations with no previous registration, cooperation with WFP will be established to increase SCOPE registration coverage. Collaboration with the host community leaders will be in place in order to target the most vulnerable individuals. Agreed and clear targeting criteria will be communicated to the leaders, community members. PAH team will disaggregate the population by, gender, age and vulnerability. 4. Joint and timely distribution of shelter and essential NFIs will be conducted to the identified and registered population affected with new vulnerability in given locations. All distributions will be coordinated and jointly conducted with other cluster sectors and partners in line with Cluster’s approach. PAH mobile teams will distribute in kind materials and NFIs from pipeline, through coordination with the cluster and state focal points. Some own resources will be used to supplement in kind distributions, through the use of local markets.5. PAH will increase CBI responses to 35% where there will be multi-purpose cash assistance of unconditional with both restricted and unrestricted commodity vouchers for meeting multi-sector needs at household level. CBI team will encourage the usage of local available materials for shelter construction and repair in traditional manner to increase resilience and coping mechanism of displaced population. CBI activities will include: market assessment, needs assessment, price monitoring, vendor selection and capacity review, vendor trainings, communication sessions to community leaders and beneficiaries, registration and voucher/cash provision, distribution and redemption, ongoing monitoring. Support capacity building and SNFI CBI strategy building and improvement.6. PAH S/NFI projects will be integrated with Education, WASH, FSL, Nutrition and Protection when applicable, mobile teams will work together in project locations. Integrated approach will be encouraged in both mobile and static responses to target the same population with multiple resources. 7. Piloting construction of 200 transitional shelters targeting in the areas of returns. PAH team will form shelter committee comprising of 50% ratio of women and men to promote community participation. There will be cash-for-work component in the shelter construction for people with disabilities. 8. PAH will conduct Post-distribution monitoring (PDM)s for up to 50% of distributions, as long as the location are still accessible and secure at least a month after NFIs distributions and with rapid monitoring where access will not allow PDM.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

PAH has high standards of conducting assessments and distributions, always concerning rights of beneficiaries and in full accountability. Before the activities are conducted, the beneficiaries are mobilized and made aware of the project activities, of their rights and selection criteria, what they will get and what is not in the project. Local authorities, chiefs and the entire community are enlightened of the selection criteria and how the registration is done. Beneficiaries will be asked to sign on the forms as a confirmation for receiving assistance. Distributions and other activities are conducted with high sensitivity to the special needs of women, children, elderly and disabled. PAH, put strong attention to cultural norms and local law, acts always within the law of South Sudan, while humanitarian standards and international human rights are our highest guidepost. "Do no harm" principle is fully respected. All beneficiaries and affected population are informed about compliant mechanisms, there are multiple ways of asking questions, introducing queries and complaints, which are always considered and answered by our staff. Personal data of beneficiaries are protected according to European standards (GDPR). PAH professional Monitoring Team pays special attention during post-intervention monitoring to check the respect of rights of beneficiaries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes Both the displaced population and returnees beneficiaries equally benefit from ES/NFI services as well as most vulnerable host community. Multi-sectoral in-depth needs analysis is timely conducted immediately after alert. The community members are consulted through focus group discussion with men, women, girls, boys and people with disability on their needs. They are given chance to mention the first priority items needed during emergency for survival recommendations are made based on the assessment. Logistic cluster quickly transports the items to the distribution locations and required items are delivered to the beneficiaries. PAH also uses its emergency mobile team to quickly provide basic needs to the affected population. The project also benefits the beneficiaries through leadership training, demonstration of proper usage of the items, payment of community incentive to field staffs locally recruited.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes This project is designed in line with ministry of disaster and preparedness objectives for long-term plan. The project is part of market system and requires access to provide range of basic needs, creating save environment for returnees. It aims to involve all the stakeholders in creating peaceful dignified return to strengthen vulnerable affected population and to cope with new vulnerability and recover from crisis and build resilience and livelihood activities. The project acknowledges the roles of both community and government in coordination, access negotiation and resource mobilization.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

YesThis project is aligned to country objectives for targeting newly displaced IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host community experiencing new vulnerability. The project is also focusing of institutional capacity building and coordination response and strengthening community coping mechanisms to build resilience

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project focuses on beneficiary’s safety through protection risk analysis survey, safety audit tools exercises and access negotiation supported by UNOCHA. PAH, field security protocol will be used to analysis security situation, use the humanitarian hubs to share security updates in the field before any intervention. Close coordination with UNDSS to receive and report security incidences.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes The project focuses on the use the available tools and resources to give stakeholders ability to continue building their resilience in sustainable way. Appropriate roles and promotion of market information for the households will be provided. Leadership training and inclusion of gender and diversity, training of community-led committees to manage the complaint/feedback mechanism will also support proper handover. Conducting skills training on proper techniques for constructing transitional shelter and energy saving stove, skills on transitional shelters construction and HLP engagement with the affected population will ensure security of tenures for returnees. PAH, ES/NFI will provide cash assistance to IDPs and returnees to meet their multi-sector basic needs (cash-for-work vouchers) but mostly to promote local vendors in CBI interventions done also by local partners. Community-led approach will be at the center of the program with training the community-led committees and leadership structures and promote inclusion of gender and diversity, sharing and enhance information management on the displacement pattern, sharing resources in joint and multi-sector response.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

PAH will conduct joint and coordinated multi-sector protection risk analysis in the project locations. PAH team will coordinate with protection cluster in every intervention and always use the shelter/NFI toolkits guides to mainstream conflict stativity, HLP and referral pathways for GBV cases. Stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data will be done to understand context dynamics, protection risks, new vulnerability level and inform the WASH, S/NFI, FSL, Nutrition, Protection and Education responses based on needs, access, safety and gap findings. This will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, host community and returnee population. PAH will work with HLP and protection partners advocate for security of tenure taken over by other groups. Involve community leaders and beneficiaries in selecting the location of shelter construction, collect data on HLP issues and report to partners and cluster for more coordinated response.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

PAH will integrate and harmonize community engagement practices across all sectors and committees. We will share challenge and innovative concepts with the community to have joint decision-making. PAH will ensure to close the information loop by informing affected community how their feedback has been used to address concerns and unmet needs. PAH’s stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the PAH program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers. Complaints are collected through paper-based system on the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within PAH mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed after complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a time frame for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making with full community involvement. All PAH programme and field hired staffs will be trained on AAP/CCE with support from protection cluster partners throughout all phases of project cycle.
