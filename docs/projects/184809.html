Project Summary

Somalia continues to face a huge internal displacement crisis. According to UNHCR, there were 2.97 million Internally Displaced Persons in the country by the end of September 2021. The displaced, most of them women and children, live in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are extremely poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Overcrowded and spontaneous IDP settlements are often at risk of fire, flood, and disease outbreaks due to improper site planning and absence of viability assessment at
the time of site creation. Chronic lack of DRR tools prohibits IDPs from mitigating natural disasters especially at rainfall when floods damage critical facilities such as latrines and clinics and result in the loss of lives. Poorly maintained drainage and waste disposal systems don’t only cause serious environmental degradation in IDP sites, but also harm health and create safety risks as well as conflict with host communities. In this context, AVORD is submitting this proposal with the aim of improving coordination,
service delivery and overall living conditions of Internally Displaced People (IDPs) in Benadir, Bay, Bakool, Hiran, Galgadud, Gedo, Middle and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia. By delivering a number of interrelated activities to enhance information gathering at site level, stakeholder coordination, camp management, settlement planning, and structure, the project will significantly enhance the living conditions of the IDPs and foster durable solutions to their plight.

1. - Projects activities

This project seeks to enhance the living conditions and access to service for IDPs in Benadir, Bay, Bakool, Hiran, Galgadud, Gedo, Middle, and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia where they are concentrated. This will be achieved through a number of complementary activities including setting up camp coordination structures where necessary, strengthening of the same where they exist, and conducting continuousdetailed site assessments. Compiling of a master list of sites and mapping of both accessible infrastructure and partners operating in sites will be undertaken regularly to enhance service delivery and coordination. An integral part of the proposed intervention is to support community-led site maintenance activities through cash vouchers to ensure the upkeep of sites. AVORD will also implement emergency site improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety. In the implementation of the proposed package, AVORD will closely coordinate with different levels of local authorities toadvocate for local reintegration and permanent sheltering to support a durable solution.AVORD will adopt a systematic approach to site selection with a clear criterion to prioritize those hosting the largest number of vulnerable IDPs such as unaccompanied children, older persons, persons with a disability, single women, pregnant and lactating mothers, single headed-households, persons with chronic illness or psychological condition, families with babies, toddlers or children attending school and persons at risk as identified by protection officers. Data collected to inform site selection will be desegregated bygender to ensure that no group is left out. The effectiveness of the aid package will be evaluated through ongoing monitoring and evaluation processes thus ensuring that beneficiaries access their services safely and in a dignified manner.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1- Mitigation measures to prevent that Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance: 1) Establish Joint project implementation committees involving different fabrics of target communities thus creating a platform where marginalized communities can share their grievances if any. 2) Operationalize a project-level hotline where target beneficiaries can share views and/or concerns. 3) Encourage communities to engage in various phases of project implementation from need identification, design, implementation, monitoring, and handover. 4) Organize periodic mobilization meetings to keep community leaders informed about project activities and outputs. 4.5- Mitigation measures for the risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area. 1) Engage local authorities, community leaders and land owners in tenure negotiations to ensure that consensus exist before proceeding with project activities 2) Ensure that land tenure is secured and no intercommunal tensions exist before carrying out any construction works. 3) Issue land titles to individual households. 4) Work with specialized agencies to ensure that project sites are free from explosive hazards. Mitigation measures to enhance protection levels and safeguard personal data of the affected populations: 1) Continuously assess local dynamics and make plans accordingly. 2) Ensure that access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution. 3) Undertake program activities during daylight hours, ensuring ample time for beneficiaries to go back home before nightfall. 4) Adapt and observe the Do-No-Harm principles at all times to preventunintended negative consequences. 5) Provide maximum protection to beneficiary data and household information to prevent misuse and exposure of target persons to risk.
