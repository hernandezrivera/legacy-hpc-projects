Project Summary

BRAC GBV programme works towards eliminating abuse, exploitation, violence, and neglect for all people within the Rohingya and host communities. BRAC runs protection-centred activities in multiple locations to address various forms of protection needs, restore dignity and ensure well-being and recovery, which will be part of the proposed programme. BRAC also will provide protection services to the Rohingya community to prevent abuse and violence. Currently, we provide Sexual & Gender Based Violence (SGBV) services in 2 camps (camp KRC, 10) and Whykong, Baharchar, Nhila, Teknaf Sadar and Rajapalang unions in host community to increase community engagement in GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response interventions. These activities will continue and be enhanced in 2023.
BRAC will work to strengthen community awareness and will continue activism to address the root causes of violence to transform gender stereotypical practices. BRAC will implement SASA (Start Awareness Support Action), Together, a community mobilization approach to prevent violence against women to challenge the power dynamics between women and men at all levels. To continue our successful SASA! Together, we plan to avail baseline data to evaluate the understanding of the Local Community and Institutional allies regarding Violence Against Women (VAW) to develop an effective implementation plan which an end-line survey will follow. Furthermore, Safe Spaces for Women and Girls (SSWG) centres will train adolescent girls and parents through the Girl Shine curriculum, where they'll learn about six critical lessons- Trust, Social and Emotional Skills, Health and Hygiene, Safety, Solidarity and Visioning. BRAC will promote men's and boys' engagement to enhance gender equality, challenge gender stereotypes, and reduce the risk of SGBV through awareness, sensitisation campaigns, and positive role model interventions.
BRAC will provide support through the referral of SGBV survivors for support and implement activities to prevent negative coping mechanisms. Additionally, religious leaders will be trained and engaged as advocates to prevent SGBV. Furthermore, BRAC will sensitise and educate adolescent girls, women, PWD and LGBTQ members to claim and negotiate their Sexual and Reproductive Health Rights (SRHR), including personal and menstrual hygiene. The Safe Spaces for women and girls; which will provide access to information and services such as case management, individual or group counselling, PSS, safety planning, risk reduction, dignity kits distribution and recreational or other activities to recover from the effects of violence, skills-building through which GBV survivors will receive vocational training on different trades - handcrafts, stitching, tailoring, block-batik, beats handwork and mask making. BRAC has developed six curriculum to train project participants on those trades. At each women friendly spaces, case management support will be provided through maintaining survival centered approach. At each WGSS, a trained case worker will be assigned with three volunteers to ensure reasonable accommodation for person with disabilities as well as each center will be equipped with assistive devices, disable friendly washroom and ramps.
BRAC will conduct an assessment to identify the needs of persons with disabilities in proposed camps and host communities. Based on findings, each center will purchase assistive devices (Wheelchairs, stretchers, hearing aids etc) to ensure meaningful access at women and girls safe space as well as at Men and boys engagement centers. In addition, some services will offer information on linkages to Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH), legal rights, and childcare according to the needs. To confirm safe referral. BRAC will ensure that the beneficiaries learn transferable skills to build resilience which will help them to resettle well in their own country when the opportunity comes.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Due to deep rooted patriarchal culture in Rohingya communities and the rapid displacement in mass, the Refugee camps are home to some of the most vulnerable women and girls, many of whom are subjected to different forms of gender-based violence, even before the displacement and those who have experienced acute trauma by the Myanmar army which caused the crisis to have a particularly gendered nature from the onset. According to the August 2022 UNHCR data, 52% of the total refugee population are women and girls, while 85% are women and children and 16% of households are female-headed. Girls represented a larger proportion (57%) of the vulnerable group and are particularly at risk of child marriage, sexual exploitation, abuse, and neglect. According to the GBV Factsheet, July 2022, Intimate partner violence holds the highest percentage; around 85% and 99.6% of survivors are female. The most concerning fact is that among those reported GBV cases, 89% occurred at survival's residence and 7% at the perpetrators' place. This data depicts the level of risk and vulnerabilities the Rohingya women and girls face in camps. Additionally, the Rohingya girls, young women, transgender, and those living with disabilities are particularly vulnerable to sexual, physical and psychological abuse. Young girls are particularly susceptible to child marriage trafficking. Few studies on host communities showed that, after the influx due to many interconnecting reasons, rate of GBV has increased. With the addition of rohingya people, many of host people lost their jobs, increased crime and insecurity and those have particular impact on gender based violence, mostly intimate partner violence. Increase insecurity and lack of income seemingly increased the rate of child marriage. Thus, considering the ever-evolving nature of GBV cases and the gendered power dynamics in the Rohingya and host communities, there is an evolving and increasing protection need. The continuation with adaptations of the programme will help to extend support which will significantly impact GBV prevention and risk mitigation actions. To minimize the potential protection threats sustainably and effectively, an inclusive protection approach in the selected area/camps is the key to avoiding duplication of services and minimising the service gaps.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Following the JRP GBVSS rationalization strategy, this project will focus on Brac’s expertise in delivering specialized holistic protection services to support the beneficiaries we assist. Therefore, the overall objective of this project will be to provide services to people most in risk of GBV through an integrated approach that would be rational in term of gap coverage and budget utilization. As one of the first responders in this crisis, BRAC will set this project on a clear vision that thrives a broader objectives of longer period to achieve significant impact. On different components, this project will be integrated with BRAC’s other sectors including WASH (MHM) and Health (SRH) as well as with general protection (referral, case management, and outreach) and child protection to develop a holistic service mechanism in the proposed 2 camps and host communities. Preventing and mitigating risk of gender based violence need wider attention with longer term planning to analysis the root cause and architecting an intervention through tailored activities to transform the gender imparities in communities as well as strengthen resilience among the vulnerable group of people including women, girls, PWD and LGBTQ , with this objective, to develop an inclusive GBV service structure, BRAC will focus on a longer term approach to ensure sustainability and accountability to the affected communities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

To mainstream protection, BRAC will address four protection principles including safety, dignity and do not harm, meaningful access, accountability, participation and empowerment. As the programme will focus on preventing and minimizing the risk of gender based violence, from project participants selection to implementation need based tailored activities (SSWG, case management, girl shine, SASA together, MABE) will be implemented. Project participants will be actively involved in operationalizing the overall activities in women friendly spaces and all facilities will be well equipped to ensure equal participation of PWD, elderly and LGBTQ . Furthermore, a well-trained project staffs will be deployed to ensure safety and do not harm principles. For the most vulnerable people including PWD, in absence of care givers, volunteers will be assigned to ensure their safe and dignified access at the WGSS.

3.2 - Gender

BRAC will set different indicators to measure the integration of gender including sex segregate data, trained staffs on gender mainstreaming to address gender needs and gaps, ensure meaningful access to services and facilities for vulnerable group of people including all gender by community outreach approach, self-help groups and role models. To incorporate different gender diverse groups and other ethnic as well as religious minorities groups, their representation will be ensured during baseline survey, periodical monitoring as well as activity designing and implementation cycle. To ensure safety and dignified participation of the most vulnerable population, gender audit and KAP survey will be designed bi-yearly. Through survival central approach, this project will enhance meaningful participation and strengthen resilience among survivors of GBV. To ensure do not harm approach, men and boys will be also integrated to prevent violence against women by transforming power imbalances.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

BRAC has strong safe guarding policy including child safe guarding. BRAC will ensure, before deployment staffs are well educated on PSEA policy and will do periodical monitoring to ensure effective practices and to oversee the functional complain or feedback mechanism(CFRM box & hotline) is in place. All volunteers, vendors as well as consultants will be trained on PSEA policy and will be strictly monitored through BRAC’s PSEA champions in each teams in field. BRAC currently has an assessment system in place to evaluate risk of sexual exploitation and abuse to capture incidents and protect. Additionally, BRAC will strongly avoid employing any child labor in any forms in BRAC’s work. People of all ages will be given due priorities in selection of participants, will be consulted during baseline in project initiation phases and based on need different tailored activities will be developed to incorporate different age groups(including adolescent and elderly) with specific indicators.

3.4 - Diversity

BRAC will encourage different sexual orientated people including transgender survivors to report GBV incidents. SGBV will set up reporting mechanisms with existing case management services to assist any LGBTQ survivors. Also, BRAC will promote the rights and safety of diverse sexual oriented individuals to advance gender equity and the elimination of all types of gender-based violence and try to eliminate stigma and taboos surrounding transgender people. Adding the intersectional lens, all facilities will be well equipped to ensure access point for person with disabilities and particular volunteers will be assigned for those without caregivers to ensure equal and dignified participation. Along with the Rohingya community, this project will also address the need of women, girls, men and boys inclusive of PWD and LGBTQ in host community based on a contextual analysis of their situation.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure BRAC’s accountability towards the affected population, a feedback mechanisms such as complaint boxes in service points, hotlines numbers for PSEA focal, sensitization on right-based assistance in all existing information provisions, train community volunteers on PSEA matters to ensure BRAC’s commitment to AAP and GBV principles and do not harm will be in place. BRAC will engage relevant stakeholders, and organize evidence-based advocacy and capacity building sessions in PSEA and AGD for service providers in other sectors and relevant local government on protection mainstreaming.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC will continue to mainstream SGBV interventions in emergency preparedness and response plans and DRR interventions, through specialized capacity building for refugees, host community and contributory stocks of dignity kits. BRAC will implement activities to reduce disaster risk at camp level and host locations. In coordination with SMSD , CiC, CPP and UNO, BRAC will organize awareness session on hazards, preparedness intervention to mitigate risk of landslide, cyclone and floods, particularly in area where refugees and host communities are unable to evacuate to safe areas. In camps, during cyclone, few of WGSSs are marked as safe space and those centers are well equipped to ensure safety and access equality e.g., disable friendly ramps, wheelchairs, assistive devices etc. are stored and trained volunteers will be standby to support most vulnerable people in coordination with PERU, SMSD & CPP.
