Project Summary

WFP and its partners are currently assisting approximately 100,000 vulnerable populations on a monthly basis in Libya. These include IDPs and Libyan host communities in the three regions of Libya.
In November 2019, WFP started its pilot of providing assistance to migrants in Libya. The most food-insecure non-detained migrants have been identified in close partnership with IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Unit and Migrant Resource and Response Mechanism (MRRM) Unit. The pilot assistance has reached some 12,000 migrants outside of detention centres for the first two months. To protect targeted migrants from risks of being detained or harassed, special arrangements will be put in place for the food assistance. Instead of calling them to come to collect their entitlements at one designated place on designated distribution dates, the targeted migrants receive their monthly entitlements through door-to-door or outreach modalities.
Based on the success and lessons learnt, WFP looks to streamline the food assistance for 12,000 vulnerable migrants outside of detention cetnres into the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 as one of the key activities under the Food Security Sector.
To enhance the quality and the impact of the assistance, building on the successful collaboration between IOM and WFP, including release of the WFP-IOM joint-publication on Migrant Food Security in Libya, WFP looks to strengthen the collaboration with IOM in joint analysis of IOM's face-to-face flow monitoring surveys on food security WFP’s web-based Migration Pulse surveys.

1 - Needs

1.1. Needs for food assistance to migrants outside of detention centresFood security remains a priority for the vulnerable populations in Libya including migrants, refugees and asylum-seekers as conflict continues to generate displacement regardless of their nationalities. An estimated 317,000 people are identified as people-in-need of food security assistance, out of which 38 percent are migrants and refugees. More than 18 percent of migrants and refugees outside detention centres are food insecure. In Tripoli, 33 percent of migrants and refugees outside detention centres are food insecure, of whom 6 percent are severely food insecure. According to the 2019 MSNA, the food consumption of migrants and refugees is lower than the one of Libyan, with only 79 percent at acceptable levels (96 percent among Libyan). The needs of food assistance to migrants outside of detention centres are also validated with their coping strategy index: 78 percent of migrant and refugee households employ crisis or emergency coping strategies to afford food, including relying on less expensive food, reducing the number of meals eaten per day, reducing the size of meals, withdrawing children from schools, or selling assets. 1.2. Needs for data-driven responses to migrants in LibyaWFP and IOM combined face-to-face and web-based interviews and surveyed over 4,000 migrants and over 10,000 Libyan citizens. The joint analysis highlighted the strongest migration drivers as economic reasons, followed by insecurity, persecution and conflict, limited ability to meet food needs, environmental degradation and climate-related shocks. Also, the said analysis revealed that main challenges faced by migrants in Libya: related to security and finding opportunities to make a living, closely followed by high food prices. Over half of migrants reported to have been worried about not having enough food to eat. The slightly different vulnerability levels were identified: migrants who come from East African and South/Southeast Asian countries were found to be more vulnerable to food insecurity than other migrants. The analysis of migrants' needs and migration drivers could be made more useful for programme designing and re-designing if the same indicators are continuously monitored on a regular basis. This idea has been shared with other humanitarian actors and local donor representatives and accepted by them.

2 - Activities

2.1. Provide food assistance to vulnerable migrants outside of Detention CentresWFP in partnership with IOM will distribute monthly food rations to vulnerable migrants outside of detention centres. The distributions will be conducted through humanitarian protection lens, which means distributing food rations to targeted migrants through door-to-door or outreaches to safe community centres. Where the resources are available, WFP will mobilize ready-to-eat rations for the assistance to migrants outside of detention centres. As per the priority agreed on within the Food Security Sector, WFP plans to target around 12,000 migrants and refugees outside detention centres, with a priority in Tripoli, Sebha, Murzuq, Zwara and Misrata. 2.2. Conduct additional data collection and qualitative researchBased on the success gained through combining IOM's face-to-face Flow Monitoring Survey and WFP's web-based Migration Pulse survey for the past years, IOM and WFP plan to conduct additional data collection and qualitative research to further explore the relationship between factors and food insecurity. In parallel, IOM and WFP plan to examine protection concerns of potential food assistance programmes that would be inclusive of the migrant communities.

4 - Protection risk analysis

The key protection risks which beneficiaries may be exposed to through the implementation of this activity are: SAFETY: Presence of armed militia at distribution sites, Inability to control the crowd at the distribution sites that might lead to seriously impact safety and security of Beneficiaries INTEGRITY: some more vulnerable migrants might be excluded;DIGNITY: Beneficiaries do not want registration to impact on their privacy.

5 - Mitigation measures

Protection concerns are integrated into all activities to ensure that people most in need can access assistance in a safe, dignified and equitable manner. In order to minimize the risk of unexpectedly having militias at the distribution point, the activity engages community leaders to improve awareness about the program and manage expectations and provides visibility materials to the cooperating partners that will help them to advocate the role of the program and communicate with armed militias. For the sake of having better control of the crowd at the distribution points, the program, at the design phase, undertakes comprehensive assessments of the distribution modalities (through either door-to-door, outreaches or community centres) in terms of security, capacity and access control. As for the risk of disclosing beneficiaries identities, WFP will utilize the IOM's Monitoring Resources and Response Mechanism. In order to minimize the exclusion risk, the Program keeps ensuring at all the project phases that the complaint and Feedback Mechanism is addressing cases of potential exclusion from the beneficiaries lists.
