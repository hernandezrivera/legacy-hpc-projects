Project Summary

"MedGlobal will provide essential primary healthcare services at the Health Post operated by MedGlobal in Camp 24 as per Minimum Essential Service package (Health Post level) following clinical guidelines of Bangladesh and Sphere Standards. Besides, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable disease will be prioritized. Special health camps and telemedicine service sessions will be organized for catering special health needs. MedGlobal will also engage CHWs in raising community level awareness, promotion and increasing preparedness on health issues and emergency response. They will be equipped with an electronic health system as developed by MedGlobal to strengthen community to facility referral systems.
From the MedGlobal Health Post in Camp 24, Leda, Teknaf -minimum essential primary healthcare services required as per health sector guideline will be provided that includes outpatient curative care, maternal and child health, adolescent health, family planning, gender-based violence, diseases, mental health and psychosocial services, capacity building for local health care professionals, and community health worker such as case-follow up and public health education keeping gender, disability and diversity inclusion considerations in programming. The activities and outputs will be monitored using KOBO toolbox and internal clinic census, and reports will be made available to the Health Sector. The project also intends to provide crucial medical supplies and equipment to other camp facilities. All activities will be prepared in accordance with strategies of the Health Sector to ensure that they are consistent with the Health Sector's priorities and strategies in Camp 24. MedGlobal will prioritize global advocacy through showcasing outcomes and lessons learned from the activities. To ensure accountability to affected population the following steps will be taken:
(1) A continuing logic model planning process based on needs assessments and baseline surveys with the goal of involving the community.
(2) Rigorous audits and budgets to ensure financial transparency; and
(3) Program monitoring and evaluation to assure program quality management.
(4) Protection, Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD), disability inclusion, accountability to affected populations, PSEA, disaster risk management, and environment and ecosystem rehabilitation for addressing the impacts of climate change to be mainstreamed in the MedGlobal 2022 sustainable operation strategy. At the health post, MedGlobal's work concentrates on 51 percent women and includes maternity care for young mothers, as well as family planning. Gender, sex, disability, diversity, and age are reported in the statistics. MedGlobal is already performing needs/baseline evaluations for 2022, with women, girls, disability, diversity, and other age groups in mind, and we will aim to incorporate the results into our next logical framework model and project management cycle. We already conducted multiple COVID 19 capacity projects in 2020 and 2021 including supporting to Bangladesh government health facility e.g., the Upazila Health Complex with Central Oxygen Systems and other medical equipment and supplies to health facilities in the FDMN camps, and we spent roughly $505,000 on the projects. MedGlobal is committed to building the capacity of the health sector partners. In this JRP MedGlobal with collaborate with health sector and SRH working group in building local capacity for reducing maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

943,529 Rohingyas are currently sheltered in 195,964 shelters in 34 camps in Cox’s Bazar, as of 31 August 2022. Ensuring a minimum essential health service package remains a top priority for 2022. However, the periodic onset of communicable diseases in the camp setting continues to create additional challenges to health and other service provision. As the crisis has reached its fifth year, people with vulnerability have faced the crisis differently. As per the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) Technical Working Group (TWG) assessments, 28% of all the people who need healthcare are people older than 60 years. 30% of households with persons with disabilities need to access health care which is more than 1.5 times compared to households with no people with disabilities. 44% of households reported having experienced or expecting experiencing barriers when needing to access health care. Long waiting time for the service/overcrowding accounted for 24% of all the reported access barriers followed by specific medicine, treatment or service unavailable (21%). 75% of households without adult males reported that they faced challenges while accessing health care. Despite all the services being free of cost, 39% of households reported having incurred health-related expenditures in the 3 months prior to data collection. Thus, health service delivery is not reaching certain geographic areas within the camp and certain populations are not accessing needed health services when they need them.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Any case of GBV and PSEA is dealt with proper confidentialty and privacy by the trained healthcare staff at the facility and community level. Any violence against children are to be notified to the proper authority and referral network for ensuring proper child protection.

3.2 - Gender

A rights-based-approach will be adopted ensuring freedom of choice and informed consent, confidentiality and neutrality ensuring standard COVID measures and gender-sensitive services. Training and sensitization will be done through regular sessions both for the project staff and community people. Confidentiality will be ensured in case of gender based violence (GBV) and psychosocial support (PSS) services. Special signage and trained community health workers will be engaged for addressing the needs of the people with vision, hearing, or any other impairment. Stretchers, wheelchairs, and necessary ramping at the facility level will be done for physically disabled patients. For monitoring, a Health Committee (HC) will be formed in each intervention area as part of the community engagement. Developing resilience at the community level, capacity building and awareness raising of the community people will be top priority for disaster responsiveness.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Age specific health care need of the people such as of elderly, children and adolescents are made avaialble through making the necessary equipments and tranied health care workers available at the facility level ensuring privacy and confidentiality

3.4 - Diversity

This project is mandated to entertain all the healthcare need of derverse population and groups of pupulation. In order to do this, the health service delivery mechanism is tailored in the triage facility at the entrance to assess the need of various groups of population

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be ensured during the different phases of the project. At the design and planning phase, the need assessment reports such as periodic situation reports published by ISCG, will be utilized in addressing the community need for support as the documents are readily available to save scarce resources. At the implementation level, consultations will be conducted with the beneficiaries. Special hot line will be set up for providing any feedback at the program implementation level. Besides, written feedback mechanisms will be installed such as complaint registers or complaint boxes will be maintained for any type of feedback. Special consideration will be given to maintaining confidentiality and neutrality in addressing the community feedback and complaints. MedGlobal will work with other organizations, advocacy groups for disability groups, and/or specific age groups, in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Community Health Workers of MedGlobal will raise awareness at the community level against soil erosion and will initiate the community tree plantation. Emergency health response during disasters will be activated immediately in case of any natural disaster incidnence.
