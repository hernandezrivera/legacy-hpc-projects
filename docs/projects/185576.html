Project Summary

SKUS, as a women-headed organization, is committed to improving meaningful access to child protection services age appropriate, gender responsive and timely individual Case Management services for vulnerable targeting unaccompanied and separated children, child-headed households, children in psychosocial distress, children with specific needs, and child survivors of CP as well as ensure 60% girls & women participation,12% person with disabilities and 3-5% GDP and at-risk children, survivors of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation, adolescents and youth through two interlinking components – qualitative case management and community-based child protection (CBCP).
SKUS proposes a mainstreaming approach to child protection that will reach 13,210 people living in Camps 16,8W and Raja Palong Host Community in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh where we are planning to provide services the total 5 MPC. The project is adapted to meet the differing needs of children, youth, women, men, adolescent girls and boys with PWD & GDP. A total of 80% female(child, adolescent and adult), including PWD & GDP with life-saving CP services, resilience building and empowerment programming, which will include Case Management, Structured Psychosocial support services (PSS), CBCP mechanism, adolescent life skills, pre-vocational skills, resilience activities and peacebuilding skills of the women and girls, capacity strengthening of the community volunteer, service providers and stakeholder etc.
CBCP activities will be structured through feedback from community consultation and participation to ensure inclusion and ownership by both the host and the refugee community. The interventions will ensure inclusion of Children, adolescent, persons with disability and the youths. Community-level interventions will include promoting social cohesion and strengthening self-protection of children. Addressing harmful practices and attitudes, SKUS will invest in active community participation and positive parenting.
SKUS is committed to coordinating the response through active participation in sectors by participating in Protection, Education, GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sector coordination meeting, training, survey on relevant issues, various messages disseminating on behalf of sector, sharing protection gap and needs, submitting 5W and Sitrap report timely and other working groups to ensure meaningful access, prioritization of safety and integration of services. The services offered will be in line with CPMS, interagency tools and SOPs, and will be ensured through structured training enhancing competencies in providing quality gender-responsive and timely services.
SKUS will undertake several assessments such as baseline, midterm, impact assessment, and will collect disaggregated data including people with disability and gender diverse population. SKUS will also use Washington Questionnaire to better understand the need and requirement of people with disability. SKUS will set up CPIMS to harmonize data collection, storage, and analysis, safe and ethical sharing of reported CP incident data.
Additionally, through community mobilization, a total of 80 % women, men, boys, and girls, including PWD and GDP, will be reached with proven strategies to address harmful social norms that enhance CP among the population and DRR, uncertain disasters or pandemics like COVID-19. Prevention and mitigation strategies, mechanisms, and initiatives to support community-based interventions aligned with IASC principles and guidelines will be strengthened. To ensure the safeguarding of beneficiaries, SKUS will continue to collaborate with the PSEA network to reinforce Sexual Exploitation and Abuse reporting and response mechanisms.

1 - Identified Needs

Since August 2017 approximately 871,618 Rohingyas are being supported in Bangladesh, and among them, girls and boys children represent 52% of all refugees, 4% older, 12% person with disability and 3-5% GDP based on UNHCR population fact sheet (Sep' 2021), CPSS findings, REACH research (May’2021) and SKUS Women Survey (Nov’20). . There is a strong need to scale up structured psychosocial support remains urgent. There is an urgent need for gender analysis focusing on identifying existing social norms and gender related barriers, which holds women and girls to access their rights. An ongoing gender analysis is an imminent need to collect and analyse sex, age and ability-disaggregated data to plan and include activities that explicitly target and support the identified needs and capacities of women and girls. The gender analysis will be done by collecting age, sex and disability disaggregated data. This analysis will help developing guidelines for meaningful and active participation of women, girls, men and boys as well as PWD and GDP.Raising awareness and building capacity of all staff and volunteers on the Prevention and response to CP concerns, Sexual Exploitation and Abuse will undoubtedly ensure equal access to and benefit from project services for women and girls in all interventions. Early marriage, forced marriage, and child labor consequences remain of great concern in the camps. SKUS understands the context and the specific child protection risks that boys also face in the community especially child trafficking, drug fiddling, child labor, physical abuse, etc. The increasing rate in early, forced and child marriages was attributed to financial pressures faced by families, as well as a desire to keep girls safe. Adolescent girls can no longer access women and girls’ friendly spaces and learning centers where they previously received support and capacity-building opportunities.Parents are mostly afraid that their children can fall victim to diseases and kidnapping. Children and adolescents are likely to experience the negative impacts on their mental health due to the loss of educational opportunities and the shutting down of safe spaces. There is a strong and urgent need to implement targeted community engagement approaches to reach high-risk adolescents, particularly adolescent girls and their families, to highlight concerns around early, forced and child marriage, child kidnapping, child labour and the short- and long-term implications of adolescent pregnancies. Considering gender inequality, discrimination and societal norms, there is a strong need to focus that specific gender consideration is reflected in the proposal. Children and adolescents are likely experiencing negative impacts on their mental health from the loss of education and play. Among the Rohingya communities, there is a noticeable change in children’s behaviour since the onset of the COVID pandemic, including mood swings and anger. Like refugees, host communities living in Cox’s Bazar District have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited access to livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services and inadequate infrastructure and markets. Recent assessment findings confirmed the presence of child protection issues in host communities, with 6% of assessed households reporting at least one member under the age of 18 already married or about to get married; 8% reporting the presence of at least one child working; and 52% of working children reportedly engaged in hazardous work for more than 40 hours per week and working with machinery/ lifting heavy objects.The needs of this project plan were determined with the aid of the discussion, as mentioned earlier.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Ensured gender-sensitive and responsive case management and referral services for the identified vulnerable girls and boys who are at risk of abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation as well as early marriage, forced marriage, child labor, child trafficking, and drug caring Activity 2: Capacity building of CP-CM staff, facilitator and volunteer (case management foundation training, basic MHPSS (PFA, PSS), communication with children, Caring for child survivor, safe identification and referrals, child safeguarding and PSEA) Activity 3: Adolescents and Youth (disaggregated by age, gender and disability) are aware of Child Protection, GBV and SRHR issues, have access to PEER to PEER self-protection, receive services including life skills and pre-vocational skills, resilience activities and peacebuilding skills, PSS, Community Based Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CBCFM) and counselling sessions in both camp and host communities. This CBCFM will ensure that complaints are addressed properly, and accountability to the affected population is ensured.Activity 4: Strengthening Community Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) and empower them on child rights and child protection, gender equality and inclusion, child development, DRR, PFA, PSS and gender norms and Community knowledge and awareness of the rights and contributions of diverse groups are increased and the community people play a pro-active role to mitigate and prevent the Child Protection Risk in any types of emergency (like-:Monsoon, flood, landslide, fire or any natural disaster, and man-made disaster)Activity 5: Capacity building of traditional leaders, religious leaders, women leaders, and another key community male/female stakeholders on CP mainstreaming, girls empowerment, gender equity, child safeguarding, and PSEA reporting mechanisms, coordination and age-gender sensitive response in supporting CP staff.Activity 6: Provide positive parenting skills sessions to caregivers and community members (disaggregated by age, gender and disability) based on child protection tools Activity 7: Increased opportunities for mass people to gather information and knowledge on CP risk, mitigation strategy, community-led cultural campaign, day observation and IEC materials distribution. Activity 8: Different individual authorities and child protection (like police, local administration, relevant Government departments, Camp in Charge and camp authorities actors) were addressed with capacity building and advocacy initiatives such as dialogue, workshops for strengthening child protection systems in both camps and host community. Activity 9: Ensure structured and sustained psychosocial support for children (disaggregated by age, gender and disability)
