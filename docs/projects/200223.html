Project Summary


Lebanon has been facing hardship on the social, political and economic levels since October 2019, which has led to a severe deteriorxation of living circumstances. The situation continues to decline to date and in a recent report published by the World Bank in May 2021, Lebanon was described as going through a “deliberate depression” that is expected to impact the wellbeing of residents directly and severely, and therefore raised the alarm on the importance of delivering essential public services (Lebanon Economic Monitor, 2021). Today, there are no recent studies measuring prevalence of mental illness in Lebanon, however the increasingly stressful living conditions are expected to translate in the disruption of wellbeing, increase in the rates of depression and anxiety, and decrease in the ability to access mental health services, leading to longer delays in treatment and therefore increased morbidity. The rate of death by suicide in Lebanon continues to average one person every 2.1 days and suicide attempt rates are estimated at once every 6 hours (Bizri et al., 2021). The availability and usage of mental health services in Lebanon faces numerous challenges, including stigma, absent government funding, reliance on restricted national and international funding, sky rocketing costs of mental health services without third party coverage, limited psychiatric units in public and private hospitals, and a critical shortage of mental health professionals. Living conditions and access to mental health services is particularly more difficult to vulnerable Lebanese, migrants and PRLs.
Under its mandate to provide quality mental health services, and aligned with the sector objective to ensure access to life- saving and life- sustaining health services for the most vulnerable, Embrace proposes strengthening and sustaining the operations of its two major programs the National Lifeline and the Embrace Community Mental Health Center EMHC that work to serve all individuals and are particularly crucial to the quality of life of vulnerable Lebanese, migrants and PRLs.
The National Lifeline (1564), launched in collaboration with the National Mental Health Program NMHP provides over the phone support 24/7 to any individual experiencing emotional distress or suicidal ideation. Using a volunteer-based model, the Lifeline trained operators provide callers with emotional support, suicide risk assessment, de- escalation, collaborative problem solving, safety and action planning and orientation to mental health and livelihood related services as needed. The Lifeline has been sign-posted in national psychological response action plans, adapting its operations to the diverse distresses faced nationally. Additionally, the Lifeline plays a role in providing integrated and accessible mental health messaging tackling the barriers preventing individuals from seeking mental health services which include social stigma, negative attitudes, and lack of knowledge of available resources through national campaigns and outreach efforts with front-liners and communities. To complement the life- saving services the National Lifeline offers, Embrace in collaboration with the NMHP launched the National Mental Health Emergency Response Mechanism NMHERM. This unit is a field operation dispatching a psychiatrist and psychiatric nurse to respond to mental health crises and/or suicide attempts with medication administration and hospital admission as needed. The Embrace Mental Health Clinic, launched in 2020, offers fully funded psychiatric and psychological consultations and financial support for relevant medical costs including psychotropic drugs and inpatient administration. Simultaneously, the clinic hosts a graduate psychology training program for students completing their final year of education to build their capacity in practical skills. In this way, the students are offered affordable training sites and are part of cost- effective mental health interventions.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

To strengthen and sustain the National Lifeline, project activities include: - Conducting over 100 hours of individual and group supervision per month with Lifeline operators for emotional and procedural assessment.- Building the capacity of 90 new community volunteers annually recruited from diverse community backgrounds trained to become Lifeline operators. - Strengthening the Lifeline’s orientation network on a quarterly basis of mental health and livelihood community services to serve the most recent needs of vulnerable populations. - Supporting the Lifeline operations with social work response managing up to 10 cases monthly.- Increasing awareness and outreach activities with around 50 diverse populations of vulnerable Lebanese, migrants and PRLs monthly. - Sustaining the NMHERM service in Beirut to respond to 5 dispatches and 3 hospitalisations monthly. To sustain the EMHC, project activities include: - Sustaining the availability of up to 300 hours from psychological and psychiatric professionals offering funded mental health consultations. - Strengthening the capacity of the EMHC to cover financial mental health costs (medication and inpatient admission) for 100 beneficiaries monthly. Medication security is achieved through annual collaborations with pharmacies on fixed payment modalities and drug quantities.Furthermore, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA training is rolled out on a quarterly basis to around 100 staff members and volunteers.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

In the area of Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA, in 2022 Embrace drafted its PSEA policy, and assigned its integrity officer launching the six-stage implementation phase. The phases take PSEA from policy development, staff and volunteer training and through to system and structure changes to ensure its integration into operations. Additionally, to protect staff from aspects of exploitation and/or abuse Embrace is planning to develop a staff care policy that focuses on retention plans revolving around capacity building, personal benefits and self- care practices at work in light of ongoing economic and social distress. With beneficiaries, staff and volunteers, Embrace invests in sustaining equitable social representation among genders and populations by studying the trends in recruitment and access to services and driving efforts of change to address the gaps, such as increasing men's awareness to mental health in response to low help seeking behaviour among men.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Both programs the National Lifeline and the EMHC are equipped with personalised data collection software that allows target reporting desegregated by various indicators including age, gender and cohort. Along with data collection from service access, Embrace implements feedback mechanism tools such as quality assurance calls and self- satisfaction and symptom severity scale rating. These tools are completed by beneficiaries themselves, capturing lived experiences on access to services and treatment protocols linked to Embrace and external mental health and livelihood community services. All this data is analysed anonymously and reported publicly on a monthly basis sharing valuable information on the profiles of individuals accessing services, the adjustments made to services from beneficiary inclusion in project development and the remaining gaps in the field of mental health.
