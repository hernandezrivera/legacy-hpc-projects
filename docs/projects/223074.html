Project Summary

NRC protection programs: Protection from Violence (general protection) and Information Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA, legal protection) will provide comprehensive services to the IDPs, returnees and the host communities in conflict affected communities in Borno, Adamawa and Plateau States. The NRC protection intervention seeks to support the affected people to enjoy their right to freedom, violence, and deliberate deprivation. Through Information, Counselling, and Legal Assistance (ICLA) and General Protection programs, seeks to address the needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), host communities, returnees, and other vulnerable populations in Northeast Nigeria. The project focuses on increasing access to protection services, basic essential services, and securing Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights by improving tenure security for conflict-affected populations.
As the co-lead of the HLP Sub-Sector, NRC aims to coordinate and enhance HLP services and structures for target communities through coordination meetings, community engagement, and tailored initiatives. Logistical support will be provided to dispute resolution centers and land ministries to enable their effective functioning. Capacity-building initiatives will enhance the knowledge and skills of local authorities and community leaders on HLP and collaborative dispute resolution, ensuring they can uphold the basic human rights of IDPs, returnees, and host communities.
Challenges such as the absence of civil administration in hard-to-reach Local Government Areas (LGAs) and the ongoing activities of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) continue to impede access to essential services, particularly for physically disabled and other vulnerable groups. The Borno State Government's camp closure and return plan has placed additional pressure on facilities and services in both return areas and integration locations, reducing access for IDPs and returnees. Reports of evictions and HLP violations are increasing, with vulnerable households at heightened risk.

1 - Needs

For the last 15 years, the protracted humanitarian crisis in Northeast Nigeria has remained profound, with persistent protection concerns. According to the Nigeria 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview, over 7.9 million people across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states face severe protection concerns with an estimated 2.2 million displaced persons as of March 2024.Access to HLP rights remains a significant challenge for most displaced persons in Northeast Nigeria. Over 1.4 million people across the BAY states need HLP support, as access to adequate housing, land, and property rights continues to be limited. The HLP issues faced by displacement affected persons includes precarious tenure, forced eviction, inadequate housing, forced relocation, destruction of HLP assets, encroachment on their land, boundary disputes, and conflicting ownership claims, among various other issues. Out of the 1.4 million people not able to fully enjoy their HLP rights, 33% are IDPs, 28% returnees, and 39% host community members. Majority of the displaced population (82%) do not have any form of written agreement with their landlords, rendering them vulnerable to evictions, and exploration. Accessible and effective mechanisms to address HLP issues are crucial for securing durable solutions. HLP rights play a foundational role in enabling former IDPs to secure housing, engage in farming, earn income, and access food. The closure of camps in Borno State has led to a significant rise in HLP issues. By mid-2024, 17 IDP camps in Jere, Konduga, Ngala, and the Maiduguri had been closed, affecting 35,522 households, or approximately 166,775 individuals. The Borno State Government (BSG) has issued directives to close all official IDP camps by the end of December 2024 as part of its relocation programme. Relocated IDPs often face insecurity and risks to their physical safety, including threats of abduction, extortion by non-state armed groups, and even fatal attacks. In March 2024, nearly 319 resettled women were abducted by Boko Haram gunmen. The search for livelihoods, such as farming, wood collection, or traveling to urban centres for essential services, frequently forces these communities to venture beyond areas protected by security forces. The relocation policy has been further reinforced by the UN’s World Food Programme, which has halted food distribution in the camps, leaving IDPs with few alternatives other than relocation. Those who choose not to relocate are often compelled to rent houses in their places of displacement, facing threats of eviction due to their inability to pay rent. Meanwhile, returnees to their places of origin frequently discover that their land has been occupied or encroached upon by family members or neighbours. Without proof of ownership, returnees have limited formal options for reclaiming their land and property, which weakens their security of tenure.From January to October 2024, NRC were able to survey 6,352 (3,963 males and 2,390 females) households using the protection monitoring household assessment from Adamawa and Borno states covering six local government areas (LGAs); Borno: Maiduguri and Jere, Adamawa: Madagali, Michika, Numan and Yola South. Out of the total households interviewed, 62% are IDPs, 83% IDP returnees and 36% from the host community. 9% of those NRC interviewed reported occurrences of protection incidents from the last six months when they were interviewed. The highest incident reported in Borno were attacks on civilians and other unlawful killings and attacks on civilian infrastructures, followed by theft, extorsion, eviction or destruction of personal property while in Adamawa, the top two protection incidents reported were theft, extorsion, eviction or destruction of personal property. The data still suggests that protection risks are eminent thus it require protection interventions.

2 - Activities

The NRC Protection from Violence (PfV), the general protection component of NRC will deliver case management services, individual protection assistance, internal and external referrals, and capacity-building for key stakeholders through training on protection-related topics. Additionally, the team will conduct protection monitoring and work to establish or enhance community-based protection structures. NRC will provide case management services, offering basic psychological first aid based on individual assessments, as well as tailored ad-hoc support to address specific needs or referrals to NRC’s other internal services or external partners in the area. Individual protection assistance (IPA) will also be provided, which is a one-time support linked directly to the individual's assessed protection needs and intended to achieve a clear protection outcome. NRC will establish or strengthen community-based protection networks through training to enhance their structures and committees, focusing on protection risks, vulnerabilities, and identifying individuals with specific needs. Additional community stakeholders will also receive protection training. NRC will carry out protection monitoring to assess and address the immediate needs of affected populations. The team will follow the harmonized protection monitoring framework developed by the Protection Sector Technical Working Group on Protection Monitoring, of which NRC is an active member. NRC's ICLA (Information, Counseling, and Legal Assistance) program, the legal protection component of NRC will establish local coordination points through partnerships with local organizations, particularly in areas of return and relocation. Leveraging the HLP Sub-Sector, the program will enhance the capacity of existing dispute resolution mechanisms—both formal and informal—by providing material and technical support to effectively address HLP disputes in these areas. Key activities will include the following;1) Awareness and Information Dissemination2) Conduct information sessions and awareness campaigns on HLP rights and legal identity to empower affected communities with knowledge.3) Counseling Services4) Provide tailored counseling on HLP and legal identity issues to individuals and households, offering guidance on their rights and available resources.5) HLP Documentation Support6) Assist beneficiaries in processing critical HLP documentation, such as certificates of occupancy, for returnees and IDPs allocated land. This will ensure secure land tenure and prevent future disputes, with special attention given to those receiving permanent shelter support from Shelter partners to promote durable solutions.7) Monitoring and Advocacy8) Continuously monitor informal settlements to identify and mitigate risks of forced evictions, ensuring proactive measures are in place to address potential incidents.9) Capacity Building for Line Ministries and Legal Structures10) Strengthen the capacity of line ministries and legal identity structures by providing material, logistical support, and technical training to enhance their responsiveness to HLP and legal identity issues.11) Dispute Resolution Center Establishment12) Propose the establishment of a dedicated dispute resolution center within relevant line ministries, supported with donated resources and equipment to ensure efficient and fair adjudication of HLP disputes.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRC will uphold core protection mainstreaming standards throughout the project. It will ensure that the proposed interventions prioritize the safety and dignity of target populations while preventing harm. NRC will make sure that all demographic groups are involved in the community engagement and governance processes to ensure better representation. Special efforts will be made to encourage and support the participation of women, youth, older persons, and individuals with disabilities. NRC will closely coordinate with community representatives to identify needs and refer individuals to appropriate services. Additionally, protection and GBV will be integrated into site improvement and maintenance activities, ensuring that specific population groups have appropriate access to communal services. Infrastructure and services will be enhanced to include accessibility and inclusion features. NRC has developed a Safe and Inclusive Programming (SIP) Action Plan and will provide training to project staff to strengthen their capacity to apply SIP principles at all stages of the program. Furthermore, NRC will ensure that any community-based structures it collaborates with receive training on safeguarding and basic protection principles as part of their induction.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) draws inspiration from the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) definition of accountability and views Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) as a comprehensive process that revolves around using power responsibly, ensuring the perspectives and needs of the people we aim to assist are taken into account, and holding ourselves accountable to them. As part of this commitment to accountability, NRC ensures that the communities and individuals benefiting from its services have accessible and efficient means to provide feedback, raise concerns, or submit complaints through the establishment of a Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM). In line with NRC's principles of transparency and responsiveness, the Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism is an essential component that guarantees the voices of beneficiaries are heard, and their concerns are addressed in a timely and constructive manner. This mechanism allows beneficiaries and community members to submit complaints, suggestions, or requests for clarification, ensuring that their feedback is considered and acted upon in a manner that strengthens the quality and relevance of NRC’s interventions. As part of the CFRM, NRC has set up various avenues through which individuals can communicate their feedback or concerns. These include physical complaints and suggestion boxes located at project sites, which provide a straightforward and confidential way for individuals to express their views. In addition to the physical boxes, NRC has established more diverse and accessible channels such as dedicated hotlines, email addresses, and opportunities for face-to-face consultations between beneficiaries and NRC staff. These channels have been thoughtfully designed to ensure that all beneficiaries, including those with disabilities, can access them without barriers, maintaining inclusivity in line with NRC’s values of equal participation and non-discrimination.
