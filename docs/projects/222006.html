Project Summary

Women and girls remain at risk of gender-based violence as they try to undertake their daily activities. GBV poses a significant concern in the community, as evidenced by the national prevalence study on violence against women and men conducted in 2022. According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan for 2024, approximately 8.5 million people require humanitarian assistance, including 2.7 million people mainly women and girls are at risk of GBV and in need of GBV services. Malakal and Canal Pigi are among the most affected areas with Malakal particularly burdened by the recent influx of half a million individuals from Sudan since April 2023. This massive movement, primarily comprising of South Sudanese returnees entering through Renk, has placed significant stress on the already strained resources and services in these areas. Upper Nile State, with Malakal at its centre, faces a compounded humanitarian crisis due to returnees from the ongoing Sudan crisis, newly displaced persons from Jonglei, and long-term internally displaced persons from a decade of civil conflict. These factors exacerbate the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV. There are extremely high risks of sexual violence and of exploitation as women and girls are displaced, in transit, in temporary shelters, and while awaiting visas at border crossings, and as the prices of basic goods including food, water, and fuel has continued to rise. Help seeking and reporting of GBV incidents is highly affected by cultural barriers. Conflict Related to Sexual Violence has also thrived in a protracted conflict setting. Safety audit exercise conducted jointly by IMC and GBV working group in Malakal in June 2024 shows increased protection risks affecting returnees. Issues identified in the safety audit included: Teenage pregnancies, risks of physically violence, sexual exploitation as risk of child labour. In addition, inadequate means of livelihood, which has caused food insecurity, has increased women and girls' vulnerability to harmful coping mechanisms such as exploitative survival-driven transactional sex and exposure to other forms of GBV such as forced marriage and/or early marriage. The Humanitarian needs Overview and Response plan for 2024 further points out that Canal Pigi County hosting internally displaced persons and returnees/refugees from the Sudan crisis faces acute needs in several sectors, including GBV, requiring integrated humanitarian responses. GBV response services are urgently needed in Canal Pigi, as indicated by the findings. The assessments revealed significant emotional and social challenges among the community, with high levels of fear (71%) economic worries (58%), anger (57%) and hopelessness (57%). The Gender Based Violence (GBV) Area of Responsibility (AoR) in South Sudan and service providers have received reports of surging incidents of GBV, including sexual violence, particularly against internally displaced persons (IDPs) fleeing from one state to another and when homes are being looted, as well as an increased number of domestic violence cases. A significant number of Adolescent girls are attending activities within Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) run by in IMC Malakal and Canal Pigi. These are out of school because their studies have been interrupted by conflict which poses increased risk of forced marriage and teenage pregnancies. Key to note is that most of these girls are teenage mothers and not in any marriage. Further, there is significant need to enhance local MOGCSW to ensure case management and timely and survivor-centered response and prevention services.

2. Project activities

This project seeks to build on existing initiatives towards addressing the above needs. The funding will scale up the current GBV prevention and response interventions supported by IMC in the proposed project locations. To enhance access to services for women and girls that have experience GBV, IMC will provide timely, quality and coordinated case management services in line with the Inter-Agency GBV Case Management Guidelines for survivors of Gender based violence. Case management services will be provided by trained caseworkers with referral for medical care, including clinical management of rape (CMR) services, to IMC, and consent-based referral to other partners for multisectoral services. Emergency cash assistance will be provided on an ad hoc basis to GBV survivors, to meet immediate needs and support referrals, as determined by needs assessments. To ensure access to multisectoral services for survivors through a functional referral system, IMC will actively participate in GBV coordination systems including sub cluster meetings, GBV working group and GBV AoR. To strengthen support for the high numbers of teenage mothers, case management services will be provided to strengthen help seeking behavior among teenage mothers as well as support them access health and PSS services that may be critical for them. Further, IMC intends to strengthen integration between Nutrition and GBV specifically through ensuring that the GBV case worker will provide case management and information sessions at the nutrition sites once a week. Women and girls at-risk of GBV will participate in activities at the women and girls’ safe spaces. 4 WGSS will be supported through this project to increase access to information and support for women and girls in the targeted location. Further, IMC will continue to work closely with other sectors providing integrated GBV, Nutrition, Health and MHPSS services to deliver information at the WGSS as well as provide basic services as may be applicable for the sectors. Open and closed group PSS sessions will be conducted at the WGSS. Structured PSS sessions will be delivered at the WGSS. IMC will deliver activities tailored to girls’ ages and needs using the Girl Shine Life Skill Curriculum (IRC 2018). The sessions enhance safety for adolescent girls and empower them to lead more fulfilling lives that are free of violence. To ensure age-appropriate conversations, there will be groups of young adolescents (10 -14years) and older adolescents (15 – 19 years). Community leaders and Care givers will be engaged to enhance support for the girls at home and in the community. 14 bimonthly sessions will be delivered to each group of 25 male or female care givers. To prevent GBV and promote lasting community change, the project will engage community leaders including women leaders using the IMC tool kit. Female and male community leaders will be engaged in a phased 7-day training that covers sessions on GBV response, GBV risk mitigation and transformative change. GBV prevention activities and GBV information sessions will be conducted in the targeted locations. Community mobilisation activities aimed at changing behaviours and attitudes that perpetuate gender inequality and violence against women and girls will be conducted mainly through community conversation in mapped community spaces. IMC will provide various targeted trainings to key GBV actors – the frontline responders such as the GBV case workers, women and girls safe space committees, Community structures supporting GBV prevention initiatives (social norm change and information sessions), health and Nutrition staff, among others. The trainings will focus on GBV concepts, GBV referral pathways. To promote risk mitigation, a multi-sectoral safety audit will be conducted biannually. During implementation, IMC will regularly report the implementation status of GBV interventions on 5W's and resources mobilized on Financial Tracking Services.
