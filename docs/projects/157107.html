Project Summary


The intervention aims to link to the strategic objectives of the HRP 2017-2019 by providing assistance during or in the aftermath of any disaster, provide displaced populations and host communities with basic services and improve the nutrition of vulnerable residents in the targeted areas. The main focus is on providing basic health and nutrition services for both IDP and host communities in the program areas and providing a response in case of emergencies in the target area if they occurring program implementation.
In each location the activities will focus on improving the health and nutritional status of the beneficiaries. The activities will continue to support the nutritional activities at the chosen locations based on on CMAM approaches, using well trained nutrition staff. This will include the screening and treatment of children under five for acute malnutrition as well pregnant and lactating mothers in the host and IDP communities.
The program will provide SAM and MAM treatment services through fixed and outreach centres using OTP and SFP programs respectively. There will also be the provision of systemic treatment of antibiotic and vitamin supplementation to all the children who need it. For prevention and to raise awareness routine nutritional education and IYCF counselling will be provided during clinic days.
For health facilities will be provided with basic preventive and curative services to treat common ailments .This provision of basic health services will include immunization for all children, referral of complicated cases and provision of reproductive care.
Concern will ensure protection for its beneficiaries by providing ensuring that all the staff, partners engaged in the
Delivery of services sign Concern participants Protection policy (PPP) and strictly implemented.
A key component of this project is the training of the health workforce and each cadre of staff will be provided with relevant on the job training as well as any other additional trainings which will be beneficial to their role. Care will also be taken to ensure that none of the activities implemented negatively affect the environment. This means the project will use ensure proper disposal of waste products from the service delivery such as empty therapeutic and supplementary sachets will be returned to the services delivery points for proper disposal.
As part of improving sustainability, the project will collaborate with WHO and the NMSF for the procurement of medicine and medical supplies. This will shorten the delivery time frame as well as use local structures to facilitate delivery

Needs

West Kordofan is one of the states with the highest levels of child mortality due to protracted conflicts and the influx of refugees.According to national statistics, access to basic health services continues to be a challenge for the conflict-affected communities with functional hospital coverage at 40%; only 48% and only 35% have recommended number and type of health workers (WHO). The state has the highest number of food insecure households in Sudan at 20 % of the State’s total population.T he nutrition situation in South and West Kordofan remains fragile, with high risks of mortality and morbidity among children below five due to malnutrition. Surveys have indicated GAM rates in South and the West Kordofan States registering GAM rates above 15% emergency threshold. In West Darfur, 41% of the population lives in IDP camps or hard-to-reach areas, limiting access to basic health services. Health services that are available, often do not meet quality standards. According to the WHO, between 60% and 83% of all health facilities in West Darfur provide these services. Malnutrition (under-nutrition) is the underlying contributing factor in about 45% of all child deaths, making children more vulnerable to severe diseases. Under-nutrition puts children at risk of death and severe illness due to infections such as pneumonia, diarrhoea, malaria, HIV/AIDS and measles.Under-nutrition contributes to stunting in 30%-40% of all children younger than five in Sudan. Among children under five, 7% suffered from malnutrition (MAM or SAM), with an equally high percentage of girls as boys. Children from host communities most often suffered from malnutrition 10% followed by children from returnees 7% and IDPs 5%. Among children suffering from malnutrition, 72% were not receiving treatment and in most cases, the parents were not aware that the child is malnourished. In the program areas, the focus will be to provide essential commodities to strengthen nutrition and health services. The focus will be to continue to provide OTP, IYCF and CMAM service as well as the integration of MAM treatment services into the ongoing OTP services. Other interventions include training of staff, community mobilization and provision of EPI services

Activities

• Free consultations and treatment of minor illnesses and provision of Reproductive Health Services (Antenatal Care, Safe delivery and postnatal care).• Provision of essential medicines, consumable supplies and equipment. • Support for Primary immunizations services for both pregnant women and children under five years. • Health education both at the facility and community levels.• Referral services for medical and reproductive patents that need secondary health care.• Training and on job coaching of health facility staff, Community Health Extension Workers and Community Volunteers.• Provide promotion of breastfeeding through counselling services and peer education through Mother Support Groups• Support supervision and quarterly joint monitoring of health services. • Home visits through the Community Health Extension Workers and Community Volunteers. • Support the SMOH to strengthen EWAR System.• Provision of IEC materials for client education on health and nutrition services. • Provide Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) and Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Stabilization Centre Services• Support for IYCF services through routine counselling at nutrition centres and through mother support groups at community level. • Minor renovation of nutrition distribution centres and provision of outreach nutrition services to the refugees. • Training of nutrition workforce including nutrition staff and volunteers on CMAM guidelines and community mobilizationcollaboration with UN agencies like UNFPA for clean delivery kits as well as with other INGO in the location on key activities to improve value for money
