Project Summary

Over the past two decades since 1998, concern by civil society organizations and Governments over gender-based violence (GBV) in rural and marginalized settings has grown exponentially. During conflicts and natural disasters, civilians are at significant risk of harm through violence, abuse or deliberate deprivation, and national governments and other relevant authorities may lack the willingness or capacity to meet their need for protection. In the context of complex emergencies and natural disasters, women and children, and sometimes men, are vulnerable to exploitation, violence and abuse simply because of their gender, age and status in society. The international Organization for Migration (IOM) has been working with local partners the Civic Forum on Human Development and Emthonjeni Women Forum (EWF) in coordinating work on GBV aimed at increasing availability and utilization of GBV services by survivors as well as reducing tolerance for GBV in communities. In some sad instances, GBV has resulted in forced displacement in fear of continued abuse or continued exposure which can lead to disability or even death . This has led to IOM working closely other sectors in health, protection and food security to ensure that GBV is mainstreamed at all levels of programming. It is against this background that IOM and local partners are proposing to implement a project titled Promoting the Prevention of Gender Based Violence among Internally Displaced Persons, returnees and host communities in rural Areas of Zimbabwe. The project is targeted 4 districts namely Mutoko, Mudzi, Tsholotsho and Mangwe targeting women, men and boys. This project seeks to address these weaknesses in an integrated and holistic manner targeting IOM local partners and their networks.
Project summary (objectives)
The proposed project is a collaborative initiative primarily aimed at working towards the elimination of all forms of sexual or gender-based violence (SGBV) through various approaches that empower women and girls to end, avoid and escape from violence and violent situations. The project will support implementation of activities in the below proposed areas of Intervention.
• Overall Objective: To increase access to sexual and reproductive health and rights for youth, women and men.
• Specific Objective: To strengthen effective engagement of civic groups and relevant stakeholders in understanding women rights as a basis for preventing sexual and gender based violence (GBV) in rural areas of Zimbabwe.
• Outcome: i) Improved coordinated gender-sensitive referral platforms for improved monitoring, documentation and reporting on violations of vulnerable women and girls’ rights. ii) Increased access to and utilization of better quality health and essential services that respond to the different needs of poor women, girls, men, and boy. iii) Platforms for strengthening action based learning on GBV prevention and responsive mechanisms are strengthened.

Needs

The proposed project action is relevant to the needs and constraints that are being experienced at the provincial and districts level that include: i) limited awareness on women and gender rights ii) limited capacity by local institutions such as the Victim Friendly Unity (VFU) and Child Care Workers to effectively respond to GBV and violation of girls and women rights iii) limited confidence by local communities on existing referral pathways on GBV, women and girls rights iv) Limited institutional capacity of CSOs and CBOs to advance the rights and interests of women, girls and boys v) limited coordination of various actors and networks involved in GBV prevention and protection. For instance, women consulted during the designing of the concept note have alleged that, when they sought assistance from the police they were told to go back home and handle the domestic dispute within the family which had discouraged them from seeking further assistance from the police VFU. Limited confidence in the legal system has resulted in underreporting of sexual and GBV cases. Women, the main victims of GBV, have many reasons for not reporting incidences of violence as legal authorities seldom take appropriate action, many women are unaware of their legal rights and women may be victimized, either by insensitive, accusatory questions or by actual assault. Another constraint has been ascertaining the extent of gender based violence lies in the failure of health care facilities and police to consistently record data on violence against women, the sex of the perpetrators and the relationship of the abuser to the victim. The 4 districts do not have facilities and infrastructure for hosting victims of GBV when they await legal assistance and psychological support which has forced them to stay with the alleged perpetrators. The action is relevant to the needs at the community level. as recent evidence strongly suggests that sexual and GBV experienced by women takes place mostly within the privacy of their homes and, to a large extent, has contributed to a culture of silence as regards the health consequences and, in particular, the reproductive and sexual health ramifications of gender-based violence in the lives of girls and women.Despite the prevailing challenges the action will build on the opportunities been presented by the Government which has expressed a desire to work with civic groups and leverage for the expansion and strengthening of local response mechanisms. The project is based on the premise that local CSOs and CBOs can play a critical role in providing a range of primary preventive and responsive activities in preventing sexual and GBV. The action will address challenges in the access of information flow will be addressed through the establishment of mobile clinics which will be responsible for the provision of regular knowledge and information sharing platforms and sharing of fact sheets and stories of change. The project will ensure that both horizontal and vertical linkages and relationships of CSOs and CBOs are strengthened and knowledge products generated are shared and disseminated.

Activities

• Conduct training of community leaders in GBV prevention and SRHR issues, methods and community based response mechanisms. • Facilitate community re-integration of victims of GBV, through case management, psychosocial care, educational/vocational training. • Establish mutual learning and support platforms for gender and SRHR for young women, girls and boys. • Conduct learning platforms with institutions and individual actors for exchange of best practices on GBV protection and prevention. • Facilitate hosting of mobile clinics to facilitate provision of counselling, psychosocial care and referral pathways. • Convening local learning platforms to exchange best practices. • CBO and community awareness raising workshops on monitoring, documentation and referral platforms.• Development of self-learning materials on gender, SRHR and women and girls’ rights.
