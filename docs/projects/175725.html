Project Summary

The goal of this proposed response plan will be to increase access and scale-up the lifesaving essential nutrition services by establishing 10 OTPs/TSFPs mobile or static sites in Yirol East and Pibor Counties to serve about 191,173 beneficiaries (.i.e. 17,759 under-five years children and 11,351 PLWs. A total of 6,408 children (3,268 girls and 3,140 boys) with SAM, 11,877 children (6,057 girls and 5,820 boys) with MAM and 11,351 PLWs. Further, LiveWell will provide and support nutrition prevention services to reach 23,128 with MIYCN services, 54, 370 with VAS and 27,452 BSFP services for under five children and 8,774 BSFP for PLWs in Pibor, Yirol East and Yirol East counties.
5 OTP/TSFP mobile sites will be establish at Tinagau, Aruopnyiel, Yangtikyom, Machuor and Lekkakedu payams in Yirol East county in Eastern Lakes. The other 5 mobile OTP/TSFPs will be an avenue to reached children under five years in hard-to-reach locations in Verteth, Pibor, Tangajon, Manyedekel, and Tenet respectively in Pibor County, Jonglei State.
The activities will aim at linking nutrition services with the existing WASH, Health and FSL activities implemented by LiveWell in the two locations. The proposed HRP 2021 activities will be aligned to the nutrition cluster’s objectives, indicators and activities for the HRP 2021,
LiveWell will coordinate with the existing Nutrition Partners in the two locations to avoid duplications. These partners includes JAM, & Plan International in Pibor County, HELP International & CUAMM in Yirol East County. We will work in close collaboration with static nutrition partners operating in the project locations for accurate referrals and to avoid duplication of nutrition services. LiveWell will engage and coordinate with UNICEF and WFP for core supply pipeline support for RUTF, RUSF, CSB++and other routine medications and therapeutic rations for the beneficiaries in both SAM and MAM programs.
This proposed plan will complemented the existing health, WASH & FSL project in the two locations in Lakes and Jonglei State. Preventive approaches to malnutrition in under five will be used as part of this response such as; use of group model approach like formation of mother-to-mother support groups, setting up of kitchen gardens at nutrition sites and dissemination of key Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) messages at community level in Pibor & Yirol East county.
LiveWell will strengthen the management of SAM and MAM in Health facilities where we are implementing the World Bank health project in Pibor. All Nutrition staff will be trained on non-specialized protection package to identify and refer children with protection concerns for specialized care.

1 - Needs

• According to an Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report of December 2020, about 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County in Pibor Administrative Area from December 2020 to March 2021 while an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. A further likely deterioration from April to July 2021 is expected to affect about 7.24 million people (60% of the total population) who are likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with 31,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo County (11,000) in Jonglei State (SS IPC Dec, 2020). In South Sudan, food insecurity status is a key driver of malnutrition among the most of the vulnerable households (IPC Dec, 2020).• Approximately 1,392,259 (313,391 SAM cases, and 1,078,867 MAM cases) children 6-59 months are acutely malnourished, and 483,382 Pregnant or lactating women are acutely malnourished in South Sudan (SS IPC 2020). Therefore, the nutrition situation is dire with 1.4 million children under five years (the highest caseload for acute malnutrition since the start of the crisis in December 2013) anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021. Malnutrition in South Sudan has been complicated with high morbidity, limited access to basic services, floods, acute food insecurity, COVID-19 pandemic effects as well as poor MIYCN practices which contribute negatively to the nutrition situation of the most vulnerable groups. Therefore, a continued scale up of treatment of acute malnutrition targeting vulnerable children under the age of five and PLWs in insecure and inaccessible areas to improve their nutrition status is a priority. • The situation in the Pibor county is projected to slightly improve with no individuals in IPC phase 5 during the second quarter of 2021 (April to July), this will not be sustained as the projection in the first quarter of 2020 (Jan – April) is expected to deteriorate with more populations moving back to their settlements with virtually nothing to begin a new life in the face of worsening economic crisis (South Sudan IPC, Dec 2020). In Yirol East, the July 2020 severe flooding displaced local populations living in fishing and river Rhine communities of Dhiandhiam, Bei, Maar, Kotwau, Kameier, Wun-Mageu, Wunliet, Panthom, Donglual, and Banak. The resulting IDPs comprised of women and Children who were observed to be wasted (IRNA report 2020). According to nutrition cluster, Yirol East has a proxy GAM rate of 13.6%. The nutrition situation is likely to get worse and thus a need to provide emergency nutrition services. • The humanitarian situation across South Sudan continued to deteriorate as a result of wider range of compounding natural, man-made, and macroeconomic shocks resulting to mass displacement and limited access to crops, livestock, and markets. This has placed Pibor & Twic East counties in Emergency Phase 4 of IPC report. These shocks have simultaneously eroded the capacity of food insecure households to employ coping strategies, exacerbating already widespread, severe, and multi-sectoral vulnerabilities (REACH Assessment Sept 2020). • In Pibor County in GPAA, 200,067 people face severe food insecurity with 11,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity and thus are in urgent need of food and livelihood assistance. This was attributed to the severe effects of the recent floods, ethnic conflicts and violence, rampant insecurity that left people displaced. Although, there are static nutrition interventions, the displaced IDPs have no access to nutrition services. LiveWell intends to reach them by establishing mobile OTP/TSFP services and provide integrated MIYCN services, establish Kitchen gardens at nutrition sites.

2 - Project activities

This project will support the realization of the Cluster strategic objectives by implementing the following activities; o Routine management of SAM & MAM in under-five & PLWs in mobile and static OTP/TSFP siteso Conduct mobile outreach services in hard-to-reach locations in Pibor and Yirol Easto Conduct Vitamin A supplementation, Albendazole tabs, and integrated with mosquitoes net distribution.o Conduct routine Rapid Malaria test, treat and track for under-five with SAM/MAM in OTP/TSFPs siteso Conducting MIYCN individual and group counselling of PLWs and caregivers of children 0 - 23 months using MIYCN counselling cards. o Training health/nutrition staff on MIYCN guidelines, kitchen garden, CMAM guidelines, SAM-ITP, and NIS capacity development.o Establishing baby mother friendly areas/shades for care and key messages education sessions. o Conduct routine and mass MUAC screening of children in the OTP/TSFP site and community by the community mobilizers. o Conducting project initiation workshop for leaders and community members to support the project. o Conducting community consultation meeting about the quality of nutrition services. o Preposition of nutrition supplies at nutrition sites and Equipping nutrition sites with data collection tools. o Submitting monthly nutrition reports in NIS on a timely basis. o Training of nutrition workers on Malaria testing for children admitted with SAM and MAM. o Establishing kitchen gardens for demonstration to PLWs and caregivers of children with SAM. o Procuring and distributing child-play kits to all nutrition sites for stimulation of children admitted with SAM at SCso Training of health/nutrition workers on GBV/CP referral.o Conducting GBV risk assessment and safety audits for all nutrition sites.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

LiveWell has a strong policy on PSEA. In this project, all staff recruited to implement this project will be given an orientation training on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. Beneficiaries will be empowered to report any case of PSEA. LiveWell will fit suggestion boxes at all nutrition sites to receive complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries in a confidential manner. All staff will be governed and sanctioned by LiveWell PSEA policy in accordance to IASC guideline.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

People living with disabilities will form the key target beneficiaries of this project. To ensure feedback from these groups, LiveWell will ensure that people living with disabilities are part of the focus group discussion and stakeholders mobilization meetings. In these forums, they will be given priority in terms of voicing their opinions and getting feedback from them.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In this project, LiveWell will conduct stakeholders mobilization and sensitization meetings and focus group discussions through which information regarding the project will be provided to the beneficiaries . Through the same meetings, we expect to get feedback from the community members and beneficiaries as well as complaints with respect to the project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Since the beneficiaries form a critical component of this project in terms of tailoring appropriate strategies for a successful implementation. LiveWell nutrition project manager and program team will take issues raised by the beneficiaries will be discussed in a meeting in Juba with other senior staff and addressed and adapted to suit the beneficiaries opinions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

LiveWell will ensure that the population at risk (women and girls) are involved and consulted during the implementation of this project. LiveWell will do this through holding of routine focus groups during implementation to have the views, options and strategies of the vulnerable groups incorporated in this project to maximize their participation and impact. LiveWell will also ensure that the complaint feedback mechanism is working so that community members/beneciaries can report complaints related to the services provided. LiveWell will do this by fitting suggestion boxes, holding stakeholders’ meetings, and empowering support groups of women and adolescent girls to share the challenges, successes and what is not working well with the project. LiveWell will work coordinate and work closely with protection partners and ministry of gender to ensure that GBV is integrated into this project. LiveWell will ensure that train nutrition staff members are trained on the GBV related issues to be able to identify, report and referral mechanism for case finding and management.
