Project Summary

CARE Somalia’s WASH intervention for the disaster affected communities of Somali will serve at least 91,000 beneficiaries in disaster-affected areas of Somalia (Bari, Galgadud, Hiran, Mudug, Sanaag, Sool, Lower Juba and Togdheer regions) over a 12-month period. The program will save lives and alleviate suffering of vulnerable Somalis, prioritizing women, children, IDPs, returnees and pastoral host and agro-pastoral communities affected by natural and man-made disasters. This 2021 project will enhance and improve the following: (a) Safe water (b) Improved sanitation, (c) Hygiene promotion (d) improve on good govern in water facilities management (F) strengthening accountability to beneficiaries to better serve the vulnerable population,(g) increased use of gender-sensitive approach. • Water, Sanitation, & Hygiene: CARE will provide water through rehabilitation of water points and emergency water trucking for vulnerable IDP, pastoral, and agro-pastoral communities and collaboration with local providers. CARE will also provide Sphere-compliant hygiene kits and NFIs to households with malnourished children (SAM cases) in regions where nutrition and WASH activities complement each other. These are delivered to prevent morbidity and mortality and complement activities from the Nutrition and Health sectors. CARE is a leading humanitarian NGO reaching approximately 6.8 million people in the largest crises around the world. CARE’s programming is implemented with strict adherence to international quality standards. CARE adopts a multi-sectoral and integrated approach to humanitarian assistance in order to meet the multiple needs of the disaster-affected population. CARE has access to isolated communities and works through partners, such as Daryeel Bulsho Guud (DBG), WARDI Relief & Development Initiatives (WARDI), and Wajir South Development Authority (WASDA), to reach communities where other international NGOs cannot reach. It seeks to maximize synergies and complementarity with programs funded by donors. The humanitarian crisis in Somalia is one of the most prolonged and devastating crises in the world. Repeated climate shocks, armed conflict and displacement have left an estimated 5.2 million people, including 1.4 million newly displaced persons and 3.4 million children in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020. The FAO stresses that Somalia has experienced climatic shocks for eight consecutive agricultural seasons. The strain on food production and livelihoods has left many Somalis unable to meet basic needs, especially internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees whose savings and coping mechanisms have already been depleted. Drought-induced population displacement has been on the rise, doubling from June to July 2019. 2.6 million Somalis have been displaced by conflict, insecurity and drought, and now live in the over 2000 IDP sites across the country.
UNOCHA estimates 1.3 million children and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) suffer from acute malnutrition in Somalia. Without an immediate response, it is estimated that one million children will be acutely malnourished, including 180,000 children with life-threatening severe malnutrition, between July 2019 and June 2020. Please provide further explanation

The project will target the most vulnerable including women, men, girls, boys,the elderly, persons with disabilities (PWD) from IDP, Returnees and host communities. The project will enhance community participation through community engagements, review & reflection meetings project monitoring, evaluation and provision of feedback using the existing CARE feedback & accountability mechanism among the community and ensure their participation is enhanced. the project will ensure the community members are part of the community structures such as; water management committees, sanitation groups members & communities, village relief committees. the project targeting will take into consideration of vulnerability including PWDs to ensure access is easy for all. The project sites will be situated in locations that is easily accessible to all groups and within reach to avoid long distances exposing others to danger. Beneficiaries will always be consulted and will remain at the centre in all decision making process. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated reportedbased on women, men, boys, girls, age groups, PWD. In order to address barrier in terms of mobility, access, the project will establish user friendly infrastructure i.e. latrines will have ramps user friendly to the elderly and PWDs, the water drawing points will be have access for PWDs, elderly and all other vulnerable categories The project will endeavor to engage all stakeholders in ensuring the right groups and vulnerable groups are taken into account.
