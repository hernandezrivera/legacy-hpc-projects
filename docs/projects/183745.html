Project Summary

The education sector in Palestine has been facing challenges and obstacles as a result of prolonged education-related violations and vulnerabilities, including attacks on schools, movement restrictions, insufficient and/or inadequate, safe school infrastructure, and poverty. Covid-19 and the escalation on Gaza have only exacerbated the suffering of the education system in Palestine. According to Education Humanitarian Needs Overview, 31% in Gaza and 14% in the WB of the HHs interviewed as part of the MSNA reported having trouble meeting essential education needs.
In this context, this proposal aims to improve students’ academic performance and remediate their learning loss caused by the political instability and implications of COVID-19. This goal shall be achieved through the provision of ToT training for 120 Educational Supervisors and equip them with skills to mitigate learning loss. Following the ToT training, the Educational Supervisor shall provide training for 3000 teachers from 75 schools on the same topic (this is based on the result of an assessment on learning loss done by MoE) with the support of UNESCO and the implementing partner. In addition, this project aims at building the capacity of 500 teachers (males and females) on distance learning with a focus on digital skills including virtual classes. Also, 500 teachers will be provided with specific equipment like microphones and mobile sticks to enhance the quality of online teaching. Moreover, 150 laptops will be distributed among schools based on the needs to support the most vulnerable schools, teachers, and students. These laptops will also be used to support teachers when conducting online classes during crisis times. Most vulnerable schools have a lack of ICT equipment which is considered as one of the main challenges and obstacles hindering the provision of quality online education.
Moreover, considering the psychological impact of COVID-19 and the political instability on teachers, students, and their parents, the project will provide psychosocial support through the implementation of recreational activities including theatre, storytelling, dance, and music. These recreational activities shall be conducted through the implementation of teachers' and students’ initiatives to improve the overall psychosocial wellbeing and learning loss of students.
To support educational counselors during the crisis, 50 of them will receive training on digital and transversal to enable them to provide psychosocial services to teachers, students, and parents during the crisis in 50 schools. This training will provide them with technology skills so they can cope with the modern culture and enhance the service provided for students. Educational counsellors are also in need of educational and counseling materials, in addition to laptops so they can provide the essential psychosocial services for students during the crisis and other times. Psychosocial services target also parents as they were badly affected by the prolonged closure of schools and the crisis in Gaza.
The most affected students during schools' closures and other crises are vulnerable students. Therefore, the project shall focus on providing around 460 students with specific learning needs with tablets and providing 100 students with disabilities with assistive devices. To improve distance learning through this project, 40 resource room teachers will be equipped with skills in the development of adapted learning materials for online classes. Other activities shall be implemented to raise awareness on the right to inclusive and quality education for students with disabilities and students with specific learning needs.
Within the protection context, all beneficiaries will be educated on PSEA and their right to report any abuse. Complaint mechanisms should be accessible to everyone and data will be confidential.

1 - Needs

Children, aged 0 to17 years, are 43.5 percent (2,115,317) of the population of which 1,083,720 are boys and 1,031,650 are girls. Of that total, there are 1.3 million children in the school’s system in Palestine . Of this population, almost half a million need humanitarian education assistance. They are the most vulnerable since they reside in the areas most impacted by the Israeli occupation. These are girls and boys who face obstacles to access or cannot participate regularly in learning activities in schools because of restrictions of movement, distance from school, harassment by Israeli settlers, arbitrary detention, limited school infrastructure, and lack of sufficient classroom space and materials. In addition to these education-related obstacles, these are the communities experiencing the highest levels of poverty, limited access to resources, and isolation in a manner that increases traditional and religious practices that negatively impact girls in particular. Resulting from these combined obstacles, these girls and boys suffer trauma and experience developmental challenges, which left unaddressed, negatively impact their educational attainment. For those students with disabilities, these negative impacts are compounded because of their unique need for assistive devices and adapted learning needs.The frequent lockdown and schools’ closure during 2020-2021, forced 1.313 million children of school age in Palestine to stay home, and the majority of the families found themselves struggling to keep the education of their children on track . For the holistic year of 2021-2022, the Government has decided to open schools with full face-to-face learning where more than a million- students in the West Bank and Gaza Strip have returned to their schools after several months of school closure. According to Multi Sector Needs Assessment results, 72% in WB and 88% in of Gaza strip) HHs with school-aged children reported that their children needed catch-up learning programs because of the loss of learning related schools’ closure.The escalation on Gaza Strip between 10 and 21 May, caused significant damage to the infrastructure of houses, primary institutions, businesses, civilian casualties and internal displacement, disruption of humanitarian activities, and access to basic services. The negative effects of the escalation have caused a high level of stress for children due to the prolonged exposure to violence. Students' ability to learn has been undermined as a result of the negative effects on their physiological and emotional well-being, as well as their neurological development .In this context, following the start of the COVID-19 epidemic in 2020, MoE launched the COVID-19 National Response Plan where distance learning, psychosocial support, and infrastructure were identified as a priority means to continue the school year. Also, in 2021, the MoE produced another document “Protection and Recovery Priorities (2021-2022) to identify the needs and priorities including rehabilitation of damaged schools, psychosocial support (MHSSP), distance learning, learning loss assessment and mitigation, inclusive education and protection, and advocacy.To ensure the support to the Ministry of Education in the implementation of its National Response Plan for COVID -19, UNESCO became the lead agency for distance learning by co-chairing the Distance Learning Task Force along with the MoE . One of the main objectives of the Task Force aimed to provide distance-learning platforms/solutions to teachers, students, and parents. Specifically, UNESCO supported distance learning platforms, the Educational TV broadcast channel, and the development of ICT skills of teachers. Consequently, UNESCO supported the MoE in the framework of the education reform in three priority areas, including distance learning, quality assurance, and governance. Policy papers and action plans were prepared on governance, quality assurance, and distance learningWithin, the framework of the Education Cannot Wait for (ECW) project, UNESCO is supporting the inclusive education component and has started its support to MoE through conducting a learning loss assessment to identify learning gaps and to prepare remedial actions to bridge the learning knowledge of students. In parallel, UNESCO conducted teaching and learning activities using sports, drama, storytelling, and music over summer 2021, in West Bank to bridge the learning loss gap in two main subjects of Arabic and Math. Similar activities will be conducted in Gaza during the scholastic year. Also, UNESCO developed 16 videos and vines and a video game on healthy lifestyles and psychosocial support to raise awareness through social media and educational online platforms. . Building on the needs and results from these previous projects and to ensure their sustainability, UNESCO will focus on the provision of capacity building for teachers to identify learning gaps and to prepare remedial actions to bridge the learning knowledge of students. The training will focus the different needs of girls and boy with specific learning needs including students with disabilities to ensure their equal access to education and active participation in the learning process. In addition, vulnerable schools will be provided with IT equipment to facilitate distance learning practices. UNESCO will also continue to improve the psychosocial well-being of students, parents, and teachers. Finally, UNESCO will support students with specific learning needs to ensure the implementation of inclusive and quality education. Threats within the project context will also be identified to protect the vulnerable population and avoid causing them more harm. UNESCO will raise the awareness of the beneficiaries on PSEA through spreading messages on their right to report any kind of abuse. UNESCO staff are trained on issues related to PSEA and will ensure their partners and field workers receive induction on the same topic.

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

UNESCO will be accountable to ensure that a system for the "Accountability to the Affected Populations" approach, which will be developed and will be part of the M&E plan. All complaints and constructive feedback will be taken seriously and handled swiftly. The mechanism will be easily accessible by people and will be set up so as to prevent barriers to access, including language, literacy, awareness of the mechanism, poverty, distance, or fear of punishment. UNESCO is considering several mechanisms to ensure a confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system has established. The mechanisms include a complaint box, satisfaction survey, focus groups and if needed a phone number for texts or phone calls with a focal point for the project, allowing for the submission of complaints anonymously. UNESCO will also build on the complaint mechanism available at schools which is also part of MoE protection policy. The school community including schools’ staff, parents (males and females), students (boys and girls) with and without disabilities will be engaged in the design of these mechanisms and in the follow-up and response to complaint mechanisms. Representatives from women organizations and persons with disabilities will also participate in the development of the feedback and complaints mechanism to ensure boys and girls with disabilities report safely on complaints related to violence and sexual abuse. UNESCO will ensure there is referral pathways in schools to serve complainants depending on the complexity of their case.UNESCO will also build a complaints database to ensure all complaints, results of fact-finding operations and investigations, follow-up actions and contacts with complainants and referral partners are recorded in the database. Also, UNESCO will consider anonymization and archiving all records related to a complaint after a certain period of time after closure of cases.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

This project will be implemented according to the Do No Harm approach in all stages of the project cycle. The project needs assessment, as well as project design and evaluation are based on community involvement and feedback, ensuring input from all stakeholders during all processes of the project cycle. This is to further understand internal social processes, develop an analysis of the possible negative consequences during implementation, and taking into account the needs of all stakeholders. Do No Harm approach enhance accountability and the relation with beneficiaries, ensure understanding the community context and dynamics and facilitate the project interventions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project took into consideration the diversity of the target groups. Males, females, students with specific learning needs including students with disabilities, groups from different ages will all have access to the project services equally. To ensure equal access to distance learning, the most vulnerable children shall receive laptops and tablets with a focus on gender and students with specific learning needs. Also, students with disabilities shall receive assistive devices enabling them to access all learning activities. In addition, psychosocial activities will benefit all the target groups in this project equally. During the implementation phase, adaptations shall be made based on the needs of the different target groups. The selection of the target groups will be based on selection criteria to be developed in cooperation with MoE and partners to avoid targeting those who received services previously. UNESCO will also network with other organizations working in the same field in order to avoid any duplication when delivering the services.
