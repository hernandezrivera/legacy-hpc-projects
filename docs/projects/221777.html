Project Summary

In 2024, Health Cluster partners in South Sudan are tackling multiple disease outbreaks, including measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E, and cholera. The conflict in Sudan and these outbreaks increase the risk of imported cases. Low vaccination coverage has led to repeated measles outbreaks since 2022. Malaria remains a major health issue, worsened by frequent flooding. Only 57% of the population can access a health facility within an hour, with distance being a significant barrier. Women and girls in remote areas face additional challenges due to limited trained personnel and supply chain issues. In 2025, the Health Cluster aims to shift from emergency aid to sustainable health services, aligning with national health policies. This includes expanding primary healthcare, improving immunization, prepositioning medical supplies, and enhancing referral services. Coordination will be strengthened through Technical Working Groups and a multi-sectoral approach, integrating efforts with other clusters like Nutrition, WASH, and FSL. Localization efforts will build local capacity for a sustainable response. The Health Cluster will target the most vulnerable populations, prioritizing women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Response prioritization will consider factors like healthcare access barriers, facility functionality, and the impact of floods. Community feedback will shape project design and implementation, ensuring responsive and inclusive programming. Comprehensive data collection will guide targeted services for mental health and GBV support. To contribute to HNRP 2025, Islamic Relief South Sudan plans a 12-month emergency health care assistance project to improve access to integrated health services for crisis-affected populations in Tonj North and Gogrial East counties in Warrap State, and Kapoeta East & Kapoeta North counties in Eastern Equatoria State. The project aims to support 37,500 vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities in four counties; {8,000 Kapoeta East (2,000 men; 2,745 women; 1,176 boys; 1,961 girls; 400 PwDs); 9,000 Kapoeta North (2,250 men; 3,088 women;1,324 boys; 2,206 girls; 450 PwDs); 6,500 Gogrial East (1,625 men; 2,230 women; 956 boys; 1,593 girls; 325 PwD); and 14,000 Tonj North (3,500 men; 4,804 women; 2,059 boys; 3,431girls; 700 PwDs) out of combined total HC target 181,767 with life-saving health services, integrating efforts with other sectors, CHD, and partners. IRW response design focuses on providing lifesaving essential health services in line with national and international humanitarian standards based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality, and trauma-related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness, response, and mitigation activities will be undertaken with MHPSS mainstreaming. IRW in collaboration with the State Ministry of Health, County health department, and humanitarian partners will support different intervention modalities suited to the needs of affected people, including disease outbreak control and prevention interventions. IRW will undertake the Inter-cluster coordination mechanism at all levels and will work towards comprehensive integration and collaboration with other clusters, including FSL, WASH, Nutrition. Integration of FSL, WASH, and Protection with Islamic Relief response plan will be contributing towards realization of MOH and health cluster strategies. The project collaborates with BHI and iCCM by training and deploying community health workers (CHWs) given incentives to provide basic health services, including health education, disease prevention, maternal and child health services, and treatment for common illnesses. Under iCCM, CHWs will focus on treating childhood illnesses such as malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhoea by distributing essential medicines and educate caregivers aims at strengthening community health systems.

1. Needs

In 2024, Health Cluster partners are responding to multiple and concurrent various disease outbreaks. The ongoing conflict in Sudan and reported disease outbreaks such as cholera puts the country at risk of imported cases. The most recent WHO/UNICEF estimates of measles and Penta 3 coverage is 72 per cent and 73 per cent respectively Due to this low coverage, there have been multiple measles outbreaks across the country since 2022. Malaria remains endemic and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, often exacerbated by frequent flooding events. Findings from the 2024 ISNA show that only 57 per cent of the population can access the nearest health facility in less than an hour, while 44 per cent cited long distance as a barrier to accessing health care. Women and girls in remote and crisis-affected areas facing additional challenges due to, limited trained personnel, recurrent supply chain breaks affecting family planning commodities and essential drugs for clinical delivery assistance and treatment of sexually transmitted infections. Thirty-eight per cent indicated that the reason their children were not vaccinated was due to lack of a health facility nearby. While the Health Sector Transformation Programme (HSTP) is currently supporting 50 per cent of functional health facilities, 18 percent are non-functional. While most counties were devastated by the flooding, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North counties in Eastern Equatoria State, were affected by dry spells which destroyed most crops between May, June and July 2024. This increased the vulnerability of the population already struggling to cope with the cycle of protracted violence, conflict, and cross border cattle raiding from Murle and Turkana communities. Across the country in 2025, Health Cluster projected 5,657,327 vulnerable people in need of essential healthcare services with the 3,089,736 PIN targeted. Islamic Relief South Sudan plans a 12-month emergency health care assistance project to improve access to integrated health services for crisis-affected populations in Tonj North and Gogrial East counties in Warrap State, and Kapoeta East & Kapoeta North counties in Eastern Equatoria State. The project aims to support 37,500 vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities in four counties; {8,000 Kapoeta East (2,000 men; 2,745 women; 1,176 boys; 1,961 girls; 400 PwDs); 9,000 Kapoeta North (2,250 men; 3,088 women;1,324 boys; 2,206 girls; 450 PwDs); 6,500 Gogrial East (1,625 men; 2,230 women; 956 boys; 1,593 girls; 325 PwD); and 14,000 Tonj North (3,500 men; 4,804 women; 2,059 boys; 3,431girls; 700 PwDs) out of combined total HC target 181,767 with life-saving health services, integrating efforts with other sectors, CHD, and partners. This emergency health care response plan will require budget cost of $ 1,500,000 at $40 USD per each beneficiary out of 37,500 individuals in the project target four counties in South Sudan.

2. Project activities

Islamic Relief Worldwide has been working in South Sudan since 2004, providing lifesaving support and implementing emergency relief and development programs. Our work focuses on providing life-saving humanitarian assistance and implementing health & nutrition interventions in emergency setting especially in 7 primary health care units (PHCUs) and 4 outreach sites to support people affected by disaster and conflict in Warrap, Eastern Equatoria, and Western Bahr El Ghazal States. IRW HNRP 2025, will be contributing to the following health cluster objectives: 1) Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations; 2) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases; 3) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. This project will be supporting two static health facilities (Lopua PHCU in Kapoeta East and Athiengpoul PHCU in Tonj North) and four mobile outreach sites (Kapoeta North2 & Gogrial East 2). To contribute to humanitarian response plan 2025 to the affected population, Islamic Relief South Sudan will be implementing the following major activities.Conduct curative consultations to crises-affected people reaching total 37,500 {8,000 Kapoeta East (2,000 men; 2,745 women; 1,176 boys; 1,961 girls; 400 PwDs); 9,000 Kapoeta North (2,250 men; 3,088 women;1,324 boys; 2,206 girls; 450 PwDs); 6,500 Gogrial East (1,625 men; 2,230 women; 956 boys; 1,593 girls; 325 PwD); and 14,000 Tonj North (3,500 men; 4,804 women; 2,059 boys; 3,431girls; 700 PwDs).Conduct routine immunization targeting a total of 4,000 U5 children (1,920 boys, 2,080 girls) to be reached with Penta 3 coverage in the project counties.Conduct reactive measles vaccination campaigns to 1,000 children (480 boys, 520 girls) between 6 months to 15 years.Conduct 12 sessions of Mobile clinics with strengthened referral systems between CHWs and health facilities.Provide antenatal, postnatal care, safe and clean deliveries to 5,000 women by skilled health workers at Lopua PHCU in Kapoeta East and Athieng Puol PHCU in Tonj North.Support 100 GBV survivors with CMR, PEP within 72 hours of reporting, and referral.Procure and preposition emergency medical supplies to the project supported health facilities, BHI, and iCCM.Strengthen disease surveillance, early detection and reportingEnhance coordination, community engagement and sensitization.Provide Joint training for staff and CHWs on iCCM treatment guideline, IDSR, EPI, and rationale use of drugs.Provide 1000 individuals with MHPSS at the static health facilities and in community outreach sites
