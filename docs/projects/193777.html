Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the HRP strategic objective 2 and to respond to the Protection Cluster Priority 2 by providing mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) to vulnerable population of 7 communities in Area C, exposed to high levels of settler violence (SV), settlement expansion and conflict-related violence.
The project is based on PUI’s Mental Health and Psychosocial Wellbeing needs assessment (2020), complemented by the Protection Cluster’s Analysis and by the 2022 MSNA findings. From these sources high perceived prevalence of mental health and psychosocial issues were identified, especially amongst children/youths, Person with Disabilities (PwD), older persons, survivors of violence. Yet existing MHPSS services are not able to adequately cover all community needs. At the same time, social norms, stigma, personal beliefs, and taboos around MH prevent people in need from accessing available services .
Through the proposed action, a series of workshops targeting diverse groups will be implemented, around the central theme of gaining skills to better help each other regarding issues related to psychosocial wellbeing. Diverse groups will be involved following an AGD-sensitive approach. The workshops will seek to facilitate people's ability to speak openly about sensitive issues and addressing the different psychosocial needs and capacities of people with diverse characteristics. The project will reach 700 individuals in the communities of Madama, Quaryut and Burqa in Nablus Governorate, Kifl Hares in Salfit gov., Sinjil in Ramallah gov., Al Jab’a in Bethlehem gov. and Far’ata in Qalqiliya gov.
The project will be implemented in 3 consecutive cycles. The first one will include PSS workshops and community-led MH awareness raising activities. The 2nd will develop 2 series of groups sessions: one with women, supporting participants in shifting the collective narrative and self-perception permeated by the longstanding occupation; and the second through a curriculum addressing the impact of living in emergency contexts for children 12-16 years old and their caregivers. Lastly, PUI will implement a 3rd cycle in which identified key members of the community will take part in a Psychological First Aid (PFA) training.
Activities will be designed so that persons socially and/or physically isolated, or with specific needs, such as PwDs, older persons, persons with mental issues/illnesses, survivors of violence, etc. have meaningful access and are encouraged to enroll. To this end PUI will interact and collaborate continuously with local actors and CBOs to support in identifying and engaging with all groups of population, including the most isolated ones.
It should be noted that this project will be implemented in synergy and complementarity with PUI’s work under the West Bank Protection Consortium (WBPC), which includes the provision of emergency response to persons exposed to/at risk of settler violence as well as tailored protective measures at community level. The communities targeted here are ones identified by PUI, the WBPC and other stakeholders as particularly affected by SV. PUI has intervened in all of them in 2021-2022 through emergency response to SV and/or through protective measures (and will continue to do so in 2023), but they have not benefited from community-level PSS activities from PUI or other actors (no duplication).
PUI’s ongoing SV response include tailored PSS activities focusing on resilience building and awareness raising on MH and psychosocial wellbeing. As such, most of the activities proposed here have been already implemented by PUI in 2021 and 2022 in various communities, and as such have been tested and adapted to the specific context. The activities themselves were designed based on PUI’s needs analysis, community feedbacks and information sharing with MHPSS actors active in the WB on similar topics. This collaboration will be maintained and reinforced throughout the project.

1 - Needs

At the end of 2020, PUI conducted a WB mental health and psychosocial wellbeing assessment. Moreover, from September to December 2021, 5 AGD-sensitive FGDs were conducted with communities affected by SV to map their primary material needs and community PSS needs and services. 1 FGD and 37 door-to-door visits were also led to understand the PSS perception, need, and capacities of the targeted population. Moreover, from July to November 2021, 18 PSS workshops were implemented that provided an additional source of feedback. From those, the following needs were derived: - There are high reported MHPSS needs in communities most affected by settler violence. - Anger, aggression and violent behavior, stress, anxiety, withdrawal, substance abuse, impulsive/reckless behaviors, deterioration of social relationships and bonds, and excessive fear were reported as the most common expressions of distress.- Communities feel underserved in MHPSS services. They reported a lack of specialized support, especially for children, and difficulties in accessing existing services. - There is a reluctance to accept referrals to more specialized MHPSS services, despite the reported need. This could be the result of stigma, social barriers, and difficulties in access (challenges in leaving communities either from fear of settler attacks/demolition, or lack of transportation and road infrastructure). Space to conduct such activities safely in these communities is very limited.- Most people’s basic food and security needs are not met, and thus it is challenging for them to engage effectively in PSS activities. Nonetheless, most participants to PUI’s workshops reported feeling that those were useful and helpful. - Women are the majority of participants in PUI PSS activities, due to the prevalent stigma within men about MH issues. In addition, the needs assessment conducted by the WBPC partners in 2021 demonstrates that the occupation affects men, boys, women and girls differently. Young Palestinian men are particularly affected by military and SV, women and girls are more affected by sexual harassment from settlers (in addition to GBV within their own communities/HHs), and military operations and demolitions disproportionately influence women and children. According to the Protection Analysis Update 2022, PwDs are being disproportionately impacted by the violence escalation in the WB, which implies a greater risk of deterioration of their psychosocial wellbeing, and they face important barriers in accessing MHPSS services. When communities lack the capacity and resilience to respond to these incidents or an adequate response is not provided, consequences include a loss of privacy, safety, autonomy, and social networks, resulting in high levels of insecurity, family separation and other negative coping mechanisms. A gender bias in the provision of humanitarian assistance, has in some cases excluded women from assistance or active participation in the design of projects, compounding pre-existing gender-based vulnerabilities.Moreover, the increase in incidents of demolitions, settler and military violence and harassment, has led to greater MHPSS needs. From January-March 2020, Medecins du Monde provided emergency MHPSS to 69% more victims of violence than in the same period in 2019. Based on their analysis, anxiety was the most common symptom in adult males and boys, trauma (flashbacks, avoidance and intrusive thoughts) was most common among adult females, and sleeping problems and a sense of generalized insecurity were the most common ailments reported by girls. 94 victims of violent events were referred to advanced MHPSS as they were at risk of developing MH issues or illness.According to the MSNA WB 2022, 11.9% of the interviewed HHs reported that at least one HH member showed signs of psychosocial distress or trauma in the past year. Likewise, the August 2022 Protection Analysis Update for WB indicates that the main risks for psychosocial distress and deterioration in mental well-being are the political situation, insecurity and violence, including threats of home demolitions, arrests, night raids and settler violence, escalations of violence and prolonged periods of high tensions.The communities of Madama, Quaryut, Burqa, Kifl Hares, Sinjil, Al Jab’a and Far’ata are all particularly affected by settler violence. According to PUI data, from June 2021 to Sept 2022, they were exposed to 130 events of SV resulting in either property damages, casualties, or both (Quaryut: 50 SV incidents; Burqa: 37; Madama 7; Kifl Hares: 7; Sinjil: 22, Far’ata: 3 and Al Jab’a: 40. In those communities, which total 20,229 ind., 15% (3,063) in average are boys of 5 y.o. and above, 15% girls (3,062), 29% (5,989) males 18-49, 24% (4,899) females 18-49, 7% (1,362) elderly men, 7% (1,361) elderly women and 3% (493) are PwDs.These communities were part of the ones identified as most affected by SV and prioritized to benefit from assistance during the Regional Workshop on Settler Violence Vulnerabilities, led by PUI in June 2021, gathering WBPC partners, cluster representatives, authorities and NGO/INGO relevant actors. The selection was based on the number of incidents triggered by OCHA, as wells on analysis from participants on specific vulnerabilities. PUI has intervened regularly in emergency response to SV in these communities. They are also communities who have or will benefit from protective measures from PUI (now or in 2023) but in other sectors that PSS: FSS, shelter/NFI, WASH, education. The targeting logic is twofold: to facilitate acceptance and enrollment in psychosocial support activities, by approaching communities that already know PUI and its work, and have benefited from it at household and/or community-level. And also, to enhance the impact of the PSS intervention by coupling it with a multisectoral response to SV, thus responding holistically to communities’ needs and ultimately increasing the impact.

2 - Activities

PUI will implement PSS activities in 3 cycles, aiming to support resilience, promote wellbeing and strengthen the capacity to understand reactions to crisis situations and help others. It will target 700 individuals (301W, 29M, 157G, 213B, 18 EP, 28 PwD) in 7 communities: Madama, Quaryut, Burqa, Kifl Hares, Far’ata, Al Ja’ba and Sinjil, who have benefited from PUI’s multisectoral response to SV (FSS, shelter/NFI, WASH, education), but have not yet received PSS.The PUI PSS curricula and SOPs have been designed according to IASC (2007) guidelines with a CB approach, aiming at providing PSS services supporting communities and families, focusing on existing capacities and resources of the population. Thus, before and during the implementation of the 3 cycles, community members will be consulted through different means on the preferred and most appropriate approach and modalities. PUI will include persons from diverse groups within the community in those consultations: women, girls, boys and men, as well as PwD, older persons, chronically-ills, survivors of violence, etc., with the aim to tailor activities to their needs and preferences, including possible adaptations to the curricula. The activities are part of a PUI-designed “package” curriculum, field-tested in several communities in 2021 and 2022 under the WBPC response, but could be adapted based on the community feedback.In regards to disability inclusion, PUI PSS team will map disability-friendly location in the targeted communities in order to guarantee their suitableness for the PSS service provision. PUI will also make sure that all materials developed are understandable for all people. Whenever possible, PUI will coordinate with local CBOs working with persons with low mobility or disabilities. Moreover, all PUI field staff will be providing specific training on how to include individuals with disabilities in humanitarian action. • Cycle 1The main objectives of this cycle are to improve participant´s MH and psychosocial well-being through strengthening their knowledge and capacities around dealing with ongoing distress, and to boost group resilience and reinforce positive coping mechanisms by empowering diverse community members to act as agents of change. It will follow successive steps including 1. Information gathering in each community through consultations; 2. Community Outreach to introduce the modalities of the activities; 3. Group thematic workshops providing a safe space for participants to contemplate and process the psychosocial impact of SV on an individual and communal level; 4. Community-led MH awareness-raising, through the drafting and dissemination of key messages around MH via community campaigns. • Cycle 2The new cycle will continue to target communities that participated in the Cycle 1 through group session workshops establishing safe spaces for participants. 1. Workshops with women: groups sessions (8 to 10) will support participants in shifting the narrative and self-perception that has been installed in their daily lives and relationships as a result of the longstanding occupation. Focusing attention on actions, ideas, stories, will allow them to return to their Palestinian identity, thus strengthening their resilience and coping resources, positively impacting their psychosocial well-being. 2. ‘I CAN’ workshops: this curriculum is a PUI designed methodology promoting psychosocial resilience of adolescents living in humanitarian emergencies. It consists of 6 sessions for boys and girls ages 12-16 and of 2 sessions for their caregivers (women/men). PUI will try to engage men as much as possible to tackle the prevalent MHPSS stigma. The curriculum explores different topics, like self-esteem and self-image, understanding and managing emotions and non-violent communication, social skills and personal identity; and for caregivers around independence and autonomy, conflict management and dealing with risk behavior. Following CPMS and CFS guidelines, PUI will identify the community locations and resources and map existing infrastructure where to conduct activities with children. PUI will seek to engage with local leaders and natural helpers in order to strengthen existing community capacities in CP. Special focus will be put on feedback mechanisms, making sure that children will have access to it to report any issues or abuse. The PUI PSS staff will be further trained in CP. • Cycle 3During cycles 1 and 2, PSS team will identify key persons in each group/community interested to participate in a PFA training, to strengthen the capacity of the communities to respond to crisis situations and emotional reactions, notably those triggered by SV episodes. The sessions will be adapted to include diverse age groups PwDs and other groups with specific needs. The training will be based on internal PUI documents, PFA Guide of the IFRC and PFA Guide for field workers (WHO). These guides aim to provide special training and a framework for people that can help others who have been exposed to distressing situations. The training will include 4 steps, addressing: main PFA concepts, identification and management of crisis and emotional reactions, how to provide PFA, as well as several follow-up sessions to strengthen participants learning and skills based on experience. To strengthen the intervention’s impact and its sustainability, PUI will establish contacts with local CBOs or centers for women/children/youth seeking to develop collaborations for activities design, implementation and referrals, following a mutual capacity building approach. Coordination with CPMHPSS partners will be key, to strengthen effective coordination and referral systems. Should PUI PSS team identifies cases in need of protection referral (GBV, child protection, MHPSS, legal aid), it will comply with PC Minimum Practice guidance, to guarantee a safe and accountable process. PUI PSS team will receive proper training and supervision.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

After PSS activity/workshop, the participants will provide informal feedback on the quality and impact of the session, which will directly inform the design of the next sessions, which may be adapted according to it. This will ensure that the content is tailored to the needs and preferences of the beneficiaries, considering their individual and collective resources. Besides, the PSS team will ensure that the participants, including children, adolescents, women, men, PwD, older persons, etc. can receive, understand and fill a short satisfaction survey. The results of the surveys will allow the PSS team to adapt the activities according to the participants feedback. To be noted that the proposed PSS curricula has been tested in the field and the SOPs have already been benefited from adaptations deriving from beneficiaries´ feedback PUI’s feedback and response mechanism (FRM) is systematically capturing the views and feedback from affected people to ensure they inform decision-making, and close the “feedback loop”. MEAL team will ensure that feedback is dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. All relevant feedback and recommendations will be directly shared with the project management team and relevant staff for improvement. It will be documented and shared in writing, via email, between the MEAL Manager and the program team (in a secured and confidential manner). Decisions and steps taken will be duly reported as well, until the closure of the case. More informal feedback received by any PUI staff in the field will be dealt with depending on its nature: either through oral response to complainants, or through the opening of dedicated case file, as described above. Monthly internal reporting will include analysis of the received feedbacks, as well as during the yearly strategy exercises.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

All participants will be informed about PUI Feedback & Response Mechanism (FRM) and the PSEAH reporting channels. This will enable effective selection of communication channels that suit the preferences of specific groups. For those who can read, information cards will be distributed, sign language will cater for the mute, Hotline/phone and email contacts will be shared. Focus group discussions will be organized within home areas for those who hardly access public spaces to access the information.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI regularly updates its mission-level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy, reflecting the type of actions required on each stage of the projects cycle to ensure that protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. This strategy is updated based on: (a) yearly consultations with the served communities around protection mainstreaming considerations. The last one was conducted in August 2022; (b) Protection Mainstreaming Staff Assessment, which explores PUI’s staff attitudes, needs, and capacities in terms of cross-cutting issues. The identified gaps are highlighted in the development of a yearly Protection Mainstreaming Action Plan. Furthermore, all PUI’s Needs Assessments include questions that aim at identifying risks and barriers to the beneficiaries’ safety, dignity, access to services, and participation. Once the project commences, all the acquired data through the above-mentioned tools, together with the already conducted needs assessment for this project, will assist PUI in producing a comprehensive Protection Risks & Mitigation Measures Matrix, dedicated to this project. This matrix will highlight beneficiaries’ feedback and PUI’s inputs - derived from previous experiences in similar projects. In addition, protection risks and mitigation measures identified through the mentioned matrix will help PUI in further updating its yearly ProM Action Plan. In nutshell, the key intervention areas will be: - A mission-wide Protection Mainstreaming Action Plan is currently available, designed on the basis of ongoing projects. Nonetheless, once this project commences, this action plan will be further refined and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the targeted communities. - Beneficiaries’ feedback and PUI’s inputs will be consolidated in a Protection Risks & Mitigation Measures Matrix dedicated to this project. According to the initial outcomes of the Protection Risks & Mitigation Measures Matrix, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. Beneficiaries will determine how based activities will be carried out/preferred modality to ensure that they are actively involved in making decisions that influence their wellbeing. - Community consultation, through different means will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. - All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. - Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). - SEAH reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PUI regularly provides its staff with PSEAH training. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH will also be disseminated to the project beneficiaries. Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. - PUI’s capacity-building plan will be further updated and adapted once the project commences to reflect on the needs identified through the initial outcomes of the Protection Risks & Mitigation Measures Matrix, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. - Selected field staff will be trained and mentored to act as ProM focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure diverse representation of community members, specific mitigation measures will be taken. Utilizing community-based protection approaches, beneficiaries / communities will be engaged from the planning / designing stages of activities and their concerns will be addressed in a participatory manner. For instance, decisions regarding community meetings or workshop venues - will be taken aligned with specific needs and preferences of the beneficiaries/participants. PUI PSS Facilitators will ensure that beneficiaries are listened to in a dignified and timely manner and that their concerns are timely addressed in a satisfactory way. The process will be continuous throughout the project circle and beneficiaries will be actively involved and engaged at all stages. Communication channels to be used will ensure that people that are traditionally excluded from the social sphere are reached. Considerations regarding the modality of the meetings//workshops will be taken to accommodate people with disabilities, like provision of sign language interpretation, etc. This meeting will allow participants to further interact with PUI regarding their needs. Accessibility to project consultations, assessment, FRM and SEAH reporting mechanism and awareness raising will be emphasized to people belonging in different age and gender groups, as well as to people with different diversities including people with disability. The protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reflect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These enforced changes in modality will also be part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people.
