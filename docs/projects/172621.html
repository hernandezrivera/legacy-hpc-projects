Project Summary

CAFOD and Caritas partners are targeting Gokwe South and North, Mudzi, Uzumba-Maramba-Pfungwe, Mutoko, Binga, Hwange and Buhera districts to support vulnerable communities with humanitarian response initiatives and setting them on recovery path. The initiatives are centred on saving lives through agricultural support and support to food access to meet basic food and nutrition requirements in 8 districts provision of emergency food assistance through in-kind based transfers to acutely food insecure individuals in 4 of the districts. The inititatives will target 30,000 individuals across the 8 districts.

1 - Activities

The project will provide in-kind food assistance to acutely food insecure people in 4 districts; support agriculture production through provision of drought tolerant inputs; training and agricultural advisory services and small livestock and forage plants. The initiatives will include rehabilitation of water resources for productive purposes. In each district, irrigation systems with potential to self sustain post support will be rehabilitated. To help communities manage the changing climatic and weather patterns, DRR and anticipatory planning will be trained along land rehabilitation/reclamation and or conservation.The initiatives by CAFOD and Caritas partners will target vulnerable (men, women and children,elderly and disabled) prone to food and nutrition insecurity due to recurrent droughts aggravated by the current COVID-19 environment. A total of 30,000 people will be targeted under SO1 and SO2. The project will employ accountability mechanisms including inclusive and participatory feedback and complaint handling mechanisms to ensuresafe, accessible and dignified inclusion of vulnerable people being targeted:• Provide monthly food baskets through in-kind assistance [7.5kg of cereals, 1.5kg pulses, 0.75l of fortified vegetable oil per person and per month] or cash [US$12/person/month] to 9,000 people (3,000/district in Binga, Buhera and Mudzi districts) acutely food insecure people in of the rural areas for 3 motnhs Feb – April 2021 and 3 months October - Dece 2021. • Provide drought-tolerant seeds for cereals (small grains (5kg/HH – sorghum or millet) and legumes (2.5kg/HH of cowpeas or groundnuts) as well as organic fertilisers (25kg top dressing) and related trainings to 2,400 households (300 HH per district) in October 2021 • Provide horticulture seeds [sugar beans 2kg , tomatoes-20g; onion-20g, covo/kale/tsunga-20g, spinach- 20g, butternuts/pumpkin-50g, carrots-20g], horticulture production training for gardeners and related utilization trainings, cooking demonstrations, etc. to 2,400 households (300 HH per district). May to October 2021• Provide small livestock as 6 hens and 1 cock per HH and 10 boer/Kalahari red bucks (He-goats) per district for restocking & improved breeds and other inputs / materials (and related trainings) to 2,400 more households (300 per district). Provide community forage seeds and related trainings March 2021• Provide training, extension and advisory services to all agriculture extension officers and 4,800 targeted farmers (600 per district) focusing on good agriculture practices with emphasis on climate-resilient agriculture Januray to Dec 2021• Provide farmers with training 4,800 targeted farmers (600 per district) to support crop pest management, post-harvest handling, storage and processing April to Oct 2021• Provide training to 2,400 farmers on improved pasture and grazing management Feb to Dec 2021• Provide 32 community trainings (4 per district) on agro-ecology interventions such as soil conservation practices, soil reclamation techniques and restoration of degraded landscapes (e.g. moringa / acacia trees, soil fertility trenching, solid & liquid organic manuring, soil compositing, diverse cropping patterns such as rotation, companion cropping, alley cropping, gulley reclamation, soil testing kits, water-shed/catchment area management etc.) March to Octo 2021• Provide training on inputs for water conservation practices (e.g. contour ridging, dead-level contours, infiltration pits, tied-ridging) March to Oct 2021• Rehabilitation of 8 irrigation schemes (1 in each district) March to October 2021

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The Caritas organisations have longstanding and permanent presence in the areas of operation and are therefore in continuous consultation, dialogue and assessment of the humanitarian and developmental needs of the targeted communities. Caritas ensures active community participation, working with relevant government stakeholders, local leadership & community committees such as Caritas committees, Ward Development Committees (WARDCOs) and with local volunteers with attention to participation of women and children. Specifically, the project will use three interrelated feedback mechanisms:Þ Community focal point: (third party person) to liaise and facilitate addressing all the types of complaints and feedback, the focal point person will be a community leader and forms part of the help desk team with project staff to receive, record and address complaints or feedback from beneficiaries .Þ CAFOD and Caritas partner teams conduct regular meetings with beneficiaries and community leaders: to address the complaints and feedback regarding programming/ service deliveryÞ Complaint Office Desk or mobile hotline: specifically, for reporting sensitive complaints and feedback.The hotline number is shared to all stakeholders and communities.These mechanisms will be refined and adapted in consultation with communities and widely publicized through project structures and events. Communities will also monitor project works and activities and give feedback through their leadership and representatives.CAFOD will conduct refresher trainings for project staff on Safe, Accessible, Dignified and Inclusive (SADI) programming and Core Humanitarian Standards including accountability. PSEA policies extend to volunteers and contractors and are shared, including in local languages.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project may negatively impact on the environment through land clearing during rehabilitation activities of irrigation schemes. This may be minimized through restricted excavation to infrastructure servitudes and will cause minimal impact on the environment. Damaged vegetation may be replenished with higher numbers of indigenous and exotic trees and grasses that can be used as fodder for livestock. The project will ensure that there is minimum harm to the environment. An Environmental Management Plan will be developed using the CAFOD’s Environmental Stewardship Tool. This tool assesses the potential environmental impact of different activities across the project and supports the development of mitigatory measures to both minimise environmental impact but also identify any areas where the project can enhance environmental health. These can be practises that curtail the degradation of fields and grazing areas whilst preserving ecosystems, and protecting catchment areas. This will also point out social effects the project interventions may have on the surrounding people and mitigation measures be taken. The CAFOD’s Environmental Stewardship tool is comprehensive in that it not only scores the impact but also suggests mitigation measures accordingly. For pumping irrigation water, the project will not use fossil fuels and will use either hand pumps or renewable energy e.g. solar-powered piped water schemes. The project will ensure direct and active participation of relevant Government Departments including, AGRITEX, VET and Environmental Management agency (EMA) within the project.
