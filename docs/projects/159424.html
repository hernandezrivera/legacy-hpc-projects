Project Summary

Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) proposes to continue, in cooperation with its national partner-NGO on the ground, the provision of education in emergencies (EiE) with the aim of ensuring inclusive EiE for 3,000 (1500f) boys and girls of the most vulnerable households (HH) of internally displaced persons (IDPs) along Afgoye road between K7 and K20 in Kahda, Deynile and Dharkeynley districts, in 30 safe Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) near the learners’ homes inside the camps or in nearby official school structures. The TLS are operated by 30 teacher associations (TA) with a total of 150 teachers (76f) that are IDPs themselves, and related to the IDP communities through 30 Community Education Committees (CEC) with 10 members each that are composed of the TA, parents, and members of the Camp Management Committees (CMC). The project targets 4,000 direct beneficiaries.
The project ensures children of 3550 poorest and most vulnerable IDP hh access to education that otherwise would be excluded from education due to the hh’s inability to pay school fees and due to the lack of schools inside the IDP camps. Vulnerability criteria for selected hh are: (female) single headed hh, minor headed hh, hh with people with disabilities, hh with many children and elderly people, all resulting in little coping capacity. The project promotes the enrollment of girls and disabled children that are otherwise disadvantaged, and it aims at an enrollment rate of 50% girls.
Payment of school fees and access to learning materials is ensured through the distribution of 2 conditional vouchers for education per learner to a) pay school fees and b) learning materials to the guardians of the learners. TLS and schools can receive payment for the vouchers from the project to ensure the payment of teacher incentives and costs for maintenance of classrooms and WASH facilities. The teachers are trained in the operation and administration of the TLS.
Child protection is on a high scale in this project since the beginning in 2016. TLS are placed inside the IDP camps in cooperation with the CMCs to enhance safety of the learners on shorter ways to school and to ensure mitigation of vulnerability against forceful eviction and short ways to school for children, hence protection through social integration into the neighboring community. The temporary design of the TLS ensures 360° escape routes around the TLS for the children in case of a physical attack or collateral involvement in violence. The TLS have open windows and soft construction materials (plastic sheeting) so the children can easily jump out of the windows or even tear down the sheeting to escape. The TLS are equipped with gender separated latrines and hand washing facilities. Learners shall also receive lessons in child protection.
To ensure school attendance instead of child labor activities for the hh's livelihood, which is also identified as a factor that prevents children’s access to education hence they need protection, DKH aims to hand out monthly partly food vouchers to the same learners, conditionally to school attendance and in lieu of school feeding. In view of the protraction of the displacement crisis, in the long run the existing TLS shall be transformed into ordinary primary schools: During the next few years, DKH will advocate with the MoE to support the TLS and seek a roadmap on how to transform the TLS into ordinary schools. As long as the displacement crisis continues, the TLS shall continue serving the same humanitarian needs for education. In a first step, this project will focus on training of preferably female teachers in curriculum implementation, live-saving activities, child protection, first aid and psycho-social counseling, and promotion of the introduction of code of conducts that prohibits corporal punishment.

Mitigation Measures

• Protection concerns stemming from displacement• Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services• Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas 3a) Mitigation of Protection concerns stemming from displacementi) DKH will provide a secure learning environment to the IDP children while the teachers will be trained in first aid (including psychological first aid) and the first aid kits will be equipped and thorough background checks on new staff working with children will be carried out. ii) DKH will continue to encourage employment of female teachers, as has been the trend, to build a more inclusive environment with tolerance and respect iii) DKH will offer psychosocial support to children who have been impacted by displacement iv) DKH will ensure all staff, implementing partners and teachers working with the school children sign and adhere to a humanitarian Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and rights of beneficiaries. v) In cooperation with protection actors, DKH will enhance the capacity of CECs and teachers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. 3c) Mitigation of Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services i) The design of the TLS offer child friendly spaces whereby the location and access routes to the TLS poses no risks to children and teachers. The children and teachers and specifically the women and girls, don’t have to travel long distances since the TLS is located within the camp site and the class timing is safe for girls and women to travel to and from school without exposure to harm. The TLS provides clearly marked gender segregated latrines ensuring the women and girls are not uncomfortable attending schoolii) DKH and partners will reduce the risk of harm to schoolgirls and female teachers by advocating for female teacher enrolment and develop and clearly communicate a school Code of Conduct that respects the safety and dignity of girls and female teachers. iii) DKH and partners will advocate for equal access to education for girls and sensitize the community through the CECs the value and benefit of girls’ education. iv) DKH and partners will maintain and enhance the existing complaints and feedback mechanisms to be more accessible, confidential and well understood by beneficiaries and in particular the girls and women. v) DKH and partners will reinforce the capacity of parents and teachers to act as a protective layer for children against violations e.g. FGM, early forced marriages, hazardous labour. 4a) Mitigation of Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areasi) DKH’s approach to risk of TLS eviction has been to capacity builds the CECs who engage with relevant government bodies (MOE), local authorities and land owners to negotiate for land use for the TLS. The CECs enter into land use agreements to allow the TLS to be set up and continuous checks done on possible threats of eviction. ii) In corporation with protection actors and CECs, the project will establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidences concerning the learning spaces to advocate against attacks on TLS. iii) Previous education projects have identified the power dynamics in the areas of intervention like who is able to influence decisions on education interventions or land tenure, so this project with the participation of the community will use this information to resolve land ownership disputes and also inform monitoring activities and TLS relocation spots. iv) The TLS infrastructure designs make them mobile and easy to move in case there is an eviction.v) DKH and the partners

Please provide further explanation

In future, and as exit strategy, as much TLSes supported by DKH and its partner as possible shall be transformed into ordinary schools that continue serving the humanitarian needs of IDPs in education in the camps along Afgooye Road in Banadir. This will be gradual as it is a process requiring the coordinated and timely engagement of different actors. The intervention aim is to have the TLSs recognised and supported by the local administration in the long run.Selection of beneficiaries will be guided by a clear criterial that will ensure inclusion of children living with disabilities, gender disaggregated information and targeting and address the needs for school feeding through FSL activities to promote retention of school children and increased access for vulnerable children (IDPs and vulnerable children).
