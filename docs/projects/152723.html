Project Summary

The signing of the Revitalised Peace agreement and the recent cerebration in Juba on 31st October 2018 with both parties showing commitments to its implementation and an international community that is sceptical, CAFAD proposes an integrated program with aspects of emergency humanitarian assistance and resilience in 2019 that is in line with FSL cluster objectives 2, 3 and 4 (FSL HRP 2019 plans)
The emergency aspect is to support the most vulnerable 20,000 women, girls men and boys classified in IPC 4 and 3 in the Uror, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi and Lafon Counties with emergency food and livelihood support; fishing kits and 3 variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds. Emergency food production activities will be carried out to support main season agricultural inputs; seeds and tool distribution and fishing kits.
During distribution, CAFAD shall provide technical information and guidance to the beneficiaries on best use of agriculture and fishing kits being distributed. This will cover handling of inputs provided with support of visual aids and captions with instructions. Good agronomic practices, seed production and preservation techniques, post-harvest processing and store management, family nutrition with special emphasis on food preparation, processing & utilization will be covered in the trainings.
Under this also livestock treatment and vaccinations will be pursued in cases of diseases outbreaks and for preventative measures for high risk diseases such as FMD, RVF. Vaccinations will target 150,500 animals; 65,000 Shoats, 2500 Poultry, 85,000 Cattle and 250 dogs. A total 55,000 animals will be treated against worm infestation, ectoparasite, pneumonia in calves etc. 20 Community animal health workers in each County will undergo refresher training on livestock disease management and vaccination.
CAFAD will also give support to seed fairs using locally sourced seeds in Kapoeta East and Lafon Counties. Voucher system will be employed for seeds procurement. Accountability with complaint and feedback mechanisms will ensure inclusion of vulnerable target groups.
In order to reduce the overreliance on food aid and the dwindling donor resources, CAFAD also proposes transitional emergency food assistance. This will target vulnerable 5,000 HH in Uror, Nyirol, Lafon and Kapoeta East classified in IPC 3 and some in IPC4 with potential for sustainable livelihoods with food for assets activities. Beneficiaries will be monitored through created template that will capturing completed outputs against set targets for FFA activities. As part of enhancing human capitals, CAFAD shall conduct integrated trainings for beneficiaries on best farming practices, agribusiness and food nutritional value during the entire period of assets creation.
This aims at strengthening food security and capacity of local communities in target counties to manage risks through investment in livelihood asset. This will be complemented by sensitization and awareness as well as strengthening institutional capacities of local systems in order to provide adequate response in time of such disasters or shocks.
Women needs, rights and voice will be taken into consideration in all the interventions and ensure integration, inclusion of youths in peace building.
Activities to support value chain addition like milk and fish preservation, trainings on farm production will be pursued

Needs

As per the NBS press release of September 28 2018, it is expected that 6.1 million people (59% of total population in South Sudan) faced crisis (IPC Phase3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season of whom 47,000 were in IPC 5 as a result of protracted conflict, fall army worm (FAW) infestation on crops across the county and other challenges faced in delivery of humanitarian assistance. In comparison with the IPC data over the years, the total number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 are at a record high. The food security indicators suggest that 5000HH in Canal/Pigi County maybe facing famine (IPC phase 5) in September 2018 (IPC update September 2018- March 2019). The situation could get worse into the lean season as families deplete the harvest that can last only 2.9 months.FSNMS round 22 data further shows that 16,000HH, 10,000HH, 20,000HH, 17,000H and 20,000HH in Kapoeta East, Lafon, Canal/Pigi, Nyirol and Uror respectively are facing emergency food insecurity in September. With early onset of the lean season, the Counties are expected to reach unprecedented levels of food insecurity in January- March 2019; all together the population facing emergency food insecurity will have increased by 31% (January- March 2019 IPC Projection) without humanitarian assistance.There is high level of Acute Malnutrition Jonglei (Critical GAM(WHZ) 15-29.9%) that is attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Canal and Nyirol Counties are classified as “critical” based on SMART Surveys and FSNMS respectively while Uror and Kapoeta are classified as “serious” based on SMART Surveys and FSNMS respectively. The situation is projected to be worse in Canal and better in Nyirol, Uror and Kapoeta East (IPC for Acute Malnutrition October- December 2018) With a combined cereal requirement of 92,386 tonnes against a net production of 32,072 tonnes, alot of effort is needed to close the large deficit (60,216 tonnes) in the target counties (CLIMIS data 2018).During the planting season the price of seeds had doubled in a period of one year in Lafon County.There was localised dry spells in April for about two to three weeks in most areas of South Sudan that caused mild to moderate damage to crops at early seedling stage (FAO Crop Watch 3rd Dekad of July 2018) A prolonged dry spell in Kapoeta in July worsened the situation in the area. On a fact finding mission to Kapoeta East County by FSL Cluster coordinator, inaccessibility to market was seen as one of the reason why families could not access basic commodities due to the sheer distance and the fact that only one market was operational with worsening purchasing power and market failure (FSL Mission Report July 2018) The main challenge for cattle keepers as reported by FGD participants is lack of veterinary services and pests & diseases at 69% followed by lack of pastures and cattle raiding at 68% and 62% respectively (FSNMS round 21)It is highly recommended that multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance is scaled up to meet the projected needs and that rebuilding of livelihoods and improving access to basic services.

Activities

Activity 1: Emergency livelihood support provided to most food insecure 20,000 women, girls men and boys in Uror, Nyirol, Lafon and Kapoeta EastMain activitiesActivity 1.1: Main Season agriculture inputs are provided to vulnerable households in Uror, Nyirol Lafon and Kapoeta East 20, 000 Crop kits; quality crop seeds of sorghum, maize, cowpeas and hand tools distributed to increase food production. Activity 1.2: Vulnerable women, girls, men and boys are given 3 variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds and agriculture tools distribution in Uror, Nyirol, Lafon,Canal and Kapoeta East.Time lines: Purchase or Preposition and distribution of vegetable and fishing kits will be done in conjunction with FAO 2019. Distribution will be done in early in the day (08:00 am 17:00 pm) and in an open area to ensure safety and protection for women and girls against sexual exploitation. A gender specialist from CAFAD will be deployed to ensure that women and girl rights are catered for during distribution.Activity 1.3: Fishing kits distribution to fisher forks in Uror, Nyirol and Canal 20,000 Fishing Kits; 2 fishing twines, a box of hooks, monofilament and tarpaulin per beneficiary. Women voice, rights and needs will be taken into account all through during targeting (at least 50% women and girls) and distribution. Activity 2: Livestock vaccination and treatment services are provided to vulnerable agro pastoral communities in Uror, Nyirol, Canal, Lafon and Kapoeta East. Main activity Livestock treatment and vaccination campaign; 150,500 livestock heads targeted, 100 CAHWs (re)trained. Time lines: Targeting for mass treatment, vaccination campaigns will be maximized during the dry season window of opportunity from January March and October December 2019 when the cattle are easily accessible near the homesteads. Refresher training of CAHW teams will be planned in the dry season in order not to disrupt their agricultural activities at the onset of rains in May.The “one health” approach will be used in the treatment and awareness campaigns especially when dealing with zoonotic diseases in the target locations Activity 3: Conditional food assistance through food for assets; 5, 000 IPC 3 and 4 population in the target locations of Uror, Nyirol, and Kapoeta East are targeted with sufficient labour. This is to build resilience and assets targeting at least 50% women and girls.Main ActivityActivity 3.1: Community participatory identification of productive assets for creation/rehabilitationActivity 3.2: Distribution of food ration to 5, 000 vulnerable householdsRationale: Emphasis will be given to households and members who are actively engaged in set out task and complete within the stipulated time. Activity 4: Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seeds in Kapoeta East and Lafon Counties.Rationale: Voucher system will be employed for seeds procurement. Accountability with complaint and feedback mechanisms will ensure inclusion of vulnerable target groups. Activity5: Training/capacity building for marginalized populations to enhance household income, livelihoods, and/or food sources in Uror, Nyirol, Canal, Lafon and Kapoeta EastMain activitiesActivity 5.1: Resilient building in the communities in emergency by establishment of vegetable and crops farmer field schools mentor programs for the farmers.Activity 5.2: Fish production (net making, fish line) post-harvest training, value addition and marketing techniques for the fisher folkActivity 5.3: Public health training for the pastoralists for milk production, safe handling of milk and preservation techniquesActivity 5.4: Training on the use of local biomass briquettes and fuel efficient stoves (FES).Rationale: This will target groups of women and girls who will be trained on the concept of briquette making and the use of FES. This reduces the protection risk of women and girls as they spend less time in the forest looking for firewood.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The patterns of relations between men and women are influenced by the marked inequality within the community. CAFAD’s Gender approach (2018-2020) aims at ways of increasing the impact of program and is committed to gender-sensitive indicators to assess the impact of actions on the situation of men and women and extend of power relations between them. CAFAD acknowledges that gender inequalities impact negatively on project outcomes and hence the need for strategies that enhance gender equality. So, the organization endeavors to take account of differences, experiences, concerns and needs specific to both sexes so that the projects it implements benefit both men and women to the same extent. This has increased the effectiveness and improvement of project quality. Gender equality empowerment will be integrated into the entire awareness programme. This will be targeted to women and girls to ensure that they understand their rights particularly on matters related to food security and how access those rights. Men will be targeted with the same in order to re-socialise young boys on government’s commitments to gender equality as a constitutional right and the reduction of gender-based violence. CAFAD has continuously encouraged women leadership in the entire project sites through involvement in the Boma Project Management Committee . CAFAD Policy seeks to mainstream gender throughout all programming, ensuring that indicators reflect the importance of different roles that men and women play in sustaining their household’s livelihoods. CAFAD will seek to increase women’s involvement in decision-making and promote equal power relations in all processes. Tackling the issue of gender-based violence (GBV) in communities is a key component of CAFAD’s approach to gender equality. CAFAD Country Office and the Field Offices will adopt a “do no harm” policy to ensure that Project approaches do not give rise to gender-based violence or other forms of intimidation on women

Barriers that hinder different groups from participation in project planning and implementation will be addressed by making sure that the activities undertaken under this program are made more gender-sensitive i.e. taking care of women, youth and men issues. Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

This will be done through rapid post distribution monitoring after distribution of inputs. This collects information on the satisfaction rate regarding timeliness of distribution, quantity and quality of inputs provided. The information is then classified with each vulnerable groups and their levels of satisifaction

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Distance: This will be done by considering the distance the beneficiaries walk to the distribution sites or to participate in other project activities (training, planning and asset creation). Efforts will be made that the beneficiaries don’t have to walk for more than 2 hrs to participate in the programs. Open area: the project activities will be conducted in open areas with ease access and basic amenities like water supply and latrines where the beneficiaries will feel free and not fear. Time of distribution/activities: Distribution points, trainings and other activities will be done from 8:30 AM up to 4:00 PM to ensure that women and girls are not caught up in the dark where they would be raped. Information: Safety information on the use of various inputs will be shared through info graphs and demonstrations.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

CAFAD will employ a robust complaints and feedback mechanism that is confidential, anonymous (to ensure that there is no undue intimidation) considering age and gender disparity. Emergency hotline will be used where beneficiaries could lodge their complaints without fear of intimidation.
