Project Summary

There are currently around 3.1 million individuals in need of humanitarian health assistance in northwest Syria (NWS). The COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated by an ongoing economic crisis worsens health service access and availability issues, which are pervasive across NWS. In February 2021, 51% of surveyed communities reported that HHs do not have sufficient access to healthcare services, and alternatively, patients seek medical advice from community pharmacies. The COVID-19 response has overstretched an already inadequate healthcare system, with available resources re-directed toward the response, leaving regular healthcare needs without necessary resources to operate at minimum capacity. Therefore, it is crucial to renew attention to the essential health services to serve the most vulnerable needs , especially in the face of economic deterioration affecting key determinants of health (HRP 2021).
This project contributes to the provisions of primary and secondary healthcare services through direct implementation and indirect implementation through local partners. The projects aims to provide support to around 375,000 beneficiaries in NWS through supporting the following health facilities:

4 - Needs

With just 58 percent of hospitals and 53 percent of Primary Healthcare Centres (PHC) fully functional, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system. In 2020, households and health workers alike experienced increased stress, placing additional demand on mental health services. Essential health services were disrupted as agencies and health workers alike rushed to respond to COVID-19. Due tocumulative effects of concurrent crises, cost of health services; lack of medicines, supplies or health staff; and fear of exposure to COVID-19 have replaced distance, overcrowding and long wait times as the main self-reported barriers to health access with persons with disabilities disproportionately affected. Disrupted medication supply chains, particularly for psychotropic drugs and Noncommunicable Disease (NCD) medicines is an urgent need and contributor to morbidities. There are currently around 3.1 millionindividuals in need of humanitarian health assistance in northwest Syria (NWS). The COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated by an ongoing economic crisis worsens health service access and availability issues, which are pervasive across NWS. In February 2021, 51% of surveyed communities reported that HHs do not have sufficient access to healthcare services, and alternatively, patients seek medical advice from community pharmacies. Therefore, it is crucial to renew attention to the essential health services to serve the mostvulnerable needs (HRP 2021).

5 - Implementation of activities

Provision of integrated primary health care services as per the Essential Health Service Package (EHSP) and service delivery modality recommended by the health cluster to provide preventive and curative health services, considering the physical and mental trauma effects of the crisis through supporting operations in 18 PHCs across NWS. PHCs integrate MHPSS and Community Health Workers services, and all are linked to referral networks and also connected to IRC protection and Early Recovery services. The current support will continue through December 2021 for 7 facilities, and till September 2021 for 9 facilities. Provision of life-saving secondary healthcare services including access to emergency obstetric care, neonatal health, internal medicine, and critical specialized services through supporting Idleb Surgical Hospital in collaboration with SEMA till December 2021.The IRC, and its contracted TPMs, will collect data for activities under this project. All guidance, methods, and tools utilized by the TPMs will be based on the M&E requirements determined by the project monitoring plan which shall provide additional and in-depth details to the definition of indicators, source of verification, frequency of data collection and analysis, monitoring methods and data collection tools. Utilized data collection methods and tools will include but not be limited to checklists, photos, out-bound phone calls, and tracer surveys in form of exit interviews or post-distribution monitoring assessments. All collected data will be uploaded and stored in real-time to the IRC data spaces for further processing by the IRC.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Beneficiaries can be exposed to various risks in the current contexts, including the risks of GBV, SEA, loss of access to services due to attacks or sudden suspension of activities, and the risks and interruptions associated with the COVID-19 pandemic. The proposed activities will mainstream, child protection and GBV in all the intervention phases (project life cycle). Do No Harm, preserving dignity and safety of beneficiaries; ensuring equitable safe inclusive access to services through addressing the different group needs and responding by designing tailored activities, targeting the most vulnerable and marginalized groups with a particular focus on addressing PwDs needs, ensuring equitable participation of community persons including PwDs, designing “gender, age, and disability sensitive” tools and channels along with hiring gender-balanced teams to ensure an increased response to different group's needs. The IRC regularly updating scenarios and contingency plans to mitigate any possible impact of attacks or contextual changes. The IRC also supports partners to ensure they have adequate risk management processes in place. Contingency plans were developed by the IRC under the COVID-19 tool kit for all programs. This kit aims to ensure the safe delivery of humanitarian assistance across all IRC's programs and ensures that all IRC staff, facilities, and beneficiaries are safe and protected. All IRC programs ensure to follow through with all mitigations using this guide and altering the response based on the changing COVID context to ensure the continued delivery of services during the pandemic and avoid the collateral cost of unprovided services.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection of our beneficiaries is of paramount importance to the IRC particularly as it may be perpetrated by humanitarian workers, including IRC employees, volunteers, interns, Board Members, incentive workers, partners, and all those that work on behalf of our mission. IRC is guided in its preventive efforts by the UN Secretary-General’s Bulletin on “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse,” published in 2003 and incorporates the Bulletin’s six core principles. IRC’s Beneficiary Protection Policy further expands on these six core principles to include a prohibition on all types of exploitation and abuse of our beneficiaries. IRC understands the unequal power dynamics between employees and beneficiaries and therefore has created code of conduct, PSEA policies and deedback and complaint mechanisms. Moreover, the IRC considers that: •Exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries, whether of a sexual nature or not, by IRC Workers constitutes an act of gross misconduct and are therefore, individually or together, grounds for immediate termination of employment. •IRC workers are obliged to create and maintain an environment that is free of sexual exploitation and abuse. When an IRC worker develops concerns or suspicions regarding abuse or exploitation by a fellow worker, whether employed by IRC or another agency, he or she must report such concerns utilizing the IRC’s Global Reporting Guidelines
