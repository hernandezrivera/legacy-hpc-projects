Project Summary

Provide MAM treatment for children and pregnant and lactating women in priority districts will be the main objective of this project.While the rate of global acute malnutrition (GAM) represents a limited public health threat currently, affecting only 2.4% of children between 6 – 59 months (NSS 2018), nearly 10,000 young children (0.6 %) at any given time suffer from severe acute malnutrition, a strong predictor of mortality. A sudden deterioration in the food security or health situation in Zimbabwe could trigger a rapid deterioration in rates of acute malnutrition, as seen in 2016; when rates of GAM were estimated at 5.6 %, just shy of the national emergency threshold of 7 %. In anticipation of the predicted El nino projects were made on the levels of expected malnutrition rates by the nutrition cluster. These projections indicated that more than 32,000 children and 20,000 mothers affected by the el nino drought will become malnourished as a result of the drought. These individuals will require life-saving treatment to avoid the lifelong effects of malnutrition. The treatment will prevent the individuals from becoming severely malnourished as well as prevent mortality due to malnutrition. The children and women will be identified through clinics as well as community referrals. The programme will conduct refresher courses of community health workers and ensure that they have adequate equipment for screening of children at community level. Emergency Infant and Young Child feeding messages will be disceminated by trained clinic and community health workers to ensure that breastfeeding continues and appropriate complementary feeding is practised. The programme will also use existing platforms including WAS, Education and Food aid gatherings to screen, refer children as well as disceminate nutrition mesaages.

Needs

Needs defined from the current projections indicate that more than 32,000 children and 20,000 mothers affected by by multiple crises, including last year’s poor harvest, and the high probability of another El Nino phenomena in 2019 (90% likelihood according to the most recent SARCOF analysis) who might become malnourished in 2019. These individuals will require life-saving treatment to avoid the lifelong effects of malnutrition. The treatment will prevent the individuals from becoming severely malnourished as well as prevent mortality due to malnutrition. The children and women will be identified through clinics as well as community referrals. The programme will conduct refresher courses of community health workers and ensure that they have adequate equipment for screening of children at community level. Emergency Infant and Young Child feeding will be trained to health workers to ensure that breastfeeding continues and appropriate complementary feeding is practised.

Activities

MAM treatment (provision of super cereal plus to malnourished children 6-59 months and super cereal to malnourished pregnant and lactating women)Monitoring of MAM treatment jointly with the Ministry of Health and UNICEFUnder five screening and referral for all Household targeted with Food assistance/CashInfant and young child feeding promotion to all HHs targeted to promote and protect the exclusive breastfeeding for under 6 months and maximize the use of cash toward nutrition food for 6 to 59 months children, pregnant and lactating women (and possibly people with special nutrition needs)
