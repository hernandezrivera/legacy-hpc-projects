Project Summary

The SCALE project aims to provide assistance for women, men, girls and boys through the maintenance & improve equitable access to basic services through rehabilitation of electricity networks, water networks, Solar Street lights, and Increase access to Livelihood and job opportunities through providing livelihood grants and rehabilitation of shops in the govern orates of Deir AL Zor, Homs, Aleppo, Raqqa, Rural Damascus and Lattakia.
These areas have been considered to have high number people of needs where services, resources and assistance are limited. As a result, substantial responses are needed to reduce the suffering of those who have lived through considerable hardship, especially after COVID-19 pandemic.
The proposed activities are under objective 1 of the ERL Sector log frame (Support the Maintenance & improve equitable access to basic services) , obj indicator at outcome level 1.1 (# of individuals benefiting from construction/rehabilitation of basic community and social infrastructure) and 1.2 (# of HH benefiting from newly functioning and/or restored services supported by the ERL sector partners), activity 1.01 (Rehabilitation of basic electricity), indicator 1.01.2 (# Km electrical networks rehabilitated), activity 1.02 (Promote / support the use of renewable energy), indicator 1.02.1 (# KWA generated from solar panel per month), activity 1.05 (Rehabilitation of schools), indicator: 1.05.1 (# of classrooms rehabilitated), and activity 1.09 (Rehabilitation water stations), indicator 1.09.1 (# of water stations rehabilitated)
And objective 2 (Increase access to Livelihood and job opportunities), obj indicator at outcome level 2.1 (## of HH that have a reduced gap between income and MEB), activity 2.03 (Create long and short-term work opportunities), indicator :2.03.1 (# # of longer-term jobs created), and indicator 2.03.3 (# of jobs created during the rehabilitation of public and private infrastructure).
ADRA will prioritize areas which include largest number of children under five years, women of reproductive age (15 – 49 years), older persons (age 60 and above), displaced persons –including refugees, IDPs and returnees, and persons living with disabilities.
ADRA will further prioritize sub-districts with highest severity scale (3 and above), dense urban settings, areas with lower/ limited functional facilities.
Where possible, ADRA will select the areas where ADRA is already present in order to provide an integrated approach including the sectors of WASH, livelihood, food security or education.

3 - Needs

Access to electricity, is a priority need for Syrian, while 73% of HHs reported having electricity for less than 1/3 of the day (2023 Syria HNO- ERL chapter)Approximately 90% of households in Syria consistently experience a gap between their income earned and expenditure required to meet basic needs, severe economic vulnerability poses increasingly higher risks of reliance on negative coping mechanisms, which include begging, illicit economic activities, corruption, child labor, the recruitment of men and children by armed groups, as well as risks of increased GBV. (2023 HNO Syria). The lack of electricity is not only affecting Syrian’s daily life, it is also affecting delivering water to house. 4 - Implementation of activities

During the selection of locations, ADRA will conduct needs assessments. ADRA will meet with men, women, boys, girls, (children, adolescents, young adults, adults and older adults), and PWDs and pre-surveys will include community’s feedback. Based on this, ADRA will ensure the needs of the local population are taken into consideration. ADRA will share the contact number of the complaint mechanism making sure the beneficiaries and stakeholders are aware of it and are able to provide anonymous complaints and to report any feedback.ADRA, in coordination with relevant local authorities in the targeted areas, (such as MoLAE, MoE, MoWR and SARC), and sectors (ERL) will conduct a detailed technical assessment of the planned infrastructure projects (electricity networks, water stations, schools, shops rehabilitation and solar street lights). The technical assessment will be the basis of the bill of quantity (BoQ) needed for the tender process to identify a qualified and experienced contractor. A number of stakeholders will be consulted ensuring that the needs of the community will be met and appropriate approvals obtained.ADRA will assure that rehabilitation work will include installation of ramps for the access of PWDs. and ensure gender segregated disability friendly WASH facilities are also installed. Upon completion, the work will be handed over to either SARC / MoLAE, MoWR, MoE will be responsible for futur maintenance works.Post-implementation surveys will assess the impact.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk:1. Beneficiaries may be placed at risk due to project interventions including the possibility to tensions between beneficiaries and community members.2. COVID-19 and Cholera outbreak and impact on project staff / beneficiaries.3. Sensitive data is leaked / released to unauthorized sources4. Discrimination of people with disabilities5. An increase in GBV if the abuser finds out about the reported cases.Mitigation measure:1. ADRA will work closely with local partners to ensure that the planned interventions are culturally appropriate, sensitive to local customs and traditions and harmonized with other forms of assistance to ‘do no harm’ and minimize the risks to beneficiaries.2. ADRA will assure the social distancing is respected , PPEs will be available for ADRA team, partners, stakeholders as well and aware the beneficiaries about the protection measure of the Cholera.3. ADRA has a data protection policy that will be implemented to ensure the protection of sensitive beneficiary information.4. During all project stages, ADRA will consult with the management of the health centers to ensure protection mainstreaming component is included. 5. ADRA will ensure that the ADRA staff present at the implementation locations are aware of the risk and know the safe referral pathways in place in the area. The staff will also be trained on communication with persons (male, female, boys and girls) with diverse disabilities.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk/ assumption:1. Delays in obtaining the necessary access and implementation approvals. 2. Changes in security and access especially with the COVID-19 status in Syria. 3. Extended delays in fund transfers to Syria or devaluation of SYP may place project activities outside the planned budget.4. Qualified staff may not be available / Local staff may not have the existing capacity or skills needed.5. The procurement of supplies and materials could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest.6. Planned activities pose protection risks to staff, stakeholders or beneficiaries.7. Undue pressure placed on ADRA staff by stakeholders to prioritize specific locations or activities outside of the selection criteria.Mitigation measures:1. ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow up on necessary permissions and approvals.2. Regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. 3. Closely monitor exchange rates, use competitive tender processes, adjust targets or search for additional funding. 4. Staff are recruited through a clear and transparent recruitment process and provided with the necessary training.5. ADRA Syria has developed and follows a clear procurement policy. 6. Temporarily pause activities when necessary and utilize staff from the target area for monitoring. 7. Work closely with stakeholders and explain criteria and red lines in the early stages of the target area.
