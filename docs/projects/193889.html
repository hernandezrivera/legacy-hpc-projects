Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve the food security and livelihoods of some of the
most vulnerable farmers and herding communities in the West Bank through
the provision of comprehensive support to a total of 160 small-scale farmers (48 female, 112 male) and herding communities, targeting indirectly (226 women and 526 men) from the following communities:
West Bank: Area C, including Jordan Valley, Masafer Yatta and Badyat al-Quds
The project will help small-scale farmers and herding communities, including women, in Area C improve access to resources to better cope with emerging crises and sustain their livelihoods.
This will be achieved through the following activities:
ACTIVITY #1: Provision of 160 tons of seeds for grazing for 160 livestock farmers
ACTIVITY #2: Provision of 160 milking machines for dairy production for 160 livestock
farmers
ACTIVITY #3: Rehabilitation of 160 livestock sheds
ACTIVITY #4: Rehabilitation of 2km agricultural roads
ACTIVITY #5: Increase public awareness of project staff, partner and beneficiaries on
PSEAH and gender mainstreaming.
The project will target small-scale food-insecure livestock farmers from Area C. Selection of beneficiaries will be in line with the general pre-set vulnerability criteria agreed by the FSS cluster. NPA and the
implementing partner will narrow down the selection criteria based on technical field
assessments in coordination with local committees/community representatives.
The selection criteria will be as the following:
- women headed households (WHH)
- small-scale farmers who are living in poverty
- households with over 3 dependent children
- households supporting elderly relatives
- households supporting people with physical impairment
- families who have lost access to livelihoods
- families forced or who are experiencing lockdowns and isolation.
- families whose land is close to Israeli settlements
- farmers who have been recently impacted by the ‘Foot and Mouth Disease’
Women participation in the project will be ensured throughout the project cycle. Women
have already participated in the assessment that informed the design of this project and
will continue to engage in the implementation and monitoring of project activities. NPA /PFU uses the United Nations minimum quota for women’s participation, which is 30% of project beneficiaries. Under this minimum quota, at least 48 of the direct beneficiaries (160 total), must be women. To ensure that the minimum amount of women’s participation is achieved, PFU will use its connections with its governorate-based farmers’ associations and women’s cooperatives to target vulnerable female-led households to benefit from the project activities and assisting them in completing the applications. To ensure that the maximum number of women benefit from the project, as long as all other criteria are met, women who apply to benefit from the project activities in target locations will be accepted as beneficiaries of the project, by being constantly aware of the social barriers that limit women’s participation and mitigate them. Women-headed households will be prioritized to participate in the project as according to HNO they are most vulnerable and under-served. NPA and implementing partner will coordinate closely with all key stakeholders including the Ministry of Agriculture, Food Security Cluster and other humanitarian actors in all project localities. Information about activities and progress, including 4Ws matrix will be shared with the FSS cluster. The proposed activities under this project have already been
coordinated and agreed with the Ministry of Agriculture, FSS cluster. A monitoring and evaluation framework will be developed to track progress made and ensure relevance, effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the project.

1 - Needs

Small-scale farmers, including women, in the West Bank continue to be affected by multiple layers of vulnerabilities. Occupation, settler violence, restricted access to livelihoods resources, COVID-19 impact and Ukrainian crisis are all drivers of food insecurity in Palestine. Palestinian men and women farmers, especially livestock breeders are still trying to recover from the negative impact of COVID-19 pandemic on agriculture sector in 2020 and 2021. Prices of essential agricultural inputs skyrocketed. Farmers, small producers and livestock breeders have found themselves unable to purchase the needed fertilizers, fodder, etc. to sustain their farms. These challenges have been exacerbated by the ongoing Russia-Ukraine war. It should be noted that 95% of fodder comes from either Russia or Ukraine and 90% of wheat supply comes from Ukraine. However, due to the ongoing war and the restrictions on imports and exports from Ukraine and Russia, prices of fodder have increased by 60% compared to normal prices in Palestine. Although international increase of fodder is 14%, prices in Palestine increased 60% because it comes from or through Israel, making fodder unaffordable for most livestock breeders. To add to the layer of challenges faced by farmers, livestock breeders in the Jordan Valley are battling ‘Foot and Mouth disease (FMD)’, which has resulted in over 3000 deaths among sheep, goats, and cows, mostly newly born. It is important to mention that livestock breeders live on two main outputs, baby livestock which they sell in the market and white cheese produced by milk from their livestock. This year, livestock breeders are facing a severe financial crisis, as they are unable to generate income through their outputs. As a result of the deaths caused by FMD, livestock breeders have a limited number of baby livestock which they can sell in the market for good prices, further complicating the financial situation for many livestock breeders. Additionally, many of the older livestock have also been infected, which means that they cannot produce milk and farmers cannot produce and sell white cheese. Therefore, livestock breeders cannot generate income to purchase fodder to feed their animals or acquire basic necessities for their households. Even livestock breeders who have not been immediately impacted by FMD have been impacted by the high prices of fodder, leaving most livestock breeders unable to purchase fodder. Without immediate intervention, livestock breeders will be unable to generate income to support their families and meet their most basic needs. The vast majority households in Area C rely solely on agriculture for their income, therefore it is necessary to provide farmers, especially livestock breeders, with productive assets and inputs, in the form of seeds for grazing and fodder. The provision of seeds will improve the livelihoods of livestock breeders, which will allow them to acquire their most basic needs and live in dignity. Not only does increased prices of fodder impact farmers, it also impacted food prices in the local market and increases reliance of the population on more expensive foreign imports. This project aims to build the resilience of small-scale farmers and livestock breeders who have been impacted by a layer of challenges, including risk of confiscation/annexation by Israeli occupation authorities, COVID-19, the Russia-Ukraine war, and the recent outbreak of Foot and Mouth Disease. 160 farming families and livestock breeders of approximately 226 female and 526 male, will receive direct assistant to improve and protect their livelihoods. As per a rapid needs’ assessment conducted in Masafer Yatta with farmers, reflected that in relation to agricultural roads, farmers where in consensus that the Ministry of Agriculture is doing an acceptable job in creating new roads. However, the main problem is that the existing roads need to be rehabilitated. In fact, in their area alone, 400 km of agricultural roads are in dire need of rehabilitation, to ensure that farmers are able to reach their land/livestock sheds. In relation to the state of their livestock sheds, farmers noted that there are seven clusters and around 75% are located in their area, with 10,000 people living in those clusters and a total of about 400 livestock sheds. Therefore, it is very difficult for them to build livestock sheds. Farmers mentioned that they usually use cave tents and barracks as livestock sheds and these are in constant need of rehabilitation, especially before and after the winter season.

2 - Activities

This project will be carried out by NPA in partnership with the Palestinian Farmers Union (PFU) in the West Bank. The design of this project was informed by NPA and partner's ongoing assessment of the targeted communities in the West Bank in close coordination with the food security cluster, ministry of agriculture, key humanitarian actors working in the same localities and affected population themselves. The assessment included a local market assessment, focus group discussions and in-depth consultations with affected population regarding their priorities, needs and the proper response mechanisms with particular focus on small-scale farmers, livestock breeders and small producers. The project activities include: ACTIVITY #1: Provision of seeds for grazing 160 tons of seeds for grazing will be distributed among farmers in Area C including Jordan Valley, Masafer Yatta and Bedyat Al Quds. The seeds provided will sustain farmers and ensure that their livestock are well-fed and able to produce milk. Target/Indicator: • 160 livestock farmers, including women, receive 1 ton of seeds each for fodder and grazing. • 160 tons of seeds for fodder and grazing is distributed to livestock breeders ACTIVITY #2: Provision of milking machines for dairy production 100 battery-powered mobile milking machines will be distributed among 160 livestock farmers, including women, in Area C to help them improve the quality of dairy production process and improve hygiene conditions. Livestock breeders living in close proximity to one another will share milking machines to maximize outreach/benefit. Target/Indicator: • 160 farmers households, mainly women-headed households, receive mobile milking machines • 160 milking machines are distributed among 160 farmers households. ACTIVITY #3: Rehabilitation of Livestock Sheds 160 livestock sheds will be rehabilitated to become resilient to disease outbreak. The rehabilitation will include technical and scientific measures to ensure proper roofing, designated quarantine areas to separate ill livestock and prevent the outbreak of disease. This will improve hygiene condition and quality of dairy products produced and reduce livestock mortality rates and increase the number of animals for sale and milk production. Target/Indicator: • 160 livestock sheds are rehabilitated • 160 farmers, including women, have had their livestock sheds rehabilitated ACTIVITY #4: Rehabilitation of Agricultural Roads 2,000 meters of agricultural roads will be rehabilitated in order to improve access to agricultural farms. It is expected that 5,000 dunums of land will become accessible by farmers and communities as a result of the rehabilitation. Improved land accessibility will increase farmers presence and reduce risk of land confiscation. Target/Indicator: • 2,000 meters of agricultural roads are rehabilitated/constructed • 5,000 dunums of land become accessible through agricultural road rehabilitation ACTIVITY #5: PSEAH and gender mainstreaming awareness; NPA will carry out a workshop to raise awareness of project staff about prevention of, and protection from, sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment and IASC Six Core Principles to ensure the understanding of, and adherence to, the PSEAH mechanisms. Additionally, NPA will facilitate a series of orientation sessions with beneficiaries to raise awareness about PSEAH and explain NPA’s whistleblowing reporting portal and complaints mechanism. Furthermore, NPA will ensure that PSEAH mechanism is accessible to all potential complainants, and a structured mechanism is in place to process and respond to complaints and formal referral to PSEAH Network and protection referral pathways. NPA will also support the implementing partner to become members in the PSEAH Network. In regards to gender mainstreaming, NPA will ensure the implementation of its gender policy throughout the project implementation process; this will be conducted through awareness raising among project teams on NPA level and partner level, as well as capacity development of the project teams on both levels. The project will ensure that men and women have equal access and control over income and productive resources provided by the project. It will ensure men and women have equal access to the in-kind donation distributed by the project, through the collection of disaggregated data for project beneficiaries. The project will ensure a reduction of burden on women through provision of technology such as the milking machines that will be provided through the project of which women have a higher percentage working in this field. Moreover, the project is directed in terms of support at 30% of agricultural aid to support women and gender mainstreaming. Finally, the project intervention and its monitoring and evaluation framework includes indicators related to PSEAH & Gender mainstreaming to ensure achieving the results related to PSEAH and Gender mainstreaming.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

NPA and the implementing partner will ensure that affected populations’ input and feedback are considered during the project life. Several communication channels are in place to encourage community members to share feedback. For examples, implementing partner welcome feedback through: landlines, toll-free hotlines, mobile numbers, complaints box, social media platforms, or emails. These channels are accessible for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. All channels are available for all people where everyone can communicate complaints or feedback. Beneficiaries also have the chance to voice their concerns or inquiries during activities as project staff will be present during the implementation of activities. Implementing partner has been trained on the importance of feedback and instructed in how to record verbal feedback and pass on to the relevant teams. M&E units to measure the satisfaction of beneficiaries along with the project through proactively soliciting feedback from targeted groups on how will the interventions meet their needs and expectations using FGDs, satisfaction forms and interviews. Beneficiary satisfaction measurement enables beneficiaries to make their voices heard and allows the project team to introduce adjustments as required to improve the satisfaction and effectiveness of the actions.All feedback received is collected, processed, logged and responded to by the project team. Community feedback is discussed during regular project meetings in order to ensure that feedback is taken into consideration. Received feedback will also feed into routine quarterly assessments that are drawn up to track the effectiveness of impact and provide opportunities for adaptation of approach. The existing complaints systems have also dedicated procedures to receive and respond to gender-sensitive complaints while maintaining maximum privacy and confidentiality.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NPA and the implementing partner are strictly committed to the Do No Harm Principle. We will make sure that the project interventions, approaches and modalities or implementation mechanism do not have any negative unintended impact on the beneficiaries nor community at large. Elements of cultural sensitivity and appropriateness have been carefully considered throughout the project planning, design and implementation, using NPA’s Conflict Sensitivity Programme Tools. A detailed risk register is in place that captures risks to our beneficiaries, with a detailed mitigation plan for each and every risk. The risk register takes into consideration programme and operational and environmental risks. We will select the most vulnerable food insecure households based on needs alone, without any discrimination by contributing to their safety, dignity, and integrity through ensuring that they receive assistance free of charge. For example, NPA and implementing partner have advanced data protection and confidentiality protocols and comply with best practices to ensure maximum safety of data. NPA and implementing partner seek informed consent by participants. NPA and the implementing partner designed the project activities alongside affected communities to empower beneficiaries without jeopardising their wellbeing. Special considerations regarding age, gender, disability, and displacement, among others, will be considered at all stages of the project. Any emerging need that is not addressed through project activities will be processed through the established referral mechanisms of NPA, PFU, and UAWC, and in close coordination with other stakeholders. In addition, full coordination will continue with the FSS cluster throughout the project activities, to ensure that no overlapping of work. NPA has robust policies in place for safeguarding including, PSEA policy, gender policy, and policy of anti-fraud bribery and corruption.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project takes into consideration the different needs of the different groups in the communities (sex/age/disability), where the communities has been informed during the designing stage of the project, the composition of the teams, frequency of the services, etc. NPA and the implementing partner strictly adhere to the IFRC Code of Conduct and the Core Humanitarian Standards. Aid and other humanitarian services are provided regardless of the race, creed or nationality of the recipients and without adverse distinction of any kind. Aid priorities formulated by NPA and partner are calculated on the basis of need alone. NPA and the implementing partner strive to Leave No One Behind in the project by constantly improving the selection criteria, guidelines and ensuring that women, men, boys, and girls, with or without disability, old or young or of any different forms of diversity have equal and impartial access to opportunities and support. This will be demonstrated by tailoring the project activities to the needs of beneficiaries and offer alternatives at the distribution points to ensure easy and safe access to assistance. Physical accessibility arrangement will be in place to ensure safe and effective participation of persons with physical disabilities or/and the elderly. NPA and the implementing partner will follow the same vulnerability criteria as set in the HNO. However, a participatory process with the community and local committees will narrow the vulnerability criteria by cross-checking potential beneficiaries against additional layers of vulnerability to improve targeting. Potential beneficiary lists will be screened against multiple databases to ensure that they are not receiving similar services by other actors. Timely information will also be shared about the project to allow/encourage potential beneficiaries and stakeholders to effectively participate in all activities. This will be ensured by utilizing appropriate communication tools that fit with each target group.After beneficiary selection is complete, community members and potential beneficiaries who were not selected will be given a chance to appeal if they believe are still eligible. All data collected for the project is disaggregated by sex, age and disabilities, which will help to improve the equal and impartial access to assistance.
