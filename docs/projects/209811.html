Project Summary


Based on HNO 2024, 12.9 million people across Syria need food assistance. Aggravating factors such as recurrent disease outbreaks, waterborne diseases, a prolonged drought and water crisis, and food insecurity are contributing to rising mortality and morbidity, including increased rates of malnutrition and increased humanitarian needs. Based on HNO 2024, Idleb Governorate has gone from acceptable to stressful GAM levels.
According to SMART Survey conducted between July and August 2023 in NWS, the prevalence of GAM-based Z Score witnessed an increase from 3.3% in 2022 to 4.1% in 2023 and MAM from 2.4% to 3.4%. The survey also showed that there is still a critical/ extreme stunting prevalence ( > 20%) besides serious/ severe prevalence of anemia. As for exclusive breastfeeding EBF, the prevalence reduced from 56.25 (2022) to 36.4% (2023) with critical/ extreme issues in Dietary Diversity.
The project will be implemented across 2 sub-districts (Dana and Maaret Tamsrin). As per nutrition sector severity report in HNO 2024, Maaret Tamsrin (97,295 PIN) and Dana sub-districts (327,935 PIN) are classified among the areas of most people in need with a severity score for both areas of 4, where 29,211 and 8,337 (SAM+ MAM) children 6-59 months in need to treatment, besides 8,384 and 2,410 PLWs in the Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts consecutively.
Human Appeal, aiming to address the nutrition needs on the ground, is planning to operate 5 OTP centers, 2 RRTs, and 2 MBAs across NWS, targeting mainly CU5 and PLWs with life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services as follows:
5 OTPs centers: integrated into five health facilities in Dana sub-district (Iman Hospital, Mashhad Ruhin PHC, Mariam PHC, and HA Mobile Clinic) and Maaret Tamsrin sub-district (Al Zohoor PHC).
2 RRTs: will be implemented in hard-to-reach communities in 2 sub-districts (one in Dana and one in Maaret Tamsrin subdistrict).
2 MBA: one integrated into Iman Hospital (Dana sub-district ) and one in Al Zohoor PHC (Maaret Tamsrin sub-district).
The project will target 103680 beneficiaries, including (34560 Pregnant and lactating women and 68020 children under five years).
The project also will provide CVA for 200 PLWs and caregivers of CU2 identified by the nutrition team using two approaches:
• Provide CVA and SBC for PLWs within a household receiving GFA.
• Provide transitional CVA and SBC for PLWs with CU 2 within a household receiving GFA.
The nutrition staff in OTPs and RRTs will provide the following services:
• Screen CU5 and PLWs.
• Treatment for identified children with MAM and uncomplicated SAM.
• Treatment for identified acute malnourished PLWs.
• Referral children with complicated SAM to nearby SC.
• Distributing preventive supplies to CU2.
• Provide micronutrient supplementation to PLW, children U5, and adolescent girls to avoid iron deficiency anemia.
• Screen PLW on infant feeding difficulties and provide skilled counseling on IYCF-E.
• Disseminating messages on hygiene, health care, and IYCF-E-recommended feeding practices.
• Train the caregivers of MAM and SAM cases on MUAC screening, recognition of deterioration signs, and referral.
• Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against child U5 and PLWs and refer the suspected cases to the nearby PSS and protection centers.
• Identify and refer the CU5 and PLWs with medical diseases or pregnancy complications to the nearby HA health facilities.
• Screen the children for EPI and refer the defaulter cases and zero-dose children, to the nearby EPI centers.
The MBAs will provide the following:
• Screening and referral for the treatment of acute malnutrition in PLWs and CU5.
• Provision of IYCF messaging and Counselling.
• Messaging on hygiene practices, handwashing, and prevention measures from communicable diseases, including COVID-19 and cholera.
• Information about service maps and facilitate referrals.
• Assistance for caregivers to troubleshoot challenges.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis: Risk 1: Women and girls may face the risk of harassment or sexual exploitation while receiving the services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation. Mitigation: HA will make sure to train the staff on PSEA and safeguarding. HA recruited female staff to deal with PLWs in all OTPs, RRTs, and MBAs. Set up robust complaints mechanisms with various channels in all service locations., clinical management of sexual assault and rape, and safe referrals as applicable to their respective role.Risk 2: Traditional habits prevent women from leaving their homes and access the nutritionServices. Likelihood: medium Impact: High Mitigation measurement: HA integrated 5 OTP and 2 MBAs into the health facilities to enhance the accessibility to nutrition services. In addition, the project will contain outreach activities besides 2 RRTs focusing on children, women, and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated and may not be able to access basic services. PSEA strategy:HA has already trained all staff on PSEA. However, HA during the project, will conduct refresher training on PSEA training by Human Appeal ToT trainers for all project and nutrition staff. HA, to protect from sexual exploitation, is keen to hire female nutrition staff such as nutritionists and CHWs to deal with PLWs. Additionally, the HA team will ensure Effective and clear complaint mechanisms are available in the health facilities, feedback/complaints box, and posters with PSEA hotline number. Confidential and independent proper investigation and follow-up of each reported case, including referrals to specialized services. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating Measures:Risk 1: Inadequate acceptance of the intervention within the targeted camps, communities, and houses. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: Most of the proposed nutrition activities (OTP centers and MBAs) will be integrated into HA health facilities, which will enhance the acceptance from the communities. Also, special consideration will be paid to hiring staff members from the same communities targeted and ensuring a high level of coordination with the local authorities. Risk 2: Shortage in nutrition supplies from WFP or UNICEF side. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Approaching other partners and local communities to secure the needed supplies if any stocks are available. Coordinate to refer cases to other treatment centers.Risk 3: Deterioration of security situation that may put project staff and beneficiaries at risk such as . Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Continuous monitoring of the security situation through different indicators and preparing evacuation plans in advance. Maintain good relations with local authorities. Respect local culture and build acceptance with host communities. Training the staff on security topics and first aid. Follow the common and safe roads.
