Project Summary

The project will strengthen lifesaving gender based violence (GBV) prevention and response in Koch, Pariang, Rubkona, Wau, Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Pibor, Twic East, Ikotos, Magwi, Torit and Juba. The proposed project will ensure provision of multi-sectoral coordinated GBV prevention and response services to women and girls including addressing the psychosocial needs of women and girls including survivors of GBV and to mitigate and prevent the risks faced by women and girls in the humanitarian crisis. Men and boys will be an integral part of the programme as allies in GBV prevention and response.
The project will target an estimated persons comprised of IDPs and host community members affected by conflict to;
- Increase access to lifesaving GBV specialized services including case management and psychosocial support (PSS) services for women and girls including adolescent girls.
- Strengthen capacities of frontline staff to ensure quality and comprehensive services to women and girls.
To ensure GBV survivors’ recovery and healing process is realized, CARE will work closely with implementing partners and service providers to ensure survivors of GBV have timely access to comprehensive and quality services and increase awareness on the availability of services to ensure timely access of available services.
The WGFS will offer specialized GBV case management and psychosocial support basic counseling, age-appropriate, individual and group-based support, information sessions, psychosocial support/recreational activities, and skills-building/livelihoods courses and activities for women and girls to gain knowledge and skills for positive coping including making healthy life-choices. CARE will also provide tailored PSS activities for adolescent girls to mitigate their vulnerability to multiple forms of GBV and offer support systems and access to information. This will be pivotal in reducing women and girls’ isolation and boosting their self-confidence.
Livelihood opportunities including income generation activities have greatly contributed to improving gender equality and reducing GBV risks particularly among women and adolescent girls. In this project, women, youth, and adolescent girls in internally displaced persons (IDP) camps and in the host communities will be supported to generate income and assets through a range of potential avenues. The start-up package will allow beneficiaries to sustain their new skills and activities beyond CARE’s support.
CARE will through this project continue to strengthen the capacity of frontline workers on the technical aspects of quality GBV response, including training on GBV core concepts where necessary, Inter-agency standing committee (IASC) Guidelines on GBV case management, caring for child survivors (CCS), psychological first aid (PFA) GBV pocket guide, referral pathways, etc.
CARE will partner with UNIDOR to implement CP activities to provide case management, PSS, referrals to support child GBV survivors.

1 - Needs

Since the warring parties signed the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan in 2018, the security situation in many parts of the country stabilized. However, the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence, and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 7 million people in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection. As the country slowly picks itself up from the brink of war, GBV and various human rights violations, especially towards women, and children continue to ensue in most parts of the country. According to the recently released IPC report, between December 2020 and March 2021, close to 6.35 million people (55.3% of the population) will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Between April 2021 and July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 7.24 million people (68.7% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. This unprecedented food insecurity is expected to increase the vulnerability of women, girl, and children’s situation to GBV in these locations. In counties like Wau, Pariang, Koch, Rubkona, Pibor sexual and gender-based violence restrict women’s mobility and have negative impacts on their health, welfare and economic and political opportunities. CARE conducted a Multi- Sectoral Rapid Need Assessment in Akobo in January 2020 which indicates that physical, psychological abuse and early forced marriage is highly prevalent. However, the assessment also show that GBV cases are highly underreported due to the feeling of shame and victim blaming culture. According to GBV IMS 3rd quarter report 2021, 9% of the reported SGBV cases is child sexual. Studies also show that SGBV cases are highly underreported and inconsistent due to several reasons which include stigma, fear for further violence, fear for isolation by family and friends or mistreated by community and family, fear for not being able to find a husband and lack of access to GBV services.Of the cases which get reported, failure to take action against perpetrators has created hostile environment for survivors. Lack of GBV services and low quality of available services is another key reason of under-reporting. Recent studies and reports have indicated that only about 20 per cent of at-risk women and girls have access to services related to gender-based violence (GBV). Another prominent cause of under-reporting of GBV cases and not accessing the services is the social stigma associated to it especially the stigma surrounding having been raped. The normalization of violence against women and girls, relating it to integrity of the women and girls, and the culture of victim blaming silenced the survivors. Additionally, lack of access to information and awareness on women, girl and child rights, and legal and social institutions embracing patriarchal system further aggravates the vulnerability of women and girls in such context. The situation is worsened by the economic crisis and COVID-19 which for women and girls, means more than just poverty and hunger, and includes risking brutal personal violence while struggling to keep their families alive. Routes to food distribution sites and firewood collection areas, water points, latrines, and checkpoints account for the hotspots where the majority of GBV incidents occur, including rape, robbery, harassment, abduction, killing, and physical assault. Despite the widespread violence against women and girls, access to protection and psychosocial support for survivors is either limited in most locations or is not sought out for fear of stigma and reprisals from family members and the perpetrator. Consequently, many women and girls ‘suffer’ quietly and are at increased risk of health problems, economic and social challenges and family breakdown.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Awareness raising on PSEA is ongoing within the community and putting clear confidential reporting mechanism in place and above all emphasizing on the reality that “humanitarian service is free without any favour and payment whatsoever” Trusted Community leaders will be trained as focal persons for PSEA where people can channel them complains

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARE will use their community engagement strategies to ensure that the vulnerable members of the community (people with disability, women and girls) and ensure that their voices are heard. This will be done by taking advantage of the existing feedback and complaints mechanisms structures that are in existent at the community level (complaint boxes, M&E staff, focal persons, community leaders etc). The above groups will be informed of these options and will be encouraged to provide feedback through any of them.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The opportunity for communities (both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to provide CARE with their feedback and/or complaints, and in exchange to receive feedback or response from CARE, is an important part of being accountable. To this end, CARE will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the Accountability to Affected Populations Mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CARE will use the beneficiary feedback to adapt the project and ensure that it is addressing the needs of the community. To this end, CARE will involve the community in all the implementation of the project including the target criteria for beneficiaries and mechanisms for referral. Regular project reviews will be conducted together with the affected population to ensure that the project is on track and is meting the expectations of the community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CARE is actively working with communities to ensure that they are aware of the existing protection-related services, SGBV particularly. In addition, referral mechanisms at the community level have been enhanced, by working with partners that provide protection and Gender Based Violence Services in the locations where CARE works. All CARE staff have also been trained on safe programming. In addition, GBV audits have been carried out to ensure that beneficiaries and staff are aware of what if expected of them in the access and provision of health services. There is also a risk associated with the weak capacity of both government and local organisations. The CARE in implementing its programmes is focusing on capacity building for long term and sustainable development. The CARE will also use strategies such as leveraging other partnerships to ensure investments made on building the technical capacity of local organisations is utilised and that partners have a constituency and resources to provide services. Further, based on the nature of partnerships, the CARE will conduct comprehensive institutional audits of potential partners to determine their institutional capacity. This process will also inform what areas of capacity building the partners need. In addition, the government of South Sudan unpredictable given the ongoing crisis, hence there is a risks that capacity building and technical support efforts and resources would fail to yield any positive efforts or results on fragile institutions and ministries that may change hands. The CARE will focus its efforts on identifying and strengthening the capacity of local organisations and women groups as a backup plan to avert any negative outcomes on the gaps in services in the event that government institutions changes hands.
