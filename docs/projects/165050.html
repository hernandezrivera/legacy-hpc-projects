Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining food and livelihoods assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb.
One-off multi-purpose cash transfers
Where money transfer agents are available and functional markets are accessible, one-off MPCT will be provided allowing HHs to determine and prioritise their most immediate needs. MPCT will be based on the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) rates of each location, which includes food assistance. MCs E-voucher modality is now up and runnign and will be used as much as possibile where conditions allow.
In-kind food
Where cash and vouchers are not feasible, MC will distribute Ready to Eat food rations for newly displaced HHs or a food basket for HHs with access to cooking facilities.
Repeat assistance to food insecure and underserved communities
Primarily targeting out of camp populations, MC will focus on vulnerable populations, such as IDPs, returnees and people of concern with repeat assistance. This will typically be provided for 3 months through MPCT, voucher or food assistance. This action will consider all HHs as potential beneficiaries and will target the most vulnerable regardless of their status. Where repeat cash or in-kind food is a viable and effective form of assistance for IDP camps, this option will be explored; with particular attention to potential protection concerns and pull factors.
Livelihoods
MC provides vulnerable households with temporary income through CFW and contributes to the restoration of productive assets through promoting adapted techniques for food production at household level and distributing agricultural inputs.

4 - Needs

NES: Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Turkish and Government of Syria control. The US presence is signaling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. Across Syria, one in three households is now headed by women, which highlights different needs. Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and large-scale destruction pose a serious risk to the physical safety of civilians, housing, land and property (HLP) and for the restoration of basic services. According to HNO data, most of NES is either critically or severely food insecure, particularly in southern Al-Hasakeh governorate, Ar-Raqqa and Sabka Districts (Ar-Raqqa governorate), and along the west and south of DEZ. MC’s post-harvest study highlights high costs of production (driven by increase in fuel prices, high cost of fertilizers, seeds among others), lack of quality inputs (fertilizers and seeds), poor market access, dwindling government support on inputs, depreciation of the Syrian Pound (SYP) against the US Dollar, and damaged infrastructure, all precipitated by the conflict, have played a considerable role on the declining agricultural growth NWS: With multiple waves of IDPs, humanitarian conditions deteriorated in 2019. According to the Northwest Syria Readiness And Response Plan the multi-layered context necessitates different forms of humanitarian assistance, from frontline emergency support to assistance for sustainably increasing the self-reliance of people in affected communities. To deliver effective humanitarian assistance meeting the diversity of needs and the rapidly changing situation in northwest Syria, humanitarian partners require the flexibility to swiftly adapt their operation

5 - Implementation of activities

Mercy Corps will use its emergency response activities to respond to the ongoing displacement of individuals across NWS as a core driver of need during and outside of major offensives, whilst addressing the recurring needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in a consistent manner and at scale. FSL activities address immediate barriers to individuals’ participation in the local economy from the household level, up to supporting through a medium-term approach to supporting local market systems by agriculture and livelihood interventions. Protection considerations are mainstreamed across all Mercy Corps activities. In a twelve-month period, Mercy Corps NWS, together with implementing partners, will reach 26,268 unique individuals (4,378 households) in camp and non-camp settings.Direct programming will extend Mercy Corps’ field presence and experience in multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA), with FSL components being delivered exclusively through local partners. Mercy Corps will provide high-quality emergency, and livelihoods support to 129,703 individuals across Al-Hasakeh, Deir ez-Zor (DEZ), and Ar-Raqqa Governorates, delivering both in-camp and out-of-camp interventions over a twelve-month period. Whereas a majority of the activities proposed will build upon existing programming in Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa Governorates, Mercy Corps will deliver a portion of the activities in newly scaled-up operational areas of DEZ.Mercy Corps will continue to lead its rapid emergency response across North Syria using cash-based approaches to deliver timely and cost-effective assistance, while at the same time supporting the local economy. Mercy Corps’ assessments in late 2019/early 2020 found that markets in proposed target areas remain functional and cash-based approaches permit Mercy Corps to respond flexibly to seasonal needs, and continued spikes in displacement in the face of the protracted offensive across North Syria.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps has existing codes of conduct and safeguarding practices for partners and staff. Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneﬁciary complaints mechanism. Partners and staff are engaged in PSS and case management exercise in areas where there are limited/no services for onward referral. MC will ensure that both program teams and partners are engaged in the appropriate cluster service mapping exercises. Partners are receive resources to conduct service mapping (and feedback to the cluster). Where it is not possible for Mercy Corps or partners to do service mapping, third party actors will recruited to conduct service mapping. Where services do not exist, Mercy Corps or partners will be able to pay for transport and other means to support persons accessing services. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favoritism: Mercy Corps has selected our partner organisations based on their existing community relationships and networks. This is triangulated through Mercy Corps monitoring and engagement with other key stakeholders. This issue is noted in particular in NES, where staff are working between different ethnic groups and authority structures to ensure that the programme intervention and approach is conﬂict sensitive. Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions. Where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavor to support partners in their intervention methodology and engagement with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. Persons without documents refused assistance: As part of a protection-mainstreamed intervention, Mercy Corps does not require beneﬁciaries of protection or non-protection projects to possess national ID documents.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps has identified the following potential risks during the design phase of this project, and planned for mitigation measures:1.Community tension over aid distributions | Mitigation: Provide clear participation targeting criteria, and original selection is done with the Local Council2.Air strikes or shelling | Mitigation: Mercy Corps has a Security Management Plan in place that is regularly updated. Operational Indicators are also established to guide decision-making that facilitates prepared contingencies.3.Fraud and theft of cash transfers | Mitigation: Mercy Corps revised its Cash Programing SOPs in December 2019 to ensure financial controls and oversight of the full cash transfer activity remains adapted and relevant to a remote management context4. Delay in staff deployment for response | Mitigation: Mercy Corps already has staff on the ground ready to scale up the response for the newly-displaced families5. Failure of UN Security Council cross-border resolution to be re-approved | Mitigation: Cash programing does not rely on the operation of the cross-border mechanism. 6. Interference from designated group | Mitigation: Mercy Corps has a dedicated Humanitarian Access team on the ground, who are able to coordinate with the Salvation Government directly. They also receive any direct requests from armed factions in order to prevent the Program or Monitoring and Evaluation teams facing direct exposure to armed groups or individuals.Assumptions:1- Average Household Size is 6 in North Syria1- An enabling political and security situation in North Syria will allow program staff to reach beneficiaries.2- Financial institutions and cash liquidity will remain functional and available.3- No further restrictions will be applied to the movement of cash and go into North Syria.4- Needed livelihood materials, food, and other essential needs remain readily available in local markets, and prices do not increase significantly during the project time-frame.
