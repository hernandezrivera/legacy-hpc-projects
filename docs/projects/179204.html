Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb (estimated 50% NWS, 50% NWS).Mercy Corps will target IDPs, returnees and host communities with the proposed interventions. Mercy Corps is a cash first agency and estimates 30% of the programme will be delivered through cash and vouchers. The modality will be decided based on detailed market assessments in the targeted communities, and therefore cash will be implemented when possible. MC will improve access to dignified shelter through rehabilitation of substandard shelters (collective shelters and individual units). And will also work on camp expansions and site improvement of the targeted informal IDP sites. This will be complemented by tents provision through other Shelter partner. MC will take into account home and land ownership along with vulnerability criteria to ensure HLP rights are considered, and vulnerable HHs are not further displaced or taken advantage of after receiving assistance. In camp and out of camp interventions will enhance security, privacy and the provision of dignified shelter. MC will further provide NFI and/or winterization kits when needs arise which include: Winter kits, Clothing Kits, Basic Shelter Kits.

4 - Needs

Over the course of the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed going beyond the capacity and remit of the Shelter & NFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. HNO 2021 confirmed that shelter situation in Syria continues to worsen, in terms of the available shelter capacity, the adequacy of shelter conditions. Around 5.88 million people are in need of shelter assistance due to inadequate conditions in existing accommodation, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters.The destruction and structural damage have also severely affected the housing stock across the country, leading to inadequate housing and shelter conditions for millions of people in Syria. Some of the displaced people are hosted by family and friends, some pay rent, other resort to illegal occupation of abandoned buildings. Poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and limited access to basic services such as electricity, water, sanitation/waste disposal and health services in informal settlements and camps do not just negatively affect people’s living conditions but increase protection and public health risks specifically. The new challenges with the communal shelters with COVID-19, across the country especially in NES and NW, have contributed to the significant deterioration of the shelter situation in relation to the sector minimum standards.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs MC will provide one-off, in-kind assistance newly displaced HHs) within 72 hours of displacement (access and availability permitting). The composition of the kits is in line with SNFI cluster guidance and take into consideration age, disability and gender considerations. 1.2 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance (e.g. winter items); including cash/voucher for these items): MC will provide winter kits consisting of mattresses, blankets, floor mats, tarpaulin and a solar lamp, in line SNFI cluster guidance. 1.3 Provision of life-saving and life-sustaining shelter: MC will provide emergency shelter intervention to improve the shelter conditions of camp and informal site, and to facilitate tents installation. 1.4 Rehabilitation/upgrading of Life-saving and life-sustaining shelter spaces in collective centres, unfinished buildings, spontaneous settlements, and/or other emergency spaces (including: cash for shelter, physical repair, etc.) and 1.5 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.): MC will assist HHs with small scale rehabilitation upgrades for individual shelters, informal settlements and collective shelters with proper HLP including approvals for the proposed work and handover. The selection criteria will factor in gender, age, and disability considerations in addition to socio-economic vulnerability such as the reduced Coping Strategy Index (rCSI), shelter type, and dependency ratio of the registered household.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

MC recognizes the importance of protection, gender and inclusion mainstreaming into all its S/NFI programming, as it will ensure participants’ safety and dignity, equal access and meaningful participation. MC will conduct protection, disability and gender mainstreaming and conflict sensitive refresher training for staff and partners prior to the implementation. MC will also complete a specific protection risk analysis (PRA) at the start of its projects, which will be reviewed throughout project implementation. MC will further deliver community awareness sessions on rights and entitlements, information on available services, selection criteria, complaints and feedback mechanisms and MC’ Code of Conduct prior to the implementation of field activities. Data collected in programming will be disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with this program, and mitigating measures that will be taken by MC. MC acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. This does not capture them all. 1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: MC monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and MC has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. 2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: MC upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming. 3. Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Safety and security of program participants and staff could be negatively affected: The ongoing conflict in Syria presents a constant threat to the safety and security of individuals and groups across Syria, including prospective project participants and staff members. To mitigate this risk. Mercy Corps will conduct ongoing context analysis including security and risk assessments to inform decisions on location, routes and timing of program activities, ensuring such decisions are in the best interest of the safety and security of participants and staff. Implementation delays due to COVID 19: Program implementation could experience significant delays due to the COVID-19 pandemic, occasioned by lockdowns and curfews. Mercy Corps will draw on experiences adapting program implementation over the last one year to ensure that as many activities as possible can be implemented. Simultaneously, opportunities for pursuing and strengthening remote program implementation capacities will be explored and harnessed. Loss of access to target communities: Shifts in territorial control or major security incidents could temporarily or permanently impede access of Mercy Corps organization to project participants. Mercy Corps will continuously monitor the situation Price Fluctuation: Mercy Corps will distribute cash in USD, as opposed to the Syrian pound (SYP) to ensure participants retain the value of the transfer, protecting them against the ongoing devaluation of the SYP and attendant market fluctuations.
