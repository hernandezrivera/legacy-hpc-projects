Project Summary

In support of the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector Objective 7 (Strengthened Communities and Participation), UNICEF through this project in 2021, will further support its No Lost Generation Agenda by empowering vulnerable adolescents and youth (10-24 years), girls and boys, to lead and implement civic and social initiatives with the objective of contributing to social change , community engagement and social cohesion.
The above will be achieved through an ADAP strategy: Adolescents, especially the most vulnerable, are empowered and meaningfully engaged at the social, civic and economic level and contribute positively to a cohesive and productive community. Focus will be on:
1. Building strategic partnerships, integration and at scale programming focusing on the most Vulnerable adolescents to strengthen skills development, employability and meaningfully engagement (factoring COVID-19 focused approaches)
2. Adolescents and youth have access to age and gender sensitive, skills building opportunities for empowerment and employability.
3. Adolescents and youth meaningful engagement and systemized participation.
4. Evidence generation, Documentation & Innovation to support adolescent’s empowerment and development.
To achieve the above four key goals: UNICEF will build on strategic partnerships, integration and at scale programming focusing on the most Vulnerable adolescents to strengthen meaningfully engagement (factoring COVID-19 focused approaches) through friendly spaces and mobile teams.
Before, during, and after the implementation of the project UNICEF will work on evidence generation, Documentation & Innovation to support adolescent’s empowerment and development.
UNICEF will make sure the adolescents and youth have access to safe, appropriate and equitable opportunities to take part both in decision-making and in the processes and activities right across the programme cycle. Adolescents and youth will have equal access to information on rights and how to exercise them, taking into consideration the specific context and preferences of different groups of people.
UNICEF will secure and appropriate means for affected populations to provide feedback and to complain about their experiences and perspectives on the programme , including on sensitive issues. These mechanisms will ensure that information is regularly collected, analyzed, integrated into decision-making and acted on, and that the results are communicated back to people in a meaningful dialogue.

4 - Needs

Syrian society lacks consistent and continuous positive engagement of adolescents, while policy makers and programmers in the country have little knowledge and capacity to ensure meaningful engagement of adolescents, especially the most vulnerable. Most adolescents, particularly those from disadvantaged communities/minorities, do not have access to the tools and channels that would allow them to effectively participate. They also do not have the skills to perform as active and responsible citizens. While adolescents generally have limited access to opportunities for positive civic, social or economic engagement, this is particularly the case for girls, whose mobility outside the household is constrained and they are most likely to report feelings of disengagement and isolation. The presence of IDPs in host communities is increasing the stress on social services and physical spaces in the present places of abode, leading to potential rapture in the social fabric . Adolescents and youth are among the most severely affected by the crisis and conﬂict, continue to struggle to cope with the crisis, as a result of the loss of adult-care providers, disruption of social structures and a sense of normalcy, and surrounded by a constant environment of violence and insecurity, limited access to education and safe decent livelihood opportunities. In 2021, they face heightened vulnerabilities and an insecure future. Girls remain especially vulnerable to forms of GBV. Participatory action research conducted inside Syria by adolescents among their peers highlighted negative coping mechanisms being adopted by their peers who are increasingly frustrated & disenfranchised. Youth can make positive choices and support resilience building.

5 - Implementation of activities

Vulnerable youth will have equal access to structured capacity development sessions, to be provided by local partners, of at least 40 hours as per the standard training package, following which they will work individually or in teams to implement civic community initiatives. These initiatives will aim at improving the overall well-being of all young people and have the added objective of contributing to community cohesion. Emphasis will be placed on ensuring sustainability by supporting capacity development of partners to explore alternate resource mobilization strategies, expanding partnerships and strengthening linkages with/integrating adolescent engagement across sectors. Capacity strengthening of partners’ monitoring systems will continue, including linking development and humanitarian data, and increased use of real-time technology. Where possible and applicable, monitoring and situational data will be used to assess performance, changes in the situation of adolescents and youth, progress towards the intended results. UNICEF will work to strengthen data collection, analysis, monitoring and applying adjustments to promote equity. Data sources include rapid assessments, surveys, information systems, monitoring mechanisms, research and evaluation. Monitoring and corrective actions will be undertaken through multiple modalities; partner and third-party monitoring, through mechanisms for community feedback & accountability to affected populations. In 2021, ADAP will generate evidence to capture changes – positive or negative – experienced by adolescents benefitting from UNICEF-supported programmes. UNICEF will adopt Strategic use of C4D approaches in capacity development. UNICEF will focus on high severity scale areas

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Adolescent and youth empowerment may lead to greater exposure for young people causing them unintended harm and also motivate youth to unintentionally put themselves in danger while addressing sensitive issues; Due to social norms, girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; Sport/cultural/civic engagement activities. To mitigate such possible risks to young people, the principle of Do No Harm must be emphasized throughout all stages of programming. Working with parents will also be critical to ensure equitable participation and rights of boys and girls. As the project will address actual needs of young people and voices of young people will be adhered to, the principle of Do Good aims to support a process whereby young girls and boys are able to share their views freely on interventions relevant to their needs.UNICEF will assess, identify and respond to protection risks and vulnerability of adolescents and youth by ensuring safe and reliable mechanisms are in place to access assistance and to raise concerns about sexual exploitation and abuse issues, and other protection issues such as gender-based violence and child safeguarding. UNICEF will work with and through local actors, first responders and national coordination mechanisms, and increase support to local actors in order to build their capacity before, during and after an event, crisis or development intervention.
