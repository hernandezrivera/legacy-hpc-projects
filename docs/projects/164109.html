Project Summary

This plan aims to provide complementary services and immediate needs for the most vulnerable newly displaced groups, supporting their immediate needs and enhancing their preparedness and resilience.
It is also to respond to the CCCM sector objectives to “Provide and coordinate streamlined humanitarian lifesaving, multi-sectoral assistance and strengthen the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites / Strengthen household and communal coping mechanisms in IDP sites and develop exit strategies”.
The provision of streamlined humanitarian life-saving, multi-sectoral assistance and strengthen the basic infrastructure support in existing IDP sites will be achieved through the following activities:
1- Support camp management to provide complementary services to 40 camps including emergency and post-emergency support;
- Camp operational and Regular maintenance activities.
- Timely reporting displacement movements.
- help in the establishment for in-camp IM system including registration.
- Establishing feedback/complaint (AAP) mechanism.
- Holding FGD sessions.
- Identifying the in-camp referral system.
- provide tools and materials required for maintaining the basic facilities/infrastructure.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
2- Obtain Housing, Land and Property agreements, demarcation, and provide site management support.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
3- Within the tents provision activities, WATAN team also will contribute in “improving the camp management quality in and accountability of IDP sites” by training camp inhabitants to establish services committees and maintain the daily needs in IDP sites;
-Establish CM committees.
-help development for committees members selection.
- training the CM members on the camp management.
- Help in development for each committees member ToR.
- Select and train members of IDP committees to maintain camp infrastructure ( WASH, lighting, fence, replacement of broken parts, welding, painting, reinforcement gravelling of communal areas and roads.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
WATAN 2020 plan aims to provide suitable environmental conditions for camp residents and increase the capacity of IDP sites. By providing these services, WATAN responds to the CCCM cluster growing needs by reducing protection risks and working towards better stable early recovery systems.
This approach aims to promote peaceful coexistence and to reduce gender-based violence by taking into consideration the different needs of women, girls, men, and boys and how they are influenced by the shelter conditions.
Based on a technical assessment proving the availability of essential services (such as markets, public services, infrastructures…), also, taking into consideration HLP rights, WATAN will be operating in vulnerable communities following the Emergency and Transitional Shelter Reference for Northwest Syria guidance and the AAP framework.

4 - Needs

Based on the primal HNO figures and the weekly and monthly updates from CCCM Cluster - XB, where updates are indicating the mass waves of displacement in Northwest Syria due to the control changes and the escalation starting from the beginning of 2020,WATAN Foundation conducted a multisectoral survey to attain an understanding of needs and verify IDP status. Implemented in 30 communities including 50 camps, the field survey included questions related to winter, harsh conditions, and imminent threats to vulnerable categories, such as women, children, the elderly. Women accounted for 20% of the survey sample. Since conducting the baseline survey, WATAN has served more than 4,500 IDP households, with NFIs, Tents and Winter Clothes from the beginning of 2020.Data are monthly updated as a monitoring tool to help to identify needs and cross-checking with CCCM Cluster online monitoring, through a scoring system contains the following: 1- Newly Displaced households who do not have secure access to camps and camp services and/or living in temporary or makeshift settlements.2- Households have more than 5 members.3- Female-headed / Elderly headed households.4- Households headed by a vulnerable person (with a disability - no secure source of income05- Households containing pregnant or breastfeeding women - member/s with chronic disease - member/s with special needs.6- Households hosting orphan, unaccompanied, or homeless relatives. Assessment findings:- 2,000 households (11,895 individuals) living in hand-made tents. Of the 11,895 individuals, 255 are people with disabilities, 243 are individuals with chronic diseases, 917 are pregnant women, 746 are orphaned minors, and 481 are female-led households. This plan was developed to respond to the CCCM Cluster continuous needs to support the most vulnerable groups, securing the minimal standard conditions of safe and dignified shelters and shelter services/conditions in-camps.

5 - Implementation of activities

The provision services in existing IDP sites will be:1- Provide complementary services to 40 camps:- Distribute 10,000 tents, and support the key infrastructure services; - Camp’s key administrative and critical humanitarian infrastructure: Solar power installation in camps public facilities, firebreaks and fire points, admin offices, distribution points, and warehouse, installation of external fences, rain drainage systems, safe spaces for women and children.- Camp operational and Regular maintenance activities.- Development of MoUs with services providers to ensure the stability of service provisions.- Reporting on in camp services provided through cluster tools. 2- Obtain HLP agreements, demarcation, and provide site management support including capacity building on registration, data collection, vulnerability mapping, community engagement and provision of gender-balanced protection services and PSEA awareness activities in 40 IDP sites.The main related activities will include the following points:- Sites assessment and sites selection.- Site planning and landmarking.- Comprehensive assessment of the protection and assistance needs.- Maintain the ongoing activities monitoring mechanisms.- Maintain the community-based assessment system.- Maintain the reporting system and feedback.3- Provide capacity building for camp inhabitants to establish services committees and maintain the daily needs in IDP sites.Camp management empowering activities will include:- Promote participatory management structures including active participation by women.- Promote equal access to goods and services in last resort sites to all residents including consideration for men, women, boys, girls and persons with disabilities.- Equip and train emergency responders and IDP committees in IDP sites to fire response, distribution in emergencies, and first AID.-Establish IDP CM committees to ensure equal access and advocacy for improved service delivery for beneficiaries.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk/Threat A.Create pull-factor/expose IDPs to physical safety risks when/(if the camps and settlements are located in unsafe areas) Mitigating Measure:1. Support establishing new IDP Sites.2. Maintain the CCCM guidance on the camp support ensuring that camps are the last resort.3. Provide complementary services and avoid piecemeal and ad hoc distributions when possible.4. Empower ISIMM locations as they are well supported by the cluster members. Risk/Threat B.Increased risk of inter-community tension between Camp residents and host communities due in part to the unequal level of assistance given to Camp residents in comparison to host communities. Mitigating Measure:1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and transparent selection criteria and groups are fully informed.2. Target a % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs.3. Develop feedback mechanisms in accordance with the AAP framework as a part of the plan monitoring with CC IDP Committees.4. Focusing on perceptions of women, adolescent girls, young girls, the disabled, and people with special needs. Risk/Threat C.The management structures would provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities. Mitigating Measure:WATAN will ensure that any management structure is representative of the entire community with strong selection criteria. Risk/Threat D.Vulnerable IDPs are evicted without their consent. Mitigating Measure:1. People are informed about their rights to receive assistance regardless of the documents they have.2. Presence and information about complaints mechanism. Risk/Threat E.Closure of the border for commercial shipments would have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities for both NGOs and UN agencies as it would prevent the transhipment possible aids across the border from Turkey into Syria. Mitigating Measure:1. Include site/camp closure plan where possible.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Key challenges and expected solutions to work in Aleppo an Idleb will be as the following: - Assumptions:1- BAH and BAS borders will continue to remain open.2- HLP will be taken into consideration before delivering field works or any assistance.3- Flexible in-site implementation/reallocation, when certain areas are inaccessible.4- Good collaboration with the IDPs and host communities.5- Access will be conducive to deliver assistance. - Risks:1- Borders closure can affect money transferring or any cross-border and local activities.2- Staff mobility and access restrictions to the affected population.3- Loss of cash or assets. - Risk Mitigation:1- Coordinate with CCCM Cluster to avoid any delay or lack of approvals.2- WATAN will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate the ownership of land and discuss with owners and available site management.3- Deconfliction referencing with UN OCHA.4- Daily inventory, control the treasury better.5- Unlikely Moderate Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels thus developing a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues.6- Coordination with local councils for distribution in their quality as partners.7- Signature of memorandums of understanding with local councils to gain confidence and to ensure the success of the project.8- Announcing project activities and criteria for selecting beneficiaries in distribution areas in order to avoid any misunderstandings.9- Have a joint programmatic contingency planning with CCCM Cluster.10- Mapping out high-security threat across geographical areas of interventions and sharing it with program teams agreeing with decision flow shared with implementing partners.11- Evacuation plans of the team should include handing over arrangements (Communications, agreements, triggers, FPs…. etc.) to other partners that might have access.12- Less visibility of WATAN Foundation in critical environments.
