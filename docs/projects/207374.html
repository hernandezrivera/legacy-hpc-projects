Project Summary

Objective: The project aims to improve maternal and child nutrition in Venezuela, with special emphasis on strengthening the early detection and treatment of wasting and other forms of life-threatening acute malnutrition in early childhood and preventing malnutrition, nutritional deficiencies micronutrients and overweight in childhood, adolescence and during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
Project Description: In line with UNICEF's global strategy and commitment to protect and promote diets, services and practices that support optimal nutrition, growth and development for all children, adolescents and women, UNICEF aims to strengthen public health and nutrition services to ensure adequate water, sanitation, basic equipment and treatment supplies for routine maternal and child care of malnutrition and management of complications. The project includes a strong capacity building component for health workers and the community in coordination with universities, academic institutions, the Ministry of Popular Power for Health (MPPH) and the National Institute of Nutrition (NIN) for early identification of malnourished and at-risk patients, timely referral and appropriate case management.
UNICEF will support in providing regular health checks through the national public system and extending services to the community through nutritional assessments carried out by health and nutrition teams from MoH, NIN or implementing partners to identify undernourished individuals and ensure the necessary supplements and dietary plans are provided. Additionally, the project will expand its reach to schools, implementing educational programs on healthy eating habits to encourage a healthy diet in children and adolescents, crucial for their long-term health and well-being.
Strategies and Implementation:
UNICEF's strategy will be based on five strategic changes to improve maternal and child nutrition:
1. A systemic approach to strengthening maternal and child nutrition services.
2. An explicit focus on addressing child malnutrition in all its forms.
3. A comprehensive life cycle approach to nutrition programming.
4. A deliberate emphasis on improving diets, services and practices.
5. Greater attention to community participation.
The project will focus on community engagement, working with local health and nutrition authorities and developing sustainable nutrition practices. Through close collaboration with local communities, we aim to adapt our interventions to effectively fit cultural dietary practices. Our collaborative efforts prioritize knowledge sharing and resource optimization, both crucial to project success. Additionally, we will achieve sustainability of this initiative by empowering communities and establishing local support networks.
Monitoring and Evaluation: To ensure the effectiveness of the project, we will implement a robust monitoring and evaluation system. This will involve regular monitoring of nutritional and health status, competency assessments of health professionals after training, and feedback from schools on accountability for the impact of nutrition education programs. It will also include monitoring the distribution and use of supplies, as well as evaluating the quality of case management based on standardized treatments.
Prioritized areas: the project will mainly focus the full interventions on 54 municipalities, located in Amazonas, Apure, Bolívar, Delta Amacuro, Distrito Capital, La Guaira, Miranda, Sucre, Táchira, and Zulia states, however this also includes the support to national programs such as deworming program.
