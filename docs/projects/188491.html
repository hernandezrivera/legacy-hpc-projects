Project Summary

The idea of this project is to do that through providing comprehensive health insurance for 25,000 people representing about 5,000 most vulnerable families in the highest severity scale areas in Syria (Rural Damascus, Aleppo, and Hama) to ensure timely access to basic and life-saving health services at all levels of healthcare (primary, secondary, and tertiary) to protect their lives and health (death and complications) caused by failure to access the vital service on time due to their inability to pay, and protect them financially (avoid falling into more poverty due to potentially burden of catastrophic costs to afford those services out-of-pocket, as a result selling what may be their source of income or property or having to borrow).
The health insurance will secure access to the following free healthcare services:
1. Emergency service (life-threatening and non-life-threatening).
2. Hospitalization (inpatient care: surgical and non-surgical/emergency and selective).
3. Medical examination and follow-up (outpatient care: primary and secondary).
4. Medical investigations (laboratory tests, imaging procedures and investigations).
5. Medications (for acute and chronic illnesses).
6. Maternity and child services (antenatal care, childbirth-vaginal and cesarean deliveries, postnatal care, newborn and child care).
7. Injuries and accidents with essential related rehabilitation services.
Why Health Insurance?
During the Syria protracted crisis, family Health Insurance might be one of the most efficient ways to increase access to health services and avoid financial catastrophe, particularly for poor families, particularly when the cost of health services is one of the main self-reported barriers to health access.

The number of health insured persons in Syria reached 866,476 people until the half-year of 2020, which represents 5% of Syria population, according to the report issued by the Syrian Insurance Supervisory Commission for the year 2020, although there are 13 insurance companies (1 public) that provide all types of insurance (in the health field, there are 8 medical expenses management companies that work as third-party administration) . meaning that 95% of the people have to cover the costs of health care at their own expense (out-of-pocket), or request healthcare service from public facilities that do not meet all the needs (as mentioned above).
To figure out the reality of health insurance and its effectiveness, in 2019 the Aga Khan Health Services (AKHS) in Syria conducted a qualitative study in Salamieh District with 74 persons (35 males and 39 females, some are insured and most are non-insured) through seven focus discussion groups. Regarding the people who have health insurance, the results showed that it still achieves its main purpose, which is to cover the high cost and life-threatening cases timely, facilitating access to routine and essential healthcare which contribute to maintaining the health status by ensuring access to the vital health services at any time, which gives a sense of protection, safety through a way respects the dignity, especially when it covers all family members, and it is preferable to have a third party (like a supervisory authority to ensure the transparency of the insurance process and to obtain high-quality services timely and deal with complains quickly and seriously), whereas out of the 64 non-insured persons, 21 had to cover high costs health services of-of-pocket during the past year as follows: 8 borrowed from others and banks, 4 Spent from their personal savings, 4 social cooperation groups, 2 used the public health facilities, 1 sold their land, 1 sold their home furniture.

4 - Needs

12.43 million people in need of health services in 2021 in Syria, and by mid-2020 more than 7.78 million people in 100 sub-districts were living below three critical emergency health standards with just 58% of hospitals and 53% of Primary Healthcare Centres (PHC) fully functional, COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system, and it has reported that 86% of surveyed households who sought health services in previous 3 months reported having to pay out-of-pocket for care – most often for medications. Geographically, the health sector severity scale reveals that areas of catastrophic need are primarily located in Aleppo, Idleb and Rural Damascus in 2021, Exacerbating this is the historical depreciation of the Syrian pound and the global pandemic – both of which are contributing to the shortage of medicine coupled with the surge of prices, A previous Aga Khan Foundation study conducted in 2019 in Salamieh District (which can be used as a marker for other areas in Syria) also showed that the average income of a vulnerable household was SYP 76,000/month. However, annual expenses for health services related to NCDs were estimated to average SYP 119,000 (SYP 16,000 for clinic visits, SYP 32,000 for lab tests and imaging, and SYP 71,000 for medications). Sources:Syria 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview:https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/whole-of-syria/document/2021-humanitarian-needs-overview-syrian-arab-republicAga Khan Foundation (Syria).

5 - Implementation of activities

This project target 25,000 individuals (females and males) including displaced persons, children under five (U5), women in reproductive age WRA (15-49), older people (60+ and people with disabilities) to be full covered by health insurance that guarantees the stability of their health condition, protects their lives, protects them economically, and prevents them from falling victim to catastrophic spending, which contributes to their resilience and their ability to recover and generate income. This will be through identifying most vulnerable families from high severity scale areas in Rural Damascus, Aleppo and Hama according to agreed criteria that reflect the severity of their needs and - that include the five most in need groups by cooperation with local stakeholders (local NGOs, United Nations agencies, and others), then they will be registered in the health insurance which enables them to have access to basic and advanced diagnostic and treatment health care services: primary, secondary and tertiary - routine and emergency (live and non-live saving), in addition, to raise their awareness about the importance of health insurance to ensure the rational use of it and encourage the culture of insurance, with continuous monitoring to the activities (field supervisors, phone helplines and random visits and call with beneficiaries) Interventions have been coordinated with the local NGOs, UN agencies especially the WHO and aligned with AKDN’s Global and in-country response.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Access to healthcare facilities sites may be physically challenging for patients (especially disabled persons and elders) or may expose them (discomfort or/and high cost of transportation). Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium.Mitigation: In coordination with the insurance company, AKHS to assign supervisors and coordinators to facilitate access to services for beneficiaries in addition to distributing a list of all and nearest healthcare facilities (map of services) to beneficiaries. Risk2: Some patients may be subject to discrimination due to negative cultural perception (because of their low socio-economic situation or their gender) Likelihood: Medium; Impact: high.Mitigation: special measures will be mentioned in the contract with the insurance company to secure the privacy of all patients and keep their medical records secure with the high level of confidentiality and dealing with them equally with regular monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of discrimination and take actions against it.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Exploitation and abuse of children and women by some staff or service providers as a condition to provide services. Mitigation: AKHS has a written safeguarding policy which will be followed and implemented in this project, furthermore awareness sessions and clear printed materials will be provided to the family to clarify their rights and the importance of paying attention not to be exploited by anyone, and to establish a hotline that they can call at any time to report any misconduct towards them, in addition to emphasizing all project workers (organization, insurance company, service providers) of the importance of respecting the beneficiaries and not to exploit them and the possibility of accountability in the event of a violation.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Healthcare services prices may rise unexpectedly based on unstable exchange rates which may make some services not optimally provided.Mitigation: Mention this point clearly in the contract with the health insurance company and make sure that this point is taken into account. Risk 2: Misuse of health insurance by an unexpected percentage of the beneficiaries, which may harm the reputation of the project and may not encourage insurance companies to contract again. Mitigation: Awareness sessions for the beneficiaries, and their signature on a declaration (after a detailed explanation) stating that the insurance should be used when needed under the threat of legal accountability and exclusion from insurance, and random periodic verification in cooperation with the insurance company. Risk3: Misuse of health insurance by an unexpected proportion of healthcare providers and exploiting the vulnerabilities of the beneficiaries and deprives the beneficiaries of proper access to services and harms their dignity which may damage the reputation of the project.Mitigation: Communicate with service providers and explain the importance and dimension of the humanitarian project with the threat of legal accountability and exclusion from insurance, random periodic verification in cooperation with the insurance company and periodic questionnaires with beneficiaries after explaining their rights and duties.
