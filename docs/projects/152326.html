Project Summary

* Provide the basic emergency humanitarian services for internally displaced persons (IDPs) in UN House POCs, Mahad and Don Bosco IDP camps, and host communities in Juba &Bor through the construction of Semi-Permanent latrine (400 stances) and bathing shelters (180 stances), construction of Institutional VIP latrines(2 schools in Juba) and household slab distribution (100slabs) within host communities of Juba.
*To sustain and improve water supply for IDPs and targeted Juba and Bor host communities through drilling (14) and rehabilitation (14) of hand pumps, Installation of solar power water scheme(0ne in Juba and 0ne in bor) and repairs of water points in POCs (20) and Mahad IDP camp (5).
* Hygiene promotion activities in Juba and Bor Host Communities through training of 32 Community Hygiene Promotors in both Juba and Bor to disseminate Hygiene messages to the beneficiaries.
*Formation and Training of School Health Club members in 2 selected schools in Juba and 2 selected schools in Bor

Needs

• There is low sanitation coverage across Baidit Payam in Bor South County and Parts of Juba. Through past assessments, it has been observed that the population in Baidit Payam in Bor South County and Payams in Juba have a wide gap to bridge in terms of sanitation. Open defaecations are rampant, latrine coverage is low as and hygiene practices need to be incessantly promoted. Peace Winds Japan being cognizant of these sanitation challenges, have elaborate plan of training the Community Hygiene Promotors (CHPs) to help in dissemination of key hygiene messages to the population of these Payams. CHPs would be playing a big role in the sensitization exercise. For the low latrine coverage, the community would be sensitized and supported to build latrines through provision of household latrine slabs. Open defecation would be discouraged through sensitization. The communities would be sensitized on how to maintain personal hygiene to prevent the spread of water borne diseases from faecal contamination. PWJ has proposed to provide household latrine slabs while the community will be encouraged to dig the pits and provide local materials for the superstructure, this will help in reducing open defecation menace. Communal Semi-Permanent latrine blocks will be built in Unhouse POCs, and Don Bosco and Mahad IDP Camps with the necessary provisions in terms of privacy and other engineering considerations to take care of disable persons, girls and women in a bid to reduce GBV risk to women and adolescent girls. • Water demand remains high. Most water supply is derived from groundwater sources i.e. Boreholes, shallow ponds on the swamp, river and water pans. For community water demand to be addressed, drilling new boreholes and rehabilitation of non-functional boreholes and water yard could be considered. And to allow for sustainability of these projects, functional water committees should be formed and existing ones strengthened to allow protection and effective management of the water points and minimize contamination and pollution of water sources as well as promoting hygiene and sanitation practices. Water technicians should be equipped with knowledge for O& M functions. Areas of priority would include Institutional centres (schools and PHCCs), a central location within a community.

Activities

1) POC and Mahad IDP Camp water points repair and maintenance through replacement of water taps, fittings/connections (pipes, valves, taps) frequent cleaning of water tanks and improving drainage of waste water at the water points. Includes materials & labour for a total of 25 water points.2) Construction of 180 Bathroom stances in Mahad and Don Bosco IDP Camps by providing a concrete floor slab and iron sheets for the superstructure and all the drainage works to include soak pit.3) Construction of 400 Semi-Permanent latrine stances by lining the latrine pit sides with concrete block and cement in order to prevent the pits from collapsing due to heavy rains and reduced the frequency of re-construction in the long run. 300 stances will be constructed in the Unhouse POCs while 100 stances will be constructed in Don Bosco IDP camp.4) Renovation of 2 old VIP latrine blocks in Mahad IDP camp. The renovation works include; desludging of septic tank and teachers block, repairs on the latrine floors, walls, roofs, doors, drainage pipe and construction of a curtain walling in front of latrine blocks to aid in privacy, a verandah and provision for special room for people with disability. The total number of latrine stances is 21, 20 for IDPs and 1 for teachers.5) Periodic desludging of the two septic tanks for the two IDP latrine blocks in Mahad IDP Camp. The desludging to be done at interval of 2 months, 6 times in 12 months. The desludging will help in averting the risk of cholera outbreak as a result of over flow liquid waste. 6) Reconstruction of VIP latrine in Mahad Primary School, will involve decommissioning of existing old VIP latrine, demolition and reconstructing new latrine in the same space within the school. The total number of stances is 18, 10 stances for ladies (including 2 Bathing rooms), 8 stances for Boys (including 1 urinal)7) School WASH Activities: Forming and training of school health club members in 5 Primary Schools in Juba and Bor. Training the school health club members with songs and dramas on best and bad hygiene practices. Each club to have 10 members and 2 teachers.8) Cholera response team of 20 members will be formed and attend 3 days training on cholera preventive activities which include; disinfection of IDPs/people foot entering/leaving the POCs, latrines, garbage collection points, houses of suspected cases of cholera.9) Rehabilitation of 14 non-functional community hand pumps in host community targeting the government priorities locations of needs(7 communities in Bor South, 7 in Juba)10) Rehabilitation of existing community water in Rejaf Payam in Juba.11) Construction of new solar powered water supply systems through pumping from borehole, 1 in Bor.12) Drilling and installation of 14 new boreholes in Juba and Bor (7 communities in Juba, 7 in Bor)13) Formation and training of water management committees for each of the newly drilled and installed hand pump and water yard in Juba, each committee consists of 7 members. (7x16=112persons) for Bor and Juba.14) Promotion of Household latrine sanitations through supporting of household latrines construction by production and distribution of concrete slabs targeting 100 households in Juba host community. The cost to include loading/off-loading, transportation/distribution to the selected households.15) Construction of a total of 22 stances of VIP latrine in one primary school (22 stances for one school) within Juba to include 10 stances for boys, 8 stances latrine and 2 stances for menstrual hygiene (showers) for girls and 2 stances for teachers in one school.16) Forming and training of community Hygiene promoters targeting 8 locations (4x4CHPs=16 in Juba, 4x4 CHPs=16 in Bor South total 32 CHPs will attend) 2 trainings (first and refresher) each with 3 days, totaling to 6 days for the project period. The CHPs will then disseminate hygiene messages to the community.17) Community empowerment on Cross-cutting issues aiming at capacity building

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Through constant stakeholders meetings and community empowerment participation fora.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The information passed to beneficiaries is always designed to address specified targeted beneficiaries. For instance, any information that would touch on menstrual hygiene would basically target adolescent ladies and women.The essence of having a context-specific information is to help in disseminating appropriate messages to the appropriately targeted beneficiaries.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Most of our projects are designed in a way that female beneficiaries are protected from sexual abuse. For instance, our semi-permanent latrines in POCs/IDP Camps have a clear separation of rooms for male and female. In addition, a curtain wall is constructed in front of every latrine block to provide privacy specifically to the female beneficiaries. They are also constructed not far from the shelters to avoid cases that would arise from sexual abuse.On the other hand, our aim has always been focused on ensuring that female beneficiary reduces the distance covered in order to access water. Drilling and rehabilitation of borehole have significantly helped in achieving this objective hence reduced incidence of sexual abuse to women who might be a target during the struggle to walk long distances to access water.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Our consultations with communities have always been a two-pronged approach; at the initial stages of assessments and at the inception of the project. Our consultations have always involved, community meetings: which were held to solicit different views and opinion of the community members on issues related to WASH needs and activities, focus group discussions with relevant stakeholders and Key Informant Interviews: Structured and semi-structured guide questions that are developed to discuss and collect information from key informants such as key government officials in the line ministries.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

To always ensure that our implementation is successful, at the tail end of the project we normally carry out an End of term evaluation to have an in-depth assessment of the extent to which the Project achieved its objectives. This is normally done by administering questionnaires to sample the views/feedback or complaints of the beneficiaries which in turn help us improve on our implementation.During project implementation, we always have community leaders engaged in our projects. These leaders act as the focal person or the bridge between us and the beneficiaries and in case of complaints that might need our attention, there are always there to pass it across to us. Similarly, when we have complaints to pass across to the community, it is always imperative on our side to engage the community leaders who in turn pass the message to the beneficiaries. For instance, In POC we've had challenges of vandalism to some of the sanitation structures that we have constructed, through a concerted effort with block leaders and zone leaders we have managed to sensitize the community about the need to own the facilities for proper use and sustainability.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

At the inception and during the implementation of the project, community engagement has always been very key. It is through such engagements that we allocate our project sites appropriately within the community without much friction. Involving the community members during pre – construction mobilization and during project implementation by forming committees e.g water committees for borehole ensured counter check measures that planned benefits are delivered to the right beneficiaries at the right time thereby promoting accountability and value for money. Also during the implementation of the projects, a lot of considerations are put in place to ensure that there’s continued usage of the facilities after the expiry of the funding. Among these considerations include institution of Water committees and training of hand pump technician as well as educating beneficiaries on the spirit of ownership. Water committees would ensure households are responsible for mobilizing funds to be used in case any repair of the borehole is needed. This can only be achieved if there is a proper community engagement.

In effort to provide WASH sevices to the bebeficiaries, some element of protection risks are encountered and usually solved through pedequate provision of such services. for instance, a crowded water point will always attract fights among the womenfolk who tend to scrumble for the little resource. When the water poinst are increased, the propability of such fights occuring are reduced. Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Most of WASH interventions are geared towards clearing the barriers that are faced by certain groups in a community. For instant when menstrual hygiene is promoted among the adolescent girls in school and further supplimented by provision of necessary sanitation structures that provide dignity to girls, then it will be evident that the enrolment and retention rate of girl child in schools are high hence that barrier to access to education is broken.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Our project support towards construction of VIP latrines and school health clubs target schools. The objective of the VIP latrines is to boost the enrolment, retention and completion rates especially of the girl child by improving the school learning environment. Consequently, the establishment of the school health clubs is instrumental in equipping the pupils with knowledge of hygiene thereby making them part of the learning system hence do not feel deprived or neglected in matters education.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Our WASH activities are specifically targeting all the groups in the society without prejudice to a certain group. Water and Sanitation are critical to human survival hence should be accessible to all.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Our WASH activities are always accessible to the intended beneficiaries but as part of our mandate to ensure that the project becomes sustainable, we always form a committee compromising of select women and men to stear up the project even after our implementation comes to an end. For instance, some Water management committees have always ensure that the community collect some small fee in bid to rehabilitate the facility incase of mechanical breakdown, This done in away that it does not deny others the opportunity to access water.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

All our intended WASH activities are geared towards provision safe water and sanitaion services to the beneficiaries. In attempt to provide these services, we ensure that the safety of beneficiaries are paramountly taken care. Adequate knowldege is paased to the beneficiaries on sustainable and proper use of the facicilties without compromising their safety. During construction of some of these facilities, the beneficiaries who are directly involved are always made to comply with certain safety measures that re put in place. Additionally, during the use of handing over of these facilities, an MoU is always signed with the community management which clearly stipulate the role of community in managing the facilities veru safely and sound.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Our projects target different groups with different needs i.e the old and vulnerable, the disable, children, women and men. Most of our hardware components for example latrine construction, we make sure that each target group is well catered for. For instance, our semp permanet latrine have child friendly spaces to ensure that they dont have fobia to use latrine but find it friendly to use hence reduces on cases of open defeacation. Similarly, there is special roon assigned for disables which they can comfortable use without assistance of any person hence taking care of their dignity. Similarly the privacy walls help women and girls to comfortably use the facilities without lowering their dignity.
