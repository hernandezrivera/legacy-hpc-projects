Project Summary

Goal: Improved food security for vulnerable HHs in conflict-affected areas of Borno.
SO1: Vulnerable HHs in conflict-affected areas meet immediate food and nutrition needs.
SO2: Conflict-affected HHs resume productive livelihoods.
CRS proposes to FFP a fifth phase of the “Emergency Food Assistance for Conflict-Affected HHs” Program in Nigeria during which it will focus on providing emergency food assistance to the most vulnerable individuals and developing self-reliance for those who have access to farmlands by restoring their farming activities.
Emergency Food Assistance: CRS is currently providing emergency food assistance to a total of 86,563 individuals (14,028 HHs) in Borno (Kaga, Gubio, and Magumeri) and in Yobe States (Gujba and Gulani LGAs) . As the conflict evolves, CRS recognizes that the needs have changed across the Northeast depending on factors such as the level of security, population movement, access to land and markets, livelihood opportunities, and assistance gaps in each LGA. Certain LGAs may now require more recovery-type assistance (Gujba and Gulani LGAs), while others still face acute levels of food insecurity. In 2020, CRS will focus its emergency assistance in Borno State where the security context has been deteriorating since December 2018.
Within “Emergency Phase” LGAs, the most vulnerable individuals (approx. 77,280 individuals/12,880 households) will receive monthly full food rations from April 2020 to January 2021 (ten distributions). In addition, 2,500 vulnerable households (approx. 15,000 individuals) with access to farmland will receive monthly full rations from April 2020 to September 2020 (six distributions) while also receiving additional farming inputs to ensure HHs self-sufficiency from October 2020 to February 2021.
CRS is an active member of the Food Security Sector in Borno and coordinates closely with key actors, such as the World Food Program (WFP) and Action Against Hunger (ACF) and Nigerian Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) in overlapping areas. CRS actively coordinates with other organizations implementing food security and nutrition activities outside of programming areas as well, but with similar or FFP funded programming, like SCI or ZOA. To ensure harmonization and avoid overlap or duplication of efforts, CRS coordinates though the FSS and the Cash Working Group (CWG), particularly with the e-voucher value, retargeting strategies, and geographical scope. CRS is the only actor providing food assistance in proposed locations in Gubio and the only international organization in Kaga. CRS remains the only actor delivering food assistance to settlements along the road between Magumeri and Maiduguri (Magumeri LGA). In Magumeri town, CRS coordinates closely with INTERSOS/WFP to reduce duplication of assistance and harmonization of modalities. CRS is also working closely with NEMA regarding the proposed scale up of activities in Benisheikh to settlements not currently covered by NEMA. In addition, CRS proposes to resume activities in Muna, Elmiskin and Simari (Jere LGA), former areas of intervention, where there is currently no food actor present. While in development of the voucher amount, CRS reached out to ACF, SCI and ZOA for ensuring harmonization efforts.

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria has been severely affected by insurgency led by non-state armed groups since 2009 and has been declared “state of emergency” since 2013. As clashes between Nigerian security forces and armed opposition groups (AOGs) persist into its tenth year in 2019, over 2 million individuals remain displaced across six states. Between December 2017 and August 2019, the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the Northeast increased by 15.6%, after a gradual decrease in 2017, an indication of a highly volatile security context. In the Northeast, Borno State is the worst affected by the conflict with over 1.48 million IDPs recorded in July 2019 . Among those displaced, 73% indicated that food was their principal unmet need. While households in CRS’ food assistance programming have shown improvement in their individual food security, WFP recent Emergency Food Security Assessment shows a worsening of the prevalence of food security in CRS’ targeted intervention areas as follows: Kaga (84.1%), Gubio (73.0%), Magumeri (62.0%), and Maiduguri (52.5%) respectively. The assessment conducted in April 2019 identified 28.9% of households across BAY States as food insecure and 3.1 % of these households severely food insecure. These figures represent an increase of 2.4% in the prevalence of food insecurity compared to the previous year (February 2018) . According to the report, 14.5% of HHs in Gubio and 10.3% of HHs in Magumeri are severely food insecure with a prevalence of poor food consumption of 25.9% for Kaga, 25% for Magumeri and 7.6% for Gubio LGAs.The latest FEWSNET Nigeria Food Security Outlook projects the following outcomes until January 2020 for areas where CRS proposes or is currently providing assistance: IPC 3 for Gubio, Magumeri, and Kaga; and IPC 2 for Jere/MMC (with pockets of IPC 3). The dire situation is confirmed by the June 2019 Cadre Harmonisé analysis which indicates that, despite improved food production in 2019, the main food security, nutrition and livelihood indicators are still at alarming levels and 2.97 million people were projected to be severely food insecure during the lean season (June-August 2019) in the three northeastern states.

2 - Activities

The most vulnerable individuals in program LGAs (approx. 77,280 individuals/12,880 households) will receive monthly full food rations from April 2020 to January 2021 (ten distributions). In addition, 2,500 vulnerable households (approx. 15,000 individuals) with access to farmland will receive monthly full rations from April 2020 to September 2020 (six distributions) while also receiving additional farming inputs to ensure HHs self-sufficiency from October 2020 to February 2021. The value of the e-voucher is still being developed as more data becomes available. CRS currently has a food assistance voucher of 3,464.45 per individual and this does not include fuel or transportation. The current voucher supports per head and is being reviewed for the possible inclusion of these costs, but CRS has no finalized voucher value or associated costs. CRS will continue with covering 70% of the MEB in all locations as per the 2017 harmonization guidelines, and requires that 30% of the voucher assistance be spent on “nutrient rich foods”. The nutrient rich foods are determined in conjunction with our Nutrition technical team. The current costing analysis of fuel indicates that 2500 NGN per household is spent in most LGAs and the transport subsidy projected will not exceed 700 NGN. This will be based on assessment, geographical needs and the on the ground context as well as coordination with other actors for harmonization. CRS will support 2,500 rural HHs with farmland access from its current caseload to restore agricultural activities. Beneficiaries will receive seed and tool e-vouchers valued at $45 before the planting season, as well as agricultural training. To improve the food and nutrition outcomes of the program, CRS community mobilizers will conduct nutrition screening through mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) measurements of all children under five to identify cases of malnutrition in all targeted LGAs during house-to-house visits. CRS will cover transport fees ($0.86 - $2) for the referral of approximatively 150 children identified with SAM. CRS adopted costs associated with specific referral pathways per actor per locations, based on the distance’s families are required to travel. CRS has had considerable success with Tom Brown Supplemental Feeding under the scale up in FFP IV, with over 80% recover rates in Round I and comparable in recovery rates in Round II (the height of lean season). Approximatively 3,000 children identified with MAM will receive a maximum of 8 weeks of supplementary feeding. CRS will work with community mobilizers and “lead mothers” to prepare the Tom Brown feeding supplement by providing them with locally procured millet, guinea corn, yellow corn, soybeans, and ground nut, as well as a small cash payment to cover purchase of cooking fuel, milling, and transportation to and from the local mill on a weekly basis. Community mobilizers will carry out follow up visits on all SAM and MAM cases and will provide training in Infant and Young Children Feeding (IYCF) during weekly group sessions. While much of the food aid in the northeast is delivered to IDPs in camps, CRS will continue to deliver food assistance to IDPs living in host communities. CRS’ targeting strategy minimizes potential conflict among groups by focusing on vulnerability, allowing assistance to reach IDPs and returnees, as well as host families who will be identified during the registration process with the aim of alleviating the additional burden that host families face. Based on lessons learned, CRS uses a weighted-criteria scoring system to assess a “vulnerability score” based on both reported and observable criteria that compound food insecurity. CRS considers criteria such as: elderly, child or single-headed households; shelter; short term residence in LGA (indicating IDP status); presence of household members with disabilities; status of ‘Host’ to IDPs; length of displacement; household size and composition and other relevant factors.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CRS’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team conducts ongoing, regular on-site monitoring surveys (OSM) during disbursement periods and post- distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys following monthly disbursements. These monitoring activities inform on beneficiary satisfaction with different aspects of the process, how beneficiaries use e-vouchers and why, who within the household decides how to spend e-vouchers, and other quantitative and qualitative data that guide program decisions and future program design. OSM and PDM surveys have been conducted using a set of pre- defined questionnaires at market and at the household level respectively. While using purposive sampling methodology, beneficiaries were randomly selected and surveyed on several sub-indicators including satisfaction with the quality of food purchased, number of vendors, and availability of stock in the vendors’ shops. Additionally, CRS offers feedback desks at registrations and verification activities. There is a toll free hotline as well to collect feedback, including anonymous feedback.
