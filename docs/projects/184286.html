Project Summary

According to FSNAU Post-Gu Technical Report released September 2021, indicates that from Oct–Dec 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations including Galgadud, Gedo and Lower Juba regions due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (Oct-Dec) season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season.
Furthermore, FSNAU Post-Gu Technical Report –September 2021, points out that estimated 55,290 people in Galgadud region including Abudwak district are experiencing food crisis/or IPC 3 and estimated 3,900 people in the rural parts are in IPC 4, translating to about 13% of total in Crisis and Emergency as percentage of total population. In Gedo region a significant number of people (66,080) are in IPC 3 and 6,430 people are in the IPC 4, translating to about 15% of total in Crisis and Emergency as proportion of total population. As for Lower Juba region, encompassing Badhadhe district, an estimated 93,830 people are in IPC 3 and 8,840 of the population are in IPC 4, representing about 12% of the total population.
Cluster Objective 1: Improve access to food for 3.1 million of people facing severe acute food insecurity including million IDPs, 2 million non-displaced people and persons with disabilities in 64 districts. ADA will aim to improve immediate access to food to at least 5,250 households.
Cluster Objective 2: Protect livelihoods and related food sources for 830 000 farmers, pastoralists, agro pastoralist and 163 000 rural IDPs across Somalia through emergency agriculture, livestock, and other livelihoods support. ADA will ensure to protect and restore livelihoods related to food and income sources at least 3,500 households will be reached.
The planned project activities are;
Activity 1: Community mobilization, identification of 10 water catchments and household that are vulnerable but can provide labour for deepening and expansion of productive facilities.
Activity 2 Procurement and provision of seeds to agro-pastoralist farmers (12kg sorghum, 10kg cowpea and 10 Kg maize) for 3,500 farmers at least 30% women.
Activity 3 Training of farmers on good agronomic practices including conservation agriculture reaching 10% of the target farmers.
Activity 4: 5,250 agro-pastoralist households have increased access to productive assets through rehabilitation of water catchments.
Activity 5: Desilt community water catchments through Cash for Work to increase water storage by 4,200,000lts seasonally in each pan in respective village. The water catchments will provide water for human and livestock and enable the local population to concentrate on crop production.
The livestock will have enough water for drinking, having enough water available to livestock allows for optimal animal performance and health. High water in-take, and the more an animal drinks, the more the milk production and better animal performance. The availability of milk and meat obtained from the livestock, will mean that the household's income will be better, since the households have substitutes to either trade the livestock or sell the direct consumption outputs such as milk or meat, thus the household will utilise the money obtained from such action in many ways, which will consequently enhance the food security situation of the households.
Activity 6: Conduct post distribution monitoring in each cycle of CFW payments to beneficiaries and food distribution, where at least 5% of the target beneficiaries will be interviewed.
Activity 7: Conduct crop yield assessments for the planted crop stages.
Activity 8: At least 3,000 animals comprising of goats and cows will be supported in animal interventions including support in treatment.

The water catchments will provide water for human and livestock and enable the local population to concentrate on crop production rather than trek long distance for water or are forced to move away from the farms due to lack of water. The livestock will have enough water for drinking, having enough water available to livestock allows for optimal animal performance and health. The availability of milk and meat obtained from the livestock, will mean that the household's income will be better, since the households have substitutes to either trade the livestock or sell the direct consumption outputs such as milk or meat, thus the household will utilise the money obtained from such action in many ways, which will consequently enhance the livelihood conditions of the households. When the households have adequate income, they have better alternatives to use the available money to purchases their essential food items, hence the purchasing power will increase, therefore resulting to an enhancement in total food security status quo of the households. 2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

When market is functioning, this is often the fastest way to provide resources to the intended beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will have full control over the way they utilise the money, lessers administrative to the implementing agency, minimal involvements required from implementing agency at the point of trade. Monitoring of spending is often more accurate because beneficiaries do not feel pressured to give a pre-determined response as for conditional cash transfers. The project endorses that there is access to the local markets, in all the three districts targeted the roads are traversable and the security risks are very minimal, besides the commodities/or goods are readily obtainable/or available within the local retailer shops. In Abudwak district, its proximity to Bossaso port creates it easier for the passage of goods from the sources to the destinations. As for Bardhere district, the road are very accessible and its close immediacy to Mogadishu city allows the transports to be easier and cost effective. Whereas in Badhadhe district, it is proximity to Kismayo port makes it possible for goods to reach its final destination, as well as the road from Kismayo to Badhahde district is safe and accessible. On the other hand, in all the three target districts, the labour market is very effective, as such there are a considerable number of manual worker who are willing to work for conditional cash transfers, working within the minimal standard wage in the specific structure. Furthermore, ADA's Monitoring Units will ensure to carry out Market Assessment before the project start, this is to establish that the market is sufficiently functioning to maximise the cash programming. ADA’s Market Assessment findings will further be substantiated with the bi-weekly price monitoring data from the food security clusters and other agencies.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization: ADA verifies and ensures that beneficiaries identified in the tripartite (ADA, elders and council members) participatory selection process meet the selection criteria (female, male, age, IDP, household heads, etc.). ADA holds sensitization and communication sessions with all targeted communities about the selection criteria of the project. The same information is broadcasted through local radio before activities start. ADA selection criteria ensures that at least 30% of beneficiaries are women-head households for each programme activity. Moreover, ADA has formal complaint and feedback mechanisms in place, including a Call Centre and a toll-free Hotline. Third Party Monitors provide further opportunity for beneficiaries to report on the selection process, provide feedback and complain External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: In addition to the above, FAO heavily engages with district, state and federal authorities to facilitate and solve issues of interference by external stakeholders. ADA triangulates information on alleged cases of interference and seeks mediation from relevant parties (elders, ADA staff, local authorities, etc.). If the case cannot be resolved, ADA reprogrammed activities to other districts. All contracts with ADA include a clause on zero-tolerance of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), and obligate beneficiaries and other stakeholders to report such cases. ADA staff and counterparts receive dedicated trainings on AAP, Prevention of SEA and gender inclusion principles of ADA activities. Protection mainstreaming: In addition to the above, ADA mitigates these protection risks by avoiding large gatherings of beneficiaries, through conducting registration and input distribution at village level.
