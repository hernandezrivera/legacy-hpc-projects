Project Summary

The economy, severely impacted by the February EQ & conflict on October 23, has seen a major downturn. Data from REACH and ILO note significant declines in economic activity and jobs, with about 170,000 workers losing jobs due to EQ in February 23, impacting 725K Ind. While there is a high percentage of females working in the agriculture sector, 80% of them indicated that there were fewer jobs compared to other years, emphasizing challenges related to operating costs, water scarcity, and a lack of agricultural inputs. In January 2023, before the EQ, over 80% of businesses in Albab and Dana were interested in expansing, albeit with high costs (cost of business assessment). According to IMMAP’s recent crop monitoring report, farmers reported a decline in production, due to factors such as drought and elevated prices of agricultural inputs such as seeds and fuel. The proposed project aims to support vulnerable populations lacking reliable income by improving access to markets and income-generating opportunities. CARE will establish VSLAs to provide disadvantaged communities with a platform for saving and accessing loans for business startups or acquiring productive assets. Infrastructure rehabilitation and maintenance of electricity stations will ensure equitable access to essential services, livelihood opportunities, and social cohesion.CARE's layered and sequenced activities, including vocational training and support for new and existing businesses, will enhance market resilience. The wheat and livestock VC initiative is pivotal in fostering economic sustainability and food security in NWS. By supporting the wheat-to-bread and its infrastructure, CARE aims to strengthen market systems, enhance sustainable livelihoods, and improve access to productive assets.CARE will provide cash assistance for agriculture support. The cash values are estimated by market assessments and FSL cluster guidance.CARE will collaborate with farmers andstakeholders to train farmers on effective soil enhancement techniques Seed quality are offered to both farmers and seed vendors, emphasizing the use of llocal varieties adapted to the agro-ecology and ensuring seed quality through germination tests. CARE will purchase flour to support bread subsidies, allocating 30% of this support as free bread to camp residents during the winter season.For bread production,high-quality ingredients from trusted suppliers will be provided, Staff will conduct regular inspections and tests throughout the baking process to monitor dough consistency, baking time and temperature, and product appearance, ensuring quality taste, texture, and appearance. CARE's livestock mobile clinics will provide essential veterinary services, while support for animal feed during the cold season and promotion of green fodder cultivation will ensure a sustainable source of fodder. Technical teams will supervise the mixing process of animal feed and ensure the purchase of both medicines and tools from trusted suppliers.- Integration of CARE ensures integration of protection priorities and provides training on the Code of Conduct & Safeguarding Policy to program staff. - Conflict Sensitivity and Risk Assessment: Utilizes tools like Do No Harm to identify potential harmful impacts of activities, prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries, especially women.- Global Protection Cluster Guidance: Committed to mitigating physical and psychological threats faced by affected populations, providing safety training and equipment, and facilitating access to assistance and services.-GBV Mitigation: Implements mitigation measures in line with IASC Guidelines, considering the risk of increased GBV due to changes in power dynamics resulting from women's. Youth and PWDs will be engaged during the design and implementation through integration with the protection team, inclusive outreach programs, tailored training sessions, and accessible communication channels.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In Northwest Syria (NWS), women face barriers to market inclusion due to male dominance. While increasing their participation risks gender-based violence (GBV), GBV itself exacerbates food insecurity by limiting access to food and livelihoods. Prolonged displacement and disability stigmas worsen livelihood challenges for IDPs. CARE and partners prioritize safety by monitoring risks, involving men in programs, and providing PSEA training. They ensure safe livelihood locations and establish complaint mechanisms. Access is ensured through transportation and childcare support, promoting gender parity and inclusivity. CARE's strategy focuses on value chains, climate adaptation, and financial access, addressing earthquake aftermath through debris removal, infrastructure rehabilitation, and MSMEs recovery. Skills training, start-up kits, and support for struggling businesses are provided, along with temporary job opportunities for infrastructure restoration. VSLAs and community committees foster cohesion and decision-making. Wheat-to-bread support enhances sustainability and bread availability.The intervention will span for 40-60 days, considering FSL recommendations 200- 250 gr/P/D.FFS and climate-smart agriculture improve farming, while infrastructure projects benefit agriculture and solar energy adoption. CFW aligns with seasonal activities, and livestock services aid breeders. Budget for activities is pending.The active sectoral participation in clusters/working groups enables CARE to remain informed on challenges, ensure effective and timely coordination, to share lessons learned, and to support a consistent approach across FSL actors. CARE remains engaged with the GBV SC for information sharing and technical advice and potential referral pathways. Risk: IDPs in NWS face prolonged displacement and limited livelihood options, compounded by misconceptions and stigmas around disabilities.Assumption: Supporting livelihoods is essential for empowering IDPs and reducing their dependency on aid.Mitigating Measures: CARE will monitor contextual and protection risks, provide market-relevant skills training, and ensure meaningful access to livelihood activities. Community committees will foster social cohesion.Risk: The budget for proposed activities is not yet secured, posing a risk to implementation.Assumption: Securing adequate funding is crucial for successful interventions.Mitigating Measures: CARE will seek funding opportunities actively, develop contingency plans, and prioritize essential activities to address potential funding shortfalls.Risk: Market resilience challenges may hinder livelihood interventions, especially in a male-dominated market environment.Assumption: Enhancing market resilience is vital for sustainable economic empowerment.Mitigating Measures: CARE will conduct market assessments, engage local stakeholders, and implement gender-sensitive approaches. Continuous monitoring will ensure responsiveness to market fluctuations.
