Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is highly complex and dynamic, requiring precise, triangulated, timely, and easily accessible information to guide effective interventions. This project is designed to equip the humanitarian community with thoroughly analyzed and actionable data to address current and anticipated information gaps. The focus is on displacement, returns, and general population movements across South Sudan. By doing so, the project aims to support a fair and evidence-based delivery of humanitarian aid as well as early recovery programming.
Project Objectives and Deliverables
Regularly Updated IDPs and Returnees Baseline:
A comprehensive baseline will be maintained at both location and area levels. This will include detailed information on population presence, reasons for movement, and the specific needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees.
Event Tracking for Sudden Displacement:
Through the International Organization for Migration (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) event tracking mechanism, regular updates will be provided on sudden displacement or return movements that require urgent humanitarian assistance.
Biometric Data Management:
Biometric systems will be utilized to ensure accountable, effective, and targeted delivery of humanitarian aid to those most in need.
Representative Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA):
Household-level assessments will be conducted to gather critical data on needs, vulnerabilities, and intentions of affected populations. This information will directly inform South Sudan’s Humanitarian Response Planning (HRP) process as well as the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO).
Population Mobility Monitoring:
Regular updates will be provided on population mobility at border points and key internal transit hubs—including displacement sites—using DTM’s flow monitoring mechanism.
Data for Solutions to Internal Displacement (DSID):
Progress toward achieving durable solutions for displaced populations will be measured through DSID data collection efforts.
By delivering these outputs, the project seeks to bridge critical information gaps while enabling a coordinated response that aligns with the priorities of affected communities.

1. Needs

South Sudan presents a particularly complex environment due to its unique mobility dynamics, shaped by both spontaneous and organized population movements. These dynamics necessitate a well-informed and adaptive humanitarian response, as well as early recovery programming grounded in timely, accurate, and accessible information. To effectively address the needs of affected populations, data must be evidence-based, actionable, and capable of providing regular updates to humanitarian actors, development organizations, and government partners. The International Organization for Migration (IOM) Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mobility Tracking Round 15 data reveals that South Sudan currently has a total displaced population of 1.8 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), including more than 300,000 individuals displaced specifically due to widespread flooding across the country. Additionally, there are 2.8 million returnees, including those who have returned from internal displacement within South Sudan as well as cross-border movements linked to the ongoing crisis in neighboring Sudan. The number of returnees has seen a significant increase in 2024 following the outbreak of conflict in Sudan; flow monitoring at border points indicates that nearly 300,000 South Sudanese nationals had arrived back in South Sudan by mid-September 2024 alone. Exacerbating this situation are continuous sub-national violence, armed clashes between various groups, and recurrent flooding events that worsen preexisting vulnerabilities among affected populations. With many years of being at the forefront of humanitarian data provision globally and in South Sudan, IOM’s DTM information management system has been well-positioned to support the population in need by providing real-time data on displacement trends, returns, spontaneous events, flow monitoring, biometric registration data, and related surveys to partners responding through various clusters across the country. IOM DTM has been working closely with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) under existing cooperation agreements. Both RRC and NBS have endorsed the DTM methodology, regularly support data collection efforts on displacement trends across South Sudan’s states, and have approved IDP and returnee baseline figures as the official data for the country.

2. Project activities

DTM will capitalize on the successes achieved since its inception by providing quality data to stakeholders, partners, and the government. DTM’s country-wide recognition as the country’s main source of displacement data among other essential data for humanitarian response and transition. DTM will conduct mobility tracking assessments with a presence in all 10 states (78 counties) and the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) to provide regular estimated displaced and returning population figures, locations, reasons for movement, and priority of humanitarian needs for IDPs and Returnees. DTM will leverage its network of key informants and enumerators in over 3,900 locations to ensure the regular provision of IDP baseline data. DTM will undertake two rounds of the Mobility Tracking activity. The event tracking mechanism will build on its existing network of 350 enumerators across 500 sub-areas within the country to track new events in 2024 - spontaneous displacements due to floods (new and recurrent), and sub-national and localized conflicts. Prompt tracking and reporting will contribute to early warning efforts, informing upon rapid responses and conflict prevention. Spontaneous updates will be provided for critical events or those requested by humanitarian partners and regularly shared with the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) as the key inter-agency platform for discussion on geographical prioritization of the response. Furthermore, bi-weekly updates will be shared with partners and available at an interactive public dashboard. In addition, IOM DTM will conduct a representative Inter-Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA) at the household level in coordination with OCHA and will continue to collaborate with partners on the design, implementation, and analysis through the Technical Working Group (TWG). In collaboration with the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), IOM will continue using enumeration areas as the basis for creating the sampling frame. Methodology and tools will be decided in collaboration with partners and relevant stakeholders to ensure information is collected and analyzed in a timely manner to feed into the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Humanitarian Response Plan for 2024. DTM will continue with daily data collection at over 20 strategic border points and internal transit hubs through flow monitoring registry. The network of observation points will be maintained and adjusted through regular monitoring and evaluation process to ensure coverage over key mobility corridors. Data collected and analysed from flow monitoring actives will provide timely information on displacement, mixed migration and return routes which would improve knowledge of migrant profiles, movement intention and motivation. Further on, the data will be analysed and shared with partners to inform planning and response in case of any public health emergencies (e.g. Ebola, COVID-19). DTM will continue with scaled-up joint flow monitoring at border with Sudan in response to ongoing Sudan Crisis, working together with UNHCR and RRC and publish arrivals on joint dashboard. With over one million uniquely registered individuals in DTM’s secure database, IOM will undertake biometric registration of new beneficiaries as requested by partners through the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) at both federal and state levels, among others. Biometric maintenance will be conducted to update the status and locations of beneficiaries in the database particularly focusing on the main displacement sites in Juba (IDP Camp 1 & 3), Bentiu (Bentiu IDP Camp), Malakal (Malakal Protection of Civilian Site) and Wau (Naivasha IDP Camp, Masna Collective Center). Biometric data sharing will be provided as regulated through relevant data sharing agreements and in adherence to IOM’s data protection principles. IOM DTM will also support partners with regular population counts at major displacement sites (Juba, Wau, Malakal, Bentiu) and other areas upon request.
