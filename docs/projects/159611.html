Project Summary

The proposed intervention (approved 2019 HRP HPSE19-FSC-150062-1) will target the food security and livelihoods needs of the most vulnerable farming and herding communities affected by protection threats in Area C of the West Bank. The activities will contribute to their enhanced protection from forcible transfer by improving the main factors involved in food production (roads, water, livestock shelter) and agricultural inputs. By doing so, the proposed intervention will contribute to improving the food security of marginalized families living under the poverty line.
The project activities will beneﬁt all members of the household, including women and children, and will be carried out on two levels: at household level through the rehabilitation of animal shelters and the supply of agricultural inputs; and at community level by improving the access to water and the communities’ accessibility (road rehabilitation).
The proposed intervention will deal with the protection threats to the livelihoods of the targeted families. Fomenting a community participation approach in the assessment, design and implementation of the activities, all the community’s sub-groups will participate in different focus group discussions, technical assessments and in the implementation process. The communication with communities (CWCs) will mitigate potential security and protection risks and, ultimately, empower the beneﬁciaries at community, family and individual level.
As outlined, the proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as participation and empowerment.

1 - Needs

The conditions in the West Bank are characterised as a protracted crisis, with repeated shocks and continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and lack of economic opportunities resulting in high unemployment and low household incomes. This, together with the high cost of living has left 1.7 million Palestinians (350,000 of them located in West Bank’s Area C) food insecure and in urgent need of assistance (HNO 2020). In Area C where the Israeli restrictions against livelihood development are increasing; more than 52% of the families are either food insecure or on the edge of the food security line (SEFSec 2018 Analysis). Families who are already food insecure and have limited resilience are unlikely to be able to retain (or rebuild) their livelihoods with the expected loss of the last of their remaining productive assets. The Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) has identiﬁed the most affected during emergency weather crisis in the agricultural sector as livestock, greenhouses, ﬁeld crops and agricultural infrastructure. Women have limited space in the agricultural sector despite their active involvement in agricultural activities. Food insecure families are spending 50% of their income on food, other basic needs such as health and education are deprioritized, the families are forced to sell part of their assets to cover their basic needs, causing a deterioration of the agricultural sector and the national food security rates. 163 communities reported a decrease in their access to land; almost 80% of them are located in Area C or East Jerusalem (OCHA Vulnerability Proﬁle Plus assessment 2017). Communities in central West Bank are under threat of forcible transfer (OCHA). Structures owned by Palestinians in Area C are also subject to demolition; between January and August 2019, a total of 410 Palestinian structures were destroyed, 42% of them were livelihood infrastructures. Palestinians are affected by adverse weather conditions with the associated high winds and ﬂash ﬂoods destroying farms and vital agricultural infrastructure, damaging crops, killing livestock and depleting savings when limited household funds are allocated to damage repair. 2019-20 winter could potentially bring high winds, storms, ﬂash ﬂooding and frosts, exacerbating the already poor humanitarian status for large segments of the targeted population, namely women, girls and boys. In September 2019, AAH carried out an assessment of 76 communities in Area C through stakeholders’ interviews, focus groups with families in the communities and questionnaires. This enabled AAH to identify communities requiring priority emergency support due to their vulnerability (unemployment rate, income, demolition/eviction orders, and sensitivity of their location). These families lose their assets due to demolishing incidents or natural hazards and are unable to recover due to their limited ﬁnancial capacities. Female headed households were identified as the most vulnerable group due to limited access to livelihood opportunities for example they cannot go to certain grazing areas (high risk). Inside the household, women and children are the most disadvantaged regarding food security status, women are responsible for cooking for the family, children are affected by malnutrition. An animal shelter assessment was conducted by AAH, updated in March 2019, covering 6 Governorates in Areas A, B and C through interviews with MOA directorate in each Governorate and questionnaires. This conﬁrmed that herding is the main source of income for most herding families (90% of the targeted families) - especially in Area C. The study highlighted that most of the animal shelters in the targeted areas are not adequate for the ﬂock size resulting in overcrowded shelters. In addition, a proportion of the ﬂock spent extensive periods of time in the open thus resulting in high abortion and mortality rates (25% in most of the targeted communities).

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will facilitate an appropriate community-led emergency response to reduce vulnerability, enhance resilience and protect livelihoods during emergencies. Partners AAH, PARC and UWAC will coordinate the ﬁnal deﬁnition and selection of localities and beneﬁciaries with MOA directorates, and with other organizations working in the same area and sector. To ensure diversity and a balanced gender and age representation, communities’ residents, mainly women children and elderly family members, will participate in all stages of the project cycle. Focus group discussions with female family members and female head of HHS will take place separately to eliminate any cultural and social barriers preventing them to actively participate in the project activities.PARC and UWAC will be responsible for the collection and analysis of baseline and end line data according to their area of expertise with AAH’s support for UWAC. PARC and UWAC have been fully involved in the project design, identiﬁcation of target area and communities, and development of technical components of the activities. The targeted areas and activities will be divided between the two partners, who will be responsible for monitoring the implementation of activities with AAH. Cooperating with line ministries and working the partners will ensure sustainability, participation, empowerment and support the development of self-protection. A.1 Improve the access to agricultural water for vulnerable farming communities (Hebron including H2, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Jericho and Tubas): the intervention will reduce the input costs for their farming activities. Families who are under the threat of forced displacement and their lands under the threat of conﬁscation will be targeted. The interventions will cover construction and rehabilitation of water pipes (20 Km), 80 agricultural cisterns and bonds, and rehabilitate 3 springs. A.2 Livestock accommodation for 350 vulnerable herders, damaged emergency and poor-quality shelters will be rehabilitated with resistant plastic covers (S. Hebron, Bethlehem, Jerusalem and Tubas): this emergency intervention will help ensure hygienic conditions and maintain production levels during milking and milk processing. A.3 Supply of agricultural inputs to improve total productivity of farming activities (Jericho and Tubas): the intervention will target vulnerable farmers with low incomes and under the poverty line and who aren’t ensuring the maximum beneﬁts of their agricultural plots due to limited ﬁnancial capacities and Israeli restrictions against construction in (Area C) by supplying them with agricultural tools and material for rehabilitating 260 green houses and 50 irrigation systems. A.4 Improve the most vulnerable herding and farming families’ safe access to livelihood assets and services through road rehabilitation (20 km roads in Hebron, Bethlehem and Ramallah), allowing them to reach their agricultural lands and grazing areas and protect them from forcible transfer by enabling them to satisfy their basic needs. The proposed activity will also protect their livelihoods assets (land and grazing areas) from conﬁscation for settler expansion or road construction. A.5 Support vulnerable farmers and herders to rehabilitate their lands under threat of conﬁscation (close to bypass roads, settlements, military areas) (Hebron and Bethlehem): this activity aims to improve land productivity and reduce inputs’ costs (rehabilitation of 200 Dunums). A.6 Improve access to locally produced pastures (20 dunums per herder, for 500 herders) by providing drought tolerant seeds. Female herders and farmers who own livestock, land, greenhouses will be supported directly to improve the productivity of their land and asset and to increase household income. The project activities will prioritise them to promote their role within the HH and to decrease aid dependency of the women headed households (from both relatives and humanitarian aid).

3 - Indicators

A baseline survey will be conducted at the start of the project targeting a representation of all gender and age groups, which will be updated and compared to an end line survey after the activities’ completion. Information will be collected through semi structured interviews, household visits and ﬁeld observations. Selection criteria will be identiﬁed by the partners in coordination with MOA and the related local councils, based on MOA classiﬁcation for governorates priorities AAH and the partners will coordinate with the related MOA directorate in each governorate to beneﬁt from their assessments and data, the project main target groups are the vulnerable herding and farming families in Area C. Ongoing monitoring will allow AAH and PARC to evaluate methodologies and the impact of the activities, to identify challenges and best practices. Particular attention will be paid to beneﬁciary selection based on diversity including their economical and displacement vulnerability, methods of working with village councils, mechanism of asset distribution and gender sensitive approach. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation; the results communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneﬁciaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through the establishment of AAH’s standard beneﬁciary feedback mechanism. Output 1: 310 farming HH (1488 individuals) receive agricultural inputs (greenhouses and irrigation systems) Output 2: 820 female and male headed households (3936 individuals) receive technical assistance to rehabilitate the water resources (pipes, springs and cisterns) Output 3: Improve the physical access for 4 communities through the rehabilitation of 20 km of agricultural roads (2880 individuals). Output 4: 350 herders (1680 women and men) receive technical and material assistance to rehabilitate their animal shelters. Output 5: 500 herding HH (2400 individuals) received climate adapted (drought tolerant) seeds for locally produced pastures (20 dunums per herder); Output 6: 70 farmers (336 individuals) beneﬁted from the rehabilitation of 200 dunums The expected objective veriﬁable indicators and targets from the project are as follows: - Outcome 1: The livestock mortality and abortion rates for the households targeted by livestock accommodation decreased by 30%; - Outcome 2: The agricultural water cost for the communities targeted by water interventions will decreased by 35%; - Outcome 3: The targeted farmers targeted by agricultural inputs will improve their productivity by more than 25%.To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the indicator “% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” is added and will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. As a specific protection mainstreaming component AAH is in the process of developing its own protection mainstreaming toolkit and to start working with its own sector specific gender and protection mainstreaming checklists. Up to date AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheets. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. AAH in oPt prioritises this also through context specific (risk) analyses as well as the protection coordinator position stirring questions and discussion. Overall AAH seeks to complement its own actions with for instance legal support to inform risk mitigation measures and as a consequence to increase complementary humanitarian action. For activities that include physical interventions (including constructions of animal shelter and roads, water pipes rehabilitation), AAH will coordinate with the relevant legal entities to evaluate how this intervention will affect the legal status of the targeted communities.AAH will do the hard construction works during Fridays and the holidays of the ICA to attract minimal attention from them, using low visibility implementation and coordinating with beneficiaries (especially animal shelter rehabilitation) to cover the new structures while ensuring that the new works will not change the overall appearance of the communities.Activities will be implemented gradually (in different stages) in order to 1) avoid catching the attention of the ICA 2) to ensure that the communities adapt to new interventions (integrating their feedback and complaints). In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH continuously works on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels. as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, in spring 2019, all staff received specific in person training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Even more, most of AAH staff have been gone through protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is increasingly using ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. AAH has safe and accessible beneficiary feedback mechanisms in place which allow beneficiaries to provide their inputs, complaints, and satisfaction regarding the activities implemented. They are designed to increase beneficiaries’ protection and comply with sensitive data management guidelines to uphold the principle of ‘Do No Harm’. Beneficiary knowledge of this system are promoted during field assessments and monitoring visits during which AAH and partners staff explain how the mechanisms work. In addition, a paragraph will be included in each agreement with beneficiaries explaining the steps to report complaints through the AAH beneficiary feedback mechanisms which are accessible by phone, email, confidential complaint box or in person (AAH or partners field staff or base members), according to AAH protocols. A service phone number will be distributed to the population in the proposed target communities, enabling them to report feedback or complaints relating to project implementation or AAH employees’ behaviour. AAH will process and treat confidentially all information collected with ad-hoc committee composed of project staff to understand the situation, identify appropriate solutions and include technical adjustments or modifications if needed. A monthly meeting will be conducted at community level to update beneficiaries and community leaders relating to project development and challenges.With regards to access in this specific project, AAH will use the blanket coverage approach for livelihood needs so that no community members will be left behind and have equal access to livelihood opportunities.The clustering approach (targeting communities living close to each other physically and contextually) will also be used to impact close communities equally. Coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture and other organisations specialised in livelihood support will ensure that the quality of the response will not leave the space for comparison between communities targeted, the types of interventions will be as much as possible unified between the different cluster members. Additionally, AAH will take advantage of MoA’s database and their list of beneficiaries and communities who already benefited from such interventions, to avoid overlapping or replications in areas previously targeted by the MoA and other organisations.
