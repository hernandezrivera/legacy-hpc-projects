Project Summary

This project aims at contributing to all three sector objectives for crisis-affected population to access basic water services, adequate, protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services, and to hygiene promotion and other environmental health interventions. It targets a total of 75,123 people: girls (22,988), boys (22,086 ), young men (14,724) and women (15,325), and particularly those with disabilities (9,015) among vulnerable residents, IDPs, and returnees. These groups of people exposed to water from unprotected open surface in household and school setting which this project intends on contributing to tackling in through interventions to be implemented in South Kordofan and North Darfur. They live in areas that have seen prolonged conflict and instability which exacerbate access issues to WASH, on top of the country’s economic crisis causing high costs of infrastructure and maintenance, and low national budgetary support and prioritisation by Government. Implementation will be in three localities in North Darfur (El Fasher, Tawila and Kutum), and one South Kordofan State (Kadugli). Plan will integrate water, sanitation and hygiene interventions in this project, and as well as with education, nutrition and health activities to contribute to reducing malnutrition and epidemics.

1 - Needs

Project localities in North Darfur are Elfasher, Kutum, Tawila, & in South Kordofan its Kadugli. Access to improved water sources is generally poor, with collection time exceeding 30 minutes for households compared to the populations in need (35%, 55%, 63% and 86% respectively for El Fasher, Kutum, Tawila, & Kadugli (WASH HNO 2022)). In Korgy, Dilaba, and Midel communities of Kutum locality, communities report their main source of drinking water as open dug wells. Where it exists, infrastructure is dilapidated or inadequate - villages of Nemado, Dogoie, Um Dirisi, Mashashaia, Kohliate in Kadugli indicate 10 water handpumps which need to be rehabilitated & solar system/ water tanks installed. Focus group discussions held by Plan International in June 2021 with girls, boys, committees, & PTAs in the Kutum communities listed above revealed that children carry jerrycans on their way to school and collect water from wells, taking a long time as these wells have low production due to non-maintenance. In Elfasher, community leaders reported during a discussion meeting in August 2021 that boreholes installed with motorised pumps and hand pumps have not been maintained for some time due to lack funds, fuel shortages, and unavailability of spare parts. Lastly, the quality of water is poor, especially for those that access unprotected water sources (54% El Fasher, 7% Kutum, 24% Tawila, and 8% Kadugli (WASH HNO 2022)) or surface water. The Director of the Water Resources and Electricity Sector in Kadugli shared with Plan International that surface water is not treated, & is only chlorinated in case of disease outbreaks. The sanitation is poor & a number of schools lack adequate gender segregated latrines. Measured against the total population of people in need (475,108 in El Fasher, 134,540 Kutum, 196,838 Tawila & 85,898 Kadugli), those accessing improved shared facilities with more than 20 people is low (14%, 2%, 3%, 10% respectively in El Fasher, Kutum, Tawila, & Kadugli). The majority access unimproved facilities so are in critical/ extreme condition (WASH HNO 2022). Open defecation is widely practiced, particular among children, and per WASH HNO 2022 the number in catastrophic position is high (145,552 in El Fasher, 65,048 in Kutum, 142,274 in Tawila, & 30,251 in Kadugli). In Kadugli, the Directors of the Water Resources and Electricity Sector and of WES indicated the need for equipment and mechanisms to manage environmental sanitation. In villages in El Fasher rural (Amarjadeed, karkara, umoshar, Umhashab), members reported that limited sanitation initiatives have taken place. During after-action & annual project reviews (SHF funded SUD-19/HSD20/SA1/E-FSL-H-N-P-WASH/INGO/12804, & a SIDA funded OPEG project respectively), children said the community practices open defecation, & non-gendered latrines are only available for teachers in the school. Children use streams & bushes. Communities also need interventions against poor waste management and disposal methods contaminating water and causing diseases. Hygiene practices are extremely poor across total population projections for all the four localities – total projected populations currently stand at 570,716 for El Fasher, 237,751 Kutum, 142,036 Tawila, and 153,636 in Kadugli. Of these, the WASH HNO 2022 indicate populations in need of hygiene and are classified as being in extremely critical/ catastrophic 2022 are 777,972, 218,116, 232,823 and 122,317 in El Fasher, Kutum, Tawila, and Kadugli respectively. Major consequences of this is high vulnerability & exposure to vector and water-borne diseases from epidemics, COVID-19 pandemic, and floods which for girls and boys can contribute to health issues such as cholera, diarrhoea, dysentery and ultimately malnutrition in children. In schools within these localities especially because of COVID-19 pandemic risks, children have no access to soap and adequate water for hand washing, or cleaning kits.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project has been designed and revised to fit within the WASH sector strategy, scope and planned budget for 2022, and thus its targeted reach is 75,123 people in need. This will be 43,177 in El Fasher, 12,105 in Kutum, 12,922 in Tawila, and 6,919 in Kadugli localities. This represents a targeted reach under 1% of the sector’s own targeted reach per locality. The project will support the following WASH activities in communities, Health Facilities (WinHF), and in schools (WinS): - Construction, rehabilitation/ upgrading of water facilities- Water quality and quantity monitoring - Support operation and maintenance of water systems - Form and train community WASH committees - Construct gender segregated and child friendly communal latrine- Rehabilitate and maintain existing school, communal, health facility latrines- Provide child friendly hand-washing facilities for new and rehabilitated- Conduct hygiene promotion campaigns and provide WASH kits- Support adolescent girls and women in adequate Menstrual Hygiene management, including distribution of hygiene kits- Conduct vector control- Support via hygiene messages and kits to OTP and stabilisation centres, and for households with children having acute malnutrition

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project intends to ensure equal and safe participation of target beneficiaries, particularly girls and women, who are most at risk of being exposed to protection violations. Plan International will ensure this through:- Involving women, men, boys and girls in design, identification of latrine/water points location, hygiene kit contents, etc. - Involving women in setting priorities and making decisions about water supply programs- Consulting women on how water is collected, and ensure water supply is also accessible to women with limited mobility- Providing containers for water collection and storage- Providing separate latrines facilities for girls, boys, and women- Ensuring facilities are positioned in safe places to ensure the privacy and security of women and girls, as identified by beneficiaries- Ensuring women’s leadership and participation e.g. in WASH committees for successful programming
