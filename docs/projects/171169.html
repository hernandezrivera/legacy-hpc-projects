Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains critical due to the impacts of ongoing displacement, conflict and seasonal floods. It is expected that the Desert Locust, COVID-19 pandemic and the anticipated below average rain will contribute towards the deterioration of agricultural production outputs and social economics increased vulnerabilities of households which will further aggravate the already poor malnutrition status of children. It is estimated that 5.1 million people are facing food insecurity of whom 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to be in Crisis or Emergency Status (IPC Phase 3 or higher). The potential consequences of severe food insecurity will affect the most vulnerable people particularly children under 5, women, elder and disable people who may experience deterioration of their nutrition and health status due to weak immunity system, poor feeding practices and limited access to basic services including water and sanitation.
The post Gu 2020 nutrition assessment findings showed marginal decrease in the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of 11.8 % down from 13.8% of post GU 2019 and 14% of post Gu 2018. The corresponding median SAM prevalence are: 1.8% (Gu 2020) down from 2.3% (Gu 2019), 2.2% (Gu 2018). The post GU 2020 nutrition assessment has also indicated critical malnutrition situation among the IDPs particularly Bossaso, Banadir, Garowe and Galkacyo IDPs. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) are disproportionally affected by nutrition crisis compare to host communities because majority of the IDPs are poor and live in urban areas in desperate conditions and have limited access to livelihood assets and social services. Overall, children affected by severe acute malnutrition are nine times more at risk of death compared to those who are well-nourished. According to UNICEF Somalia Situation Report of Sept 2020, Nutrition Programme treated 15,755 children with severe acute malnutrition (56% being female) in UNICEF supported programmes, despite a month-on-month decline in the number of children screened for malnutrition since April as a result of COVID-19 limitations. This brings the total SAM cases reached from Jan to Sept to 181, 242, which surpassed UNICEF annual target by 35.7%. The nutrition cluster estimated that 962,000 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through October 2021, including 162,007 who are likely to be to be severely malnourished. Under UNICEF HRP, out of the 162,007 children with SAM, UNICEF will target 75% of Cluster target which is estimated at 121,500 (61, 965 girls, 59,535 boys) and 25,000 children with moderate acute malnutrition under the simplified protocol for the management of acute malnutrition in close consultation with WFP.
Gender inequality exacerbates food insecurity, malnutrition and poverty in humanitarian crises. UNICEF recognizes that girls, boys, men, women and people with disabilities have different nutritional needs at different life stages with different capacities and risks in accessing nutrition services. Therefore, UNICEF will ensure provision of nutrition services through life cycle approach and target nutrition sites will function as a platform for the delivery of integrated multi sector services particularly WASH, C4D, health, GBV and safety net. Key interventions of this HRP will aim to deliver promotive and preventive services (Micronutrient supplementation, deworming, IYCF, PLW) and early detection and referral for treatment of SAM targeting in priority rural and IDP communities with equal access to boys, girls, and women. UNICEF as main provider of nutrition supplies for management of severe acute malnutrition, will ensure procurement of nutrition supplies for nutrition cluster partners in Somalia (e.g. SHF, SHINE, Consortium and those with UNICEF PCAs) including the 25% buffer stock at any given time in the year.

Mitigation Measures

To prevent sexual violence and sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access tohumanitarian assistance , UNICEF organized training and capacity building ofimplementing partners on prevention and response to sexual exploitation. UNICEFcontinues to stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct. Therefore, allUNICEF implementing partners, are obliged to create and maintain an environment thatprevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Managers at all levels have aresponsibility to support and develop systems that maintain this environment, arecommitted to the protection of vulnerable populations in humanitarian crisis, includingfrom sexual exploitation and abuse. By entering into an agreement with UNICEF andimplementing partner undertakes to adhere to: (i) the standards set out in theSecretary-General’s Bulletin “Special measures for protection from sexual exploitationand sexual abuse” (ST/SGB/2003/13); (ii) any minimum operating standards adoptedbecause of the Statement of Commitment on Eliminating Sexual Exploitation andAbuse. (iii) any other Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy orguideline. Accountability to affected population and need based principle : UNICEF and implementing partner will ensure affected populations to play an active role in the decision-makingprocesses that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practicesto engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalised and affected arerepresented and have influence. UNICEF and partners will adhere the principles of humanitarian principles including "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project effaced population and beneficiaries. women, men, girls andboys will be engaged in decision-making and enhanced their resilience / capacity toprevent and response to severe acute malnutrition
