Project Summary

As the crisis in Syria continues into its thirteenth year, the needs of the population, including the Palestine refugees, are higher than ever. Years of protracted conflict and ongoing socio-economic decline have taken a heavy toll on the population. The situation has been further aggravated by the earthquakes and multiple aftershocks in Türkiye that impacted northern Syria from 6 February 2023 onwards.
To support Palestine refugee youth access to employment opportunities, the UNRWA TVET programme will continue to provide long-term courses to 1,367 trainees in Damascus, Aleppo, Homs, Hama, Latakia, and Dera'a. To ensure the quality and effectiveness of the training courses, the instructors and support staff will receive relevant training, and the learning environment will be enhanced through necessary maintenance and updating of equipment. Furthermore, UNRWA will deliver short-term courses to 800 trainees in Damascus, Dera’a, Homs, Aleppo, Latakia, and Hama offering a variety of specializations that respond to the needs of the local market. Short-term courses (three months on average) are designed to strengthen technical skills, with minimal financial costs to the refugee students, to improve their prospects of accessing the labour market. Both long-term and short-term courses are available for the vulnerable Palestine refugee youth including persons with disabilities.
UNRWA will also provide employability and soft skills sessions to TVET trainees to encourage their confidence and abilities in approaching companies and communicating with potential future employers during job interviews and facilitate the recruitment opportunity through Open Day / Job Fair. To provide comprehensive support to the Palestine refugee youth, resident psychosocial counsellors will provide necessary psychosocial support to trainees attending and/or living in the dormitory of Damascus Training Centre. Transportation of vulnerable students will also be subsidised to mitigate the impact of the fuel crisis and associated rising transportation costs on the ability of students to access VTCs.
The various components of UNRWA's interventions in 2024 will also contribute to supporting refugees who have spontaneously returned to their places of residence either internally or from other countries. Where possible, UNRWA will aim at restarting services in camps that were heavily destroyed during the conflict (Dera’a, Yarmouk and Ein el Tal) through the rehabilitation of the Agency’s installations (including schools and clinics) in these camps. UNRWA will also conduct minor maintenance of its installations to improve its effectiveness, safety, and accessibility.
Finally, depending on the availability of funds, UNRWA will explore the feasibility of conducting small-scale cash-for-work initiatives in 2024 mainly focusing on unskilled job placements supporting UNRWA works in camps and Palestine refugee gatherings.
Lastly, this action contributes to the achievement of the strategic objective 3 (Increase resilience and access to services) under HRP 2024-2025.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection risk: Violence against children in and around UNRWA schools. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: 1. Deliver training to address and decrease violence in and around UNRWA installations, including peer-to-peer violence. 2. Counselling and psychosocial support is provided to the students 3. UNRWA addresses cases of violence against students from staff members using available investigative and sanctions regimes. Risk: Exposure to Explosive Ordnance as student access UNRWA TVET centres. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: 1. Facilitator and focal point training of staff and awareness-raising activities are conducted with students to increase their knowledge and reduce their exposure to these risks. 2. Advocacy for rubble removal around UNRWA installations.Risk: Students/families resorting to negative coping strategies due to the declining economic situation (dropout, early marriage, drug abuse). Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation:1. Psychosocial support activities in TVET centres enhanced. 2. Youth targeted for awareness activities at TVET and community centres. PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN ClearCheck recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations.
