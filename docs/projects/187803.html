Project Summary

The malnutrition situation continues to deteriorate in NWS, the main drivers include poverty, low socioeconomic status, unsafe living conditions and diseases, suboptimal maternal, infant, and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices, and high rates of maternal malnutrition. The economic downturn and depreciation of the Syrian Pound in addition to the impact of COVID-19 in NWS had an intensifying effect on the staggering results. In response to the identified need, NGO X will implement the following package of activities which will benefit 37,800 individuals
1. Provide community-based one-to-one counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding and maternal nutrition practices.
2. Provision of specialized nutritious food (LNS) to children 6-59 months
3. Early identification and referral of Acute Malnutrition cases in PLW and children 6-59 months
4. Provision of supplementary assistance via e-vouchers for households with PLW and/or children under 2 years to access nutritious foods
MIYCF messaging and counselling is a component in varying delivery methods including posters, group sessions and household visits. Family MUAC Approach (FMA) is also included on group and individual levels, providing the caregivers MUAC tapes and the nearest functioning nutrition services for self-referral where needed. Screening and referral by the nutrition staff will be provided at household visits.
Ongoing activities blanket distribution of LNS-SQ for children 6-59 months for 6 rounds (months), coupled with MIYCF and FMA activities, to address micronutrient deficiencies and malnutrition. This is implemented in Maaret Tmasrin in Idleb and Azaz, Aghtrin, Suran, and Jandairis in Aleppo. MIYCF messaging and counselling in addition to FMA and screening, through household visits and group sessions will be implemented in Armanaz, Qourqeena, Darkoush in Idleb and Azaz in Aleppo.
A planned intervention will provide 5 rounds of e-vouchers for households with PLW and/or children under 2 years to access nutritious foods as they are the most vulnerable, have the highest malnutrition rates, and are within the 1,000 days window of opportunity. These HHs will also receive counselling on MIYCF practices to support healthier decisions for nutrition outcomes. This will be implemented in Azaz, Aleppo and Maaret Tmasrin, Idleb.
The project targets the most vulnerable populations living in camps, with a focus on women and children within the following categories: caregivers of children 6-59 months of reproductive age, and pregnant and lactating women. Adolescent and youth (10-24 years) are not targeted directly but included if they fall within the previously mentioned categories.
All projects will follow SPHERE standards, cluster SOPs, and internal SOPs and regulations. A set of indicators are in place to track progress according to standards and to report on 4W indicators to the cluster on monthly basis. Monitoring is conducted by the M&E team and by third party monitoring, at point of activity/distribution and 3-4 weeks after to assess the quality, usefulness and retention of assistance and information shared. Evaluations includes a base and end line assessment TPM will share reports upon completion of the project.
NGO X will be implementing this project directly, and 50% of funding has already been secured from multiple different donor sources.

4 - Needs

The HNO indicated 5.5 M people are in need of nutrition support, the majority are women and children, and especially IDPs, such as the context in NWS. A third of children are at risk of losing their future due to the irreversible damage it bears. In 2021, GAM rates in children multiplied 4 times since 2019 , and according to the 2022 HNO all NGO X’s project locations are at a severity scale of 5 with malnutrition in PLW at 12%, and anaemia prevalent in 46% of 6-59m children and 54% of women of reproductive age. The SENS report demonstrated poor MIYCF practices where only 19.1% of women are initiating breastfeeding within the first hour of birth, very low exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) rates 53.5%, with only 52.2% continuing to feed at 1 year. The exacerbated negative nutrition outcomes are compounded by a multitude of underlying causes, including food insecurity from the unstable context due to displacement, devaluation of the SYP combined with a fluctuating economy, and reduced access to healthcare and markets, thus basic needs. Only 1 in 11 children are consuming minimum acceptable diets, an increase in stunting from 19.3% in 2019 to 24.5% in 2021, and almost half of women and children suffering from anemia. These results reflect long-term inadequate dietary intake and thus micronutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of EBF and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency.

5 - Implementation of activities

Nutrition activities targets highly vulnerable IDP families living in informal settlements and IDP camps in Idleb and North Aleppo, who are simultaneously receiving food security assistance from NGO X as a multi-sectoral intervention. All targeted caregivers of children under 5, women of reproductive age, and pregnant and lactating women will be trained on the family MUAC approach and provided with a MUAC tape to take home alongside information on the nutrition treatment and prevention services available nearby if they need them, aiming to increase early self-identification of acute malnutrition within households and health seeking behaviours. Nutrition counselling will be provided to the same populations on prevention and treatment of malnutrition and MIYCN. One component provides LNS-SQ, to children 6-59 months and another will target specific communities with e-vouchers for households with PLW and/or children under 2 years to access nutritious foods. Coordination and referrals will be carried out between health & nutrition actors in the area through the Nutrition Cluster and bilaterally with food security actors in the area to ensure the most vulnerable to malnutrition receive both food assistance and nutrition services. Monitoring and evaluation of each project is conducted by NGO X, our partner, and a TPM. NGO X will collect data that includes 4Ws indicators to report monthly to the cluster.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (less likely): Beneficiaries exploitation violence or abuse during activities. Mitigation: PSEA orientation for all teams, accountability and monitoring teams will monitor the services. Risk (likely): Access restrictions to area due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines would prevent the humanitarian actors providing timely emergency relief to the affected population.Mitigation: a) Constantly monitor the security situation on the ground b) Develop contingency planning c) Explore different modalities Risk (likely): GBV and CP cases discovered during the screening. Mitigation: Team to observe visual signs and refer the victims to the case management teams according to service mapping in the region. Risk (less likely): Lack of privacy for women and physical risk of traveling to site of activity Mitigation: a) Gender balanced team, the nutritionists will be both male and female, however, NGO X will hire a higher proportion of female staff given the target population are PLW and children under 5. b) Visit beneficiaries at their homes. C) Locations are coordinated with camp management/local council to ensure safety and proximity. Risk (less likely): Variation in voucher value per HH based on eligibility of PLW or children under 2 to receive nutrition top-up. Mitigation: E-voucher modality avoids in person distributions and thus reduced community tensions of varying values. Inclusive eligibility criteria covers the whole area of operation.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

NGO X will ensure a Do No Harm approach, and mainstream PSEA in all interventions Risk (less likely): Risk of sexual exploitation/abuse for beneficiaries at or travelling to and from activity points Mitigation: Distribution site considerations include proximity to active hostilities, presence of armed actors, and timing of distributions. Distribution points are located close to beneficiary villages so they do not have far to travel, minimizing the risk of violence (GBV and theft). Ensure gathering points are accessible and safe for women and children to travel to in daylight and all activities are undertaken in daylight. If beneficiaries can’t or don’t want to attend distribution points they can authorize another household member to collect their assistance for them. In cases where a beneficiary is unable to attend the distribution point, home delivery can be organized. Risk (less likely): Risk of sexual exploitation or abuse by NGO X staff, partners or stakeholders, Mitigation: All staff, partners, and stakeholders receive comprehensive training upon starting their contracts on NGO X’s safeguarding policies and measures, behavior protocols and reporting mechanisms. NGO X has a dedicated safeguarding focal point at national level, trained in handling sensitive issues arising from actual or perceived breaches of policies, as well as focal points in all field sites. A confidential safeguarding reporting mechanism and a framework for investigation, response is in place.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, security conditions remain sufficiently stable to allow NGO X to operate. Risk (less likely): Continued military offensives presents the risk of death or injury to NGO X staff damage to or destruction of NGO X premises or assets; reduced access to programme areas and of border closure. Mitigation: Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and Standard Operating Procedures are in place, and an experienced security team. Assumption : It is assumed that large scale vaccinations will not be possible in NWS in 2021 and therefore assumed that COVID19 will continue to be a risk for all of 2021. Risk (likely): Spread of COVID19 in NWS. Mitigation: NGO X has identified food security as an 'essential service' in light of COVID-19, which means it will aim to continue food security delivery as possible. As the nutrition delivery is highly integrated within the FS programme, NGO X foresees continuation of this activity. Focus on family MUAC approach, safe screening, HH level visits, reduced number of beneficiaries in group sessions are all other mitigations in light of the pandemic and in line with the cluster recommendations.
