Project Summary

CAID has existing Health Post (HP) in Camp 15, the largest Rohingya refugee camp, which is being operated through implementing partner, DSK. The health post is structed to provide critical health services to 16,800 individuals in nearby area aligned with Minimum Package of Essential Health Service for Primary Health Centers in the Rohingya/FDMN community by health sector. In addition to providing general consultation services to Rohingya nearby host, health awareness door-to-door visits by Community Health Workers (CHW) has been a vital part of the health activities. As a part of outreach initiative, systematic community level health promotion and preventive services will be rolled out through the CHWs linked to the primary health facilities. CAID along with the implementing partner DSK has been a part of the health sector working groups including Community Health, Epidemiology and SRH Working Group, and would continue to do so to ensure quarterly monitoring for quality assurance and adherence to the existing health sector mechanism.
Through the HP, comprehensive services will be ensured, including the needs of the gender diverse population for palliative care, disability, emergency care and enhanced referral systems. Maternity care, long-acting reversible contraceptives, facility-based deliveries, and specialized cares for ophthalmology (eye) and otolaryngologist (ear, nose, and throat) will be expanded. Systematic community level health promotion and preventive services will be rolled out through the CHWs linked to the primary health facilities. The health post has been offering outpatient curative care, ANC, PNC, IMNCI, Routine Immunization (0-2 years), Adolescent friendly services, Family planning counselling, Survivor Identification through referrals, self-reports, accompaniments, gender-based violence, nutrition, communicable and non-communicable diseases, Mental Health and Psychosocial Service including referral. Further, ENT services have been also providing additionally as per RRRC and CiC’s recommendations. The health post is also providing ENT services as part of other services inclusion as an integrated part in close collaboration with CiC and sector and camp authority. An important focus will be on the vulnerable groups including pregnant and post-natal women, new-borns, the under-fives, including those with malnutrition and PWD. Moreover, beneficiaries including women and girls have been receiving healthcare under mother and childcare, Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) and Family Planning (FP) activities in the health facilities and will continue to be supported with specialised care. For Host community, we will increase service availability, accessibility, and community engagement same as for the refugee population. The HP will continue to offer free services for host community members. Increased linkage to SRH services and referral systems, availability of FP services and commodities, will equally benefit the host community. In addition, we will participate in any emergency response (such as MMT) through our expertise and medical supply both in the camps and host community.
At our Health facilities, efforts will be made to ensure crosscutting issues. Efficient and accountable Information management, data collection and reporting will be maintained with Health Sector and government systems, along with EWARS emphasised. In addition, CAID has an in-house accountability tool called COMPASS (digital data collection tool) to ensure community’s feedback is systematically recorded and resolved. Moreover, CAID is an active member of the PSEA network and regularly receive trainings and learning materials from the network to further disseminate in the community. Besides, CAID adheres to its own accountability and safeguarding policies at all levels.

1 - Identified Needs

The prevailing COVID-19 situation in the camps and host community have hindered service provision and exacerbated the vulnerability of the Rohingya community, disproportionately affecting women, elderly, and people with special needs to access services such as health, WaSH, and other basic needs in their daily life. All 34 camps have confirmed cases of COVID-19. As of 12 September 2021, there are 2,975 confirmed cases of COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2) out of 61,366 samples that have been tested. The total positivity rate now stands 4.8%. The Rohingya refugees remain acutely vulnerable to health risks in Cox’s Bazar. The World Health Organisation’s (WHO) survey randomly conducted in amongst the refugee and host communities in Cox’s Bazar found 24.6% exhibited at least one symptom of three most common COVID-19 symptoms: fever, dry cough, and fatigue. The Health Sector highlighted critical gaps in the Joint Response Plan (JRP) for 2021, including comprehensive emergency obstetric care, new-born care, family planning provision, particularly long-acting methods, availability, and timely access of 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape/intimate partner violence services. However, this remains largely unchanged for 2022 with a fundamental lack of funding for crucial sectors, namely WASH and Health. This is presenting existing actors with the inexorable choice to withdraw from the Rohingya camps and presents gaps in essential services for Rohingya and Host communities who are heavily dependent on humanitarian life-saving assistance. With additional funding required and lack of actors to aid, most people are relying on negative coping mechanisms including reducing the food portion sizes or the number of meals per day and more to meet these needs. COVID-19 has also created barriers to the availability, accessibility, awareness, quality, and utilisation of critical services for the Rohingya and nearby host communities. Essential and non-essentials services such as supply of medicines, NFI distribution, restricted access to the camp, delay in procurement and restricted staff mobility was among some of the major drawbacks during the pandemic. The need for stronger medical and health facilities has deepened. Households who were already struggling to access assistance and services to meet their specific needs are the most gravely impacted and face the highest risk of extreme poverty and severe long-term impacts, such as increased mortality and morbidity.A sectoral need assessment conducted by CAID in 2021 highlights some of the most crucial health care need of the refugees and the host communities especially of person with disability, elderly, women, and girls remain acutely vulnerable to health risks in Cox’s Bazar. Congested living, poor hygiene and sanitation and lack of nutritious food are among the factors adding to the vulnerabilities related to health especially for elderly, PWD and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). The need for essential healthcare services remains persistent while there has been a shift in the trend of disease and type of care needed. The scale and impact of diphtheria, chickenpox, and measles outbreaks have been witnessed in the past and chronic diseases are also vivid among many of the refugees. COVID-19 remains a key risk to the densely populated communities. During a FGD conducted in Camp 15, the participants emphasised the long waiting time consumption at the existing facilities due to the lack of doctors. In addition, they mentioned often they are asked to return the next day due to the overload of the patients (https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/12SyPGeJtoUIG3fZGw9Wrm-m_dzRIukPf?usp=sharing).
