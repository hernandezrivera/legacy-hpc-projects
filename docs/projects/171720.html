Project Summary

For almost 3 decades, Somalia has been faced by political instability, power vacuums, recurrent and devastating droughts and armed conﬂicts that has exacerbated the situation especially for IDPs and made the environment for returning of refugees unpredictable. Latest estimations of displacement conditions in Somalia indicate that around 2.3 million people have settled in around 2,400 IDP sites, most of which are informal, small and scattered settlements in urban or peri-urban areas. Living in private land often means that IDPs are at constant risk of eviction, which further stresses their already vulnerable situation. Protracted conﬂict-driven displacement aggravated by natural disasters, limited livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services and limited access to durable solutions all contribute to the continued increase in the number and vulnerability of IDPs.
The main objective of the proposed action is to strengthen camp coordination and camp management at the site level, improve information management, and empower community engagement in order to improve access to life-saving services for displaced persons. Through the delivery of comprehensive CCCM assistance, CESDO aims to facilitate better living conditions and protection of IDPs, while facilitating clear focus on moving towards durable solutions with full community participation. CESDO will continue to strengthen its existing site coordination and management in Baidoa, Berdale, Bay region to provide life-saving services to flood and drought affected IDPs.
This project will target Baidoa/Berdale, Waajid, Wanlawayn and Afgoye districts, Somalia and the objectives are to support the improvement of conditions in IDP sites through strengthening the predictability and effectiveness of service provision and protection for displaced populations, to enhance community self-management and information sharing between all stakeholders. CESDO will ensure that all displaced persons are enabled to partake meaningfully in the governance structures and management of the sites by facilitating sites maintenance through cash-for-work and establishment or strengthening of an inclusive community governance structures and support on Camp Coordination and Camp management capacity buildings. Through this project CESDO will reach more than 4,000 HHs - 50 % female, ensure disability inclusion at all stages of the project, Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the speciﬁc consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data.

Project Activities

CESDO will continue CCCM activities in Baidoa/Berdale and start new Camp Coordination & Camp Management activities in Afgoye & Wanlaweyn districts. Support and take lead on conducting site veriﬁcation on 3-month basis where the CCCM cluster needs to - Establish/update service mapping of partners operating in sites - Monitor service delivery at site level to ensure that gaps are responded to or duplication of activities are prevented. Provide capacity building trainings to existing governance structures/focal points. CESDO will coordinate the delivery of services in sites by representing CCCM in relevant coordination forums, - Provide training onsite maintenance and site planning to camp committees. CESDO will strive to improve living conditions of displaced people by supporting community led site maintenance activities through Cash For Work to ensure sites are up to the required standards; conducting safety audits to identify areas of high protection risk, implementing emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites. We will also provide community feedback mechanisms using free hotlines, suggestion boxes, CCCM mobile outreach team and helpdesk. CESDO will continue COVID 19 awareness and conduct RCCE assessment.Women & Girls Participation:The Camp Management Committees (CMCs) and community members will be involved at every stage of the project to ensure their maximum participation in the activities. The implementation will be based Core Humanitarian standard, SPHERE and cluster standards will be adhered as well. Beneficiary selection will also consider the gender needs, Women, disabled people, boys and girls from minority and marginalized groups will be systematically involved not only as beneficiaries but as stakeholders during all the project phases. The incidence of COVID-19 is expected to double the disadvantages of women and girls because of their gendered roles in families and communities and their vulnerability to GBV (inability to escape violence and threatening environment, difficult access to basic services and support).Through this project, CESDO strives to support IDP women who participate in Camp Management Committees to have meaningful and active roles in the decision-making process, response to the COVID-19 pandemic and other outbreaks and site level responses. The overall objective of the intervention is to contribute to improved gender equality and positively contribute to the protection of women and girls in the target locations.Disability Inclusion:Through this project, CESDO will ensure disability inclusion by:  Conducting Data collection on disability and using it for better programming (timely response and advocacy). Build and strengthen partnerships and capacity building with organizations for persons with disabilities (PDs)  Improve monitoring, reporting and learning on persons with disabilities throughout the project period.  Ensure that people with disabilities and their Care givers, are informed of their relief entitlements, the targeting criteria being used and the mechanisms through which they will receive assistance. Be sensitive to protection risks that could arise from measures to facilitate access to assistance for people with disabilities (e.g. prioritizing them for assistance may increase stigma and the risk of theft or even violence) and CESDO will ensure they are integrated, and responses incorporated with IDPs and other persons of concerns.

Mitigation Measures

CESDO will ensure all staff operating in a camp and camp-like setting are trained in the Humanitarian Principles and Do No Harm and abide by the Code of Conduct including PSEA, conduct safety audits of sites in collaboration with the other partners and coordinate with GBV partners to ensure that GBV referral pathways are operational and known to community members in sites, ensure diverse and inclusive governance structures are present in sites and that responsive community feedback mechanisms are in place and operational. We will also ensure the participation of women through activities speciﬁcally geared towards reducing barriers to women participation and persons with disabilities. CESDO will support the establishment of inclusive community governance structures where there are no governance structures and will work with HLP partners to ensure that the sites have their eviction status mapped and relevant advocacy with government authorities are carried out. The community leaders and respective committees will be fully involved in project design, implementation, monitoring and representation and that the community committees are inclusive and that segments of the communities are well represented at all levels such as age and gender diversity.
