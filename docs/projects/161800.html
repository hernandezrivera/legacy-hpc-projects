Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview for Nigeria (2019), North East Nigeria is one of the world largest protection crises, in which civilians face serious risks to their safety, well-being, and basic rights. The protection crisis has largely been triggered by an ongoing regionalized armed conflict, characterized by massive and extremely prevalent abuses against civilians including killings, rape and other sexual violence, abduction, child recruitment, burning of homes, pillaging, forced displacement, arbitrary detention, and the use of explosive hazards, including in deliberate attacks on civilian targets.
It is estimated by BAY State level Consultations on 2020 HNO that over 6.7 million people are still impacted by the ongoing armed conflict and are in need of humanitarian assistance in Borno, Adawama and Yobe States.
Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 180 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community-based integrated response. An operational office was opened in Monguno, Monguno LGA in 2018 to implement health, nutrition and protection activities in Kukawa LGA, and more recently in Monguno. The expertise as well as the acceptance that PUI gained in Northern Nigeria will optimise the impact of the proposed project.
The proposed intervention is designed to ensure strong community involvement from the onset throughout all project implementation and gives a primary place to the partnership with and accountability to affected populations. The proposed project is articulated to provide both individual and community level protection assistance by responding to specific protection needs and risks of affected individuals and enhancing the overall protection environment of targeted communities.
Responding to specific needs and risks at the household/individual levels will include timely identification, needs assessment, provision of tailored individual assistance, and/or referral to specialised service providers. Enhancing the overall protection environment of targeted communities will be achieved through disseminating information on services available at both household and community levels, building the capacities of influential community members on protection concerns and principles, widely raising awareness among community members on protection-related topics and setting up community safe spaces. In addition, the protection information and data collected throughout the proposed protection activities will form a base of evidence for informing current and future programming, as well as advocacy messages on gaps in service provision, alarming protection concerns, evolution of the protection trends etc.

1 - Needs

The on-going regional conflict affecting North-East Nigeria has resulted in massive civilian displacement across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States, fleeing from life threatening threats posed by armed forces/groups, including killings, kidnapping and abduction, forced enrollment into armed forces, rape and sexual assaults, and arbitrary detention. As a result and according to the 2020 BAY State level consultations on HNO, 6.7 million people are currently in need of humanitarian assistance in North East Nigeria, of which 4.84 million are in need of protection services. During the second half of 2018 and throughout 2019, PUI protection teams conducted regular community, household, and individual level protection assessments and protection monitoring in MMC, and Monguno LGA, Borno State. Key findings have shown that in addition to rights violation incidents suffered prior and during displacement such as serious threats to life, sexual violence, forced enrollment into armed forces, and kidnappings, significant protection concerns continue to be faced in areas of displacement. These include exposure to abuse and exploitation, sexual and gender based violence, forced and early marriage, child labour and begging, and harmful survival practices. In IDP settlements inter-community tensions, insecurity and restriction of freedom of movements are additional threats. According to the 2019 HNO, women and children form 80% of the displacement population in NE Nigeria, as most adult men were unable to flee, forcibly enrolled into armed groups, abducted or killed. PUI’s protection assessments and monitoring findings identified high rates of female and child-headed households, as well as high number of unaccompanied and separated children as a result of the conflict and massive displacement. Of this group, women and girls are particularly at risk as conflict and displacement crisis have seriously exacerbated pre-existing gender inequalities and increased their exposure to threats, increased their vulnerability levels, and continuously limited their capacities. Risks for women and girls particularly include - but are not limited to - gender-based violence, of which intimate partner violence, forced/early marriage, and sexual exploitation and abuse are the most widespread forms. While women and girls are the most at risk groups, men and boys are primarily exposed to violence, forced recruitment into armed groups, kidnapping, killing, and torture. When escaped or released, former combatants and captives face serious discrimination, stigmatization, social exclusion and distrust from community members and authorities, resulting in extreme poverty and further exposure to abuse and exploitation. Despite these significant concerns over the overall protection situation in Borno State, and the continuous threats to life, most fundamental rights, and access to basic services for displaced populations, significant gaps remain in the provision of humanitarian assistance, and particularly of specialized protection services. PUI proposed intervention aims at preventing, mitigating, and responding to the above identified protection risks in areas of displacement through targeted actions, including community-based protection, and individual protection assistance. Activities will be conducted following a community-outreach approach and include timely identification of individuals at risk, tailored referrals, information sharing, awareness sessions and sensitization campaigns, set-up and management of community safe spaces, collective PSS activities, and distribution of dignity kits. In addition, PUI will continue to conduct regular protection assessments and protection monitoring activities to collect, analyse, and share key protection information and data, identify trends, inform PUI’s programming and advocacy efforts, and other protection service providers; and promote displaced populations’ fundamental rights, safety, and well-being.

2 - Activities

Protection Monitoring will be conducted on a regular basis through “protection monitoring visits” in all targeted communities. PUI Protection Monitoring methodology includes general observations, key informant interviews, and household surveys. Sampling will be calculated per location, based on the profile of the area. Based on previous experiences and program assessment, the protection concerns of a minimum of 1,540 individuals will be monitored every month. Key protection monitoring findings will be shared with the Protection Sector and other relevant partners through ad hoc alerts when emergency concerns are identified and on a regular basis through monthly protection monitoring reports. These reports will include information on main protection concerns, trends, service gaps, and most spread right violation incidents, and form evidence for current and future programming, and local advocacy efforts. Tailored individual referrals: Individuals in need of further assistance identified throughout the proposed protection activities and PUI’s presence in the targeted locations will be timely referred to specialized service providers. It is anticipated that at least 1,210 individuals will be timely assisted and referred (upon informed consent) to specialized service providers pre-identified though service mapping developed and maintained in each area of intervention. Additionally, a minimum of 5,200 individuals (650 households) exposed to serious protection risks will be provided with livelihoods activities. Referrals will be both internal to PUI health, nutrition, MHPSS, and FSL teams, and external to GBV and CP case management, legal aid and reintegration support services. Each referral will be closely monitored and followed up on, in order to ensure referred individuals have accessed needed services and that assistance was provided in a timely, safe and dignified manner. Individual Protection Assistance (IPA): PUI protection team will assist 150 individuals with specific needs through IPA, a one-off emergency cash or in-kind assistance aimed at addressing urgent and specific protection risks. In addition, 450 dignity kits will be distributed to survivors of GBV and women and girls at high risk of GBV. These dignity kits will be composed of age, gender and culturally appropriate garments and other items (such as hijabs, wrappers, whistles, torches, underwear, and small containers for washing personal items) in addition to sanitary supplies and basic hygiene items. Protection awareness: PUI protection team and volunteers will provide targeted communities with awareness on a variety of protection topics, including through weekly community sessions and radio broadcasting (in Maiduguri). Topics will be tailored according to risks identified through protection monitoring and according to community consultations results. Protection awareness will target 21,520 individuals. Community-based Protection: 2 community safe spaces in Maiduguri and 1 in Monguno will be created utilizing existing facilities to be rehabilitated. Individuals will be able to benefit from various services including protection awareness sessions, information on available/accessible services, collective PSS activities (including recreational and life skills activities). Community-based PSS sessions will target a total of 7,230 women, girls, men and boys. Additionally, PUI will provide training sessions on protection principles to 930 influential community members (including teachers, traditional/religious leaders, community volunteers). These trainings will empower targeted communities, enhance safe identification and referrals of cases and reinforce community-based protection mechanisms. Partners and non-protection staff Capacity Building: Training sessions on protection principles and protection mainstreaming will be organized in each area of intervention, targeting 400 national/international NGOs non-protection staff.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PUI has a strong mechanism in place that relies on various channels. Feedbacks are mainly captured through:(1) Community Committees Meetings that are organised once a month with community leaders, elders, women and PUI program staff to discuss project activities, gaps and needs; (2) Community Volunteers Network which comprised all outreach workers who directly link PUI to targeted communities; and (3) PUI Complaint Mechanism which consist of help-desks in the community and a toll-free helpline managed by dedicated trained staff.
