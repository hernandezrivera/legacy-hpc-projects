Project Summary

UNHCR and its implementing partners propose to target - for cash assistance - refugees, asylum seekers, as well as the most vulnerable conflict-affected Libyan nationals (IDPs, returnees, and non-displaced) referred by central and local authorities and actors. Following the initial referrals, multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) will be provided to people that have been assessed against harmonized socio-economic-protection vulnerability criteria based on a household-level scorecard. These criteria (and/or their significance) will be tailored to different population groups and to identify the most vulnerable. The assessments will continue to help ensure that scarce resources are directed to those most in need, and not based on their status only.
In particular, UNHCR and its implementing partners will provide MPCA through unrestricted multi-month cash grants to the aforementioned target populations in the form of gift- or pre-paid cards, and one-off (one-time) MPCA for the vulnerable recently displaced, shipwreck survivors and released from detention centers.
UNHCR implementing partners will identify eligible households and individuals using vulnerability scorecards, thresholds and cash transfer values endorsed by the Cash and Markets Working Group (CMWG).
Through the proposed project UNHCR and its implementing partners will, therefore, mitigate the need of the most vulnerable conflict-affected persons of concern to resort to negative coping mechanisms, by helping them to address their basic needs through facilitated access to basic goods and services available in the market, empowering them and promoting the dignity of choice of its recipients, stimulating local markets, and reducing operational costs of assistance delivery (compared to in-kind modalities).
Last but not least, by targeting the extremely vulnerable families, MPCA will play an essential role to bridge the potential time gap when transitioning from humanitarian assistance to social safety net inclusion, therefore reinforcing the complementary with the MoSA vulnerability model. The adoption of scoring models and the diversification in terms of the number of cash transfers will facilitate a more granular response that recognizes the varied socio-economic-protection needs of families who live below the poverty line across the different Mantikas in Libya. Finally, the MPCA response intends to maintain the capacity of responding to the socio-economic needs of households affected by sudden emergencies/crises and make provisions to provide as soon as possible after the event (based on access).

1 - Needs

The overall operational landscape in Libya remains volatile and increasingly complex. In 2020, the instability and ongoing conflict may continue to lead to forced displacement, deteriorating living conditions, impacting on people’s safety and security, as well as restricted access of persons of concern to UNHCR (POC) to essential services. The crisis in Libya may continue to present targeted attacks on civilians, including women and girls, rising civilian casualties, restricted access to safety and freedom of movement, alarming levels of SGBV, arbitrary detention and torture, child protection risks, an increasing number of civilian accidents caused by landmines and explosive devices. Under these conditions, the civilian population is likely to remain highly vulnerable, exposed to violence and unable to have sustained access to life-saving medical assistance, essential medicines, food, safe drinking water and sanitation, shelter and education. The current liquidity crisis will continue to affect people’s ability to access basic commodities and services due to a decrease in their purchasing power to acquire goods. The political stalemate has resulted in a governance vacuum, and coupled with widespread violence andinsecurity have disrupted the economy and public service delivery across the country. The living conditions, including access to clean drinking water, medical services, and safe housing have all degraded due to the protracted situation, particularly for women and children.A combination of high inflation, a decrease of purchasing power, a systemic liquidity crisis that triggers limited withdrawal rates and delays salary payments, the lack of necessary documentation limiting access to income-generating activities and raising barriers that exclude the most vulnerable from using financial services such as savings and letters of credit to help them for everything from long-term goals to unexpected emergencies, significantly affects a conflict-affected household’s capacity to pay for basic items and services such as medicines and healthcare, food, education supplies, fuel, rent, etc... As of 31st December 2019 (https://data2.unhcr.org/en/country/lby) there are an estimated 46,453 refugees and asylum seekers in need of humanitarian assistance in Libya, of which approximately 43,618 coming mostly from Syria, Iraq, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Sudan, and Palestine have been registered with UNHCR. Available information and assessments concluded that refugees and asylum seekers are the least resilient to the impact of the current crisis in Libya. They are more likely to have been displaced several times, to live in more vulnerable shelter types and to have been exposed to threats, intimidation, harassment, physical assault or violence.IDPs remain one of the most vulnerable population groups, as they seek out temporary shelter and scarce livelihood opportunities in urban centers, many without access to basic services. There have been multiple waves of displacement in Libya since the initial outbreak of conflict in 2011. At the same time, where the security situation is improving, the displaced families are returning to their communities, finding their homes damaged or completely destroyed and being in dire need of humanitarian assistance before they can start rebuilding their lives. According to the HNO 2019/2020 (https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/sites/www.humanitarianresponse.info/files/documents/files/hno_2020_executivesummary_final.pdf), there are 219K IDPs and 74K returnees in need. The AGD analysis identified the risk of increased disagreements and violence amongst family members over the use of cash assistance, or of having just one member of the family controlling the financial resources. The mitigation measures will include appropriate confidentiality in FCM, briefing of HH members and cash holder on the purpose of assistance and close monitoring of distributed cash, sensitization prior to and during distribution.

2 - Activities

Data from 2019 PDMs and recent 2019 MSNA findings suggest that cash assistance is to a large extent the preferred assistance modality of vulnerable persons in need. However, cash assistance delivery requires a minimum set of activities to be performed. UNHCR and its implementing partners will focus on the following critical activities:1)Cash assistance eligibility determination for refugees, asylum seekers, IDPs, returnees and host communities (socio-economic vulnerabilities plus emergency response)Eligibility is based on vulnerability scorecards and inclusion thresholds endorsed by the CMWG.AIl eligible PoC will be correctly and accurately recorded in the respective beneficiary database/IMS used by accredited partners and be updated on a regular basis to reflect changes. When eligibility determination is concluded successfully (i.e. PoC is cash eligible) leads to the issuance of payment means which is also assigned to the household on the relevant beneficiary database. Following the eligibility assessment, PoCs issued with a payment means will be instructed on how to use the means of payment, the payment schedule of the programme, the entitlement amounts, the coverage period of each payment, how to access a helpdesk.2)Cash Transfer (Payment means distribution, onsite helpdesk support, distribution lists preparation, entitlements calculations, payment instructions) based on HH size3)Post-distribution helpline and other means of POC support.PoC queries relating to cash entitlements will be addressed and resolved in a timely manner. Referrals to appropriate feedback and complaints response mechanisms and tools, where appropriate, will be conducted.4)Process monitoring and outcome evaluation (Post Distribution Monitoring)5)Periodic assessments related to mixed migration and data collection/publication of Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI), providing the evidence-based guidance for better response planning and timely corrective measures.

4 - Protection risk analysis

1) Insecurity at distribution venues leads to theft of payment means or cash from PoCs en route to/from the distribution sites and to local markets. 2) A volatile security context prohibits implementing partners from being able to access targeted locations.3) Assistance Inclusion/exclusion errors4) Beneficiaries spend on anti-social and low priority items5) Prices are fixed arbitrarily by retailers and vendors6) Vendors and retailers charge POCs extra when paid by electronic cards7) New arrivals, newly displaced, recently released from detention, rescued at sea do not receive assistance (a gap between their arrival and the following distribution exists).8) Delays in distribution due to the liquidity crises9) Unauthorized sharing of POCs personal data (incl. biodata) with third parties10) Persons with specific needs/ disabilities/older persons cannot access distribution sites.11) POCs who live in remote areas do not have access to public transportation.12) Restriction of movement for asylum seekers and refugees limiting their ability to access local markets.13) Complaint mechanism not responsive14) Increased disagreements and violence amongst family members over the use of entitlement (domestic violence and negative family dynamics)

1)Ensure tightened security at distribution sites. Distributions to be kept low profile. Ensure adequate crowd control measures are in place. Selection of alternate locations and/or change of distribution modality or delivery mechanism3)Enforce high-quality data collection, data cleaning procedures, and mechanism for regular registration data updates, including photos and biodata to facilitate identity verification. Pre- and post-distribution list monitoring process in place to spot inclusion/exclusion errors. Inclusion, in the SOPs, of mechanisms to assess borderline cases (periodic cash panels, etc…)4)Sensitization campaign, possibility to shift to vouchers incl. in the plan. Provide restricted cash assistance to promote program objectives. Enforce regular PDM to capture negative behavioral patterns in beneficiaries and adopt corrective measures at the earliest opportunity. 5) & 6)Regular vendor and market monitoring; FSP monitoring merchants that are within their POS network. Pricing surveys against FSP's vendors and non-FSP's vendors, to ensure that the prices are consistent.7) New arrivals (if eligible) are fast-tracked to receive emergency one-time cash assistance. 6 - How is your project accountable to affected populations?

Common feedback mechanism

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNHCR implementing partners will connect to the Common Feedback Mechanism (CFM) that the Emergency Telecommunications Sector (ETS) will implement in 2020.Implementing partners will enforce accountability mechanisms to ensure persons of concern are enabled to provide feedback on the activities, processes, and outcomes of the program. The information periodically collected from partners' M&E teams - independent from programme staff to guarantee confidentiality to the highest degree possible within the organisations - will be analyzed and used to take corrective measures and, more in general, to improve further the response and the approach through prompt adaptive programming in consultation with the partners. Partners will use dedicated phone lines, email and other communication means to receive feedback and dedicated database to register the complaints and feedback, to manage the response workflow based on the level of sensitivity and prioritization in compliance with organizational CFM SOPs. To respond to the dynamic context and complex needs of the targeted vulnerable populations, the implementing partners will conduct regular assessments and interviews (KYI, FGD, etc..) to obtain a detailed understanding of the impact of the interventions. The overall feedback will be collected from the communities directly and protection monitoring activities conducted by all partners requiring informed consent from beneficiaries. The partners M&E teams will receive and record all incoming feedback, to be analyzed, and guide appropriate corrective measures if needed, including program revisions.
