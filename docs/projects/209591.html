Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to support various crucial aspects including school rehabilitation, NFE, MHPSS, hygiene promotion, parent-teacher associations, teacher training, parenting support, and in-kind distribution activities. This initiative targets school-age children (5-17 years) and youth (18-24 years), along with their teachers and caregivers. The targeted locations were selected based on a multi-sectorial needs assessment conducted by COOPI in September 2023 and ongoing areas of COOPI's intervention, integrating Education, Protection, and Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) programs to provide holistic assistance to the most vulnerable communities, scoring between 3 and 5 on the 2024 severity scale. Importantly, the suggested project is informed by the needs outlined in the HNO 2024.
The challenges in Syria's education system have been significant over the past years. Before the earthquakes in February 2023, about one-third of schools were non-operational. Approximately 2.4 million children, or one in three of school age, were not attending school, and about 1.6 million were on the verge of discontinuing their education. The earthquakes further exacerbated this crisis, causing additional harm to school buildings, interrupting the studies of millions, and compounding the emotional distress of children, educators, and families.
COOPI focuses its efforts on remote and underserved areas where educational access is hindered by inadequate infrastructure, untrained teachers, and challenging socioeconomic conditions resulting from crises or earthquakes. Access to schools is facilitated through an MoU with the Ministry of Education. Activities are designed to align with other projects submitted to HRP 2024-2025 (Protection, ERL, WASH and FSA), building upon ongoing interventions and assessments in the targeted areas.
COOPI will provide in-kind assistance (learning kits) directly to vulnerable families and schools (school kits) to address barriers in education. Non-formal education will be prioritized, including remedial classes and homework support, to bolster the public education system. Light rehabilitation of damaged schools and school equipment will focus on creating a safe environment and increasing access for students with disabilities. The intervention will equip students with life skills, enhancing their resilience through committee participation and recreational activities. PSS sessions will be provided to improve general well-being and strengthen positive coping mechanisms. Health and hygiene awareness activities, coupled with hygiene kit distribution, will contribute to overall well-being.
To ensure sustainability, COOPI will emphasize capacity building for trainers, teachers, and staff in leading and monitoring education services and basic principles related to Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS), Education in Emergencies (EiE), and INEE Minimum Standards. The proposed activities will be adapted based on ongoing and previous programs and assessments, considering input from stakeholders and adopting different approaches for urban, rural, or remote areas.
A robust M&E framework is in place to ensure the project's effectiveness and impact. COOPI's dedicated MEAL team closely follows up on project activities, conducting regular monitoring visits to schools and communities to gather data on various aspects of the project's implementation. Through this framework, we track the impact of our interventions, evaluate the effectiveness of educational programs, and identify areas for improvement.
Finally, the PSEA strategy remains a cornerstone of COOPI's operations. All staff members undergo comprehensive training on PSEA standards to ensure a safe environment for all beneficiaries, especially vulnerable populations. COOPI has established a robust reporting mechanism to promote transparency and accountability, safeguarding the dignity of those served and fostering a culture of awareness and prevention within the team.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

COOPI's intervention proposed for the Humanitarian Response Plan 2024 - 2025 is informed by the multi-sectorial needs assessment (MSNA) conducted in September 2023. The intervention aims to address the deep-rooted educational crisis in Syria, exacerbated by over a dozen years of crisis and intensified by earthquakes in February 2023. The description of needs and suggested response is below: Identified Needs and COOPI's Response: Educational Barriers:• Non-Formal Education (NFE): Implementing NFE programs to supplement existing schooling, targeting children who are out of school or at risk of dropping out.• Teacher Training: Capacity building for educators to enhance the quality of teaching and support.• Parenting Support: Providing tools and guidance to caregivers on the importance of education and ways to support their children's learning.• School Rehabilitation: Conducting light repairs and equipping schools with necessary materials to create safe and conducive learning environments. Mental Health and Well-being:• Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS): Offering sessions to address emotional distress among children, educators, and families. This response is integrated with COOPI's existing Child Protection and Protection strategy.• Hygiene Promotion: Conducting awareness health and hygiene campaigns and distributing hygiene kits to improve overall health and well-being. Economic Challenges:• In kind assistance: Provision of stationery and learning materials to families can remove the economic barriers to access to education. Additionally, providing in-kind material support to schools, will improve the quality of education. Safety Concerns:• Mine Risk Education: Providing education on the risks of Explosive Ordnance to ensure the safety of school children and communities. Capacity Building: • Training Programs: Building the capacity of trainers, teachers, and staff on Child Protection, Education in Emergencies, and INEE Minimum Standards to improve program effectiveness and sustainability. In addition, training teachers on MHPSS, to be able to provide school children with safe learning environment, and help them to overcome emotional difficulties. COOPI's holistic approach integrates Education, Protection, Early Recovery and Livelihoods and Food Security and Agriculture programs to benefit people in need across targeted areas. This initiative is designed to create sustainable improvements in education access, quality, and overall well-being for vulnerable communities impacted by the Syrian crisis and earthquakes, through NFE, teacher training, parent-teacher associations creation, in-kind distribution, and provision of MHPSS support to vulnerable children.
