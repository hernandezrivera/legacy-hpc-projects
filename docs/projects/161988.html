Project Summary

IRC seek to implement a Protection and Rule of Law Project that will encompass, Systematic Protection Monitoring, Legal assistance and Protection Mainstreaming.
The IRC has been present in Cox’s Bazar since September 2017 and obtained registration in March 2018. IRC Bangladesh has a portfolio of approximately 8 Million USD annually and a team of 330 staff including 315 nationals and 15 expatriate staff. The IRC is implementing protection project in camps 1W,E 15,16 and 21. IRC’s partnership with Bangladeshi organizations is at the forefront of its implementation and local engagement approach, IRC has engaged with BLAST in legal training and will seek to enage them further in implementation of this Project.

Needs

Refugees are exposed to severe protection threats that undermine their safety, physical integrity and wellbeing. Their ability to justice system is limited by legal, economic and informational barriers. Refugees face challenges in accessing legal assistance, resulting in risks of arrest, detention and movement restrictions. The evolving context makes the availability of accurate detailed protection data instrumental to providing effective services to meet protection needs. The lack of information about available services; gap in services adapted to the specific needs of the most vulnerable; lack of entitlements for refugees; and lack of clarity around legal procedures constitute the primary constraints in refugees accessing services and enjoying their rights. IRC’s Access to Justice Assessment within the camps; indicate gap in accessing justice system and legal documentation. The lack of information about available services; gap in services adapted to the specific needs of the most vulnerable; lack of entitlements for refugees; and lack of clarity around legal procedures constitute the primary constraints in refugees accessing services and enjoying their rights. Lack of civil documentation significantly contributes vulnerability of conflict affected communities as it represents barrier to safe and voluntary return and access to durable solutions. These finding are fortified by the Protection Analysis conducted in the camps that reveal limited knowledge and access to legal services noting those affected as, women, elderly and girls. Related to this, the IRC Protection Analyses Report of September 2019, revealed that there is a general lack of awareness among the refugee population about their rights and options when it comes to access to justice. The finding of the analysis is further consistent with the results from the Protection monitoring report Further to the forgoing, On July 25th, 28th, and 29th, the IRC conducted a needs assessment of the host community, specifically of the Ukhiya and Tekaf Upazilas. The finding of the assessment; revealed that although 23% of household respondents reported that legal services are available in their community. However, women, girls, and the elderly were most frequently reported as being able to access and/or easily access legal services. Reason included the fact that the persons listed cannot access and/or cannot easily access legal services is that the legal service providers cannot properly provide the service. The assessment further indicated that although legal services are available to the community there are not sufficient to meet the demand of the huge number. In Teknaf specifically household respondents also mentioned that women and girls cannot access and/or easily access legal services because of 1) harassment, 2) male dominance (i.e. gender inequality), and 3) a lack of awareness of available legal services. In Ukhiya, household respondents mentioned that women and girls cannot access and/or easily access legal services because of reasons 2 and 3 mentioned above.

Activities or outputs

Protection Monitoring (PM); Legal Aid; and Protection Mainstreaming: IRC will identify community level protection risks and individual protection needs for direct intervention, referral and specialized services in five camps (1E. 1W, 15, 16, 21). Through its Protection Monitoring, information generated will be used to develop and support evidence-based durable solutions advocacy initiatives. The finding will generate recommendations for humanitarian stakeholders, including the PWG, donors and camp level actors. IRC will conduct protection information sessions targeting camp and Host communities. The session will include mainstreaming of disability inclusive and adaptive approaches to ensure that people with communication challenges, limited cognitive ability, physical or other impairments are informed of their own risk determination and can provide informed consent for service response decision-making.Through its Legal Officers will provide legal assistance to individual persons through legal information sessions and one to one counselling on rights and entitlements. For case management, The Legal officers will make referral to specialized agencies for legal case management. IRC will build capacity of key community members to resolve conflicts and disputes in line with international standards through awareness raising on issues like legal procedures and mediation. IRC will also engage camp level structures and support in capacity building to Mahji on key protection considerations, justice mechanisms and international standards. Support will be provided on a case-by-case basis for the most at-risk refugees, facilitating access to formal justice mechanisms and linkages with the local authorities. Within the Host community, IRC will use the services of a Local Partner. The local Partner will mirror the activities that IRC shall undertake in the camps; these include information session, one on one counselling and making referrals to specialized agencies for legal case management. All activities will have protection mainstreaming lens, promoting safety, dignity, and meaningful access, participation of Women, girls and Elderly, and empowerment of beneficiaries. IRC will provide protection mainstreaming trainings to internal and external teams of local Partners, using the Global Protection Cluster training Package. IRC will ensure adherence to humanitarian best practice and conflict sensitive and Do No Harm approaches. PSEA will be central to the Project. All staff will have a detailed orientation and training. Partners will be accorded similar training and required to sign and abide Protocols relating to PSEA. IRC will continue to proactively engage at the PWG, and the sub sectors. Engaging the PWG, IRC will support the identification of key issues and coordinate messaging and positions for advocacy at national, regional and international levels. IRC will implement a solid community feedback mechanism in both Host and refugee community. The AAR will be closely aligned to the Core Humanitarian Standard. Accountability activities will be established on all core dimensions and integrated into all sector activities. These dimension are Information sharing, consulting with affected populations, creating opportunities for the participation of communities in IRC responses and gathering feedback and complaints from communities. IRC will deliberately establish mechanism that are Age and Gender appropriate. Deliberate consultation will take into account the needs to women, girls and the elderly. IRC will support the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU) of PWG to identify cases during the monsoon and cyclone season so that they can be referred to appropriate service providers.
