Project Summary

The project is aiming to support communities in the Blue Nile, North, West, and South Kordofan states and the project targeted the IDPs, Returnees, and vulnerable host communities by Multi-sectorial response "Education, Protection(CP), WASH and FSL) interventions.
The education activities will target 13,140 beneficiaries (2,575 Men, 2,681 Women, 3,863 boys and 4,021 girls) from IDPs, Returnees, and Vulnerable Host communities in (South Kordofan(Abu Jubayhah, Abu Kershola, Ghadeer &Um Durein)/Blue Nile (Al Kurmuk, Ar Rusayris, Baw & Geisan)/West Kordofan (As Sunut & Al Meiram)/North Kordofan (Gebrat Al-Sheikh & Soudari) through support and improve the School environment by rehabilitating the classrooms, Latrines with hand wash stations and provide water sources.
the WASH interventions targeted 119,530 people (23,428Men, 24,384 Women, 35,142 Boys, 36,576 Girls) from IDPS, Returns and affected HC in the Blue Nile (Al Kurmuk, Ar Rusayris, Baw & Geisan), South Kordofan (Abu Jubaiha, Abu kashola, Gedir and Umm Durein), West Kordofan (El Nehoud and Elmeiram) and North Kordofan (Um Dam) through the drill of 11 new hand pumps, rehabilitation of 11 hand pumps. AORD will Operate and maintain 11 water sources (includes water quality treatment and monitoring), construct/install new household/s emergency/ communal latrines (295 latrines). AORD will be Constructed 1,100 handwashing facilities in a communal setup and provide 550 personal hygiene Kits and Build capacities, provide training, to enhance the WASH of affected communities (220 community members).
AORD provides Child protection services through target 16,260 beneficiaries in Blue Nile (Al Kurmuk, Ar Rusayris, Baw & Geisan), South Kordofan (Abu Jubaiha, Abu kashola, Gedir and Umm Durein), West Kordofan (As Sunut and Ghubaish) and North Kordofan (Gharb Bara, Gebrat Al-Sheikh and Um Dam).
CP intervention through Establish or constructing Five semi-permanent safe spaces/multi-purpose (child and adolescent)/ Procurement and distribution of 13 tents for emergency CFS/Provision of Mental health and psychosocial support services to 650 children and adolescents / 26 Community engagement and awareness/Capacity building activities, including mentoring and coaching, for 650 governments, NGO staff and community-based child protection structures / Assistance to 260 children with Disability
The project will directly target 6344 vulnerable individuals (1269 HHs) the form IDPs and HC in the Blue Nile (Al Kurmuk, Ar Rusayris, Baw & Geisan), South Kordofan (Abu Jubaiha, Gedir and Umm Durein), West Kordofan (Al Meriam, an nuhud and Ghubaish) and North Kordofan (Gharb Bara, Gebrat Al-Sheikh and Um Dam) within line with the sector objectives to improve access to basic services, HH food security and stable income. Through providing the Agricultural Support (Seeds, Tools)/ Livestock Support (Distribution, Treatments)/ Livelihood Cash/Voucher Support/ Livelihood Kits Support/ Vocational Training.
This proposed intervention is designed according to the gaps and needs. AORD
will coordinate, consult, and collaborate with all the related stakeholders (targeted communities and governments) in their different levels while designing, implementing, monitoring, and reporting the activities.

1 - Needs

Blue Nile state and Kordofan states classified as war-affected states since the last conflict erupted in Sudan in 2011. The conflict has resulted in massive new displacements and badly deteriorated the basic services and affected the social wellbeing situation in four states. This created vulnerable groups to lose their homes and shelters.According to a recent child protection assessment conducted across 16 states, CP risks were already on the rise in the first half of 2020 in the 4 targeted states, with a reported 24% increase in child marriage (girls more affected than boys) and a 35% in child labor; Women, girls, boys and men continue to face the risk of different types of GBV which are contributed by several factors that include low income with no or limited/little livelihood opportunities, lack of communities’ awareness of women’s rights and GBV due to traditional norms.Children with extreme and catastrophic needs (severity categories four and five) live in 140 localities across Sudan includes the localities in 4 targeted states. Women and girls in IDP camps are particularly exposed to protection risks, such as threats, harassment, and sexual violence from armed men and/or unemployed youth.According to the HNO 2022, the struggle to meet basic needs and services is aggravated amongst IDPs and Refugees with 52% of IDPs being food insecure. According to IPC Sudan Acute Food Insecurity Analysis for 2022, 18% of the population in the BN is in Phase 3 (crisis, facing high levels of food insecurity) while a further 8% are in Phase 4 (Emergency level), 19% of the population in the NK is in Phase 3 while a further 6% are in Phase 4, 13% of the population in the WK is in Phase 3 while a further 5 % are in Phase 4 and 18% of the population in the SK is in Phase 3 while a further 8% are in Phase 4.The protracted conflicts in SK have negatively affected the social, human, physical, natural and economic capital in both states and have brought about serious negative effects in the neighbouring states. These states have % IDPs, Returnees and Refugees. IDPs exist due to the ongoing internal conflicts; the returnees have come after the secession of South Sudan; while the refugees have come from South Sudan after the current armed clashes that started in December 2013. Together these groups have further stretched the limited socio-economic capital such as community socio-economic infrastructure and limited livelihood opportunities in both the states and the fringe states. This situation has created a disproportionate effect on the lives and livelihoods of the unemployed youth, IDPs, returnees, refugees, women, children and host communities in the affected areas in general.The acute economic crisis since October 2018 continues to impede the available humanitarian assistance to reach populations in need 7.3 million Individuals in Sudan (16% of the overall population) are currently experiencing a food crisis or worse levels of food insecurity, according to the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification. Major concerns exist for the States classified under Phase 3 and above, including WK, BN, SK and NK. The main drivers of this drastic deterioration are related to the macroeconomic challenges prevailing in the country, resulting in soaring food prices and shortages of foreign currency and cash.The most vulnerable are women-headed households who suffer from acute poverty since their husbands have either been killed in war or have migrated to urban areas in search of work and all other at-risk groups in the targeted areas to access the basic food they required to feed their children, and other family members.
