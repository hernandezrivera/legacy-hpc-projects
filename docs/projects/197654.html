Project Summary

After more than 10 years of conflict in Syria, the Routine Immunization program was affected, subsequently, children suffered outbreaks of Polio and Measles during the course of the years. Strengthening routine vaccination activities is still an essential concern to increase the national coverage rate at national and governorate level, regular routine immunization activities are provided in all governorates, the vaccination activities are continued in coordination with all related partners (including UNICEF and NGOs) to cover the needs of children under 5 and to increase the community immunity especially among all vulnerable groups , WHO has facilitated the delivery of vaccines( by road& Air ) to all governorates in addition to support the operational cost of the filed work including: transportation cost, incentives for mobile teams , operation cost , which will facilitate the field work of the EPI program at national, governorates and district level. Capacity building will be provided to the healthcare workers and managers involved in the reporting, planning, implementation, and monitoring of the immunization activities. MOH is providing vaccination services to all children of Syria (target 2022 is 584282 children under the age of 1 year) in all 14 governorates. Several tactics are used for service delivery through fixed vaccination sites and mobile teams as well in coordination with the local communities to facilitate the access for the families and service providers; WHO and partners support in the coordination, technical and financial support of the activities including the IPC material procurement and cascade training for service providers and supervisors’ capacity building for quality services and gain community trust as well.
In northwest Syria, WHO supported re-establishment of EPI services, and has increased the number of active vaccinations centers from five in March 2017 to 93 in 2021. WHO supports the operational costs of 40 of these centers, covering 62 of a total of 134 vaccination teams. This work has been impeded by security issues leading to huge population movements at the beginning of 2020 and loss of accessibility to some areas. The latter issue necessitated the reallocation of 23 EPI centers to new areas to serve displaced populations inside NWS.
Within the implementation of this HRP project WHO in cooperation with Syria Immunization group (SIG) and in close collaboration with UNICEF and other NGOs/INGOs actors in the north of Syria is planning to sustain services of the Expanded Program on Immunization EPI in the NWS, including routine re-mapping of communities and new settlements to be able to follow population movements inside the country. The routine Immunization program is targeting children under two years of age with the following antigen: BCG, Hep B 0, OPV, IPV, Penta, MMR. Also, the children in school-age (first & sixth grades) and females in childbearing age CBA (15-49) with Td vaccine especially pregnant women.

3 - Needs

Due to low functionality of HFs (about 46% of HFs are partly or not functioning at all). Since COVID 19 started, there is a noticeable drop in vaccination coverage in all antigens (from 72% in 2019 to 66% in 2021). The MoH adopts several tactics to boost the immunity of the children through Fixed sites and Mobile teams hence the continuous need for technical support of WHO and partners for training, supervision for quality activity and reach to HRA. WHO field teams coordinate with partners and local community as an integral component to improve the population engagement for children protection. WHO do coordinate the immunization activities in the Hard to Reach Areas (HRA) as well; the ministry of health and the local health authorities under the SDF are committed to vaccinate children in the Eastern Euphrates side of Der-Ez-Zor and Raqqa through the Periodic Intensification of Routine Immunization (PIRI); this ensures the equal rights of children of the target group in HRA get an opportunity to get routine vaccines at least once in a quarter.Disruption of routine immunization services was significant in the north of Syria that was not accessible for cross-line operations for vaccine delivery at least for 5 years from 2012 till 2016 and was relaunched as a result of the tedious efforts by the NGOs, SIG, WHO, and UNICEF in March 2017. The work in Aleppo is technically supervised and directly supported by WHO. It is and critical need to support the presence of technical body.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks have increased recently following the eruption of the COVID-19 outbreak and the extra challenges faced by the families to access EPI services in addition to high transport cost and remoteness of some of the health centers. Lack of health personnel is particularly acute in areas affected by recent hostilities and displacement, with majority of the population living below minimum standards for healthcare workers. Overcrowding and long waiting times are top access barriers reported by patients. Also issues access constraints areas controlled by different parties; WHO coordination/facilitation is clue in these situations beside provide financial support for transport and incentives to enable the EPI workers perform their duties on outreach basis as well as investment in the solar system for the cold chain in Syria. Careful security assessments and coordination with the community leaders could mitigate this risk to certain level. Access to immunization centers for disabled children or their parents and for female-headed households leaving children un- vaccinated. Outreach vaccination teams including female staff to reach families facing access constrains. Discrimination against vulnerable groups or certain geographical areas.. The availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement. Attacks on health care is an additional risk.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Sudden change of the political control of the area. WHO hubs will coordinate the geographical response and shift the responsibility as situation evolves to ensure continuity of immunization programs. Lack or miss-leading information from the field WHO network of local staff in the field will provide accurate and timely information and coordination with local authorities. The continuous devaluation/depreciation of the Syrian pound risks the implementation of the routine immunization activities especially on the transport of logistics and mobile teams to High risk Areas and different outreach activities. WHO teams will provide both technical and financial support to facilitate access to life saving vaccines to children. While the cold chain maintenance is crucial and is a risk. The solarization, when complete, will suffice the maintenance of quality vaccines till the village level (currently the vaccines are stocked at district level) Sudden massive population movement due to any conflict and substandard living conditions increase the risk of outbreaks A contingency plan already prepared with HR,Fund and supplies for immediate response. Failure of the renewal of UNSC resolution in NWS. Escalation of immunization activities from DAM office in coordination with MOH and UNICEF.
