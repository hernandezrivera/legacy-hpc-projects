Project Summary

HNO 2021 estimates that 2.3 million South Sudanese children are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in need of immediate Child Protection. Of the 800,000 CPSC’s target for HRP 2021, War Child Holland (WCH) proposes to provide critical and life-saving Child Protection (CP), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services, through direct interventions reaching 40,000 individuals comprising of 40,000 individuals comprising of 17,150 children (7,750 girls: 9,400 boys) and 22,850 adults (11,050 women: 11,800 men) made up of parents, caregivers, community leaders and different categories of duty bearers in 4 counties – Bor South, Pigi/Canal, Fashoda and Malakal, all categorized as CP ‘Severity Level 3.
With its wide experience in the protection sector, WCH will provide community based child protection, PSS, case management and cash based programming with focus on child protection risk prevention, mitigation and response in line with child protection sub cluster strategic objectives and selected Child Protection Sub- Cluster (CPSC) indicators for 2021. This project will increase reach and access to critical Child Protection and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services for South Sudanese children - including those with disabilities- who have been disproportionately impacted by constant exposure to violence, abuse, exploitation, loss of friends and family members and who also continue to experience severe disruption of normal routines as a result of displacements either caused by violence, natural disasters for example flooding and food insecurity, dysfunctional family settings as well as due to Covid 19 related school closures and movement restrictions.
The CP services to be provided include MHPSS support to children and their caregivers, Family tracing and reunification for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASCs), Case Management (CM) for at high-risk children including cash based assistance per assessed needs, justice for children as part of CM and strengthening of Community based child protection systems and awareness raising on CP and MHPS issues. As an active member of the CPSC and relevant TWCs both at national and state levels WCH’s proposed interventions are aligned with cluster priorities and minimum standards per the 2021 objectives; (CP) PO1 and (CP) PO2.
To increase resilience and sustainability opportunities, the project is designed to use a system approach, where all levels of the socio-ecological model are activated for timely life-saving response and for promoting longer term resilience, for example, the community based child protection and psychosocial support capacity strengthening of local community structures and actors to prevent and respond to violence through effective coordination and self-driven evidence-based responses.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2021 identifies approximately 2.3 million South Sudanese children and adults as being in need of protection support. These include IDPs, separated and unaccompanied children (UASC), GBV survivors, children associated with armed groups and forces (CAAFAG) and communities living in counties severely impacted by the protracted crisis. Furthermore, the sub-national violence that escalated in May 2020 caused displacement of about 80,000 people. Jonglei was further affected by the June 2020 violence the prolonged flooding between August and December 2020 (HNO 2020). The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification further estimates that in April to July 2021, an estimated 7.24 million South Sudanese (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Based on a recent Protection Consortium assessment conducted in January 2021, Fashoda which is predicted to be IPC 4 in the lean season is already experiencing significant protection, safety and displacement concerns due to severe flooding, exacerbated food insecurity and increased pressure on diminished household resources. The COVID-19 pandemic has also increased to unprecedented proportions the risks to which children, women, girls, families and communities are exposed, including mental health and psychosocial distress, partner violence, family separations, violence against children, physical and emotional maltreatment, and risk of exploitation including child labour. Left without access to the protective environment of school by the pandemic, the risk of children being neglected, abused or exploited is reported to have increased significantly. Increased cases of child marriages, psychological distress and mental health problems including suicidal tendencies have also been reported. The escalating cases of Covid 19 in the country has also caused uncertainty about safe and timely opening of schools and is aggravating the already severe mental anguish that children and adolescents are going through. Additional concerns are also faced by children living with disabilities.The four (4) targeted locations of Bor South, Pigi/Canal, Malakal and Fashoda are impacted by all the above mentioned crises and are categorized as Child Protection Severity Level 3. They are host to a significant size of children and caregivers in need of protection response including people in PoCs, IDP settlements, returnees and host communities. The positive outcome of the national peace processes have also resulted in an increased number of returnee population in all the 4 counties both from neighboring countries and the surrounding IDP locations. Also, the draw-down of UNMISS protection forces and the attendant transition of POC sites to IDP camps, and the hoped-for sustainability of peace, may further trigger increased return movements into the 4 counties causing increase in the pre-existing needs and place increased pressure on the already strained CP and MHPSS services. Specific CP and PSS support issues affecting children in the proposed Counties include: • Increased risk of girls and boys to recruitment, sexual violence, school dropout, neglect, child marriage, child labor and mine risks; • Existence of large number of children affected by separation and neglect due to population movement and stretched caring capacity of families. - Psychological distress and self-harm behaviors among children and caregivers due to exposure to violence, displacements, livelihood disruption, prolonged school closures • Glaring gaps in CP and PSs support services available in the proposed locations including technical capacity gaps of relevant ministries and community based structures. • Deterioration of traditional support mechanisms for children and other vulnerable community members due to the impacts of conflict, displacement and effects of Covid 19. • Exposure of children to risky activities including recruitment, exploitation, robbery and street life.

2 - Project activities

War Child Holland has been implementing child protection and psychosocial support projects for more than five-years in Jonglei and Upper Nile States targeting community members in IDP camps, PoC and host communities and the organization is an active member of CPSC both at national and state levels. As such, the following proposed activities are based on practical experience and understanding of specific needs in the proposed locations. The following are key activities proposed based on identified needs in the target locations. • In Coordination with others, conduct child protection risk assessment to identify and map concerns and needs in the proposed 4 locations with focus on girls and women; • Organize project launching meeting with participation of 120 key community level actors and stakeholders in respective locations; • Develop community based child protection action plan in consultation with relevant stakeholders including children and community representatives; • Recruit, train and deploy 60 community level child protection and psychosocial support facilitators (30 M: 30 F) in 4 project locations; • Train, mentor and support 180 community leaders and representatives to strengthen community based child protection mechanisms in 4 target locations;• Facilitate child protection and psychosocial support in emergency capacity development training to 400 community actors, social workers and other key stakeholders to strengthen child protection and PSS response in the target 4 Counties; • With age, gender and disability sensitive CP and MHPSS messaging targeting IDPs, returnees and host communities, reach 40,000 individuals comprising of 17,150 children (7,750 girls: 9,400 boys) and 22,850 adults (11,050 women: 11,800 men) through awareness raising sessions and events. Participants will include parents, caregivers, community leaders and different categories of duty bearers in the 4 target counties of Bor South, Pigi/Canal, Fashoda and Malakal; • Reach 7,372 children (3,686 boys, 3,686 girls) through static and mobile CFS based recreational and psychosocial support services in Bor South and Canal/Pigi (2 counties); • Provide psychological first aid (PFA) and community based PSS services for children and caregivers in need of additional support; • Organize parenting and PSS sessions for 5,000 parents/caregivers experiencing psychological stress and facing difficulty to provide adequate emotional support to their children; • Facilitate development of referral pathway and service coordination mechanisms in 4 Counties; • Identify, assess and provide case management support to identified extremely vulnerable children especially girls including referral to other service providers; • Facilitate family tracing and reunification support and services to identified separated and unaccompanied children in target Counties; • Provide unconditional cash support for extremely vulnerable HHs caring for children in the project locations using broader case management vulnerability criteria (HHs with child labour and disability cases, children facing neglect and malnutrition, children in female headed HHs etc);• Provide child protection and PSS mainstreaming training and technical support to 10 non-protection actors in the 4 locations; • Provide periodic update on grave child rights violations through monitoring and reporting mechanism (MRM); • Strengthen feedback/complain mechanism to ensure accountability to affected population; • Conduct monthly monitoring and support visit to target communities and organize quarterly project progress review meeting with stakeholders in the 4 Counties;• Submit periodic activity and budget utilization progress report using agreed templates.

Narrative reports on the CP/MPHSS messages covered, Activity feedback /evaluation results and attendance lists will be used as data sources while KII and FGD with girls and boys including CLwD will be used for data verification during project evaluations. 6. 5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Based on learnings from other on-going WCH’s and partners’ humanitarian assistance in the 4 counties, both in-kind and cash assistance provided under this project are based on individual needs, are small and deemed unlikely to disrupt any market functionalities in the targeted locations. In addition, a rapid market survey will be conducted ahead of either in-kind or in-cash grants.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

This project will provide both in-kind support and small cash grants, both of them determined through a cost-benefit analysis. Cash support will be provided as IPA support where referrals to other services is not feasible for example where no other actor can provide support, and the child protection risk can only be resolved or reduced through this means. While the sole aim is to reduce, prevent or respond to a protection risk cash support will not be provided to children but rather to an adult who is vetted and approved of by the Community Based CP system in place and will be provided only if deemed safe for the person receiving it.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All staff, volunteers and workers will be trained on PSEA as well as be oriented and sign to WCH Child Safeguarding Policy which includes information on how to confidentially report any suspected or identified cases of PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WCH will strengthen the existing feedback and complaint mechanism to ensure the participation of vulnerable groups including individuals with disability, illiterate community members, women and girls. Assessment will be conducted to identify the most appropriate feedback channels to ensure access to vulnerable groups. Based on the assessment findings, each group will be given an opportunity to provide feedback and complaints about the project using their preferred option.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Targeted communities will be engaged and informed on the criteria to be used for selecting children and caregivers in highest need of support, identifying and responding to at-risk children, available services that they can expect and also on how they can launch complaints or provide feedback to WCH and its partners in a confidential manner.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Analysis of the beneficiary feedback results will be undertaken on a regular bases and included in topics discussed during project reflection meetings and where feasible be used to improve project delivery outcomes including project ownership by the communities.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project is centred on provision of timely and needs based response and support to children and their caregivers in critical need of CP and MHPSS support. The project will also work with other actors to ensure strengthening of referral systems and to provide information to a wide range of the population on the availability of specialized services including justice for children, mental health support, medical care among others. The project is also designed to respond to the unique challenges faced by girls and women as they continue to be disproportionately and negatively impacted by the myriad of gender-based risks and cultural practices, many which pre-existed the Covid 19 pandemic but are further compounded by the restrictions and other forms of disruptions caused by the pandemic.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5
12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The proposed project activities are designed to build on existing community structures and capacities with the view of sustaining impact and fostering community ownership. Hence, community structures including child protection committees, schools, faith based organizations and CFSs will receive capacity strengthening support to enable them to continue providing basic child protection and psychosocial support services in the longer run, with minimum to zero support. The interventions will also contribute to a smoother recovery and to strengthen resilience of both children and caregivers to better cope with the new environment. With the ongoing peace agreement implementation process, the targeted locations are expected to receive high numbers of returnees both from IDP and refugee camps on top of the existing IDPs and returnees present currently.
