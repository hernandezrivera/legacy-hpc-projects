Project Summary

Primary health care visits and vaccination coverage have sharply declined in Somalia amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, raising concerns that vaccine preventable diseases such as respiratory infections and acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) as well as malnutrition could be going untreated, all while floods have forced people from their homes. While the healthcare system’s resources are already limited and overstretched, this development could easily degenerate into a public health.
More cases of AWD are being reported in children under five years old, who are particularly vulnerable to disease, especially if their immune systems are compromised. Critical gaps in Balcad, Marka and Wanlaweyn where no WASH assistance has been received despite high risk levels for acute watery diarrhoea (AWD). Aamin is currently implementing mobile service project funded by SHF reserve allocation 2020 in Balcad district. We as well are implementing SHINE project under consortium led by ACF through support of DFID in Banadir region. Aamin Ambulance provides a twenty four hour, unparalleled free ambulance service in Mogadishu, Somalia. July-Sept 2019, Aamin ambulance transported and facilitated 638 affected people (427 female, 211 male) within the ten districts covered by the SHINE project. This was a significant increase compared to last quarter by 284. The increase can be attributed to increased awareness of the availability of the free ambulance service in the ten districts. Aamin ambulance seeks to bridge the gap of unreliable health and emergency response system through this free ambulance and first aid system to the people of Mogadishu. We take cognizance of the needs the vulnerable in the community and hence our project also plays focus on the needs of the vulnerable including women and children. Through a recent needs assessment conducted by Aamin ambulance, it was established that over 2100 expectant mothers in our target districts are in need of maternal health services (including referrals) to ensure a safe pregnancy and delivery, emergency obstetric services. The situation is even life threatening when the mothers are in need of these emergencies during the wee hours of the night. Our emergency project hence seeks scale up efforts in ensuring that no woman or new-born dies during birth by avoiding prolonged labour. Aamin ambulance has 16 fully equipped ambulances staffed with qualified and well trained professionals to provide pre-hospital care to patients. The project in Mogadishu with plan to expand operations to three other states. in this Aamin ambulance will targets 22,270 people with mobile OPD consultation treatment of common illness, incidences, and early referral for further treatment and care. Aamin Ambulance will adopt mobile clinic strategy to reach out to vulnerable people in IDPs areas and bring service close to their community. The team will provide Out-patient services to the people at the convenience of their villages. We will go to remote area, we will attain the set target and deliver high quality service the community has been yearning. ambulance will bridge it by bringing service close to the people and providing free referral services for people in critical condition. Scale-up early warning and response (EWAR) and case management of epidemic prone diseases through surveillance. The project will adopt a health informatics software that will display real time reporting, transmission and processing of epidemiological data for timely detection, verification and prompt public health response. Integration of Mental Health/Psycho-social Support (PFA) services, community engagement, and health education to community members to enhance identification and referral of persons of concern in need of MHPSS while also conducting a focused MHPSS intervention through trained social workers. Conduct psycho-education sessions for persons in need, including raising awareness on key protection risks faced by PSNs i.e discrimination, stigma

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1: Protection of civilians • We shall ensure the access routes to the healthfacilities and services are safe by identifying routes for our ambulances to operate andavoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets,crossfire or violence. • The project shall employ staff with skills and experiencesworking with children patients • We shall consult both displaced and host communitiesabout health needs and assess whether Aamin ambulance interventions could causetension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. Risk 2: Exclusion andmarginalization • We shall ensure that beneficiaries know of available health servicesand assistance where/how to obtain it. • We shall communicate information throughvarious means to ensure communication reaches the broader community • We shallinvolve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifyingand responding to health needs. • Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons withdisabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women andmarginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs andpreferences for emergency health services. • Engage the community and committeerepresentative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-makingprocesses that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership and improve therelevance and sustainability of the response Risk 3: Protection mainstreaming • Incooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report securityincidents concerning hospitals to advocate against attacks on hospitals and militaryuse of hospitals • Ensure the location and access routes to health facilities andservices are safe: • We are aware of how power dynamics between our staff and thepatient can affect the patient’s response and attitude towards the staff/organisationand service provided • Ensure all our staff, implementing partners and volunteersworking with the beneficiaries understand, sign and adhere to Aamin Code of Conduct• In cooperation with protection actors, we shall enhance the capacity of communityhealth workers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse andexploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures.
