Project Summary

Extensive use of explosive weapons has been reported since the outbreak of the ongoing conflict. As a result, the civilian population in Khartoum and other cities and urban areas in Al Gazeera, White Nile, Kordofan and Darfur states affected by the ongoing violence face an immediate risk of accidents caused by explosive ordnance (EO), which often damage critical infrastructure and subsequently deny safe access to it, further intensifying vulnerabilities caused by the conflict. Majority of the IDPs whom displaced from the areas newly affect by the conflict have not been sensitized to the threat of EO. Hence, more than 6 million people, including humanitarian actors, across the country need to learn how to mitigate the threat of EO and to safely access areas contaminated with EO. Thus, this project is aiming to support the at-risk population and humanitarian actors in mitigating the risks of EO through provision of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) to raise their awareness about the threat. EORE will be promoted through different interventions including direct sessions, community-based approach, radio broadcasting, public information campaigns and distribution of awareness materials. More than 270,000 people will receive EO awareness sessions in urban and rural areas, impacted by the current or previous conflicts. Community Liaison Officers (CLOs) that accompanying the land release teams will provide EORE interventions to the affected communities in addition to their community liaison work.
Additionally, safe access to areas contaminated with EO will be assisted for affected populations and humanitarian actors through assessment, survey, and clearance of EO in rural and urban areas. More than 3,544,000 people including local residence, IDPs, returnees and service providers will gain access to services, residences, and infrastructures. Considering the security and access issues UNMAS will establish a mine action advisory team (MAAT) and deploy EOD specialists to Chad to assist the mobility of cross-border humanitarian assistance into the Darfur states and also including other areas where the Humanitarian Actors wish to carry out cross border operations and supply aid. The newly contaminated urban and rural areas will be prioritised for land release interventions due to the expected immediate return of IDPs when the situation allows. Thus, UNMAS will deploy survey clearance teams for urban areas clearance and ensure the rapid data collections ready for deployment upon cessation of conflict or access to address the new contamination left behind from the ongoing war and maintain the minimum needed workforce of UNMAS.
Furthermore, the capacity of mine action management will be enhanced, and the mine action sector will be managed in a prioritised, principled and efficient manner to ensure quality delivery of MA interventions. In this regard, mine action actors will be accredited to operate in accordance with an agreed prioritised plan.
Considering gender and diversity is key to delivering equitable and inclusive mine action interventions. Through gender/age-tailored EORE and gender/age-disaggregated data collection, mine action responds to diverse needs of vulnerable populations. Gender equality and women's empowerment will be promoted through close consultation and collaboration with women-led organizations. Furthermore, most of the CLOs are normally ladies however EORE teams are gender balanced. Additionally, UNMAS will ensure that female deminers will be given equal competitive opportunities to be recruited with the LR teams.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mine action is basically a protection intervention aiming to protect people lives from the threat of landmines and explosive remnants of war, MA sub sector ensures equal participation, consultation and inclusion of affected populations in the planning and implementation of mine action programs as well as ensuring that the different exposure to and knowledge of risks related to explosive ordnance among women, girls ,boys and men from diverse groups is incorporated into risk reduction efforts, including clearance and risk education. It will also facilitate and support multi-sectoral assistance to victims of explosive ordnance that is responsive to the needs of women, girls, men and boys from diverse groups, and advocate for the mainstreaming of gender and diversity considerations in national mine action policies, institutions and programs. Additionally, mine action implementation partners are encouraged to ensure they “do no harm” to the environment or livelihoods and avoid increasing the long-term vulnerability of affected communities. Therefore, this project considers protection mainstreaming through all phases of the project starting from the project concept throughout project design, implementation and closure of the project.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Mine action response in its nature is community-based approach, i.e. during designing, planning and implementation phases mine action consult and consider the need of the affected communities. Assessments will be conducted, and community consultations will be taken through all project phases. Community feedback will be discussed with the implementing partners and relevant stakeholders. CLOs will collect information from the affected communities to priorities or identify lands for clearance in consultation with UNMAS and NMAC. EORE teams will ensure EORE messages designed based on feedback from the communities to ensure the message addressed the risk posed on the community. EORE teams will collect feedback from the communities to ensure the messages understood and behaviors changed.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Inclusion of local communities in planning and implementation of Mine Action activities is crucial for the success of the intervention, this will be done through consultation with the different groups in a non-discriminatory manner while accounting for the diversity of communities. Mine Action Community liaising is an effective approach to link communities to Mine Action through improving their participation and inclusion as well as look at their complaints and feedback. Furthermore, UNMAS is undergoing the process of inclusion of PSEA components in its Mine Action Hotline where agents will be trained to respond to PSEA related violations and treat cases with high confidentiality and coordination level among the relevant service providers, NGOs and UN agencies.
