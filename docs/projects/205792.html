Project Summary

The education project in the two counties shall be accountable to the affected population and will observe the conflict gender sensitive protocols in all aspects of the project programming. In this case, 23% adult male 17% adult female, 60 % of school going age children including those with disability who are above 10 years. The elderly persons shall also be considered in the recruitment during construction of Temporary learning spaces (TLPs) as primary beneficiaries fulfilling the requirement of the project planned targets for each location mentioned. Returnees, IDPs and host communities’ children, youth and the elderly shall be given priority in recruitment process in the project. Children boys and girls from marginalized groups shall be given priority to access learning opportunities. The project shall identify beneficiaries based on gender, age and disability consideration to serve a positive role model for women, men, girls and boys in the communities selected to ensure that the staff recruited will understand how to articulate the gender transformative goals of the project. Channels of hope gender and social analysis and action (SAA) are key models on which the project shall be built and in this case, it will require face to face interactions to ensure that the facilitation skills are obtained from the recruited teachers and staff. The WASH, peace building and conflict sensitive components shall be mainstreamed. There is also a need to enhance the understanding of the underlying needs and cross cutting protection challenges by seeking durable solutions provided to beneficiaries. All project staff shall receive training or refresher training on protocols for disclosure and referral mechanism before the commencement of the project. Some of the essential activities will include distribution of essential basic school materials both for learning and teaching to schools. The project will connect children to learning environment where parent, teachers and persons with disability shall play a vital role in connecting them to livelihood programs through cash- based interventions to provide social support to improve their learning outcomes by renovating and repairing the damaged learning spaces enabling children to access quality education. The project shall cater for education needs of the refugees, returnees, internally displaced persons and host communities by training teachers on pedagogy and code of conduct. As the influx of returnees continues to increase due to relevant peace acquired in the Country, the complexity and breadth of response requires a more rigorous intervention approach than before to better understand the strategy of how to resolve the huge education needs in the project locations selected for intervention. 1. Needs

In 2008, the population of South Sudan was enumerated at 8.4 million, with 2022 projections placing this figure at 14.2 million, and representing an increase of almost six million inhabitants or 69% in 14 years. The population growth rate averaged 2.9% between 2015 and 2023. This overall population growth has been paralleled by the growth in the school-age population, which expanded by over one million between 2016 and 2022. In 2022 nearly half (49%) of the population was under the age of 14, with a median age of 18 years. The youth of the country is further reflected in its dependency ratio of over 100%, indicating the important levels of pressure such a young population exerts on the resources being produced by the working population. This demographic pressure indicates the need for the education system to expand to meet the needs of its growing young population. Gender-based violence (GBV) is deeply entrenched in South Sudan, where women and girls continue to be at high risk both at public and private spaces. Gender based-violence is not only manifested in the physical and sexual violence, but also lack of equal opportunities for women and girls to lead a fulfilling life, high rate of illiteracy amongst girls and women, high rate of child marriage, high maternal mortality due to preventable causes, limited access to livelihood opportunities, are some of the indicators that highlight the dire need to address the gender inequality as the root causes that diminishes the education progress in South Sudan’s population faces pressure from various patterns of migration, including external migration, internal displacement, and the return of previously displaced populations. New internal displacements have intensified in recent years, with more than 550,000 of the 2 million internally displaced persons in 2021 alone (OCHA, 2021). Early marriage is widespread in the country, with 2010 estimates citing that 52% of South Sudanese girls between the ages of 15 and 18 were married, with some marrying as young as 12 (Ministry of Health, 2010). This contributes to the high adolescent birth rate also observed in the country, with one-third of women aged 15−19 already having begun childbearing in 2018 (World Health Organization, 2019). Early marriage and childbearing contribute to girls’ dropout from school, which in turn contributes to overall lower levels of education. This is reinforced by other harmful traditional practices such as very high bride wealth that is believed to be behind the cattle raids, forced and child marriages. 20% of returnees in Malakal County reported to their respective locations., assessment has been conducted on their arrival and on transit to camp by UNIMISS and IOM .Malakal and Rubkona Counties Experience cases of Teachers who are not paid on time with inadequate compensation which normally drive conflict especially in a fragile context therefore it is critical that the education program and policies addressing conditions of work and compensations should timely be implemented in a conflict sensitive education environment. Some schools have been occupied by armed groups and returnees and have been damaged beyond recognition. In some cases, even if schools are not destroyed or damaged, children cannot go to their schools because they are occupied by people who have been forced to leave their homes (OCHA, 2022f). In 2021, 17 schools were used as temporary shelters (OCHA, 2022d). Conflicts and disasters also have the potential to impact children’s education indirectly, but measuring these effects are more complex and less resolved. There are many elderly and youthful people in Makal and Rubkona that have not stepped in any class or have no access to any education therefore, it is advisable for them to access the non-formal education which consists of the Alternative Education System (AES) being flexible education program.

2. Project activities

1.Conduct school assessment to ascertain the number of schools functional in Rubkona and Malakal Counties to feed the EMIS data 2.Distribute and supply the learning and teaching materials that can support retention and improve the progress of the learners to under their educational cycles well. 3.WAO shall target to Construct and rehabilitate 18 learning spaces which have been destroyed during the conflict in Malakal and Rubkona Counties .4. WAO shall embark on training 90 teachers on ethical subject matter and pedagogical skills in Malakal and Rubkona Counties . 5.WAO shall conduct training of school management committees (SMC) and parents Teachers Associations (PTA) and other education authorities in the two counties on school management ,governance , climate change and disaster risk reduction (DRR). 6.WAO shall mainstream the WASH activities in the project where schools shall be targeted with the construction of standard Latrines for boy and girl Child to improve the learning outcomes in the two counties. 60 WASH Clubs shall be established in schools to ensure that girls improve their learning habits this will be accompanied by the distribution of 500 dignity kits to adolescent girls.7. The communities and local authorities in the two Counties shall receive 2 days training on the conflict sensitive approaches after conducting conflict analysis. 8.WAO shall also conduct 2 days training of the community leaders and other partners on disaster risk reduction and Do no harm approaches and principles in the flood prone areas due to climate change .9.WAO shall establish and support 20 School gardens for the pupils to understand the concept of vegetable production to improve the teachers and pupils nutritional conditions. 10.WAO shall increase the efforts of supporting the ECD program to 1-6 years Children in Rubkona and Malakal. 11.WAO shall initiate the program of tree planting by establishing the Environmental Clubs in Schools to improve the natural habitat around the schools . 12.WAO shall conduct two go to School campaigns in the Communities encouraging more Children to join education sector to reduce illiteracy rate. Numeracy and literacy classes shall be established for the elderly and youth to compensate the yeas they lost without education due to conflict in Rubkona and Malakal Counties. 13. WAO shall involve all the education actors to be engaged in peace building coordination mechanisms and education coordination mechanisms to promote equitable and conflict sensitive distribution of education activities. 14. WAO shall engage the decision makers and gate keepers in the Communities and families to address the deeply rooted beliefs and practices that sustain child marriage to make more girls access education in the two Counties . This will ensure adolescent girls aged 10-19 actively participate in lifeskills , age appropriate health education , economic empowerment and social protection. 15. WAO shall get involved in the payment of Teachers Salaries and stipends to compensate their time of being school 16 WAO shall target 250 Youth on TVET -Program providing different activities, Hair dressing, Computer application, Tailoring , Soap ,Shampoo and Sanitizer production for 6 months in Rubkona and Malakal Counties .
