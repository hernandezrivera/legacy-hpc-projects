Project Summary

In South Sudan, only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64 per cent of people across the country rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41 per cent), either take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19 per cent), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4 per cent). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water were found in Upper Nile, with 66 per cent, followed by Jonglei with 43 per cent and Central Equatoria with 41 per cent. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17 per cent of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39 per cent of people reporting insufficient access to water for hand washing.
Access to sanitation remained low, with only 17 per cent of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4 per cent have access to a shared latrine, and 2 per cent have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This means that 77 per cent of people do not have access to a latrine. In 34 counties, zero to 10 per cent of households reported using latrines. At the country level, open defecation is practiced by 73 per cent of the population. Regional access to sanitation varied greatly, from 75 per cent in Western Bahr el Ghazal to 8 per cent in Warrap, 9 per cent in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and 10 per cent in Jonglei.
Because poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs. Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water at domicile jeopardizing the benefits gained in nutrition programs, World Vision International South Sudan intends to reach the IPDs and Host community with an integrated WASH intervention which will target the population of Upper Nile (Melut, Renk, Malakal, Kodok, Baliet, Panikang counties), Western Equatoria (Yambio, Nzara, Ezo, Nagero, Tambura), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil North and East) , Warrap ( Twic, Tonj North, Gogrial West and Gogrial East), and Central Equatoria (Juba) states. The proposed WASH project will be implemented in the communities, Schools, health facilities, including nutrition sites.
Through this proposed project, WVSS will aim to achieve the following specific objectives for the 2021 - Humanitarian Response Plan Specific:
- Reduce morbidity and mortality, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, of the most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5.
- Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5.
- Enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, support returns and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas
To materialize the WASH cluster objectives, World Vision will provide lifesaving WASH emergency interventions in the former Upper Nile, Warrap state, Northern Bhar El Ghazal and Western Equatoria states. The WASH emergency interventions will improve access to safe water, secured and gender appropriate sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion, which will be integrated in other sectors, among them health, nutrition, Food security and livelihood, and child protection/ GBV.

1 - Needs

The protracted armed conflict which started since December 2013 in the Republic of South Sudan has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population, and stretched the population’s coping capacity. Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the living standard and wellbeing of 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities.In South Sudan, only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing locations, in particular in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of cholera and other diarrhoeal or vector-borne diseases. IDPs in PoC sites do not have sufficient access to hygiene and sanitation facilities and are at risk of disease outbreaks in the congested conditions. WASH needs are also high among IDPs and returnees in non-camp settings and among their already stretched host communities. While host communities and non-displaced people are in need of WASH items and facilities, increasing needs are expected with recently returned refugees and IDP groups, or those settling in new locations. Around 15 per cent of households have at least one disabled family member. Provision of with household water treatment products to households in the target locations. 3. Water quality testing, analysis and chlorination for water source points. 4. Rehabilitation of water points from non-functioning water sources rehabilitated. 5. Construction of household level sanitation facilities at selected beneficiaries to support open defecation free communities in target locations in Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, and Unity states 6. Training water management committees of 8 people per each WMC committee. 7. Conduct hygiene promotion activities to improve the knowledge on prevention and mitigation of WASH-related diseases. 8. Provision of WASH integrated interventions starting from the OTP/SC will target the couple “mother/caretaker – malnourished children” until the household level aiming to break the vicious circle of diarrheal diseases/nematodes with malnutrition, addressing also smallstock open defecation affecting the young children. 9. Carrying out comprehensive water points management for cattle and small stock requirement during the dry season,with promotion of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent animal open defecation down to the household level. 10. Provision of WASH in Health lifesaving engineering for outbreak control (cholera/AWD, typhoid, HEV, EVD etc.) by transmission contexts (waterborne, intra-domiciliary, school, market, funerals, health facilities upon specific demands)upon epidemiological investigations. 11. Using WASH intervention to address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectorial evaluations and analyses. 12. Conduct WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation in and outside PoC settlement will be based on Protection assessment conducted by female staff with women, girls and children for WASH activities adequately planned with the community input 13. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be considered as a key pillar of empowering communities during implementation of the project. World Vision will ensure the project is owned by beneficiaries from onset by sharing information regarding the project for instance, project goals, objectives, project roles between World Vision and beneficiaries will be shared. PSEA, will also be included in the project through creation of awareness where men, women, boys, girls and people with disabilities are sensitized on the causes and dangers of PSEA and reporting channels. World Vision will do this by facilitating selection of community members who are trained on PSEA. The members will be part of the local structures that are responsible of other communities. Through the knowledge given to them, they would support on passing of PSEA messages during the project life-span and beyond. Other mechanisms of passing information to community members include conducting of local campaigns where various members are encouraged to participate in. At the time of registration of beneficiaries for assistance which is usually done from doo-to-door of households, World Vision staff and volunteers will use the opportunity to discuss about PSEA

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will conduct needs analysis of identified prior to implementation of project activities to identify needs of vulnerable groups. From this phase of project, World Vision will establish a system (complaint feedback mechanism) that makes smooth communication of every beneficiary feedback to World Vision. Individuals with disabilities and those who are illiterate will be given special attention by mobilizing them to participate in activities and provide feedback. Local translators will be trained on the project and asked to disseminate information to the illiterate beneficiaries. On the other hand, they would return feedback that were gathered to World Vision for analysis and use in the project. Local project committees that are formed in each area will be representatives from vulnerable groups including disabled, illiterate, women and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will raise awareness by hoisting posters in public places, burners and using the mass media to inform beneficiaries about the project. The project will provide a toll free number for beneficiaries to report their concerns regarding the feedback mechanism and complaints handling

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

the project will use feedback from beneficiaries to modify the manner in which assistance will be provided to project beneficiaries. This will be done for instance engaging community leaders and representatives of all targeted groups to inform location for water points and public sanitation facilities. The project will ensure adequate consultations with key beneficiary representatives before making a final decision that will affect beneficiaries
