Project Summary

UNICEF through its partners aims to contribute to scaling up equitable access to quality formal and non-formal education for 25,000 most vulnerable Libyan school age children (boys and girls aged 6 to 17) in in conflicted-affected/underserved areas namely in the east, Benghazi and Derna, in the west Tripoli, Sebratha, Misrata, Sirte, Zwara, Azzawiya and in the south, Sebha, Ubari, Murzuq and Wadi Ashati. UNICEF and partners will support access to non-formal/formal education services through the provision of educational supplies, teacher trainings, enhancing the evidence base and small-scale rehabilitation of schools. UNICEF will also work to strengthen the capacity of teaching personnel in Education in Emergency (EiE) and conflict resolution and tolerance to ensure needs of the children and adolescents are timely addressed in the protracted crisis/in sudden onset of emergencies.

Needs

-As highlighted by the Humanitarian Response Plan 2018, the protracted conflict has damaged vital education infrastructure, 489 schools had been affected by the crisis of which 40 have been fully damaged, 423 partially damaged, and 26 are accommodating Internally Displaced People (IDPs) consequently affecting the education and led to the shortage of essential educational supplies including textbooks, laboratories and furniture. - Libya’s protracted crisis continues to adversely affect access to quality education among as many as 343,176 Libyan IDP, migrant, refugee school-aged children and adolescents. Out of this figure, there are 92,981 school-aged IDP, returnee, refugee and migrant school age children remain the most vulnerable and in need of education support, particularly, in conflict affected areas and/or under-served areas including Sebha, Ubari, Murzuq, Tripoli, Benghazi, and Derna. - The protracted crisis has impacted boys and girls differently as the lack of education services leaves children vulnerable to a myriad of protection concerns. Education provides children with a sense of normalcy and routine that supports the reduction of psychosocial stress for children. Furthermore, studies have shown that the longer that children are out of school, it is more difficult to enroll them in non-formal education opportunities that strengthen the pathways back into formal education. Girls are particularly vulnerable in times of crisis, as they are often kept at home due to a lack of a secure environment (whether en route to school or inside the school setting).- An estimated two out of five registered teachers are inactive in Libya. Many of those who are working as teachers lack essential education and classroom management skills, which have affected the quality of education children receive across Libya. Teachers are not well-equipped to respond to the needs of children/adolescents in conflict-affected areas or in emergencies. This has a significant impact on the quality of education that children receive, particularly, when teachers themselves are also in need of psychosocial support.- Regular access by humanitarian/education partners into conflict affected areas has been constrained particularly in the southern part of the country, thus, impacting the timely support provided to children in need. - Data collection and analysis has been extremely limited and challenged, in addition to the little presence of national/local partners that can respond to humanitarian/education needs.

Activities

- Proposed activities include provision of essential teaching and learning materials to school age children, to enhance the education process. - Provision of non-formal education along with psychosocial support in a safe and protective environment, aiming at the integration of children/adolescents into formal education or alternative learning opportunities, with a focus on children with disabilities. Activities will be tailored based on the needs of age group, gender, and status. This will be in coordination across sectors, namely with the Child Protection and the WASH sectors. - Improving learning environments through using a two pronged approach of addressing violence in schools, through teacher training, awareness raising and child centered interventions and secondly through the small scale rehabilitation of schools or alternative learning spaces including inclusive (ie also children with disabilities) and gender-sensitive WASH facilities in coordination with UNICEF’s WASH program/partners. - To enhance the quality of education, professional development of teaching personnel in Education in Emergencies (EiE) including psychosocial support, positive discipline and conflict-resolution & tolerance will be supported. - Support will be provided to strengthen the evidence base to inform programming, through standardized assessments and capacity building interventions for partners in terms of data collection.
