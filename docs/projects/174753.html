Project Summary

WFP’s programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 1000days. The programme activities include: Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment, Social and behavioral change communication in favor of appropriate infant and young child feeding practices and other Family practices, distribution of specialized nutritious food to children aged 6-23months and and MAM children aged 24-59Months for treatment of moderate acute malnutrition(MAM) and
promotion of the use of the blanket feeding platform for provision of complementary services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning, etc. WFP in 2021 intends to target about 33946 beneficiaries across the North, East, and Adamawa regions.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The consequences of the various humanitarian crises in Cameroon are aggravating factors for the deterioration of the already fragile nutritional status of affected populations. Indeed, the prevalence of stunting in children under 5 is above the WHO the serious threshold of 30% in all regions affected by humanitarian crises, and rates of acute malnutrition do not show significant changes for several years and remain on the poor thresholds. Children under the age of 5 with acute malnutrition are more likely to die than their healthy peers. Pregnant and lactating women are also particularly affected because they have limited access to adequate quality and quantity of food in a context of limited access to basic social services. Added to this are the close links between the nutritional status of mother and child during the first 1000days. In a context of massive displacement of people (internally displaced persons or refugees), women and young children are particularly exposed to poor living conditions and the accumulation of trauma that can affect practices feeding infants and young children, in particular the ability of mothers to ensure optimal breastfeeding, further weakening the nutritional status of the already most vulnerable populations. The results of the latest SMART nutrition survey conducted in November 2019 by UNICEF and thegovernment of Cameroon classified the Far North, East and North regions in a poor nutrition situation with global acute malnutrition rates of 5.2percent, 5.5percent and 6.2percent respectively. Stunting remains a public health problem in all 4 regions with prevalence exceeding the WHO serious threshold of 30%. The Far North has the highest prevalence at 38.2%.
