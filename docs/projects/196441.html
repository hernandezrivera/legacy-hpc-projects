Project Summary

Throughout 2023, Violet organization plans to implement comprehensive livelihoods support interventions by applying a variety of modalities to address the needs of the different groups with proper modality.
The proposed project will effectively contribute to supporting the self-reliance of the most vulnerable crisis-affected groups in NWS and improving the livelihoods and value chains through income generation activities as follows:
1. Cash for work for crops and vegetable value chains, targeting 2000 (10000 individuals, 2500 men, 2700 women, 2200 boys, and 2600 girls) CFW workers for one month, and an estimated number of 200 farmer benefit from the service through CFW workers in Idleb and Aleppo governorates.
Among the total target under this activity, at least 15% PWDs will be reached, and 50 female beneficiaries.
2. Vocational training for agribusinesses and Food processing, targeting 300 households (1500 individuals, 375 men, 400 women, 300 boys, and 425 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates.
Among the total target under this activity, at least 15% PWDs will be reached, and 50 female beneficiaries.
3. Business grants for agribusiness and food processing, targeting 500 households (2500 individuals, 625 men, 700 women, 500 boys, and 675 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. With an estimated total cost of 1150 USD per household.
Among the total target under this activity, at least 15% PWDs will be reached, and 50 female beneficiaries.
4. Agriculture inputs provision through various modalities (in kind, voucher, cash for inputs) with relevant training for farmers on GAP and climate-smart agriculture, targeting 500 households (2500 individuals, 625 men, 700 women, 500 boys, and 675 girls) in Idelb governorate with an estimated cost of 700 USD per each one.
Among the total target under this activity, at least 15% PWDs will be reached, and 50 female beneficiaries.
5. Provision and Installation of solar powering irrigation systems; Violet aims through this project to install at least 20 solar-powering irrigation systems for the group of farmers in the surrounding river areas to benefit from the clean energy and reduce the costs of using fuel-powered irrigation systems.
6. Supporting assets building and assets protection through the provision of livestock, animal feed, and fodder production support with relevant training for technicians and livestock breeders, targeting at least 350 livestock keepers (1750 individuals, 450 men, 500 women, 350 boys, and 450 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates with livestock assets and fodder distribution.
7. Provide emergency livestock treatment and training for veterinary services, targeting at least 75000 heads (Sheep, goat, and poultry) with veterinarian services such as treatment and vaccination and artificial insemination for cows. With training for 50 livestock technicians (vet and vet assistants), including undergraduate and early graduated technicians.
8. Establish/strengthen the capacity to provide essential services for local communities, including an early warning system.
This includes weather station fixing and soil tester for analysis and training on operating those systems and devices.
Out of the total amount requested, 80,000 USD is already secured.

3 - Needs

According to the FSA HNO 2023, The continuous deterioration in food and nutrition situations and the adoption of negative coping strategies have impacted the resilience of households. Lack of employment and income opportunities continues, with the burden driven by lack of purchase power which creates an increase in household food insecurity and difficulties in securing minimum food needs. Moreover, Livestock has not been spared from the impact of the prevailing water shortage and scarcity, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed and generally unfavorable weather conditions. The unfavorable 2021 – 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development, increased demand for fodder, limited supply, and high prices, especially imported feed. This trend will likely continue if the forecasted below-average rainfall for 2022/2023 is realized. The above-mentioned challenges continue to undermine the severely undermine health and nutrition of livestock and reduce the viability of livestock-based food production and livelihood systems. To address the challenges, there is a need for emergency provision of agriculture inputs for vulnerable farmers and livestock breeders, combined with resilience-building actions, such as supporting early warning systems and DRR approaches, rehabilitation of irrigation systems restoration, and enhancement of the bread value chain, which will help them to be more self-reliant.

4 - Implementation of activities

VO aims to respond to emergency needs by the implementation of different activities with an appropriate modality based on the needs, BNFs preference availability, and functionality of the markers.Prior to the implementation, VO will engage the BNFs, and project stakeholders in the project design and implementation to ensure the local ownership of the project. Thus, VO will sign MOUs with the LCs demonstrating their roles and responsibilities. Moreover, VO will ensure close coordination with the FSL cluster and humanitarian actors in the field to avoid potential overlapping.During the implementation, VO will ensure the BNFs protection and do No Harm principle by consulting the BNFs on the distribution location, Timing and arranging the priority ques for elderlies, women, and PWD, but also through training the staff on the Organization code of conduct and PSEA policy. Additionally, VO will ensure the field staff is trained on the COVID-19 prevention measures and make sure the measures are applied to avoid the harm that may occur to the BNFS and targeted communities.To avoid the tension that may occur between the communities and different groups, VO will include a % of the most vulnerable HC members in the project but also will announce the project criteria and make sure it's clear to the BNFs and activate the CRM mechanism.For the CVA modality, VO will ensure the market is functioning prior to the distribution in order to avoid the harm that may occur to the non-BNFs

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excludedLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighBefore implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria are transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate information with communities about alternative services.2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from assistance or distribution.Likelihood: Low; Impact: HighInformation sharing and distribution points are suitable for PWDs, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance (transportation/delivery) for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, and training staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to and from or in overcrowded distributionsLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighSelection of suitable distribution (time, location, accessibility), referral of special cases, avoiding long queues and allowing clear space for waiting, operating with different locations.4) At-risk groups do not receive needed servicesLikelihood: Medium; Impact: MediumCoordination with relevant sectors to avoid duplications, collaboration with local protection team to ensure equitable access to services, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, systematically use Washington group Questions as tool to include disability in the selection, considering intersectionality in targeting.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, and vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks: 1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs for the trainingMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return, in any case, emphasis on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as an alternative, gender-balanced staff during training, also in MEAL observation. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staffMitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender-balanced project and MEAL teams, assign suitable training sites with gender-segregated WASH facilities, identify at-risk households and provide special assistance to PWDs- Violet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure the participation of women during all phases of the project, especially in the design of the activities and monitoring.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: The security situation worsensRisk1: prevent the movement of Violet staff and delay project implementationMitigation1: VO established SOPs in place, staff trained, and an experienced and dedicated security team will continue to monitor trends to enable rapid and appropriate changes to implementation plans;Assumption 2– Changes in local power structures.Risk 2: delay of program delivery.Mitigation 2: Ongoing communication with actors operational across Violet’s area of intervention and continued adherence to acceptance strategies built on community and stakeholder understanding of Violet’s programming.Assumption3: The money transfer agent is following all Violet policies, instructions, and methodologyRisk3: Diversion of cash when using a money transfer agent (MTA)Mitigation 3: The MTA will be requested to provide the beneficiaries’ signatures and beneficiaries’ original vouchers alongside the invoice requesting payment. The transfer agent will not be reimbursed unless he gives the original vouchers and the signed lists.Risk4: crossing borders are closed due to COVID-19 or the suspension of the UN security council resolutionMitigation4: the project was designed flexibly to ensure the continuous delivery of humanitarian assistance as all project materials are available in Syria
