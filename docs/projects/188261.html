Project Summary

The ongoing escalation of conflict in various parts of Syria is causing a continuous high influx of IDP in various safer locations in Northern Syria. Majority of these IDP are living in the settlements, makeshift camps or formal camps. The existing water and sanitation system in Northern Syria are overstretched. With the huge influx of IDP maintaining existing WASH facilities and upgrading to cope with the increasing demands of at risk population is urgently required. This will ensure that people can full ll their basic water, sanitation and hygiene needs and risk of diseases and environmental degradation is minimized. Through the proposed project, SYCAC aims to support those who are in need for wash assistance. 100,000 people will benefit from improved access to water and sanitation facilities. The project is primarily designed based on Humanitarian Need overview 2022. The project will support the formal and informal settlements as well as well host communities with core WASH services.
The activities included in this proposal are in line with the WASH cluster objective which is support to water, sanitation and sewage systems to ensure continuous services for affected people in Syria and deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to most vulnerable people. SYCAC prioritizes WASH support interventions for the conflict affected population in the target location to enhance beneficiaries’ resilience in coping with ongoing crisis through the provision of access to clean water as well as supporting them in cleaning the wash facilities in a clean and healthy environment. SYCAC preferred to work on the project through water trucking instead of providing a water and sewage network connected to power grid or equipped with solar panels in the targeted areas for several reasons, including:
SYCAC will target the camps and slums of housing for those targeted in the project, and this makes it difficult to work on the water and sewage networks because of the infrastructure of camps, in addition to providing the camps with complete networks means that those people has become permanently residing in the camps, and this contradicts the humanitarian action plan,
As the humanitarian action plan is to help those people most in need to bear the conditions of living in the camps on a temporary basis and not to build a sustainable water network, and the main goal is to return these people to their homes and villages from which they were expelled or to find a better and sustainable alternative that achieves basic human rights standards, and this project is a temporary solution to provide the beneficiaries with one of the most important necessities of life and the human right, which is clean drinking water.
Below will be the intended activities to achieve the project objectives:
1 . Adequate quality and quantity of water: Water supply will ensure through water trucking. SYCAC will sign an agreement with a contractor to supply water to the targeted camps on daily basis (30 L per person will be provided on daily basis). This water will be sufficient to cover the drinking, bathing and household need of the individuals.
2. Water testing and treatment (chlorination): Incident Response team will conduct water testing and chlorination before it is available for use by the beneficiaries. This will help to eliminate the risk of using contaminated water.
3. Waste removal service (solid waste management): Through a service provider (contractor) SYCAC will ensure to collect the garbage from the camps and deport it to the agreed damp sites on daily basis. This will improve the overall environment and decrease the risk of diseases spread. SYCAC will also place garbage bins
4. Operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities: Sanitation facilities (latrines and bathing areas) will be gender segregated and will have protection measures this will be considered during the regular maintenance of the facilities (lockable doors, lighting of paths to facilities)

4 - Needs

communities purchasing water exclusively from commercial water trucks received on average only 56 liters per capita per day, with at least 10 per cent of them receiving less than 32 liters per capita per day. Areas well served by free water from networks use far more than the average of 74 liters per day. Families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 10 per cent of their income on water, with 3 per cent of the population spending more than 20 per cent of their income to purchase water. The situation is even more difficult for people living in informal settlements, where families can spend more than 50 per cent of their incomes on water. Regarding sanitation, more than 99 per cent of the surveyed population has access to a functioning toilet.Nevertheless, this figure may give a false impression that the sanitation situation in Syria is satisfactory, while at least 70 per cent of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are either not functional or only partially functional leading to significant community-level health risks. Recent estimates indicate that only 18 per cent of the population is still served by functional wastewater treatment plants. Dysfunctionality of existing wastewater treatment plants and sewage networks contributing to the discharge of raw sewage, will continue to have a significant impact on water sources, soil and the overall water resource management.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1 . Risk of theft of NFIs and chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be attractive to armed groups inside Syria.(SYCAC will promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock). 2. Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities. (MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities.) 3. Closure of Turkish border will hinder the access of some material and staff, we will look for other alternative to get the material from the Syrian market or coordinate with the Turkish authority to deliver the items through humanitarian border.
