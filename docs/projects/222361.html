Project Summary

The emergency Education project will be majorly tailored to provide Education in Emergency EIE) services to the vulnerable Children including CWD of IDP, Returnees and Host communities who are affected by crisis in Aweil North, Pigi, Fangak, Gogrial East and Aweil East counties , Targeting 13,747 beneficiaries (7006B, 6505G, 141M & 95W) of which 10% will be children with disability(CWD) through contributing to the following three objective; 1) Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level and need.2) Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle. 3) Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response.
The objective of the Lifesaving EiEs intervention will be met through the following key activities; Back to school and sensitization campaigns to increase enrolment of children and adolescents (3-17yrs) to PPE, Primary and Secondary classes; Enrolment of pupils in Formal and Non formal education, Establishment/ Rehabilitation of Temporary learning spaces, Construction of gender sensitive WASH facilities, Capacity building of Teachers on EiE, Distribution of learning kits, procurement and distribution of learning and recreational material for children and youth, Establishment and orientation of PTA/SMC including training, and offering deworming health services to targeted learners.
In addition, this project will directly target teachers with 40% as female teachers across the targeted locations. Women participation will be encouraged across the board. While this project purposes to primarily focus on the above-mentioned school age children, SAADO will also target children (age 3 - 5 years) with ECD interventions. The following key areas will be the focus of this project: Access to education and retention of boys and girls including out of School of children, children with disabilities and teenage mothers is enhanced: Barriers to accessing education, such as lack of learning materials, inadequate learning spaces, WASH facilities will be reduced through provision above mention activities including provision of assistive learning materials to CWDs. The quality of teaching and learning is sustained through the capacity of teachers to adapt teaching to challenging conditions, psychosocial support for children, provision of teaching and learning materials for learners and teachers. Statistic shows that the risks of child marriage and early pregnancy is lower if girls stay in Schools. Children are safe in schools and can be able to build resilience and positive coping mechanisms to face shocks. Through this project protection mainstreaming will be key including prioritizing Prevention of sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) during the implementation. For Accountability to affected population (AAP), a committee will be established in each county where all the complaint from the beneficiaries will be channels through them to the partner.
To strengthen Synergies with other sector, SAADO will work closely with other partners implementing project in different sectors including Nutrition, WASH, Health, GBV & Protection and FSL. Generally, through this project a strong MEAL system will be put in place to monitor report, Community feedback, document, and share evident lessons. The project is also designed to ensure maximum impact is achieved through the proposed interventions besides ensuring accountability to the affected population. The estimated cost for this education in emergency project will be 619,447 USD. The main overall objective of these project is to increase access to, and quality for education in emergency (EIEs), targeting vulnerable pupil (boys and Girls) in the targeted areas that are hard-to-reach

1. Needs

More than a decade after independence, South Sudan remains in a protracted humanitarian crisis, impacted by conflict, Influx from Sudan, economic stagnation, and instability as well as being highly prone and vulnerable to climate-related shocks (Flooding and drought). One of the outcomes of this protracted crisis is the lack of educational opportunities for children and adolescentsAccording to UNICEF report more than 2.8 million children, or over 70 per cent, are out of school in South Sudan, putting at risk their futures and the future of the country. Some of the out-of-school children are living in pastoral communities especially is SAADO targeted counties, moving with their cattle and are not able to attend regular classes. The largest group of out-of-school children in South Sudan are girls. Poverty, child marriage and cultural and religious views all hinder girls' education. In South Sudan, yearly flooding has caused many children to drop out of school as their schools are submerged in the water. Families have been forced to relocate to higher land to rebuild their lives. According to South Sudan education cluster in the year 2022, it reported 895 schools (partially or completely) were destroyed by floods, directing impacting 215,000 children. As of 31 October, according to UNOCHA humanitarian snapshot, multi-agency assessments identified about 1.4 million flood-affected people across the country. Over 377,000 people have been displaced by floods, seeking shelter on higher ground. The UNOCHA report state that Aweil North 31k people are affected by flooding, 91k in Aweil East, 40k in Pigi and 154k in Fangak, all being displace to higher ground.The flooding has led to closer of schools and in area that the population have move, most have taken refuge in schools thus limiting learning. As of 31 October, 855,000 people have arrived from Sudan since 16 April 2023. Of those, an estimated 32,197 arrivals were registered in October alone – an average of 1,038 per day according to UNOCHA report. The influx due to Sudan Crisis is putting more pressure to the limited existing schools in South Sudan. Furthermore, barriers are even extreme for the marginalized groups in the country. For girls, forced marriages and other negative traditional practices have had a significant impact on enrolment, retention, and transition to secondary school for the girls. Statistic shows that the risks of child marriage and early pregnancy is lower if girls stay in Schools. Schools in the targeted areas far from certain villages thus children have to trek for long distance through bushes which exposes girls to Sexual exploitation and gender base. Children with disabilities face more worse challenges in relation to access to education services. Stigma from the community, lack of accessible learning materials, school infrastructure that are not responsive to their needs and lack of trained teachers are just among some of the barriers. At the school level, the barriers include lack of clean water and WASH facilities, inadequate learning spaces, low teacher /student ratio, and lack of trained teachers. Barriers are amplified for the Poorest adolescent girls and teenage mothers who lack money to buy dignity kits. Lack of menstrual health and hygiene and washrooms for girls in some schools has resulted in irregular attendance or even drop out of girls from schools. Children in IDP camps are finding it very difficult to gain access to education services due to several social-economic reasons. In addition, the fees associated with education is the greatest barrier to access to education. Most of children are likely to miss out on education due to these challenges. Accountability to affected population is low in the targeted affected counties due to limited involvement of communities to day-to-day activities in schools.

2. Project activities

This project is meant to improve access to, and quality of education in emergency to 13,747 beneficiaries (7006B, 6505G, 141M & 95W) in the targeted counties of Aweil North, Pigi, Fangak, Gogrial East and Aweil East through the following plan activities • Conduct back to school, community mobilizations and sensitizations campaigns to encourage enrolment of children and adolescents (3-17yrs) to PPE, Primary and Secondary classes. • Support targeted school and pupils' enrolment in Formal (ECD, Primary and Secondary and Non formal accelerated learning Programme (ALP) reaching 13,511 pupils (7004B & 6505G)• Establishment/rehabilitation of 10 temporary learning spaces and classrooms in targeted counties in line with INEE-Minimum standards for education in emergencies. • Construction of 15blocks of gender sensitive WASH facilities in line with MoGEI/education cluster standard • Training of the 179 (71F: 107M) teachers on EiE- education in emergency and Pedagogical skills including PSEA • Procurement and distribution of 58 ECD Kits to the targeted schools • Procure and distribute 120 learning and recreational material for children and youth (6-17 years) • Establishment and orientation of PTAs in the targeted schools to ensure retention and sustainability • Training of School 57 Management committee (SMC), PTA and education authorities on school Management, Governance, climate change, Disaster Risks Reduction (DRR) and PSEA • Offering deworming health services in 17 selected learning centres targeting 13,511 pupils (7004B & 6505G)) in Aweil North, Pigi, Fangak, Gogrial East and Aweil East Counties.• Provision of Sector Cash assistance to the most vulnerable Pupils especially poorest adolescent girls to meet their immediate need targeting 275
