Project Summary

Access to clean water and improved sanitation is a daily challenge. This problem is particularly acute in northern Nigeria, where only 30% of the population has access to safe drinking water and adequate sanitation. In Borno State, several shelters for IDPs and key facilities, including WASH infrastructure, have been destroyed or damaged by severe storms in recent weeks. The decreasing rainfall has led to problems with water supply, especially in Borno, and thus to difficulties with food security, but also to violent conflicts between smallholder farmers. 37% of IDPs have less than 15 litres of water per day available. Only 23% of water needs and 9% of sanitation needs met by 87300 IDPs in host communities in Maiduguri. Access to drinking water is generally through hand pumps or solar/electric powered pumps rather than piped connections. According to an assessment of WASH infrastructure by the WASH Severity Classification (WSC) , conducted in three Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Borno State in 2021, 43% of water points were functional, 21% partially functional and 35% non-functional. In the previous project, Borno had the least amount of WASH infrastructure built due to the difficult security situation. The WASH analysis for northeast Nigeria classifies 45 of the 60 LGAs assessed as Phase 4 (critical), where public health is at high risk, particularly due to a cholera outbreak that has been ongoing since mid-2021. This is due to inadequate access to sanitation, water quality issues and lack of access to basic hygiene. WASH remains a critical need in the target countries, especially as many women and girls do not have access to menstrual hygiene kits. Overcrowding in host communities and camps is a critical challenge, as access to water points, dilapidated sanitation facilities and indiscriminate waste disposal contribute to health risks. The lack of behaviour change can be linked to the lack of hygiene awareness. The project aims to promote hygiene education and social responsibility awareness among host communities, returnees and IDPs through the provision or creation of additional water sources and the implementation of awareness-raising activities.
The project targets at least 59,084 direct beneficiaries (50% women, 50% men, 16% persons with disabilities) from host communities or living in communities affected by the protracted armed conflict. Target groups include people with disabilities

1 - Needs

The number of people with disabilities is high at 16% in the target region. People with disabilities are among the marginalised groups most vulnerable in conflict and disaster-affected areas and most affected by the negative impacts of climate change. IDPs with disabilities often face barriers to accessing housing, WASH, health care, education and humanitarian assistance. Disabilities are often not perceived as a health problem. As a result, no action is taken to include people with disabilities in the health sector and this is often overlooked in national disability strategies and action plans to implement and monitor the CRPD. People with disabilities face stigma and discrimination and are more likely to slip into poverty due to lack of employment opportunities (see chapter 1.1). The needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities are high: 5.1 million people need food security, 2.9 million people need WASH services, 4.1 million people need protection and 2.1 million people are in need of early recovery and livelihoods (HNO 2022). According to the latest Displacement Report , which documents the period February to March 2022, 2,197,824 individual IDPs and 452,219 IDP households have fled. At the same time, there are 1,983,130 returnees and 323,277 returning households. The ongoing crisis situation is taking a heavy toll on the mental health of those affected. 75% reported functional impairment related to mental health symptoms. Women, pregnant and lactating women, and children under 5 years of age are most affected by malnutrition and thus constitute another target group. According to UNICEF, only 17% of infants can be breastfed by their mothers in the first 6 months. 18% of children aged between 6 and 23 months receive the minimum acceptable nutrition. Women of childbearing age (between 15 and 49 years) suffer disproportionately from health problems, especially related to childbirth. The maternal mortality rate is 576 per 100,000 live births, the fourth highest in the world. Infant mortality is 69 per 1,000 live births. 262,000 newborns die at birth each year - the second highest number in the world. More than half of under-five deaths (64%) are due to malaria, pneumonia or diarrhoeal diseases.

2 - Activities

The selection of the target region is justified by the tense humanitarian situation caused by the violent conflicts and natural disasters. Due to the ongoing conflict and constraints in basic services, including agricultural, financial and socio-economic resources and capacities, the quality of life of the people living there is affected, livelihoods are at risk and their resilience is limited. Target communities in BAY states are mostly host communities with and humanitarian crisis-affected communities with IDPs, but also returnees. IDPs have been sent back to their communities of origin by the government. In addition, Nigerian refugees who had fled to neighbouring countries such as Cameroon have returned and have no livelihood security. Based on the pre-selection conducted by GREENCODE and its other partners and building on the experience of the previous project, the project will be implemented in 10 communities in Borno states. Other criteria considered are the number of displaced families and returnees, vulnerability, poverty levels, available resources, security situation, number of people with disabilities, number of female-headed households, WASH infrastructure and economic engagement of community members. An estimated 20,000 indirect community members, including from neighbouring communities, will benefit from the services provided under the project Borno state.
