Project Summary

Operating in Libya remains challenging, with numerous security, logistical and access constraints. Strong and effective sector coordination mechanism is required to ensure humanitarian action is timely, effective & meets the needs of vulnerable people. Collaboration with national/International Humanitarian actors and Human Rights organizations are critical to ensure that assistance is provided safely and respects people’s dignity.
Functional sectors and inter sector coordination group support humanitarian actors with coordination, information management, needs assessments, advocacy for access, safety and protection. It will benefit the entire humanitarian community to effectively and timely reach the most vulnerable people in Libya, as well as to sustain and extend their members’ operational reach and impact.
The annual review of existing common coordination mechanisms and structures, includes the review of the sectors and the Inter-Sector Coordination Group, ensuring that the system is fit per purpose and able to deliver as expected.
Information management products, Periodic Monitoring Reports, dashboards and snapshots are key elements well functioning sectors and ISCGs should deliver as they constitute the primary source of information for planning and decision-making.

1 - Needs

The sector approach aims to address gaps and to increase the effectiveness of humanitarian response by building effective and efficient partnerships. It ensures that international responses to humanitarian emergencies are predictable, accountable and have clear leadership role ensuring a balanced division of labour among organizations, and their roles and responsibilities in different sectors. Sector approach in Libya has become overly process-driven and in order to enable delivery, sectors need to return to their original purpose and refocus on strategic and operational assessment, gaps analysis, planning, and results. Therefore, there is a incontrovertible need to strengthen their capacity and provide enough resources allowing them to fulfill their six core functions: support service delivery, inform the HC/HCT’s strategic decision-making, plan and implement sector strategies, monitor and evaluate performance, build national capacity in preparedness and contingency planning and support a robust advocacy. INGOs co-chairing different sectors in Libya have limited financial capacity and it's often challenging to divert their already limited resources for coordination activities. To be able to provide dedicated capacity to the sector, there is need to provide the organizations with enough resources and support to avoid extra burden on the already tight INGOs structures. Information management capacity at sector level is normally provided by the UN Agency sector lead, impacting sometimes the timely delivery of products and analysis to the sector partners and the Inter-sector Coordination Group (ISCG), limiting their ability to fulfill their mandate.

2 - Activities

The proposal aims to provided adequate resources to the sectors to enable them to fulfill their core functions and their role in the ISCG. In order to achieve that, the applying organizations will ensure that sectors have dedicated coordinator (UN agency staff) and co-coordinator (INGO staff); and that there is sufficient information management support to deliver in a timely and effective manner.Specific main activities by agency include, among others:1. UNFPA: Human resources (GBV coordination including GBV co-lead and costs related to their deployment); Roll-out of GBV- Information Management System, Capacity building for local, national and international partners, recruitment of the PSEA Network coordinator for Libya.2. WFP: Recruitment of the Food Security sector and Emergency telecommunications sector coordinators.3. UNHCR: Recruitment of Shelter, Protection, Cash Coordinator and one information management officer dedicated to three sectors.4. UNICEF: Recruitment of Child Protection, WASH and Education Coordinators, and one information management officer dedicated to three sectors.5. UNMAS: recruitment of coordinator and co-coordinator.6. DRC: recruitment of Protection sector co-coordinator.Sectors will strengthen the engagement with national partners and counterparts in order to ensure full coordination and complementarity among humanitarian actors. Sectors will also strengthen the coordinated assessment component to be able to plan based on evidence and make informed decisions.
