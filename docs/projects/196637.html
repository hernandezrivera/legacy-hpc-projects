Project Summary

The project aims at effectively contributing to building the resilience of the community and enhancing equitable access of most vulnerable to essential health services through three main approaches: (1) Providing community based health services and reaching out to PoCs by community health workers of NGOs and health volunteers capacitated in the community, building the capacity and mobilizing the community in addressing and mitigating health risks through wide range of health promotion and disease prevention activities including RCCE for COVID-19 vaccine, cholera outbreak and other areas in line with national health priorities. Main groups of health volunteers and thematic groups of women volunteers capacitated in neonatal home care program, volunteers capacitated in elderly health shall be the sustainable empowerment of the community that will enhance its resilience. Community led health initiatives are the tools used to achieve community participation and responsibility in addressing health problems. (2) Contributing to the revitalization of the health system by conducting light rehabilitation of selected health infrastructure and engaging in policy dialogue with health authorities, UN agencies and other stakeholders aiming at reviewing and developing more resilient and inclusive health system.(3) Enhance preparedness to public health threats and protection risks at points of entry by establishing, furnishing and equipping medical/protection posts at seven points of entry of priority jointly with WHO, where support to international health regulations alongside with protection services targeting returnees could be provided starting the second quarter 2023 The project will also include partial support to three SARC PHC facilities in rural Damascus and Homs providing the package of essential PHC services up to mid-2023 when SARC will take over to independently continue to run the services The project will target nearly 650,000 vulnerable returnees, IDPs and affected communities in geographic areas of moderate to high needs and priority based on HNO severity scale, in urban, rural, camps and camps like settlements, with special focus on health vulnerabilities in children, women at reproductive age, older persons and persons with disability including mental disability. Community and right based approaches will be adopted in addition to networking and collaboration with health actors and interlink with WASH, nutrition, protection and shelter sectors for more efficient response to health needs.

3 - Needs

Based on the HNO, health needs continue to be one of the priorities for 15.3 million with 25% increase compared to 2022. Diverse and complex needs, that need joint intra-sectoral and intersectoral efforts to support, of 14.3 % U 5 children, 27% women at reproductive age, 4,5 % older persons with multiple health care needs including non-communicable diseases that account for 45% of the morbidity countrywide, and 5 million persons living with some levels of disabilities. As observed by UNHCR offices, reported in HeRAMS and by the sector, access to health is challenged by non-functional or partially functional health facilities at different health care levels, impacted by infrastructure damages and shortage in health professionals estimated at nearly 50% of the needs. The severely deteriorated economic situation, limited livelihood opportunities combined with poor housing conditions, all added and led to increased morbidity and affected the access of PoCs to health and medicine services. COVID-19 threat continues to exist with vaccine coverage not exceeding 15% country wide. Poor environmental health including drought, reduced coverage with potable water and poor sewage networks increased the risks of outbreaks, including AWD/cholera outbreak. 4,727,699 individuals recorded in the database of 113 CCs run by 15 NGO partners in 9 governorates, showed 9,3% with health needs (mainly chronic conditions), 6.7% with MHPSS needs, while 4,1% reported disabilities.

4 - Implementation of activities

Community health will be implemented through NGO partners. Static and mobile modality by CHWs in static health points embedded in the CCs and mobile units. 800-1370 trained community health outreach volunteers including thematic teams to cover neonatal home care and elderly health. Joint work with UNICEF, WHO and UNFPA in capacity building and referrals. Engagement and participation of the community throughout the program cycle are warranted by the selection of outreach representative volunteers, FGDs, and over 300 community led health initiatives.. Linkages with levels of care will be ensured through local mapping, coordination, and cooperation with formal and non-formal health actors to address health needs and optimize community resources. Rehabilitation of health facilities will be coordinated with the sector and conducted by partners or contractors based on MoH local standards. UNHCR contractors will establish seven posts at seven points of entry, based on joint UNHCR-WHO and MoH standards. WHO will support with furniture and equipment, while MoH and SARC shall run the service. Monitoring will be conducted weekly by partners, monthly by UNHCR the field and third party when need be. UNHCR project reporting and management database “PRMD” will be used by partners to report to the sector lead. Specially designed monitoring checklists for PHC and CBH services will be used and will feed the online HIS on the progress and quality of services.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Gender-based violence: Mainstreaming and reporting on GBV prevention and response, using sector adopted templates and customized checklists to examine the physical environment, processes, policies, partners capacity and SOPs in place covering prevention methods and effective response including safety and confidentiality of survivors, and coordination with GBV actors to ensure referral pathways are in place are some of the mitigating measures in place. Risk 2: Lack of community engagement and equitable access to health: The community-based approach is adopted and integrated into the project design and implementation. Outreach volunteers, community outreach, and the establishment of community feedback mechanisms are mitigating measures in place.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Health services are heavily dependent on human resources and interaction, hence ,PSEA risk should always be considered. Mitigation measures include: (a) a zero-tolerance policy adopted by partners. (b) Assigning a PSEA focal point to ensure mainstreaming and development/update of dedicated SOPs. (c) Regular training of staff of partners, volunteers, and contractors. (d) Code of conduct with PSEA commitments clearly explained must be signed by each worker/contractor. (e) Free services and information regarding PSEA and safe reporting mechanism are posted in health points and PHCs.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational risks include high staff turnover especially in rural/remote areas, fuel and electricity shortages that can hinder the provision of services, access, and outreach services. depreciation of the currency and increase costs of services, scarcity of medicines in the local market, and access to service delivery in remote areas for monitoring and capacity building. The high turnover is mitigated by having backup staff and recruitment policies that give priority to residents in remote areas who meet qualifications requirements, in addition to having a component of resident outreach volunteers and allocating additional budget for transportation. Adequate coordination with health actors is essential to ensure maximum utilization of resources. Fraud and PSEA are major reputational risks, especially when activities are taking place in remote-rural areas difficult to reach and monitor by UNHCR and its partners. Regular monitoring, directly, through partners, and through third-party monitoring contractors are measures that can be adopted alongside measures described under the PSEA section.
