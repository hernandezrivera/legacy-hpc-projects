Project Summary

The project aims to provide live saving humanitarian food assistant integrated with livelihoods support and resilient intervention activities to prevent the deterioration of livelihoods assets and promote resilient and sustainable livelihoods of crisis affected population to cope with human and climate induced shocks in South Sudan. CARE intends to particularly target the most vulnerable households and places women and girls in the center and front of its program design to enhance the ability of the project to deliver tangible results. CARE program strategy focuses on three strategic directions including 1-increased women and youth economic empowerment across value chains; 2 increased access and consumption of diverse and nutritious foods to save lives of the most vulnerable households and 3 – increased resilience of households to manmade and climate induced shocks in both fragile and stable states of South Sudan. The program will direct reach out 37,710 households reaching out a total of 226,260 beneficiaries (70% women and girls) including female headed households, people with special need and disabilities, households with SAM/MAM in acute food insecurity regions, the elderly and mother support groups in the nutrition facilities supported by the CARE Health and Nutrition programs. CARE will integrate peace building and conflict mitigation measures across all the program thematic areas. CARE will deliver its agriculture and livelihoods programs in the more stable environment including the Greater Equatoria and Western Bahr el Ghazal states and parts of Jonglei (Bor South) through the Farmer Field and Business School (FFBS) and the Village Saving and Loan Association (VSLA) approach to enhance resilient and sustainable livelihoods of people affected by climate and manmade shocks. While in the Greater Upper Nile Regions, CARE will provide lifesaving support through cash and voucher assistant (CVA) integrated with early recovery programming including Village Saving and Loan Associations in Emergencies (VSLAiE). CARE VSLAiE model integrate with CVA to enable vulnerable crisis affected communities particularly women and girls build their resilience to shocks in fragile context (Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs) in Emergencies - CARE). CARE partners with Women Led /Right Based Organizations (WLO) to strengthen its localization agenda to empower national and civil society organizations to sustainable deliver sustainable benefits to the communities affected by conflict and violence as well as natural calamities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is experiencing one of the worst hunger crises globally, with 8.3 million people (over 60% of the population) in crisis or worse levels of acute food insecurity, an increase of 600,000 people compared to the 2021 figures. The hunger crisis in South Sudan is categorized as alarming . Food insecurity is among the greatest humanitarian crises in South Sudan. 62.7% of the population (7.76 million people) facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with 2.22 million people are in Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity levels. Whereas 61,000 people are classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Akobo of Jonglei State; Pibor County in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) according to the recent IPC analysis . The worst affected State is Jonglei where 68% of the population is in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, followed by Unity (66.7%) and Upper Nile (58.5%) as well as Warrap (57.1%), and Lakes (56.9%). The food insecurity levels are expected to remain elevated or worse during the coming year 2023. During the lean periods from April to July 2023, 7.6 million people (62.7% of the population) are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity including Akobo, Canal/Pigi and Fangak counties of Jonglei State; and Leer and Mayendit counties of Unity State. During the same period, an estimated 2.90 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity.Chronic acute food insecurity in South Sudan are driven by multiple shocks including, conflict and insecurity, climatic shocks (floods, drought and dry spells), climate- and conflict-induced population displacements, persistent low agricultural production levels, the prevailing macro-economic crisis caused by the depreciation of the local currency and the global impacts of the conflict in Ukraine, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion that continue to erode households’ coping capacities, and the loss of livelihoods are the key drivers of chronic food insecurity in South Sudan . WFP has drastically reduced its food rations by 3 months (9/12) and reduced by half per households and food distribution in other locations have been suspended until further notice due to funding shortfalls in South Sudan. There is marked decline and cereal deficit of 541,000 metric tons in 2021 largely because of the flooding that severely affected the eastern half of the country – particularly in Jonglei, Warrap, Lakes, Unity and Upper Nile states. This likelihood of further deterioration of food production that will be determined by the Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) analysis scheduled for December 2022 is high.During January to December 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition. Approximately 345,893 cases will need SAM treatment while another 1,057,703 will need MAM treatment and 737,812 pregnant and lactating women are acutely malnourished. Acute malnutrition burden is anticipated to be higher in the four states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap with about 61% of the total burden. Inadequate feeding practices of infant and young children is one of the major contributing factors for acute malnutrition in the worst affected states.As of October 2022, more than 900,000 people were reportedly displaced by floods in 29 counties across South Sudan and in the southern part of the Abyei Administrative Area. The most affected people in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, Unity, and Western Equatoria states, are the worst affected. Climate change is increasing eroding the natural resource base and aggravating poverty, escalating food and nutrition insecurity and constantly constraining development across fragile contexts in South Sudan. 3 - Project indicators

CARE has a robust monitoring and evaluation system that will be used to track project activities and measure performance. During project inception, the Country MEAL Manager and the M&E Officer assigned to the project will develop a monitoring framework. The monitoring framework will have detailed indicators to be tracked, detailed implementation plan, frequency of data collection, staff/community groups involved, how and where the data will be collected from. Description of indicators will be made user-friendly to ensure that the project beneficiaries, especially women, are able to participate effectively in both the targeting and monitoring process. CARE will adopt WFP and FAO mandated tools such as SCOPE registration model, distribution forms to register selected project beneficiaries and beneficiaries who received food assistance and the livelihood kits. Post distribution monitoring questionnaire will be used to interview project beneficiaries who received livelihood kits in order to gather information on satisfaction of beneficiaries in terms of timeliness of input distribution, relevance of the inputs distributed, quality and quantity distributed, overall organization and modalities of distribution including security and protection issues in collaboration with FSL cluster. The PDM will be conducted within a period of 2 weeks after delivery of assistance to follow up on beneficiary satisfaction with the quality of assistance provided, security and distance distribution sites, and time for distribution and collection particularly to women and child headed households and people with disabilities. Post-planting surveys will be conducted to get more information on field preparation, planting, germination rate, crop vigor, weeding and other early-stage agronomic practices, including challenges faced in the process. Data from the post-planting surveys are used to estimate percentage of farmers who prepare the land and planted on time, total acreage planted and percentage of farmers who weeded on time. The post-harvest surveys will be used to gather information on crop yields, challenges faced by farmers during the season and post-harvest handling and management. Findings from the surveys will be used to sensitize farmers on improved agronomic practices like good land preparation, proper weeding, integrated pest management and soil fertility maintenance practices, among others to increase production and minimize post-harvest losses. Findings from the assessments will be shared and presented in the FSL cluster meetings and other stakeholders. The planned monitoring and evaluation activities will be coordinated with FSL cluster, specific donors and other stakeholders. CARE will embed gender sensitive monitoring in all the different stages of intervention through conducting gender analysis, that aims to identify and analyze gender relations, identify gender specific needs and suggest specific measures to strengthen women rights in relation to access and control over resources during the different stages of intervention. CARE will continue to use the existing feedback and complaint mechanisms based on community consultations, to avoid marginalizing the vulnerable community members, more than one channel will be available, focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. Analysis and results of the feedback and complaints provided will be shared monthly and this will contribute to learning and provide information for evidence-based decision making in order to improve to program performance

This is mainly based on trend analysis of the market prices and availability of essential goods and services, based on seasonal changes.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and/or voucher assistance is the most flexible systems of assistance in South Sudan provided market functionality is ensured. Affected communities particularly women and people with disabilities find it more convenient and easier to assess assistance wherever they need because it often reduces the risks to travel to collect assistance from remote distribution sites that require movement with associated delays food collection time during the distribution processes. More importantly, it enables beneficiaries to purchase the most preferred food items and types that are not limited to the food baskets choices provided. Cash is more efficient in terms of value for money compared food delivery that require high levels logistics arrangement. And cash provides timely and rapidly response the people especially during the onset of the disaster. Current joint market assessments in the areas where CVA are being proposed indicates that the markets are either functional or have high likelihood of recovering within the project period. This applies for both basic needs and livelihood inputs. CARE is one of the key players and active participants in the routine joint assessment organized by REACH and WFP including other actors in South Sudan. The joint market information always indicate that project inputs are available and local procurement are possible and financial service providers are as well present in most parts of the locations proposed.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will integrate some climate resilient practices in the food production system and female male participants of different age groups including people with and without disabilities shall be trained on natural resources management (NRM) for sustainable land and water resources management. Training on Agroforestry system and practices and promotion of and production of fuel efficient stoves shall be provided to all participants including the internally displaced persons, returnees and refugees who are the main consumers of natural resources products. More than 90 percent of the population in South Sudan primarily depend on forests for fuel and food products to sustain and enhance their livelihoods. Similarly wetlands encroachment for agricultural expansion and animal trampling are rampant and main sources of land and water resources degradation in South Sudan. Capacity building components around strategic FSL cluster objective 3 shall be centered around building local capacity on wetland restoration and management, alternative livelihoods, climate- resilient agricultural best practices, market system development, and natural resource and disaster risk reduction strategies, postharvest handling techniques and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies (e.g. hermetic storage bags), diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes & micro finance and use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will ensure to create awareness on timely manner in accessing services especially GBV services with clear messages on benefits of timely access to service and risk of delay in accessing services especially those lifesaving services on a timely manner. CARE integrates its programming with GBV activities included in FSL farmer field and business school (FBBS) curriculum to address, offer and manage some of the GBV services and referral system.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Targeted vulnerable populations (IPC phases 3, 4 and 5) will be identified, mobilized, assessed and verified based on a widely consulted agreed upon selection criteria for support through appropriate cash / food vouchers. People will also be supported to promote emergency food production through distribution of emergency livelihood kits including crops, livestock and fishing gears. This intervention shall also be integrated with village saving and loan association approaches to promote resilient pathways to crisis affected populations.
