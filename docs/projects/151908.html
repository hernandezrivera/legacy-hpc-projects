Project Summary

As part of UNHCR 2019 Child Protection (CP) interventions and aligned with the strategic CP objectives for the HRP 2019 and the centrality of protection, the current project seeks to improve the protection environment for displaced and returnee boys, girls and adolescents affected by the crisis in 3 states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe).
Through its protection monitoring network and vulnerability assessment approach, UNHCR will invest in early identification and documentation of child protection problems (including the grave violations of children’s rights) as well as girls, boys and adolescents at risk (UASC, “children in detention center”, CAAFAG, out of school, survivors of Sexual and Gender Based Violence, Victims of violence, abuse and exploitation). UNHCR will also maintain a system of referral for appropriate services.
An important component of the project is the “Prevention rooted in the community”, which devolves the primary responsibility for protection to the community itself. The project will support community organization for the prevention of violence, abuse and exploitation through beneficiaries’ ownership of child protection issues and their effective participation (including boys, girls and adolescents) in promoting a sustained safe environment for children. Community-based Child Protection structures will be established and linked with the existing Protection Action Groups (PAGs).
Integrated responses will be provided through a “multifunctional child friendly centre” as a front line mechanism (Case management; Legal support services and Documentation for boys, girls and adolescents victims of violence, abuse and exploitation; Recreational activities; Psychosocial support including life skill acquisition, material support).

Needs

The north east crisis is a protection crisis that negatively impacted the environment and wellbeing of boys, girls and adolescents IDPs and returnees in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. Some 2,6 million girls, boys and adolescents (HRP Nigeria 2019) remain in need of protection, mental health and psychosocial support despite multiple advocacy and interventions carried out by child protection actors to strengthen child protection and ensure a favourable protection environment. Due to the extreme vulnerabilities of conflict-affected children and recognizing the outstanding gaps in the provision of child protection services particularly in hard-to-reach areas in the North East, UNHCR is planning to expand its operations for 2019-2021 to the delivery of comprehensive child protection operations. The crisis has affected children in a disproportionate manner. Such negative effects include abduction, child abuse and exploitation, Sexual and Gender based Violence, association of children with armed groups including the Civilian Joint Task force, use of children for “suicide bombing”, killing, arbitrary detention, depriving children of the right to education resulting in a high number of out of school children, etc. It is to be noted that the overall context of Child protection in North East which was already fragile due to cultural constraints, lack of adequate access to basic and essential services as well as insufficient socio-economic opportunities. This situation has been exacerbated during the current security crisis, leading to the community adopting negative coping mechanisms, majorly affecting boys, girls, adolescents and their caregivers. Protection monitoring has highlighted severally the negative social effects of the crisis on boys and girls (unaccompanied and separated children including orphans, child marriage, children headed households, etc.). There are currently at least 39,500 children in need of social care and protection (Child protection Humanitarian Response Plan 2019 Guidance). The UNHCR 2018 Sexual and Gender based Violence half-year report showed that 67% of the survivors were under the age of 18 and almost 97,5% were girls. Regarding their safety, children reported that they don’t feel safe in 99% of the sites (DTM Round 24 report, August 2018).

Activities

1. IDENTIFICATION AND DOCUMENTATION• Identify and document children at risk including unaccompanied and separated children (IDPs and Returnees), boys, girls and adolescents in detention centre as well as those in rehabilitation centre • Identify, document and refer girls and boys survivors of Sexual Gender based Violence • Identify adolescent in need of life skills acquisition and livelihood support through vulnerability assessments • Monitor the welfare of boys and girls placed in alternative care arrangements• Build the capacity of CP actors to identify and document children at risk• Build the capacity of Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS) to identify, register and refer boys, girls and adolescents unaccompanied or separated at the border who return spontaneously. • Support the identification and verification of children used or associated with armed groups • Identify and Document grave violations against children’s rights 2. PREVENTION ROOTED IN THE COMMUNITY• Establish community-based child protection to identify, prevent and refer cases of child protection (child protection volunteers, community based child protection networks). Establish groups of peers based on age and gender to build confidence and give adequate opportunities to boys, girls and adolescents to participate in identification, prevention and referral of child protection cases. • Community-based child protection joint actions with community based protection structures (Protection Action Groups) to promote sustained reintegration in their community of boys, girls and adolescents used by or associated with armed groups • Community awareness on child protection issues and different ways to prevent violation of the rights of the child• Establish age and gender sensitive CFS and provision of recreational activities for both male and female children and adolescents • Build capacity and understanding of security actors (military, police, CJTF, CDFC, etc.) on child protection issues as well as prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. Build capacity of groups of peers, community based child protection structures on mitigating child protection risks/issues including sexual and gender based violence • Advocate with National Human Right Commission (NHRC) for access to, and monitoring in detention centre of cases involving boys, girls and adolescents associated with non-state armed groups• Support life skill acquisition and livelihood for vulnerable male and female adolescents in order to eliminate or reduce risk of survival sex and other negative coping mechanisms, as well as sexual exploitation 3. INTEGRATED RESPONSE THROUGH A MULTIFUNCTIONAL CHILD FRIENDLY CENTER (MCFC) • Establish innovative multifunctional child friendly centre (MCFC) to support boys, girls and adolescents affected by the crisis including survivors of Sexual and Gender Based Violence • Provision of child sensitive legal support through referral and facilitate documentation (birth registration), psychosocial support and case management, material support for girls and female adolescent of reproductive age (dignity kits) • Provision of PSS and child protection material support (NFI including dignity kits) to boys and girls released from administrative custody• Build capacity of PSS workers, legal counsellors, case management workers on managing and responding in an integrated manner to child protection issues • Address legal needs of boys, girls and adolescents associated with non-state armed groups in detention centres • Support life skill acquisition, livelihood and reintegration for boys, girls and adolescent survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation• Support livelihood programmes for caregivers to sustain the reintegration of their children
