Project Summary

The Tdh Health Project aims to enhance access to essential health services for women and children under five in Teknaf Camps 26 and 27, as well as in surrounding host communities, through integrated clinic- and community-based solutions. Leveraging existing health programs, the project will provide continuous health services, preventive measures, and responsive strategies, especially during health outbreaks or crises.
To meet these objectives, Tdh will continue operating two Primary Health Centers (PHCs) in Camps 26 and 27, serving approximately 239,075 patients annually. Following the Health Sector Joint Response Plan (JRP), an Electronic Medical Record (EMR) system established during the 2020-2024 JRP period will be further developed at both PHCs this year to enhance data capture, validation, analysis, reporting, and informed decision-making. Each PHC will offer a full package of essential health services, including mental health support, basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), non-communicable disease (NCD) management, and nutrition services. Additionally, Tdh has introduced specialized ENT, dermatology, ophthalmology, and dental units near each PHC, staffed by eight specialist doctors, to serve both Rohingya and host communities.
In alignment with health sector guidelines, a digitalized NCD protocol has been implemented at the PHCs to support effective NCD service delivery. Pharmacy upgrades will also be made to improve medicine storage, dispensing, and management, enhancing patient care, diagnosis accuracy, and stock control. The multi-skilled community outreach team will continue integrated outreach activities, updating community-level service mapping, refining referral pathways, and conducting health, child protection, and hygiene awareness sessions in both camps and host communities. This integrated approach has shown improved efficiency and reduced outreach costs. Tdh will also deliver high-quality antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC), ultrasound services, Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH), and 24/7 BEmONC and family planning services at both PHCs. Key protocols, such as Helping Babies Breathe and Helping Mothers Survive, will guide these services. Training will be provided to physicians, nurses, and midwives on newborn treatment protocols, while outreach sessions by IYCF Counsellors will promote exclusive breastfeeding for infants under six months and appropriate supplementary feeding thereafter.
Through nutrition screening, treatment, and referral for severe and moderate acute malnutrition, the outreach team will ensure complete follow-up of malnourished children in the project area, with ongoing nutrition awareness campaigns to promote healthy practices.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Access to quality healthcare services in refugee camps, particularly in Teknaf Camps 26 and 27 and the surrounding host communities, remains an ongoing challenge due to high patient volumes and limited resources. The 2024 Joint Response Plan (JRP) identified substantial gaps in maternal and reproductive health, including family planning, neonatal care, child health services, and treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs) (JRP, 2024). Overcrowded healthcare facilities, high costs of medication, and long distances to healthcare centers are significant barriers to healthcare access, creating a complex landscape for refugees seeking medical attention.According to the Refugee Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-6), approximately 55% of households in both Rohingya and Bangladeshi communities reported obstacles in accessing healthcare, marking a 7% increase since 2021 (REVA-6, 2023). Communicable disease risks are further exacerbated by the overcrowded and unsanitary living conditions in the camps. Persistent gaps in services such as NCD treatment, laboratory diagnostics, and mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) add to the challenges faced by these communities. Limited referral pathways to higher-level care facilities and gender-based disparities in access further impede equitable healthcare access for all.Malnutrition also remains a significant concern. Only 49% of Rohingya children aged 6–59 months and 38% of children in host communities are currently enrolled in nutrition programs, according to the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG, 2023). The 2023 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) identified additional barriers to healthcare, with households reporting long waiting times and overcrowded facilities (45%), unavailable specific treatments or services (35%), and instances of incorrect medication (27%) as critical issues (ISCG, 2023). These findings underscore the urgent need for improved healthcare delivery systems to ensure that refugee and host community populations receive timely and effective medical care.To address these needs, this health project will implement gender-responsive and inclusive healthcare solutions specifically tailored to meet the varied needs of different demographic groups within Teknaf Camps 26 and 27 and the surrounding host communities. Recognizing the distinct healthcare needs of various groups, especially through a gendered lens, will be central to the effective delivery of healthcare services. Ensuring equitable access to healthcare, with a focus on gender-sensitive approaches, will enhance the health outcomes and overall resilience of both the Rohingya and host communities

Embed mental health and psychosocial support within primary care, with safe referral options for cases requiring specialized assistance. This approach fosters equitable, respectful healthcare access, enhances patient resilience, and strengthens community trust.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The Kalyana Health Project adopts an inclusive, age- and disability-responsive approach to ensure equitable healthcare access in Teknaf Camps 26 and 27 and surrounding communities. Recognizing that emergency health responses often overlook diverse age groups and individuals with disabilities, the project implements targeted interventions to address these gaps.Healthcare services are tailored for specific age demographics. For children under five, the project offers Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), routine immunizations, nutrition screenings, and caregiver education on nutrition and preventive care. Adolescents receive Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) education and mental health resources, while elderly individuals benefit from chronic disease management and mobility support to access primary care.Accessibility for individuals with disabilities is a priority. Each Primary Health Center (PHC) will have disability-friendly features, including wheelchair and accessible waiting areas. Outreach teams will identify individuals with disabilities to provide necessary referral support. Health education sessions will focus on their specific needs, promoting their rights to healthcare and fostering inclusivity. Training for health staff will emphasize disability-inclusive practices to enhance care quality.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure that beneficiaries are included in the program's design and implementation process, the ISAC AAP framework identifies three key steps: (I) Taking account: the project team will consult with beneficiaries at the beginning of the project to update the baseline data and relevant information related to the project needs to ensure their involvement in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: Tdh will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions. (3) Being held to account: Tdh will conduct assessment at key moments of the project (start, annually and end-line evaluation) to allow the communities to provide their opinion related to the project performances and for further improvement. Tdh will conduct regular community meetings and interactive sessions, including consultation sessions, short interviews, and exit interviews, to update community members on how their feedback has influenced program decisions and actions. During these sessions, TdH will provide detailed information about previous feedback and the resulting changes implemented. These gatherings will foster two-way communication, allowing community members to ask questions and offer additional input. To ensure continuous improvement of feedback mechanisms, their effectiveness will be evaluated every six months, and new channels will be explored as necessary. Periodic feedback reports, summarizing the feedback received, the actions taken, and the outcomes achieved, will be shared with the community through these meetings. This approach will promote transparency, accountability, and active community engagement in the program.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The Project prioritizes the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) within its healthcare delivery strategy, especially in refugee camps and host communities where vulnerabilities are heightened, particularly among women and children. To address these risks, the project will provide comprehensive PSEA training for all healthcare staff and establish clear, confidential reporting mechanisms for community members to report concerns without fear of retaliation. The project will integrate PSEA considerations into health operations by ensuring female patients have access to female healthcare providers for sensitive services. Outreach will focus on the importance of consent and bodily autonomy, fostering a culture of accountability. Gender-friendly services, including gender-segregated washrooms and triage areas, will be implemented at all facilities. In response to any PSEA concerns, actions will be taken in line with the organization's PSEA policy, coordinating with relevant agencies. Terre des hommes (Tdh) will ensure that all staff, volunteers, and cleaners receive PSEA training and that a robust reporting system is in place for SEA cases. Tdh maintains a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, safeguarding survivor rights and enhancing collaboration through active participation in the PSEA network.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project incorporates climate action and environmental considerations into its healthcare delivery model by integrating disaster risk reduction (DRR) and climate change adaptation strategies. Recognizing the vulnerability of both the Rohingya population and host communities to climate change and natural disasters, it prioritizes developing resilient health infrastructure and enhancing the capacity of Primary Health Centers (PHCs) to respond to climate-related health crises like floods and cyclones. The project will ensure 24/7 healthcare services at established facilities, maintaining access during natural and human-made emergencies. In response to health outbreaks, it will provide rapid support to minimize risks, including ongoing facility maintenance and routine disinfection for public health protection. Additionally, the project will raise awareness about disaster preparedness and climate change adaptation through educational initiatives, empowering residents to understand risks and take proactive measures to protect their health and the environment. This comprehensive approach aims to enhance the overall well-being and resilience of the communities served.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The Project incorporates Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures to ensure the continuity of health services for the Rohingya population and host communities during emergency period. It establishes Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for swift health service delivery during emergencies, facilitating rapid mobilization of health teams. A contingency Continuity Plan is in place to maintain operations at the two Primary Health Centers (PHCs) during disruptions, with contingency measures like alternative staffing and resource allocation. Contingency funds are designated for immediate emergency health interventions, allowing for quick access to necessary resources. Each PHC will have two emergency response teams, each consisting of seven trained members prepared to act during crises such as cyclones or floods. Team members will have specific roles, including logistics support, patient triage, and maintaining facility safety. Regular drills and training will ensure the teams remain prepared to effectively respond to emergencies, ensuring uninterrupted healthcare services for the community.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project addresses the needs of youth in the Rohingya and host communities by integrating targeted health services and educational initiatives that focus on their unique challenges, including reproductive and mental health concerns. It will implement comprehensive health programs to empower adolescents with the knowledge and skills needed for informed decision-making, reducing vulnerabilities related to early pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections. Additionally, the project will enhance access to mental health and psychosocial support services, providing safe spaces for discussion and promoting resilience among young people. Engaging youth as peer educators will foster community involvement in health awareness campaigns on hygiene, nutrition, and healthy practices, while implementing gender-responsive, rights-based health services tailored to adolescents, youth, and the elderly in Camps 26 and 27 and surrounding areas. Community engagement will include youth participation in sensitization efforts related to hygiene, disaster risk reduction, and health issues such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD) and dengue, covering essential topics like waste management, sanitation, handwashing, and menstrual hygiene management.
