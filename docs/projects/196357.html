Project Summary

In 2023, UNRWA will continue to deliver basic, quality and inclusive education to 49,500 Palestine refugees through 102 schools managed by the Agency across Syria, of which 39 are running a double-shift in buildings on loan from the Ministry of Education (MoE) to compensate for UNRWA schools that have been destroyed or severely damaged by the conflict.
UNRWA, through its Education in Emergencies (EiE) programme helps to ensure that Palestine refugee children can continue to access education, even in times of emergencies, and facilitate linkages between the school and the community through PTA platforms. The UNRWA approach to EiE involves "doing things differently" (delivering education in alternative ways), "doing more of other things" (more PSS to children) and "doing things that had not been the focus before" (safety and security training for students, staff and parents).
In 2023, UNRWA will provide back-to-school kits (including school bags) at the beginning of the next school year (2023/24) to all 49,500 students; stationery will be procured and provided to students at the beginning of the 2022/23 second semester in January 2023, and also at the beginning of the next academic year (2023/24) to alleviate some of the economic burden faced by parents. UNRWA, will also support at least 880 students returning to damaged camps such as Yarmouk and Ein el Tal by providing transport (contracted buses) to take them to schools in nearby areas. In 2023, this service will also be extended to students in Senaya. UNRWA will focus on children from grade 1-9.
The Agency will continue to provide PSS to address anxiety, stress and trauma caused by years of conflict and deteriorating socioeconomic conditions. Psychosocial counsellors will be deployed in all Agency schools to provide individual and group counselling and support to students and teachers. Recreational activities will also be provided if funds become available.
A number of training sessions will be conducted for teachers and school principals on topics such as GBV, child protection and addressing violence against children (AVAC). Similar training sessions will be delivered to parents through the PTAs, which will continue to play a pivotal role in developing a holistic approach to education for Palestine refugees.
UNRWA will also conduct minor repairs to 73 UNRWA school facilities that require urgent maintenance. This does not include facilities already included in this appeal under the Emergency Repair, Rehabilitation and Maintenance of UNRWA Installations section.
Through this appeal, summer learning activities will be organized for at least 12,000 students, contingent on the availability of funds. This intervention will ensure that students do not miss out on quality teaching and learning and that any learning loss is made up for through this summer programme. In addition to academic support, the programme will include extracurricular and PSS activities for students to support their well-being. Catch-up classes will be organized for Grade 9 students in preparation for official end of cycle exams.
This action contributes to the achievement of the strategic objective under the HRP:
Strategic Objective 3: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.

Implementing Partners

The United Nations Relief and Work Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) has one of the most significant humanitarian operational presences in the West Bank, both geographically and in terms of sectors of intervention. Next to the Palestinian Authority, UNRWA is the biggest service provider in the West Bank. In total, the Agency serves 1,078,772 Palestine refugees and other registered persons (UNRWA, Registered Population Dashboard, January 2021), which amounts to one-third of the population in the West Bank, of whom more than half a million are women and girls. UNRWA was established by the General Assembly in 1949 and is mandated to assist and protect some 5 million registered Palestine refugees in the region. It has five fields of operation - Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, the Gaza Strip and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. As the main service provider for Palestine refugees in the region, UNRWA delivers primary education, health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement, microfinance, and emergency assistance. The Agency has extensive experience providing emergency assistance to Palestine refugees during humanitarian crises to save lives, preserve human dignity, and prevent and respond to the impacts of such crises. In the West Bank, the Agency's programmes seek to reduce the impact of the protection crisis stemming from over 50 years of the Israeli occupation by addressing the critical needs of the most affected individuals. UNRWA's protection work is fundamental to further the ability of Palestine refugees to enjoy their rights fully; including the provision of services to address violence, coercion, deliberate deprivation, and abuse, both by working actively to prevent these and mitigate their consequences. UNRWA is working on prevention through protection advocacy efforts; by actively promoting changes and shifts in policies and practices amongst duty bearers and working at the community level to enhance protective behaviours amongst refugees, and by improving the protective nature of its service delivery. In terms of response, UNRWA provides critical services to those exposed to protection threats, including psycho-social counselling, medical assistance, cash-based interventions, and the provision of dedicated programming to specific vulnerable groups. UNRWA is the best-placed UN agency to engage with both Israeli and Palestinian duty-bearers in the West Bank - it has wide-reaching access to all the relevant actors and decision-makers on the ground. UNRWA itself has developed a broad operational presence alongside its protection mandate. UNRWA utilizes and carefully cultivates its unique access to the duty-bearers to further the protection of Palestine refugees. As such, UNRWA is uniquely well-placed to take on a wide-reaching role in protection advocacy.

3 - Needs

After twelve years of conflict, it is critical that formal and non-formal education services are approached as long-term investment by not only children and families but also by humanitarian actors. COVID-19 and worsening economic conditions have exacerbated the consequences of underinvestment in education service delivery. The consequences of this will continue in 2023-2024.Formal education will continue to have a limited absorption and retention capacity and be carried out in largely inadequate and at times unsafe, learning environments. Education capacity and effectiveness will continue to be constrained by insufficient numbers of paid teachers, lack of teaching and learning supplies and a lack of continuous professional development for teachers and other education personnel.UNRWA has lost 40% of its classrooms due to the conflict. Almost 60% of Palestine refugees, including school children have been displaced at least once since the start of the conflict. Many of UNRWA schools are run in second shift in schools building on loan from Ministry of Education. Schools are in dire need of maintenance and new furniture/equipment. The Agency is striving to deploy and maintain educational staff that can deliver quality education, following the displacement or departure of many qualified staff outside of the country. COVID-19 has added another layer of needs for increased space and provision of sanitization materials. Palestine refugee students are dealing with trauma and stress

4 - Implementation of activities

The Agency will implement the following activities: •Direct provision of educational services to up to 49,500 Palestine refugee students, through 102 schools; •Provision of psychosocial support for students and staff by counsellors in all UNRWA schools; •Regular maintenance of UNRWA schools and availing basic furniture where necessary •Provision of support and catch up classes to students, in addition to summer learning activities; •Offering students the supplies and materials they need for learning; •Delivery of training to school principals, teacher and students on safety and security, including ERW awareness) and protection; •Procurement and distributing internet packages to staff and vulnerable students where remote learning becomes necessary due to COVID-19 measures; •Procurement and distribution of disinfectants, sanitizers, and hygiene materials to schools; • Procurement and distribution of masks to students and staff; • Offering transport to students returning to damaged camps. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has different structures in place to monitor its interventions, including Education. There is a dedicated Area Support Team that follows up on Protection interventions and a Programme Support Office that handles monitoring and evaluation of activities. UNRWA has an extensive Result Based Monitoring (RBM) systems with indicators for the Education programme and holds quarterly management reviews. The Education Programme conducts regular monitoring field visits.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Violence against children in and around UNRWA schools • Likelihood: Medium • Impact: Medium • Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA is delivering training and ToT sessions to staff to address violence against children (AVAC) and to decrease violence in and around UNRWA installations, including peer to peer violence. 2. Self-care sessions are conducted with the UNRWA education staff and counselling and psychosocial support is provided to the students through UNRWA school counsellors 3. UNRWA addresses cases of violence against children from staff members using available investigative and sanctions regimes. 4. UNRWA provides training on positive behaviour management techniques in the classroom to education staff. 5. UNRWA creates child-friendly learning environments. Protection risk: Students/families referring to negative coping mechanisms due to the declining economic situation (drop out, child labour, early marriage, drug consumption) • Likelihood: Medium • Impact: Medium Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA is aware of the lack of professional support for children and their families in need of PSS. UNRWA is doing the outmost possible to contribute to fill this gap. Psychosocial support activities in schools are enhanced, in close cooperation with the UNRWA protection team. 2. UNRWA PSS counsellors provide personalized assistance to students who are identified as having very specific and/or urgent need.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, and SEA including in UNRWA schools• Likelihood: medium • Impact: high • Mitigation Measures: 1 . UNRWA provides training on protection and gender mainstreaming and disability inclusion. 2. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. 3. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 4. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 5. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. 6. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is responded to.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of Palestine Refugees in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk: UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to Palestine Refugees needs because of lack of funding or due to overwhelming increase in humanitarian needs. Mitigation: 1. Ensure efficient and sustainable delivery of operations and services prioritizing those most in need 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to mobilize funding 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems to make best use of available resources 4. Continue to monitor interventions through results-based monitoring framework. Risk: UNRWA staff and Palestine Refugees are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. Adapt programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. Provide PPEs to frontline staff and avail sanitization materials at offices and distribution centres.
