Project Summary

This project will respond to the high nutrition needs of the most vulnerable groups of the population in hard to reach areas of Ezo county especially U5s and PLWs.
CIDO will implement the proposed emergency response for twelve months through complementing the existing nutrition services in the static nutrition facilities by establishing 12 mobile OTP/TSFP outreach sites in hard-to-reach areas of Ezo county while coordinating with existing nutrition partners to improve the nutrition response coverage though rapid response and referral mechanisms. Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) will be engaged to conduct routine active case finding, rapid assessments, and continue with surveillance of the nutrition situation to ensure the targeted population has access to timely, appropriate and treatment care on a needs basis. Supported by CIDO, the CNVs will also conduct massive community mobilization and mass screening, by use of MUAC approach to ensure early detection, identification and referral of Severe Acute malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute malnutrition (MAM) cases among children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). Under the technical support of CIDO’s Nutrition specialist, 60 Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) will be trained on Community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), while an orientation training on GBV and child protection for all nutrition staffs will be conducted. CIDO will recruit, train and deploy additional 18 nutrition assistants to ensure timely and appropriate management of acute malnutrition service is provided to all children 6-59 months and PLWs who are acutely malnourished identified at 12 outreach sites while providing s service for five days a week. To improve the quality of services and ensure sustainability of the program, CIDO will provide refresher trainings for 60 CNVs and 24 Nutrition assistants on CMAM and Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN). These will be recruited locally in those Payams where the mobile sites have been established.
This will be in response to low service coverage of nutrition services as a result of inaccessibility for people living in hard to reach areas to enhance focused rapid response mechanism through provision of lifesaving nutritional services in the identified hard to reach areas of Ezo County. Along with the provision of treatment services and care for children 6-59months and PLWs identified with acute malnutrition, CIDO’s mobile teams will provide gender-sensitive MIYCN counselling and support sessions for mothers and fathers, with key focus on breastfeeding; nutritious complementary foods for infants and young children; care and feeding of malnourished children; referral to health services for care and growth monitoring; and information on safe storage and treatment of water, proper hand washing, and sanitation.
CIDO will use the group model approach by forming 30 Mother Support Groups (MSG) for promotion of optimal MIYCN practices. CIDO will focus on dissemination of key nutrition promotion and prevention messages to the targeted community in Ezo County. To ensure that the nutrition services are provided in accordance with national and international standards, the CIDO will print and distribute to all nutrition staffs guidelines, reference tables, and look up tables with relevant key nutrition messages and cut offs, while providing regular supportive supervision to the nutrition staffs. CIDO will rely on UNICEF, WFP and others partners and CHDs to provide nutrition supplies for treatment of SAM and MAM beneficiaries respectively, whilst ensuring timely request, receipt and prepositioning of all nutrition supplies to the nutrition sites.
All children and PLWs identified with Acute malnutrition will be treated in accordance with SS CMAM guideline.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, the cumulative effect of years of conflicts, violence and destroyed livelihoods has led to a humanitarian emergency of high proportions. South Sudanese women and children continue to suffer the greatest proportion of effects with high levels of acute food insecurity (SS IPC, August 2019). The IPC (2019) projections estimates 4.54 million people (39% of the population) likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food from September to December 2019 and a further likely deterioration from January to April 2020 affecting about 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) who are likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. According to FSNMS round 23 (August, 2019), the nutrition situation in South Sudan remains critical with a GAM rate at 11.6% and SAM rate at 2.3% and this is attributed to a combination of factors; poor harvest, dry spells and past conflict. The IPC, 2019 report for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) indicate that Renk County in Upper Nile state was classified as IPC AMN Phase 5 with the highest GAM rate of 32.1%. Accordingly, the national stunting level indicate that 17.9 % of children under age five are stunted, and 5.2 percent of children are severely stunted. According to the national nutrition cluster, Gogrial West county has the highest burden of 14,679 children with SAM the September, 2019 classified it as IPC AMN phase 4.Based on the HRP 2020 projections, the nutrition cluster estimates that 950 and 4,780 children under the age of five are severely malnourished and moderately malnourished in Ezo county respectively. CIDO in partnership with UNDP/SSHF and OCHA is implementing Health interventions with two mobile health facilities in Yirol west and Morobo counties and thus CIDO has operation presence in Yirol and Morobo with a better understanding of the local context. Additionally, CIDO has experience in implementation of nutrition interventions with OCHA and UNDP under SSHF round one in 2017 in Yei and Lainya (Wonduruba) counties. CIDO is a member of the consortium implementing the Scaling Up Nutrition (SUN) movement implementing in Four States: Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and Unity States. Based on its planned nutrition response in Ezo county for the first time, CIDO is planning to establish new office in Ezo County. With this, CIDO is applying for nutrition fund in order to provide an integrated nutrition activities into its routine outreach health interventions, operate and start outreach/mobile nutrition specific and sensitive interventions in Ezo and others locations or counties in South Sudan.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. Women and children mostly suffer the highest magnitude of the effects of violence and conflicts coupled with deprivation of their rights. CIDO's nutrition program will will ensure that rights of children and women are prioritized and taken into serious consideration. CIDO has zero tolerance to Violence against women and children. In this project, CIDO will orient all nutrition staffs on the respect code for individual rights. Staffs will be trained on seeking consent with the mothers and caregivers, sensitize them very well and educate them on the importance of the program.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This nutrition project is targeting vulnerable people who are affected by the accumulated effects of violence and conflicts. All the children, adolescents and women affected either by displacements from tribal or armed conflicts will be catered for in this program irrespective of gender, ethnicity or religion.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. This grant will enable CIDO to establish mobile OTP/TSFP sites in hard to reach areas of Ezo county. To ensure linkage with the government and community systems, this project will be linked to the boma health initiative by engaging the home health promoters in payams where mobile sites are established to trainings on prevention of malnutrition. Further, CIDO will ensure that all malnourished children with medical complications will be referred to the government health facilities with stabilization centers to ensure a continuum of nutrition care services are provided to the beneficiaries.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. The national objective formulated by ministry of health is to prevent and treat malnutrition among the vulnerable population of South Sudan through coordinating with nutrition partners, UNICEF and WFP to support the different nutrition interventions. Accordingly, CIDO will implement this project with the aim of contributing to the reduction of malnutrition among children <5years of age and PLWs by preventing and treating SAM and MAM which is in accordance to the national objective.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. During program implementation, emphasis will be given to proper disposal of waste through health education sessions on the importance of proper use of pit latrine, proper hand washing among others.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. During the implementation phase of the project, CIDO will ensure that all community actively participate in implementation through trainings and distributions of therapeutic food rations, dissiminating MIYCN messages and referral systems of the severely malnourished children. To ensure sustainability of the program, CIDO will ensure that all project recruitments are done locally from the locations where the project will be implemented. secondly, all nutrition will be trained on active case finding and referrals using MUAC method of screening ad thirdly, mothers will be educated on diet diversification using the locally available foods through conducting routine individual and one on one counseling sessions and nutrition education. This way, when the project comes to the end, then community members are able to carry out routine nutrition interventions to prevent malnutrition in their locations which is good for sustainability

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

In this nutrition project, CIDO will form 6 focus groups 2 in each county comprising of women, men, local chiefs and adolescent girls. These groups will be charged with consultations with the community members about the community needs that require prioritization during the planning of this project activities. CIDO will ensure that women and girls form majority of the mother support groups for this nutrition program. This will make women be fully engaged in decision making of the program. Additionally, women and girls will be provided with information on how to prevent gender based violence against children, they will be informed on how GBV survivors can get or access help from GBV actors for rehabilitation and support. This information will be provided to the women and girls in support groups formed by CIDO. CIDO will make sure that chiefs and landlords are sensitized on peaceful existence and tolerance of women to own properties and to ensure that women have access to land owning in order to help them cultivate and engage in other agricultural activities so as to alleviate hunger and in the end preventing malnutrition. Men and local leaders in support groups established by CIDO will be the first to target for this information. By establishing mobile or outreach OTP/TSFP sites in hard to reach areas, CIDO hopes to reduce the long distances that mothers and caregivers travel to accesss nutrition services in the existing health facilities. Such modalities employed by CIDO will help to reduce exposure of women and girls to violence as a result of gender. Since CIDO’s nutrition programing aims to enroll more women in their work and activities, it’s assumed that, it will contribute to women empowerment by giving them incentives and salaries to enhance their livelihoods which is significant to reducing the rates of malnutrition in the targeted locations or nutrition within Ezo County

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CIDO is accountable to the women, men, boys and girls and places this responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. Before implementation, CIDO will conduct a needs assessment through focus holding group discussion with stakeholders at community level.In line with CIDO’s commitment to social accountability, which includes a complaints and feedback mechanism, In this nutrition program, CIDO will form four (4) project committee, one in each County. These will be tasked to engage the beneficiaries about their comprehensive understanding of the project and how they perceive it in terms of meeting their expectations. Further, CIDO will hold quarterly stakeholders meetings in the Payams where mobile OTP/TSFP outreach services are being offered to enable community members communicate issues and concerns associated with the nutrition care services they are being provided, this will give them platform to provide feedback and monitor implementation of the nutrition response. The community will be routinely consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation through the community mobilization project officer. CIDO’s nutrition project prioritizes highly vulnerable groups, including children and women, to improve their and improve their access to service. CIDO will ensure gender equality is at the core of its nutrition program by collecting sex and age disaggregated data. At the community level, CIDO works to minimize the vulnerability of women to gender-based violence (GBV) by minimizing the need to travel to receive services through mobile teams. In addition to the quarterly stakeholders meetings and project committee, CIDO will form focus groups in the Payams where nutrition services are being offered. These will hold discussions on quarterly basis to evaluate the progress of project in terms of reaching the intended beneficiaries. Issues and complaints raised in such fora will be taken seriously by the project management to address them.
