Project Summary

This project will contribute to prevent and treat malnutrition by providing Infant and young child feeding interventions, Micro-nutrient deficiency prevention and control and improve equitable access to high quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW. The project will be implemented by nutrition Rapid Response Teams (RRTs). This distinctive RRTs will conduct HH visits and orientation sessions in areas populated with IDPs and affected people in Idleb, Aleppo, Ar Raqqah and Al-Hasakah governorates. These RRTs will Screen, refer and follow up children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished and provide emergency IYCF counselling for mothers whose children are moderately malnourished. Moreover, RRTs will distribute nutrition supplements and micro-nutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women. Also, they will screen the children regarding general danger signs and educate their mother about them. They will do hygiene promotion through their activities and will distribute IEC materials for targeted PLW, girls and boys. There will be 25 nutrition RRTS teams distributed in various locations in Idleb and Aleppo. Each team will provide basic nutrition services (MUAC screening, distribution micro-nutrient, and high-energy biscuits, and case referral for treatment). The project duration is 10 months and the target beneficiaries are 127,170 including boys and girls <5 years, pregnant and lactating women in IDPs and Host communities,
Anther hand, This project important foundation for integrated primary health and nutrition services and community-based and outreach activities, it will include a network of RRT, to provide integrated health and nutrition services focusing on IYCF counseling and screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at a community level. These RRT will be linked with 8 health facilities, This project will contribute to treating malnutrition cases in 8 facilities in Idlib and Aleppo.
The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health and nutrition staff on IYCF counseling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility staff and RRT will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help to raise the awareness of the related communities. Relying on RRT will cover a big gap resulting from poor coverage of nutritional services in health facilities, QC will provide training IYCF principles for all staff in RRT and health facilities, will be conducted 6 times, one time for 25 people .will be organized in Syria.

4 - Needs

Since 1st December 2019, nearly 390,000 people have fled from their homes, mainly from southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates, mostly escaping hostilities by moving to urban centers and IDP camps in northwest Idleb. Tens of thousands of others have reportedly moved to areas such as Afrin, A’zaz and Al-Bab in northern Aleppo governorate. Most of these displaced people in northwest Syria have been already displaced multiple times before. Each new displacement introduces new risks, intensifies existing vulnerabilities and winter conditions are compounding the vulnerability of displaced people. Many who fled are in urgent need of humanitarian support, particularly nutrition assistance for children U5 and PLW. Chronic malnutrition is evident in most sub-districts throughout Syria – notably in the Northwest of Syria (Aleppo, Idlib) and it is also on rise with almost half a million children stunted (low height for age) in 2020 and face losing their future due to hindered learning and physical developments, as well as early death. Acute malnutrition remains relatively high among internally displaced children and mothers as well as the population affected by conflict. Throughout Syria, about 240,000 children under the age of five years and over 50,000 pregnant and lactating mothers are in need of urgent lifesaving curative nutrition services. At least 36,000 boys and 35,000 girls are acutely malnourished and are at increased risk of healthcomplications, including death. Furthermore, it is the youngest children that are the most vulnerable and affected, with higher numbers of acute malnutrition among infants under 6 months of age. For all these problems, we need to scale up nutrition response to reach part of displaced mothers and children within the accessible areas in NW Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by supporting 25 nutrition Rapid Response Teams "RRTs" in high severity communities and IDPs hosting areas. Each RRT will consist of "nutritionist, 2 CHWs, Data entry, nurse and midwife. The teams will provide basic services such as screening of children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition signs and referring them to the mobile clinic or OTP or SC, this in addition to PSS cases. Moreover, the RRTs will disseminate IYCF-E messages among mothers of children under five through individual counseling and group sessions, facilitating IYCF-E campaigns and integrate it to the Child Health Days, registering all screened cases and reporting to the nutritionists and data entry team member. In addition, the RRTs will distribute micro-nutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women, monitoring distribution and use of breast milk substitutes and distribute nutrition supplements.The RRTs will disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, support hygiene promotion through raising WASH awareness by providing key messages such as Hand Washing, Food Processing, etc., identifying the abused cases among boys and girls 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women and refer them to child protection GBV partners for appropriate action. QC ensures programs and projects are monitored, controlled and evaluated with a recommendation to improve the services delivered in the targeted areas. The MEAL structure at present in QC consists of; MEAL Coordinator, and MEAL Field Assistants. The Field assistants work inside Syria, make visits to all projects' locations and report the work progress. They are responsible for data collection and tracking beneﬁciary’s vs targets, conducting surveys and assessment at the locations and facilities and community-based feedback in addition to staff and partner.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Gender-Based Violence (GBV) affects accessing nutrition services. Mitigating Measures: Provide nutrition services in accessible easy to reach areas. Training of nutrition RRTs on Prevention and management of sexual violence. The presence of female staff in the nutrition teams’ increases access of women and girls to the nutrition services. Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) of women: Mitigating measures: Establish effective community-based complaints mechanisms, awareness-raising on SEA amongst nutrition team members, with continuous training on the topic. Performance management and monitoring of all team members. Risk: Lack of Duty of care for Nutrition RRTs. Mitigating measures: DOC policy in place and applied for all staff, measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights.Risk: Inequitable access to nutrition services by Vulnerable Groups. Mitigating measures:Implementation of standard essential services package across the sector interventions,including improved quality reporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs.Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicatednutrition subgroups groups (IYCF, CMAM, Capacity Building...etc.)

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks (ﬂoods, earthquakes, etc.) may have an effect on the implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster happens in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with all staff and schools’ principals. In this intervention, QC assumes that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. In regard to this, some risks might be beyond QC control. 1) The reinforcement of health facilities does not provide suﬃcient protection for patients, exposing them to physical harm (some nutrition activities are provided within health facilities). * These can be mitigated by putting in place task-shifting guidelines as part of care protocols - providing Training and adequate follow- up of competency - decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care 2) Increased malnutrition rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention. * Mitigating measure: - Hire a qualiﬁed medical staff - Distribute nutritional supplies and rations. After the Sushi treaty between Russia and Turkey, QC assumes that areas out of Syrian government control in northwest Syria will be stable for the whole of next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services inﬂux of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs in some areas suddenly increases
