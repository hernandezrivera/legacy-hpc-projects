Project Summary

This project aims to address the needs of the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in the states of North and Central Darfur. According to the HRP Prioritization plan for July – December 2019, small-scale farmers are facing increased restrictions, including the availability of, and accessibility to inputs such as machinery, seeds and fertilizers, due to high and increasing inflation rates. This is expected to lead to a surge in food prices. Fuel shortages have resulted in reduced application of herbicides, pesticides, fertilizers and delayed harvesting operations. The Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot - Food insecurity, seasonal hazards, and displacement (July 2018) report flagged North Darfur as being at a “Crisis” level on the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC).
51% of those in need are women. The number of women and adolescent girls heading households and needing to provide for their families despite limited livelihood opportunities is high and exposes them to gender based violence (GBV) including sexual harassment and abuse. There is urgent need for support for vulnerable women and girls through economic empowerment to ensure dignity and mobility and enhance their access to basic social services, in order to reduce their exposure to basic protection risks.
The needs identified in the communities of North and Central Darfur are related to the economic and environmental causes of malnutrition in the area. In an effort to address these issues, Sahari aims to build the capacities of women in order to diversify sources of income, and add to household assets. In accordance with the humanitarian protection principles, Sahari aims to tackle both the root causes of malnutrition through economic empowerment of the most affected and vulnerable women, as well as attempt to rehabilitate children under the age of 5 with moderate acute malnutrition and acutely malnourished pregnant and lactating women through community based management of acute malnutrition and behavioural change communication activities. To complement these activities, there will be the rehabilitation of shallow wells, community led sanitation campaigns, as well as hygiene promotion and awareness activities.

1 - Needs

Nutrition:Malnutrition prevalence remains high in Mellit, Um Keddada and Laiet localities of North Darfur due to continued insecurity, poor dietary diversity, a high disease burden among children under five, and low awareness among caregivers in effective feeding and caring practices of infants and young children. Chronic malnutrition is a major public health concern in North Darfur where about 38% of under five years children have already been stunted so micro nutrient supplements to infants and young children, and home fortification of foods are effective strategies to improve, also will be considered in this proposal. The coverage of vitamin A supplementation is still low in Sudan. With a poorly performing health system to delivery vitamin A supplementation at a high coverage, it is important to look at the other options for providing vitamin A if both dietary diversity and supplementation are insufficient. Because it can also be assumed that more people are affected or at risk of micro nutrients deficiencies than the number of people who is being covered with the fortified foods. Therefore, home-fortification with MNPs is a good solution to improve intake of essential micro nutrients. Iodized salt intake is also considered low, with the national average of household use of iodized salt at 9.5%. FSL:According to the HRP Prioritization plan for July – December 2019, small-scale farmers are facing increased restrictions, including the availability of, and accessibility to inputs such as machinery, seeds and fertilizers, due to high and increasing inflation rates. This is expected to lead to a surge in food prices. Fuel shortages have resulted in reduced application of herbicides, pesticides, fertilizers and delayed harvesting operations. The Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot - Food insecurity, seasonal hazards, and displacement (July 2018) report flagged North Darfur as being at a “Crisis” level on the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). Furthermore, Um Keddada was very affected by the recent floods; Sahari conducted an assessment in Oct. 2019, in cooperation COOPI, Oxfam America and relevant line ministries. The assessment found that 1986 houses in the locality were affected; 171 completely damaged and 1815 partially affected. The rains have also affected the health status status of many of the inhabitants of the state; a report from Radio Dabanga stated that the health authorities say that North Darfur has never seen such epidemics of malaria and dengue fever before. In the locality of Azum, Central Darfur, according to the State of Central Darfur’s food security analysis, 60% of the population have only minimum food consumption and are only able to meet some non-food needs, having to choose between access to education and access to health services. A further 20% are unable to meet the minimum food consumption requirements. Due to the rising prices and the unstable harvests, the capacity of those in need has decreased sharply. WASH:In the locality of Azum, Central Darfur, only 30% of the population has access to safe water sources, according to the State of Central Darfur’s food security analysis. Most communities practice open defecation and there is a need for hygiene promotion activities.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Most of the projects Sahari operates are aimed at building women's capacities and resilience. This is done through projects targeting the increasing the economic resilience of women, focusing on women's literacy, building household assets for women headed households and improving women and children's nutritional status.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

At the onset of the projects, Sahari informs the beneficiaries of the PSEA policy it operates under and who to approach in the case of any issues. Furthermore, HAC, local authorities and community leaders will be involved in frequent monitoring of the quality of activities.
