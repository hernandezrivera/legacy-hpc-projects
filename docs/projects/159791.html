Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to provide immediate access to food and livelihood support to the vulnerable and food insecure populace of the target locations in Somalia that were most affected by the deteriorating food security situation.
The project entails cash for work component for pastoral livelihood locations which involve the construction/rehabilitation of communal water catchments in the target locations in order to reduce the vulnerability and enhance the resilience of the target beneficiary households composed of 50 skilled labourers who earn 5.5$ a day for 20 days a month for 4 months and unskilled labourers who earn 5$ a day for 20 working days a month for four months and an unconditional cash grant aspect for 500 labour inactive rural households in the target locations for 6 months.
The project beneficiary selection criteria, the daily workload and the general implementation will be cognizant of minority interests, disability, age factor, protection, gender and accountability to affected populations.
The cash intervention will adhere to the CMWG recommended transfer value target and guidelines and the transfer will also be mobile technology-based. The intervention will support the market as it enhances the purchasing power of the beneficiaries and injects cash flow to the local economy. The Organization will coordinate the cash based intervention with the FS Cluster, the CMWG and other organizations engaged in cash based intervention in the areas to avoid overlapping and ensure proper coordination.

Project Activities

Establishing project committees composed of seven persons (4 men and 3 women) from the community in each location of the target regions.-Conduct the identification and selection of 400 most vulnerable women headed households, pregnant and lactating mothers and elderly persons from the IDPs and Host Communities and pastoral and agro-pastoral households in the target locations-Conduct the distribution of cash to the selected 400 beneficiary households through the identified mobile money transfer vendor. Each household earns 80$ for six (6) months. -Undertake community mobilisation, conduct re-identification and rehabilitate/construct 20 strategic communal infrastructure including river banks, water pans, irrigation canals, feeder roads and culverts.-Conduct the identification and selection of 150 poor and drought-affected households to be engaged in CFW exercise targeting poorest and unskilled households in critical livelihood crisis in Agro pastoral and Pastoral Livelihoods. Each of the 50 skilled labourers will earn 5.5$ per day for 20 working days a month for four (4) months while the 100 unskilled labourers working as foremen will earn 5 $ a day for 20 working days a month for 4 months.-Provide business skills training and a seed grant of 100$ to 100 IDPs/Returnees and Host community households prioritising vulnerable women and youth in the target location.-Provide seasonally appropriate and livelihood specific agricultural inputs such as certified quality seeds, farm tools, training, land preparation and irrigation support to 100 beneficiary households in Lower Juba.-Training of the project staff and local authority representatives with at least 40% representation of female on standard program implementation, disability inclusion, accountability to the affected population, gender mainstreaming, protection mainstreaming, monitoring and evaluation and project objectives.-Monitoring progress of implementation of unconditional and cash for work activities.

Project Indicators

According to FSNAU Food Security and Nutrition Post Gu technical release 2019, the food security situation in Somalia is likely to deteriorate and an estimated 6.3 million people across Somalia are facing acute food security crisis or worse. An estimated 2.1 million people in the Country are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phase 3 & 4) and a further 4.2 million are in IPC (2) stressed. The figures of 6.3 million facing acute food security crisis or worse are truly unsettling.Acute food insecurity is prevalent among populations who lost most of their animals and became destitute during the drought period, those affected by the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall and others who were affected by the large scale and protracted displacement due to a combination of conflict and natural hazards.These population groups are expected to face large scale food consumption gaps and need sustained humanitarian assistance to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation.The FSNAU report and FEWSNET East Africa reports consistently identify the Juba, Galgaduud, Mudug and Nugaal regions populace as some of the areas of concern.Over 14% of the population in Lower Juba is in crisis and emergency and is likely to rise 25.6% between the period of October to December 2019, over 6% of the population in Nugaal is in crisis and emergency, 7% of the population in Galgaduud is in IPC 3 and 4 while a further 9% of the total population in Mudug is in crisis and emergency. The percentage of the population in IPC 3 and 4 in each of the regions is expected to rise during this final quarter of the year. A significantly higher proportion of the population in the aforesaid regions is in stressed phase. Majority of the affected populace are the IDPs, pastoral and agro-pastoral livelihoods. The IDPs remain extremely vulnerable and represent a major proportion of the total number of people who face crisis and emergency in these regions and across Somalia. Limited humanitarian access in some of the affected areas of the target regions further exacerbates the situation. Mitigation Measures

Protection of civilians: Communities are forced to leave their place of residence in order to access humanitarian assistance.Mitigation: The Organization will adopt a mobile-based technology for payment of project beneficiaries and will further ensure that points of distribution for any inputs is a central, convenient, safe and accessible location to all beneficiaries especially the disabled, elderly, and the pregnant and lactating mothers. Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacementThe main risks facing the displaced population is resource based marginalisation and conflict with the host community and the project has factored the possible risks in its targeting criteria that the displaced population will be targeted with a host community population. The Organization further anticipates that there is risk of security in some of the project locations and to avoid the risk of exclusion, Southern Aid will do an assessment of the security situation to establish the feasibility of operation. During the formation and community entry process Adeso will ensure members from the minority and displaced person will be part of the village committees that are task to facilitate project beneficiary selection. Further, Southern Aid will use the FSC Protection Mainstreaming checklist as a technical guidance. This seeks to ensure that interventions are done in a non- discriminatory and impartial ways that promote the safety and the dignity of the target population.Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and ageSouthern Aid appreciates and considers the significance of gender mainstreaming in its program design. Gender mainstreaming is prioritised in the entire project cycle. In a bid to ensure attention to priority cross cutting issues (sex, age, disability and health), the project will take into consideration gender mainstreaming and will give priority to women led households, households with children aged below five years, older persons,the disabled and those with sick persons). The daily workload will be reduced to encourage women participation. The project will ensure the significant representation of either sex in project committees. The elderly and the minorities will be considered in targeting and enlisting of the beneficiaries.Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violenceSouthern Aid has a long established presence in the regions and a good understanding of the context. The Organisation appreciates the context of operation and thus established a good rapport and direct relations with the local elders and the community to promote humanitarian principles, gain access andensure the effective implementation of the program. The organisation is also cognisant of the clan/sub clan dynamics of the area and considers this in the implementation process. This measures strengthen the local acceptance and protection of the organisation and its activities by the communities and the the local authorities. The organisation further analysed the security situation the target areas and it has been relatively calm, although there has been recent sporadic attacks in the some of the localities. No prohibitive security challenge is foreseen at this stage of the project given that the organisation has other ongoing activities in the area and they remain accessible. However, the organisation will continue to monitor any change of the situation and will update the donor and adjust accordingly.Complaints MechanismThe Organization will set up community friendly complaints and feedback mechanism that appreciates the literacy, convinience and safety of the target beneficiaries.
