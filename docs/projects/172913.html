Project Summary

Following the crises that has affected Sudan since February 2003 and, in response to the need for accurate information on displacement and the identification of beneficiaries for the delivery of humanitarian aid, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) began its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) program in 2004 to support the Government of Sudan (GoS) and its humanitarian partners in establishing an accurate data collection system and providing data on displacement in areas most affected by the conflict and natural disaster.
IOM has been working in close collaboration with the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) and partners on the field to establish a unified and systematic method to collect individual data on internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in seven states. Up until 2018, the DTM program in Sudan focused mainly on registration, including biometric registration (since 2010), of IDPs and returning IDPs. Registration was mostly carried out in response to requests from the government and humanitarian partners for the identification of beneficiaries and the delivery of humanitarian assistance (i.e. food vouchers by WFP), positioning DTM Sudan as the reference source of displacement data in the country.
As the emergency levels of the crisis in the country reduces and the situation stabilizes in most of the conflict affected areas, there remains a need to restructure the program and tailor the operation to better suit the current protracted displacement and return scenario. Specifically, there is the need to further update displacement figures more frequently, comprehensively, systematically, and in regular intervals to ensure that data is continually collected, provides a meaningful resource and is available through IOM’s DTM system.
DTM is a system to track and monitor displacement and population mobility. It is designed to regularly and systematically capture, process and disseminate information to provide a better understanding of the movements and evolving needs of displaced populations, whether on site or en route. Data collection strategies and tools are based on the DTM global methodology but have been adapted to reflect the context and population movements specific to Sudan.

1 - Needs

Despite significant political successes in achieving peace throughout 2020, instability remains high in certain areas of the country. Sporadic intercommunal tension and violence in parts of Darfur, eastern Sudan and the Kordofan regions, coupled with recurrent environmental shocks, such as flooding and disease outbreaks, are placing continued stress on the needs of the country. Commencing in November 2019, DTM Sudan expanded its operations to implement Mobility Tracking (MT) across an initial seven states. The MT methodology utilises key informant networks to systematically track presence of mobile populations in locations of interest over consistent data collection rounds. Enumerators revisit each location and interview key informants to update the locations list and verify population presence on a quarterly basis. MT Round One identified the presence of 2,399,433 internally displaced persons (IDPs), 703,596 permanent returnees from internal displacement, in addition to 137,870 seasonal returnees, 39,083 returnees from abroad, and 140,661 foreign nationals currently residing in Sudan. DTM will continue to implement MT throughout 2021, to frequently update population figures and allow for subsequent analysis of trends. Throughout 2020, DTM captured the new displacement of an estimated 351,451 individuals (of which 243,862 were displaced due to floods and 106,839 individuals due to conflict) using its Emergency Event Tracking (EET) tool. As a subcomponent of MT, EET will continue being regularly deployed to track sudden displacements and population movements and provide more frequent updates when needed. EET is activated within 72 hours – one week after an event (such as natural disasters or conflict) to assist in rapid response planning. To complement EET, Situation Assessments will collect data on populations in non-emergency settings or protracted situations caused by conflict or natural disaster. DTM teams piloted the implementation of a Situation Assessment in six locations within Gharb Jabal Marrah and Wasat Jabal Marrah localities, Central Darfur to collect data on those who have been affected by previous and ongoing forms of conflict, as well as limited humanitarian response due to access impediments. While continuing to operate these core components in the initial seven states of implementation, IOM will expand its DTM operations to the east of the country – laying the foundation for mapping affected, displaced and returnee populations present in Red Sea, Kassala and Gedaref states.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Main Output: Accurate and reliable DTM data on affected populations is disseminated to the humanitarian agencies, sectors and other relevant stakeholders. Activities: 1. Gathering target population estimates through a broad network of key informants at the lowest administrative level available, for tracking and monitoring key trends in population movements over time. 2. Assessing demographics (collecting sex and age disaggregated data).3. Identifying priority needs, vulnerabilities and protection gaps of the target population groups. 4. Building local capacity for tracking and analyzing internal displacement and mixed migration inside Sudan through joint implementation with government stakeholders. 5. Utilizing Mobility Tracking, Emergency Event Tracking and Situational Assessment estimates as an evidence base for operational planning and programmatic design for a more targeted humanitarian response. 6. Providing trainings, workshops and refreshers on standard DTM tools and protection practices for IOM enumerators. 7. Producing regular DTM narrative reports, maps and graphs to be shared with GoS, humanitarian actors, relevant sectors and sector partners. 8. Activating the Mobility Restrictions data collection activities in response to any mitigation measures enforced to reduce the transmission of COVID-19. This activity utilizes key informant networks to collect and compile information related to mobility restrictions and other measures implemented at airports, blue border crossing points, land border crossing points, as well as internal transit point restrictions. Data from IDP camps including impediments to camp access, impact of COVID-19 on IDPs and IDPs affected by mobility restrictions are also collected. Based on IOM’s global methodology, but adapted to reflect the context and the population movements specific to Sudan, DTM aims to enhance its past operational approach and focus on conducting the following activities over the upcoming year: Mobility Tracking (MT): The new methodology deployed in 2019 through periodic baseline assessments provides multi-layered information on the different target groups within defined locations and update such figures at regular intervals. As a result, IOM will be able to provide a complete and up-to-date picture of displacement and other forms of mobility in Sudan. These figures are already used by the Government and humanitarian and development partners to better protect, assist and advocate on behalf of these populations. Additionally, MT aims to produce an evidence base for strategy and programme design to support humanitarian, transition, and recovery operations within Sudan; and will facilitate joint assessment and response planning and joint funding appeals. Emergency Event Tracking: As a subcomponent of MT and activated on a need’s basis, EET will be deployed to track sudden displacements and population movements and provide more frequent updates on the scale of displacement and quantify the affected population when needed. It is activated within 72 hours - one week after an event (i.e. floods) to assist in rapid response to meet lifesaving needs.To supplement EET, DTM Sudan has designed and implemented Situation Assessments to collect data on populations in non-emergency settings or protracted situations caused by conflict or natural disaster. As with EET, Situation Assessments utilize a broad network of key informants to provide an overview of the context and inform response planning.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM's DTM activities are conducted in strict adherence to data protection principles and international best practice. The exercises draw on IOM's global expertise in conducting similar activities to ensure that information is non-identiﬁable, stored securely and only accessible, by request, by approved partners. Furthermore, DTM is committed to collecting information on main age and sex breakdown per location of assessment, which is the foundation to protection programming, providing insight to the demographic and specific gender nuance for protection focused programming.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The DTM program provides baseline figures that are then used to build more detailed and nuanced data collection exercises like the peace building fund profiling exercise. In addition, area-based indicators that monitor the progress towards durable solutions will be collected through MT and analyzed together at the individual level collected under different exercises to provide the full picture and measures of durable solution indicators and criteria. Yearly surveys are conducted with data users and partners to allow a continuous improvement and relevance of the data collected. Data is also shared during Information Management Working Group (IMWG) and Durable Solutions Working Group (DSWG) meetings, thus providing another venue to elicit comments and feedback from users. Information on Accountability to Affected Populations(AAP) will be shared with the Intersector Coordination Group (ISCG) and IMWG in line with international data protection standards and established IOM principles. DTM intends to explore the possibility of conducting follow-up restitution meetings with affected communities.
