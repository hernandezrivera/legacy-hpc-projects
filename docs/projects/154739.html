Project Summary

Mercy Corps will continue working in four priority governorates (Anbar, Kirkuk, Ninewa, and Salah Al-Din) to provide WASH assistance in camps and out of camps. In camp settings, Mercy Corps will conduct activities including water supply via water trucking, desludging, hygiene kit provision, hygiene promotion, latrine installation, and rehabilitation of existing WASH infrastructure. In out of camp settings, Mercy Corps will focus on rehabilitating water treatment plants (WTPs), water networks, water pumping stations and WASH infrastructure in schools and public health centers. Mercy Corps also plans to reach vulnerable populations residing in collective centers and populations returning to their areas of origin, through sanitation infrastructure rehabilitation, hygiene promotion, and provision of hygiene kits.

Needs

As specified by WASH Cluster guidance, it is estimated that 2.3 million people, (roughly 7% of the total population of Iraq) will continue to be in need of WASH support in 2019. In camps: Sanitation conditions, water supply, and solid waste management in camps remains a concern. For example, roughly 229,900 IDPs in 33 camps receive less than 35 liters of water per day. In addition, the sanitation conditions of 22 camps are also concerning, with more than 20 people using one latrine. Handwashing facilities are also insufficient, with most camps failing to provide even one tap per camp block, greatly hindering efforts to prevent the spread of disease throughout the camps. The solid waste management situation in camps continues to pose a public health risk, further exacerbating the situation. Because of the deteriorating WASH systems in camps, the rehabilitation of existing WASH facilities remains a high priority throughout Iraq. Out of camps: Newly retaken municipalities face an accumulation of years of debris from the time spent under ISIS control and the ensuing military operations to retake these areas. While services have stalled due to conflict, districts lack the capacity to collect solid waste routinely and trash continues to accumulate while local populations wait for services to resume. Needs assessments and context specific experience from previous programs highlight the immense need for WASH programming in newly retaken areas such as in Mosul District and Western Anbar. Mercy Corps has found that vulnerable individuals in these areas have difficulty meeting WASH needs due to limited resources and conflicting priorities. Gender sensitivity: Women, in particular, face additional challenges due to the lack of WASH services throughout the country. In camps and newly retaken areas, women face limited access to menstrual hygiene management items due either to limited movement or limited income and the lack of financial decision-making power within the family. A market assessment in recently retaken areas of Western Anbar found that markets, while functioning and able to meet the increasing demand for commodities, did not have any sanitary pads available at the time of the assessment. This highlights the relevance and need for WASH NFIs through distributions (in camps) or voucher programming (in retaken areas where markets are functioning) as an effective modality to supply vulnerable populations with essential items. Age Sensitivity: Young children and elderly individuals in Iraq face added pressures across camps, collective centers, and in newly retaken areas. Youth are particularly vulnerable to lacking access to safe water, good hygiene practices, and sanitation infrastructure, particularly youth in newly retaken areas with little infrastructure. In addition, elderly individuals may often have disabilities or lack the mobility to access WASH services provided to the community. Mercy Corps recognizes these vulnerabilities and will work to address these needs in project implementation.

Activities

Water Supply: Mercy Corps will provide highly needed water through water trucking in camps and out of camps (in areas of return), through water vouchers, and through the rehabilitation of water treatment plants (WTPs), water networks, and water pumping stations. Mercy Corps will monitor the water quality and provision of water throughout activities to ensure that the water being supplied is clean and safe. Mercy Corps will also build the capacity of Directorate of Water staff through trainings to ensure sustainable impact of the programming when rehabilitation projects are handed over to government authorities. Sanitation: Mercy Corps will improve access to sanitation for vulnerable individuals in camps, schools, and public health centers by providing latrines with hand washing stations, rehabilitating existing WASH infrastructure (latrines, showers, septic tanks, and cesspools), providing garbage bins and waste disposal, and conducting desludging and solid waste management activities. Hygiene Promotion: In camps, collective centers, schools, and public health centers, Mercy Corps will conduct hygiene awareness sessions (including topics on safe water chain, safe excreta disposal, hand washing, personal hygiene, solid waste management and other hygiene awareness topics). In addition to these sessions, Mercy Corps will host trainings on hygiene, organize events for important days like World Water Day and Global Hand-Washing Day, and develop information, education, and communication material. Non-Food Items: Mercy Corps will provide hygiene kits (one off), monthly hygiene kits (consumables), baby hygiene kits and student hygiene kits (in schools) as part of programming for the coming year. Gender-Sensitive Activities: One example of Mercy Corps attention to gender sensitivity in the program will be the rehabilitation and construction of sex-segregated latrines. Sanitation facilities, particularly in IDP camps, are severely limited and sub-standard. Due to a lack of facilities, often both men and women share these facilities, which negatively affect ease of access and may increase women’s exposure to protection risks. Under this program, Mercy Corps will provide sex-segregated latrines that are well lit and located close to vulnerable households in order to mitigate security risks and promote dignity, particularly for women and girls. Another example will be the development of hygiene promotion activities, specifically for women and girls. Mercy Corps will develop resources to raise awareness about specific hygiene related topics, where there are gaps in available resources. Particular attention will be given to resources focused on menstruation hygiene management, with materials targeting girls/women and boys/men. Age-Sensitive Activities: One example of Mercy Corps approach to targeting young children and adolescents in activities is through hygiene promotion lessons with this age group. For example, drama, quiz competitions or games will be designed for children, with themes of hygiene awareness raising and hand washing, while focus group discussions (FGDs) (or group sessions, separate for women and men) will be conducted with adults. Another example of Mercy Corps attention to age will be the development of handrails and sloping thresholds for elderly beneficiaries when providing latrines in camps. *Of the amount proposed, $12 million has already been committed to Mercy Corp’s WASH Program through OFDA funding.*

Protection Risk Analysis

* Due to a lack of sanitation facilities in camps, often both men and women share these facilities, which negatively affects ease of access and may increase women’s exposure to protection risks.* Beneficiaries that are vulnerable have increased exposure to abuse and/or exploitation and other psychosocial risks by authorities and/or armed groups.* The security situation can be dangerous for IDPs returning to their areas of origin, as retribution or insecurity in areas of return remain high concerns.* The security situation sometimes does not allow for program staff to access remote areas in order to provide unmet humanitarian assistance needs.

Mitigation Measures

* Mercy Corps will provide gender-segregated sanitation, including placing latrines in well-lit areas and providing handrails, etc. to improve access for the elderly.* Mercy Corps will ensure that the targeting and selection of beneficiaries will be fully based on vulnerability criteria. While local authorities will be contacted and informed of activities in the locations under their jurisdiction, Mercy Corps will conduct needs assessments in a fully independent manner, ensuring the most vulnerable households have access to the assistance provided and minimizing their exposure to abuse and/or exploitation and other psychosocial risks by authorities and/or armed groups present in the area. In addition, Mercy Corps will have dedicated program and monitoring staff to conduct home/site visits for identification, which better enables access to individuals that may face constraints in publicly seeking assistance (e.g. female-headed households that may have concerns related to movement outside their homes, elderly and people with disabilities with limited physical mobility).* Mercy Corps will ensure that basic services are provided in areas of returnees, in order to limit vulnerability in the area and unfair access to goods and services.* Security SoPs are in place and Mercy Corps' security team conducts security and monitoring assessments frequently to ensure that staff can access operation areas safely and frequently.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Mercy Corps will hold consultative sessions with beneficiaries, local authorities and other stakeholders to ensure that activities maintain the highest levels of transparency, necessity and accountability. Mercy Corps will also inform all stakeholders regarding the nature of activities, durations and limitations of the program. Through these sessions, Mercy Corps will communicate the transition strategy for all program activities, including handover of rehabilitation works to municipal governments. Mercy Corps will negotiate the specific terms of handover requirements with local authorities and sign MoUs to ensure that all roles and responsibilities are fully documented. Mercy Corps will conduct FGDs prior or during implementation and will improve services based on community feedback. Mercy Corps will also consult communities prior to designing hygiene kits, baby kits, and school hygiene kits and will adapt kit contents based on the feedback. A complaint mechanism will be established in all areas of intervention and feedback will be collected on a regular basis.
