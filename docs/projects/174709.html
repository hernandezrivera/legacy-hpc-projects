Project Summary

Malnutrition is the prime cause of morbidity for all groups. Iron deficiency is the leading cause for maternal deaths. Poor maternal nutrition, suboptimal infant and young child feeding practices and limited access to nutritious food in many conflict affected communities of north-eastern (NE) Nigeria have contributed greatly to the high incidences of malnutrition, morbidity and mortality rates among the under-five’s and pregnant/lactating women in the North East.
As a result, there is a great and urgent need for CMAM and SCs to be established at close proximity to caregivers to easy access and for quicker live saving response.
From April 2017 to date, Caritas Nigeria has been implementing CMAM, SC and IYCF in Sothern Borno and Northern Adamawa region. Our target LGAs of implementation in this region include: Shani, Bayo, Hawul and Askira Uba LGAs in Borno State and Michika, Madagali and Mubi North LGAs in Adamawa states. Caritas Nigeria has been working to promote access to services for the treatment and prevention of malnutrition among vulnerable groups, focusing on IYCF in emergencies, and pregnant/lactating women, for improved health for both Mother and Child.
Between February 2018 and July 2018, Caritas Nigeria under its Nutrition PD with UNICEF screened Ninety-Four Thousand, Nine Hundred and Fifty-Five Children (94,955) out of which Eight Thousand Two Hundred and Seventy - Seven (8,277) children were identified and treated at both OTP centres and mobile outreach stations, as SAM without complications. Three Thousand, Nine Hundred and Three (3,903) children between 6-23 months were reached with MNP at the treatment sites, 89 food demonstrations. Within this period 15 cases of SAM with complications were identified and referred to SCs, which in most cases were too far from the patients.
Also, at least 100 previously malnourished persons will receive agricultural inputs to address the root causes of their malnutrition. This will, of course, layer on a different project already being implemented by Caritas Nigeria within the target locations and would serve as the organizational contribution to the project.

1 - Needs

The Boko Haram conflict continues to have a direct impact on people’s nutrition status. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months remain consistently concerning, with over one million children suffering from SAM and MAM across 18 LGAs in the BAY states. This includes 367,000 children under five (179,830 boys and 187,170 girls) suffering from SAM and 727,000 (356,230 boys and 370,770 girls) with MAM . The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors, including: weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity.The 2020 HNO as published by OCHA indicated that “The majority of the people in need are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis stating that, one in four of the affected population is under 5 years of age with women and children being at the face of the crisis. The HNO further states that women and children constitute 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the recent displacements. More so, the 2020 HNO have highlighted the current nutrition situation as worrying, with 1.1 million children and women in need of immediate nutrition services and states that the Global acute malnutrition (GAM) in children aged 6-59 months remains highly concerning. GAM rates increased from 6.7% in 2017 to 11% in 2019, exceeding the WHO threshold of 10% disaggregated as 367,000 children under five (179,830 boys and 187,170 girls) suffering from SAM and 727,000 (356,230 boys and 370,770 girls) with MAM. These increase was attributed to weak health infrastructures, poor infant and young child feeding practices, limited access to safe water and sanitation services, poor hygiene conditions and food insecurity as two-thirds of health facilities in the BAY states been damaged by the conflict – a clear indication of the impact of the crisis on the nutrition and health system. The Nutrition Sector 2019-2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for Persons in Need (PiN) however puts the indicative populations of children with SAM burden in the target LGAs are Bayo – 3557, Kwaya Kusar – 2868 and Shani – 4914 making a cumulative of SAM targets in the proposed host communities 11339. The 2019 HRP identifies a SAM burden requiring Stabilization Center (SC) in the Bayo and Shani as 279, and 338, respectively, amounting to a total burden of 617 with 70% minimum target being 432 cases. Caritas Nigeria, therefore proposes to target the Nutrition sector’s 100% 2019 target estimate for the two LGAs for total maximum coverage which is 617 SAM SC case burden.Through this intervention period, Caritas Nigeria will focus on reaching a minimum of 35% of the estimated 11339 SAM targets (Minimum estimated target 3969 for target locations) and 35% of the estimated 4804 MAM (Minimum estimated target 1682 for target locations) target as highlighted in the HNO for PiN. Since on the average, 5% to 10% of children with SAM are expected to be with complications, this means that about 199 to 397 of the SAM children would have complications and the intervention will ensure a 100% (397) referral to inpatient services for appropriate treatment in Stabilization Centers, SCs managed in the target locations. For the PLW on the HNO PiN, the total burden for the three LGAs in focus is 4355. The project will target 35% of 4355 (1550) for IYCF and health talk delivery. And 25% of 4355 (PLW-1090) will be supported with first line Anti natal drugs such as Folic acid, Fersolate and multivitamin supplements.

2 - Activities

Borno State continues to be the epicenter of the conflict and has experienced a rise in NSAG-military clashes in recent months. The most severe and acute humanitarian needs remain concentrated in areas affected by conflict and locations hosting large numbers of internally displaced people and returnees. Bayo, Kwaya Kusar and Shani LGAs of Borno state are relatively accessible compared with areas in the hinterland that remain unreachable due to the ongoing insecurity. This has allowed for a sizable humanitarian presence in the area to be established by providing access for humanitarian operations to scale up, providing a more stable presence for aid to be delivered. This current level of access allows for a window of opportunity to reach a large number of venerable people, many of whom have been displaced multiple times since the start of the conflict.According to the Cadre Harmonisé Result prepared 11th March, 2020, the increase in displaced population due to the recent sporadic attacks by the non-state armed groups (NSAG) in the northeast states of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe has continued to negatively impact on livelihoods, and consequently on food and nutrition insecurity. The results have also projected that, of the total number of people in need of emergency assistance during the current period in Borno State, 314,047 are currently situated in completely inaccessible LGAs or inaccessible communities of partially accessible LGAs and humanitarian agencies lack access to these people. In line with the Nutrition Sector strategy, the Nutrition intervention will seek to reach populations that are the most critical in terms of malnutrition and food insecurity. The Nutrition intervention proposed by Caritas Nigeria will complement other ongoing emergency/recovery programs in the target project location. Additionally, the populations in target LGAs are particularly exposed to hunger, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Cholera outbreaks due to the low immunity from non-healthy feeding habits, low levels of hygiene and sanitation. During the coming months this risk will only increase as the rainy season escalates, raising the potential need for standby treatment points especially at stabilization centers for SAM children.Community Management of Acute Malnutrition, CMAM strategy for this response has been designed based on the understanding that populations in some target areas could quickly become inaccessible or subsequently displaced due further insecurity or other unforeseen events such as low food supply and flooding. The proposed Nutrition intervention will have a directed approach on providing the capacity and knowledge at community level for Community Health Extension Workers, CHEWs and Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs) for managing community-based care as needed, counselling and effectively running CMAM programmes through administration of RUTF, MNP and other necessary health tips to community members and caregivers. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Caritas Nigeria have Child Protection policies which is aimed at protecting the child from all forms of abuses. We practice a zero tolerance to child abuse and exploitation. In our response, we will conduct Child Protection assessment risks and have in place a mitigation plan. All our new staff and partner staff and volunteers will be screened at recruitment for Child Protection risks and asked Child Protection related questions in interviews. Caritas Nigeria will provide Child Protection training to all staff and volunteers and ensure they sign the Child Protection Code of Conduct. Caritas Nigeria have confidential reporting procedures in place for allegations of child abuse and exploitation, which will be clearly communicated to staff, volunteers and beneficiaries. For child rights more broadly, measures will include provision of safe spaces for children and women; provision of counselling, psychosocial support and referrals for children; facilitation of access to health and education services for children; and provision of IEC material accessible to children. The practical measures that will continue to address gender mainstreaming, equity and sustainability measures in this programme include: establishment of women support groups (Women’s club) and father groups for exchange ideas and information as well as serve to encourage one another on ways of improving their children/family nutrition. Establishment of breast feeding corners in health facilities to encourage pregnant and lactating women practise exclusive breast feeding.We will raise awareness among men through town hall meetings, house visits, speaking to men groups during their meetings, social or religious gatherings, on the importance of exclusive breast feeding for their babies and preparation of nutritious meals for their infants and young children so as to avoid the grave danger of malnutrition in their children. This awareness creation will serve to support, encourage and mobilise men to serve as a support to each other and their partners to initiate exclusive breast feeding for the first six months, and to support their households to take into cognisance the importance of good nutrition. This programme will be very sustainable in that it will teach the various groups within the target communities on how best to use locally available food items to prepare nutritious meals which will help to stem future malnutrition among infant and young children and other members of the communities. Zero tolerance to abuse and exploitation of women and children will also be included in MOU between our partners.Caritas Nigeria will also ensure the minimum package for protection mainstreaming in Nutrition is implemented in accordance with global and national standards for protection. This includes the following actions:1. Identify local authorities responsible, strengthen and support their role where possible2. Assess whether inequitable access to nutritional needs either at community or facility level is causing tension or conflict3. Make sure that the beneficiaries are aware of their rights to equitable nutritional care and services4. Monitor whether any individuals or groups control services and/or discriminate against certain individuals or groups gaining access to facilities 5. Include representatives of different groups in participatory processes and committees, mother to mother support groups and father to father support groups

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback and complaints actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction.Caritas Nigeria has a robust strategy that enable persons of concerns to complain any form of dissatisfaction through several mediums like patient satisfaction monitoring, a free toll line for phone calls, customized email address and suggestion boxes.This mechanism is designed to be easily obtainable by all including the most excluded and marginalized persons in Communities. There are also special considerations to illiterates, poor and the most vulnerable through the provision of safe feedback channels.To ensure effectiveness of the mechanisms, all parties involved are carried along from the design stage to implementation and review. Caritas Nigeria works with local partners to ensure provision of compliment and complaints channels suitable for them.All complaints and compliments are handled in a fair and unbiased manner. Conflicts of interest will be identified so as to ensure justice and fairness. Therefore, any staff member(s) whose conduct or service is being complained about will not be part of the team handling the complaint. All feedback received will be treated respectfully and with utmost privacy. This is to encourage all project participants, vendors and relevant Stakeholders report any concerns, compliments and complaints without fear of reprisal. This mechanism is designed in such a way that the aggrieved party does not have any right to retaliate against the Complainant. It incorporates ways of preventing harassment and harm to those that wish to raise any issue.
