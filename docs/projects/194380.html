Project Summary

Latest IPC November 2022 projects at least 6.64 million people in the country are facing severe food insecurity with the number expected to rise to 7.76 million as at July 2023. This has been attributed to climatic shocks (floods and dry spells), protracted conflict, low agricultural production, pests and disease in addition to access constraints more so in the lean season (IPC November 2022). To address this, Pilgrims of Hope's intervention that will target 10,000 HHs - 20,000 girls; 20,000 boys; 7500 men; 7500 women; 2500 elderly men and 2500 elderly women; will utilize an integrated approach to address the food security and livelihoods needs of the population in the two States of Central Equatoria (Kajokeji and Juba counties) and Unity States in (Panyijiar, Mayendit and Leer counties). The main overall objective of the interventions will be to reduce food insecurity of the above selected Counties sustainably and having as main targets food insecure communities classified in IPC phase 3 and above with special attention to most vulnerable including those with disabilities and elderly, child headed households, IDPs and returnees. FSL service delivery will integrate emergency, peace building and resilience building, aiming to strengthen the productive capacities of agricultural communities in order to enhance overall production. while targeting the most vulnerable by integrating them into active roles within more-developed groups and production systems. Activities will target both the local institutions [with a strong component of capacity building and resilience building] and the local communities [with direct support, capacity building and access to improved agricultural inputs - crop, vegetable and fishing inputs and tools coupled with capacity building training. At the institutional level, the interventions will focus on creating and strengthening monitoring systems during implementation so that IDPs/returnees and ‘host’ communities are both considered in order to avoid conflict. POH will ensure that, activities focus on their integration/interaction through linkages with other clusters - Nutrition, Health, WASH for holistic outcomes. Special attention will be given to climate change adaptation and climate smart agricultural practices with the aim of fostering a sustainable and agro-ecological production systems and fishing methods that will as well foster preservation of natural resources. Accountability to affected population (AAP), protection, conflict sensitivity, equitable gender representation and participation of men, women, boys, girls, youth including those with disabilities will be mainstreamed.

1 - Needs

Latest IPC November 2022 projects at least 6.64 million people in the country are facing severe food insecurity with the number expected to rise to 7.76 million as at July 2023. This has been attributed to climatic shocks (floods and dry spells), protracted conflict, low agricultural production, pests and disease in addition to access constraints more so in the lean season (IPC November 2022). Most vulnerable girls, boys, men and women in Central Equatoria and Unity continue to grapple with multiple shocks, which have been characterized by extreme flooding and cyclical waves of sub-national violence coupled with revenge attacks starting late 2021 with at least 42,000 people displaced (mainly girls, boys and women and people with disabilities) displaced in Southern Unity. These have been displaced from Adok, Kaigai, Pilieny, Rubkuai, Thonyor, Guat among others with some fleeing into Leer TPA, Muom, Leer Primary School, Juong and other makeshift displacement sites in addition to neighbouring Counties; coupled with armed group movements in parts of Central Equatoria and farmer-herder conflicts; in addition to destruction of critical basic support amenities including humanitarian assets and supplies in Leer (IRNA Leer April 2022; OCHA 2022 Updates). Displaced populations have had to grapple with deteriorating access to food and livelihood activities among other basic support amenities, more so among children (girls and boys), with most vulnerable being those with disabilities. Deteriorating economic conditions, inflation with high food and other basic commodities prices further impacts people’s access to food, in market dependent counties in Central Equatoria, and flood waters further limiting livelihood activities in Unity State (JMMI 2022 Updates). The current food insecurity in the target ears continue to negatively impacts people’s health and nutritional well being, and their ability to carry out livelihood activities. Recent assessments including REACH 2022 overview reports projects that Leer, Panyijiar and Mayendit will have significant populations that have slipped back to IPC phase 3 and 4 in addition to high Prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition for children between 6 and 59 months, above 15 percent. The plight calls for timely life-saving and life -sustaining Food security support through support of crop, vegetable and fishing livelihood kits and tools and capacity building through training. Proposed actions ill in addition establish a nexus between Humanitarian response, Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programming. Pilgrims of Hope has in addition all staff signed a code of conduct that includes Prevention from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment, in addition to having measures in place for reporting cases through local focal points and contact details for confidential reporting. Proposed actions will also ensure that actions have an environmental mainstreaming component, use a do no harm approach, AAP, protection, gender and disability inclusion including facilities that will ensure people with disabilities have access to proposed services, in addition to gender sensitivity where key activities will be gender sensitive.

2 - Project activities

Provision of vegetable, fishing and crop livelihood kits in the main season targeting 10000 HHs – these will be prepositioned through FAO pipeline. Provision of evidence base technical guidance to beneficiaries on agronomic practices and setting of 20 kitchen/ demonstration plots at Payam level or OTP sites for nutrition sensitive agriculture. Training of fisher folks on appropriate fishing techniques and post harvest handling. Post distribution monitoring/ post harvest assessments. Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed in Central Equatoria; seed security and multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality. Natural Resource management and agro forestry - Formation and training of natural resource management committee. - Establishment of fruit tree seedling. - Conducting community level sensitization on natural resource mapping and utilization with minimal conflict. - Encouraging use of improved cooking stove reducing the amount of emission into the atmosphere and training on Agroforestry technics.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

With protection, POH will work in collaboration with other actors including UN agencies, INGOS and NNGOs, FBOs and CBOs that are involved in child protection issues to safeguard the delivery of protection services in the schools and communities within these locations. POH will be accountable to the beneficiaries by ensuring that they participate in the project stages beginning from the planning, implementation, and then evaluation phase. In addition, appropriate and preferred mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints will be set up. On regularly or daily basis, POH conducts community engagement meetings that includes awareness campaigns, innovative youth events that can allow open and equal participation over the quality of progress of the activity being conducted. POH embrace a last-mile approach through field-based personal and daily visits to the field and conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which later are analyzed to distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention and beneficiaries barriers to access services. It is important that projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The continuous presence that POH has in the communities targeted by the intervention and local Staff recruited locally among people recognized by the communities as focal points in every location and also including senior positions working in the field and being used extensively and this ensuring that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the project cycles. All project’s beneficiaries (female and male IDPs and returnees’ children, CWDs and host communities’ children) will be considered as capable and active resources rather than as passive recipients who do not have a say in the project. It is therefore very vital applying localized approach with POH staff regularly present and usually coming from the same community of project locations, and with such, accountability is pursued also directly through its staff that can act as bridge between top management and beneficiaries. Besides this, considering the purposely field-oriented management team (officers and also project managers are field based), beneficiaries and affected population at large as constant possibility to interact and file complain to staff at different level. quarterly, POH conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, whereby the county and payam officials and traditional leaders Boma and sub Boma Chiefs forms the basis for community representation. In such meetings, POH explains to the community its strategic plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. POH field extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, do establish a case specific dialog where POHs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in the communities in order to evaluate possible challenges and intervention failure chance if any. Beside the surveys, POH’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings of which they provide platform to address issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can find the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported and such do increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Pilgrims of Hope staff have signed code of conduct in relation to PSEAH in addition to information dissemination on protection of women, girls, boys and men on PSEAH including ensuring key reporting channles are instituted at field level by male and female manned desks. More importantly, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at service access points (distribution points and at the community). But also ensuring that, safety audits are conducted and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of beneficiaries and reducing risks. Community members, staff and stakeholder’s capacity will be strengthened through specific PSEA trainings accordingly.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

POH has in place a tested and working system of four years multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 300 questions (including question on FSL, nutrition, GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a good number of interviews more than 300 HHs that are capable of providing detailed reference at the Counties and Payam levels, an analysis centered on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge becomes the internal foundations by POH staff and thus the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. Finally, the coordination bases that POH has across the targeted communities are equipped with phones of hot-lines, suggestion boxes; focal point protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complaints whenever it occurs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

POH will ensure initial involvement of women and local women groups representatives in AAP committees and during the planning activities implementation; at project implementation they will be provided with information about available GBV services and collecting feedback from women and girls about their needs/safety concerns; at the time where community workers are going door to door where covid -19 pandemic is still active, the project will ensure CNVs are trained/oriented on how to safely respond to a disclosure and that they have the most up-to-date referral pathways. With support and coordination with GBV specialists from available local actors, household field screenings will be used as a modality to proactively disseminate information on available GBV services. Workers and volunteers will be equipped with pocket cards containing relevant contact information; Representations of the GBV referral pathways will be posted in all focal points possible. POH has managed to install suggestion boxes in all sites, and appropriate, there has been a few feedbacks being received because majority of beneficiaries are illiterate (not able to write). Beneficiaries access to information in form of communicating complaints and receiving feedback will be enhanced through formation of AAP committees at Boma level with representatives from each village who will be in touch with beneficiaries to receive such complaints and feedback. POH will meet with the AAP committee to discuss on the complaints and feedback from time to time preferably twice in a Month, on quarterly basis and there will be a help desk at field sites attended by one personnel from POH at a higher position to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries accordingly.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will contribute to the collective outcome 3 acting on different levels. The emergency activities will focus on the most vulnerable especially of Unity State, among which flood related IDPs represent a priority group; these activities, such as emergency distribution of agricultural inputs and basic trainings, will help such flood affected population in finding relief from the emergency situation while building the basis for a new system of self-reliance. Along with this, POH will work with such Host Communities to strengthen the support network that take care of those displaced ensuring they have all the support needed. POH intervention aims at building the strong collaboration between host communities and displaced people as a synergic tool to ensure the growth of the whole community at large and the reduction of internal conflicts over the use of natural resources.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Pilgrims of Hope shall ensure that the assistance during the project does not harm the safety, dignity and integrity of women, men, girls and boys receiving as beneficiaries, and is provided in a way that respect their rights. Protection sensitive services will as well be mainstream in the project, such includes: facilitation of child stimulation and early child development in all nutrition sites, GBV/PSEA awareness to workers and beneficiaries, referral of GBV cases to access service at the management sites. And also making sure trainings targets at least an equal number of female and male, that such monitoring includes sex and age disaggregated data, that the project personnel is trained on gender/protection topics including GBV identification, handling and referral. ensuring equal participation through the involvement of women and men, equally in decision making right up from induction meetings, and routine focus groups discussions. For instance, Inclusive selection process with set % for specific population components typically victims of inequalities (min 60% of females in project implementation, inclusion of elderly and people with special needs, and returnees). POH’s projects include activities of livelihood and capacity building especially tailored to women, individually and as women group, and is matched by educational projects that always include GBV and peace building sensitization for pupils and training on GBV and referral pathways for teachers and PTA/SMCs members. And to avoid crowding of beneficiaries per sites, POH operates through continued bases available across the targeted communities and serves an adequate number of service sites equally distributed though out the counties, and this reducing distance to access services, and thus diminish the gap for remote, inaccessible communities. POH have had gender & protection officers, cultured to the analysis, supervision and adjustment of activities from the gender perspective accordingly.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Particularly in Central Equatoria State, the activities will focus much in the field of food security and livelihoods with the main aim of reducing the proportion of population facing famine, emergency and or crisis situations. The activities will be oriented to delivering basic agricultural assistance to the most vulnerable while building the capacity of the communities towards an ever-growing resilience and independence from humanitarian aid. Working to improve on production and utilization of nutritious locally available food at household for optimal MIYC feeding, interventions will integrate nutrition to food security component by establishing sustainable solutions as demonstration kitchen gardens at household level, at nutrition sites, in schools and churches among other institutions. Continuation in engaging in cereals largescale production through progressive farmers’ groups and enhancing the mechanization of cereal production in the targeted areas with active farmers who have large coverage of ready and well prepared land under cultivation and supporting in production management and post-harvest methods. Facilitating in cooking demonstration to enlighten the mothers, fathers, and caregivers on preparation of nutritious foods harvested from the kitchen gardens and encouraged to replicate the same at home is another priority. Mothers and caretakers of children with acute malnutrition will be link to community general food assistant to improve on household food security. People with chronic illnesses and mothers of children admitted in SC for management of severe acute malnutrition will be link to institution feeding program to receive food assistance accordingly.
