Project Summary

The north east region has been faced with the Boko Haram insurgency for over a decade. With huge displacement and high mortality of the citizens, this conflict has dragged for so long and the copying mechanisms of the affected population mostly in camps and host communities has reached breaking points. Over 1 million people still remain displaced in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states.
With slow recovery of livelihoods for the displaced and other victims of the insurgency in return and safe locations, the impact of their insurgency was slowly being addressed mainly through the efforts of international humanitarian and development agencies working with state and non state partners. However in late March 2020, the dreaded Covid-19 pandemic hit the world. With over 2 million people infected and over 200,000 fatalities, the Covid-19 has no cure nor a vaccine to stop its spread. Nigeria like other nations in Africa lack a functional health system to support the affected populations, in particular in conflict ravaged north eastern states. The state government approach to containment through social distancing and lockdown make matters worse for the affected population, whose access to life saving commodities and livelihoods is now limited.
The Covid-19 pandemic brings in a new set of challenges to the affected populations and in other instances it worsens the impact of other difficulties faced by the affected populations. These challenges for the affected populations in urban areas that is different to those in rural areas include:
1. Increased risk of the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic due to a lack of correct information and messaging to the right audience.
2. Decreased access to livelihoods to meet daily need due to lockdown
3. Increased tension and friction amongst the affected population due to over crowding in camps and host communities and lack of Covid-19 palliative
Our intervention aims to address these challenges through the underlisted objectives:
Objective 1: Prevention of spread of Corona virus infections through social media campaign with adolescent youths
Objective 2: Provision of livelihoods support to affected population through training and empowerment to enable them make an income from making and selling Covid 19 protection materials
Objective 3: Provision of livelihoods support through cash transfer to affected adolescent girls and boys to promote social cohesion in their communities through social media messaging

1 - Needs

The Covid-19 pandemic is spreading rapidly all over the world. In Nigeria the case load as reported by the Nigeria centres for disease control (NCDC) is now over 4000 persons infected as at the May 15, 2020. Within Nigeria the number of reported cases for major cities are: Lagos, 2099; Kano, 753; Abuja, 379; Borno, 191; Yobe, 32 ; Adamawa, 21In areas where the affected population live in the three BAY states, the prevention strategies for Covid-19 are difficult to implement. In most of the IDP camps and some host communities, there is serious over crowding. According to the DMS/CCCM most recent assessments of 2020, over 100,000 IDPs of the over 400,000 IDPs live in highly congested space of less that 15 square metres. Social spacing here is not an option and with reduced incomes due to lockdown, the ability to purchase other Covid-19 prevention supplies such as face mask and washing liquid/soap is limited.To address these needs the goal of the UN Strategic Response Multi Sectoral Response Plan of April 2020, for recovery strategy is to re-establish the conditions for a quick return to a path of economic growth, improved social contract, and overall human development that can foster more inclusive societies in the future. Under this goal, AUN activities will address strategic priority 2 and strategic priority 3.In this regard, affected populations needs in urban communities in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states that will be addressed are either immediate (0-3 months) and or mid term (3-6 months. 2 - Activities

AUN activities respond to the needs under priority 2 and priority 3. These activities are: Priority 2: Stop the deterioration of the existing conditions to save human lives, infrastructure, social cohesion and livelihoods in conflict affected areas.1. Cash transfer support to 4,000 households (2000 in Borno and 1000 each in Adamawa and Yobe states). Vulnerable households in urban camps/host communities will selected from UN registers of households already screened to receive support. Each head of household who has lost source of livelihoods due to Covid-19 will receive N10,000/month. Each youth who has lost source of livelihoods due to Covid-19 will receive N5000/month for three months. The funds will be used for purchase of food items and an agreement will be made with suppliers who will provide goods to affected populations.2. Immediate (0-3 months) - Mass telecommunications through social media to raise awareness of Covid-19 prevention measures for urban youths to control the spread of the pandemic. AUN will develop and adapt Covid-19 content and messaging for prevention including translating them into Kanuri, Fulfude, Hausa etc. languages. 3000 urban youths from activity 1 above will be targeted and formed into 300 groups. Each group will access the content through social media. Each youth will be provided with a monthly airtime of N2000/month for three months for data to use the internet. Data will be transferred directly to youth mobile number. Each youth group will plan and host three monthly Covid-19 information dissemination campaign within their communities. Groups will be provided with Covid-19, IEC materials and a small fund for hosting the community event. The 300 groups will reach a minimum of 50 community members/campaign. In three months, the youths will reach 45,000 community members with Covid-19 prevention messaging3. Provide livelihoods kits for vocational training center beneficiaries whose skills and technical training programs are likely to disrupted due to closure of training Centers. 1000 heads of households and 1000 youths whose livelihoods has been affected by Covid-19 either lock down or sickness or death of a household member, will receive training in tailoring, soap making, carpentry, animal rearing, and other small and micro business. The HHs and youths will receive training in the selected skills area they have chosen and for which they have been assessed as capable of learning. Trainings will last between one week to 8 weeks. At the successful completion of training including 5 days of apprenticeship to master the trade, the HHs and youths will receive between N40,000 - N80,000 in the form of starter packs and business startup expenses. AUN will deploy its monitors to visit the beneficiaries at their business sites and provide additional advice and support.Priority 3: Protect, assist and advocate for IDPs, returnees and host communities particularly vulnerable and exposed to the pandemic. 1. Immediate (0-3 months)- Engaging and orientation of community volunteers for continued service delivery- the 1000 youths who had not started any business before Covid-19 and were part of the cash transfer beneficiaries activity 2 above, will receive training on Covid 19 prevention and managing conflict mitigation within communities. The youths will be engaged to promote peace in addition to Covid-19 information They will also be trained on how to conduct social cohesion assessments within their own communities and come up with drivers of conflict. After the assessments have been completed, AUN counsellors will work with the youths to come up with strategies including IEC materials in local language to address the drivers of conflict they have identified.Youths in groups will then hold community engagement peace and GBV brainstorming session for youths and other adults. in groups of 5 members each. 200 groups will reach 60 community members over 3 months for a total of 12,000.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Identification of beneficiary will be with active involvement of community leaders and other gate keepers. Program design will be share with community leaders during advocacy visits to get their feedback and advice, where necessary, such feed back will lead to revision of approach.At beneficiary levels, the social media engagement is an effective means of getting feedback, we will initiate online client satisfaction surveys on quality of service, quantity and timeliness of service etc. The feedback will inform project management on what is working and what needs to be reviewed. On the side of those who are not active on social media, we will make use of pre and post assessments during training and in local language to get their feedback. These feedback will also reach project management for needed actions.On a semi annual basis, senior project management staff will take part in online questions and answer sessions where beneficiaries will be encouraged to send in their feedback. Similarly senior project management will attend on a quarterly basis various training sessions and also address questions and concerns from beneficiaries
