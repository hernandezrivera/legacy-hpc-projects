Project Summary

This Livelihood project activities are designed to promote an inclusive market system that enhances agriculture productivity, income generation and poverty alleviation in households, especially households with Persons of Concern (PoC). During the implementation of this project, INTERSOS plans to achieve the expected project outcome by focusing on these thematic areas, namely: 1. Inclusive Financial and Market system, 2. Women and Youth Economic Empowerment, and 3. Agriculture and Food Security. This project focuses on addressing the life-sustaining needs of the affected population in Borno State.
This proposed project will hopefully lead to a reduction of the dependency on emergency food assistance and will create an opportunity for economic revamp in these communities.
INTERSOS plans to train the targeted beneficiaries on skills acquisition and business management skills on their chosen Livelihood activities. INTERSOS will support them in managing their business to ensure it is profitable and has a positive impact on the household Livelihood Change status. INTERSOS will apply the Minimum Early Recovery Standards (MERS) in delivering the project at all its locations. INTERSOS will work with the LGA authorities and existing markets and financial institutions in the community, ensuring project beneficiaries' inclusion and project sustainability. Based on the same sustainability-oriented approach, the LEI beneficiaries will also be connected with locally based women and youth groups to encourage participation and sharing of information. INTERSOS will be will plan for participatory planning sessions in the communities at the start of the program implementation. This is to get the community involved in the planning of the project activities
As part of its operational approach, INTERSOS has a strong commitment to monitoring and evaluation. These include an organizational focal point for MEAL activities, whose role is to train, advise and develop organizational approaches, as well as liaise with other INGOs to share experiences, lessons learned and best practices. INTERSOS uses a robust internal tool called PAT (Project Appraisal Tool) to monitor the project implementation level. The PAT is filled at the end of every month by the MEAL team and reviewed by the Project Manager and Coordinators to track the level of achievements and expenditure, identify gaps and take appropriate corrective actions. The field-based project staff, together with MEAL officer assigned to the project will be responsible for the day-to-day collection of raw data from the field. This data will be validated by M&E Manager, Livelihood Specialist, Project Manager and Coordinators for accuracy and completeness. INTERSOS will harness its resources within and outside the country to deliver this project, It will not be engaging any third party in the delivery of this project.
The project goal is to contribute towards restoring livelihoods of the IDPs and returnees in the BAY state of Nigeria, through introduction of early recovery livelihoods and Economy inclusion activities to support IDPs and returnees to generate income for self-reliance.
Specific Objective 1 – Addressing key underlying barriers of the POCs to livelihoods and Economic Inclusion opportunities, such as poverty and unemployment, through provision of training and capacity-building activities for the POCs.
The proposed livelihood and economy inclusion activities have mutually reinforced and integrated technology concepts, a Business toolkit which will include entrepreneurship skills development, business management training, market system enhancement support and business advisory services to strengthen the sustainability of their livelihood restoration.
Specific objective 2 – Provision of innovative, profitable, and sustainable livelihood opportunities, that would restore households’ livelihood capacity and productive assets of the POCs to enhance their economic inclusion in the communities.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in the BAY states continues to persist despite several interventions and is expected to persist unabated in 2023: the continuing conflict will still severely affect millions of people in 2023, leading to more uncertainties on displacement patterns and unpredictable attacks. With the continued worsening of the security situation, there will be more populations that will be in dire need of humanitarian assistance in 2023. It is estimated that more than 1.44 million people including women, children, and those living with disabilities have been displaced as a result of the conflict that has raged for over two decades, since 2009. The continued multiple displacements and confinement of people in camps, coupled with restricted movements of host communities is crippling local capacities to meet essential survival needs in health, nutrition, WaSH, protection, and livelihoods without external humanitarian support. Whereas the prospects for displaced people’s safe return to their areas of origin are perceived as possible with some areas of origin looking conducive for return or could be made conducive for return, the major challenge remains the unpredictability of the OAGs.According to the Cadre Harmonize released in October 2023, indicated that 18.6 million (8.9%) of the analyzed populations are in the critical (Crisis to Emergency) Phases of FNI. Going into the lean season (June to August 2024) FNI situation may deteriorate which may increase the number of vulnerable populations to 26.5 million (12.6%) unless conscious actions are initiated and implemented to de-escalate vulnerabilities through targeted humanitarian food aids and resilience livelihood support interventions in the worst affected areas. The continued hostility and unpredictable attacks by the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) is expected to make a significant increase in the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022/23. The situation is further aggravated by the congestion and overcrowding of camps following the ongoing massive dissolution of camps in Maiduguri. Besides, unforeseen shocks and threats from natural hazards such as floods and disease outbreaks (e.g., malaria, cholera, diphtheria, etc.,). The continued multiple displacements and confinement of people in camps, coupled with restricted movements of host communities is crippling local capacities to meet essential survival needs in livelihoods without external humanitarian support.

2 - Activities

INTERSOS intends to build on the current ERMS activities, which were successfully started in 2019. INTERSOS will continue to address the recovery needs that arose from the Boko Haram insurgent actions in North-eastern Nigeria, using its standard humanitarian mechanism that aligns with developmental principles. INTERSOS will motivate the affected population to use the benefit of its humanitarian actions to seize development opportunities and build their resilience, to establish a sustainable process of recovery from the crisis. INTERSOS will embrace multisectoral, integrated, and conflict-sensitive modalities and approaches aimed at saving lives and improving the living conditions of crisis-affected populations to facilitate early recovery and resilience where possible.The proposed livelihood and economy inclusion activities have mutually reinforced and integrated technology concepts, Business toolkit which will include entrepreneurship skills development, business management training, market system enhancement support and business advisory services to strengthen the sustainability of their livelihood restoration.INTERSOS experience, capacity and lesson-learnt on the market and financial systems in the past and ongoing livelihood projects in the BAY states will be brought on, to ensure this project mitigates economic inclusion barriers for the PoCs. INTERSOS will further enhance the Livelihood capacity of the PoC, through the introduction of new income-generation activities product designs and innovative product marketing systems. Critical to this project is ensuring the key project outcomes are achieved, specifically, INTERSOS will work towards achieving the following outcomes: 1. Ensure targeted households attain a sustainable resilience level through engagement in diversified livelihood activities. 2. Ensure targeted households have an increased ability to access the market and financial institutions. 3. Ensure target households' ability to gain useful employment in the community is increased through the planned training and capacity-building activities. During this project, INTERSOS will aim to address some of the underlying causes of food insecurity (such as poverty and unemployment) through capacity building, income-generating activities and enhancing the marketability of the produced items. This project aims to move towards an integrated protection and early recovery response by building on existing structures in these locations, our experience and lesson learnt. INTERSOS will tailor this Livelihood assistance to the most vulnerable survivors, especially women in the most secure and appropriate manner to provide resilience-building support.INTERSOS will work to build the resilience of the targeted survivors by starting and effectively managing Income Generating Activities in the community. Furthermore, INTERSOS plans to train the targeted beneficiaries on business management skills and support them in managing their business to ensure it is profitable, and it enhances their livelihood. INTERSOS will support the beneficiaries with the provision of start-up kits and grants. The start-up grant is aimed to support the targeted beneficiaries on the operational cost of the ERMS activities. INTERSOS aims to address the economic and social recovery needs of the selected survivors and their households.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All groups including women, children and elderly are engaged in gathering data on their specific needs. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored into the programme design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. The programmes include estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. According to the periodic reports from UNFPA and UNHCR, GBV is a prevailing protection risk in Borno State emanating from gender inequality and unbalanced power relationships. Women and girls are predominantly affected due to their gender while other factors such as disability, mental health, age, and marital status heighten vulnerability and exposure to shock. Some of the risk factors for GBV and general protection include lack of awareness among community members and perpetrators. GBV and protection issues are high in crowded IDP camps with weakened social systems and poor living conditions which are conducive environments for perpetrators of violence. Women constitute above half of IDPs, and a significant percentage are also children. Older and disabled persons are equally at higher risks due to social degeneration and loss of social responsibility.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

INTERSOS embraces participatory community-led approaches, that involve target beneficiaries, INTERSOS Will work with Project Management Committees (PMCs), key local leaders and local authorities for a good understanding of the project objectives, and the expected contributions from the project implementation in the community. Community entry messaging include information like source of funding (donor), selection criteria, targeting criteria and expected humanitarian support among others. As part of the CHS commitments, INTERSOS establishes functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. Compliant help desks are also set up in INTERSOS-supported safe spaces, where people can report their complaints or give feedback about the project in a confidential manner. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including: face to face with field staff, WhatsApp, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. These information is sent to the INTERSOS office in Maiduguri, where they are managed by the INTERSOS internal control and accountability unit. In addition to the aforementioned set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls, is at the base of the project design. Since the onset of INTERSOS action in the region, it has become well-known and respected within the targeted communities, positively engaging with both the hosting and IDP communities. INTERSOS conducts regular focus group discussions with target beneficiaries to improve its programming, taking into consideration the suggestions coming directly from the affected communities.
