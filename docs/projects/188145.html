Project Summary

The action aims to respond to immediate and priority needs in WASH, Health and Protection for the conflict-affected population in Azaz and Idlib, Northwest Syria. Over a period of 12 months, the action will be implemented in coordination with communities and local authorities. In total, the project will target 272,078 beneficiaries (141,663 women and 130,415 men) who will benefit from a package of actions with a focus on Wash in Azaz and based on an integrated approach in Idlib.
This project is aligned with ECHO's 2022 HIP to address the life-saving and protection concerns of the most vulnerable people inside Syria, as well as the priorities of the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for Syria. Beyond continuity with previous intervention in Azaz and Idlib, the need to implement this project is justified by : The activities will ensure a better coverage of the needs related to the continuous deterioration of security and post Covid 19- economic crisis. The proposed action takes into account the additional needs identified in Idlib, where SI will extend its current humanitarian response. Through this response, SI expects to strengthen access to water, hygiene and sanitation services for targeted communities

4 - Needs

WASH : According to the HNO 2021, 12.2 million of people are in need of WASH assistance. In Azaz and Idlib, water systems have suffered from damages owing to hostilities, years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance and poor water resources management. An SI assessment showed that 91% of beneficiaries were relying on NGO supported water supply in Azaz (48% for WT and 52% on water network). Sanitation : Azaz and Aghtrin sub-districts are categorized under WASH severity score 4 (which indicates, poor desludging, insufficient water, garbage left open and no hand washing observed, HNO 2022). An SI survey shows that almost all (99,7%) beneficiaries indicated that they had access to functional latrines but the results also indicate that beneficiaries are reluctant to use communal latrines or even private latrines shared between a few families. This survey highlighted the needs of installing private latrines rather than communal ones.SWM : the provision of waste removal services and bins has led to positive results in Azaz. KIIs conducted in June 2021 confirmed that there were changes of behavior in the way people deal with solid waste and improvement in the general hygiene of the community. Hygiene: the relevance of hygiene promotion sessions was confirmed by the endline survey (SI, June 2021), 89% of the beneficiaries declared that the hygiene promotion sessions led to a change of behavior amongst community members.

5 - Implementation of activities

Over a period of 12 months, SI will respond to immediate and priority needs in WASH, Health and Protection for the conflict-affected population in Azaz and Idlib, Syria.The action will be based on a flexible approach adapted to the evolving humanitarian needs on the ground. The project will focus onlife-saving activities for the vulnerable communities, planned as follow:Azaz: Focus in WASH to deliver safe drinking water, provide sanitation and hygiene services for targeted communitiesIdlib: Multi-sectorial approach (3 sectors) to cover the most urgent unmet WASH needs, provide quality health services, and prevent/respond to violences (Gender Based Violence -GBV and child protection-CP). SI will focus on full-service delivery and will adapt the project according to potential change in context or new needs that may arise. The intervention will have different impacts by covering beneficiaries needs (infrastructure and services), strengthening their capacities (skills and knowledge) and empowering them.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include: • Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across locations • Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees due to varied assistance packages • Abuse of power by staff distributing goods or delivering services to vulnerable populations. • Road accidents, physical harm • Restricted access (due to insecurity of COVID-19 lockdowns) • COVID-19 public health concerns.Mitigation measures for the identified risks are integrated in SI’s implementation strategy, starting with transparent selection criteria agreed upon in coordination with the relevant clusters. The risk of abuse of power is mitigated by thorough vetting of locally recruited staff, who all abide by the SI Code of Conduct. SI also has a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) in place that allows for real-time feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The FCRM is an accountability tool that simultaneously facilitates the collection of lessons learnt that will be used to improve future interventions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks:Gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), tends to increase during times of crisis. Risks of this occurring further increase in situations of aid dependency and power imbalances. SI is cognizant of the inherent risks related to service delivery, notably for cash and in-kind distributions. To ensure that assistance delivered is safe, relevant and timely, SI has accountability systems and procedures in place.Mitigation and prevention measures:SI is determined to prevent the sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers and reaffirms its goal of achieving full implementation of the six core principles adopted by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. SI's PSEA Policy complements the Code of Conduct and Protection Policy, both of which state that it is strictly forbidden for any employee i) to use its position to obtain any favor, including sexual favors, and ii) to have sexual intercourse with minors. SI’s contracts and partnership agreements include a standard clause requiring sub-contractors to adopt policies that prohibit SEA and to take adequate measures to prevent and respond when required. A Whistleblowing Policy and a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanisms Framework are key pillars of SOLIDARITES INTERNATIONAL Policies package. SI has PSEA focal points to identify and address any PSEA concerns and implements a referral system when require


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks, assumptions and mitigating measures:Assumptions:• Access to vulnerable population and vendors is not further reduced by additional border-crossings restrictions • Operations are not further limited by regulatory restrictions inside Syria • Safety and security of SI and partner staff is not compromised • SI is able to appropriately manage the risks of aid diversion to designated terrorist groups or through corruption and bribery • Exchange rates and material/service costs remain reasonably stable throughout implementation period of the project • Sufficient materials and skills required for the output are available in the targeted geographic areas• Construction companies with sufficient capacity are available inside Syria• Labour with sufficient technical skills is available inside Syria• Security situation allows regular sharing of information between stakeholders• Communication channels remain open and enable relevant monitoring methods• Payment mechanisms remain open for the reimbursement of suppliers• Inter-agency coordination mechanisms minimize the risk of duplication and variations in levels of assistance. Risks: Non-materialization of the assumptions mentioned aboveMitigation: • Use money transfer system (hawala) for operations in North Syria including for the payment of its staff and contractors • Cooperation with Turkish authorities and capacity to work Cross-Border from Turkey. • SI and IMC maintain coordination offices outside of NWS.
