Project Summary

The CHESS project will provide a dedicated medical treatment facility for the for moderate, severe and critical cases of COVID-19 infection and related illness among UN staff and dependents and “frontline” humanitarian workers in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. Under the project, the UN will contract an international healthcare services provider that has experience working with the United Nations in deep field settings to establish, staff and run a fifty (50) bed facility, with all required medical expertise, capacities and capabilities. UNHCR, WFP, IOM and UNICEF are working together to deliver the CHESS project as a joint programme and common service for the UN and broader humanitarian community.

Needs

"The United Nations agencies and humanitarian partners continue to deliver critical life-saving programmes in the Rohingya refugee settlements and nearby Bangladeshi communities, while also undertaking the intensive preparedness measures needed to respond to the risks posed by the COVID-19 pandemic. These include establishing Severe Acute Respiratory Infection Isolation and Treatment Centres (SARI ITCs), with new teams of personnel, and working to strengthen the public health infrastructure in Cox’s Bazar District. Working in these conditions exposes frontline UN and humanitarian workers to a substantial risk of COVID-19 infection. The new SARI ITCs will also bring significant numbers of new personnel into the operation. Inevitably colleagues will experience moderate, severe or critical illness. To Stay and Deliver, humanitarian personnel must be confident that medical treatment will be available, should they fall ill. The humanitarian operations should not either place additional strains on the District’s already overburdened public healthcare infrastructure. The CHESS Project will respond to the gap in medical treatment and care options in Cox’s Bazar for UN personnel and dependents and the frontline workers, whose contributions and continued health and wellbeing are critical to the success of the COVID-19 response. Treatment for moderate, severe and critical COVID-19 cases is currently not available, and the UN is not able to meet its Duty of Care.The Project will respond the moderate, severe and critical COVID-19 treatment requirements of a population of 6,500 UN personnel and dependents and frontline humanitarian workers, The UNHCR and WFP regional medical services have established that the requirement is for a fifty (50) bed COVID-19 facility with ten (10) Intensive Care Unit (ICU) beds, ten (10) High Dependency beds and thirty (30) Moderate to Low Dependency beds."

Activities or outputs

"Activity 1. Provide adequate medical support for moderate, severe and critical COVID-19 infection and related illnesses on a 24/7 basis for eight (8) months to 6500 UN personnel and dependents and frontline humanitarian workers, in order to enable to them to Stay and Deliver critical life-saving programmes and meet the UN’s Duty of Care. Output 1 – Deploy medical and support personnel and related capacities, equipment and supplies to operate the fifty (50) bed COVID-19 Medical Treatment Facility (MTF), with laboratory, pharmacy, radiology, morgue, etc., and administrative management.Output 2 – Establish and maintain the MTF infrastructure, including wards, facilities, water treatment and supply, sanitation, power, lighting, air-conditioning, ventilation, waste management and laundry and catering services, either through modification of an existing facility or construction of a purpose-built facility."

Key project/performance indicators

- Preparation of facilities to receive medical team - Preparation Target: 1 month - Phase 1 - Rapid Deployment Team (RDT) - Phase 1 Target: Medication Services Facility - 20 beds (2 ICU / 8 HDU / 10 M/LDU) (mobilisation in 15 days, deployment subject to travel restrictions) - Phase 2 – Full Operating Capability (FOC) - Phase 2 Target: Medication Services Facility - 50 beds (10 ICU / 10 HDU / 30 M/LDU) (mobilisation in 35 days, deployment subject to travel restrictions)
