Project Summary

MSJM aims to implement three Community Centers to foster Community Based Protection services and to reinforce the person’s of concern resilience to overcome the effects of the current crisis as well as ensuring the local social cohesion through adequate coping anti-risk awareness and related mechanism through protection programs and activities.
With this Project MSJM wants to fill the gap of the critical lack of Community Centers in the remoted areas of rural Aleppo, rural Raqqa and rural Deir Ez-Zor. We aim to open three New Community Centers that will be run by skilled team and will offer the whole range of Protection services to vulnerable persons, especially women, girls and children in special safe spaces dedicated to them.
The key objectives à our Community Centers are to provide to vulnerable people with GBV related services, proper PSS follow-up, awareness campaigns, protection based vocational training to fight violence, social tabous like children marriage or children labour, exclusion, inequality, social education to improve and increase their protection status. Each center will have a safe space for women, girls and children as reported later in this form.

Needs

The Syrian conflict inflicts multiple adverse effects upon the Syrian people. The violence displaces many families, leaving countless broken homes in its path. Due to the unprecedented violence in Syria, many families have lost their customary head of household, usually a male figure. The loss of either a husband, son, or brother has shifted the responsibility of the entire household to the women of Syria. Almost one in every three households in Syria are now women-headed. These Syrian women assume both the role of caretaker and breadwinner.The needs in the community are tremendous and include many serious protection risks. All three Provinces assessed present those protection issues in percentages varying from 15% to 65% of the persons of concern: 1. Child labour preventing school2. Child recruitment 3. Domestic violence 4. Early marriage 5. Economic Exploitation 6. Explosive hazards Common7. Family separation 8. Harassment 9. Housing/land/propert issue 10. Lack/loss of persol/civil docum11. Sexual harassment 12. Sexual violence Through discussion with the community, many concerns were raised including GBV issues, access to protection services and awareness campaign. In our close interactions with people in need, vulnerable women and adolescent girls stated the importance of educational skills to enhance their capacity of insertion. Following MSJM needs assessment in the targeted hard to reach areas, we can describe the situation as follow : Disintegration of the social network, a high percentage of women households (widows 12.5%, without the presence of males between 20% to 40%) Lack of safety and security: Women, girls and the community had been consulted in order to understand the security risks in the community, and which type of community support systems existed for women and girls before the crisis and actually. This part of the assessment helped us to determine the need for such centers. The vulnerable women and girls need a safe space where they feel physically and emotionally safe. The term ‘safe,’ in the present context, refers to the absence of trauma, excessive stress, violence (or fear of violence), or abuse. They need a space where women and girls, being the intended beneficiaries, feel comfortable and enjoy the freedom to express themselves without the fear of judgment or harm. The key objectives of a safe space are to provide an area where women and girls protection needs are met like :• Socialize and re-build their social networks;• Receive social support;• Acquire contextually relevant skills to bypass their vulnerability; Reduce the discrimination and helplessness because of social status and gender• Access safe and non-stigmatizing multi-sectorial GBV response services (psychosocial, legal, medical);• Receive information on issues relating to women’s rights, health, and services

Implementation of activities

A) Implementation1-Coordination with relative authorities to get approvals to open and run Community Centers with protected safe space. 2- rent and equip the three Community Centers3- Hire skilled staff and train them accordingly to compete with their new tasks. The staff consist of : Project Director – Center Coordinator - Accountant – Lawyer – Legal ORV – GBV Coordinator – GBV volunteer (2 to 4 depending on the density of beneficiaries) – 2 Case Management per Center -Reception and registration – Activities Facilitator – Education specialist –– Social workers volunteering - PSS specialist – 2 Drivers – a documentation issue facilitator – 2 Janitors. 4- A volunteering local committee is formed in the targeted area for the different activities. B) Activities.1- Running the Centers with reception, registration (the beneficiaries are vulnerable persons of all ages). 2- Safe space activities (only for women, girls and children of both sex): Awareness – Educational and Recreational activities Protection related vocational training – 3- Coping mechanism used in our Community Centers : Protection services: Pschosocial support services - Pschosocial support for survivors of sexual violence/domestic violence – Transient Medical treatments for survivors of sexual violence/domestic violence - Protection services for children - Recreational activities - Care mechanisms/services for elderly persons - Services for persons with disabilities 4- Case Management - Quality specialized GBV services and tailored measures to prevent and reduce GBV risks -Psychosocial counselling and consultation provided that do not need a mental health specialist.5- Providing hygiene and health accompaniment to GBV survivors with the utmost delicacy and dignity6- Outreach in the surrounds of the Community Center at least twice a week to offer special home care to the elders and to the persons with disabilities.7- Have one day to provide awareness and consultation for the whole family.8- Have three days per week in the afternoon at least for males to provide anti-GBV awareness and ad hoc PSS and GBV consultations.9- Protection based vocational training: Adopting a community-based approach, MSJM, following UNFPA minimum standard for prevention and response to protection issue, including GBV i.e., will focus on building the capacity of community-based structures to raise awareness on those issues in order to strengthen prevention and to be better able to support the persons of concern and, especially the GBV survivors. 10- Multi-sectoral GBV response services 11- Support to persons with specific protection needs through preventive empowerment vocational training with grants to the most vulnerable that have succeeded in the sessions and has offered a viable project that will enhance the protection status of the individual and of the family with impact on the community environment. Among the vocational training we list : literacy courses – language courses - alternative primary education - increasing the level of women's self-reliance to enhance their dignity and independence - Support generating activities such as agriculture, hand-made food industries, core relief items – special focus on people with disabilities to mitigate their status and offer them suited protection solutions. 12- Community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms that will disseminate information and organize awareness campaigns about GBV and protection issues with the participation of the local community as per the updated guidelines of the IASC.13- Regular refresher training/ mentoring for our staff14- Training sessions for humanitarian actors from the hosting communities and beneficiaries that have an impact on their local environment in terms of awareness, reducing risks and developing an active approach of GBV and related matters.15- M&E visits and reporting 16- Mid-term narrative reports. 17- Final narrative reports.
