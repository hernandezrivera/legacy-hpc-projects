Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services, to reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases, and to enhance resilience to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms among Vulnerable persons 14,857 (male 7428& female 7428) including Returnees/Internally Displaced Persons and host communities under Emergency (IPC;3), due to pressing circumstances including insecurity caused by intercommunal and cattle related conflicts, climate change flooding and drought, stress of food insecurity and essential service gaps. both counties have inadequate health infrastructure, underdeveloped and limited capacity of the health care systems, such as a shortage of qualified human resources for health, and inconsistent medical supplies, this exposes the vulnerable communities to negative coping strategies, (dependents on traditional doctors, excessive use of local herbs and late health care seeking behaviors)
Among the bad health indicators, the two countries of Terekeka recorded 25%, and Juba county; outside Payam (Tijor, Bungu, Gangi, and Northern Bariya )
Based on 2023 ISNO/HINO data it shows; 41 % of households had an under 5 passed away, 77% of households had Experienced health problems and needed healthcare in the past 3 months in Juba, 81 % of households were unable to access healthcare in the past 3 months in Terekeka 45 % of the HH suggested a shortage of Specific medicine, treatment needed unavailable, in both counties beside a Long waiting time for the service were reported, 49 % of HH said Health facility is too far away, only 32% of HH experience of having access to ANC service.
Therefore, the project will focus on the provision of essential emergency critical lifesaving healthcare services through static and mobile clinic teams amongst the displaced population within the host communities, Ono aid will recruit qualified staff locally and deploy to the sites, and employ Minimum Package for Health Interventions in Rapid Response mixture approaches, (Mobile health clinic Mechanism, and Temporary & Static PHCU).
Main project activities,
1) Health Systems and Clinical Support; the project team will be involved in the Provision of OPD consultations including diagnosis, treatment of common communicable, management of cases of SGBV (Clinical Management of rape) dressing of wounds, and support of lab services including the use of RDTs and referrals of cases for further management.
2) Conduct Epidemic disease surveillance, this will be done through general Health education and, immunization, screening of those under 5 for malnutrition with MUAC including rapid testing and Screening for Tetanus vaccination for pregnancy, vaccination needs to all under 5 and TB Screening, and providing referrals where necessary, this will reduce access to morbidity and mortality, in hard to reach. Weekly reporting on data collected to Health Clusters at both State and National levels on common morbidities like Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Measles, and COVID-19 reported through EWARS and IDSR.
3) Mother and Child Health. The team will provide maternal and child health care activities guided by MISP and BPHNS guidelines including; ANC services, post-natal care within 7 days, family planning, STI/HIV prevention and management, and GBV prevention & management, and Mama Kits are distributed, the midwife will conduct counseling on the birth preparedness plan and do screening of pregnant women and those found malnourished referred for nutritional services at the respective targeted Payams. immunization will be done twice a week every Monday and Thursday until the project period.
4) Community Health /BHI: The Home Health Promoters will provide at the community and household level twice a week every Monday and Friday.

1. Needs

Terekeka County and peripherals Payams of Juba County remain an extremely challenging environment to improve health indicators due to recurrent effects of climate change instigating unprecedented (flooding and drought), besides inter-communal conflicts causing looting and destruction of limited investment, paralyzes services delivery as well as economic activities. This is worsening the Already limited capacity, and coverage of health care services, compared to the huge health burden due to poor living conditions, food insecurity, and poverty this is supported by continuous humanitarian needs assessments conducted across the year First there was an IRNA assessment conducted on 29th -31st, MARCH 2023 in Tijor Payam of Juba County the finding shows; o Tijor PHCC has not functioning since 2016, due to a lack of essential drugs, medical equipment, and inadequate human resources.o the facility structure has broken doors, windows, ceiling boards, and a broken ground floor. the affected Community in general has no OPD/IPD, Laboratory, EPI, ANC, MCH care Services, o There is no disease surveillance in the community and referral system in place, therefore the following Morbidity and mortality were: recorded - Child Health: cases reported were 7 Measles, 30 Malaria, 40 diarrheal diseases 12 Watery diarrhea, and 19 Respiratory Tract Infections, Pneumonia, cough, and Skin infections, being reported therefore requiring immediate health responded - Mothers health /ANC: 2 Pregnancy related conditions, 2 malnourish conditions, - Disability conditions, (1 hearing, 3 walking, 4 visual impairments, and 5 speaking disorder) - Communicable diseases: 4 TB/HIV, STI, and 23 Eye disease cases - Neglected tropical diseases: lymphatic filariasis (elephantiasis), bilharziasis, and suspects of onchocerciasis (OV), Another IRNA assessment was conducted on 27th July 2023 in IN LURI-ROKWE, JUBA COUNTY; the report indicates more than 4000 vulnerable children, mothers, and elderly were displaced due to conflicts and were exposed to unfriendly weather (rain and head) with - Children reported with Measles, Malaria, and diarrheal infections with no health care services near - no plastic sheets to help them erect a shelter as they wait for their place of origin to normalize. - no sleeping mats, Mosquito nets, or Blankets. - to mention, source pots, cups, or plates. They only do share with others. - No NFI kits like soap, buckets, and Jerrycans. It limits them from practicing good hygiene. Another IRNA assessment was conducted in Terekeka County from the 14th to the 15th of September the findings show - There are limited Health Facilities and therefore limited coverage, leading to a huge health services gap among big populations missing services such as routine Immunization, Antenatal, OPD consultations … etc - Shortage of essential drugs in many of the functional health facilities- Several home deliveries were reported supported by unskilled birth attendants. A very risky practices - Poor access to clean water was reported all over the Payam, most of the boreholes in Reggo have dried up, and it's now a Risky water-borne disease with limited services. - Most of the facilities in Tali and Tindilo do not have cold chains and the vaccinators in those PHCUs usually walk for long distances to carry vaccines from Tali PHCC and this is done ones in a month. The facilities lack delivery kits; NO VCT center at Tali, Tindilo and tijor , Most facilities like in Kiu , tindilo, and others have no water reserves, 90% of the population in Tindilo, and Tali, was noted practicing widespread open defecation;, and weak EWARS.

2. Project activities

The project will focus on the provision of essential emergency critical lifesaving healthcare services through employing Minimum Package for Health Interventions in Rapid Response mixture approaches, (Mobile health clinic Mechanism, and Temporary/ Static PHCU). Main project activities, 1- Health Systems and Clinical Support the mobile health clinic teams will be involved in conducting OPD consultations including diagnosis, treatment of common communicable, management cases of SGBV (Clinical Management of rape) referrals of cases for further management, Nurses will provide basic nursing care, taking patient’s vital signs, dressing of wounds, establishing Oral Rehydration Corner and carry out vaccinations of children. The Lab Assistants will carry out malaria tests using RDTs in the current setting. The Dispenser’s role is to issue drugs to patients as prescribed by the Clinical officers. 2- Conduct Epidemic disease surveillance, this will be done through general Health education and mobilization for routine, immunization, screening of under 5 for malnutrition with MUAC and lactating women for malnutrition, including rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women as well as Screening for Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy, vaccination needs to all under 5 and TB Screening, the mobile health approach is employed to achieve rapid response to fragile health stations, communities under ( IPC 4) of this project areas to reach affected patients or provide referrals where necessary, this will reduce access to morbidity and mortality, in hard to reach.3- Communicable Diseases ONO AID will work to prevent, detect, and respond to infectious diseases including case management. In addition, the services are carried out through the mobile clinics’ sites. National MOH treatment guidelines will be used for case management. RDT Para-checks for malaria and syphilis will be done at mobile outreach sites. Weekly reporting on data collected to Health Clusters at both State and National levels on common morbidities like Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Measles, and COVID-19 reported through EWARS and IDSR. 4- Mother and Child Health. ONO AID will provide maternal and child health care activities guided by MISP and BPHNS of the National Ministry of Health. At each mobile clinic site, a midwife will provide ANC, skilled community deliveries, post-natal care within 7 days, family planning, STI/HIV prevention and management, and GBV prevention & management. ONO AID will ensure that other commodities like trauma and rape kits for the management of rape cases, family planning commodities, and Mama Kits are distributed, the midwife will conduct counseling on the birth preparedness plan and do screening of pregnant women for malnutrition during the ANC services and those found malnourished referred for nutritional services at the respective targeted Payams. immunization will be done twice a week every Monday and Thursday until the project period.5- Community Health /BHI: ONO AID mobile health teams will provide health education sessions at all established mobile clinic sites and in the communities. Community Health workers will provide at the established mobile clinic sites while the Home Health Promoters will provide at the community and household level twice a week every Monday and Friday. Community mobilizers in their respective locations will mobilize community members to utilize health care services and for adequate turn-up for efficient service uptake. necessary topics covered will be disease prevention and health promotion, ANC services, awareness rising on GBV issues, COVID-19 preventive measures, and personal hygiene.
