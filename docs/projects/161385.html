Project Summary

GOAL’s primary purpose for 2020 is to deliver quality life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk girls and boys 6-59 months and PLWs in Melut and Ulang counties, Greater Upper Nile. Protracted conflict, displacements and flooding in South Sudan have contributed to insufficient crop production, worsening levels of food insecurity and malnutrition throughout the country, with most areas categorized in IPC 4 and IPC 5, "Crisis and Emergency" phases of food insecurity. Communities in Melut and Ulang are particularly vulnerable to malnutrition; both counties were classified as IPC 4 and IPC 5 “crisis" and "emergency” phases of food insecurity as of September 2019, and this is projected to continue into the first quarter of 2020. Recent analysis indicates GAM rates of 21.7% (Melut) and 20.1% (Ulang), which are above the emergency threshold rates for treatment of malnutrition. To respond to these needs, GOAL will implement management of acute malnutrition in Melut and Ulang. Further, GOAL will provide MIYCN counselling services to prevent under-nutrition for most vulnerable and at-risk. GOAL will conduct enhanced nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response decision making and support of at-risk communities to sustain their capacity to cope with significant threats, while reinforcing protection, promote access to basic services, and integrate FSL and hygiene activities for the displaced and other vulnerable people. GOAL proposes to provide nutrition services through a network of 21 TSFP sites in Melut (7 sites) and Ulang (14 sites) Counties, where GOAL has been operational since 2014 and 2007 respectively; in Melut, these services will be integrated with GOAL’s health services through 2 clinics to provide a comprehensive package of care. GOAL will strengthen linkages between partners implementing management for severe acute malnutrition (SAM), referring identified SAM cases to OTP for treatment and receiving beneficiaries discharged from OTP into TSFP for continuum of care. GOAL will enhance integration across sectors to provide comprehensive services for beneficiaries. GOAL will focus on integration of food security and livelihoods (FSL) into nutrition services by linking TSFP beneficiaries to ongoing FSL programming to address drivers of poor health and nutrition in the community. GOAL will also incorporate integration of WASH, introducing WASH Fit at nutrition sites and ensuring facilities have adequate latrines and handwashing facilities. GOAL’s programming will focus on enhanced integration of protection into project activities to safeguard beneficiaries. GOAL health facilities in Melut are equipped to respond to and manage any cases of gender-based violence (GBV) presenting at the facilities; GOAL will also strengthen the referral mechanism to InterHealth, the Protection partner in Melut, and ARC, the Protection partner in Ulang.

1 - Needs

Protracted conflict and increasing levels of food insecurity, morbidity, and difficulties in accessing humanitarian aid have contributed to a deteriorating nutrition situation in South Sudan; the latest World Bank poverty assessment report states that malnutrition amongst children is worrisome, with an estimated 1 million children under five years expected to be acutely malnourished and almost 300,000 severely malnourished. Despite this need, and because the conflict in South Sudan led to a disruption in the newest nation's systems, government institutions face challenges in effectively providing for its people; further, because the country has minimal economic opportunities, the government is unable to invest in necessary systems, including nutrition services. The fragile health system is characterized by lack of skilled staff, supplies, equipment and leadership at all levels, resulting in inadequate access to life-saving health and nutrition services. Community vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening food security situation country-wide; with Melut and Ulang counties, classified as IPC phase 4 ("Crisis") and phase 5 (“Emergency”) in September 2019, are expected to experience no improvements between October and December 2019, the most recent IPC analysis predicts they will continue in phase 4 and 5 during the first quarter of 2020; these projections predict they would need immediate humanitarian assistance. Alarmingly high GAM rates have been observed alongside significant gaps in acute malnutrition coverage throughout Upper Nile State; in Melut GAM prevalence is 21.7% (SAM 4.9%, MAM 16.8%; WVI SMART Survey, March 2018), while in Ulang a GAM rate of 20.1% (SAM 2.7%, MAM 17.4%; UNKEA SMART Survey, March 2018) was reported among children 6-59M and GAM 18.8% among PLWs. These GAM rates in both counties are above the emergency threshold of 15%, thus treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) for children below five years continue to be a key need for saving lives of most at risk children and PLWs. As a result, GOAL proposes to provide nutrition services through a network of 21 TSFP sites in Melut (7 sites) and Ulang (14 sites) Counties where GOAL has been operational since 2014 and 2007 respectively; in Melut, these services will be integrated with GOAL’s health services through 2 clinics to provide a comprehensive package of care to prevent children and PLWs further deteriorating into SAM. While equally targeting males and females, the latter are the largest service user group, and GOAL will endeavour to make facilities safely accessible and gender-inclusive, providing mobile services as needed to facilitate access. While primarily targeting most vulnerable members within communities, children < 5 , PLWs, and women of child-bearing age, GOAL will ensure women and men participate equally in project implementation; community leaders, heads of household and family members with influence will be sensitized and participate in community mobilization and stakeholders meetings to provide feedback about project implementation. “Do no harm” will be central to activities, ensuring they do not negatively affect safety, dignity and integrity of intended beneficiaries. GOAL will also integrate FSL, WASH, and Protection components into programming. GOAL will refer TSFP beneficiaries into ongoing FSL projects to address drivers of poor nutrition at the household level. GOAL will refer any GBV cases to relevant Protection partners, while taking steps to promote safe access for beneficiaries to its services; GOAL will conduct refresher training for all staff on safeguarding and PSEA, while also encouraging communities to utilize its feedback, complaints and response mechanism (FCRM) as a resource to give feedback or voice concerns about programming.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

GOAL will work in partnership to strengthen the relationships with the local authorities with the aim of promoting equitable participation of all beneficiaries while ensuring project activities are gender-sensitive to meet needs of women, men, boys and girls. GOAL will ensure stakeholders' participation in the project; involvement of local community through project planning and implementation, and through committees, and awareness raising sessions etc., to identify the most vulnerable to ensure equitable access to services. GOAL will ensure its Community Feedback Mechanisms is used effectively to engage better with communities. Most primary health facilities are accessible by road, and teams will be equipped with off-road vehicles that can withstand bad weather and rough terrain to ensure GOAL can reach those most in need who have no other way of receiving life-saving services. GOAL will preposition supplies in facilities that risk accessibility challenges during rainy season to prevent stockouts so communities can continue to access services

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints. Community involvement is an important aspect of GOAL’s programming and service delivery. To foster strong community involvement and engagement, GOAL will conduct key community leaders consultative meetings and stakeholders quarterly review meetings to further identify needs and gaps, taking lead in developing workable solutions, review and monitor programme progress. Community leaders will lead in selection of CNVs, who will conduct active case finding, home visits, dissemination of hygiene and nutrition messages, cooking demonstrations using TSFP commodities. Community leaders and CNVs will mobilize the community to access services, provide safety for staff, programme assets and supplies utilized within communities and provide affordable locally available labour to support activities.
