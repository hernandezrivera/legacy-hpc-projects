Project Summary

This project is aimed to conduct risk education on explosive ordinance and ERW to conflict‐affected populations, particularly targeting vulnerable groups of IDPs, returnees, and host communities, children and adults living in Fangak and Canal/Pigi of Jonglei State. The above target counties have been facing explosive hazards that impact negatively on the ability of civilians to safely access basic services which include; education/TLS, CFS, health facilities /centers and natural resources such as water and farmlands as most of this people are depending on agriculture and small scale farming which is reducing their coping mechanisms. Contributing to the restoration of a safe and secure environment can improve the living condition of the rural population especially on the most contaminated areas and improve outcomes across almost all sectors.
The project will target men, women, boys, and girls who are affected by conflict. 12,500 (3,500 women, 2,500 men, 4,000 girls and 2,500 boys). The project will be implemented in a period of 12 months and it will cost $150,000. CAO will implement this project directly in the target locations in collaboration with the target population, the local administration, line ministry and partners on the ground to achieve maximum results. The project activities are designed to prevent and respond to “explosive ordnance risk education (EORE)” It is expected that by the end of the project period, the lives of vulnerable population at risk of ERW (explosive remnants of war) have increased access to EORE awareness and safe behavior. Women, men, girls, and boys will be better informed on the dangers and safe behaviors regarding ERW in emergencies that will make a meaningful contribution to the population's needs for safe behaviors.

1 - Needs

During the most recent conflict especially in Upper Nile and Jonglei states of South Sudan, UNMAS reported over 75 victims, over half of which were fatalities. Due to the lack of access and poor connectivity in some parts of the northern border region, especially in Upper Nile State, it is anticipated that the number of accidents and fatalities is actually far greater as many incidents go unreported. The ongoing crisis in South Sudan and overall deteriorating humanitarian situation requires a strong emergency response to save lives, while at the same time building resilient communities and to the extent possible ensuring that reclaimed food security as well as peace and reconciliation initiatives are more durable with interventions taking root in the communities. By mid-2019, 61% of the South Sudanese population were considered food insecure including 1.8 million on the brink of famine (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, June 2019). Approximately 150 previously unknown hazardous areas are discovered each month. Following the pullout of G4S who conducted the ERW clearance in New Fangak, CAO conducted an assessment in Fangak and Canal/Pigi and the report shows that the area is contaminated with mines / ERW following several accidents that have happened to women, girls, men, boys and also animals. During the assessment, several incidences were reported involving 2 women, 3 children, and 1 animal who were confirmed dead due to explosion of land mines in Fangak. There is widespread trauma among women, men, girls, and boys because of the existing protection threats of the mines/ERW and UXOS in Fangak which stops many farmers from accessing their farms.During the assessment, it was found out that mines and ERW were planted during the conflict of recent civil wars that resulted in the killing of people and massive displacement. CAO as a partner on the ground, was trained and accredited by Danish Church Aid (DCA) in 2018 on how to conduct awareness and taking coordinates of hazard areas on EORE in the community structures and at schools/TLS, CFS, and on safe behaviors in the areas that are contaminated with ERW items. The relevant authorities have assessed that the areas are contaminated with mines and ERW. CAO is providing MRE awareness to over 10,000 Host communities, returnee and IDP in Fangak, these communities include women, girls, men, and boys. There is a need to liaise with partners to ensure girls, women, boys men and animals are protected from mines and ERW.

2 - Activities

Activity. 1. Safer Community Plan’ component, which aims to assist in the development of community-based plans and activities in communities in the 2 counties so as to promote a community and people-centered approach to safety. Activity. 2. Community Dialogues (Community Meetings). This will be general community meetings where ERW threat, risk-taking behavior and target groups, priority setting and media communication issues will be discussed. Activity. 3. Community Awareness Workshops (direct MRE presentations). This will be a one-day awareness after every 1 month for target communities in the 2 Counties covered by the project. Activity. 4. Drama Sensitization. This will be based on pre-rehearsed skits with key EORE messages to target populations depending on the kind of risk and behavior being target for change. Activity. 5. Public Information Dissemination. This will be part of the integrated EORE package to reinforce the RE strategies mentioned. Activity. 6. Formalizing the set-up of a data collection process and system at county headquarter level to integrate EORE-related data (ie victims and survivors data) into the classic IMSMA forms and share with NMAA and UNMAS database. Activity. 7. Data Collection. CAO will collect Hazardous area and accident information which will be handed to UNMAS, NMAA & UNOPS and used internally for planning and prioritization. Activity. 8. Teachers' training on MRE as part of integrating EORE into School Curriculum

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Yes, CAO upholds stringent measures to ensure beneficiary rights are upheld, including the right to information, dignity, safety, and accountability. Standing agreements are in place with stakeholders to ensure the rights of the beneficiaries and upheld. CAO has a risk management policy, which is periodically communicated to the employees and volunteers as appropriate to ensure that all risk management processes and practices are carried out in accordance with it. The policy includes statements as to risk tolerance and risk appetite which help the organization decide what actions to take if any for risks assessed at different levels i.e extreme, high,medium or low. Risks are assigned to nominated risk owners (senior management) who are then accountable for managing them. Risk assessments are carried out at inception level and will be carried out before any project facility setups, through risk identification, risk analysis, and risk evaluation. A risk matrix will be developed, risk levels determined, a risk profile developed and risk ownership assigned.
