Project Summary

In 2024, Tdh will scale up its WASH response through gender-responsive, rights-based, adolescent and youth-friendly inclusive services in camp 26, camp 27 and the surrounding host communities (including another new H Block). WASH activities will offer knowledge and awareness to maintain personal hygiene and WASH facilities in appropriate ways. Tdh will also address adequate and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene. All activities will ensure the do-no-harm principle by prioritizing the safety and dignity of women and girls. Tdh will continue its sensitization and prevention measures before and during natural disasters, including disaster risk reduction, AWD, dengue outbreak issues etc., and will also provide support in man-made disasters, like fire. WASH operations and maintenance committee and key stakeholders were included throughout the project development, and community complaint feedback and response mechanisms will be maintained during the implementation period.
Tdh WASH activities will be based on the support gaps and needs at different community levels, being inclusive to age (05-17 years boy and girls, 18-59 years men and women, and 60 years plus elderly men and women), gender and diversity ( if other gender families within project areas will also support). Tdh will continue to operate and maintain the Feacal Sludge Management plant (desludging, treatment, and disposal) that is already built (including an incinerator) in camp 27 as well as waste management activities (collection, transportation, segregation, and disposal; organic composting and plastic recycling). Tdh-operated FSM and waste management services will continue to receive, sludge, treat, dispose (dry sludge and waste), and plastic waste recycling from the camps 26 and 27, the two Primary Health Care Centers run by Tdh and the host communities, as well as the Tdh Multi-Purpose Community Centres. The two PHCs operated by Tdh already have two incinerators but given the current large volume of contaminated waste generated by the PHCs which cannot be treated at the PHCs, so all wastes after sterilization of the plastic parts of the infectious wastes will be handled at the SWM Plant, where crusher machines, pits (ash and sharp) and incinerators are already installed as per appropriate standards. Tdh will continue to operate and maintain the WASH facilities in the PHCs, Camps and surrounding host community as well as construct/rehabilitate/decommission WASH facilities (latrine, bathing cubicle, garbage bin, and connecting drain) based on their condition and needs. Additionally, a desalination/water treatment plant will be set up at Camp 27. Due to the scarcity of water in Teknaf, this plant will help ensure better access to water for the beneficiaries within Camp 26 and27 which will reduce the water scarcity challenge somewhat. Tdh will conduct continued management and improvements of all facilities to ensure adequate Infection Prevention and Control (IPC)/disinfection.
Awareness raising and WASH-related messages will be disseminated to both refugees and host communities through household sessions/meetings, campaigns, day celebrations and the development and distribution of IEC/BCC materials. Tdh WASH and outreach team carry out these activities covering the topics of waste management, sanitation, handwashing, AWD/Dengue, and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) among adolescents and women. This will be complimented by the distribution of NFI/WASH kits (during emergency Tdh will distribute NFI items as a contingency plan, such as, handwashing devices, WASH kits, MHM kits, garbage bins at HH’s level), and emergency latrines will be provided to ensure adequate WASH assistance to the beneficiaries during any disaster.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In camps 26 and 27, lack of access to proper waste disposal services continues to be a major challenge, according to the 2023 AFAs monitoring report, 30% of Camp 26 and 10% of Camp 27 HHs often find visible waste in the vicinity of their house. Tdh-PRM-funded project in 2020-2023 has already addressed this through the O&M of FSM and SWM. While continuing SWM and FSM services, improvements have been made to handle LDPE and a plastic recycling machine has already been set up to recycle them. Most of the HHs reported that their waste was being segregated at HHs level and reported a designated pit/bins as the main source of waste disposal, as per J-MSNA 2023 findings (as per WASH sector information), currently 59% of waste is properly segregated in Camp 26 and 88% of waste is segregated in Camp 27 waste management is a challenge. Due to the density of the population, latrines quickly fill up and inadequate desludging services can lead to pits overflowing and contaminating surrounding water sources. Block cleaning services are needed, as adequate wastewater disposal from HHs and WASH facilities is mostly lacking. As per 2023 J-MSNA findings 85% of Rohingya refugees reported having water, 93% of sanitation facilities functional, average 71% of the waste is currently properly processed, and as per 2021 J-MSNA only 26% of HC respondents reported having drinking water. In the host communities, most of the HHs reported not having access to soap and water after utilizing latrines. At camp level, 6% of HHs reported loss or diminished access to clean water and sanitation as an impact of COVID-19, while 17% of the host community recorded loss of diminished access to basic services.As per the 2023 J-MSNA findings, ongoing WASH needs in camp 26, 27, and surrounding HC include the lack of access to functional sanitation, waste disposal services, FSM, and the potential for water source contamination and health and hygiene issues. As per J-MSNA 2023 findings, FDMNs reported 93% of sanitation facilities are functional. Challenges remain, however, especially in hygiene and SWM. FSM facilities need to improve efficiency and coverage. Need to strengthen SWM systems, promotion of waste segregation, recycling, and composting. There is a need to harmonize SWM systems and to engage WASH agencies in sustainable, and female participation engagement.Uptake of appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviors remains low. For example, only 32% of HHs reported washing their hands after handling children’s faeces (REACH, 2019). Safe water handling practices at HH level are not widely observed, with the reported levels of contamination of stored water exceeding 90% (WHO/UNICEF 2019). J-MSNA findings only 37% of women and girls reported receiving MHM materials. After 6 years of implementation of WASH activities in Cox’s Bazar, WASH agencies now need to focus on carrying out continuous improvements to existing latrines and BC to increase durability, quality, privacy, and accessibility while addressing gender, disability, and age factors. The construction of new latrines following the unified designs, will address coverage gaps, and the upgrading of FSM facilities through full-chain treatment processes. Another priority area is to strengthen the SWM systems, in conjunction with assessing the factors that motivate people to segregate waste. There is a need to follow the HP strategy and focus on strengthening participatory approaches for FDMNs to contribute to changing high-risk behaviours. Community mobilization will be reinforced to ensure the participation of women and girls during the design, feedback mechanisms and implementation of WASH services. As Teknaf is a water-scarce area in Bangladesh, there is a need to focus more on the development of water resource management system, and appropriate SWM that will contribute to the preservation of the environment, as well as innovative and new interventions that can save more water.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project is designed to address WASH strategies in close consultation and coordination with the WASH Sector and other relevant sectors in the response of Rohingya and host communities in Teknaf to implement quality, inclusive WASH interventions, and support the Government of Bangladesh in contributing to reach SDGs (3: Good health and wellbeing, 4: Quality education, 5: Gender equality, and 10: Reduced inequalities). The main objective of the project is to increase access to quality WASH including SWM services, and strengthen the inclusiveness of facilities and community participation, empowerment, and awareness. This is an integrated project where the main components are CP, Health, and WASH and It will be integrated through the Tdh integration roadmap. In Tdh working areas, targeted beneficiaries will get all mentioned component's services from the project, and the outreach team will enhance community people’s knowledge on all components that will contribute to an increase in meaningful participation of the beneficiaries. This will also increase in knowledge of community people about their responsibilities which will support to stabilize the maintenance of WASH facilities in the community. The project will be implemented following the prevailing guidelines of the RRRC and CiC through approval of projects from NGOAB and regular consultation with the authorities for the implementation of different activities. All these activities will directly contribute to the WASH facilities' functionality and sustainability which will bring positive changes in their lives (between FDMNs and the host community) and will establish gender equality, age and disability inclusion appropriateness and environmental protection in WASH needs. During the implementation period project will follow CHS and the ‘do no harm’ approach. and will always coordinate closely with WASH sector to get necessary technical and management support and get expert opinion which will enrich our work.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project is part of an integrated project where the main components are CP, Health and WASH. Targeted beneficiaries will receive services from all the mentioned components of the project, as well as the outreach team will engage both community (Rohingya and host) people to implement all the components and increase the meaningful participation of the beneficiaries/stakeholders which will help them in decision making during project activity and facility designing, planning, and implementation, for example; The same beneficiaries will get WASH, CP and health services, WASH project we will construct gender sensitive, age and disability friendly structures, hygiene promotion (including NFI), messaging (including handwashing, sanitation, MHM, waste management, water safety plan, AWD, Dengue and related to other diseases) and will receive their feedback and complain from CFRM system and address them, and will follow do no harm and CHS, all service directly contribute to protection and to save lives. Also, after providing the mentioned support meaningful access will be created in the community such as WASH facilities will be user-friendly and fear of using latrines, especially at night will be reduced, team and community people will be more aware of protection, referral route, if there are any protection issues through this, will be taken forward along with the implementation of the protection policies of the organization.

3.3 - Age & Disability

In regular WASH activities (Including Hygiene Promotion sessions, meeting campaign, and NFI distribution), will include different age, gender, third gender and person with disabilities in decision-making time, and also project will ensure their smooth access to the WASH facilities (all requirements based on physical with disability will be considered during construction)and will ensure safeguarding issues during implementing WASH activities and will follow do no harm and CHS standards. To reduce open defecation, the project will provide children under 5 years of age with plastic potty to promote defecation habits, which will help them develop toilet habits. Also, any protection issue detected will be immediately reported to the protection team which will ensure protection, and in coordination with the power sector, solar lights will be installed near latrines, which will help reduce GBV cases and create fear-free latrine access at night.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Under the AAP IASC and Tdh framework, Tdh will implement activities following three main phases. (I) Taking account: the project team will consult with beneficiaries at the beginning of the project to update the baseline data and relevant information related to the project needs to ensure their involvement in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: Tdh will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions. (3) Being held to account: Tdh will conduct assessment at key moments of the project (start, annually and end-line evaluation) to allow the communities to provide their opinion related to the project performances and for further improvement. Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) that is already established at the camp level -will continue getting direct complaints and feedback (through phone, boxes, group discussions, individual consultations, etc.) from the beneficiaries and will address them in compliance with Tdh guidelines.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

During the implementation of the project, gender segregation WASH facilities will be set up and solar lights will be ensured with the help of the energy sector which will help in reducing PESA -Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and in case of any problem related to PESA, necessary action will be taken as per the organizational PESA policy and will be contacted with the concerned sector and area focal agency. Also, to prevent any types of SEA cases Tdh will ensure all staffs, volunteers, guards, and cleaners receive PSEA orientation and CoC. An effective reporting mechanism will be established to ensure SEA cases are reported through the proper channel. Tdh follows the zero-tolerance policy and doesn't allow any deviation from the victim's rights for SEA cases. Additionally, Tdh is an active member of the PSEA network and dedicated PSEA focal for the network is in place.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In part, the project will contribute to disaster risk reduction during floods/flush floods, landslide, dengue and AWD outbreaks, and as well as the project will manage all types of waste in the community to reduce flood and water logging situations. The project will provide portable toilets to meets basic needs of the beneficiaries, and the project will provide emergency assistance in case of WASH-related outbreaks that will mitigate the situation. Also, the project will provide WASH facility operation and maintenance support, disinfection support, dissemination of disaster awareness messages, emergency health support during and after disasters in proper coordination with camp authorities and WASH sector.
