Project Summary

This project aims at contributing to address the unmet humanitarian needs, reduce the avoidable mortality and morbidity, and improve well-being of old and newly displaced people and host communities who are severely affected by the natural and man-made disasters in selected target locations in three states (South Kordofan and Blue Nile) . ADD proposes to support 15 Primary Health Care (PHC) facilities to serve an estimated number of more than 275,000 conflict-affected people and host community members in the selected sites, with particular focus on women of reproductive age, and children under-five years. ADD will provide a core, yet comprehensive, primary and reproductive health services, which include both curative and preventive care. Services to be provided include; (i) General clinical services: outpatient consultation
services for management of the common illnesses, basic laboratory investigations, short stay admission, and emergency referral system to hospitals for severe cases; (ii) Maternal and newborn care: full RH package including antenatal care and pregnant women vaccination, skilled care during childbirth, essential newborn care, basic emergency obstetric care, postnatal care, family planning, and access to management of sexually transmitted infections etc.; (iii) Child health: routine EPI vaccines by trained cadre, IMCI services, MUAC screening for malnutrition and referral; (iv) Communicable diseases control: supporting the sentinel sites for early warning system of epidemic prone diseases with established surveillance system, weekly timely EWARS reports, free-of-charge access to diagnosis and treatment of malaria; (v) Environmental health: supporting procedures for proper safe waste disposal and management, functioning basic incinerator, sanitation facilities. These activities are complemented by community-based outreach and health promotion activities to multiply impact. They include production and distribution of IEC materials for
behavior change communication (BCC) on subjects of malaria and self-referral, STI/HIV Prevention, pregnancy and childbirth danger signs and where/when to seek help, breast feeding, child home care, hygiene promotion and water and sanitation

1 - Needs

According to HRP 2020, the total number of people in need of humanitarian assistance is more than 8.6 million with an increase of 40% compared to the year 2019. This increase can be justified by several factors such as large scale population displacement, influx of refugees and climate change and the deteriorated socioeconomic conditions across the country. Host communities and residents are estimated to be 1.94 million which represent 23.2% of the total people in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan. Considering the type of the need, there are 7.8 million people are in need for basic health services; 59% of them are female, 63 % are children and 8% are older people. It is estimated that 2.2 million are women of reproductive age who are in need for comprehensive reproductive health services. During 2019, episodes of violence and armed conflict continued to occur across Sudan resulting in more than 500 fatalities, mainly in Khartoum, Red Sea, Darfur, and South, West and North Kordofan states . Imports of medicines and medical supplies dropped by about 35 % compared to the same period of last year, which contributed tocost increase of 50-100 per cent, reducing the overall availability of essential medicines in the public sector from 60 percent in 2018 into 43 percent in 2019, and restricting access to essential life-saving health care services. The resulting availability of essential drug supply during 2019 in Sudan was of only 43 percent in the National medical supply fund, 48.7 percent in the national health insurance fund, and 59 percent in the private sector, with lowest availability of medicines in Red Sea Red State (10 percent availability)availability, followed by West Darfur (11.7 percent), Northern State (29.6 percent), and South Kordofan (31 percent) Health services providing complete healthcare packages are lacking. Currently, only 33 percent of health facilities in Sudan offer the complete basic healthcare package (which include maternal and reproductive health care services, nutrition services, immunization, infectious diseases, and free medicines). In Eastern Sudan, the availability of these packages goes down to 15 percent in in Gedaref and Sennar states, and less even in Jazeera State with 12 percent availability and North Kordofan 10 percent. As a consequence, between 2018 and 2019, the percentage of women who receive the minimum four anti-natal care visits during their latest pregnancy across Sudan was 51 percent, with the lowest visits reported in West Kordofan (41 percent) Gedaref (37 percent) and 40 percent on average across Darfur States. .Women of reproductive health constitute one of the key vulnerable groups, representing approximately 24 percent of total population in Sudan. . Vaccination coverage remains below standards in Sudan. The Expanded Program of Immunization (EPI) coverage is still low with approximately 60 percent overall coverage of DPT3 vaccine across Sudan, which is significantly lower the targeted minimum average of 80 percent. This lack ofimmunization coverage contributed to a low herd immunity and influenced the appearance of vaccine preventable diseases in Sudan such as a Measles outbreak during the 1st half of 2019 and Polio outbreak that was declared in August 2020. Moreover, Sudan has been affected by COVID-19 epidemic since March 2020 and expected to continue during the first quarter of 2021. The COVID 19 response activities are ongoing however still sub-optimal- due to the clear funding gap and inability to support all isolation centers with trained health cadre and sufficient PPE.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The proposed interventions are in line with the Humanitarian sector Response Strategic and health cluster objectives(Increase equitable access to humanitarian life-saving and life sustaining health services for those the most vulnerable and in need). The project activities include: 1. Implementing community –based Health insurance initiatives in Blue Nile state through community financing/ community empowerment 2. Delivering a minimum basic package of primary health care services including maternal and child health. 3. Supporting referral services to secondary health facilities with ensuring beneficiaries' dignity and privacy. 4. Providing the target health facilities with medical supplies, equipment and medicine including the provision of essential and life-saving medicines for communicable and noncommunicable diseases. 5.Expanding andmaintaining the health surveillance system with early warning mechanisms. 6. Providing a comprehensive reproductive health services package including antenatal and postnatal services, STIs treatment, family planning and Obstetric, newborn care according to MOH Standards. 7. Supporting the implementation of the expanded program of immunization through fixed sites, mobile and outreach activities. 8. Supporting the referral system for the emergency cases especially obstetrical complication. 9. supporting Rapid response to diseases outbreaks in affected areas with close coordination with the health sector. 10.Performing essential/minimal rehabilitation and maintenance of health facilities. 11. Building the capacity of health facility staff through basic trainings, refresher trainings and on-job trainings. 12. Supporting monitoring and evaluation of the proposed services and close supervision. 13. Supporting social behavior change and communication activities among the targeted communities by conducting conducting several health and hygiene awareness campaigns. To ensure effective implementation, ADD will be committed to follow the MoH policies, procedures, and technical standards as well as the health cluster's guidance. In addition, ADD will implement these proposed interventions in full coordination with other health partners (UN agencies, national and international NGOs) working in the target states.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ADD staff trained on prevention of sexual exploitation of beneficiaries by aid workers will orient the field staff on the subject matter as a part of ADD’s commitment to ADD’s values and Humanitarian Accountability Framework. Envisioned complaint response mechanism for ADD shall have adequate scope to address the protection concerns of the target population. Wherever applicable, ADD will develop selection criteria to choose individuals/households for assistance with dignity kits, soap, latrines, etc. Relatively vulnerable individuals and households shall be prioritized, when the servicesare scarce. ADD and partner will also mainstream protection concern in activities like latrine site/design selection, water point location selection by identifying locations that women and girls (and the wider community) consider as safe. ADD and CBO will ensure that the women have a say in when and where the Health committee (HC) meetings are conducted. ADD will work with HC to address specific protection issues in a community related to Health interventions.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Feedback and Complaint Mechanism: add will consult communities on establishing a viable feedback or complaint mechanism. Most possible options are use of mobile feedback and complaint boxes and/or a telephone hotline system to collect feedback from beneficiaries. Complaints will be validated through initial screening processes; an investigation will take place in case of valid and serious cases. ADD has a pool of senior staff with experience in handling complaints during the project cycle, ADD will make necessary arrangement to ensure that staff in field locations are well trained in handing complaints. ADD will train its CBOs in Feedback and Complaint Mechanism. Information sharing: ADD will organize sessions with beneficiaries and HCs to communicate relevant details about the project, especially related to the activity to be implemented in their respective community. ADD’s mission, presence of feedbackmechanism, entitlement of the community (if applicable) is few other elements that will be shared. Do no Harm: ADD will follow principles of ‘Do no harm’ prior to and during the intervention of the project. One of the necessary things is to be aware of ‘connectors’ and dividers. In order to avoid confrontation between host communities and refugees, a portion of activities will be allocated to adjacent host community. Some health non-food items like dignity/hygiene kits, soap will be provided also tovulnerable host community members. ADD understands that many communities in the project areas are diverse and will explore ways to unite the community; one of the ways is to promote participation of people from various ethnic backgrounds among IDPs and host communities together as members of HCs. ADD hopes that serving in a social platform like HCs can be a way to increase coherence and connectedness. ADD may include the refugees and host communities around target in its interventionrelated to conflict resolution through creation of economic opportunities
