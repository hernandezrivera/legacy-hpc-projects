Project Summary

The proposed project “Improving GBV prevention and SRH response services to girls and vulnerable women through Hub and Spokes model in Yobe and Adamawa States” centres on the protection of girls and vulnerable women in IDPs and Host Communities of Potiskum, Damaturu LGAs of Yobe state and Michika, Mubi North and South LGAs of Adamawa State through the “Hub and Spokes Model”. This is an innovative way to provide a more access to services and efficient/effective way to address the complex challenges posed during GBV and SRH services delivery for women and girls and most especially “GBV survivors”.
The Hub and Spokes Model will address the need for better resource utilization, expertise concentration, collaboration, data-driven decision-making, and accessible services for survivors across the above mentioned LGAs of Adamawa State.
This model will enhance the overall effectiveness and reach of GBV and SRH response efforts, ultimately contributing to the well-being and safety of individuals affected by GBV in these locations. The model will enable efficient emergency response, coordinate rapid assistance during crises, and dispatch resources and support to spokes based on the severity and urgency of GBV/SRH incidents. The Hub and Spokes Model will be at different levels; Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary levels.
• The Primary Spokes will include the Women and Girls Space (WGSS), Integrated Health Facilities (IHF), and or Primary Health Care Centres (PHCC) and will be operationalized within settlements, camps, and host communities in Potiskum, Damaturu LGAs of Yobe state and Michika, Mubi North and South LGAs of Adamawa State. Service Personnel required at this level include Community volunteers, Caseworkers, Counsellors, J. CHEW, Midwives, TBAs, and Nurses. The GBV/SRH services that will be provided at this level include Health Education & Promotion, Case Management, Psychosocial Support Services (PSS), Mobile Outreaches, Family Planning, Ante Natal Care & Post Natal Care, and Referrals. At this level, commodities required for service delivery will include Essential drugs, Clean Delivery Kits, IEC materials, Family Planning Supplies (condoms), PSS materials, and Stationeries.
• The Secondary Spokes will include One Stop Centre (OSC) or General Hospitals and will be operationalized across Potiskum, Damaturu LGAs of Yobe state and Michika, Mubi North and South LGAs of Adamawa State. Service Personnel required at this level will include; Nurses/Midwives, Spoke supervisors, CHEW, Caseworkers, Community Volunteers, PSS Counsellors, and Toll-free line handlers. The GBV/SRH services that will be provided at this level include BeMONC, Family Planning and treatment of minor Injuries, ANC & PNC, Syndromic Management of STIs, PSS and CMR. Toll-free line services, Rapid Diagnostic Tests (HIV, HEP, RBC, Malaria), and Referrals. For smooth service delivery, the following commodities will be required at the Secondary Spokes, such as ANC and PNC Drugs, Delivery medical supplies, STI treatment supplies, Rapid Diagnostic Test kit, PEP Kit, Psychosocial materials (games), IEC materials, Clean Delivery Kits, Menstrual Hygiene Kits, Dignity Kits.
• The Hub model will be operationalized at the Tertiary level of the Health care systems. At this level, service personnel will include; Hub Coordinator, Pediatrician, Gynecologist & Obstetrician, Psychologist/Mental Health Practitioners, Nurses/Midwives, General Medical Practitioners, Police, Lawyers, and Toll-free Line Handlers. The GBV/SRH services that will be provided at this level are CeMONC, Case Management, ART services (PMTCT), CMR, Specialized MHPSS (clinical), Coordinating services, and Toll-free line services. Commodities required for service delivery at the Hub level include Rape Kit, Medical Drugs/supplies, Stationeries, IEC materials, Pregnancy scan equipment, Dignity Kit, Menstrual Hygiene Kit, Clean Delivery Kit, Psychosocial materials, Family Planning Supplies, HIV Test kit, and Drugs.

1 - Needs

Thirteen years on, the conflict in Nigeria’s north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) continues unabated. The impact of the conflict in the BAY states, home to some 16 million people, has created a protracted humanitarian and protection crisis affecting millions of people. The protection of civilians remains a major concern. A fundamental resolution to the conflict is not expected in the foreseeable future. Already vulnerable people are facing additional risks due to climate change impacts on food security and other needs, as well as disease outbreaks. The crisis has devastated civilian infrastructure and livelihoods, leaving many dependent on humanitarian assistance and in urgent need of access to basic services. An estimated 1,829,002 million individuals (32 per cent IDPs, 25 per cent returnees, 43 per cent host communities) need GBV interventions across the BAY states. Borno State has the bulk (48 per cent) of those in need, followed by Adamawa at 34 per cent and 18 per cent in Yobe. Eleven LGAs spread across the BAY states that are hosting IDPs were identified as having the highest severity of needs: Girei, Madagali, Maiha, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Yola North, Bama, Chibok and Magumeri. A similar number of host community LGAs were also identified to be in dire need of GBV response services including MHPSS, livelihoods support, access to justice and safe-house shelter, which are lacking or minimal. These include Gombi, Lamurde, Maiha, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Askira/Uba, Gubio and Gujba. Five LGAs have significant numbers of returnees: Askira/Uba, Chibok, Gubio, Konduga and Mafa. It is worth noting that incidences of GBV, especially intimate-partner violence (IPV) including sexual violence, continue to be reported across the BAY states, with women and girls disproportionately affected. Trend analysis from the 2022 Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) report (January-June) highlighted that 99 per cent of the reported incidents of GBV affected women and girls. IPV was reported by 65 per cent of survivors who sought help at various service points. In terms of prevalence across the states, Borno had the highest percentage of reported incidents (83 per cent) followed by Adamawa (13 per cent), and Yobe (4 per cent).There continues to be a glaring gap in the provision of GBV response services in several LGAs across the BAY states, especially in hard-to-reach locations. In Yobe State, for example, access to health and legal services for GBV survivors is a challenge as there is only one functional Sexual Assault and Referral Centre, located in Damaturu town. There is also a lack of safe spaces for women and girls across a number of LGAs. The increasing influx of new arrivals in some locations (Bama, Gwoza, Ngala and Dikwa) due to the Borno State Government-led camp closures in Maiduguri, the mass exodus from extremely-hard-to-reach locations all continue to strain the already overstretched resources for GBV response, as well as heightening GBV risks for women and girls in some locations. MHPSS as well as livelihood opportunities are lacking or inadequate in several hard-to-reach locations. The absence of civil authorities, especially court sessions, in several LGAs outside Maiduguri continues to hinder access to justice and other legal services for affected people including survivors of GBV. The GBV AoR will seek to address the highlighted needs by scaling up GBV response services including MHPSS in these locations.Protection remains a key concern in all the populations affected, not only in terms of the impact of violence and conflict, but also through the weakness of rule of law institutions and the protection risks generated by the ongoing conflict, particularly in terms of displacement, and lack of means of sustenance and access to basic services. Sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) remains a major concern.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will contribute to protection of girls and vulnerable women's access to basic life-saving health care services and reduced mortality and morbidity in the selected locations. The trained TBAs, JCHEWs, CHVs, CHEWs and facility staffs are going to collaborate together in making sure the objectives are achieved. The proposed project centres on improving the capacity and dignity of survivors - collective, transformative action to scale up prevention, and access to life-saving services across the 5 LGAs. The need to maintain and establish more multipurpose support centres like the proposed HUB and Spokes to address protection, psychosocial and SRH services for women and girls will be prioritized. The focus will be on the delivery of life-saving medical and psycho-social support to survivors of GBV and support to women and girls facing protection risks and threats. The response will ensure that women and girls have access to timely GBV and SRH response services through a multi-sectorial approach including health, psycho-social, and protection.RHHF will conduct Medical Outreach across the 5 LGAs: The program is designed to bridge the gap in access to healthcare and other services by reaching out to communities that might face geographical, economic, or social barriers. Conduct 3-days training for 9 CVs and 3 JCHEWs for SRH to support Hub and Spokes related activities to support SRH/GBV services at the Hub and Spokes. Conduct 4-days MISP Training for 21 CHEWs and 18 Midwives to support SRH/GBV services at the Hub and Spokes. Conduct 5-days Training for 18 Midwives on BeMONC and CeMONC to support SRH/GBV services at the Hub and Spokes. Procure RH kit 3 (post rape treatment kit); RH kit 6A (Clinical delivery assistance- reusable kits) and RH kit 6B (Clinical delivery assistance- drugs and disposable kits). Procure RH kit 3 (post rape treatment kit); RH kit 6A (Clinical delivery assistance- reusable kits) and RH kit 6B (Clinical delivery assistance- drugs and disposable kits). Conduct Supervisory/Field Visit to project sites. Support the provision of comprehensive SRH and GBV prevention services at the Hub centre. Support the provision of SRH and GBV prevention services for women and girls at the Spokes Centre in Potiskum, Damaturu LGAs of Yobe state and Michika, Mubi North and South LGAs of Adamawa State. Conduct Outreach across the three LGAs on GBV mitigation and prevention. Strengthens the referral from the Spokes to the Hub, the project will provide a mobile Ambulance to support the referral of patients from the community to the point of service. Strengthen and maintain state-level GBV toll-free helpline/call centre at the Hub centre in Yobe and Adamawa States to provide remote services and referrals. Collect, Collate and analyze data. Establish Hub and Spokes across the 5 LGAs. Training of the front liners and service providers on Clinical Management of Rape, Case management, MHPSS and PSEA. Conduct Orientation training for Community Volunteers across the 4 LGAs. Conduct quarterly coordination meeting between the staff of Hub and Spokes on service provision and feedback from the beneficiaries. Advocacy Visit: Conduct advocacy visits to relevant stakeholders at the Ministry of Health, Ministry of Women Affairs, Community Leaders to obtain their support and buy-in. Conduct quarterly community sensitizations/awareness campaigns on GBV, root causes, response and prevention for returnees, persons with disabilities, women, adolescent girls, men and young people of Potiskum, Damaturu LGAs of Yobe state and Michika, Mubi North and South LGAs of Adamawa State. Conduct monthly supervisory Monitoring by the Project Management Team to project sites to ensure the activities are implemented according to the plan. Procure and distribute 600 Dignity and Clean Delivery kits to Women and Girls across the implementation LGAs

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Recognizing the need for multi-sectoral and multi-dimensional approach, the project is also designed to conscript and convert the participation of critical actors to catalyze a concerted programme action to be able to achieve objectives of the project. Hence, the need to engage women, men health workers, community and religious leaders, CBOs. The Project will also engage GBV task force teams at the community level to identify protection concerns and provide practical solution for risk mitigation. The project will ensure a comprehensive approach to dignity and menstrual hygiene management, beyond provision of materials. It will address storage of the materials, privacy to manage menstruation including washing, drying and disposal; Information on usage, and enhance the dignity of the women and girls by addressing social norms on menstruation, promoting awareness on reproductive health rights environmentally friendly disposals, and community engagement as part of the awareness messaging.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

RHHF will listen to and be guided by the concerns and views of vulnerable women and girls affected by the conflict in Potiskum, Damaturu LGAs of Yobe state and Michika, Mubi North and South LGAs of Adamawa State. Through this project, GBV Survivors and vulnerable persons/communities will access vital information, including 24 hours toll-free telephone lines, complaint and feedback boxes, counselling, in-person information spaces, MHPSS and/or focus group discussions. In addition, the Radio Program will provide more information on the referral mechanisms. The toll-free helpline for GBV survivors will enable community members to call and report personal GBV incident, or a GBV incident they have witness as well as to receive coordinated and sustained support in term of access to services including the Police, One-Stop Centre, and legal aid. The project team will include women and girls with disabilities in OSC, IHF, WGSS and community awareness and trainings and will put in place monitoring mechanisms in place to gauge progress regarding PSEA and AAP. The project will include accountability to affected populations, community engagement (AAP/CE) and localization elements to build on feedback from affected people. Specific efforts will be made to engage women and girls who are often the most vulnerable and bear the burden of fending for their family.
