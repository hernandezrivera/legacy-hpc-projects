Project Summary

IOM, as Cluster Lead Agency, has been successfully managing the Shelter and NFI Core Pipeline since 2011 and has the capacity to continue providing critical services to Cluster HRP partners. Based on its experience and on-the-ground presence, IOM will ensure swift delivery of life saving assistance to partners and beneficiaries through effective supply chain management. Under the HRP 2019, S-NFI Cluster Pipeline plans to reach 838,328 (84% of total target) beneficiaries across South Sudan. Pipeline will procure new 85,509 non-food items kits and 15,090 shelter kits that can serve Cluster target beneficiaries. S-NFI Pipeline is responsible for procuring, transporting and storing S-NFI material. Upon S/NFI Cluster endorsement, the Pipeline will receive and process partners’ requests for distribution and prepositioning. The Pipeline team will coordinate with the Logistics Cluster to ensure cargo movement for planned responses and for stock prepositioning. Further, Pipeline team will ensure transport and storage from Juba to the main logistics hubs, ensure support to distributing partners on cargo movement by road, and coordinate with the Logistics Cluster for cargo shipment by air.
The Shelter-NFI Pipeline provides partners with rapid access to critical materials, to meet the emergency needs of most vulnerable populations. In order to allow partners timely access to S-NFI supplies, items are procured centrally by one organization and prepositioned in strategic locations, mainly Bentiu, Bor, Wau, Malakal and Rumbek, but also Torit, Mundri, Yambio, in order to expedite the process by which items are moved to the final distribution sites in field location. The Pipeline system optimizes economies of scale, reduction of cost, harmonized quality of items, non-duplication and supply chain efficiencies.
The Pipeline will conduct a survey with partners to gather feedback on efficiency, appropriateness and coverage of the pipeline, the findings of which will be utilized to adjust programming as appropriate. It will also collect beneficiary’s feedback through Post Distribution Monitoring and Rapid Monitoring conducted by its partners on quality, appropriateness and usage of supplies.

Needs

According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the country’s protracted crisis, spanning over half a decade, has left 7 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance. Displacement trends across geographic regions remain dynamic, and nearly 2 million people are internally displaced, with some 200,000 people living in UNMISS Protection of Civilians Sites and many more in collective centres across the country. In addition, there are more than 2.4 million South Sudanese refugees. Eighty-two per cent of South Sudanese live below the poverty line of $1.99 per day. South Sudan S-NFI Cluster estimates that approximately 1.7 million people will have limited or no access to adequate shelter and NFI in 2019. Those displaced have often left behind vital household items like cooking tools, blankets, mats and mosquito nets, and there often exists scarce community infrastructure and services, which further jeopardizes the livelihoods of affected populations. South Sudan has less than 100 kilometres of paved road and one of the least developed road networks. Seasonal precipitation turns much of the country to swamps and, according to HNO 2019, some 70 per cent of the country’s road network is inaccessible during the rainy season from July to December. Airstrips in general are of poor quality, with less than 10 airstrips that can be used continuously in the wet and dry season. South Sudan is therefore one of the most complex humanitarian operating environments in the world. The deteriorating infrastructure, lack of maintenance, cumulative effects of long and heavy rainy seasons, have left the humanitarian community with limited options to deliver relief items. During the dry season, insecurity along the main supply routes is a major challenge restricting movement and increasing transportation costs due to a high risk operational environment for transporters. The use of pre-positioning relief items in the field by road where possible remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. Currently, 18 partners have signed MoU with the Pipeline team. These partners depend on the common S-NFI pipeline and require sufficient and timely access to a common pipeline of key items. IOM has the capacity and operational presence to support mobile response partners with reliable, timely, and quality supplies of NFI and shelter materials in an environment characterized by major logistical challenges, where ongoing insecurity and a lack of basic infrastructure makes accessing field locations exceptionally challenging and costly.

Activities

The activities are in line with SO1 and SO2 of HRP 2019, as well as the Shelter and NFI Cluster Objectives 1 and 2, namely to 1) Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement, and 2) Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs. In line with SO3 of HRP 2019, and as part of the gradual transition to resilience-based approaches, the S/NFI Pipeline will coordinate with the S/NFI Cluster to continue to advocate for the use of the pipeline as a last resort. Where feasible, the Pipeline team will support the Cluster in capacitating partners towards other approaches, such as cash-based interventions (CBI) that capitalize on the existing coping mechanisms and community participation, with minimal reliance, if any, on pipeline. One example could be CBI that facilitates skills training and build upon communities’ local knowledge and resources, for example in weaving sleeping mats. Further, in lieu of sourcing material through pipeline, products handmade by the community can be bought by S/NFI partners and distributed to the most vulnerable in the community who are not able to provide for themselves. The Core Pipeline managed by IOM is composed of WASH Core Pipeline and SNFI Core Pipeline. Partners that sign an MoU with the WASH Core Pipeline can also be signatory partners for S-NFI Core Pipeline (and vice-versa) if they are eligible and wish to do so. Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Pipeline distributing partners inform beneficiaries before distribution on items distributing and their usage. Partners have already set standards for information sharing with beneficiaries, which include community meetings, and information disseminated through radio and local chief/community elders.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The unique needs of women are taken into account through the procurement of gender-sensitive items such as solar lamps, kangas, cooking sets, and mosquito nets, which can be used as clothes, swaddling clothes, or to provide privacy. Feedback from women gathered through monitoring exercises confirmed the utility of these items and constitute the basis for further procurement.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IOM team have all been trained on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse which makes them aware of all important and relevant concerns related to project design and implementation prior to rolling out of any intervention. Pipeline team also works closely with the IOM Protection Officer to ensure protection is mainstreamed in all activities. Pipeline partners have well established procedures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

Post-Distribution monitoring conducted by Pipeline partners collect feedback on material appropriateness and quality, which is used by the Pipeline team to inform items to be procured

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Pipeline conducts partner satisfaction surveys on management of Pipeline. The quality of S-NFI items are assessed by partners through post-distribution monitoring and community feedback mechanisms.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Pipeline does not have direct interaction with the communities. This notwithstanding, partners include the community in monitoring and evaluation of the quality and utility of supplies through post-distribution monitoring and community feedback. The pipeline team is then informed of this feedback through PDM reports available on the S-NFI Cluster website.
