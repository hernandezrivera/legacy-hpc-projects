Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen disaster resilience among vulnerable Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar district through effective multi-hazard risk management. The project will target vulnerable Rohingya refugees (100%) in Cox’s Bazar. The Project will work in close collaboration with ISCG Sectors, government, camp disaster management committees, and other relevant stakeholders to support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy through the following project activities:
(i) Landslide Risk Management training for the government representatives, camp disaster management committees, and humanitarian stakeholders, in consultation with CM leads (target 200).
(ii) Improving localized weather forecasting, landslide early warning, and rainfall monitoring systems (target 12,600)
(iii) Support and collaborate for inclusive disaster risk assessment, disaster preparedness, and anticipatory actions, with specific focus on landslides (target 11,500).
(iv) Facilitate and support landslide DRR analysis, plans, and products for government, ISCG sectors (SCCCM, EPRWG, EEN, FSS, Shelter, AAP), and humanitarian stakeholders (target 200).
(v) Capacity building (training and provision of specialised equipment) of first response agencies/professionals (camp frontline volunteers, CPP, FSCD, etc.) to ensure inclusive landslide preparedness and response (target 200).
(vi) Training for government and humanitarian stakeholders on the application of nature-based solutions in landslide risk reduction/mitigation (target 120).
(vii) Implementation of landslide mitigation interventions (slope stabilization, etc.) by adopting nature-based solutions in the Rohingya camps in coordination with CCCM and camp actors (target 15,000).
(viii) Implement landslide DRR awareness-raising events in the Rohingya camps, particularly landslide drills and street theatre/drama (target 11,500).
The project targets the most vulnerable Rohingya refugees (51,000, see activity-wise targets above), especially women (50.8%), children (11.02%), elderly (8.24%), persons with disabilities (1.42%), LGBT groups, and ethnic communities.
The project will conduct landslide disaster risk assessment & contribute to implementing ISCG’s multi-hazard response plans. The project will train first response agencies/volunteers on landslide DRM and will provide neccessary equipment and gear to support landslide response and early action. The project, in collaboration with camp actors, will support the capacity building of camp DMCs and DRM planning at the camp level. The landslide risk reduction schemes adopting nature-based solutions will be implemented in coordination with the CCCM sector, EEN, and CiCs in the camps.
The Project will coordinate closely with DRM partners, SCCCM sector, and AoR SM agencies, ensuring synergies, optimum use of resources, and effective programming.
Gender issues will be effectively mainstreamed across all disaster risk management (DRM) interventions and approaches. For instance, the project will ensure the active participation of women, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups in project DRM interventions. The project will support gender-sensitive and inclusive DRM training for local government and DMCs. The project will also train women volunteers/ representatives on DRM aspects. The project will ensure that local DRM plans include gender and disability-inclusive elements, such as the needs, risks, vulnerabilities, and capacities of women, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. All assessment, monitoring, evaluation, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data. The project targets 50.8% of women as beneficiaries and prioritizes women and female-headed households for direct support. The project will implement community education and awareness-raising initiatives on DRM, particularly targeting women, girls, and persons with disabilities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is one of the most disaster-prone districts in Bangladesh and is highly exposed to natural hazards and extreme weather impacts, including cyclones, landslides, flash floods, torrential rains, storm surges, and extreme temperatures. Cox’s Bazar is now home to around one million Rohingya refugees since 2017. The Rohingya crisis has significantly increased the size of the population at risk, as well as added pressures on the environment, which undermine resilience. The location, climate, topography, and human interference makes the refugee camps and hosting areas more vulnerable to natural hazard and extreme weather impacts. According to the SMSD Daily Incidents Yearly Report, natural hazards and extreme weather events such as windstorms, heavy rains, slope failure, landslides, and flooding impacted 208,699 individuals in camps in 2021, 139,687 individuals in camps in 2022 and 191,194 individuals in camps in 2023. Similarly, natural hazards and extreme weather events adversely affected the host and local communities in Cox's Bazar. For example, in 2023, Cyclone Mocha affected 334,620 individuals in May, heavy monsoon and flooding affected 479,503 individuals in August, and Cyclone Hamoon affected 476,549 individuals in October in Cox's Bazar. A recent 2023 MSNA analysis identified several gaps in the SCCCM sector, including disaster risk management in camps. The analysis found that 96% of households are dissatisfied with the humanitarian response during natural hazards. While 88% of households feel informed on how to act in case of a natural hazard, there is a need for more information and training on specific hazards, including fire (8%), landslides (6%), flooding (6%), cyclones (5%), and waterlogging (3%). Cyclone Mocha Flash Appeal and several other assessments highlighted the need to enhance Disaster Preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction measures in the camps and the host community through the prepositioning of emergency stocks, strengthening infrastructure, and anticipatory action to ensure that the most vulnerable communities are better equipped to prepare for and withstand disasters and sudden onset events. The major challenges for comprehensive disaster risk management in the district include: the lack of disaster risk management capacity of the government, DMCs, and humanitarian stakeholders; inadequate early warning systems for anticipatory action especially landslides; insufficient search and rescue capabilities of first response agencies/professionals; limited use of nature-based solutions for DRR, especially for landslides; limited DRR awareness among excluded groups; insufficient consideration of gender and disability in DRR; weak integration of DRR into sectoral planning. The project will closely work with the government, ISCG, CCCM sector and other relevant stakeholders to address these challenges and needs. The women, girls, children, elderly, persons with disabilities, and other minorities were particularly affected by these natural hazards and extreme weather events in Rohingya camps. Female Rohingya refugees have a lower socio-economic status. This leads to unequal access to opportunities and risks, making them more vulnerable to natural hazards and extreme weather events. Despite the high vulnerability to disasters, women increasingly play a critical role in disaster risk management. Therefore, empowering women is the key to strengthening disaster resilience among Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar district. The vulnerable communities will be identified following consultation with the government, CiC, CCCM, and humanitarian stakeholders, with priority areas identified and verified against known hazard and risk maps. For the selection of participants from the communities, representation of both genders, youth, the elderly, people with disabilities, and other minorities will be actively sought.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project was designed and will be implemented in alignment with the ISCG’s rationalisation principles, particularly focusing on principles 1, 3, and 4. The Project will work in close collaboration with ISCG Sectors, government, camp disaster management committees, and other relevant stakeholders to support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy. The project will consult with ISCG sectors/working groups and the government before engaging in an activity. The project will develop the capacity of implementing partners to meet basic requirements. The project will contribute to and improve inter-sectoral camp-based coordination when implementing disaster risk management activities at the camp level. The project will contribute to improving DRM-related service delivery for Rohingya refugees. The project will build relationships with the government in the camps (particularly with the camp in charge) for effective project delivery. The project will contribute to ensuring stability in the refugee operation and will improve relations with refugees, the Government, and other partners working in the camps.

3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will mainstream the protection issues across the activities. The project targets the most vulnerable refugees and will contribute to enhancing safety and dignity, and promoting the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to discrimination, particularly those arising from disasters and extreme weather impacts. The project will follow do-no-harm and non-discrimination principles. The project will set up appropriate mechanisms to ensure the active participation and inclusion of affected populations throughout the project cycle and will place measures to address concerns and complaints. The project will support the participation and empowerment of individuals and communities and will contribute to realizing their rights. The project will provide PSEA training to staff and partners to ensure PSEA issues.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will contribute to age and disability inclusion and child safeguarding throughout the project. Despite the high vulnerability to disasters, the elderly, children, and persons with disabilities increasingly play a critical role in disaster risk management. So, the project targets the elderly (8.24%) children (11.02%), and disability (1.43%) groups to reduce their disaster risks and build their DRR capacities. The project will prepare a gender and inclusion plan and will ensure that project activity is tailored to different age and disability group needs. The project will ensure the active participation and inclusion of the elderly, children, youth, and persons with disabilities in the DRM interventions. The project particularly mainstream disability inclusion. The project will ensure the active participation of the elderly, children, persons with disabilities, and other diverse groups and will address their needs, risks, and capacities throughout the project. All assessment, monitoring, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project mainstreams the ‘accountability to affected populations (AAP)’ principle into the project cycle. The project will follow the IASC and ISCG AAP guidelines and tools. The project will consider and take action to promote ‘information, communication, and transparency;’ to ensure ‘participation, engagement, and inclusion;’ to place ‘complaint and feedback response mechanism;’ and to improve ‘learning and adaptation.’The project will set up appropriate mechanisms to ensure the active participation and inclusion of affected populations throughout the project cycle and will place measures to address concerns and complaints. The project will train staff and partners to ensure that core humanitarian standards are respected during the project implementation. The project, in collaboration with CwCWG, will implement the information, education, and communication materials on DRM. Implementing partners will ensure transparent beneficiary selection processes informed by engagement with relevant stakeholders and the affected population. The implementing partners will foster community participation through different meetings, consultations, and awareness-raising activities. A complaint and feedback mechanism will be established by implementing partners to address concerns and provide regular updates on assistance delivered, planned, and the resolution of complaints and feedback received. Community dialogues and messaging will be conducted by implementing partners, employing clear, accessible, and understandable information disseminated through appropriate communication channels.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will mainstream Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) into the project cycle. UNDP is a member of the PSEA network and regularly participates in meetings. UNDP has a strong PSEA policy, confidential and accessible reporting mechanisms, and a focal point in place. The project will utilize the existing PSEA system to ensure that it is mainstreamed across project activities and approaches. The project will prepare a gender action plan with PSEA as a key component. The project will provide mandatory PSEA training to its staff, partners, and volunteers. The project will utilize PSEA awareness materials, including key PSEA messages, and ensure that the available Complaint Feedback Mechanisms are visible to beneficiaries. The project will ensure that confidential, accessible, and culturally appropriate complaint and feedback mechanisms are available. Implementing partners (DRC, RIMES, NGI) are also members of the PSEA network and actively engage in its meetings. These partners will take proactive measures to cultivate a safe and secure environment, safeguarding against sexual exploitation and abuse. This includes enforcing clear PSEA policies, implementing accessible and culturally appropriate PSEA awareness materials, conducting regular orientation sessions for staff, volunteers, and project participants, implementing confidential and accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms, ensuring transparent investigation and accountability measures, coordinating with other organizations, and fostering a strong commitment from organizational leadership.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project solely focuses on strengthening disaster risk management, disaster risk reduction, and community resilience for Rohingya refugees. The project will support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy. The project will closely involve and assist ISCG in advancing DRR/DRM within the realm of the Rohingya crisis response and will facilitate coherent DRR efforts among the stakeholders working within the camps. The project also substantially focuses on environmental issues and climate change mitigation measures by adopting nature-based solutions. UNDP and its partners will coordinate with relevant sectors (SCCCM, FSS, EPRWG, EEN) and organizations (FAO, AoR SM agencies) for early warning systems during the implementation phase.
