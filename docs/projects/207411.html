Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food security and, the economic and social well-being of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families in the target areas. To build the resilience of vulnerable households, NRC will complement food assistance with livelihood activities using a market-based approach that refers to local traders so to improve access to income and revive/strengthen the local economy. The intervention will be geared towards supporting the most vulnerable displacement-affected households and at the same time building their capacity in food production, food transformation and marketing.
NRC will identify project participants using household-level socio-economic vulnerability assessments. The assessments will prioritize the most vulnerable households, taking into consideration, the age and gender of the head of household, the number of pregnant and lactating women, the number of elderly people, and the number of people living with disabilities and chronic illnesses, as well as other relevant vulnerability indexes such as food consumption score, number of meals per day, possession (or lack of) productive assets.
Market assessments and cash feasibility survey will confirm the modality of each distribution, and whenever possible, NRC will prioritize cash-based intervention.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Displaced households have lost most, if not all, of their productive assets (seeds stocks, agricultural tools, livestock) thus preventing them to conduct activities that have traditionally be their first source of income. Moreover, they have also lost access or have limited access to farmlands due to enduring insecurity. The most vulnerable households have had to sell their remaining productive assets (negative coping mechanisms), thus further decreasing their capacity to meet their basic needs and increasing their dependency on humanitarian assistance. According to an assessment conducted by NRC, less than 30% of respondents have sufficient income to cover their basic needs. The data also shows also more almost three out of four households spend more than 80% of their income on food expenses. Conflict has had a significant impact on households’ ability to conduct livelihood activities, particularly in the departments of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga. Furthermore, agricultural production and livestock activities haven also been affected by floods, late rains and drought, plant diseases, inflation of agricultural inputs, as well as epizootics diseases resurfacing due to the interruption of vaccination and animal disease prevention activities. The combined security situation and the structural poverty jeopardise the launch of local economic initiatives, and prevent households from building their resilience to future shocks. In Logone-et-Chari and Mayo Tsanaga divisions, the acquisition of food in markets represents a great challenge for those who lost their livelihood during displacement. The Cameroon-Nigeria border was a major commercial trading axis before the deterioration of the security context. The disruption of this economic route has limited access to manufactured products (traditionally coming from Nigeria) and hindered the ability of farmer households to sell livestock and agricultural product on the Nigerian market. In addition, attacks have weakened the local trade fabric, impoverishing thousands of traders who depended on trade with Nigeria and worsening the already precarious economic situation of the inhabitants of this ecologically fragile region. The unemployment rate of IDPs and youth is disproportionally high in the region. The economic activity most practiced by the displaced populations is currently Unskilled work in exchange for daily wages.
