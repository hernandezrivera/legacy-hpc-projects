Project Summary

The ‘Provision of life saving interventions among vulnerable pregnant and lactating women and cash for nutrition trainings and referrals of complicated SAM cases to Stabilization Centers in kapoeta East County is aimed at improving MIYCN, immunization coverage, use of therapeutic foods and WASH practices among households with SAM cases (6-59 months) both complicated and uncomplicated. It also seeks to increase coverage of SC services through increased enrollment of eligible cases. A total of 18 health/nutrition centers (PHCCs/PHCUs) in Kapoeta East County will be reached and Mother-to-Mother support groups (MtMSG) will be formed and trained on WASH, MIYCN, gender and protection packages during SC and OTP clinic days. Cash beneficiaries will only receive cash after attending and completing all the training packages. As well, cash will be given to mothers/caregivers of children 6-59 months diagnosed with complicated SAM on condition that they have arrived and have been enrolled into SC following referral from the OTP and discharged as cured. Prior to the project implementation, AFSS staff, health workers and Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) will be trained on the project implementation modalities. The Cash component will constitute 40% (cash for referrals and cash for trainings) and the remaining 60% will cater for life saving interventions.

Needs

Recent SMART survey conducted in 2018 in Kapoeta East County (KEC) reflected a GAM of 10.4% and SAM of 1.8% which is below the WHO emergency threshold. However it is still classified as serious. In EES, the prevalence of minimum dietary diversity was at only 8.0%. The average meal frequency was equally very poor. Only 29.6% of the children 6- 23 months had received solid, semisolid or soft foods, the minimum number of times or more during the previous day of the survey. Only 2.0% of the children had received the Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) (December 2017 FSNMS). According to the Interim Guidelines on the CMAM of South Sudan (2017), children with complicated SAM are to be referred to SC for inpatient care. Yet, due to factors like; long distances, inadequate financial resources to care for children left at home, poor attitude towards SC, and limited male involvement in key family care practices, there is a relatively low enrollment of referred complicated SAM cases into SC. This results into late case management, poor adherence to treatment and subsequently malnutrition related deaths. To combat this situation, AFSS in partnership with SSHF commenced the implementation of a ‘cash for Nutrition trainings and referrals to SCs project with the objective of increasing adherence to nutrition Services, attendance & completion of nutrition trainings as well as social behavior change in KEC from March to August 2018. This resulted into improved admissions and retention in SC from 8 cases in 2017 to 34 cases by August 2018 and up to 870 mothers, 116 men attending and completing nutrition training packages. The cash component increased caregiver’s commitment to take their children to the SC as compared to the previous year without cash incentives for referrals. Also the Mother to mother support group numbers increased from 8 to 65 groups with improved participations in nutrition related activities. After this period/project to date, AFSS commenced the implementation of the “provision of life saving interventions among vulnerable pregnant and lactating women in KEC EES” project for 2nd allocation to sustainably strengthen and improve uptake and outcomes of CMAM (OTP and SC) services through training sessions on MIYCN, WASH, nutrition education, GBV with focus on SBCC especially for men as they are the final decision makers at homes. AFSS therefore, proposes to resume the implementation of cash for nutrition training and referrals to SC so as to increase SC admissions in to SC and reduced child morbidity and mortality resulting from late management of complicated SAM and as well improve uptake and outcomes of CMAM services and promote positive social behavior change. How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

AFSS will work closely with the local communities and local authorities and also share feedbacks. Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

AFSS will design visual pictorial visibility items / IEC for beneficiaries with specific messages and will conduct orientations training and sensitization meetings to beneficiaries in the communities and schools with focus on Nutrition, WASH,MIYCN, GBV and importance of honoring referrals to SC and also SBCC .The messages will ensure the project is non discriminatory, respectful, promotes safety, security of beneficiaries and confidential. The IEC materials will be designed to portray specific messages in relation to MIYCN, casuses of Malnutrition, exclusive breastfeeeding,Cholera, hygiene, and accountability. The GBV messages will focus on Rape, exploitation, early marriages that can be understood by both educated and non educated community members. The messages will be as specific and clear as possible to the Nutrition context. Other already existing literatures and suggested key messages from other donors, partners and stakeholders like Government Ministry of Water, UNICEF, WFP, SSHF, WASH cluster, Nutrition Cluster will be used as well to provide uniform messages to the beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will also be given IEC materials like banners, T-shirts, flyers,stickers to hung on their walls at homes and clearly visible areas at homes so as to always remember, reflect and implement the key messages in the banners or flyers.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The AFSS cash for referrals and nutrition project takes in considerations issues of protection from sexual exploitation and abuse seriously. AFSS as an organisation has strong rules and policies concerning sexual exploitation and abuse whereby the perpetrators of the abuse are handled in a disciplinary committee, terminated and or referred to the law enforcement authorities where it deems so. The policies are inline with the national guidelines and humanitarian policies on sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries / office. Beneficiaries will be educated about their rights, project entitlements among others. The community/ beneficiaries will be given Toll free hotlines to report any sexual harassment case directly. The Nutrition Project will also benefit all vulnerable groups in the targeted 18 Nutrition centres in Kapoeta East through advocating for prevention of theft and looting of nutrition supplies among concerned parties as it deprives vulnerable children with acute malnutrition of treatment. AFSS will train its staffs, Health workers, community leaders including women leaders and hold community dialogues to be able to detect any exploitation by implementing partners like issuance of less ration to malnourished child or giving less cash that entitled to the beneficiary. The vulnerable women (PLWS), children under 5 years (disabled persons with special needs) will be identified and given first priorities during activity days. Programme activities will be conducted under shelters. Breastfeeding/baby friendly corners will be established at each nutrition site to ensure that breastfeeding mothers and their children are protected. During activity days AFSS will screen for victims of gender based violence. Any identified case will be linked and referred to GBV implementing partners and the local authorities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

At the start of the project, a baseline assessment will be conducted. This will be used to re-structure project actives to meet the community specific needs. Locations where the community meetings will be held will be chosen and agreed upon after consultations with the community leaders to avoid protection risks. During sensitization meetings messages on protection will be communicated in friendly and respectful manner that does not bring segregation, discrimination on either gender or age and barriers to access of services will be identified and responses effected. Any cases of violence or protection issues identified will be linked to protection partners or the county gender and protection office. Kapoeta East is currently safe and secured in terms of security. AFSS will consult and coordinate with the State and county government security wing and the office of the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) every time it plans to make site travels about the security of the area. This will help in keeping the humanitarian workers and the beneficiaries safe in the areas of operations. The Locals of the areas of implementation shall be given priority in the recruitment so that the community will own the project and not disturb and destabilize staffs stationed at the facilities. In case of any outbreak of insecurity in a field site location, the staffs shall be relocated until the government settles the insecurity issue and beneficiaries informed to stay safe. AFSS will provide the cars to relocate the staffs. In terms of logistics and movements, AFSS will ensure that the hired Vehicles have third party insurance or comprehensive insurance that will ensure safety of the staffs moving in the vehicle.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The cash referral and nutrition training project activities takes into consideration protection threats like violence, coercion, exploitation, deprivations and neglect of beneficiaries. The project design is based of respect of gender, dignity and accountability. All gender markers will be treated as equal when it comes to entitlements. Men, Women, Boys and girls will be given equal treatments and opportunities.The following activities addresses the inequality issues: Public awareness campaigns, Dissemination of WASH ,GBV IEC materials and Community dialogues on Nutrition, Music Dance and drama, GBV, Protections all stresses Equality, supporting and strengthening local women associations to promote Gender Equality, accountability and protection. The admission of beneficiaries will dis-aggregated into Male and Female. The programme will target all households of children with severe acute malnutrition without segregation based on gender and age, The community Nutrition volunteers shall consist of both male and Female. During community mobilization and sensitization, identified gender issues will be emphasized. Gender Equality Programing will be emphasized to ensure that nobody is discriminated against because of his/her gender/age and all efforts are made towards inclusion as well as targeting those groups that need more attention or more assistance. Men will be encouraged to participate in key family care practices especially in looking after the rest of the children when the mother is away with the hospitalized child at the SC. During sensitization sessions, the responsibility of men and women in child upbringing and nutritional wellbeing of the family will be emphasized. In general, all program activities will seek to reach men, women, boys and girls (MWBG) and build their capacity on social behavior change.
