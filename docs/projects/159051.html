Project Summary

Project has started since 1.08.2019 and will be finished on 31.07.2021. Project has already received donor funding
The total number of direct beneficiaries for 2020 is 12.690 including:
10810 individual beneficiaries (food, health, MPCG, transportation):
• Food: 2.700. Distribution of 5.400 food packages to 2.700 conflict-affected people: Immediate access to food via distribution of 1-2 food packages per individual to cover gaps in nutrition for 1-2 months. One beneficiary may obtain a second package 1 month later depending on severity level.
• Health (physical): 2910. Distribution of 11760 medical cash grants and/or pharmacy vouchers to 5.480 beneficiaries: Beneficiaries are provided with purchasing power (cash grant via bank cards) to access life-saving medicines, surgeries or diagnostics OR are provided with immediate access to in-kind medicines (via vouchers). The value per beneficiary is 4.000 UAH as one-time payment or 4 monthly instalments per 1.000 UAH depending on individual needs.
• Health (psychosocial): 1700. Provision of 456 psycho-social group sessions to 1040 beneficiaries
and provision of 3960 psycho-social individual consultations to 660 beneficiaries: Depending on their needs beneficiaries will receive up to 6 individual sessions (face-to-face or via phone) or participate in up to 6 group sessions (with 6-12 participants each).
• MPCG: 240. Provision of 960 multi-purpose cash grants to 2400 individuals: Selected beneficiaries receive 4 unrestricted and unconditional monthly cash grants amounting to 970 UAH each to cover gaps in their basic needs. If needed, also a one-time payment is possible. Funds are transferred to the family head via bank card. Up to 4 family members may be supported depending on the severity level.
• Transportation: 300. Provision of 1200 transportation services to 300 beneficiaries in 10 remote settlements of buffer zone: This activity is a pilot, which will be implemented by Caritas Mariupol in the Southern part of the buffer zone. The aim is to provide beneficiaries with access to essential infrastructure hospitals or government agencies outside the buffer zone, in order to enable them to secure their basic needs.
• Winterization:1880 households support (with average household size of about 2,6 beneficiaries). Distribution of 3760 tons of solid heating material to 1880 conflict-affected household: Households are provided with immediate access to solid heating material in form of 2 tons of sunflower briquettes sufficient for half a winter season - or coal/wood in equivalent - to cover gaps in winterization needs.

1 - Needs

• Need to overcome isolation due to movement & security restrictions: The introduction of the Joint Forces Operation (JFO) and adoption of the ‘Special Order on implementation of the security and defense measures’ in April 2018, have resulted in additional restrictions on the freedom of movement. Access to certain settlements is allowed only for people who have documents confirming their residence in these settlements. People continue to report about additional checks when passing through these internal checkpoints in GCA, which leads to delays in movement. Restrictions of movement for people, goods and services between GCA and NGCA reinforce a strong sense of isolation amongst the affected population. • Need for functioning basic infrastructure (health, shops etc.): In many villages and settlements close to the contact line basic services such as electricity, shops and markets, medical care including ambulances or water supply are not available due to geographic isolation and security restrictions. In the whole buffer zone there is a lack of specialists, who could provide services, as most of the specialists have left and moved to more secure places. Millions of people living along the 'contact line' and in NGCA are still unable to meet their basic health care needs due to difficulties to reach appropriate health care services, lack of access to quality medicines and medical supplies and lack of sufficient income to cover the out-of-pocket medical costs (HNO2019, OCHA).• Need for transportation services: Roads have partly been destroyed by military operations or are in a bad state. Public transport is rare and does not reach all villages, private transport providers are afraid of related risks. Increasing transport prices are largely not affordable to vulnerable population. The lack of accessible and affordable transport prevents the affected population from accessing still functioning basic infrastructure both within and outside the buffer zone• Need for improved purchasing power: The monthly income of people living in GCA of Donetsk/Luhansk Oblasts is low with about 2.090 UAH per household member (about EUR 70), which is far beyond the subsistence level calculated by the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine (UAH 3,252 as of September 2018). The share of the population in GCA living near the 'contact line' with inadequate food consumption is about 16%, while food consumption in winter is generally lower than in other times of the year and while certain segments of the population are more affected than others. Thus, it is estimated that 30% of female headed households have poor and borderline levels of food consumption. (HNO2019, OCHA).Research has shown that in line with increasingly harsh living conditions 78% of households in GCA and 71% in NGCA applied negative coping strategies, the highest level since April 2016 (REACH MSNA-2 data related to Febr. 2018). Most vulnerable within GCA are the nearly 30,000 persons residing in villages closest to the 'contact line'. Moreover, population living in rural areas of the buffer zone is more affected than people living in larger settlements or urban areas. A REACH-led winter assessment of humanitarian needs conducted in the 5-km zone along the contact line of GCA has shown that 37% of households in rural areas reported insufficient heating compared to only 11% of non-rural households. Adapting REACH’s figures to the population residing within 20 kilometres of the 'contact line', the population with insufficient winter heating can be estimated at 84,450 persons in 35,000 households. The project will take these regional specifics into regard, when it comes to both prioritization of locations and kind of support to be delivered (e.g. distribution of cash assistance in places, where functioning markets are accessible versus distribution of in-kind support in places without such access or with only low quality supplies). Priority will be given to rural locations within 0-5 km

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Beneficiaries of food assistance have improved coverage of basic needs in nutrition 1 month they received their last relief pack.Activity 1: Distribution of 5400 food packages to 2700 conflict-affected people: Immediate access to food via distribution of 1-2 food packages per individual to cover gaps in nutrition for 1-2 months. One beneficiary may obtain a second package 1 month later depending on severity level. Outcome 2: Beneficiaries of winterization assistance have improved coverage of their needs in fuel for half a cold season 2 months after they received in-kind heating material.Activity 2: Winterization: Distribution of 3760 tons of solid heating material to 1880 conflict-affected household: Households are provided with immediate access to solid heating material in form of 2 tons of sunflower briquettes sufficient for half a winter season - or coal/wood in equivalent - to cover gaps in winterization needs. Outcome 3: Beneficiaries of medical assistance access quality life-saving and essential health care services, including psychosocial and mental health supportActivity 3.1: Distribution of 11760 medical cash grants and/or pharmacy vouchers to 2910 beneficiaries: Beneficiaries are provided with purchasing power (cash grant via bank cards) to access life-saving medicines, surgeries or diagnostics OR are provided with immediate access to in-kind medicines (via vouchers). The value per beneficiary is 4.000 UAH as one-time payment or 4 monthly instalments per 1.000 UAH depending on individual needs. Activity 3.2: Provision of 1200 transportation services to 300 beneficiaries in 10 remote settlements of buffer zone: The aim is to provide beneficiaries with access to essential in hospitals or government agencies outside the buffer zone, in order to enable them to secure their basic needs. In a first step and in cooperation with local authorities and other NGOs Caritas Mariupol will select 10 villages/ settlements with no or strongly limited access to essential infrastructure. Activity 3.3: Provision of 456 psycho-social group sessions to 1040 beneficiaries and рrovision of 3960 psycho-social individual consultations to 650 beneficiaries: Depending on their needs beneficiaries will receive up to 6 individual sessions (face-to-face or via phone) or participate in up to 6 group sessions (with 6-12 participants each). Once a new location is entered, an initial awareness raising and information session is provided to a big audience. Further sessions are offered to more specific and smaller groups taking into account individual needs. Sessions are conducted by trained psychologists and include practical exercises on how to care about mental health. Volume, content and target regions of assistance will be coordinated with the MHPS sub-cluster and other relevant NGOs. Selection of beneficiaries and identification of suitable premises are organized by mobile teams in cooperation with local authorities and activists. Outcome 4: Beneficiaries of multi-purpose cash assistance increased the purchasing power and have improved coverage of basic needs 1 month after they received their last cash payment.Activity 4: Provision of 9600 multi-purpose cash grants to 2400 individuals: Selected beneficiaries receive 4 unrestricted and unconditional monthly cash grants amounting to 970 UAH each to cover gaps in their basic needs. If needed, also a one-time payment is possible. Funds are transferred to the family head via bank card. Up to 4 family members may be supported depending on the severity level.
