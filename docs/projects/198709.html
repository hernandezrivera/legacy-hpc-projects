Project Summary

Following the rate at which the community of Dikwa LGA is faced with the irregular and yet high tensioned attacks by the members of the armed opposition group, leaving the community tensed and in a worser situation, due to the destruction the AOGs left after each attack; this has left the community vulnerable as livelihoods have been destroyed, public and private infrastructures destroyed and businesses crippled. These has corresponding effects which has left many displaced with little or no means of livelihoods which has resulted in the need for humanitarian assistance.
Women and girls are affected by various forms of GBV, ranging from the culture of Purdah and forced and early marriages, to physical, denial of resources, mental or sexual assault. Nearly 1 in 3 Nigerian women have experienced physical violence by age 15 . Regardless of the social context in which GBV occurs, incidences of GBV are usually covered in silence and under-reported due to the fear of stigma faced by the survivor and high leaders/shareholders in community. Further, the disruption in the socioeconomic lives of the women in North-East Nigeria by AOG insurgency has placed women and girls at a high risk of all forms of abuse from the insurgents, security agents deployed to protect the communities, and other members of the community. Particularly in Dikwa LGA in Borno state with latitude 12029.92 longitude 13555, which is a flood prone LGA and most dreaded and has being constantly attacked by AOGs. This constant attacked has left Dikwa LGA with high rate of GBV/ASRH cases. Dikwa LGA is still affected by the long time norms and social tradition, where girls are not allow to go to school, early marriage, silence on act GBV in communities and camps. Several cases of GBV has being recorded in the camps as most comps are congested due to the flood recorded from July to September 2022. This heightened the denial of resources, physical violence, sexual assault etc. in the LGA.
This project is aimed at improving access promoting awareness on Prevention of Gender Based Violence (GBV) and Adolescent Sexual Reproductive Health (ASRH), targeting mostly women, girls, boys and men. The project shall tackle the shortage of GBV in the community through the direct implementation of strengthening referral pathways, creating awareness and enabling dignity in the life of the conflict affected population through the GBV/ASRH component.

Implementing Partners

GREENCODE is adopting a gender aware approach that provides for immediate response (case management, MHPSS, outreach, life skill, ASRH services) and building resilience. It targets the immediate protection (GBV/ASRH) and WASH intervention for GBV survivors from both IDPs and host populations. The approach ensures meaningful consultation with organisations, stakeholders, boys, girls, women and men especially GBV and SEA survivors through FGDs and continuous input through participatory monitoring to inform the direction of intervention. This approach will ensure that specific needs of GBV survivors participants benefit from the project interventions, while understanding that the root causes of exclusion and gender inequality, SGBV/GBV and ASRH issues require longer term interventions. Local community structures will be actively involved in mobilizing and implementation, participating in direct implementation, and other partners in the field, and GBV/WASH clusters Working Group. GREENCODE strategy focuses on gender participatory programming throughout project cycles and will encourage women and men in communities to take agreed responsibilities within this specific project cycle and beyond. The specific activities proposed in this project—GBV/ASRH will also address broader emergency response plan.

1 - Needs

Armed Opposition Group (AOG)-related violence and military counter insurgency operations have severely increased the protection risks faced by the affected population, estimated at 14 million people in the three worst affected States of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. Many people are forced to flee their homes and communities in search of safety and security causing large-scale displacement undermining human development and livelihoods and increasing protection risks. Persons with special needs and vulnerabilities including the elderly, the chronically sick, people with physical and mental disabilities, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied/separated children, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant and lactating women have been the most affected.Over 26 million people living in the North-East have been affected by the crisis – and an estimated 14 million are in need of humanitarian assistance. It is estimated that 80% of the 1.8 million internally displaced people (IDPs) live in host communities. The epicentre of the crisis, Borno State alone hosts 67% of all IDPs. Over 52% of IDPs in host communities are said to be in need of urgent lifesaving humanitarian assistance, posing strain on infrastructure and other resources in an already economically challenged region of North-East Nigeria. The incidence of gender-based violence (GBV) is growing astronomical with the activities of the insurgency in the North East. From forced and early marriages to the physical, mental or sexual assault on a woman, 1 in 3 Nigerian women have experienced physical violence by age 15 (NDHS 2013).Scant resources are depleted, causing tension between IDPs and host families. There are also potential tensions between those affected by flood, returning IDPs and those who never left their LGAs due to alleged suspicion of association with Boko Haram. Armed elements were reported as returning with the IDPs and this poses significant threats to the host community. Many women and children stay at home for fear of abduction and in some communities the absence of men and adolescent boys revealed serious threats of arrest or forced recruitment. Dikwa Local Government Area (LGA) is located in eastern Borno. Dikwa town lies 90km east of Maiduguri (the state capital) and is a gateway to Bama, Ngala, Mafa and Marte LGAs. Nine (9) of the ten (10) wards in Dikwa LGA are inaccessible to humanitarian partners due to insecurity. The humanitarian response is therefore limited to the people in Dikwa town, who are dependent on humanitarian assistance due to lack of livelihoods opportunities

2 - Activities

SGBV survivors access quality, survivor-centered and professional clinical care for sexual violence and all forms of SGBV,SGBV survivors safely access quality, survivor-centered mental health and psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery, GBV survivors access appropriate, quality case management services including coordinated care and support to navigate available services, SGBV programming addresses harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality and informs communities about available services, Adolescent girls and boys adopt sexual and reproductive health behaviour protecting themselves from health risks and Adolescent girls and boys develop adolescent-adult partnerships in their communities. the project will target women, adolescent girl, boy and men which will be supported by ECO funding.
