Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve WASH conditions of the vulnerable areas in Gaza Strip and ensuring better access to safe water and sanitation facilities. This is going to be accomplished through the construction and upgrading of existing storm water and wastewater infrastructure. The intervention will in turn will help protect vulnerable individuals in Al Salam and Abu Hamad areas in Khan Younis governorate from the risk of percolation of wastewater from existing cesspits and from flood during the winter season. It will also help protect people in Alkaram in area surrounding al-Sawari School and Almanayaa areas (in Jabalia) from floods during the winter season.
The project is in line with the strategic objectives 2 and 3 and is also aligned with WASH cluster objectives 2 and 3. The proposed intervention responds to SDG # 6 which aims to ensure available and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all, and includes targets for universal access to drinking water, sanitation, and hygiene for all by 2030.
The project will target 16,861 people in need who depend on only cesspits to discharge their wastewater (3801 women, 3957 men, 3800 girls and 3955 boys, 505 elderly and 843 people with disabilities).
The project will be implemented in cooperation with Youth Empowerment Center (YEC) and includes the following components:
1. Installation of sewer collection network and HH connections: Construction of 6-kilometer-long wastewater networks including 310 HHs connections and 900 m storm water pipe collection in Bani Suhaila in khan Younis governorate
2. Installation of 650 m storm water system through establishing collection network with all fittings in Al Karama area in Jabalia.
Details:
In south Governorate 1:
Activity 1.1) Installation of 6,000 meters of sewer collection network at Al-Salam & Abu Hamed areas. The Target: 310 households (1,860 individuals, 948 males, 912 females).
Activity 1.2) Constructing of 900 meters storm water system through establishing collection network at the targeted area suffering from recurrent flooding including all required fittings (e.g., manholes, gullies, …etc.) at Al-Salam & Abu Hamed areas in Bani Suhaila in khan Younis Governorate.
In North governorate 2:
Activity 2.1: Constructing of 650 meters storm water system through establishing collection network including all fittings (e.g., manholes, gullies, …etc.) at Al Karama area
-2.1.1: AL-Manayaa area: Installation of concrete rain pipes of 100 cm diameter, with a total length of 250 meters, and manholes with a diameter of 150 cm.
- 2.1.2: Surrounding of al-Sawari school: Installation of concrete rain pipes of 100 cm diameter, with a total length of 400 meters, and manholes with a diameter of 150 cm and Square manhole 2x2m.
The activities will be implemented in full coordination with the municipalities of Bani Suhaila and Jabalia, CMWU, and PWA.

1 - Needs

Based on HNO 2022, more than 1.35 million Palestinians across the oPt are affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and will be in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2022-2023. The WASH Cluster partners’ assessments and consultations identified four main key issues generating people’s need for WASH assistance: insufficient water supply to households, poor sanitation and solid waste services, limited WASH services to public facilities, and risk of flooding.According to the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU, WASH service provider), around 27% of the Gaza Strip population are not connected to municipal wastewater networks and discharge their sewage to poorly managed cesspits.QRCS has conducted a rapid assessment with 10 participants representing 10 households (3 women, 7 men) on the 29th of September 2022 at Abu Hamed area in Bani Sohaila in Khan Younis. The assessment included assessing transportation network, domestic water network, sewage and storm networks, the public health and environmental impact assessment on different gender groups from all ages. The results showed that the area suffers from very poor sanitation system, 100 % of households reported that they have no access to functional or improved sanitation facilities, and that they depend only on cesspits. There are 120 cesspits in the area which potentially increase the risk for the spread of waterborne diseases and exposure to hazardous substances. In addition, the results showed that 100% of households reported that their daily life is affected by floods and there were at least 6 out of the 10 households that totally flooded in winter. In summer, the rancid smell attracts flies and increases risks facing children playing outside as there is kindergarten for children in the area. Moreover, the participants reported that the cesspits have negative impacts on their socioeconomic situation as they need to pay to the municipality to empty the cesspits which increase the disputes between the neighbours especially the poorest HHs that don’t have any source of income. In addition, the participants reported the high risk of landslides resulting from cesspits which led to different injuries and death in some times.In addition, according to HNO 2022 93,000 Palestinians in Gaza reside in low-lying areas at risk of flooding events during winter due to heavy rains and overflow of stormwater facilities and sewage pumping station. Recently, due to climate change, flooding became more frequent and has caused significant losses of assets, displacement, disrupted access to livelihoods and services, and exacerbated the risk of waterborne diseases. Based on MSNA 2022, 12.0% of Gaza households are affected by flooding, 41.2% of these households reported their shelter being impacted by flooding and 87.1% reported floods disrupting their daily activities. Due to the traditional gender division of labour in Palestinian society, women and girls bear the bulk of the burden related to the water shortage and wastewater shortage networks, as they are expected to secure the water needed for domestic consumption and care for the children’s hygiene. Women and girls also have additional water needs related to menstrual hygiene and health. Other groups disproportionally affected by poor access to water include the elderly, the sick and people with disabilities. The poor access to WASH services plays a significant role in increasing GBV against women. 68.4% of women reported that lack of domestic water exposes them to violence.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

QRCS and YEC adopt an open-door policy that focuses on the open communication between the organization and the people. The population in Bani Suhaila, Khan Youner and in the northern governorate will be educated on their right to report any issues they face during all phases of the intervention. QRCS and YEC will try to use active and passive reporting methods. As part of the passive methods, QRCS and YEC will encourage beneficiaries to use the complaints boxes and generic emails in order to assure a proper address of their concerns. Regarding the active data collection, QRCS and YEC will conduct field visits and individual interviews with the beneficiaries in order to get a clear perspective of their issues and concerns. Field visits and individual interviews will be used for two main purposes, the first one aims at informing the population about their rights to speak out, the importance of their voices, the feedback mechanisms, and how the organizations can respond. The second one aims at collecting data about people’s concerns, complaints, recommendations and suggestions. Moreover, QRCS and YEC plan to conduct a satisfaction survey in order to collect quantitative data. QRCS and YEC document the feedback according to the organizations’ templates and refer them to the relevant personnel. QRCS and YEC deal with any feedback according to the internal accountability policy ensuring proper addressing of the feedback in a timely, ethical, dignity-saving and humanitarian manner. In addition, the feedback represents a core part of the project evaluation, and QRCS and YEC take into account this feedback in future project designs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

QRCS and YEC have developed organizational policies- based on international and local protocols- to assure the maximum benefits and the minimum harm of the beneficiaries. Those policies emphasize on the safety of the beneficiaries as a top priority and adapted a violence-free environment and minimum threats. QRCS and YEC follow strict rules to ensure that procurement and contracting procedure follow rigorous reviews and conform with procurement best practices. Quality and safety are important factors when deciding on items procured and services obtained. The activities implemented will be in line of the needed decided through ongoing consultations with the population and will be implemented in a manner that respects their privacy, takes into consideration individual concerns, and meets highest standards and specifications. Activities planned to be carried out in the north have at the heart of them children’s safety as they aim to prevent street floods caused by storm water during winter especially in streets leading to schools. QRCS and YEC highly consider the adaptability and the cultural accessibility to respond to the cultural backgrounds of the beneficiaries. The working staff includes both women and men and is trained on using gender-sensitive language. Furthermore, there are clear lines for the privacy and confidentiality that are shared between the working staff, and there are clear rules for data collection that include not collecting unneeded information nor sharing information with anyone but except the working staff. If any risk to the beneficiaries is shown, QRCS and YEC will act in a timely manner to protect their safety and dignity.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

QRCS and YEC are independent organizations that are not affected by commercial nor political powers. QRCS follows the seven standards of the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent in humanitarian action (IFRC) to assure the equitable distribution of services to the vulnerable population regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. At the start of the project, QRCS and YEC will discuss the possible barriers to equity, and they will prepare a plan to mitigate those barriers and assure equal opportunities. The plan will be followed at all phases of the project alongside the M&E plan. In addition, QRCS and YEC try to adapt the project activities to respond to the special needs of vulnerable people in order to maximize equity. The services will be provided to all the population in designated areas of implementation regardless of social status, disability status, or beliefs. QRCS and YEC will respond to any inequity concerns in a timely effective manner. Furthermore, during the tender process, QRCS will ensure a transparent process according to its guideline without leaning to any commercial power.
