Project Summary

The myriad of protection concerns coupled with scarce resources across Syria has led to harmful coping mechanisms reported to be on the rise, including child labor and child/forced marriage. The crisis continues to have a gendered impact evident in adolescent girls exposed to harmful and discriminatory gender norms including gender-based violence (GBV) both within and outside their homes, impacting their safety and capacity to move freely. Covid-19 coupled with the economic crisis compounds protection risks leading to multiple issues including an increase in corporal punishment against children due to caregivers’ stress throughout Syria and if unaddressed, the impact(s) can last a lifetime. Findings from the IRC multi-sector needs analysis (MSNA) conducted in April 2021 confirm high levels of psychosocial distress for women and girls, with 62% women households (HHs) and 31% adolescent girls HHs highlighted signs of distress in the previous three months. In NWS, over 82% of informants reported child labor activities that prevented access to education, while 43% reported children’s separation from original caregivers due to economic reasons or death of parents, which resulted in children trauma /psychosocial distress6 and exposure to street life. There is minimal funding across northern Syria, to meet general protection needs.
The goal of this project is to reduce protection risks respond to the needs of children and families in Northwest Syria through the provision of holistic child protection services for
children, caregivers, and community members. Our interventions include:
1) Specialized case management teams providing: Comprehensive child protection case management to identify the most vulnerable children in NWS and supports them to full recovery from violence, abuse, neglect, and coercion. In addition to awareness-raising for community members.
2) Emergency Child Protection Response team responding to new displacement and Adhoc situations by providing:
- Emergency Parenting Sessions for caregivers (Male and female) to equip them in dealing with the stress and implementing positive discipline techniques that ensure a safe and healthy child development.
- Structured and non-structured psychosocial support to children and adolescents aimed at helping them overcome trauma and distress as well as learning how to healthily express themselves and their emotions.
- Awareness-raising for community members on important topics such as early marriage, child labor, child development, and psychological needs and COVID-19 precautions.
- Training and establishment of child protection committees from community leaders and influential individuals who support the child protection team in raising awareness, supporting existing positive community structures, and channeling assistance to the most vulnerable community members.
- Provision of PFA and referrals as appropriate.
IRC accommodates the needs of children with disabilities whenever possible through the rehabilitation of spaces (static and mobile centers) to ensure access for CWDs and by adjusting curriculums and materials, such as printing materials with Brail, using interpretation for children with hearing disabilities, prioritizing case management for CWD's and using cash for protection to purchase mobility/disability devices and conducting small scale home rehabilitation for CWD's access.

4 - Needs

IRC relies on the most recent Humanitarian Needs Assessment, specific field and sector-based assessments, and internal resources such as Multi-Sectoral Needs Analysis.According to 2021 HNO, 13.1 million people require protection services across Syria with a staggering breakdown of figures classified as; 35% severe, 39% extreme, and 21% catastrophic.As of July 2021, displacements remain a major issue with 2.7M internally displaced persons (IDPs) reported in (NWS).IDPs face dire conditions and fears of reoccurring violence as well as occasional disputes between the different parties present in NWS. Movement restrictions in and out of settlements, and lost documentation. Civil status documentation (CSD) such as birth, family, and marriage records lost through multiple displacements, is a significant barrier to housing, land, and property rights and access to humanitarian assistance and puts people at risk of abuse or exploitation. Meanwhile, almost 60% of Syria’s nearly 21 million people are food insecure, according to the UN’s (WFP), and lack secure access to sufficient safe and nutritious food.Issues facing Syria are multifaceted – from health to protection and livelihoods, children and CWDs due to existing power dynamics and social norms, are overburdened with the aftermath of the Syrian crisis where their rights are undermined on a daily basis. Major identified risks include separation from caregivers, child labor, lack of access to education, and psychosocial distress.

5 - Implementation of activities

CP team operates through outreach with WPE and PRoL teams in Maaretmisreen and Dana areas.Most vulnerable children and families are identified through community outreach and coordination with local councils and service providers while providing regular information/introduction sessions to community members on available services and complaint and feedback mechanisms. Our interventions include raising awareness in the community on CP concerns, forming and training child protection committees, providing structured and non-structured PSS and case management for children and adolescents, providing parenting skills to caregivers, and conducting assessments to identify persons in urgent need of support to be linked to services, including cash assistance which increases their coping capacity facing unexpected and significant shocks which may be mitigated through a one-time injection of cash - this intervention is integrated with case management.IRC provides monthly and on-needs basis reporting of activities including 4W's, use of case management tools, cluster awareness-raising material to maintain oversight and mitigate risk in Remote Management environments to collect regular data on service satisfaction and client responsiveness and uses (CPC’s) and discussions with caregivers to assess implementation and promote child participation. IRC promotes Client Responsiveness and Feedback Mechanisms-child friendly to ensure children’s opinions inform the design and delivery of services.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is limited for specific persons/groups and risk of lack of safe, accessible, and inclusive feedback and complaint mechanism.IRC recognizes the unequal power dynamic and the resulting potential for exploitation inherent in ourwork with local beneficiaries and that exploitation undermines the credibility of our work and severely damages victims of these exploitive acts and their families and communities. Accordingly, IRC maintains and upholds PSEA policy which explicitly prohibits all forms of beneficiary exploitation and abuse by IRC staff. IRC expects staff to uphold the highest ethical standard of integrity, accountability, andtransparency in the delivery of goods and services while executing the responsibilities of their position. This includes prohibiting sexual favors or relations, exploitation, discrimination, engaging in sexual misconduct with children, and abuse of power. IRC considers: Exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries,whether of a sexual nature or not, by staff constitutes an act of gross misconduct grounds for immediate termination of employment. IRC staff are obliged to create and maintain an environment that is free of sexual exploitation and abuse, if developing concerns or suspicions regarding abuse or exploitation they must report. Reporting mechanisms are made clear and introduced to the community via email, WhatsApp, hotlines and MEAL staff.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Security conditions and COVID-19 disrupt activities and limit client access to sites - Mitigation: Ongoing security planning and flexible project design allow for adaptive management in line with internal contingency plans related to each operational area, including remote service delivery building on IRC’s experience in 2020-2021 and MEDEVAC is in place in case of emergency as we will as a contingency plan.2. Intimidation and/or Local authorities' interference in project design and delays in implementation - Mitigation: IRC will engage timely with the local authorities to obtain the No Objection Certificate (NOC) to be able to start the activities. IRC will sign MOUs with the relevant Local Councils which clearly state Local Authorities' roles in supporting the Project implementation without interference. A low visibility strategy will be adopted. Ad hoc briefing to the staff on the low visibility. IRC field staff will avoid mentioning IRC names in media and report to security focal points any potential risks they might encounter due to affiliation with IRC.3. Movement Safety - Mitigation: conduct necessary assessments prior to movement to inquire about weather conditions and potential risks or targeting. Vehicle equipped to withstand weather conditions such as heat, cold, heavy rain, and flooding. Staff should be briefed on personal measures they should take to avoid being severely affected by this threat (e.g., hydration).
