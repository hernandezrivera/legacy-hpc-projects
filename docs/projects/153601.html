Project Summary

The current humanitarian and protection crisis in South Sudan requires an integrated, multidisciplinary strategic response and support to the widened and deepened humanitarian and protection needs. The main objective of this Oxfam GB project in South Sudan is to improve the protective environment and protection of civilians in South Sudan. To contribute towards that, Oxfam GB will apply a static and mobile modality to respond with protection programming. Static programming will link humanitarian and development/resilience programming through strengthening community based protection mechanisms whilst sustainable linking local actors and national actors on issue affecting women, men, girls and boys.
Mobile response will contribute towards serving the hard to reach areas through its protection mainstreamed and integrated rapid response missions. This broad objective contributes to the HCT, Protection Cluster, HRP and HRP Protection Strategic Objectives as well as Oxfam Country Protection Strategic intervention for 2019 and for emergency preparedness and response protection strategy. Specifically, the following activities will be undertaken to achieve the main objective of this project: Protection assessments and protection advocacy; information dissemination, awareness raising, and campaigns on protection messaging, rights, entitlements and protection from sexual exploitation; individual protection assistance in-cash and in-kind; service mapping and referrals; strengthening community based protection; supporting safe programming (protection mainstreaming) and supporting local peace and security for women, youths and communities.
Oxfam executes its protection programming through protection mainstreaming, protection integration and standalone protection initiatives. Protection integration into Food Security and Livelihood, WASH and Education programs will be framed on an overarching protection analysis and on sector specific risk analysis thereby ensuring that protection risks and potential violations are taken into consideration and addressed effectively. Specific protection objectives will be embedded to execute protection integrated multi-sectoral (EFSVL, WASH, Education) projects/programs- that will outcome to promote safe and meaningful access to services, build community resilience and curb the risks of exclusions and violence.
The project targets approximately 150 000 ( 61 620 women, 56 880 men, 18 270 girls and 15 120 boys,). Targetted geographical areas are tier one, two, and three through mobile (RRMs, EP&R, mobile response) and static programming. Specifically, the areas include: Pibor, Nyirol, Uror, Akobo, Ayod, Mvolo, Mundri, Yirol, Juba, Maridi, Kapoeta, Wau, Yambio, Kajo Keji, Rumbek, Cuiebet, Torit, Malakal. That is basically in Jonglei State, Central Equaotoria, Western Equatoria, Eastern Equotoria, Western Barh el Garzal and Upper Nile State. In order to support quality programming in other sectors, this project will support ‘safe programming’ (protection mainstreaming) through the risk reduction approach, conflict sensitivity approaches, as well promoting inclusive local peace and security for women, youths, and communities. At the same time, the project will strengthen gender mainstreaming and gender justice through transformative gender approaches in community based protection structures, and promoting women participaction and in leadership. A strong influencing strategy of this project will be guided by centrality of protection, protection of civilians, accountability to affected populations, promotion of gender justice, community accountability and Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Collaborative interaction with the protection cluster will be done towards supporting and strengthening deep field cluster coordination in order to enhance coordination, complimentary, local humanitarian leadership, and maximising resource utilisation through shared responsibilities.

Needs

Since the July 2016, violent conflict between the government and opposition increased across the country - this has widened and deepened the humanitarian and protection crisis in South Sudan. The violence increased the food insecurity situation, economic situation, displaced almost 5 million people within and to neighbouring countries. Approximately, 2 million children are out of school. Majority of the displaced people in PoCs, are already planning and other have already returned to their homes other are being denied safe and free returning. Although the prevalence of relative peace due to the October Peace Celebrations, the impact of violence is severe, returning to normalcy when access to services, rights and goods coupled with limited freedom of movement remains a challenge. Long term political instability, localised inter/intra-communal violence, persistent deprivations and coercion due to cultural practices, limited or challenged governance system and practices increase exposure of protection risks to girls, women, young men, and men including children. Other challenging protection concerns are: For instance in Pibor, Food Consumption and dietary diversities are very poor and people are adopting emergency coping mechanisms according (Food Security Nutrition Monitoring System) FSNMS Round 22. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate is 20.7%. Considering the huge needs to prevent the elevated risks of catastrophe (IPC phase 5); immediate and short terms interventions are being considered under Food Security and Livelihoods. Oxfam recent research points to increased use of negative coping negative mechanisms like 'early and forced marriages', use of violence to access services; In Northern Jonglei: Without humanitarian assistance in Uror and Nyirol counties there is a high risk that the population may slip into catastrophe from October 2018 to March 2019. Therefore Oxfam has been implementing a 60 days General Food Distribution (GFD) in Uror and Nyirol now; on the other hand a 90 days GFD is ongoing in Akobo West and East which is characterized with poor road network, revenge killings, cattle raids predominantly conducted by armed youths from neighbouring hostile communities. To address these needs and protection challenges, there is need to improve the protective environment for the affected civilians, strengthen the community protective capacity, improve access to basic services and rights; reduce or mitigate existing and potential risks; to that end, any action by any section requires that it is safe and dignified. Support for safe programming and conflict sensitivity is required. And it is this background this proposed action attempts to address the humanitarian and protection needs through a multidisciplinary approach of protection, gender and peacebuilding whilst supporting safe programming.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This project will provide beneficiaries with information on services available, rights, entitlements, protection from SEA and on key protection messaging based on issues as identified relevant to their context. Context specific rural participatory approaches informed by the Communicating with Communities strategies recommended by the CwC working Group in South Sudan will be taken into consideration. Context and vulnerability factors, such as access, appropriateness, reliability, affordability, age, literacy levels, among others will be used. Given that South Sudan is an 'oral culture', existing established community engagement strategies will be utilised for two way communication, feedback and complaints mechanisms, learning from the affected population. The project staff will provide beneficiaries with information on services available, how to access them, where and on what criterion to access them so as to improve access to basic protection services including SGBV.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Oxfam’s approach to community engagement around Protection aims to support women and men to identify risks in their communities and to find practical, pragmatic ways to mitigate them. Community members are therefore trained on basic protection concepts and principles, and produce a community protection plan with mitigation measures which include practical actions, information dissemination, referrals and advocacy. This helps to ensure that messages used in information dissemination are relevant and tailored to each context. Messages and dissemination modalities are then subjected to a risk analysis and tested in target communities to ensure that they are appropriate and accessible before IEC materials are created and prior to further information dissemination.These IEC materials are gender, age, and disability inclusive. This project intends to employ international and national staff with protection expertise for thematic, context and local areas in order to be conflict sensitive, Do No Harm and use local languages. In other words, It is important to choose an appropriate means of information dissemination to ensure two-way communication with community and to provide information through the channel that is widely accepted, accessible and trusted by the target community. In addition, different people in the communities can be engaged differently. To improve women, men, boys and girls access to information, different engagement ways will be used. Information about gender, age, disability, literacy, Language, culture, displacement patterns, etc. of the community will be taken into account to determine the contents of messages and ways of engagement and means of information dissemination.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Misconduct include sexual exploitation and abuse, harassment, fraud, aid diversion, bribery, or other malpractice in the workplace. Oxfam has a zero-tolerance policy towards SEA, and harassment. Oxfam has several global policies and tools that are implemented in all countries, eg: Code of Conduct, Reporting Misconduct SOP; Policy on Whistleblowing and Complaints; Child Protection Policy; Anti-bullying and harassment; CAMSA; Humanitarian Handbook etc. In November 2017, a dedicated Global Safeguarding Taskforce was set up to implement measures to prevent and investigate sexual misconduct across Oxfam. In South Sudan, Oxfam has a safeguarding focal point in each base; information on Oxfam’s SEA policies will be disseminated in every project meeting, staff and women and men in target communities will be trained; partners and contractors sign a Code of Conduct; and Oxfam is linked in to the UN PSEA mechanism. Trained and committed staff are available who can receive disclosures, provide support, and take forward investigations and other responses. Oxfam is committed to complying with the misconduct reporting requirements of contracts it signs with donors. On 10th February 2018 Oxfam released a ten-point action plan, and committed to make significant and necessary changes to our policy, practice, and culture to help stamp SEA. South Sudan has a safeguarding focal point in each base; information on Oxfam's SEA policies will be disseminated in every project meeting, staff and women in target communities will be trained; partners and contractors sign a Code of Conduct; and Oxfam is linked in to the UN PSEA mechanism and Country Taskforce strategic plan.

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes! The project has a built in feedback and complaint mechanism. The mechanisms include 1) Help Desk during Rapid Response Missions (RRMs), 2) Information Centre for instance in Akobo East (where beneficiaries can submit and receive feedback); 3) Toll free line, 4) whistleblowing and among others. Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project intervention is based protection risk analysis. Protection assessments and service mapping will provide specific protection gaps, risks, actors and actions vital for reducing the protection risks. The project will contribute towards organisation and protection cluster protection risk analysis through protection research, assessments and regularly updating the service mappings for improved, accessible and usable referral mechanisms. Addressing protection issues that intersect with their sector-specific responsibilities through protection mainstreaming: Design and implementation of programs to mitigate protection risks through the risk reduction approaches. Another way is through protection Integration. That is to incorporate protection objectives intoprogramming of other sector-specific responses like Education, FSL, WASH, Governance and even Gender within Oxfam. Moreso, is through having specific specialised protection activities; engaging collectively with other partners and stakeholders to achieve meaningful protection outcomes; mobilizing actors including those outside the humanitarian and development community to contribute to collective protection outcomes, as appropriate and when needed. Finally, is continuous monitoring and evaluation of our progress towards towards centralising protection in our action. Women and men in communities are trained on protection and supported to identify risks which affect them. Using this information, they create a Community Protection Plan, detailing practical measures for mitigating risks which have been identified. On another level, all substantive activities detailed in the project, and the activities set out in community protection plans, will be subject to risk analysis which identifies potential risks which the activity could cause to beneficiaries, to staff, and to Oxfam and its partners. Specific measures will be put in place to ensure that risks identified though this process are mitigated or eliminated.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

limited humanitarian access to affected populations, bureaucratic access challenges, and limited access to services has been the major protection risks facing youths, girls, women, people with disabilities and people or groups with concerns in South Sudan. The project will address this in four ways: 1) Through reporting and documenting barriers to access to the Access Group for documentation, advocacy and evidence based protection advocacy with different appropriate stakeholders including the the Protection Cluster, HCT, UNMISS etc2) Applying the risk reduction approach by ensuring that safe programming approach is taken in other sectors like Gender, Governance, FSL, WASH and Education; including RRMs.3) Providing protection individual protection assistance to those people at risk, people with specific needs, - NFI for protection, and or cash for protection. 4) Inclusive engagement, participation and empowerment of groups/populations facing barriers to access.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Oxfam uses three different approaches in our protection work in the field:Specific protection activities and programmes. In any society some people are more vulnerable than others. This may be because of their sex, age, disability, ethnicity, sexual orientation, religion or political affiliation, or a combination of factors. Oxfam makes conscious efforts to consult communities, to understand who is at risk and why, and taking into account the possible consequences of everything we do (or don't do) on people's exposure to threats. Oxfam has been building up a body of activities and projects specifically to improve the safety of civilians and has run large-scale protection programmes across continents. This include, for instance, setting up local Protection Committees that provide a forum to negotiate with military or other authorities, helping communities develop emergency protection plans for when attacks take place.This project intends to address inequalities and protection threats through the following ways: 1) through proactive engagement with the affected populations in assessments, project implementation and monitoring, 2) Improving the protective environment of the affected populations by sharing evidenced based protection needs and analysis with the protection cluster and relevant stakeholders;3) Supporting local peace and security initiatives, platforms and dialogues for women, youths and communities towards promoting peaceful coexistence and peacebuilding 4) through continuous protection analysis, situational analysis and contextual analysis, conflict sensitivity approaches will be employed to maximise positive impact of the interaction between the conflict context and the action; minimise negative impact between the interaction of conflict context and our action. on addressing protection threats, Oxfam’s approach is based around supporting women and men to identify protection threats and concrete actions which they can put in place to mitigate them.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safe programming (protection mainstreaming) through the risk reduction approach will be measured by the indicator of % of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age and diversity) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner”. This can be done either in PDMs or end of the project endline survey, market surveys among other engagements with beneficiaries.
