Project Summary

The proposed project aims to support vulnerable and crisis-affected Farmers and Breeders in the Gaza Strip to sustain their livelihood assets, recover their production capacity, meet their food security needs, and reduce the use of negative coping strategies. The project is designed to align with and achieve the outcomes of the following Strategic and Cluster specific objectives: (Strategic Objective 03): The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. More specifically, it will contribute to the Food Security Sector Objective 01: The resilience of vulnerable households is protected, and their livelihoods, food security, and productive capacity restored/enhanced.
Through unconditional and restricted cash assistance, Mercy Corps and its local partner – Al Foukhary Association for Country Development (Al Foukhary) – will identify, select, and support 500 of the most vulnerable farmers and breeders from Khan Younis and Rafah governorates (2,800 individuals).
The project will focus on highly vulnerable and crises-affected male and female farmers and breeders in four groups, who will be selected from Khan Younis and Rafah through combined household vulnerability assessment and livelihood technical assessment.:
1) Agricultural Open-Land: 136 Farmers (70 from Khan Younis and 66 from Rafah): Each selected small farmer will receive an average of $ 900 to rehabilitate 2.0 dunums of open land (ILS 1,500 per open land dunum).
2) Greenhouse Rehabilitation: 90 Farmers (40 from Khan Younis and 50 from Rafah): Each selected small farmer will receive an average of $ 900 (ILS 3,000) to rehabilitate one Greenhouse.
3) Poultry Farms Rehabilitation: 149 Farmers (70 from Khan Younis and 79 from Rafah): each poultry farmer will receive an average of $ 1,200 to rehabilitate their farms. Out of these, 12 farmers will receive photovoltaic (PV) systems with an average cost of $ 2,300 per PV system.
4) Livestock Farm Rehabilitation: 125 Breeders (65 from Khan Younis and 60 from Rafah): each livestock farmer will receive an average of $ 1,200 to rehabilitate their farms. Out of these, 5 farmers will receive PV systems with an average cost of $ 2,300.
By utilizing cash as a modality for direct assistance through Electronic Debit Cards (eWallet), participants have greater power to determine how to meet their urgent needs, empower them, and maintain their dignity while reducing their vulnerability and contributing to restoring their food security. The project will help farmers and breeders to improve their physical and economic conditions and enhance their resiliency in an effective and efficient way. Additionally, local markets are functional in the Gaza Strip, and vendors continue to have adequate stock available; therefore, cash assistance is an appropriate humanitarian response modality to meet critical basic needs.
Through well-established evidence-based vulnerability and technical targeting, the project will use the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) and Technical Vulnerability Assessment Tool (TVAT) to identify, assess and verify selected vulnerable HHs to receive cash assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the community and primary stakeholders. Mercy Corps will be the focal point of coordination with the main actors, and Al Foukhary will coordinate closely with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) to cross-check the names of vulnerable Farmers and Breeders.
Additionally, Mercy Corps will promote community ownership, accountability, and participation throughout the project through baseline assessments, orientation sessions, Post Distribution Monitoring, and its Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM), while also mobilizing key stakeholders to more deeply engage with identified vulnerable farmers.

1 - Needs

Driven by the longstanding Israeli blockade, internal Palestinian political divides, and recurrent escalations of violence between Israel and Palestinian armed groups, the Gaza Strip is in a state of chronic humanitarian crisis. The humanitarian needs of its more than 2 million residents are further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the escalations of violence in May 2021 (and August 2022), negatively impacting livelihoods and access to essential services in Gaza (REACH MSNA, July 2022). Israeli forces have mainly restricted access to areas within 300 meters of the Gaza side of the perimeter fence with Israel; areas several hundred meters beyond are deemed unsafe, preventing, or discouraging, agricultural activities. Longstanding access restrictions imposed by the Israeli authorities have undermined Gaza's economy, resulting in high unemployment, food insecurity, and aid dependency. Unemployment levels in Gaza are among the world's highest: the Q1 jobless rate in 2022 was 46.6%, compared with an average of 34.8% in 2006. Youth unemployment for the same period (age 15-29) stands at 62.5%. 1.3 million out of 2.1 million Palestinians in Gaza (62%) require food assistance (OCHA Factsheet on Gaza Blockade, June 2022). According to the REACH MSNA conducted in July 2022, livelihood and employment opportunities are extremely limited under the longstanding Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip. About half (50.5%) of Gaza-assessed households reported NGO or charity assistance as one of their primary sources of income. Among aid-recipient households, this rate was even higher (68.3%). 57.3% of households reported that their typical monthly income had decreased in the year prior to data collection, 3.8% reported having lost their income permanently, and 1.2% reported having lost their income temporarily. Taking on debt, primarily to meet basic needs, was a widespread practice - 83.4% of households had any outstanding debt, and 79.0% of households had taken on recent debt in the three months prior to the data collection. These factors, combined with the fact that 60.3% of households reported a member of their household unemployed and unable to find work at the time of the data collection, further highlight the precarious financial situation of Gaza households. This should be considered alongside the frequently reported financial barriers to accessing essential services and the highly reported use of negative coping mechanisms, particularly those related to practices of taking on credit, debt, or borrowing. The recent escalation of hostilities has significantly impacted agriculture in the different areas of the Gaza strip, severely limiting access to water, land, and resources and amplifying the already high levels of food insecurity. Farmers, poultry, and livestock breeders in Gaza suffer from the prolonged blockade and political conflicts that undermine their livelihoods, the lack of governmental regulations for setting the price ceiling, and the lack of control over exports and imports of goods over the agricultural sector overall. In addition, the economic situation in Gaza continued to deteriorate. Consequently, farmers are not capable of exporting their products outside Gaza, and at the same time, the agricultural inputs are imported at high cost and low quality. The local market witnessed significant deflation and the purchasing power of consumers as well. Farming families suffering from poverty and food insecurity struggle to set up reliable irrigation systems to make their small land plots productive. Therefore, farmers sell out their crops and products at meager prices that do not cover the operational costs, which incurred significant amounts of debt. A few farmers abandoned agriculture and sold their lands or parts of them (Mercy Corps Needs Assessment Report for FSS in Gaza. March 2022). From Mercy Corps Needs Assessment Report, March 2022, assessed 114 farmers (47 Open land and Greenhouse Farmers, 28 Poultry Breeders, and 39 Livestock Breeders) who reported needing to be supported with unconditional and restricted cash assistance to enhance their livelihood assets and build their resilience to the prevailing shocks. Poultry breeders reported that the most important thing they need is to secure appropriate environmental conditions for the poultry to reduce deaths and ensure that chicks are growing up perfectly. In contrast, livestock breeders reported that their most crucial need is to access a better quality of sheep and fodders to enhance their production capacity. They also need to advance their skills in raising livestock by securing advanced equipment like electric Sheep Shears, manual drinkers, and linear feeders. The economic situation in Gaza creates high levels of vulnerability for many households who are without any sustainable means of livelihood as a means to meet their basic needs in the absence of humanitarian aid. Assessments have shown the widespread use of negative coping strategies by vulnerable households in other to meet their most basic needs. The coping strategies most frequently employed by households due to a lack of food or money were those related to taking on debt or purchasing on credit, borrowing, and reducing expenses on essential services and this may provide insight into the precarious financial situation of households in Gaza. Mercy Corps used the Gender Analysis Framework tool to help identify the gender-specific information needed to design and deliver smart, targeted project activities that meet the needs of both men and women. The tools help to analyze gender around five domains 1) Laws, regulations, and institutional practices; 2) Cultural norms and beliefs; 3) Gender roles, responsibilities, and time used; 4) Access to and control over assets and resources and 5) Patterns of power and decision making.

2 - Activities

Mercy Corps and its local partner - Al Foukhary - will identify, select, and support 500 farmer households (2,800 individuals) of the most vulnerable farmers and breeders from Khan Younis and Rafah governorates (245 from Khan Younis and 255 from Rafah). The identification and selection of Farmers and Breeders will be carried out through an open application process and based on combining two assessments and scoring systems: 1) The Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT) endorsed by the Cash Working Group. Based on the data collected through UVAT, Mercy Corps will apply the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) scoring system. The PMTF will be used as the standardized selection criteria for targeting all the project's participant HHs. It is a methodology that helps to identify and target the most in need of humanitarian assistance programs. It uses multivariate regression to correlate specific proxies, including HHs' characteristics, employability, and assets. In addition to the PMTF score, the project will also consider other vulnerabilities, including Food Consumption and Coping Strategy Index. 2) The Technical Vulnerability Assessment Tool (TVAT) developed by Mercy Corps will be adopted based on the project's established selection criteria. Technical selection criteria for farmers will include: i) dependency on farming or breeding as their sole source of income; ii) ownership of small land – 1 to 5 dunums – that is arable and currently used for agriculture; iii) small poultry and livestock farmers with a farm shed of about 300 square meters iv) have incurred losses to their livelihood because of the economic decline and decreased purchasing power; and v) have suffered loses in their agriculture production or on the verge to losing their agricultural production cycles due to increases in operating costs and agricultural inputs costs. Under direct and close technical and administrative guidance and capacity building from Mercy Corps, Al Foukhary will conduct vulnerability assessments through hired and trained agricultural engineers, enumerators, and assistant enumerators using mobile data collection tools. The data will be integrated into Mercy Corps' database (CommCare), which will autonomously score the applications based on the pre-programmed selection criteria. The most eligible farmers who are Poor (below the national Poverty Line) and collect the highest technical scores will be prioritized to benefit from cash assistance. All final selected lists of participants will be cross-checked with the agencies providing similar assistance and line ministries, including the MoA. Mercy Corps and partner financial service provider PalPay will create an electronic wallet account for each selected participant farmer and provide them with an Electronic Debit Cash Card (eWallet). Farmers will own and have complete control over their eWallet accounts even after the project is completed. They will use eWallets to access the cash from ATMs or purchase directly from vendors of their selection. Before cash distributions, Mercy Corps and Al Foukhary will provide printed orientation material packages to all selected participants. The orientation materials will provide all project information and communication with Mercy Corps and will include information about the project and activities; clear instructions on using eWallet for the first time; ATM locations in Khan Younis and Rafah governorate; project Monitoring and Evaluation; and Mercy Corps' Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM) channels of communication. Additionally, in the locations where program activities will be conducted, posters will be displayed describing the CARM and providing a Mercy Corps' Call Center Free Number and other channels for participants (or any other interested party) to contact program staff with concerns, complaints, or questions. Al Foukhary must also adhere to Mercy Corps' CARM, PESA, and Safeguarding policies when implementing program activities. The cash transfer top-up amounts will be distributed to each participant in multiple installments. Each of the selected 500 farmers and breeders will receive an average amount of $ 900 to $ 2,500 in two to three installments according to their livelihood groups as follow:1) Agricultural Open-Land: 136 Farmers (70 from Khan Younis and 66 from Rafah): Each selected small farmer will receive an average of $ 900 to rehabilitate 2.0 dunums of open land (ILS 1,500 per open land dunum).2) Greenhouse Rehabilitation: 90 Farmers (40 from Khan Younis and 50 from Rafah): Each selected small farmer will receive an average of $ 900 (ILS 3,000) to rehabilitate one Greenhouse.3) Poultry Farms Rehabilitation: 149 Farmers (70 from Khan Younis and 79 from Rafah): each poultry farmer will receive an average of $ 1,200 to rehabilitate their farms. Out of these, 12 farmers will receive photovoltaic (PV) systems with an average cost of $ 2,300 per PV system. 4) Livestock Farm Rehabilitation: 125 Breeders (65 from Khan Younis and 60 from Rafah): each livestock farmer will receive an average of $ 1,200 to rehabilitate their farms. Out of these, 5 farmers will receive PV systems with an average cost of $ 2,300.The PV systems will ensure that poultry farms and livestock farms have reliable power sources needed for vital services including lighting source, ventilation, and water supply. Also, the PV system will relieve some of the running costs for farmers. Finally, Mercy Corps' comprehensive monitoring combined with a well-established CARM system and implemented PSEA and safeguarding policies allows program staff, partner team, and community to have real-time access to information on the ground and ensures the highest standards of protection and accountability to project participants and community at large. This also allows program teams and managers to quickly adapt programming as necessary and maintain our strict and highest quality standards.

3 - Indicators

Throughout the project's life, Mercy Corps will ensure multi-layered monitoring, evaluation, and learning (MEL) at all levels. Mercy Corps' MEL unit will be involved in all stages of the project's design, planning, implementation, and closure. It will ensure that progress on achieving the outputs and outcomes is tracked by conducting regular site visits, spot checks, Data Quality Assurance (DQA), Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), and frequent participants' feedback surveys. Learning and PDM processes will be standardized across all groups and designed to capture the project's impact on the participants, their livelihoods, and households, including goods and services purchased, the amount spent and used, how decisions were made, and intrahousehold dynamics, accountability, and their satisfaction and feedback. Local partners' (Al Foukhary) enumerators and agricultural engineers will conduct PDM visits under the supervision of Mercy Corps, and data will be collected using mobile data collection tools (tablets) and will be synced and protected on Mercy Corps central participants database (CommCare). All Data collected from and about project participants will be disaggregated by age, gender, and disability. Mercy Corps MEL team will then analyze PDM data and prepare an analysis report with a focus on measuring progress in Food Security Indicators and provide improvement recommendations on activities implementation if needed. Under technical guidance from Mercy Corps' Project Coordinator, Al Foukhary’s Agricultural Engineers will conduct technical follow-up visits to the farmers' lands to assets to verify their utilization of the cash assistance and will provide any specialized technical assistance and awareness needed by farmers, poultry breeders, and livestock farmers. CARM system will be leveraged to allow community members and participants to voice their feedback, concerns, and complaints to Mercy Corps. To ensure the highest levels of accountability, internal controls, and segregation of duties, Mercy Corps' CARM, Safeguarding, MEL, and Program are four separate functions and separate teams. Mercy Corps project team will work closely with partners' project teams to provide them with all required technical and administrative support to ensure all project activities are jointly planned and implemented consistently and per quality standards. The Mercy Corps project team will oversee the implementation of the overall project activities under Mercy Corps' management direction. In contrast, the partner NGO teams - Al Foukhary - will be responsible for implementing Farmer and Breeder related activities. Regular reports will be prepared and shared with project management. 5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The Cash Assistance modality allows vulnerable HHs to have flexible, reliable, and timely financial support to address their urgent needs, restore their livelihoods, build resilience, and restore agriculture production capacity. By utilizing cash as a modality for direct assistance, beneficiaries have greater power to determine how to meet their urgent needs, empower them, and maintain their dignity while reducing their vulnerability and contributing to restoring their food security. Additionally, local markets are functional in the Gaza Strip, and vendors continue to have adequate stock available; therefore, cash assistance is an appropriate humanitarian response modality to meet critical basic needs. Moreover, Cash installments will be distributed through Electronic Debit Cards (eWallets) provided by Mercy Corps' financial service provider PalPay – a subsidiary company of Bank of Palestine which has extensive geographic coverage and is highly accessible through ATMs and Branches across the Gaza Strip. Additionally, the eWallets accounts will be created by Mercy Corps and PalPay for each participant, and these accounts will be owned and managed by the participants themselves during and after the project completion. Using eWallet as a delivery mechanism will foster the vulnerable participant's financial inclusion, giving them greater access to valuable and affordable financial products and services that meet their needs.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Mercy Corps Palestine has a robust Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM) system backed by a global system. The CARM system is managed by a dedicated CARM team, which is managerially separated from all other program and operations functions to boost accountability to the highest level possible. The system includes a Mercy Corps-hosted and dedicated call center with a free hotline through which a trained operator can address simple project-related inquiries from participants and other community members. All CARM complaints or feedback are registered in six categories on an online tracking database. The six categories are i) Request for information, ii) Requests for assistance, iii) Minor dissatisfaction with program activities, iv) Major dissatisfaction with project activities or the behavior of Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners, v) Fraud or corruption by Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners vi) Sexual exploitation and abuse, child abuse, sexual misconduct, human trafficking by Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners. The first four categories (i, ii, iii, and iv) are sent to respective Mercy Corps members as per CARM policy, who prepare a response and communicate feedback back to callers promptly. The program team immediately adopts corrective actions and reports to community members who submit information requests or complaints. Data from the CARM tracking database are collected and analyzed regularly, and an internal report is produced in line with Mercy Corps Global CARM Minimum Standards policy. Other categorized sensitive complaints (v, and vi), which include fraud, corruption, child abuse, or sexual misconduct, are processed through a well-established dedicated safeguarding committee trained by Mercy Corps under direct supervision from both the Country Director and Mercy Corps’ Headquarters Integrity Team.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

To appropriately address identified needs, Mercy Corps proposes a high-impact project that expands on the success of the current Mercy Corps Cash and Livelihood Programming to urgently meet the basic needs of highly vulnerable farmers and breeders in Khan Younis and Rafah to help them reduce their use of negative coping strategies, avoid incurring further debt and restore their production capacity, resilience, and food security. In designing this project, Mercy Corps built on the success and accumulated learning of the current cash and livelihood programming and the extended capacity in addition to its standard procedure of gender and protection. As a Mercy Corps project design standard, thorough risk, protection, and gender analyses are conducted to inform the design of activities, project management, mitigation plans, and safety and security measures for both participants and project staff. Mercy Corps provides refresher training regularly to all Mercy Corps team members and local partners on Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanisms (CARM), Safeguarding policies, including the protection of program participants, and code of conduct. Likewise, all program participants and community members will be oriented on approaching Mercy Corps and providing feedback or complaints using CARM. Each project participant receives an orientation package with information about the project, CARM, and safety guidelines on cash handling. Mercy Corps collect and share data about participants using the Data Minimization Approach, Participant Privacy, and Data Security Protocols: i.e., Mercy Corps gathers only the data that is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary to determine the eligibility of humanitarian assistance and required to measure the impact and to cross-check to avoid duplicate assistance. Participant Data are collected, synced, and stored on Mercy Corps' highly secured CommCare database, which is only accessible to authorized MEL staff. Mercy Corps will distribute cash assistance in this project through Electronic Debit Cards (eWallet) from PalPay, which has extensive geographic services coverage and is highly accessible by participants through Bank of Palestine ATMs across the Gaza Strip. This will ensure that cash assistance will be accessible to participants even outside regular banks' work hours.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through well-established evidence-based vulnerability and technical targeting, Mercy Corps and its local partner will use the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF), and Technical Criteria to identify, assess and verify selected vulnerable HHs to receive cash assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the line ministries (Ministry of Agriculture), the community and all relevant stakeholders. The identification of eligible participant farmers and breeders will be carried out through an open and transparent application process using established vulnerability criteria and mobile data collection tools. The potential participant's data will be synced on the Mercy Corps database (CommCare). Home-to-home vulnerability assessments will be conducted on all participants to confirm their eligibility according to established selection criteria for each participant group. For farmer or breeder participants, the selection will be based on two assessments: The vulnerability assessment of farmer HHs using the PMTF, combined with a technical vulnerability assessment tool developed based on established selection criteria. Final lists of selected HHs will be cross-checked with the FSS member's database, INGOs providing similar assistance, and local authorities to avoid duplication. People with specific needs (older persons, pregnant and lactating women, people with chronic health conditions, and disabled) and their caregivers will be consulted during the life of the program to understand their safety and dignity concerns. Mercy Corps and local partner' teams will identify the people with specific needs during the household assessment surveys. Mercy Corps will provide printed orientation material packages to all selected participants, share information, educate the participants on the project, and clarify the best possible ways to benefit from the assistance. Participants with specific needs or have difficulties due to illiteracy or cultural reasons (e.g. needing a male relative to accompany a female household member) will be assisted in identifying and selecting trustee "Helpers," such as first-degree family members, to assist them in receiving their cash/cashing out from ATMs. During the implementation and monitoring of project activities, the Mercy Corps program team and local partner teams can go to the home of people with specific needs to collect feedback and ensure they receive assistance.
