Project Summary

The project will increase access and delivery of quality, life-saving sexual gender based violence services to girls, boys, women and men in the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY States) in North East Nigeria. The project will target 25,000 persons (8,100 girls, 6,900 boys, 5,400 women and 4,600 men). Those in need of advanced mental health support will be referred to appropriate specialized care services for treatment and management. Community volunteers will ensure the organization of weekly PSS activities for all beneficiaries’ and will hold bi-weekly life skills sessions for young women, men, adolescent girls and boys, including people living with disabilities (PLWDs), on protection issues, gender norms, and gender- and age-appropriate, inclusive protection community services. Community leaders including religious and traditional leaders will be targeted with gender specific training aimed at increasing their level of awareness around SGBV related topics and protection risks as well as encouraging them to lead and play supportive role in strengthening SGBV prevention and response strategies in their communities. The Ward Development Committees (WDC), including Community-Based Child Protection (CBCP) sub-committees will conduct quarterly safety audits to identify SGBV and protection risks using existing tools developed by PIN. The safety audit will be used to develop community action plans to prevent and mitigate incidents of SGBV that will be shared and implemented with relevant partners and community-based structures to inform the design of services and increase safety and security for women and girls. Increased awareness of SRH/SGBV services will be supported by improved service provision by health care workers and mobile case management workers. PIN will conduct a rapid needs assessment in existing health facilities and mapping of existing SRH and SGBV service points in the new project districts to understand the care provision needs of these facilities as well as update the critical referral pathways with other services (such as legal, protection etc.). Vulnerable adolescent girls will benefit from dignity kits for their SRH wellbeing. Case management emergency protection teams will ensure gender and age-appropriate SGBV case management is delivered to survivors of SGBV including PLWDs. Case management workers and supervisors will receive a series of specialized refresher trainings to strengthen their capacity. This will include training on enhanced mobile outreach and case management techniques, MHPSS, confidentiality, child and adolescent friendly centered services, gender equality, child safeguarding and PSEA. The case workers will also focus on providing timely and appropriate referral for treatment and management of SGBV for victims/survivors. Case management workers will also be trained on Plan International’s Program Approach to work with child survivors of SGBV in humanitarian setting and will complement its SGBV response with the Caring for Child Survivors (CCS) guidelines.

1 - Needs

According to HRP 2022 approximately 1,287,221 persons have being targeted for gender based violence prevention and response interventions in North East Nigeria. Due to insecurity and breakdown of of local protection mechanisms, women and girls are under threat of violence, abduction and rape. Gender Based Violence (GBV) including sexual violence, denial of resources, forced and child marriages continue to be reported. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are at a higher risk due to poor shelters/living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. According to GBV IMS mid year report (2021), between January and June 2021, 99% of the reported incidents of GBV were perpetrated against women and girls across Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. Majority of the cases (83%) affected adult survivors while 17% of the incidents were perpetrated against children – below 18years. In Borno and Adamawa state 100% of the reported incidents of GBV were perpetrated against women and girls. In Yobe State however, 81% of incidents of GBV was perpetrated against women and girls and 11% against of incident perpetrated men and boys. Due to conflict, social and gender roles and responsibilities have changes. Women and girls ahev now taken the role of being the main provider within their households. In Borno, women and girls are at risk of abduction and GBV particularly when going outside the perimeter fences that surround IDP camps to collect firewood but also within IDP camps. Meanwhile men and adolescent boys are at risk of being killed or recruited. Due to limited number of GBV actors and lack of information on available services, IDPs have limited access to services (especially health services) resulting in protection risks (domestic violence, school dropout, psychosocial distress) and adoption of negative coping mechanisms (survival sex and forced marriage). Moreover, women and girls have limited access to services compared to men and boys due to lack of opportunities and cultural barriers that reinforce gender inequalities. Since May 2020, the Borno State Government started facilitating return of IDPs to their local government areas (LGAs) of origin, from Maiduguri. The aim is to relocate all IDPs from Maiduguri by December 2021. Most of those returning or relocating are women-headed households with children with no source of livelihoods and no shelter or land in the areas of return. Consequently, there is a high chance that the women and adolescent girls will engage in negative coping strategies to be able to meet their basic needs. According to anecdotal reports, due to lack of food, women and girls are exchanging sex for food and basic necessities. They will also be at risk of GBV due to lack of shelter. Adolescent girls (10-17 years), particularly those in camps face the highest risk of sexual violence and are most at risk of denial of realization of their rights, especially the right to education. Adolescent boys (13-17 years) face conflict related risk of being abducted, killed or recruited by armed groups. Adolescent girls are also engaging in negative coping mechanisms due to lack of food and other basic supplies such as sanitary pads.

2 - Activities

1. Provision of individual GBV case management and other specialized services to 200 persons (100 women and 100 girls). Plan will identity, recruit and train case workers on GBV case management process, GBVIMS and survivor centred approach. The trained case workers will be responsible for providing individual counselling to survivors whereas GBV Nurse will facilitate the survivor to access medical care. Plan will support at least 8 Primary Health Clinics to provide survivor centred services. Based on needs assessment, Plan will provide cash assistance to survivors of GBV in a confidential and safe manner. Additionally, Plan will refer the survivor to safety and legal assistance upon the consent of the survivor. Plan will also support the sub-sector to update the GBV referral pathways in the target locations, print and disseminate them.2. Provision dignity kits to adolescent girls of reproductive age and young women. Plan will procure and distribute 1000 dignity to in school and out of school adolescent girls, as well as to those accessing the WGSS.3. Strengthen and equip 20 women and girls safe spaces (including WASH facilities) to provide opportunity for safe participation of women and girls in program activities. Including provision of peer to peer activities for adolescent girls, mentorship for adolescent girls and age disaggregated group counselling for women and girls. Approximately 800 individuals will be targeted (4500 girls, 3500 women)4. Training/capacity building of Plan GBV case workers, GBV nurses, Government community health workers (CHWs) and Social workers on GBV case management, GBVIMS, caring for child survivors, mental health psychosocial support and referral pathways. Plan will also conduct protection form sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding training to the project staff. A total of 632 persons will be trained (500 women and 132 men)5. Provision of start-up kits for income generation activities to 300 persons (150 girls, 150 women). Plan will provide business training to adolescent girls and young women at risk of GBV, attach them to master trainers for atleast3 months and then provide start up kits for business of their choice such as tailoring, baking, cap knitting and soap making.6. Conduct GBV community sensitization, outreach and dialogues targeting 5,400 women, 8,100 girls, 4,600 men and 6,900 boys.7. Conduct 30 safety audit to identify percentage of women, girls, boys and men who reported feeling of increased safety and wellbeing in their communities such as in hospitals, schools. These will be facilitated by case workers using the Interagency GBV risk mitigation tool.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Plan International shall consider differentiated needs women, men, girls and boys may have and give attention to persons with special needs, such as elderly, disabled or chronically ill people. Disaggregated data is key. Plan International shall work with GBV cluster to put in place a standardized dignity kit in close collaboration with the Protection Cluster during pre/post distribution exercise. Protection considerations, notably the principles of do no harm and non-discrimination will be adhered to during assessments, programme design, while implementing as well as when monitoring and evaluating the project. Thus Plan International will prioritize (1) Safety & security, including land tenure security, and dignity; (2) Meaningful access, including for women, children, persons with disabilities or other persons with special needs; (3) Accountability, for example through community-based feedback mechanism; and (4) Information, consultation, participation and community empowerment.
