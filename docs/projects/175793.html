Project Summary

The ongoing instabilities in most parts of South Sudan, combined with effects of floods, covid19 and lack of food security and livelihood opportunities result in the displacement of communities and the economic downturn and have created or reinforced existing protection needs. Five million people are in need of protection services across the country.
PAH is a very experienced organization with broad portfolio of successfully implemented projects in various sectors. PAH will use its expertise in Protection, team of specialists and well-established cooperation with various stakeholders to further provide assistance to vulnerable communities across the country. PAH approach of assistance is integrated, multi-sector responses meeting priority needs of the beneficiaries.
PAH will provide psycho-social support to women, children, elderly and people with disabilities. In created women and girls-friendly spaces PAH will conduct capacity building sessions in topics adjusted to needs of the groups, including protection rights, health, hygiene etc. PAH may also provide livelihood trainings for women. In child-friendly spaces PAH will encourage learning and play as part of psycho-social support. It will also cooperate with Nutrition and FSL partners or work by itself to provide feeding programs in child-friendly spaces and schools, to combine Protection and nutrition synergies and encourage education.
PAH will collaborate closely with stakeholders to ensure proper information and provide assistance in terms of law, house and land property rights, family reunification and other protection issues. For GBV survivors PAH will ensure access to information, effective referral and transport services to access health care and psycho-social services.
PAH will create awareness on protection related issues (GBV, child protection, early warnings and early response, peaceful co-existence, dignified access to WASH, S'NFI, FSL, Education services, the inclusion of people with disabilities etc.) through interactive sessions and use of information/education/communication (IEC) materials
Recognizing the cross-cutting nature of Protection, PAH combine the described activities with other sectors inside own multisector responses or in close cooperation with other partners in the areas of intervention. Mainstream protection across Shelter NFIs, FSL, Education, and WASH and integrating approach to cash programming across all clusters. PAH will ensure meaningful access to services for men and women, including people with disabilities, the elderly and children. The PAH team will encourage community participation in the project design. PAH will establish safe and accessible feedback mechanism. During the interventions, mobile units will focus on empowering communities and promote resilient approaches. Safety and dignity of the beneficiaries will be at the centre of interventions.
PAH conducts safety audits and other protection assessments to ensure proper responses, both by PAH and other partners, as reports are shared with the cluster and other stakeholders. PAH also will coordinate the protection activities in the locations where necessary, as in 2020 in Mangalla IDP site.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planing the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.

1 - Needs

More than 7.5 million people, including 4.1 million children, need multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. Five mln people require protection services. In 2020 UNMISS documented more than 1,200 human rights incidents, including arbitrary killings, injuries, abductions, conflict-related sexual violence, arbitrary arrests and detention, torture and ill-treatment, forced military recruitment the looting and destruction of civilian property. The ongoing instabilities in some parts of the country (like Upper Nile State, Tonj, Pibor) still cause community displacement and the economic downturn. The second factor of dire humanitarian needs is floods and droughts across the country, combined with food insecurity. An estimated 1,042,000 people were affected by floods between July and December 2020. An estimated 480,000 were displaced. Jonglei and Greater Pibor Administrative Area have been the worst affected. Displaced families are squatting in informal areas, unfinished buildings and marginalized neighbourhoods. These stressors present a burden for displaced individuals and families and may negatively affect their mental health and psychosocial wellbeing. There is a need for safe and secure displacement sites where all site residents and service providers live, work, and learn in a dignified environment away from harm and violence.The flood-related protection response shall address the 53,658 people in need of general protection response, 150,400 children in need of critical child protection response. The GBV sub-cluster are targeting 170,066 people.The Covid-19 pandemic negatively influences the market, accessibility of goods, people movement and humanitarian access. Older persons, persons with disabilities, chronic illness, persons suffering from mental health issues and IDPs face challenges related to safety and assistance in the event of restrictions on movement. Collective sites, IDP settlements and other densely populated areas with people in humanitarian need will have a potentially increased risk of forced eviction with the stated basis being decongestion or COVID-19 transmission prevention. The Protection Cluster and partners have already noted an increase in incidents of actual or attempted evictions. The pandemic poses risks to children, families and communities including mental health and psychosocial distress, family separations, violence against children, physical and emotional maltreatment, and risk of exploitation including child labour. The Ministry of Education estimates that over 2.2 million previously enrolled in formal and non-formal schools have seen their education disrupted due to schools' closure nationwide to prevent transmission of the disease. Such measures can negatively impact children and their families. Sexual exploitation and abuse, especially among girls forced to be out of school, are anticipated to increase. Gender-based violence is one of the most critical threats. Some studies indicate that some 65 per cent of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime, and some 51 per cent have suffered intimate partner violence (IPV). The majority of girls and women experience sexual violence for the first time under the age of 18. Flooding, armed conflicts, sub-national violence and COVID-19 are escalating the risk of GBV including Conflict-Related Sexual Violence (CRSV), sexual exploitation and abuse. In 2021, some 2 million people are estimated to be affected by GBV.According to PAH assessments in Jonglei, Bor South county, women and girls with disabilities are sexually abused due to their vulnerabilities leading to pregnancy. They later receive no support from the social support networks. Women and girls reported they are exposed to sexual abuse while collecting firewood and going to the bush to defecate. Most of the rape assaults are not reported.Exact needs and existing protection gaps will be identified at the needs assessment level prior each intervention.

3 - Project indicators

MEAL plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to becollected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible personand track the progress against key results and output indicators and the informationcaptured in different databases. PAH MEAL team will support the development of common data collection methodologies and it always takes part in joint needs assessments to ensure common standards. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings are incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. PAH will utilise sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and/or Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: # of women, men, boys and girls provided with group or individual specialized PSS or other related services# of women, men, boys and girls reached with awareness raising campaigns# of women, men, boys and girls receiving support in referrals or in transportation to services’ centresPAH will conduct the endline survey to compare the data from baseline and collect the feedback from the targeted population and have the lessons learnt for next activities. Despite of quantitative indicators, PAH will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. PAH conducts periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, including clusters. Post-intervention monitoring (PIM) will be done by independent PAH MEAL team across the project, as long as the location is still accessible and secure. PIMs gives a clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will give clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve the overall quality of PAH's projects. Regular evaluation of the projects will ensure accountability.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by PAH officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project - volunteers.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, PAH will try to adjust mechanism paths. In case of children with disabilities, with limited mobility, PAH officers will visit them directly to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate parents, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through a institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time PAH and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. As the GBV cases are often unreported, PAH officers will engage the caseworkers from the community and will be present in the community as often as possible to build trust and encourage effective psychosocial support. TOR for all activities will be presented to the authorities, plans are discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based caseworkers in GBV referrals, translators, enumerators. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, PAH staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge.To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.The records of complaints and feedback is kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. The implementing programme team, under supervision of Senior Management, if necessary, conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. Protection risks are most often higher for marginalized/minority groups’ members, so they will be the main focus in the project. All vulnerable groups will be targeted with attention to their needs and capacities. PAH has the flexibility to use innovative solutions to meet those needs, to ensure inclusion and empowerment. Complaint and feedback mechanisms are always in place to find out about any gaps and address them.Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation PAH can avoid aid diversion, ensure ownership of project results and involvement of the community in monitoring and control of the results. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, inc. logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored continuously. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information, between others about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, local regulations that may try to include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the two Collective Outcomes, i.e. Food insecurity and Gender-Based Violence?

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project activities are described in detail in the dedicated proposal part above. The sense and aim of the project is to provide quality, timely and inclusive protection services, mainly in areas of GBV mitigation and treatment, psychological support to children, inclusion of disabled, minority members. HLP issues will be also part of the project. PAH will work closely with other specialised sectors to make sure that affected population receive the needed quality and timely protection support.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The protection services are crucial and cross-sectoral, necessary to ensure acceptance and ownership of all programmes targeting the community. If community members live in constant insecurity, face threats, stress, hold traumas, and resolve conflicts, their capacity to develop is limited. Personal and communal safety and essential psychological comfort are vital for development. All general and targeted protection activities are provided to ensure synergies with other sectoral projects, overlap and influence their effectiveness. PAH will always cooperate with existing partners and other stakeholders to ensure protection mainstreaming across all programming.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

PAH ensures the equitable access to project’s activities and products, thanks to transparent registration, selection of beneficiaries, cross-checked by various stakeholders (from the community, local institutions and NGOs, and internally by PAH MEAL team). This effectively minimizes the risks of exclusion and misuse of the provided assistance. PAH experienced protection team guides the process of project implementation according to clusters and international standards. The team gives special attention to disabled, marginalized, minority representatives, unaccompanied children, GBV survivors and other vulnerable groups. PAH protection team will continue to strengthen community self-protection measures, and resilience that will identify, and mitigate protection risks through an organized series of community-based activities to ensure all members of the community are supported.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The malnutrition of children can have many reasons and influencing factors. One of them may be the lack of safety for them and their care-givers. Chronic stress, traumas, violent environment influences strongly the women – young mothers and later the child. Then the protection services, provided to mothers may, in the result, influence the well-being of the children. The safer protection environment in terms of community and institutional support, secure access to the farmers' plots, and livelihood sources can positively infect the families' nutrition level.
