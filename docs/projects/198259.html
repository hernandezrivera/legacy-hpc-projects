Project Summary

Deteriorating public health conditions threatened by annual outbreak of infectious diseases like Cholera, reduced humanitarian footprints due to insecurities and dwindling funding, Increase Protection risks, Government mandated camp closures leading to the influx of IDPs/returnees into camps and host communities along the transit routes, floods, rainstorms, and other adverse weather conditions have further complicated the humanitarian response in the BAY States (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe State), requiring a holistic approach that will appropriately address gaps and advance coordination.
LPF through this project will reach a total of 98,740 (30% female, 20% male, 30% girls & 20% boys) conflict-affected individuals in Askira Uba, Dikwa, Damboa, Gwoza, Nganzai, Gombi, Mubi North and South and Fune LGAs of BAY state.
This project aims to promote personal and public well-being of conflict and flood affected populations through improved access to basic water, sanitation, and Hygiene services that will avert or reduce the incidences of acute watery diarrhea /cholera and other infectious diseases. It also seeks to strengthen livelihood & economic activities in targeted locations.
The project acknowledges that vulnerabilities vary hence particular emphasis will be placed on the most vulnerable groups such as children, adolescents, women, men elderly, and groups with special needs” in targeting beneficiaries. The project is committed to reaching people with disabilities and has put in place deliberate measures to achieve this objective.
Through this WASH response, LPF will consciously mainstream Protection and support Nutrition, to leverage on knowledge, expertise, reach, and resources, benefiting from combined and varied strengths and shared goal of improving living conditions and building resilience of affected populations in the BAY States.
The response will adopt WASH Sector harmonised facilities designs & approaches in improving and sustaining access to WASH services in the target locations prioritising improved access to safe water through rehabilitation of twelve strategic broken-down water facilities, operation and maintenance of twenty eight water facilities, Construction and rehabilitation of emergency and household sanitation facilities, support health institution and schools with latrine cleaning materials, establish solid waste management systems. Conduct Door-to-door, focus group discussions, mass campaigns, and the use of Information, Education and Communication materials for hygiene promotion activities while increasing access to hygiene items that include menstrual hygiene management items to adolescent girls, Pregnant and lactating women, and survivors of GBV.

1 - Needs

The conflict stemming from the insurgency of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2022, 8.4 million people in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022, only slightly fewer than a year ago. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. This figure also includes the majority (an estimated 733,000) of the 1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors. Of the 61 LGAs that the Joint Inter-sectoral Analysis Framework (JIAF) classified using the MSNA and other data, 22 are classified as ‘extreme’ in terms of severity of needs; another 20 have ‘severe’ needs. Ten LGAs are at ‘stressed’ level, while the remaining one has ‘minimal severity’ of needs. As have been recorded: Formal, informal camps and host communities stand at the risk of a WASH related diseases outbreak due to lack of access to safe drinking water and poor sanitation and hygiene practices especially during the raining season. In the return areas of Borno state, returnees have found up to 75% of the water supply infrastructure that existed prior to the insurgency damaged or destroyed. In the IDP camps and host community selected for this intervention, the average allocation of water is 7.8 liters per person per day, below minimum standard of 15 liters. Borno state Government started to gradually relocate IDPs to their LGAs of origin and to other identified locations, while at the same time closing existing IDP camps in MMC/Jere. Most locations of returns and relocation do not have the necessary infrastructure or amenities to address the basic social service needs of the relocated IDPs and returnees neither to guarantee their safety and security. Therefore, returnees are exposed to new threats and the possibility of further displacements. Furthermore, the influx of returned populations might also overstretch existing humanitarian capacities on the ground. All these situations have caused huge overcrowding which exerts great pressure on existing Infrastructures in both Host Communities and IDPs camp. This increases dangers of disease outbreaks and Protection Cases (including Sexual and Gender-based violence (GBV) and Child Protection (CP) cases). In this setting, access to WASH facilities diminishes due to wear and tear of emergency type constructions, constant displacements, which intensifies open defecation and reduced hygiene practices, including hand-washing at critical times.

2 - Activities

This WASH Project is strategically designed to promote personal and public well-being of affected populations through improved access to basic water, sanitation, and Hygiene services that will avert/reduce the most severe threats to life and health, in order to reduce(excess) mortality and morbidity. It also aim to sustain support and see that Crisis-affected people enjoy a safer and healthier environment for living with adequate access to essential services while also seeking to strengthen livelihood & economic activities in targeted locations. This will be achieved by localising the intervention to enable a higher percentage of the Grant to be expended locally at project target locations, including prioritising local materials for an effective activity implementations whenever possible and deploying the use of local labour to be paid through the cash modality of Cash for Work; thereby stimulating livlihood and overall economics of the project target population.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The Project intervention is planned with high level of sensitivity to gender, age and disability and activities are targeted based on desegregated population to effectively serve all elements of the population. Women, Girls and People leaving with disability will be represented at all levels of the Programme implementation. Also, all Sanitation Facilities will be marked with clear and obvious gender Segregation markers while maintaining the WASH Sector Barricaded design for all such Facility. Each block of Sanitation Facility will have atleast a Unit of Latrine and Shower space constructed specifically for people living with disability. Furthermore, that Project will provide an effective and efficient Feed back Mechanism, through a Complain Response Mechanism (CRM) that will provide avenues for gathering beneficiary input and level of satisfaction, including complains and reports. The CRM will be designed capable of communicating sensitive complaints and a robust alternative will be provided as Reporting part-ways including for cases of GBV and SEA. Confidentiality will be the order of such report and investigative provisions of the project.
