Project Summary

In 2024, BRAC targets 22,800 children aged 3 to 18 years from both the Rohingya refugees (12 camps) and the host community (8 unions under Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila). Among them, a total of 16,800 (ECE-8,656, Myanmar Curriculumn-1,344, and Adolescent-6800) girls and boys from the Rohingya community, along with 6,000 girls and boys from the host community will be benefited from this programme. Among them, at least 51% will be girls, and 2% will be children with disabilities. The programme will be implemented by BRAC and its 07 other partner organizations.
In line with Education Sector Objective 1, BRAC is planning to construct 15 two-storied Learning Centres (LCs) subject to the approval of government and renovate 100 one-storied LCs to ensure the accessibility to quality education for Rohingya children. These LCs will create opportunities to provide quality education for the existing leaners of other organizations. In addition, 200 new pre-primary schools will be opened in the host community, wherein 6,000 learners (aged 5-6 years) will get access to the mainstream primary education in the host community. Furthermore, in coordination with 07 partner organizations, BRAC will also create opportunities for 15,600 (ECE-8,656, MC-1,344, Adolescent-5,600) learners from the Rohingya camps to access into inclusive and quality learning opportunities. Moreover, BRAC plans to repair 90 gender-segregated toilets. Additionally, to enhance the quality of education BRAC also aims to establish 03 language labs cum libraries and 03 mobile libraries, which will benefit 1,200 learners from the Rohingya community.
In accordance with the Education Sector’s objective 2, BRAC aims to provide education in Myanmar curriculum for the 1,344 Rohingya children aged 5-14 years through LCs and Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLFs). They will be supplied with necessary teaching-learning materials, like pens, exercise books, and science-related materials, including geometry boxes, globes, skeletons, etc. In addition, for the host community, 6,000 children will get pre-primary education from BRAC pre-primary school with all necessary teaching-learning materials. Besides this, regular teachers' training sessions will be conducted for both the Rohingya community and the host community, so that teachers can be more skillful in delivering their lessons in the Myanmar curriculum as well as pre-primary education.
In line with Sector’s objective 3, all community stakeholders, involving; parents, community leaders, the School Management Committee (SMC), and other education groups, will be actively engaged in capacity-building and mobilisation activities to support inclusive education initiatives and promote the return to school/LCs. BRAC will sensitise the community on gender, environment, social cohesion, increasing girls’ attendance, safe back to school, climate awareness, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), and roles and responsibilities of community members to minimise pre-existing socio-cultural norms. BRAC will emphasise rigorous monitoring and the Sector’s contingency plan to prepare for disasters and emergencies for man-made and natural disasters. BRAC will coordinate with all stakeholders, including the RRRC office, associated government agencies, and other implementing organisations, for the smooth implementation of programme activities. For localisation and smooth operation of the programme, BRAC will involve the local implementing partners, who will be supported and monitored by BRAC.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Education provision for Rohingya children in camps is entirely informal. According to the Gender and Adolescence: Global Evidence (GAGE) consortium report published in March 2022, 49% of Rohingya adolescents attended informal schooling. Gender and age critically influence educational attendance and retention; Rohingya girls aged 10+ are most adversely affected; only 2% of the mentioned age group reported engaging in learning. They face multifaceted challenges, including age and gender-based violence, disrupted educational prospects, widespread psychosocial distress and worry, and limited health service uptake. Children with disabilities face more significant challenges in accessing and progressing through education. Further challenges have also arisen, such as the marriage of girls and/or pregnancy, lack of guidance from learning facilitators, and children entering an age group that has lost interest in education. According to the Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA) 2023, the main concerns of education are: girls are significantly out of reach due to patriarchal attitudes against girl education and the lack of menstrual-friendly WASH-segregated facilities in LCs. Children with disabilities have additional barriers to accessibility (camp, road, and geographical terrain) and a lack of inclusive teaching-learning materials. They are less likely to attend learning facilities. The lack of qualified and trained teachers from the Rohingya community is also one of the significant challenges now. Besides, congested camps in Cox’s Bazar have further constrained possibilities of establishing adequate learning centres. The main reasons for constructing the two-storied LCs are mainly open space limitation for building new LCs for the new student/new enrollment, lack of multiple classroom facilities in existing learning centres for the students who pass from lower grade class to higher grade class. In addition, the need for safe spaces in the camps during any emergencies, like; cyclone ‘Mocha’ is paramount where these two-storied LCs can play an important role. The advocacy efforts for the approval are ongoing with the relevant Govt. authorities including RRRC, and Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief. BRAC is hopeful and confident that in early 2024 BRAC will get the approval to build two-storied LCs which will be vertically extended LCs in the camp. Apart from this, the lifespan of a bamboo structure is about two years, and most of the BRAC learning centres were constructed in 2018. These structures need to be reinforced to ensure the safety of the attending learners. Apart from the Rohingya communities, The host community is having an adverse impact on the schools of Ukhiya and Teknaf, where enrolment and retention were the lowest in the country. The quality of education has also deteriorated due to community teachers seeking higher-paid jobs in the camps with humanitarian agencies. Moreover, in the selected host communities, the opportunities for pre-primary education are very limited. Last but not least, almost one-fifth are using unimproved sanitation facilities, and there is a need for gender-segregated WASH facilities and raising awareness of the habit of using WASH facilities among families and the community. In both the Rohingya and host areas, the engagement of the community members is essential to address the following issues; low attendance, quality education, girls' enrollment, child protection, safeguarding, etc, through capacity-building initiatives and regular meetings.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This proposed education programme is aligned with the rationalisation principles of the Education Sector. This programme will implement the Myanmar Curriculum for the learners from the Rohingya community, including Early Childhood Education, and Pre Primary Education for the learners from the host community. In consultation with the Cox's Bazar Education sector, BRAC will select its geographical area of operation to avoid any kind of geographic overlaps and gaps. BRAC is now operating its different services in all 33 camps of Cox’s Bazar; however, BRAC is planning to consolidate all its services in 15 camps gradually in the future. As BRAC plans to undertake multi-sectoral activities in the same camp, its programmes are aligned with rationalisation principles 3. Moreover, BRAC has been ensuring stability in the refugee operation for the last five years and improving relations with refugees, the Government, and other partners in the camps. As a result, BRAC is now considering multi-year funding, especially for critical activities that may require sustained longer-term engagement in Education, particularly adolescent girls' education. In addition, BRAC is committed to serving in the education sector in the same camps for several years, which is mentioned in its 05 years humanitarian action plan that will support strengthening relations with government agencies, the refugees, and other implementing partners at the camp level. All activities are aligned with the JRP following current Sectoral/Working Group objectives and strategy. We also actively participate in the relevant Sector and/or Working Group and are active members of the Cox’s Bazar PSEA Network and appointed PSEA Focal Points and Child Safeguarding Focal Points. BRAC has a strong government Liaison team to obtain the relevant approvals from the government promptly.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project will address protection mainstreaming as a cross-cutting issue to serve the Rohingya community by integrating protection concerns across all aspects of implementation. This will promote the active participation of the Rohingya community specially during parents meeting in decisions affecting their life, ensuring that their protection needs and concerns are considered where special attention will be given to the protection of women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. To mainstream protection in Education, few key elements will be focused like ensuring the safety, and dignity of each staff and program participants following the do no harm policy. BRAC has a strong safeguarding policy including child safeguarding and PSEA. All staff of BRAC will receive safeguarding training, and the programme will follow BRAC safeguarding policy to ensure the safety and security of Persons of concern (PoCs). Additionally, BRAC will strongly avoid employing any child labour in any form of BRAC’s work. BRAC has a referral mechanism in partnership with the child protection sector to provide support to any child. By meaningful access, accountability of intervention will be assured. Moreover participation of all gender groups despite age, disability will be prioritized in overall project implementation. The desired output will be ensured by the establishment of empowerment of participants. BRAC respects and strictly follows four protection mainstreaming principles for their stakeholders, staff and volunteers such as; Prioritise safety and dignity and avoid harm, secure meaningful access, ensure accountability, and ensure participation and empowerment. BRAC will ensure sufficient safety protocols for educators and students, irrespective of their gender, age, sexual preference, religion, ethnic background, or country of origin. Activities and policies will be implemented to foster a wider range of diversity.

3.3 - Age & Disability

BRAC always believes in diversity and inclusiveness in the project or workplace. A wide variety of activities will be conducted with the support of a diversified group of people. In the learning centres, the learners aged 3- 18 years will get the enrollment opportunity, with the inclusion of 2% disabilities. The reasonable accommodation will be ensured for the children with disabilities. Disable friendly toilet facilities will be constructed in accordance. BRAC will create a supportive and empathetic environment among all staff and learners so that they can assist the learners with disabilities and motivate them to overcome their mental stigma for their sustainable future. Moreover, BRAC also welcomes the employment of persons with disabilities to ensure inclusiveness.However, all gender, ethnicity, race, and religion-diversified children will be eligible to enroll in the learning centres. Regarding the recruitment of staff, everyone has the opportunity to be part of this project regardless of their gender, age, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, or nationality. A broader spectrum of diversity will be promoted through activities and policy.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

BRAC acknowledges the importance and value of involving affected communities throughout the project cycle. Participation and inclusion of the affected population will be ensured from the design phase to the final evaluation phase. Community-level meetings will be arranged to share relevant and actionable information with communities. Some community groups will be formed, regardless of sex, age, disability status, and other diversity, who will identify their education-related needs and address their vulnerabilities. Community feedback systems will be in place to enable affected people to assess and comment on the performance of our services, including on sensitive matters such as sexual exploitation and abuse, fraud, corruption, etc. Regular consultation meetings with the community people will be organised for this purpose. The monitoring team will also manage time-to-time evaluations to capture community feedback. The project team will respond to the feedback of the community.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

For PSHEA mainstreaming and response; BRAC has the Safeguarding and Grievance Management Committee (SGMC) and focal points to operationalize awareness-raising, capacity-building initiatives, reporting, and referral services in the humanitarian and development sectors. BRAC has a comprehensive safeguarding policy to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation, and child abuse for all stakeholders. BRAC ensures prompt response when any offense is reported. In addition, to ensure the survivors' assistance, close monitoring and support for reporting and response processes are there. The organization demonstrates a Zero-Tolerance Approach to sexual harassment and other safeguarding concerns. BRAC's protection and harassment policies are strictly followed, and every staff and volunteer will be trained on protection policies. This program will also be supported by BRAC's Child Protection and Protection sector to mainstream the protection in education.All staff and volunteers should understand, be trained on, and agree to uphold the child safeguarding and PSEA policies and reporting system. It is our responsibility to ensure that all staff, volunteers, and teachers will follow these policies and sign to confirm they have understood and will follow them. BRAC beneficiaries and volunteers can provide feedback through different channels that allow us to prevent/mitigate adverse situations. We have three options for reporting including whistle-blowing. Moreover, by pasting posters with details of focal points and the Community Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) box in every facility for staff, volunteers, and community people so that they can raise about any incident.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Considering the camps' disaster risk assessment, this programme will provide Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) training to the volunteers, teachers, and community members so they can act as early responders to any disaster. BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguarding Framework (ESSF), which sets out the scope and implementation modalities for identifying and avoiding or mitigating environmental and social risks. This framework focuses on safeguard requirements, while BRAC’s environmental policy includes the “Do Good” criteria and the “Do No Harm” requirements embedded in this framework. BRAC is committed to avoiding, minimising or mitigating adverse environmental, social or economic impacts associated with the projects it supports. As per ESSF, BRAC will not directly or indirectly support any activity that will significantly convert or degrade critical natural habitats for environmental sustainability. BRAC will ensure that the operation does not have potentially adverse severe impacts on biodiversity and ecosystem services.
