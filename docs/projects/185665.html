Project Summary

"The proposed project will provide promotional nutritional services (nutritional assessment, counseling, diet management and referral services) through 12 nutrition corners situated in BRAC health facilities in 10 camps. The objective of the project is early detection of malnourished children aged 0-59 months and pregnant and lactating women. For that, all the children under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women visits the BRAC health facilities will be screened by anthropometric measurement tools. If the nutritional status found satisfactory then they be provided with nutritional counseling only. However, if the assessment founds any malnourished under 5 children and pregnant & Lactating women, the project will refer the case the dedicated integrated nutrition facility of that specific camp and ensure that they are enrolled with the program for the treatment of malnutrition. Alongside, children of 0-36 month age group will be provided with age specific Early Year’s Stimulation services for optimum physical and mental development and improve nutritional status form the BRAC nutrition corners. Furthermore, the project will focus on improving Nutritional status of pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) by providing necessary nutrition related counseling while they will be visiting BRAC health Facilities for ante-natal and post-natal care. At the same time, adolescent girls will be targeted for counseling on nutritional practices and recommended diet intake. With all these, the project will significantly, contribute to improve nutritional status of elderly people (>60years) and patients with chronic disease, like Tuberculosis, Hypertension, Diabetics, Cancer, Hepatitis, CKD, HIV, etc. by providing nutrition counseling and diet management (diet recall for last 24 hours, provide diet plan based on the type of chronic disease by Nutritionist).
The project will deliver its services equally for all, giving preference to the most vulnerable ie women and girls, children, elderly, and persons with special needs. All the centres will prioritise access for women and girls. There will be separate waiting space and gender segregated WASH facilities in the health facilities where nutrition corners are situated.
In the nutrition corner, there will be feedback and complaint system in place and the issues will be considered as priority and address accordingly. The project will conduct exit interview with the beneficiaries to assess the quality of service delivery and will improve as needed.
The nutrition corners are in the BRAC health facilities which are more durable and disaster resistant and will facilitate uninterrupted service delivery during monsoon and other natural disasters.
Promoting Gender equality through nutrition services will be the key focus of this project. Therefore, whatever the interventions are designed women, girls and other marginalized people will be at the center of the services. So, all the nutrition facilities will emphasize on an inclusive approach to reach women, girls, elderly, persons with disabilities and gender diversified individuals. Project staff will be trained on safeguarding, gender equality, inclusion and protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). "

1 - Identified Needs

"The level of both acute and chronic malnutrition in Rohingya refugee camps have been reduced significantly since the influx in 2017, however still these remain unacceptably high. As per Emergency Nutrition Assessment Round 5 which was conducted during November-December 2020, the prevalence of GAM per WHZ among children 6-59 months was found 11.3%, which is in the public health threshold category of “HIGH” according to the new 2018 WHO/UNICEF classification for Wasting. The prevalence of Combined GAM was 11.8% with a Combined SAM 1.4% among children 6-59 months in Makeshift settlements. The rate is still fall under public health threshold category of “HIGH” according to the new 2018 WHO/UNICEF classification for Wasting. Also, the prevalence of global chronic malnutrition or Stunting per HAZ among children 6-59 months was 34.2%, which is considered ‘Very High’ based on the new 2018 WHO/UNICEF malnutrition threshold classification for Stunting. The prevalence of underweight per WAZ among children 6-59 months was 30.8%.In the Emergency Nutrition Assessment round 4 conducted in 20219, it was found that the prevalence of anemia among children aged 6-59 months in makeshift camps was 37.4% and anaemia prevalence for 15-49 years women (non-PLW) in the Makeshift camps was 20.3%.The prevalence of diarrhoea among children 6-59 months as per two-week recall period is 14.9%. Where World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 50% of cases of child under-nutrition are due to repeated diarrhoea and intestinal infections caused by poor sanitation and hygiene practices. Hand washing with soap is a critical determinant for achieving and maintaining good nutrition. This healthy behavior plays an important part in preventing micronutrient deficiencies, stunting, wasting, and deaths. Though the exact number of patients with chronic diseases in the camp is not known, they need special support regarding lifestyle modification and diet management to maintain a good nutritional status and physical wellbeing.To address the above critical situation of malnutrition in the Camp, BRAC has planned to deliver promotive nutrition services through existing 12 Nutrition Corners situated in BRAC health facilities in the 10 Rohingya Camps. The nutrition corner will provide nutritional assessment of children under 5 years, pregnant and Lactating Women, IYCF and other nutrition related counseling, early years stimulation services for 0-36 month children, diet management and life style modification for pregnant, lactating women, elderly people and patient with chronic diseases, distribution of IFA, Vitamin-A, deworming tablets."
