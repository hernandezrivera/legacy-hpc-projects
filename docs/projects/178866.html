Project Summary

This project aims to support the olive sector and vulnerable olive farmers in Idlib and Afrin districts to support workers group’s resilience through the provision of agricultural services and inputs in addition to the cash for work related to income generation activities through supporting farmers and households.
The project’s interventions will target IDPs and host residents regardless of their ethnic, cultural or social background, focusing on females headed households by increasing their knowledge on how to benefit and use the olive chain value by food production and marketing awareness sessions provided. Moreover, the elderly and persons with special need to be targeted through the project activities with appropriate modalities.
Sadad well-trained and experienced technical team will focus on improving food security and supporting livelihood by providing two main components:
1-Support for best agriculture services to increase the productivity of 1000 hectares of Olive trees (2000 HHs, most of them females headed by support 5 dunums per household) through providing the following:
• Autumn and spring land ploughing;
• Harvest service;
• Two rounds of leaf-nutrition high K and high N;
• Pest controls (olive fly, olive moth, stem digger and peacock eye disease);
• Technical pruning service;
• Providing the targeted 2000 olive farmers' HHs with 2 olive-oil extraction vouchers per household (each voucher will be used to extract olive oil from 1250 kg of olives;
• Conducting the olive oil extraction process in the selected and contracted mills;
• Conducting awareness-raising seminars for 700 farmers on integrated olive-cultivation management, pest’s control, olive trees' services calendar, and avoiding negative methods related to olive cultivation, food production, and marketing of products for local usage.
2-Improving livelihoods of crisis-affected groups by creating short term cash for work opportunities including vocational training linked to conducting agriculture services (2415 beneficiaries 915 IDPS / 1500 host community) through providing cash for work related to agricultural activities as follow:
• 200 male beneficiaries will be hired and provided with cash for 50 working days to conduct the project's related services and activities, in addition to a three-day training workshop on the integrated olive -cultivation management, and an introduction to minimum standards in humanitarian action including how to deal with beneficiaries according to humanitarian standards;
• 1400 beneficiaries will be hired, divided into groups and provided with cash for 25 working days to conduct olive fruit harvesting service;
• Vocational training on technical olive trees pruning, 450 male beneficiaries will receive 7 days of training on technical olive trees pruning and then will be provided with cash for 36 working days to perform technical pruning to the targeted hectares;
• 365 Males' beneficiaries will be provided with cash for 30 working days to conduct Spraying service, two rounds, for the targeted 1000 hectares of the olive;
• Mainstreaming General protection through conducting 36 awareness-raising sessions on; GBV related risks، child labour and child marriage, awareness on mine action issues and this will include distributing awareness-raising brochures and leaflets.
As a result, the project will contribute mainly to enhancing food security by supporting olive agriculture and income generation means and supporting the resilience of the vulnerable groups within the targeted communities.

4 - Needs

The agriculture sector, which was once a cornerstone of the Syrian economy, has also been severely impacted. High cost and limited availability of agriculture inputs, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, direct and knock-on effects of COVID-19 on food security and the agriculture sector, the devastating wildfires, and other climate-induced shocks such as erratic rainfall, have all contributed to the worsening situation in Syria.According to needs assessment analysis conducted by Sadad’s field teams shows that:• There is a high cost of agriculture services like; pesticides, pruning, and milling leading to an increase in production costs. • The high cost of fuel for supplementary irrigation, that led to the olive farmers depending on only rain to water the trees as a negative adaptation;• The spread of pests and the high costs of the control process (insect diseases, and fungi diseases);• Marketing difficulties and limited access to local markets.• The high costs of daily workers led to low farmer incomes and high services costs.Also, the needs assessment shows that 90% of those residents of the surveyed areas were depending on olive trees production or service provided to it as a source of income with a lack of provision of assistance from organizations and local authorities, especially in light of Covid-19 epidemic and the devaluation of the Syrian pound. Sadad makes sure to target the most vulnerable groups and high Districts (Afrin and Idleb), high severity needs based on the available sources shared by the FSL Cluster and other partners.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented through Sadad well trained technical teams in Idleb and Afrin Districts. The implementation process will be done with the collaboration of local councils and the agricultural offices in the targeted villages. After that, announcements of the project's job opportunities will be done, then signing the contracts •Conducting deep need assessment to select beneficiaries accordingto specific criteria. •Contracting with200 service workers, they will be trained on theintegrated management of Olive cultivation and how to deal with LCs and BNFs accordingto humanitarian standards.•Contracting with pruning workers and providing themwith training on techniques of olive pruning to conduct pruning service of the targeted1000 hectares of olive trees. •Purchasing leaf-nutrition and pests control materials (high K and high N).Then contracting with spraying workers who will conduct leaf nutrition and pests control (high K and highN) service to 1000 hectares of olive cultivated lands. •Contracting with tractors and relatedplowing equipment then starting Autumn-spring land-plowing for 1000 hectares of olivecultivated areas. •Contracting with1400 males and females harvest-workers to conductharvesting for 1000 hectares of olive cultivated areas, then MOUs will be signed betweenSadad and 8 mills•Distributing plastic bags, gallons to beneficiaries and olive-oilextraction vouchers•Conducting workshops for 700 farmers on integrated olive-cultivation management•During implementing the whole project’s services, Sadadprotection team will conduct awareness-raising sessions about common protectionissues and feedback from beneficiaries.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers. Mitigation: Sadad ensures to have in a place an accessible mechanism for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse in line with Sadad safeguarding policy. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Risk: Lack of understanding of employees about PSEA issues. Sadad understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about Sadad position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation within the targeted villages is estimated to be safe to ensure the best implementation and provision of services as the villages related to rural Idlib and Afrin districts both of them are considered to be safe after the spread of Turkish monitoring points and located under Turkish government control areas (Olive Branch areas and Idlib). Risk 1: beneficiaries registration process. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: • beneficiaries to be selected based on clear selection criteria;• conducting meetings with a community representative, interested parties to be invited through a public announcement to ensure fair distribution and selection of Bens;• field staff will be trained on the code of conduct, and PSEA procedures;• Verification process of selected beneficiaries;• Feedback mechanism to be activated in the field to respond to these issues.Risks 2: Community tension between host and IDPs. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Low. Mitigation:• A community-based selection of beneficiaries of most vulnerable host and IDPs in close coordination with local authorities;• Clear selection criteria of beneficiaries;• Feedback mechanism to be activated in the field to respond to these issues.
