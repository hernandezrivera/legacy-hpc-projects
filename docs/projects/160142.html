Project Summary

South Sudan remains in a serious humanitarian crisis due to the cumulative effects of years of conflict and violence against civilians, which have destroyed people’s livelihoods and forced 4.2 million people to flee their homes—nearly 2 million inside and 2.2 million outside the country. The number of people who require humanitarian or protection assistance in 2019 remains high at seven million— the same as in 2018.
In the 2020 HRP, ARC proposes to upscale on-going life-saving interventions to affected populations by promptly and effectively responding to the WASH needs identified through assessments conducted jointly by UNOCHA and other partners. ARC is also proposing a more strengthened integration among its sectors of WASH, GBV and Nutrition. To this end, WASH activities will target locations where ARC is implementing both GBV and Nutrition or either of the two by co-creating technical/ infrastructure solutions with the people from the communities, human-centered and interactive approaches in hygiene and sanitation promotion such as the PHAST, Wash’em and Super Momas; and gender balanced-community lead operation and maintenance practices. Being human centered and design through co-creation cultivate the radical transparency atmosphere as statement of Accountability to Assisted Persons. Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) sector will provide services to men, women, boys and girls IDPs, returnees and host communities in Ulang County in Upper Nile State, Kajo-Keji in Central Equatorial State, Budi, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East Counties in Eastern Equatorial State and Aweil West and Aweil Center in Northern Bar Ghazal. Besides ARC having presence in the selected areas under the OFDA, all the Counties selected are rated as Level 4 (Alert) in the WASH severity mapping in Food and Nutrition Survey of July 2019. In Kajo-Keji, ARC will be working with GBV and nutrition sectors in targeting IDPs, returnees and host community. ARC is already in the county offering the same services to the mentioned category of beneficiaries, but struggling to ensure proper coverage. Similar responses are on-going in all the others selected counties of Budi, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East in Eastern Equatoria and Ulang in Upper Nile. In Bar Ghazal, ARC is operating fifteen (15) nutrition centers in Aweil West and one women center in Aweil Center. WASH will strengthen integration by working with women and adolescent girls on operation and maintenance of WASH facilities and promoting hygiene at the centers.
Despite all the challenges, revitalized peace process is promising new opportunities for South Sudanese. Cumulative effects of years of conflict and violence destroyed livelihoods and left more than 7 million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection in 2019.Even though the situation is no longer escalating at a rapid speed, the country remains in the grip of a humanitarian crisis. According to humanitarian needs overview (HNO) 2019 of November 2018, an estimated 5,712,000 women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in 2019.In the 2020 HRP, ARC will base its response on well demonstrated needs. The needs will be informed by key cluster players like WASH Cluster technical working groups, assessments conducted jointly with credible entities like UNOCHA, REACH and ARC assessments that are evidence based.

1 - Needs

Kajo-Keji County: UN-OCHA fact finding mission in June 2019 was primarily to assess the humanitarian situation and establish the number and areas of settlements and check on the accessibility of people in need. There is high demand for life-saving humanitarian services. The threshold of partners operating in Kajo Keji has been overstretched due to limited resources to respond to emerging needs. Access to safe water remains a major gap in the IDP sites with high population. In rural areas where people are returning to reinstate their households and livelihoods through cultivation of their lands and resume their lives at their places of origin, majority of water points are dysfunctional due to lack of maintenance. During the assessment mission, interviews with affected persons established diarrhea and typhoid as most common communicable diseases and suffering due to lack of health services. Open defecation is evident at temporary camp sites. Reproductive age groups of women are most affected, particularly adolescent girls lacking adequate space or items to practice intimate hygiene. Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East: Food and Nutrition Survey of July 2019 has categorized the two neighboring counties as being at level 4 (alert) of needs for assistance. The two are also marked as cholera and AWDs prone counties based on the recurring outbreaks from 2014 to 2018. With the long rains, the possibility of cholera outbreak is high. ARC is not among EPR partners but can play crucial role in preventive measures through hygiene promotion,construction of household latrines, rehabilitation of bore-holes, water quality monitoring, testing and treatment. ARC understands WASH challenges in Kapoeta South having successfully participated in cholera response in the 2014 outbreak that affected more than half of the then ten states of South Sudan. Aweil West and Aweil Center: ARC will undertake WASH, GBV and nutrition integrated interventions through provision of technical support to WASH facilities within Nutrition and GBV operated Centers in the two Counties. When assessed in mid-2018, only 40 per cent of nutrition treatment sites had access to safe water in Aweil West.Latest floods affected millions of people in October 2019. As UNICEF reports on October 25, 2019: “An estimated 490,000 children are affected by heavy flooding in South Sudan.Exceptional heavy rain since July, have affected some 908,000 people in 32 counties.The current scope and scale of the flooding is severe especially in the former states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Warrap and Northern Bahr El Ghazal. The rain is expected to continue for another four to six weeks” In this emergency context and the context of existing needs, WASH needs within the community will be addressed through rehabilitation of bore-holes, provision of latrines construction materials and public health promotion.The proposed integrated Nutrition, GBV and WASH programming will have impact as GBV related risks and acute watery diarrhea that contribute to high nutrition needs will be addressed through GBV and WASH interventions. Budi County: Budi County, besides its unfavorable WASH severity mapping of level 4,it is very much undeserved. There is increased need for WASH services in Budi County after NIRAS funded WASH program implemented by Community Development Support Services and Rural Water and Sanitation came to a close in September, 2019. This left ARC the only active WASH partner in the County. Ulang County: IRNA assessment conducted between 14th-17th October 2019, 25,758 people have been affected by the floods and majority has been displaced with no facilities to support them.The team recommended provision of water storage cans, water purification tablets and encourage alternative water treatment methods like boiling, filtering and use of ash. ARC will continue providing technical support in maintenance of GBV facilities operated by ARC and extend emergency WASH services to returnees and flood affected.

Water Supply and quality testing and treatment: • Rehabilitation of 60 boreholes with hand pumps in KajoKeji host community settlement using contractors who will be identified from the area. • Ensure regular chlorination of water points and provision of aqua tabs for use at households.• Rehabilitation and repair of 24 hand pumps in Budi, 8 in Kapoeta South and 6 in Kapoeta East through provision of spare parts and hand pump tool kits for repair and maintenance of hand pumps • • Rehabilitation and recovery of bore-holes affected by floods in Aweil West and Aweil Center Counties and Ulang through shock chlorination.• Provision of water treatment reagents and water filters to people affected by floods in Ulang and Aweil Counties.• Provide new training/refresher training for Hand Pump Mechanics to provide repair and maintenance to hand pumps. • In Budi, upgrade 2 hand pumps to motorized pumps to improve water quantity and quality and shorten walking distance to beneficiaries through installation of submersible pumps, water tanks and construction of water networks for IDPs in Kajo keji IDP settlement. • Conduct water quality testing including water chlorination and bacteriological testing, using water testing devices and supplies. Sanitation Activities • Construct a total of 240 household latrines, which are gender separated and safely accessed and equipped with hand washing facilities in IDP/returnee settlements in Kajo keji, West, Aweil Center and Ulang. • Establish, train and support Women Hygiene Committees each committee consists of 10 female. • Conduct hygiene promotion education campaigns activities in IDP, returnee and host community camps and settlements. • Undertake promotional activities to in Kapoeta South, Budi, Kajo-keji and Aweil Center and Aweil West to prevent cholera outbreak due to flooding and poor hygiene practices. • Support to GBV and Nutrition Centers Technical design, supervision of construction, operation and maintenance of all WASH infrastructures within GBV and Nutrition Centers operated by ARC• Work with other partners in Ulang to address the effects of flood and inflow of returnees through increased hygiene promotion and latrines construction targeting returnees and IDPs.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project targets 50% IDPs, 30% returnees and 20% host community. To ensure that the displaced people that forms 50% of the target group and all other categories targeted have full access,they will be informed and involved in the process of project monitoring and evaluation. Also, displaced beneficiaries will be made aware about the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) system which ARC will analyse from time to time to measure beneficiaries’ access barriers to services. Through period program activity reports, ARC will documents the bureaucratic and physical access impediments, its magnitude, scope and impact on programming and corrective measures taken. All the information will be documented in the Complaints and Response Mechanism database held by the MEAL Coordinator at a central location in Juba. Documentation of issues related to access of benefits will be useful in designing future programs that will address identified and documented barriers.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

ARC involves the government, community leaders and community members) in the design and implementation of projects in order to strengthen their ownership. ARC ensures full beneficiaries and local actor’s participation as an essential part of project planning, implementation and handover that forms exit strategy. The IDPs participate mainly through Focus Group Discussions (FGD), especially women and girls. During post-Distribution Monitoring the beneficiaries are able to formulate their questions and needs, which is also possible in the FGDs and Interviews on household level. The WASH Committees planned for each water point in IDP settlements as well as host communities are an important step towards preparing community to take up their roles and responsibility in future program management.Under the mentorship of ARC Program Officers, the Committees conduct monthly meetings, identify and prioritize WASH needs, share with the larger community to explore possibilities for improvement, lessons learnt and solutions. Through the stated inclusiveness to programming, future program designs borrow heavily from homegrown solutions.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is hinged on the three WASH Cluster strategic objectives namely (i) Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys (ii) Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialized and integrated services (iii) Support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses. South Sudan WASH cluster ensures that its objectives are not in conflict with the overall objectives of South Sudan Government, that is the Ministry of Water and Irrigation. As the project targets people affected by conflict,it is heavy on emergency response and social protection of IDPs,returnees and community playing host to IDPs and returnees.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

ARC works to minimize the risks to vulnerable populations in its water and sanitation programs by developing an in-depth understanding of the community dynamics in relation to individual and group interests and sensitivities such as ethnicity, religious beliefs, economic status etc., in order to ensure equitable access for all. Through its WASH technical designs, ARC strives to reduce the risks from exposure to GBV, create safe and friendly environments for women and children, and make the facilities accessible to individuals with special needs. ARC adheres to the set of guidelines that are an intrinsic part of our program design paradigm. These include the IASC/ Global Protection Cluster Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, WASH Thematic Area Guide (TAG), and the Handicap International guidelines on design of interventions for people with disability. Illustrative actions ARC takes to ensure protection and gender are mainstreamed in WASH programming include:Separate communal latrines for women and men constructed at suitable locations where health teams will provide their services regularly. • Positioning of latrines at visible locations in the village to discourage GBV perpetrators to commit act of violence in clear view of the community, while at the same time, ensuring dignified access to women, adolescent girls and other users through installation of partition screens between latrines used by men/ boys and by women/ girls, or positioning the block for women/ girls separately from the block for men/ boys where more suitable. • Solar lights and strong door locking system at each latrine cubicle.ARC communal latrine design includes positioning of baskets inside the latrine cubicles/ blocks designated for women and girls to dispose sanitary items in dignity, with clean water jerry cans positioned inside.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Sustainability element is embedded in transition or exit strategy which is deliberate collaborative effort between local authorities, partners and shared with the IDP and host community population. This involves identifying potential partners to take over WASH activities either partially or wholly. The phase out strategy will involve the step by step handing over of roles and responsibilities, starting with the local authorities in charge of the relief and humanitarian aid, camp management, followed by WASH committees with the active support of the IDP and host community population. Capacity building is a central pillar in this project with the outcome to increase the capacity of community actors, local NGOs and local authorities to provide timely WASH assistance to vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities that meet the differing needs of women and girls. The project components aimed at host communities will be implemented in close cooperation with the sub-county structures, in consultation with women and girls, putting their specific needs and the different ethnic, religious and linguistic diversity of the communities into consideration. Capacity building undertakings will be delivered in the form of training, mentorship, coaching, and the self-construction of pit latrines in a way that prepares the community for the sustainability of the projects beyond the project life cycle.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ARC is fully aware that people with disabilities or marginalized groups are the most knowledgeable about their own needs and are the best source of expertise on what works best for them. The impact of this project is going to affect people living with disabilities, marginalized or minority groups, men, women, boys and girls all differently and efforts will be made to ensure that their needs, priorities, suggestions and capacities are considered during project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation to avoid perpetuating gender inequalities in any way. ARC's commitment to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) focuses on the effective implementation of three long-held principles: 1) our customer is the IDP/Returnee/host community, 2) IDPs/Returnee/host communities define quality and 3) we solve problems alongside IDPs/Returnee/host communities. These principles hold us accountable to improving the quality, relevance and impact of our work in the interest of beneficiaries. To demonstrate ARC's commitment to AAP in practice, ARC will ensure strong accountability in both emergency development interventions targeting communities and individuals directly affected by disasters by involving them directly in program design and encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them via periodic focus group discussions (FGDs) and an ongoing feedback/complaint response mechanism throughout this project.Furthermore, the affected people have the channel to contact the field staff on daily basis to present their needs.There are continuous monitoring where the beneficiaries can express concerns about their satisfaction with the project implementation. Project designs like post distribution monitoring (PDM) and reporting on NFIs like hygiene kits are useful feedback on how to improve service delivery to the affected people.
