Project Summary

Cyclical climate shocks, including drought and flooding as well as ongoing conflict contribute to growing internal displacement in Somalia, with an estimated 2.6 million women, men, girls and boys currently displaced. The IDPs are currently residing in urban and Peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. They remain in need of Shelter and NFIs support for protection from harsh climatic conditions, improvements to their living conditions and improvement to access livelihoods and durable solutions. Armed conflict and widespread violence, as well as recurrent climatic shocks, perpetuate high levels of humanitarian needs and protection concerns. The most vulnerable groups, who are exposed to the highest risks and are consistently socially excluded, include women, children, the elderly, child- and female-headed households, the physically and mentally disabled, people living in conflict zones, and marginalized clans.
Under the proposed intervention, SFH aims to address the immediate humanitarian needs of the newly displaced, the protracted IDPs and host communities living in a deplorable shelter conditions in Kismayo, Dolow and Afmadow districts of Lower Juba and Gedo region by providing life-saving and life-sustaining Emergency Shelter and NFI assistance to the most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls. The project will ensure affected population have protection, privacy and improve their living conditions through the provision of appropriate emergency shelter assistance, non-food items (NFI) while transitioning to durable solutions. The proposed intervention aims to provide NFIs/core relief items to 1000 (6000 persons) newly displaced IDPs and those in protracted situation through in-kind. Further, 1000 (6000 persons) newly displaced IDPs will also receive Type 2 emergency shelter kits to improve their shelter condition. The content and the composition of both the NFI and ESKs a will be in compliance with most recent standards provided in the latest Shelter Cluster strategy. Thus, a total of 6000 most vulnerable persons in the target region with have an improved shelter and Core relief items. While strict community and need based targeting will be applied in the registration and distribution of the assistance, female and child headed households, the elderly of above 65+, the chronically ill and person living with disability will be prioritized. SFH will undertake post distribution and post construction monitoring for each round of activity completed. Responsive community response and feedback mechanism will be established.
SFH will use age, gender and diversity approach to address the needs of the affected population. Different population groups including men, women, children, older people and disabled will be consulted and their specific needs will be considered in planning and implementation of the planned interventions.

Project Activities

The following main activities will be undertaken to achieve the program objectives and desired outcome1) Distribution of CRI/NFI Kits to 1000 Households (6000 persons) among the newly displaced and Protracted IDPs in Juballand as per the cluster standards.2) Distribution of 1000 Type 2 Emergency Shelter Kits to 1000 (6000 persons) newly displaced households3) Conduct Post constructions monitoring and post distributions monitoring (PCM/PDM) for every activity completed as per the cluster standards.4) Establish effective, responsive and accessible Community response and feedback mechanism (CRFM).Implementation Approach:SFH will target the most severely affected populations that demonstrate acute, life-saving need for either shelter materials or basic household items. Support will be provided on the basis of identified need regardless of a beneficiary’s “status,” such as IDP or returnee. At the same time, SFH recognizes that gender, age, and status are often factors that enhance vulnerability and thus need to be taken into consideration when analyzing the degree of need. Persons who are exposed to a combination of risks and have limited ability to cope in the face of those risks will always be prioritized. This groups includes:A) Pregnant and lactating mothers; B) Unaccompanied elderly (60+ years); C) People with physical disabilities or chronic illness; D) Female-headed households or single parents; E) Unaccompanied minors (under 18 years old); F) Persons with no effective community links or Minority groups

Mitigation Measures

1) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services.Mitigation:a) SFH will Identify central locations to ensure beneficiaries do not traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.b) Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.c) NFI will be distributed in an appropriate NFI bag that can be carried to allow beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them.2) Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance Mitigation: a) All SFH staff and suppliers are required to sign PSEA policy and its violation will lead to disciplinary measures including termination of contract.b) SFH will ensure appropriate gender balance and composition of registration and distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community.c) We will establish separate male and female queuing areas and ensure female staff presence at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls.3) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities a) Targeting will be guided by needs, access and vulnerability and the vulnerable groups include women headed household, child headed householdsb) Inclusive representation of the committees will be prioritized to ensure persons with disability or special needs are represented and their data made available.c) SFH will make special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the kits. they will be assisted and their safe arrivals ensured.4) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanismsa) SFH will establish accessible and functional response and feedback mechanism include real-time free hot lines b) Establish information desks at the distribution sites to receive timely complaints or compliments from the beneficiariesc) Prior sensitization of the target beneficiaries on how the feedback and response mechanism work.5) Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resourcesa) AAP committee member and local authorities including IDPs existing governance structures will be involved in the sensitization, registration and distribution process this will strengthened feedback mechanisms and ensure transparency.b) Prior sensitization on the targeting criteria, individual entitlements, distribution day and date will enhance transparency and accountability.c) Gender inclusive and Needs-based targeting regardless of displacement status will be prioritized.6) Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project areaa) In close coordination with the CCCM actors and particularly camp management agencies engaged in HLP and site development-SFH will ensure land tenure security guaranted by the local authorities for atleast 5 years prior to any shelter constructions.b) As a member of the CCCM and protection clusters SFH have been engaged in site planning and decongestation processes before, we will harness this capacity to ensure shelter standards are meet.
