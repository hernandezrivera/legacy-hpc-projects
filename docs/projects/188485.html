Project Summary

Following and further improving the results of the previous implementation in 2019, 2020, and 2021 (Phase 1-2 and3), the project phase 4 continued to aim at supporting municipalities and health facilities in North East Syria (NES) to guarantee a safe and sustainable cycle of waste management in previous targeted HCFs and new ones which are belong to Department of Health, as well as the project aims in this phase at strengthening capacities of local administrations and NGOs to safely manage health waste, in order to improve environmental health against war conflicts and pandemic-affected population in NES. Indeed, focusing on local capacity building is crucial in order to ensure the sustainability and long-term functionality of the system that has been set up in the previous years.
the main and prioritised aim , is the improvement of waste management at local and regional level in DoH HCFs, with particular attention to the safe and sustainable management of hazardous health care waste.

4 - Needs

The increase in COVID-19 cases deeply affected the services and humanitarian response in NES. along with the the lack of awareness of safe system of hazardous health waste management based on Local institutions who have also reported the death of a waste worker caused by infectious and hazardous waste managed in big hospitals, Covid centres and primary health cares without safety measures. the main need is ensuring that HWM system is fully functionality. taking into consideration, the risk and limited access to few locations in NES, the capacity of Health waste zones particularly with Covid-19 outbreak, where the amount of infectious HW produced has sharply increased, and in particular the soft waste, that includes the PPEs. The further needs of studies and researches on more ecological and long-term solutions for health waste management, especially for the treatment of expired pharma disposal, with the lack of experiences and expert people.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project supports in this phase, the continuously of improvement of a correct HW segregation system in public and private HCFs, as well as of correct systems for HW collection, transportation and disposal. This is done through the following activities :i) training and coaching HCFs staff about correct HW segregation; ii) provision of HWM equipment for the target HCFs;iii) maintenance and rehabilitation of the target Health Waste Zones;iv) regular monitoring of HWM system (quantities and segregation qualities); capacity building of the local authorities (DoH and DoLAE). The project, even if in the WASH sector, adopts in fact an early recovery lens and has as one of its priority the capacity building and involvement of local authorities in order to ensure stronger sustainability.v) expansion of the greening areas around HWZs, by planting new trees, in order to increase the positive environmental impact of the project activities and to reduce incinerator’s negative effects.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. health risks associated with unsafe disposal of health waste: o health risk for the workers manipulating with waste – Mitigation: UPP with the partner ensures the provision of PPE for the workers transporting the HW and operating the HWZ facility, and provide training and SOPs to the workers for safe waste handling and operation of HWZ. 2. health risk for the population living in proximity of open dumping sites, where the medical waste is disposed off if a HWZ is not available or the waste collection – disposal system is not well established. This poses a risk of injuries and infection, especially for extremely vulnerable groups of waste scavengers (often women and children). The mitigation is the core of the action - increasing the availability of safe HW disposal in various locations by establishing the system of safe HWM from the point of waste generation at HCFs to treatment/disposal of the HW, coordinating with health care providers, local authorities and other actors and by regular monitoring ensuring the sustainability of the mechanism. 3. health risk of environmental pollution by the disposal of the hazardous waste, partners mitigate the risk by conducting feasibility assessment with consultations of local stakeholders, considering the distance from populated areas, water sources and agricultural land as key criteria, together with adaptation of the designs to eliminate the risks that could affect the public health and livelihoods.
