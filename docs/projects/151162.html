Project Summary

Action against hunger provide food security and livelihoods support to Pastoralist, agro-pastoralist, peri-urban/urban and IDP populations affected by persistent periods of armed conflict, political instability as well as environmental and economic shocks in Somalia.The interventions areas target to Improved adaptive capacity of individuals,HHs,and communities through support to livelihoods diversification and improved access to markets,financial services and basic livelihoods services; Improve absorptive capacity of HHs, and communities through collective actions in support of effective disaster risk management,adoption of positive coping strategies and improved access to formal and informal safety nets;Inclusive market systems developments and establish transparent and accountable governance structures at community,districts and national levels to ensure eco-system health and promotion of equitable and sustainable natural resource management, especially rangelands and water.

Needs

Protracted conflict, impact of climate change and disruption of basic infrastructure has led to persistent humanitarian needs for a significant proportion of the people in Somalia. While above-average performance of the Gu rains has continued to improve food security situation, the needs remain critical with 4.6 million people, including 2.5 million children requiring assistance (FSNAU Technical release September 2018). In 2019 proposed ACF areas of interventions, which include Bakool, Banadir, Nugaal and Woqoyi Galbeed, a total of 391,000 people are projected to be in IPC 3 and 47, 000 in IPC 4 (FSNAU September 2018). Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and the host communities are most affected. In Nugaal, 68% of IDPs and 39% of the host community surveyed reported inadequate food security with 41% of IDPs and 52% of the host community indicating a poor FCS (REACH MCJA 2018). Similarly, in Woqoyi Galbeed, 50% of the IDP and 39% of the host community had inadequate food security with 53% of the IDP and 64% of the host community classified as having a poor FCS. An ACF integrated rapid Needs Assessment (August 2018, Xudur) district in Bakool region showed the main challenges contributing to food insecurity include unsustainable households’ food stocks, low and inconsistent income sources and fluctuating market prices of food and other essential commodities. The analysis shows majority of the households employed negative strategies with a low coping strategy index of 18.665. The majority (89.7%) had a borderline FCS (21.5- 35) and low HDDS (1.29) indicating severe vulnerability in the community. Moreover, acute food insecurity is prevalent among populations who lost most of their animals and who became destitute during the 2016-2017 severe drought. These population groups are expected to face large food consumption gaps and need humanitarian assistance to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed intervention is aligned to the Food Security and livelihoods HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building activities in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. The regions where the interventions will be implemented are characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to deforestation, land degradation, increasing aridity, overgrazing and water scarcity. By increasing access to water through rehabilitation of essential water sources and construction of new ones, the project reduces abnormal migration of human and livestock to other regions where they inflict significant environmental damages through over grazing and exerting added pressure on scarce water sources. In addition, the project will support communities to improve natural resource management to reduce land degradation and conflict over natural resources. This proposed project is foreseen to impose minimal risks to the surrounding environment. With the planned rehabilitation of key water infrastructure and establishment of effective community based natural resource management mechanisms, he project reduces negative pressure on the environment and significantly contributes the health and wellbeing of the habitat. The project will jointly work with line ministry representatives support the establishment and capacity building of Natural Resource Management Committees to ensure resilient community systems are in place. The immediate/emergency components of the project will be linked to ACF’s resilience program in the country and handovers to the capacity built community resource committees will ensure that the resources established are maintained and contribute to building resilient communities.The services and commodities planned for transfer to beneficiaries under this project adhere to government policies and have been designed jointly with community user groups. The transfer of the services/commodities will be witnessed by local authority representatives to ensure it fits within the existing legal framework at district level.More specifically, the project will: • Support formation and strengthen transparent and accountable governance structures at community, district and national levels such as village development committees, Water management committees, Natural resources management committee and link to local/district authorities – to ensure eco-system health and promotion of equitable and sustainable natural resource management of rangeland and water.• The projects will be implemented in close collaboration with relevant line ministries for technical support and establish good pathways for exit strategy.• Promotion inclusive markets and economic opportunities development through VSLA, sustainable animal health services delivery and Technical skill development.• Lobby/advocacy for Inclusivity – Women/youth/PLWD in governance structures of programs and targeted as vulnerable beneficiary.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main target population for the project are internally displaced people and the surrounding host communities. Displaced populations, youth, women and the elderly, most notable those in camps suffer from serious lack of productive assets to engage themselves in sustainable livelihoods, and therefore prone to economic exploitation and sexual exploitation. Moreover, access to basic services and opportunities is further compounded by the pervasive marginalization and exclusion of IDPs and certain sections of the host communities mostly attributed to clan dynamics. IDP and host community families who are members of weaker clans or minorities face extra vulnerabilities and protection threats including economic exploitation, trafficking and discrimination. To mitigate against the risks identified, ACF will ensure protection sensitive data collection and analysis that seeks to identify the specific protection challenges of the displaced population at project implementation stage. Targeting of project locations and beneficiaries will be guided strictly by need with specific attention to households that may be marginalized due to family and clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. ACF staff are already trained and sensitizes on PSEA and have signed a code of conduct that commits them to strictly adhere to humanitarian principles when providing services. There also exists a clear reporting framework for PSEA related issues. This is expected to curtail any cases of humanitarian staff exploiting the beneficiaries.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Main risks include: lack of involvement of women in resilience activities, and therefore activities selected only focus on the activities preferred by men; marginalization of women from key project activities, especially those seen as mainly male only activities and vice versa; older individuals, both men and women, may be discriminated because they are not labor active; child labour, especially where child headed or where parents are very old. Village relief committees will benefit from trainings will include an introduction on the project objectives and approaches to its implementation and their specific roles and responsibilities including criteria for vulnerabilities that inform selection of beneficiaries for inclusion in the project; sharpening their community mobilization and problem solving skills; and receiving, handling and relaying complaints. Selection of activities will involve both men and women and will be sensitive to the needs of the older members of the households. The project will also ensure that targeting criteria prohibits children from being working in project activities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

ACF works in areas where there is active conflict between state and non-state armed actors or between clans. The key risks include Service delivery being appropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary on their way to service delivery points and attacks on humanitarian staff among. ACF applies conflict sensitivity approaches in its project implementation and this will be mainstreamed in the project. All volunteers engaged in this action will be sensitize on the implication of this engagement in line with power dynamics at the household and community level. Deliberate action will be taken to discuss also the implication of this action on individual beneficiaries at the community level and if they are concerns, proper mitigation measures will be instituted. ACF local teams are well trained and experienced in handling sensitive community issues, especially if related to power dynamics and protection of all involved in the successful completion of this project. Additionally, beneficiary information will be protected and treated as confidential with consent being sort if any information such as pictures are to be shared. ACF will also be strictly guided by humanitarian principles, key among these principals being neutrality when providing services. Strict security protocols will be employed to ensure staff are not put in harm’s way, for example by ensuring that staff are back from the field stations early enough. Mobile outreach services will also target the remote but accessible areas, regardless of the community dynamics
