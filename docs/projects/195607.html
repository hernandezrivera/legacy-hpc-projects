Project Summary

Two years after formation of Revitalized Transitional Government of National Unity, humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire. Weak health system, recurrent inter-communal violence, devastating floods and global effect of COVID-19 continuously hamper timely access to equitable and affordable health care services. Such situation continues to increase vulnerability, hence high risk of disease outbreaks, low immunization coverage, poor hygiene and sanitation practices in crowding settlements, and therefore a likelihood of humanitarian catastrophe in 2022. In UNICEF`s humanitarian situation report No. 164, 1.4 million children faced life-threatening acute malnutrition, whilst approximately 7.2 million people were at risk of hunger largely driven by the impact of floods and displacement in 2022. Malnutrition rate extremely high with GAM rate of over 15%, of emergency threshold and Jonglei state reported the highest admission of SAM admission of 24%. According to health cluster`s response plan 2022, only 8% of the health facilities in the country provide full package of BPHNS and 53% moderately functional. Maternal mortality is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births. 39% women have access to recommended Four Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy, institutionalized delivery as well as skilled birth attendance low at 12.3% and 14.7% respectively. According to health cluster`s response plan 2022, Ayod is among the counties at health severity level5, with over 20K population at Catastrophic phase. For the past 15 years, CMD has been implementing multi-sectoral humanitarian interventions in Upper Nile Region specially Jongeli and Upper Nile states & GPAA. Capitalizing on its vast knowledge of local context and collaborative partnership with local authorities, community and other humanitarian actors, CMD aims to implement this project in Ayod, and Kapoeta North counties. The project will directly target 20,000 host communities & IDPs of which 9,600 will be males and 10,400 will be females including 4,000 boys and girls of under5 year old and 800 pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Among the target groups will be 1,440 male and 1,560 female Persons With Disabilities (PWD). The model of implementation shall be through thirteen static sites, five mobile clinics and the support to community-based case management using an estimated cost of $600K in 2022. Activities include essential life-saving health services, disease prevention, detection, surveillance & reporting, strengthening RI & NIDs, disease outbreaks & NTDs. For quality of care, BEmONCs/CEmONCs with institutionalized Skilled Birth Attendance 80% live births. 800 pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) receive the recommended FOUR ANC visits & 100% SGBV survivors who report to health facilities receive CMR and basic psychosocial support services. Deploy qualified health cadres to maintain and continuously improve quality of care to mothers of childbearing age, children & persons with disabilities. Construction and rehabilitation of health facilities. Beneficiaries` involvement and capacity building of CHDs. Inter-sectoral synergies with WASH, Protection/GBV, FSL and Nutrition. Use EWARS and weekly IDSR system of reporting to monitor trends of morbidities and outbreaks. Utilize HMIS and other internally improvised reporting tools to ensure data quality and consistency. Obtain beneficiaries` feedback through existing community networks; organize FGDs and installing suggestion boxes in health facilities and CMD offices sub-nationally.

1 - Needs

South Sudan plunged into civil war merely two years after its independence & though a collapsed Peace pact of 2015 again revitalized in 2018, protracted skirmishes and localized violence rage continuously. Coupled with climatic shocks that this country has never experienced in decades and COVID-19 pandemic, inter-communal violence cause frequent displacement, hence impeded access to the much-needed humanitarian services. HNO 2022, estimated that 8.9 million people expected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022, a 7% rise since 2021. South Sudan is one of the fewest in the world with the worse public health indicators. According to WHO, the country has only one physician for every 65,574 individuals, one of the highest under-five mortality rate of 90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births. COVID-19 vaccination rates remain among the lowest in the world at 1.4 per cent with 14,843 cases and 135 deaths as of December 2021. Because of high prevalence of diseases that affects up to 36 per cent of U5yrs children, poor quality of food/limited food diversity and the rise in food insecurity level, an estimated 2 million people including 1.3 million U5yrs children & 675,548 PLW expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. In 2021, IDPs in the country increased by 13% compared to 2020, and a likelihood of further increase in 2022. 2021 experienced low immunization rate with only 49% Penta3 coverage with Jonglei state recording the lowest in the country. Trend of Malaria cases remained high, AWD cases rose with 20 counties marked as hot spots for potential Cholera outbreak. 30% of girls impregnated before turning 18year with only 6% contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods and 5% for modern methods and over 835,000 people affected by floods. Localized violence, poverty, displacements and insufficient shelters expected to increase risk of more SGBV incidences in 2022. According to UNICEF data, 4.5 million children expected to be in need of humanitarian assistance & protection in 2022. IRNAs conducted in the counties in 2021 with participation of CMD reported continuous rise of displacements, lack of access to essential health care in the new settlement areas. For instance, IRNA conducted in Ayod county, May 2021, reported 5,230 HHs displaced by floods & settled along the shoreline of Canal river with no access to health care, hence an urgent establishment of mobile clinics and deployment of mobile health teams highly recommended. Furthermore, the country`s annual budget in 2021 only accommodated just below 2% of the health needs, amidst weak health system with no effective national to sub-national coordination mechanism except for the humanitarian actors as intermediaries. According to Health Cluster`s humanitarian response plan 2022, 5.47 M people are estimated to be in need of health care services with over 52% of them children, 1.01M IDPs and 750K returnees country wide. In response to highly anticipated deteriorating humanitarian situation in Ayod and Kapoeta North counties of Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria states respectively in 2022, this project will directly target 20,000 host communities and IDPs of which 9,600 will be male and 10,400 female including 4,000 boys and girls of under5 year old and 800 pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Among the target groups will be 1,440 male and 1,560 female Persons With Disabilities (PWD).

2 - Project activities

• Provide routine and national immunization campaign services against common preventable childhood illness through static and mobile outreaches in two counties to 1,920 boys and 2,080 girls of under5 year old.• Conduct timely disease surveillance and outbreaks detecting, reporting and response within 72hours of occurrence • Conduct OPD curative consultations for common morbidities including management of non-communicable diseases in 7PHCCs, 6PHCUs and 5mobile units to 1,920 U5 boys, 2,090 U5 girls, 7,680 A5 male and 8,320 A5 female.• Support laboratory capacity to undertake testing, counseling and referral for communicable and Neglected Tropical diseases including inpatient case management in the PHCCs• Provide Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services, referral and necessary counselling to the SGBV survivors in 8counties • Support Preventative measures against the spread of COVID-19 through vaccination, risk communication and community engagement and control by triaging, screening, case detection and isolation at the health facilities• Provide ANC, delivery by Skilled Birth Attendants and Post-natal services at the health facilities including BEmONC and CEmONC centers to 800 pregnant and lactating women.• Provide basic psychosocial support and mental health services at the health facilities • Provide OPD curative and other essential health care services to 1,440 male and 1,560 female persons with disabilities • Support WASH in health facilities through rehabilitation/construction of latrines and handwashing facilities and Waste Management Areas• Conduct MUAC screening and referral of malnourished children and Pregnant & Lactating Women to the nutrition centers • Support the installation of suggestion boxes for feedback mechanism in the health facilities and the organization`s offices• Enhance the capacity and knowledge of health workers and the County Department (CHD) officials through formal and in-services trainings • Procurement of drugs, medical supplies, laboratory reagents and other consumables • Support sub-national humanitarian coordination and reporting activities

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project staffs will organize individual and group meetings but to avoid discussing any gender sensitive issue in a group of mixed sex. Also, considering the age so that beneficiaries share their views without any fear of repercussion. Identity of those who provide feedbacks from individuals and/or different groups of beneficiaries be kept confidential and considering the patriarchal nature of the society in South Sudan women and girls will have separate avenues to avoid intimation from men.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project staffs will support the existing Health Committees per each health facility and ensure to establish committees where they do not exist. Use the minutes of their meetings to track how the beneficiaries perceive the progress of the project implementation. Use the Focus Group Discussion and individual conversations as part of getting feedback from the beneficiaries and suggestion boxes stationed in the health facilities and CMD offices so that the beneficiaries who are able to read write use this means to share their feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CMD has PSEA policies and operational guidelines to protect every beneficiary and hold any culprit liable of any attempt of sexual exploitation and abuse. The project staffs with support from the Senior management will organize orientation sessions to all employee in this regard before they engage with the beneficiaries. Organize awareness sessions for the beneficiaries, women leaders, community leaders, law enforcement agents and every stakeholder in the counties on what this project considers as sexual exploitation, channels of reporting and the available resources within the organization. Employees sign code of conduct and PSEA guidelines as part of their contractual obligation toward the organization and their beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Any feedback from the beneficiaries will instantly be analyzed and accordingly incorporated into the project implementation modalities during subsequent project review meetings with the stakeholders. In case the feedback doesn`t fit into the existing resources, then the organization will use it as part of resources mobilization strategy and the beneficiaries will also be informed on what they have reported and what the organization is doing to address them.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project staffs will work closely with local authority and community leaders and request surveillance and escorting of the beneficiaries in roads considered risky for few people especially women and girls. The PSEA focal persons will continuously engage the project staffs and other stakeholders at the sites of project implementation to ensure their uninterrupted access to services. Regular feedback will also be sought from women, girls and persons with disability to understand from them barriers and risk associated with access to health care services and corrective actions be taken accordingly. The organization will conduct self-assessment and evaluation about the policies, Standard Operation Procedures, guidelines and existing mechanisms before the launch of the project implementation. Once gaps are identified, the organization will allocate to resources to filling those gaps as part of the preparatory phase of the project implementation. The organization will work closely with other humanitarian actors in the counties who have expertise in other fields to ensure a coordinated humanitarian assistance. A coordination platform under the leadership of County Health Department and other relevant sectors in the counties will be supported so that partners come together and plan on how to tackle programmes impediments. The project will put in place mitigation measures that include organizing separate engagement approaches with vulnerable groups of the beneficiaries and directly pick up their views on how they would expect the project to address their felt needs. The project staffs will be tasked to continuously meet these vulnerable groups individually and/or in groups to understand what they think the project is achieving and what can be adjusted to fit their needs. Awareness sessions on how and where beneficiaries share their concerns will be organized during the inception period of the project implementation and periodic evaluations on the progress will be done so that any barrier is addressed and rectified. Employees will receive comprehensive orientation sessions before they begin their engagement with the beneficiaries and the management through HR department will convey uncompromised directives in that regard. Supervisors will keep checking their subordinates and any breach on the right and dignity of the beneficiaries will be dealt with impartially in accordance with existing rules of engagement. A periodic review of the project and community needs will be organized with the involvement of the direct beneficiaries to ensure timely correction of any mismatch. The organization will involve every stakeholder and sensitize people to follow due process in accordance with the existing laws governing the land. Advocacy and awareness shall conducted to encouraging peaceful resolution and minimizing harm on individuals, groups and the community. The organization will support advocacy and awareness on the protection against explosive hazard and contamination and ensure that relevant stakeholders receive the information timely to remove and/or reduce harm on individuals, groups or community.
