Project Summary

The project aims to improve the food security situation for the most vulnerable families in Aleppo and Idleb covernorate by providing live-saving humanitarian food assistance for 7000 families (42000individuals: 9009men, 9471women, 11540Boys, 11980girls) That will be achieved through two inventions:
1-Distribution of regular food assistance for 1000 vulnerable families (6000 individuals: 1287 men, 1353 women, 1649 Boys, 1711 girls) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates while each family will be supported with monthly food baskets for continuous 8 months. and that will be through 2 modalities:
a- cash modality through restricted e-vouchers (113 USD balance, that will cover 100% of the food needs for the families based on CWG recomindation) to address their food needs (500 families/3000 individuals : 644 men, 677 women, 825 Boys, 854 girls)
BINAA will prepare a vendor map and provide the selected vendors with devices and training on how to use these devices. in addition to conducting orientation sessions on using e-voucher cards for the beneficiaries.
b- in-kind modality (500 families/3000 individuals: 644 men, 677 women, 825 Boys, 854 girls) the e-voucher modality provides beneficiaries with the freedom to choose from a variety of healthy food items.
This food baskets will cover at a minimum 70% of the daily kcal intake which is around 1550 Kcal per person per day in a monthly family food ration. Calculation will be made on a reference family size of 5/6 for a period of 30 days.
2-Free bread distribution (The essential food of the Syrian families) for 6000 vulnerable families (36000 individuals: 7722men, 8118 women, 9891Boys, 10269 girls) on a daily basis for continues 6 months in Idleb and Aleppo governorates while each person will receive 330 g of bread per day based on the FSL cluster recommendations BINAA will contract with the closest bakeries to the targeted areas and supporting them by the sufficient flour and the operational cost in order to produce the needed amounts of bread, then distribute it to the beneficiaries on a daily basis. BINAA will take the COVID-19 precautions in all project phases starting from beneficiary selection till the bread distribution according to BNAA COVID-19 Guidance
We are targeting both males and females in this project including all ages. We will also take the elderly and the people with disabilities through the project implementation while we will deliver their portions to their houses
For Bread, the distribution will be blanket distribution in the targeted camps.
For the food baskets: the beneficiary's criteria will be as the following:
1- Disaster-affected households
2- Internally displaced (IDP), refugee household (HH), or recent returnee HH
3- HH with persons with disabilities (PWDs)
4- No income or lowest HH income per person in the village (total HH income/# of people living in the HH)
5- Female-headed households
6- Child-headed households
7- Families with multiple children under 5 years of age
8- Labor-poor HHs (HHs in which there is no one who can work)
9- No food stocks or HHs with recorded malnutrition
10- HHs not covered by other interventions (cash, food aid, etc.) from another agency
Those activities are not secured yet.

3 - Needs

The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population, the result shows a 7% increase in the PiN from last year. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of sliding into food insecurity, The alarming increase of people at risk of shifting to become food insecure during 2023, if the crisis continues at same worsening levels , is of major concern to the sector. BINAA conducted a rapid need assessment in 6 sub-districts (Aghtarin, Ghandurah, AlBab, Suran, Azzaz, and Jarablus). A semi-structured questionnaire with Key Informants was designed to identify needs, affected groups, and geographical areas and interventions requiring immediate assistance and develop an evidence-based strategy and implementation plan to improve the vulnerable people's situation. The rapid assessment results indicate an urgent need for Food security intervention in 18 camps to respond to the acute needs of 42,924 individuals (8524 HHs) including 8,256 men, 9,399 women, 13,279 boys, 11,990 girls. With increasing prices and the huge financial burden purchasing bread places on residents’ shoulders which most of them cannot afford.

4 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1- Supporting 1000 households (6000 individuals: 1287 men, 1353 women,1646 Boys,1711 girls) by food assistance through in-kind food baskets and cash for food using restricted E-vouchers for 8 months. 1.1Community engagement. 1.2 Conducting Market assessment to identify the prices. 1.3 Preparing the vendor map through a competitive and transparent vendors' selection process. 1.4 Contract with E-voucher service provider (cash) and food baskets provider (in-kind). 1.5 Identification of the targeted beneficiaries to be supported by food assistance. 1.6 Distribution of the vouchers cards to beneficiaries (cash) and the food baskets (in-kind). 1.7 Distribution of the POS devices to the selected vendors and conduct training on how to be used. 1.8 Supervising the voucher redemption process. 1.9 Vendors payment process. Activity 2- Supporting 6000 households (36000 individuals: 7722 men, 8118 women, 9891 Boys, 10269 girls) by free bread distribution for 6 months. 2.1 Community engagement. 2.2 beneficiaries' selection and verification. 2.3 Contracting flour providers. 2.4 contracting with bakeries and providing them with the flour and operational cost in order to prepare the needed amount of bread. 2.5 Distribution of the bread to the targeted families. Activity. 3- Conducting awareness campaigns against COVID-19 and Colera. Activity 4- M&E activities including monitoring the distribution process, and conducting baseline, end-line, and PDM visits.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

**The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points, or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm to some women and may cause domestic violence. - Likelihood: M. Impact: H. - Mitigation: -We will not distribute at a late time of the day. -We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute in each village separately. -During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. **The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers. -Likelihood: w. -Impact: H.- Mitigation: -Choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. -In case any exploitation happens, BINAA will take appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation where allegations of SEA/GBV are reported involving our personnel (suspending staff till the investigation is conducted, referrin the victim to PSS, and even witness protection program if needed) and investigate allegations in a timely and professional manner.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: The risks of SEA can be compounded during project implementation Likelihood: L. Impact: H. Mitigation:1-Ensure effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by integrating PSEA in BINAA policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed code of conduct, and that all contracts with partners/suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA or GBV.2-BINAA has a PSEA officer who is the PSEA focal point, and he will make appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing prevention strategies. This could include collecting and analyzing information on actual/potential risk factors for vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse and elaborating measures to address them. Make sure that PSEA materials and posters are visible and in place in all targeted locations. Receive complaints, reports, and questions about alleged acts of sexual exploitation and abuse committed by the organization’s personnel. And track all PSEA-related activities for the Organization and ensure that all information on PSEA-related activities is shared with relevant persons in the Organization


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas-Mitigation: -Regular monitoring for INSO security and incident reports. -Assess the access security records for each targeted location. -If it’s during the implementation, will look for other accessible locations with beneficiaries meeting the criteria. -BINAA has regular access to the proposed locations. Exploring different distribution modalities and plans for flexible deliveries in areas that are inaccessible2-Attacks may affect the vendor shops-Mitigation: -Implemented works will be reported daily by BINAA filed team -Temporary suspension in the targeted location and reassess the security situation. -Change the targeted communities/locations in coordination with clusters.3-Fraud in redeeming vouchers-Mitigation: -Using the E-voucher system will limit this risk, -Conduct daily monitoring through the dashboard and the control panel. -monitor the markets through the field team on a daily basis. -publishing the complaint posters and the complaint. -The MEAL team acts as a focal point.4-Bakery malfunctions:-Mitigation: The contract between BINA and the supplier will include a topic that the supplier will be committed to secure the daily planned amounts.5-COVID19 and Cholera-Mitigation: organizing the distribution to avoid gatherings and consider social distancing. Put awareness posters against COVID-19 and Cholera and conduct awareness sessions.
