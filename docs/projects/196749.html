Project Summary

In 2023, building on years of expertise and lessons learned COOPI aims to continue and expand its response to growing Food Insecurity and early recovery of the agricultural sector in Aleppo, Dar’a, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Raqqa and Rural Damascus.
The proposed project is a continuation of the project approved in HRP HSYR22-FSC-188341-1 and aims to respond to SO1 “To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance” and SO3 "To improve communities' resilience and capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods by improving linkages with value chain through the light rehabilitation of productive and economic infrastructure of the Food and Agricultural Sector".
Projects will target most vulnerable HH’s- especially women HHs, HHs with elderly, PwD, vulnerable returnees/IDP, families with high number of children, HHs with pregnant/lactating women, unaccompanied children in areas with high needs (Severity scale 3 and above).
Vulnerable IDPs, refugees and/or Host Community members who are moderately or severely Food Insecure and who are lacking the economic means or assets to fulfill their immediate food needs will be provided with CVA- that, in line with FAS sector recommendations, through a minimum of 8 distributions-covering 70% of the food SMEB price to meet the minimum the daily kcal intake per day per person. Value will be based on the recommendation of FAS for 2022/2023.
COOPI will provide agricultural kits for those with no stable income and lacking of reproductive assets, but with access to land and irrigation means The kits will be distributed in accordance with sector recommendations and will include tools for irrigation and combined with training on post-harvest loss reduction techniques, use of compost and fertilizer and improved farming.
Finally, infrastructure for agricultural means (irrigation canals, roads) will be rehabilitated through CFW targeting families that have abled members to work, but lack sustainable and long-term jobs.
The beneficiaries’ participation will be promoted throughout the project implementation, during the market assessment, trader surveys, focus groups discussion, monthly redemption activities and feedback mechanisms. In order to ensure high quality of the project implementation and beneficiaries’ feedback mechanisms, COOPI staff and volunteers will be trained on PSEA, MEAL tools and Vouchers Assistance (VA) processes.
Programmatic monitoring will be done through regular field visits, PDMs and focus group discussions. COOPI field staff and MEAL will attend all distributions and redemptions. Three rounds of PDM, including FCS and CSI surveys at each location will be conducted (baseline,mid-project and endline). Traders monitoring and price survey will be conducted before every distribution of CVA to ensure that prices are aligned with prices in community (compared to WFP price bulletin).
In order to facilitate the relationship with the several stakeholders and promote a synergic and multi-sector approach to challenges at all different levels, COOPI will ensure the coordination with local authorities, as well as Food Security Agriculture Sector (FSA), ERL Sector, Cash Working Group and international and national actors active in the same target areas. With the aim to adopt an integrated area-based strategy, COOPI FSL project will be implemented in areas where COOPI is implementing Protection and Education activities, while strong coordination will be ensured in order to refer and advocate for needs in other areas identified throughout the implementation. Through it's area-based approach COOPI will continue to collaborate with local authorities, as well as active coordination and participation with sectors at sub and national level, and will refer identified needs and gaps found during the implementation that cant be addressed within COOPIs implementation.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex protracted humanitarian emergency with rising needs. Rising inflation, chronic lack of employment, loss of agricultural production due to severe agro-climatic fluctuations and commodities prices increase, worsened with Ukrainian crises, put additional pressure on vulnerable hhs. Food insecurity has reached historically high numbers and, Syrians in need of food or agricultural support will represent 68% of total population. As a response, COOPI is targeting beneficiaries in R.Damascus,Dar’a,Homs,Hama,Aleppo,Idleb and Raqqa where COOPI is currently present and where the PIN is around 8.7million (HNO 2023)COOPIs assessment of June 2023 confirms that in most of the mentioned areas more than half of the people struggle to reach an acceptable FCS. Respondents reported spending on average between 59% and 69% of their income on food which makes them either marginally food secure or moderately food insecure. Root causes are lack of employment opportunities and owned livelihood assets, such as land, livestock, machinery etc., with many of the respondents reporting to have lost some of their sources of income over the previous 6 months.The proposed action aims at offering a response with an integrated approach of CVA, livelihood assistance and restoration of productive infrastructures- responding to both immediate Food Security needs through CVA and early recovery of the agricultural sector through provision of assets, IGA and rehabilitation of infrastsructure

4 - Implementation of activities

Project will be implemented in R.Damascus, Dar’a, Homs, Hama, Aleppo, Idleb andRaqqa. Activities will complement and build on COOPI ongoing interventions. Through the strengthening of agriculture inputs and activities COOPI will contributeto reduce the dependency on food assistance of the supported families. Families unable to be involved in the agro activities due to limited access to land or low capacities will be supported by CVA. Action will target moderately food insecure HHs (the supplementary food items will be included) for monthly life-sustaining assistance- to prevent further malnutrition among vulnerable hh's25000 people (5000 HHs) will receive CVA for 8 rounds to cover MEB gap as per FAS and CWG sector recommendation. Through agro kits distribution the project will grant longer term FS to 2500 HHs. 500hhs will benefit from IGA through, i.e. trainings and food processing kits. 500 HHs will receive livestock to support animal production. Through CfW, 4 infrastructure for agricultural production (roads, canals) will be rehabilitated. In total around 42.500 will be assisted during 2023. COOPI team will be trained on PSEA, market assessment, trader survey, distribution andredemption process, MEAL tools to ensure high quality of project implementation andmonitoring. Trainings on agro best practices will be organized for beneficiaries to enhancetheir knowledge and to maximize the food production. All activities are implemented in accordance with sector guidelines

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk(R): exclusion of some groups due to the choice of the location. Likelihood(L): medium. Impact(I): high. Mitigation Measures(MM): consult with communities in the choice of the location. R: tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. L: medium. I: high. MM: establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities; criteria are communicated in transparent manner with all actors.R: the population targeted is put at risk as a result of the facility location; lack of consideration for the accessibility to services (in terms of freedom of movement, safety and security for men, women, boys, girls, elderly, PwD). L: medium. I: high. MM: consider mobility patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing a static facility/mobile service provision or combine both; conduct safety audit to ensure safe access (route and location) by all the targeted groups; select staff for all activities with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile. R: different vulnerable groups might not have the same access to services and assistance. L: high. I: high. MM: make use of existing tools/mapping; coordinate at local level with other protection actors to ensure balanced coverage; avoid overlapping or concentration of activities if possible; ensure consultation with communities that may be at particular risk of exposure and risk of exclusion.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers during delivering assistance. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation measure: establish formal and informal feedback and complaint mechanisms in each area of intervention; provide staff with training on Code Conduct and PSEA; ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; establish mechanisms and procedures for ensuring compliance with PSEA zero-tolerance policy. Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA focal point have been established in COOPI country offices and headquarter. All staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitations and abuse.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R:FV fraud and identity theft, voucher sold or exchanged for unauthorized items. A: heightened visibility for beneficiaries and voucher spent on non-food items, causing a negative impact on food security outcomes. MM: vendors’ selection at community level with a good reputation. Random quality controls of suppliers. Include in the FV design unique serial numbers and laser tags to avoid copies. Crosscheck of voucher supplied with COOPIs copy. Random monitoring at HHs’ level the quantity and quality of the goods exchanged and utilized (as PDM questionnaires). R: direct access to the targeted areas and HHs is not allowed or has become unsafe for hum. workers. A: escalation of hostilities in the target area.MM: COOPI will communicate constantly with agencies, government and security monitoring networks in the country. All staff will receive trainings on security.R: selected beneficiaries cannot access their land. A: the presence of EOs may hamper access to agri. fields MM: COOPI will organize awareness raising sessions for all staff, volunteers and communities in EOs recognition, will coordinate with SARC and UNMAS on progress of EOs clearance. R: Staff/volunteers/beneficiaries are infected with Covid-19 during implementation. A: staff and volunteers are not properly protected.
