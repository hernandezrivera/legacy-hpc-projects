Project Summary

Médecins du Monde – France (MdM-F) is operating in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) for more than 20 years and working mainly in the fields of emergency preparedness and response, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), and Sexual and Reproductive Health. Gaza is suffering from protracted crises for more than 15 years now. The last attacks on Gaza in May 2021 and August 2022 also caused an aggravated negative impact on the already fragile health system and infrastructure, as well as on the well-being of the communities. In the West Bank (WB), the increase of occupation-related incidents, military operations, violent attacks, the regular closure of roads and the difficult access for some communities in Area C to healthcare services, adding to the context the reduction of funds for the humanitarian assistance in occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) leave people in WB with a need for access to health care, including mental health (MH), to overcome the impact of protracted crises on their wellbeing.
This proposed intervention is contributing to the health cluster objective 1, related to Strategic Objective 2: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in the oPt . MdM-F is focusing its intervention on strengthening the capacities of the health care system, providing structured and quality MHPSS services, and targeting the local community to raise their awareness about MHPSS and promote the use of available services. This proposed intervention aims at increasing access to quality MHPSS at the hospital, Primary Health Care (PHC), and at community levels.

At the hospital level, the intervention aims at scaling up MHPSS services through the establishment of a mental health unit and by capacitating the medical staff in 1 governmental hospital (Indonesian Hospital), 9 PHC Centers (PHCCs) in the North Gaza Governorate, and in 24 PHCCs in Jenin Governorate on the mhGAP Intervention Guide which was designed for the aim of scaling up MH Services at non-specialized healthcare facilities in low- and middle-income countries. This intervention is informed by the success of the MdM MH integration project in the Al Aqsa Hospital and 15 PHCCs in the Middle Area Governorate in Gaza and 24 PHCCs in Jenin Governorate as part of the Ministry of Health (MoH) strategy.
At the community level, MdM will work in Gaza with local partners to ensure the delivery of MHPSS services to the communities and empower them by increasing their knowledge, acceptance, and access to available MHPSS services at different healthcare levels in the targeted area.
The intervention of MdM-F will ensure the continuum of MHPSS services from community-based preventative MHPSS activities to specialized MH services through a functional referral system. Community members detected with severe signs of distress by the PHC and hospital staff will be referred to the MoH Community Mental Health Centers (CMHCs). Protection, gender, and inclusion of PwDs will be mainstreamed in MdM-F activities at the 3 mentioned levels.
MdM is working with both the MoH and Health cluster to coordinate its intervention and to ensure the effectiveness of its actions as well as to avoid duplication. This intervention complements the MoH national strategy as main provider of health services in Gaza and WB who is responsible for the supervision, regulation, and control of all health services. MdM is an active member of both the Health/Nutrition and Protection Clusters and its Areas of Responsibility (AoRs). Finally, MdM has involved the MoH in the designing phase of the project and integrated them all along the implementation of the project. MdM also coordinated this proposal with other relevant health actors.

1 - Needs

oPt lives in a protracted crisis characterized by decades of Israeli military occupation, violation of international humanitarian and human rights law, and recurrent escalations of hostilities. The protracted crisis has been devastating primarily to the Palestinians’ lives and dignity, and across the oPt, where too many Palestinians live in coercive socioeconomic conditions. Out of the 5.3 million Palestinians, 2.1 million in the oPt [64% in the Gaza Strip and 36% in the West Bank (WB)] are in humanitarian needs; most of them need protection and proper access to essential services, including health (HNO 2022, HNO matrix 2023) The crisis is believed to be contributing to the increasing and unmet MHPSS needs among Palestinian families and individuals in both in Gaza and WB.In Gaza, the socio-economic vulnerabilities are compounding and a catalyst to increasing protection risks, including persistent insecurity, physical and psychological violence, prolonged exposure to severe psychosocial distress, severe deprivation with limited to no access to critical and basic services.A recent KAP Survey Study (Knowledge, Attitude and Practice) conducted by MdM France and issued in June 2022 among community members in the Middle Area Governorate in Gaza showed that two-thirds of the participants (male and female participants over 16 years old) attributed the causes of MH problems to conflicts and wars, while most of them (80.5%) attributed the treatment of psychological problems to prayer and religious guidance. Seventy-six percent (76%) of individuals who suffer from psychological problems do not seek help, out of fear of sarcasm, ridicule, bullying, or discrimination. Findings also showed that the level of social stigma regarding MH was very high (73.6%). More than half of the respondents (53.5%) don't know how to seek MH services in their region, whereas 66.8% have inadequate information about MH services in the targeted area. Another KAP Survey conducted by MdM-F among PHC service providers in the Middle Area Governorate issued in October 2021 showed that knowledge about MH among PHC providers was limited. Attitudes toward MH, people with mental illness, and MH services were negative, especially among specialist doctors and General Physicians (GPs). Medical practitioners lacked skills regarding MH services (only 7% of GP`s expressed confidence in their MH skills) and lack tools for screening, diagnosis, and management of MH disorders at the PHC level. More than half of the PHC staff (represented by doctors and nurses) never received training on MH, Gender Based Violence (GBV) and People with Disabilities (PwDs). This is especially evident among doctors where 61% of them never received training in any of the three thematic (mental health, GBV and PWD).In addition, a recent a recent assessment conducted by MdM-F in June 2022 covering 16 level 4 PHCCs in the Gaza Strip showed that MHPSS services were among the least provided (3rd most affected service) at PHC level during the last military escalation on Gaza in May 2021. Moreover, the primary health care providers deemed that psychological distress was the 2nd potential barrier they faced to providing high-quality PHC services during emergencies. In the WB, the humanitarian impact of the Israeli settler violence is significant in terms of fostering a chronic environment of fear and intimidation, characterized by episodes of violence and harassment. This environment has a serious psychological impact on victims and witnesses of violent attacks.MdM is currently implementing a direct MHPSS emergency response program in the North WB. The types of impacts recorded by MdM during the current provision of MH responses to victims of settler violence (since 2012) include strong feelings of frustration (particularly for men), constant feelings of fear and insecurity, eating disorders, sleep disturbances (particularly for children), anxiety (particularly for women), depression and behavioural problems. These symptoms lead to aggressiveness, violence, social withdrawal, and academic deterioration and poor school performance for children.In the same assessment conducted in north WB, targeting 20 PHCCs and 5 Hospitals, only two PHCCs provide MHPSS services, and disaggregated data on the monthly consultations was almost unavailable. None of the PHCCs provides groups sessions, sensitization, or other group activities. Concerning MH training, none of the PHCCs had their staff trained to deal with GBV cases nor trained in Psychological First Aid (PFA). For community members, MHPSS services were the second most reported health need, next to improving the quality and capacity of the existing PHCCs. At the hospital level, only one hospital (Jenin hospital) had MHPSS services, including MH consultations and group sessions. One of the issues surrounding MHPSS services in those hospitals is a major gap in communication among the medical teams and the social workers. Additionally, psychotropic drugs, specialized care, and hospitalisation for severe cases were unavailable, and referral structures offering specialized services do not exist at the mentioned hospital. Moreover, MHPSS services are currently concentrated in 15 Community Mental Health Centres (CMHCs), which are usually located in city centres. The widespread violence, along with the lack of MHPSS services and the remoteness of available CMHCs, while considering the barriers that impede people from accessing health services, explains why MHPSS is among the highest needs cited by community members.

2 - Activities

Activities under this intervention are directly aligned with the MoH MH strategic priorities, aiming to increase the accessibility to MHPSS for all groups, as well as reinforcing the resilience of the communities considering gender needs and priorities. The logic of the intervention is outlined by the following: Outcome 1: Access of vulnerable populations to safe, protected, and confidential MHPSS services at community level is reinforced.• Output 1.1: Community awareness about MH symptoms and services availability has increased• Output 1.2: The capacity of local partner staff to provide safe, protected, and confidential MHPSS services is enhanced through ensuring tailored services and mainstreaming the principles of do no harm.Outcome 2: The availability, accessibility, and quality of MHPSS Services at healthcare facilities is strengthened through improving protocols and procedures as well as reinforcing the referral pathways.• Output 2.1: The capacity of Medical Staff to safely identify, manage and refer individuals with mental, neurological, and substance use (MNS) conditions, SEA prevention and reporting is enhanced• Output 2.2: People with severe MNS conditions are properly referred according to the National MH Referral System.• Output 2.3: The accessibility, safety, and privacy of MHPSS service users at healthcare facilities are strengthened through incorporating protection mainstreaming and conducting minor rehabilitation and adaptation for the needs of PwDs Main activities Awareness at the community level, MdM-F will strengthen the psychosocial well-being of the targeted communities while improving the access to MHPSS at different levels of non-specialized and specialized healthcare services. The community-based activities will include 1) awareness and psychoeducation sessions., The aim is to increase community awareness about MH topics and symptoms, sharing information about services available and how participants could ask for support. Including vulnerable groups such as GBV survivors, Child Protection (CP) cases, PwDs MH anti-stigma, myths, facts, misconceptions among community members, psychological well-being, and children's behavioural problems. After the awareness sessions, 2) individual consultations will be proposed for those in need through protection mainstreaming activities. Awareness sessions will be tailored to be culturally and contextually acceptable within a needs-responsive approach ensuring a do no harm principle. Moreover, the activities at community level will include the activation of the Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM), PSEA protocol, and protection mainstreaming.Integration of MHPSS at the PHC levelThe PHC staff will be capacitated on MH services based on mhGAP. The quality of the services will be reinforced through technical supervision all along the project. This approach shall address the gap in MHPSS between community level and specialized MH services. The management of identified service users will include a psychosocial management plan, MH drug prescription and follow-up, and MHPSS emergency response, with a special focus on the needs of vulnerable populations (PwDs, GBV survivors, and CP cases). Psychotropic drug supplies will be procured and delivered to MoH and then to the targeted PHCCs to complete the management cycle. The activities at this level will also build, promote, and maintain functional referral pathways between the PHCCs and the other service levels including community actors, secondary healthcare level (hospital) and specialized MH services (Community Mental Health Centres (CMHCs) and psychiatric hospital. In addition to the mhGAP training, the medical staff will receive training, Problem Management Plus, and PFA. The intervention will ensure that the capacitation curriculum is based on valid (Health Cluster, MoH) SOPs and protocols to guarantee the ownership and the sustainability of the activities. Integration of MHPSS services at the hospital level: MdM-F will increase the capacities of the medical staff (doctors and nurses) at the hospital level to deal with common mental, neurological, and substance use (MNS) disorders based on the mhGAP. The quality and sustainability of MHPSS services at hospital level will also be ensured through the establishment of a permanent MH Unit inside the hospital, to run the daily MHPSS activities. The MHPSS activities at the hospital level will include: 1) Identification of people needing MHPSS services. MoH Emergency Department (ED) staff and MH Unit team (specialized MH Team from MoH composed of a Psychologist) will ensure that service users and caregivers arriving at the ED with possible MH needs are early identified in the triage area and screened on timely manner. 2) Provision of MHPSS emergency response. The teams will be capacitated to provide PFA for shocked and distressed service users arriving at the emergency department. 3) Management of acute and mild MH conditions. Based on the screening results the teams will proceed with an assessment in a safe and private space. The management decision will be taken in accordance with the medical condition of the service user. In case of admission, the service user’s MHPSS needs will be followed during the hospital stay and involving the caregivers based on psychosocial interventions and psychotropic medications if needed. If the case is directly discharge from the ED, then a proper referral decision should be made to ensure the identified needs have been adequately addressed and an appropriate technical decision has been made. 4) Deliver bad News: MdM-F will reinforce the skills of the hospital teams to communicate bad news in a safe and proper way that does not expose the users to further harm.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The majority of the proposed activities will be related to service delivery while a small percentage will be in-kind (rehabilitation, provision of equipment, supplies, drugs etc). The MdM team will directly support the MoH and local partners in improving the provision of MH PSS services in Health Facilities (HFs) and will reinforce the local community coping mechanisms. Based on the organization’s experience in the field, capacity building of the MoH and MdM’s partners on MHPSS services delivery has been proven to be the most effective modality to ensure a quality provision of MH services and referral, taking into consideration the different needs of vulnerable populations (PwDs, GBV survivors etc.) as well as the needs of MdM’s partners in terms of drugs, material and furniture. Moreover, awareness sessions at community level are the most effective way to reach the community and break the stigma around MHPSS services and improve the access to such services. Through the mentioned modality of assistance (capacity building process), the impact and sustainability of services are ensured, and better MH intervention is provided.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MdM accountability system is based on sharing information in a timely manner with the actors involved in the project as well as regular reporting to the MoH and Health Cluster. The field presence of MdM teams makes this flow of information continuous and bidirectional, establishing a system that allows the participation of the health staff at MoH and MdM’s partners level, as well as targeted community members in the definition and adaptation of MdM activities. Regular communication is kept with the International Cooperation Department (ICD), PHC, MH and Hospital Directorates of the MoH to ensure comprehensive information sharing, participation, and feedback. MdM will work closely with MoH and Al Ataa to establish and/or strengthen their existing complaint and feedback mechanism. When feedback or complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place (complaint box located in the clinics, phone calls etc.), a reply will be provided to the user with a reference number to follow-up the process. The feedback/complaint will be processed first by the focal point who will categorize it into 2 main categories: regular or sensitive feedback/complaint. Regular complaints are non-sensitive complaints related to programmatic issues. A sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse/inappropriate behavior with beneficiaries involving verbal, sexual abuse (PSEA), physical assault, or discrimination. Based on the details of the complaint, MdM will treat the feedback internally or with the partner. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. Users who provide feedback will be updated about the process through email or phone, and final decisions and actions will be communicated formally. All complaints will be registered anonymously by the focal point in a protected database and analyzed regularly. Feedback will be used to improve service delivery, re-orient approaches .

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MdM has functional Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) available for beneficiaries, partners, and suppliers through FCM cards which highlight that positive or negative feedback will all be taken into consideration and addressed independently from project team, respecting the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. The cards, written in Arabic language, include a unique hotline and WhatsApp (accessible for persons with physical disabilities) number to receive the feedback calls or text messages, the working days and hours to receive the feedback, and a unique email address to receive electronic feedback. In-person complaints are also available for people who do not have access to electronic means. All of these modalities are in place to allow safe access to the FCM by different groups regardless of sex, age, disability status, literacy, etc. MdM teams distribute and explain the FCM cards to the beneficiaries during their presence in the field while MdM logistics department also distributes and explains the card to all suppliers involved in the project. A satisfaction survey will also be conducted to collect feedback from the beneficiaries of services (trainings, awareness sessions, HF rehabilitation, etc) to identify the degree of satisfaction and elicit suggestions for improvement by beneficiaries. The FCM and surveys will be key elements in the project to take the necessary actions in enhancing the service quality and accessibility via timely analysis of feedback from the target groups who have utilized MdM services. At local partner level, as a best practice, MdM will ensure to dedicate a phone number for feedback, complaints, and suggestions to the service users receiving the MHPSS support. Information coming through this number will be discussed amongst a committee composed of MdM and the partner, and they will work accordingly to a TOR that will be developed.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The form of humanitarian assistance that MdM is providing ensures that it does not further expose people to physical hazards, violence, or abuse. One of the components of the present proposal aims to increase access to quality services taking into consideration age, gender, and safety for healthcare providers and beneficiaries. Within its intervention, MdM, from a human rights perspective, is combating the stigmatization of MH through an awareness raising campaign at community level within the health premises. MdM is following specific rules for the data collection, gathering, and publishing based on MdM Guidelines, “Ethics in the Field”.Moreover, the proposed project also aims to improve accessibility to services in PHCCs through the rehabilitation of HFs for PWDs, such as installation of ramps and handrails, as well as promoting dignified access (awareness sessions to fight stigma and promote normalization to access MHPSS and GBV services). HF’s assessments were also recently conducted where the privacy, confidentiality, and safety of PHCC’s users were evaluated. Some PHCCs were found not having gender-separated toilets, not having locks on the door, and not having enough lighting in the surroundings. To ensure safety and protection of PHCC’s users, these elements will be addressed during the PHCC’s rehabilitation. Trainings will also be conducted among target groups (PHCC’s staff and Hospital staff) on inclusive service delivery without discrimination and the principle of Do No Harm approach, ensuring that both genders are represented among training participants. While delivering MHPSS services to the beneficiaries, privacy and confidentiality during consultations will be strived for by ensuring minimum requirements such as rooms with doors that can be closed and high walls to ensure visual and auditory privacy. The health staff delivering MHPSS services will also be gender-balanced, ensuring that beneficiaries feel comfortable talking to the person of their choice, removing the possible barrier existing to access MHPSS services for both genders. As a rule, all MdM teams signed the MdM confidentiality charter. MdM is also keeping safe storage of the beneficiaries’ data and information by using coding system and only allowing access to relevant people from the project team. When providing feedback to communities and stakeholders, maintaining confidentiality is critical for MdM and its team. The interest of beneficiaries is always protected, and individual names are not revealed in public or group meetings. Human Resources involved in the project, including that of the partner, will be trained, supervised, and gender-balanced and it will be ensured that they are updated about the cultural and religious contexts. The project will also ensure that the communities are aware of the protection and complaints mechanisms in place. MdM interventions at the community level are provided by experienced teams through outreach programs which reduce the exposure of communities to harm and ensure equal access, including easier access for persons with disabilities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender, and inclusion are adopted and put into practice by MdM within its intervention in oPt. During MdM’s existing interventions, the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff and partners on GBV, CP, disability were reinforced to promote inclusive access for vulnerable populations, as well as gender and PSEA for the health staffs to deliver adequate and safe MHPSS services to the beneficiaries. Refresher training on these topics will be done regularly. MdM is addressing the needs of the local community based on gender mainstreaming approach (gender-specific health), for instance, developing different IECs for women, men, and children, developing different protocols based on gendered various manifestations, and addressing the barriers preventing vulnerable groups such as GBV survivors, children at risk of protection and PwDs to access services. MdM reaches vulnerable communities through its programs especially those with mobility problems through HF’s rehabilitation, improving access for PwDs (infrastructure and service provision). MdM MEAL processes take into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities including that of PwDs. PWDs are taken into consideration in all of MdM’s monitoring, evaluation and reporting processes The established gender policy of MdM guides gender mainstreaming in its intervention. Gender analysis is integrated into all the phases of project management based on MdM gender and PSEA policies. For MdM, gender equality is one of the primary conditions for social sustainability in development and emergency interventions. For MHPSS support, the specific needs per gender will be taken into consideration with the provision of services, with an equal number of trained women and men delivering MH support. The age and gender of children also will be taken into consideration while providing the service, paying particular attention to children at risk of protection issues.Through MdM recent assessments which was done in consultation with community leaders and different gender and age groups (2022), the needs of vulnerable groups, such as women, elderly, children, and PwDs have been identified and interventions within the proposed action are tailored to target the specific needs: having a gender-balanced team, trained staff on inclusive service delivery, to ensure access of PwDs, privacy and confidentiality for MHPSS services delivery
