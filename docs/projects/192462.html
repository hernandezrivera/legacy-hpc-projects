Project Summary

Since its establishment SIDC has been working with the most vulnerable populations that include, but are not limited to, individuals from the LGBTIQ+ population, sex workers (SW), people who use and inject drugs (PWUD, PWID), people living with HIV (PLHIV), youth engaging in risky behaviours, and the families of all the previously mentioned individuals.Reaching out to these individuals is focused through reproductive, sexual,and mental health service provision,harm reduction services,and case management. Moreover, SIDC advocates for the rights of these individuals and continues to deliver capacity building activities to police officers,religious leaders,media professionals,employees from private sector companies, employees from NGOs, frontliners, social workers, syndicates, universities, schools, and many others.
Throughout the past years, Lebanon has been experiencing a series of compounded crises beginning with the uprising of 2019, leading up to the Beirut Port explosion on August 4th, continuing with the hyper price inflation, the pandemic, and the devaluation of the Lebanese Pound.These crises have exposed the structural gaps and fundamental inequalities that marginalized communities are subjected to, hindering their access to basic needs, protection, social, and economic development.Vulnerable populations are at an increased risk of unemployment,unstable housing, violence, worsened mental health, and food insecurity. The worsened conditions have also been evident in assessments that have been collected from beneficiaries,PLHIV and DUs,after the Beirut Port Explosion and COVID-19 by SIDC. From the 161 responses, 60% had indicated their lack of access to a source of income, 28% had partial access to income, and only 12% had access to income. Additionally, from the beneficiaries assessed, 18% had asked for mental health support, 10.5% had asked for psycho-social support,25.5% had required food boxes, 22% had needed support with rent, 17% required medical support, and 6% required COVID-19 related awareness.
Therefore, as a result of the ongoing crises, increased violations experienced by individuals from marginalized communities, results from assessments and outreach feedback, SIDC aims to focus on the provision of:1.mental health services for individuals from marginalized communites, 2.in kind transportation to individuals from marginalized communities, particularly, PWUD, PWID, and PLHIV, to increase their access to receive health services, check ups, assessment, and most importantly, their medication, and 3. a planned increase in community outreach activities to raise the awareness of individuals from marginalized communities on the topics of sexual health (SH), mental health (MH), reproductive health (RH), harm reduction (HR), gender based violence (GBV), substance use (SU), and referral to services within SIDC centers as deemed necessary. The MH service provision will be conducted virtually following SIDC’s tele-mental health guidelines and the community outreach will be provided through SIDC’s mobile unit.
By collaboratively working together and including beneficiaries from marginalized communities, the whole project process would ensure that any and all risks that may expose individuals from these communities will be identified and mitigated against.A range of communication modalities will be made available to targeted communities to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible,taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture,as well as project resources and beneficiary vulnerabilities and preferences.For this reason, a strong M&E system, continuous evaluations, communication with key stakeholders and beneficiaries, will allow SIDC to monitor the impact of aid within the existing context.Finally, all service provision within SIDC is done to ensure the inclusivity of all individuals & they are tailored to fit all.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

SIDC will be providing 200 individuals who have been made vulnerable with emotional support through SIDC’s telemental health support services.For the remaining 6 months, SIDC’s planned telemental health support services will cover 400 sessions per month, totalling 2,400.The support provided within this activity is particularly targeted towards individuals who are from the marginalized communities, that include, but are not limited to, individuals using drugs, injecting drugs, PLHIV, and their families, youth engaging in risky behaviours, PWD, individuals from the LGBTIQ+ community, and sex workers.Moreover, 435 beneficiaries will be supported with in-kind transportation to allow for increased access to services. The primary challenge identified by SIDC through service provisions has been transportation. To overcome this barrier,SIDC has been including transportation costs within its projects to increase beneficiaries’ access to services.For the upcoming 6 months,in-kind transportation will be planned for 200 beneficiaries,who use and inject drugs and arrive at SIDC's center for treatment and follow-up. Additionally, 235 PLHIV will be provided with in-kind transportation costs to arrive at the center to receive their medication and conduct regular testing.Moreover, SIDC will engage 4 additional peer educators on the field from LGBTIQ+ community, ex-drug users, and PLHIV to conduct one one one awareness. These individuals will conduct 2 field outreach sessions per week.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protection risks that may arise involve exposing beneficiaries to stigma and discrimination when arriving to financial service providers to receive cash support.Moreover,there exists protection risks to beneficiaries from members of the law enforcement due to the criminalizing laws within Lebanon.To mitigate these risks,SIDC reaches out to all FSPs, to raise awareness on the specificity of the populations involved in the activities. Outreach workers have organizational cards with SIDC and MOPH logos for their safety.A hotline will be available to receive any immediate protection concerns they face from law enforcement, and inform beneficiaries that they may identify SIDC as a reference in the case they were exposed to any risk. Affected populations include,drugs users,injectors,sex workers, ex prisoners,PLHIV,& individuals from the LGBTIQ+ community are exposed to higher protection risks.For COVID19,prevention messages disseminated,orientation to vaccine centers and applications.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

SIDC is committed to maintain and establish a system that allows beneficiaries (& others) the possibility to leave comments, feedback,complaints,&suggestions on our work in a secure,appropriate& accessible way,& allow complaints to receive a timely response.F&C mechanisms include regular service evaluations,complaint box within SIDC,and direct access to management for expressing any complaints needed. At the right up stage of the project, beneficiaries are involved through expressing their needs whilst documenting their violations,case management,&contributing to service evaluations.Community involvement takes place as community members will be acting as key informants to “hotspots” where the outreach activity can take place.Moreover, focus group discussions and satisfaction surveys are conducted, based on which activity within this appeal have been indicated.
