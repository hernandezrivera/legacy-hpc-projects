Project Summary

The proposed project will support the ongoing WASH sector activities in Borno state by providing a comprehensive WASH approach to be implemented in the hotspot locations of Gwoza LGA in Pulka Ward specifically in (camp A, camp B, camp C, camp D, and camp E); Konguga LGA in Njimtilo Ward (Ngumari community); Kunduga ALG in Dalori Ward (Alau Ngururi, Dalori 1 IDP camp, Dalori 2 IDP camp, Konduga boarding school camp and Kofa community), Jere LGA in Dusman Ward in (Sabon Bolori community), Jere LGA in Gongulon Ward in (Yerimari community).
MI will provide WASH life-saving services through improving access to safe and sufficient drinking water supply, safe, adequate and dignified sanitation facilities, hygiene supplies and information to mitigate risks related to public health with mainstreaming of protection and gender for vulnerable IDPs, returning refugees and host communities that meet the differing needs of men, women, boys and girls. Activities will include: Cholera preparedness and response, construction of water and sanitation infrastructure to newly arrived IDPs, operation and maintenance of existing WASH infrastructure including water supply systems, latrines and bathing facilities, solid waste management and distribution of hygiene kits, which are in line with the WASH sector strategy.
The exact beneficiaries in this response will include IDPs, host communities and returnees, and most vulnerable segments from the communities such as female-headed households, elderly persons, and persons with disabilities who will be identified and prioritized in collaboration with authorities and community leaders.

1 - Needs

Borno state in Nigeria continues to be amongst those areas with the highest levels of vulnerability in Nigeria. In the absence of durable solutions, the situation of IDPs depends heavily on continued humanitarian assistance. Permanent returns of IDPs to their areas of origin are hindered by the prevailing insecurity situation. In September 2019, Malteser International (MI) conducted a KAP survey in Gwoza LGA in Pulka Ward (camp A, camp B, camp C, camp D, and camp E), and hosting community in Bokko 2. MI also participated in a multi-sectoral needs’ assessment led by REACH in July 2019, which was conducted in KIVA1, KIVA2, Bulama-Manyatada, and Bulabulin. MI also in late October 2019 conducted a Real Time Evaluation for its WASH operation in MMC and Pulka. Additionally, in other locations where MI operates, MI has also observed from ongoing operation, there are urgent WASH needs need attention.WASH needs analysis results: In Pulka, specifically in (camp A, camp B, camp C, camp D, and camp E), results show beneficiaries have access to water supply as much as 13l/p/d which is below the sphere standards of 15l/p/d, though not representative for the entire Pulka. WASH needs remain overwhelming inside IDP camps, and host communities where most affected people live. Access to water supply In Pulka - specifically in (camp A, camp B, camp C, camp D, and camp E), from the assessment results show that, beneficiaries have access to water supply as much as 13l/p/d which is below the sphere standards of 15l/p/d. Many people living outside of camps rely on water supply schemes which were constructed by INGOs, but the quantity of water is insufficient either due to the limited number of water schemes installed or un-functionality of the existing schemes. They IDPs and host communities face frequent shortages of water due to intermittent provision of already stretched water facilities or an inability to afford services in a consistent manner. This is exacerbated by increased number of IDPs in camps or host communities due to the protracted crisis, with female headed households (FHH) particularly affected. Families cope by reducing water consumption, which increases risks to their health. Access to hygiene and sanitation services in Pulka and other locations where MI operates remain dire. In Pulka from the KAP survey results show; only 17% wash their hands during three critical times, and only 28% of the total respondents wash their hands with soap due to unavailability of soap. There is a high need for additional new latrines-especially for the newly arrived IDPs, since the existing latrines are over-stretched. Many existing latrines are in urgent need for rehabilitation and Operation and Maintenance (O&M), since many were vandalized by the harsh weather during the rainy season. Under this action Malteser is proposing to construct full water supply solar-motorized schemes in Pulka for newly arrived IDPs to improve access to safe and sufficient water supply. In other operational areas including: Konguga LGA in Njimtilo Ward (Ngumari community); Kunduga ALG in Dalori Ward (Alau Ngururi, Dalori 1 IDP camp, Dalori 2 IDP camp, Konduga boarding school camp and Kofa community), Jere LGA in Dusman Ward in (Sabon Bolori community), Jere LGA in Gongulon Ward in (Yerimari community), MI is proposing under this action to rehabilitate hand pumps, upgrade existing hand pumps to a full water supply solar-motorized systems and build capacities of community members to operate and maintain their water facilities. MI will also rehabilitate existing sanitation facilities, construct new latrines for newly arrived IDPs, construct bathing facilities, support community led waste management, provide hygiene education and supplies to improve the living conditions for the affected population.

2 - Activities

Water supply activities:1- Construction/Upgrading of water supply systems; including drilling of boreholes, installation of hybrid motorized pumps, installation of water tanks and solar panels, construction of tap-stands and laundry aprons.2- Operation and Maintenance of existing water supply systems through provision of Spare parts and Tool Kits and capacity building trainings for communities to improve access to safe water supply3- Conduct periodic/monthly Water Quality Testing through provision of Water Quality Testing Kit (H2S strips and Pool Testers) to test Free Residual Chlorine and Coliforms bacteria during to ensure water quality. Sanitation activities:1-Construct gender segregated latrines/bathing facilities to eliminate open defecation and reduce public health risks. 2-Rehabilitate gender segregated latrines/bathing facilities to eliminate open defecation and reduce public health risks. 3-Support Operation and Maintenance (O&M) of gender-segregated latrines through provision of latrines cleaning kits to WASH Committees to ensure latrines cleanness.4-Support Drainage Control and Solid Waste Management through provision of Sanitation tool kits to WASH committees.5- Support Dislodging of latrines in IDP camps in Pulka.Hygiene Promotion:1- Conduct new/refresher hygiene promotion trainings and sessions to new/existing Hygiene Promoters, and provide Information Education and Communication (IEC) to aid Hygiene Promoters to facilitate dissemination of hygiene messages. 2- Distribution of Emergency Hygiene Kits which include soap, jerry-cans, sanitary pads for new arrival.3-Distribution of Hygiene Replenishment Kits (sanitary pads, soap) to ensure improved personal hygiene.4-Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to monitor quality, quantity and use of hygiene items distributed in hygiene and replenishment kits.Cholera Preparedness and Response1- Deployment of Cholera response team and Provision of Cholera outbreak kits2-Conduct General hygiene awareness to contain cholera out break

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Malteser International (MI) feedback arrangements activities shape MIs ongoing response improvement to the emergency in Borno state in the IDP camps and host communities in which it operates. Data from feedback mechanism is is collected and analysed on a regular basis, allowing Malteser to verify progress and improve performance in accordance with MI and donor guidelines and international standards.During project implementation, MI ensures community participation in the implementation and monitoring of activities through community feedback/complaints which will be used to improve response strategy. To ensure Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), MI establishes anonymous complaint and feedback mechanisms to capture community complaints and feedbacks to ensure community involvement and to improve project implementation. To do so, Malteser MI will install complaint boxes in its areas of operation in rural communities hosting IDPs and coordinate with Camp Management to gather complaints from already installed boxes. MI also will conduct periodic meetings with community leaders, women groups, water user committees and any other existing community structure to have their views on project implementation and community involvements.Information from these feedback mechanism boxes will be collected on monthly basis and analyzed with participation of community members to improve project implementation. All recommendations from complaints will be implemented with full community involvement to ensure community participation.
