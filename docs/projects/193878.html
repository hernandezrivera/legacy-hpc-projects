Project Summary

Through this emergency project, and in compliance with the HRP strategic objective 2 and the WASH Cluster objectives 2, ACTED aims to ensure that vulnerable communities and households in the West Bank have improved access to equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed WASH services – in line with the HRP expected outcome. ACTED and ARIJ (Applied Research Institute – Jerusalem) propose to cover outstanding WASH needs in the identified 19 most vulnerable communities in the Jerusalem, Hebron, and Nablus governorates, in complementarity with other ongoing and planned WASH interventions. Activities will include:
- Water network (1) rehabilitation and construction of a reservoir in Beit Hasan
- Provision of 2 water sources facilities (Beit Hasan, Rafat)
- Rehabilitation and provision of key materials to ensure the maintenance of WASH facilities in 8 schools and 5 Health Care Facilities (HCF) in the Jerusalem and Hebron governorates (Beit Iksa, Deir al ‘Asal al Fauqa, Halhul, Hebron Old City, Umm ad Daraj, Deir Samit, Umm Qussa, Yatta)
- Support 82 of the most vulnerable households with the rehabilitation of their WASH facilities at the household level through cash assistance in the Jerusalem governorate (Abu George Road Bedouins – Kassara and Nkheila; An Nabi Samwil Bedouins; Az Za’ayyem Bedouins; Ma’azi Jaba’; South Bir Nabala Bedouins, Al Khalayleh Bedouins, Al Baqa’a)
- Strengthening of 11 CBOs in target areas, to ensure protection mainstreaming within communications and accountability to affected populations
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, PWDs and older people. Gender and age principles will be mainstreamed throughout the entire action, adapting each step of the implementation to the needs of specific categories. CBOs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV with a particular focus given to women and children. During the need assessment, special focus will be provided to assess needs of older people, women and PwD, in order to plan a tailored shelter rehabilitation response plan. To this aim, ACTED will ensure that those categories have the possibility to express their needs during the assessment as well as ensuring that during the planning and rehabilitation phases a protected and adapted place and time where the field team can reach them. More specifically, as PwD are sometimes deliberately prevented from expressing their needs, the project team will conduct household-level visits to assess their specific needs and explain the purpose of the project. PwDs and their caregivers will be consulted throughout the project to better understand their safety and dignity concerns. AAP will be core to the proposed Action by giving account to, taking account of and being held to account by affected populations.
Across all activities, beneficiaries will be made aware of both organisations’ complaint response mechanisms (CRM) and PSEA awareness leaflets will be distributed. At last, any outstanding needs detected all along the different phases of the project, especially protection-related ones (PSEA, MHPSS, GBV) will be referred to sectoral partners.
In total 20,990 beneficiaries across the three targeted governorates will benefit from this proposed action.

1 - Needs

The 2022 WASH HNO had outlined that in Area C the restrictive planning system enforced by the Israeli authorities prevents development, including even routine repairs and maintenance of water structures, leaving the West Bank with inadequate WASH infrastructure (wells, cisterns, pipelines, etc.). The Water Sector Regulatory Council had also noted that 620,000 Palestinians in the West Bank reside in areas (primarily in Area C) that are not connected to piped water services or are poorly supplied; consequently, they consume less than 50 litres of water per capita per day, well below the 100 litres recommended by WHO. Moreover, more than 100 communities in Area C still relied on rainwater harvesting cisterns and trucked water to satisfy their water needs, leading to high water prices that could reach more than 30 ILS/m3, as well as water quality issues. During the 2022 mid-Year Humanitarian Response review, OCHA stressed that Israeli restrictions in Area C prevent WASH partners from reaching the most vulnerable populations. OCHA also highlighted that improving their access to water and sanitation services should be prioritized and that more than 100 communities in Area C are still not connected to piped water services. Furthermore, in its 2021 Contingency Plan, the oPt WASH cluster had identified that WASH facilities in schools and clinics were particularly at risk due to the significant increase in the coercive environment. The proposed intervention was designed based on the abovementioned observations, the findings of the Multi-Sectoral Need Assessments (MSNA) in 2022, ARIJ’s assessment on WASH conditions in Area C under its EU-funded project implemented from 2019 to 2022, requests for assistance under ACTED’s on-going and recently-ended projects, PWA conducted assessments, and consultations with the WASH cluster. It must in fact be noted that for this year, 86,357 individuals in Area C are considered to be in severe, extreme, or catastrophic need for assistance in the WASH sector, with notably 24,436 persons in extreme or catastrophic need for assistance. As such, through the MSNA 2022, nearly all households in the West Bank (96.3%) reported employing some kind of coping mechanism in order to adapt to a lack of sufficient water for drinking or domestic purposes, with 66.7% of these households reporting having reduced water consumption and 36.3% reporting increased spending on water by diverting household expenditure normally intended for other purposes. Furthermore, the strata with the lowest reported rates of access to a sufficient quantity of water for drinking and domestic purposes in H2 (Hebron) (81.2%), Nablus Area C (82.3%), and Hebron Area C (83.6%) (MSNA, 2022). ACTED and ARIJ will also inform their beneficiary selection based on request for assistance under recently closed projects, including one with joint partnership between ACTED and ARIJ. Under these projects as well as its on-going project under the West Bank Protection Consortium, ACTED received requests for WASH assistance through its CRM from key communities which will be targeted under this proposed action, notably in the Jerusalem governorate (An Nabi Samwil Bedouins, Bir Nabala, Az Za'ayyem Bedouins, Jaba’) and in the Hebron governorate (Umm Qussa, Hebron Old City). Regarding the selection of the community which will benefit from the water network rehabilitation and the reservoir construction, ACTED and ARIJ consulted with the PWA to identify the community most in need of these works. As such Beit Hasan was selected since the network suffers major water losses (more than 40%) based on PWA assessments. The community is currently in need of water network and water source rehabilitation, expansion of the water network to the neighbouring communities, as well as the construction of a reservoir. Finally, this action was designed keeping in mind that women and girls are disproportionally impacted by water quantity and quality limitations affecting substandard sanitation facilities, for their own health and as well as in terms of bearing the burden of household duties for all the family. Therefore, through the improvement of inadequate WASH facilities both at community and HH level, the proposed action aims to contribute to gender equality in the targeted communities.

2 - Activities

A1. Based on recommendations from the PWA and the WASH cluster, and considering the pressing needs in the Beit Hasan community (Nablus governorate) for water network installation, extension and HH connections, as well as the construction of a reservoir, the project team engineers will conduct technical field visits and develop BOQ with support of PWA engineers and community representatives. ACTED will launch separate tender processes for both water network rehabilitation/extension and reservoir construction to select contractors to proceed with the works. The project team will conduct regular on-site monitoring visits to ensure works are completed as per BOQs and in respect of WASH and SPHERE standards. For the water sources, costs of the rehabilitation will be co-shared with communities to ensure ownership. ACTED/ARIJ will also conduct on-the-job briefing sessions with the water service providers staff on operation and maintenance to ensure sustainability. ARIJ will lead this activity with support from ACTED. A2. Based on recommendations from the PWA regarding the target communities in each of the governorates (Beit Hasan, Rafat) and following technical assessment, the project team will select 2 water sources with the highest rehabilitation needs to be targeted for maintenance. BoQ and workplans will be developed and validated by the PWA. ARIJ will launch a tender process for the water source rehabilitation to select contractors to proceed with the works. The project team will conduct regular on-site monitoring visits to ensure works are completed as per BOQs and in respect of WASH and SPHERE standards. The aim is to cover the provision of water facilities’ critical operation and maintenance materials and tools, such as chlorine dosing pump. ARIJ will lead this activity with support from ACTED. A3. Following the technical assessment of selected schools and health care facilities, in collaboration with MoE and MoH and with special focus on needs of people with disabilities (PWD), as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns, ACTED and ARIJ will develop Bills of Quantities (BOQs) for 13 institutions (8 schools and 5 HCF) outlining rehabilitation and maintenance WASH needs and related action plans. ACTED/ARIJ will select local contractors through a transparent tender process to implement the rehabilitation works. ACTED/ARIJ will conduct regular follow-up and monitoring visits to ensure respect of the plan and of the WASH and SPHERE standards, as well as to provide corrective actions where needed. ACTED/ARIJ will also provide operation and maintenance training to ensure sustainability. Moreover, HCFs will receive environmental and personal cleaning kits. ARIJ will lead the rehabilitation activities with support from ACTED, and ACTED will be in charge of the distribution activities. A4. For HH-level WASH facilities rehabilitation, ACTED/ARIJ will identify the 82 most vulnerable HHs in target communities through transparent application processes and socio-economic/protection vulnerability assessments. Once the 82 HHs are selected, ACTED/ARIJ will develop tailored BOQs with each HH, to establish rehabilitation needs and costs. During the technical assessment, the project team will ensure that specific needs of PWD, as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns, are integrated into the BoQs. A self-repair approach will be used with the provision of restricted cash in 3 instalments. ACTED/ARIJ engineers will monitor progress and address technical issues through regular visits. When signing grant agreements with each BNF, ACTED will distribute a PSEA awareness leaflet and a leaflet on environmentally friendly best practices for households. To confirm the use of funds, assess beneficiary satisfaction and formulate recommendations, ACTED will conduct a Post-Rehabilitation and Post-Distribution Monitoring involving both male and female household members. ACTED will lead this activity with support from ARIJ. The provision of restricted cash assistance to 82 HHs will enable them to adopt adequate WASH practices by facilitating rehabilitation of their WASH facilities, especially those related to hygiene, protection, and health. All HHs will receive Hygiene kits and for those with limited water storage capacity will receive PE tanks. A5. Strengthening of local CBOs: A community-based protection approach, established in 2016 by ACTED under previous projects, will be followed throughout the whole project to ensure solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee better respect of the safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups (female headed households, PwD, older people). ACTED and ARIJ will identify and strengthen the capacity of 11 CBOs in the targeted areas, which should be comprised of at least 5 to 7 members each (of which at least 30% should be women). Should target communities/areas not be represented by any CBOs, ACTED and ARIJ will establish CBOs of 5 to 7 members, including at least 30% female members. The capacity of CBOs members will be strengthened, based on project needs, notably on the main protection-related topics (protection, PSEA, case referrals), climate change awareness, disaster-risk reduction, legal framework of the intervention, and project-related topics. The contents and the pedagogy will be adapted to ensure a better understanding and the relevancy to the context specificities. At each step of the implementation, ACTED and ARIJ will ensure to prioritize the most vulnerable categories, such as PwD, older people, and girls/women, with a particular focus on beneficiaries affected by protection-related concerns. Finally, ACTED and ARIJ will ensure that a standard close on PSEA is included in all agreements with CBOs, in accordance with the IASC (Inter-Agency Standing Committee) principles and guidelines. ACTED will lead this activity with support from ARIJ.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects and anticipate and mitigate any challenge. ACTED’s Appraisal Monitoring & Evaluation Unit (AMEU) and ARIJ will measure the following indicators: # of households sanitation facilities rehabilitated, target: 82;# of household hygiene kits provided, target: 82;# of HC WASH facilities constructed/ rehabilitated, target: 5# of school WASH facilities constructed/ rehabilitated, target: 8# of km length of water pipeline, network installed/rehabilitated, target: 5 km;% of females, elderly, people with disabilities who report improved safety and dignity after the intervention, target: 80%; # of Community-Based Organisations (CBOs) reinforced throughout a refreshing training, target: 11; % of CBOs' members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the refreshing training, target: 80%;In particular, the project team will conduct a baseline and end line assessment to measure progress against pre-identified indicators, as well as beneficiary satisfaction, and draw lessons learned from the project. Those activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups (people with disabilities, female-headed households). ACTED will share the findings and results with the WASH cluster and the PWA so as to contribute to the wider humanitarian community’s understanding of WASH challenges and issues in the most vulnerable communities of the West Bank, and disseminate best practices. Satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP standards and ACTED’s SoPs. Finally, ACTED and ARIJ Programme, Project Development and Finance Department will closely follow-up project activities and ensure the action is implemented in conformity with the HRP guidelines, IHP, as well as locally defined Food Security Sector recommendations. Using ACTED’s global Project Cycle Management tools, project team will report monthly to the Project Development Department, on project implementation, outputs and data to ensure that all indicators are on track, delays are prevented and any difficulties are identified and recorded. Similarly, the Finance and Audit department will ensure a constant follow up of all procurement, expenses and transactions. Those monitoring activities will ensure the highest level of compliance and transparency.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Service provider solution is chosen for those activities, such as rehabilitation work or trainings, which require specific technical skills. In this way ACTED will ensure the quality and effectiveness of the results and ensure a no harm approach for beneficiary, especially for WASH infrastructure and facilities construction/rehabilitation. For this methodology, ACTED will identify experienced and professional local contractors who will be selected through a transparent procurement process thereby ensuring the completion of activities on time whilst fulfilling all technical requirements. In kind assistance ensures an equitable and harmonized response to all beneficiaries in all areas, following the relevant cluster standards for kits components. ACTED will go through a transparent procurement process to purchase items, to ensure the quality/price balance. Kits will be delivered directly to the field to ensure equal access to all beneficiaries, mainly the most vulnerable such as PwDs and older people. As noted in the 2022 MSNA findings, cash assistance is beneficiaries’ preferred method, as 64.2% of households surveyed in the WB reported that they would like to receive aid in the form of cash and voucher assistance. This is particularly accurate for women and PwD, as cash provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost-efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will contribute towards restoring the Palestinian economy. Finally, cash assistance ensures multisectoral coverage, while keeping beneficiaries involved all along the activities, as they can choose the more adapted items to cover their multiple needs.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED and ARIJ will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co-applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that co-applicants learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Beneficiaries will also have the possibility to access the CRM through WhatsApp.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address, complaints boxes and directly through ACTED staff during field visits. This plurality of means aims to ensure CRM accessibility to all, including vulnerable groups with specific needs. At the first stage of the project, during the assessment, field officers will provide a CRM card to each BNF, including all communication channels mentioned above, and explanations on the CRM, to ensure that all BNF feel comfortable and in measure to reach it if needed. Female staff, part of the team to which BNF will be able to refer to, will notably ensure female BNF feel safe to complain. AME staff will dedicate specific time to vulnerable groups during field visits to re-explain the CRM and emphasise that BNF can directly complain to them during visits - if one person would not have the capacity to use/access a phone/email address or the complaint box (notably children, PwD, and the elderly). ACTED’s commitment to accountability to affected populations is fully integrated into its recruitment process, staff inductions, trainings and performance management and partnership agreements. All ACTED staff working for the ACTED oPt mission are trained on the Code of Conduct, on the Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection Policies. Trainings on PSEA aim to ensure that all staff understand the Code of Conduct and the PSEA policy, their obligation to report any allegation or suspicion and the sanction measures taken for any breach of the Code of Conduct. In early March 2022, all ACTED staff also participated in a refresher session on PSEA organized by the PSEA network, to strengthen their capacities to recognize exploitation and abuse situations, as well as adopt a good behaviour to report. This session also provided an overview of the Interagency Community Complaint Mechanism and its SOPs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED and ARIJ will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary households to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do-No-Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do-No-Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED’s project team will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project.ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per household will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are considered, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, older people and other marginalised persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. In addition, CBO members and ACTED staff will be present to ensure the integration of household concerns into BoQ and workplan design. In the event beneficiaries are dissatisfied with their respective BoQ and/or workplan an appeal mechanism will be offered through the CBOs and ACTED’s CRM. If the CBOs or ACTED/ARIJ staff consider the request justified, a new BoQ and/or workplan will be produced by a different engineer. To mitigate this, all staff involved in the assessment process will undergo extensive refresher training in protection standards prior to the assessments to ensure equal and impartial access to assistance. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. Efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBOs will conduct household visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including elderly, people with disabilities) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
