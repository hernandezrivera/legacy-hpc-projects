Project Summary

The unique socio-economic conditions of blockade, with its movement restrictions and destruction of productive capacity, are further compounded by repeated cycles of conflict and political instability and now the COVID-19 pandemic. All schools in Gaza have been mostly closed since March 2020 in order to prevent the further spread of the COVID-19 virus while protecting the health and safety of children. The new school year of 2020/2021 was ready to start with over 287,000 registered students between 1st to 9th grades in 278 UNRWA schools throughout the Gaza Strip, yet again disrupted upon the announcement of lockdown measure due to the detection of locally transmitted COVID-19 cases on 24 August 2020. As of end of October 2020, UNRWA is finalizing the resumption of Education plan taking into consideration COVID-19 circumstances and developments that are expected to continue throughout the academic year 2020/2021. In order to ensure that no child is left behind and that access to education is available to all students regardless of their available resources under this circumstance, UNRWA will provide: (1) support classes for Arabic and Mathematics including supporting materials , (2) mental health and psychosocial support to students and parents by experienced school counsellors through tele-counselling and in person if the situation allows , (3) support to special education for children with disabilities through Community Based Rehabilitation Centers, (4) application of preventive measures including school hygiene and physical-distancing requirement to be compatible with the WHO and MoH regulations related to COVID-19 , and (5) self-learning support to assist students’ learning experience at home.

1 - Needs

As soon as the first COVID-19 cases were confirmed in Bethlehem, the Palestinian Prime Minister declared the State of Emergency on 5 March 2020 followed by the closure of all schools in the Gaza Strip. All schools in Gaza have been mostly closed since March 2020 in order to prevent the further spread of the COVID-19 virus while protecting the health and safety of children. The new school year of 2020/2021 was ready to start with over 287,000 registered students (138,450 girls and 148,550 boys), including over 11,500 (5,300 girls and 6,200 boys) students with disabilities, between 1st to 9th grades in 278 UNRWA schools throughout the Gaza Strip (UNRWA, EMIS 2020), yet again disrupted upon the announcement of lockdown measure due to the detection of locally transmitted COVID-19 cases on 24 August 2020. As of end of October 2020, UNRWA is finalizing the resumption of Education plan taking into consideration COVID-19 circumstances and developments that are expected to continue throughout the academic year 2020/2021. The plan is focusing on a phased dynamic modality of different scenarios depending on the COVID-19 situation of local transmission with lockdown measures, and no-lockdown measures. UNRWA will respect measures taken by local authorities in terms of returning children to schools and will pivot between the different scenarios as the COVID-19 and local authorities’ decisions evolve. In the case of total lockdown, UNRWA will deliver education through self-learning at home with coordinated follow-up by UNRWA teachers to ensure support and proper education for all children at home. When the situation allows, UNRWA will reopen school adapting a blended learning approach that students’ learning will be divided between in-classroom and at-home. The COVID-19 pandemic imposed new challenges in relation to the functionality and accessibility of the distance learning programs, schools’ readiness in terms of WASH facilities and hygiene kits to implement the health protocols, school staff capacity to adapt to the blended learning approaches and to ensure children’s safety during the pandemic. Furthermore, the parents’ role is also changing with the use of distance learning, where they are now asked to provide more educational and emotional support to their children (HNO 2021). It is estimated that 60% of students in Gaza require assistance in overcoming a variety of challenges, including poverty, additional learning needs, access restrictions and electricity shortages at home, which is translated to over 172,200 students (83,074 girls and 89,136 boys) registered at UNRWA schools in need of assistance. Education Cluster identified some challenges under the current situation, including the lack of access to distance learning for most vulnerable children such as children living in remote areas and/or children with no adequate internet or IT equipment, poor school learning environment considering the fact that many schools in Gaza are operating in double shift bases resulting in reduced hours in core subjects and crowdedness in classrooms, and lack of support to children with disabilities to secure adapted school facilities and staff capacity to accommodate their needs (HNO 2021). As a consequence, the loss of children’s learning opportunities due to the closure of schools and limited access to education has a significant impact on the lives of children as well as their families that the mental health and psychosocial support should be strengthened in order to respond to emerging needs (HNO 2021).

2 - Activities

UNRWA, as one of the largest primary education service providers in Gaza, is at the time of submission of this proposal finalizing the plan for resumption of education in the academic year 2020/2021 to continue providing basic education to over 287,000 (138,450 girls and 148,550 boys) elementary and preparatory school students. This includes over 11,500 students with disabilities (5,300 girls and 6,200 boys) attending at UNRWA schools. A phased dynamic approach will follow where UNRWA will immediately pivot learning scenario based on the developments on the ground. When the situation imposes restriction on movement, full self-learning at home will be implemented and a coordinated follow-up by UNRWA teachers will be provided to ensure the quality of deliverables. A blended learning approach of three-days in school learning and three-days self-learning at home will be adapted when movement and health measures at schools are in place. In order to provide quality education in enhanced health and safety measures in place with no child left behind, UNRWA aims to implement various adjusted activities assuming that the COVID-19 emergency will continue to have an effect on its operation in coming year. While UNRWA supports all 287,000 registered students, it is estimated that 60% of the students in Gaza are in need of assistance, which is translated to 172,200 students (83,074 girls and 89,136 boys) registered at UNRWA schools. UNRWA will provide support classes in Arabic and Mathematics with support remedial education materials to over 68,000 students (32,960 girls and 35,233 boys) in order for students to meet minimum academic standard. Mental health and psychosocial support will be accessible by all students and their parents remotely as well as in person if the situation allows, while targeting 13,500 (6,600 girls and 6,900 boys) students for individual counselling. The counsellors will be following up with existing cases on their files as well as responding to newly emerged cases using holistic and integrated approach to child and family well-being. The parents and students have an access to school counsellors’ dedicated help lines that they could call to seek for support. School counsellors act as case manager, and support can be sought internally from other UNRWA programmes or referred externally if the case requires interventions that UNRWA cannot provide, such as serious protection cases. UNRWA will continue to support Community Based Rehabilitation Centres that provide basic education to 700 children with disabilities (290 girls and 410 boys) to protect their right for education under the new norms with COVID-19. In order to safely operate schools following WHO and MoH regulation under the strict oversight of UNRWA Health Programme, health and safety precautionary measures will be implemented. This includes the implementation of enhanced cleaning and disinfection of school facilities, staff and students’ hygiene requirement, and proper physical distancing by decreasing number of students to half in all classrooms. Finally, to ensure no child is left behind and considering the challenging environment in Gaza, offline self-learning materials will be distributed to all students to facilitate guidance for students and their caregivers, such as parents, to better understand and explain the textbooks at home.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. UNRWA regularly assesses the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. UNRWA schools are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do no harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; and (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. UNRWA provides basic education to protect the safety and well-being of the students. UNRWA schools are equipped with child-friendly tools providing space for boys and girls including students with disabilities to study comfortably eliminating the risk of getting harmed or exposed to any danger as much as possible. The pandemic resulted in an increase in the protection threats facing groups with multiple overlapping vulnerabilities, which includes those at risk of domestic and intimate partner violence and the elderly. The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse may have also increased, as evidenced in other crisis situations. The Agency is ensuring that protection principles are mainstreamed and integrated in the response, and that special attention is paid to the needs of the most vulnerable, including children. Providing support to children who were forced to adjust their learning routine due to COVID-19 will protect their right to education during this challenging time.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA welcomes all Palestine refugee children from grade 1 to 9 in its schools across the Gaza Strip. The location of schools takes into consideration of safety concerns, especially for girls, including the accessibility, potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. Furthermore, schools are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Educational staff, who are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection and gender mainstreaming, are able to refer specific protection cases to MHPSS counsellors for follow up. In COVID-19 emergency, students were forced to switch their learning routines from face-to-face in classroom to self-study at home. UNRWA provides self-learning materials to all students in order to support their learning experience at home. Community Based Rehabilitation Centres provides education to children with disabilities in order to ensure that these vulnerable children are not left behind.
