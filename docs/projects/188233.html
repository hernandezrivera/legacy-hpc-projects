Project Summary

TGH’s intervention is closely in line with the 2022-2023 WASH HRP Objectives 1, 2 and 4, as TGH aims to contribute to life-saving WASH interventions and support the resilience of existing WASH systems by addressing the needs of people affected by the conflict in Syria, coupled with the deteriorating context due to the COVID-19 pandemic and worsened by hyperinflationary economic crisis.
According the draft WASH 2022 HNO report that 13.2 million require WASH assistance in Syria with 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water with at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, overall 2.4 million children out of schools and over 40% of caretakers in households with children attending school received complaints from children on WASH-related issues.
The intervention will be integrated intervention in covering WASH, Education and Shelter activities with protection, gender, and environmental issues mainstreamed. This intervention will be focused on 4 areas: South Idlib, Hama rural areas (Sub Districts: Eastern Bariy, As-Saan), Rural Damascus and Dar’a rural areas. The targeted areas were selected based on their accessibility, the severity of their needs varying between 3 and 5 as per the draft WaSH 2022 HNO, TGH field assessments during 2021 and consultations with Water authorities, SARC, local authorities and municipalities.
The proposed intervention has been designed based on TGH 4-year experience in implementing WASH projects (with a specific emphasis on WASH-in-school interventions) in Syria. TGH follows a multi-sectorial programming approach that relies on complimentary multi-donor funding to ensure comprehensive coverage, addressing a broad range of critical humanitarian needs that exist in Syria, including WASH, Shelter and education
In line with Objective 1, activities 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3, TGH will propose intervention will include:
• In 2022, TGH will commence rehab 6 boreholes equipped with solar system, rehab 3 water networks, support Water trucking, support Water quality test for targeted water supply systems, distribution of 400 water tanks 1m3 at household’s level, and rehabilitation of 2 Sewage network.
• In 2023, TGH will commence rehab 8 boreholes equipped with solar system, rehab 3 water networks, support Water quality test for targeted water supply systems, distribution of 500 water tanks 1m3 at household’s level, and rehabilitation of 2 Sewage network.
As part of Objective 2, activity 2.1, 2.2, and Objective 4, activity 4.1, and in line with Education Sector objectives, TGH will propose intervention will include:
• In 2022, TGH will commence rehab WASH facilities in 7 schools, and TGH will target 5 schools in 2023 with rehab WASH facilities in order to secure the access to water for drinking and hygiene purposes and ensure excreta evacuation to increase measures against infection, prevention and control both at school and at home in Idlib, Hama, Rural Damascus, and Dar’a . The intervention will be part of an integrated approach with Education HRP.
• In 2022 TGH will target 7 schools to distribute of hygiene kits and soap to students, and in 2023 TGH will target 5 school to distribute of hygiene kits and soap to students.
• Provision of emergency WASH services, including response to COVID19 according to need in 2022 and 2023.
The above the proposed activities will be implement during two years project (2022-2023), and TGH is estimating of 30 % of budget already secured for planned 2022 WASH Activities.
As part of its activities, TGH puts a specific emphasis on abiding by protection principles through providing gender and disabled friendly WASH facilities in schools taking into account menstrual needs, including doors lockable from the inside and lighting. TGH will also ensure to provide WASH facilities accessible for children with disabilities.

4 - Needs

TGH conducted complemented multi-sectoral assessments done during August and December 2021 focusing on WaSH and education needs in the targeted location in Rural Damascus, Hama, Dar’a and Idlib governorates. TGH noted that 70% of water systems (boreholes and network) are not function due to different reasons such as it suffered from damage, no maintenance, poor water resource management and electricity shortages. Many communities are still completely dependent on water trucking to cope with their basic water needs, as confirmed by needs assessments in Eastern Ghouta in Rural Damascus, Dara’a and Hama, where WT is handled by communities at a high cost barely affordable for many. The overall water scarcity leads people to resort to harmful practices, such as reducing water consumption for hygiene purpose. Additionally, destruction of sanitation infrastructure and lack of services have increased the risk of water contamination, TGH assessed the area of Tassil and Izra' and identified big gaps in terms of sanitation, with not functional sewage networks. In a context of COVID-19, it is a priority to implement WaSH interventions in social institutions, such as schools, in order to reduce the virus propagation among the entire community. The identification of the WASH needs in schools has been done in collaboration with the DoE, and taking into account the severity scale of the Education Sector. WaSH-in-school needs have been identified in 11 schools within intervention areas.

5 - Implementation of activities

TGH will coordinate with the relevant local authorities such as water authorities, municipalities, SARC and WASH Sector to identify damaged facilities to be rehabilitated and avoid any overlapping or duplication with other organizations. A detailed assessment of the facilities will be conducted by qualified TGH engineers to identify the specific works to be carried out by selected local contractors. Contractor selection will be based on TGH procurement and policies procedures ensuring transparency and accountability all along the selection process. TGH WASH engineers will be in charge of monitoring the works to ensure delivered as per standards. All the works will be handed to over to local authorities. TGH will also ensure to provide training on how to maintain facilities if specific needs are identified in order to ensure the sustainability of the intervention. As part of kit distributions, TGH will rely on SARC volunteers to conduct the distributions & hygiene awareness sessions under the constant supervision of TGH staff. M&E will be carried out by TGH MEAL team through baseline & end line household surveys aiming at evaluating the impact of the activities on living conditions. To support M&E activities and ensure accountability towards beneficiaries, TGH also set up a strong Complaints & Feedback mechanism through hotline, complaint boxes & regular consultations of the community in order to allow beneficiaries of all age, gender and background to reach the organization.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Content of kits not gender sensitive: TGH follows the WaSH Sector guidelines to harmonize the content of the kits. The hygiene kits to be distributed are not gender oriented and all the items can be used by both genders.- Toilets safety and risk of GBV: TGH ensures that when working in toilet blocks in schools, these are separated by gender, TGH will ensure that include locks for doors and lighting in the facilities.- Abuse and exploitation: All of TGH staff receives PSEA training when joining the organization. Additionally, in each of TGH’s base in Syria there is a PSEA focal point in order to follow any potential issue, TGH ensures that they sign an Ethics Clause along the contract.- Exclusion of beneficiaries: TGH ensures that all the people with disabilities have access to the facilities built. In the schools the rehabilitations always include accessibility features such as ramps and handles. - Risk of theft of WaSH rehabilitation material: TGH agrees that all the safety of the material while doing the rehabilitation is responsibility of the contractor. Once the works are finished, everything is handed over to the local authorities. We ensure that the authorities and local population takes responsibility and ownership.- Exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO): TGH ensures that the area of work is safe and has no explosive devises. TGH coordinates with local population, local authorities and official mine action organisations to assure that the areas of work are safe


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

- Abuse and exploitation (sexual exploitation and abuse): All of TGH staff receives PSEA training when joining the organization. And, in each of TGH’s base in Syria there is a PSEA focal point in order to follow any potential issue. For external contractors, TGH ensures that they sign an Ethics Clause along the contract. In addition to the standard complaint mechanism, one focal point is designated in each operational base to collect the potential PSEA complaints and a dedicated hotline was set up to receive reports. The different channels appear in all visuals that are put in TGH office and areas of intervention (i.e. schools, distribution sites, etc.). All PSEA focal points receive specific trainings on how to deal with SEA complaint and their roles and responsibilities are defined in Terms of References. - Content of kits not gender sensitive: TGH follows the WaSH Sector guidelines to harmonize the content of the kits. The hygiene kits to be distributed are not gender oriented and all the items can be used by both genders.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Security tensions impacting TGH and its activities: Acceptance approach, TGH presence in the area of intervention since 2020 maintaining the good relationship with local community. Security and safety protocols in place and regularly updated.- Access to project locations remains granted but access to beneficiaries is limited by local authorities: Strong coordination and good relationship with local authorities to get approval on time, and impeding adequate monitoring and targeting TGH will count with staff from the area of intervention and avoid travels from Damascus.- Increasing of the conflict related fighting. TGH constantly monitors the security situation in the areas of intervention. There will be preventive measures in place in order to ensure the safety of the staff.- Currency fluctuations (SYP towards USD) - Inflation rate increases. Price monitoring and co-financing with other projects from other donors if possible.Update budget forecast on regular basis, Inflation monitoring based on market survey, in case of continuous inflation TGH will adjust the BoQs accordingly in coordination with the donor.- Cases of COVID-19 continue to multiply. Continued adaptation of all activities in response to Covid19, Adherence to COVID-19 preventive measures and SOP.- Intervention of the authorities among the selection of beneficiaries. TGH will ensure transparency in the selection of the beneficiaries based on criteria previously agreed upon.
