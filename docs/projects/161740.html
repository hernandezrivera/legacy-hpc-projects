Project Summary

Street Child will address the critical barriers to quality learning and retention for 50,000 conflict-affected children across North East Nigeria. The focus will be both on improving access to quality, protective education for out-of-school children and improving the quality of teaching and learning for children in formal schools. In addition to basic literacy and numeracy, teachers will be trained on PSS, child protection and positive pedagogies to ensure that schools are safer, more protective spaces for all children. Street Child will work closely with communities and with local partners and stakeholders to build the capacity of those working in education in the region, improving sustainability and contributing to long-term systems strengthening.
In Yobe, LGAs which will be targeted are Gujba and Gulani. In Adamawa, the targeted LGAs will be Mubi, Michika and Madagali. In Borno the targeted LGAs will be MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Hawul, Mobbar, Dikwa and Konduga.

1 - Needs

A characteristic of the conflict in North East Nigeria has been the targeting of schools, teachers and students by armed groups. The protracted conflict has severely impacted the already-weak school system in the North East, leaving the limited system unable to absorb the high numbers of displaced children looking to attend school. In addition to high numbers of out-of-school children, learning outcomes for children in-school (including foundational literacy and numeracy rates) are low and drop-out rates are high. An estimated 1,060,129 conflict-affected children across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states are estimated to be in need of education services. 697,683 of these children are displaced. A further 17,598 teachers are in need of training on child-centred pedagogy and psychosocial support (PSS). Factors limiting access to education in North East Nigeria have affected both boys and girls, but access to quality education has tended to be even more limited for girls than for boys, and adolescent girls are amongst the most vulnerable to dropping out of school. Rates or enrolment, retention and learning are even lower for children with disabilities (CwD) who are often excluded from mainstream learning and social life - a consultation conducted by Street Child has found that 57% of CwD are out of school. Challenges faced by CwD includes high levels of stigmatisation, a lack of trained teachers, a lack of inclusive facilities and inclusive learning aids, and the high cost of education and specialised education materials. There is a need to better integrate and respond to the barriers that particularly vulnerable groups, including girls and CwD, face in accessing quality learning opportunities in safe and protective learning environments. Recognising the key role education plays in providing immediate physical, psychosocial and cognitive protection in emergency contexts, there is a need to build safe and protective learning spaces. Building the capacity of teachers on pedagogical skills, psychosocial skills and child protection knowledge is crucial to ensure that education is protective and builds life-savings knowledge on topics such as health, hygiene and GBV (gender-based violence). At the same time, given that the low educational outcomes in NE Nigeria pre-date the current crisis, there is also a need to ensure that the support provided contributes to strengthening the system as a whole and in the long-term.

2 - Activities

learning centres (TLCs). Community volunteer teachers (CVTs) will be trained and receive an allowance to facilitate these non-formal classes, including training on PSS and child protection. Where structures are lacking, semi-permanent TLCs will be constructed in line with best practices prioritising children's safety and security, including safe locations, perimeter fences and the availability of first aid kits. Construction and renovations will also aim to meet the accessibility needs of CwD. The implementation of the national EiE curriculum, which Street Child has worked on, will be scaled-up and used as the accelerated learning curriculum in our TLCs. The learning progress of children attending TLCs will be regularly assessed, with the aim of mainstreaming children into the formal system to allow them to continue their education. Street Child will work closely with the local and state education authorities to facilitate this mainstreaming. Similarly, Street Child will work with the education authorities to achieve official accreditation of CVTs through the Federal Teacher Scheme, allowing these community members to become government teachers and be included on the government payroll for sustainability. To support the strengthening of the formal education system and improve learning outcomes in formal schools for all children, Street Child will train government primary school teachers on pedagogical skills and psychosocial skills. This will include training teachers to implement the innovative Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) approach, with the aim of remediating lack of skills in foundational literacy and numeracy. Formal school support will also include the provision of teaching and learning materials and adaptation of school structures (including WASH facilities) to be more inclusive, and the training of teachers on inclusive, child-friendly pedagogical skills. This will empower teachers to be able to meet the differing learning and support needs of different children, particularly with the aim of ensuring the needs of CwD in accessing education and quality learning are met. Community Education Committees (CECs) or School-Based Management Committees (SBMCs) will be established or strengthened, and trained on children's rights and protection mechanisms, inclusion, school management and disaster risk reduction. These committees will be responsible for managing and maintaining the learning centres, and for sustained community engagement on the importance of education. In addition to building sustainability, this will also support the process of community entry and acceptance. In line with Street Child's commitment to integrated education and child protection programming, teachers, teacher mentors, CEC/SMBC members and other school personnel will be trained on basic child protection principles and on how to prevent, identify and respond to child protection concerns. This will include supporting schools to develop safeguarding clear preventive and responsive safeguarding mechanisms, in order to build more protective learning environments.

7.3 - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

Street Child's approach to EiE bridges the humanitarian-development nexus, providing immediate protection whilst also contributing to resilience-building and system strengthening. A key element that Street Child will work on is ensuring that the learning children and teachers gain in learning centres and non-formal settings during the current crisis is validated and recognized, so that they can move into the formal system. Nexus activities will therefore be undertaken in all targeted LGAs: Gujba, Gulani, Mubi, Michika, Madagali, MMC, Jere, Gwoza, Damboa, Monguno, Biu, Hawul, Mobbar, Dikwa and Konduga.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Throughout project implementation, Street Child will use proven mechanisms to engage community members and stakeholders and to ensure accountability and transparency. Community participation will be integrated into all stages of the project, from the involvement of community leaders in beneficiary selection to putting in place effective Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) to collect feedback to inform monitoring and evaluation. All feedback received through these diverse mechanisms will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond to feedback where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning. A volunteer project management committee (PMC) will be established in each community of implementation. This PMC will be empowered to hold Street Child accountable to the planned project aims, activities and implementation timeline, and will be able to raise complaints and concerns at monthly meetings with Street Child staff. A dedicated complaints mechanism (CRM) officer will be identified for all targeted communities. This person will be responsible for collecting feedback and complaints, and liaising between community members, partners and Street Child to share this feedback. The CRM officer will hold weekly help desk sessions in each community, where beneficiaries and community members can share feedback. A register of all complaints made, and responses to these complaints, will be kept. The help desk will also be set up during all key activities, such as grant disbursement. In addition to programmatic feedback mechanisms, Street Child has a toll-free line which can be called to report emergency incidents and safeguarding concerns. The number for this line will be displayed at all project sites.
