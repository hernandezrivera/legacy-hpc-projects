Project Summary

To meet the immediate critical WASH needs, the project will provide appropriate quality and quantity of safe drinking water through rehabilitating/repairing community water sources, as well as installing water tanks. HOPE will use multiple approaches including rehabilitation of existing water supply systems/, Water quality monitoring and surveillance, water treatment, and provision of temporary water storage facilities. All relevant stakeholders including line departments, Political Administration, Public Health Department, Wash Cluster, Education Cluster, Taleemi Islahi Jirgas (TIJs) will be involved in the implementation of the project activities. Bills of quantity and designs will be approved from UNOCHA and WASH Cluster. Water quality will be ensured in the project targeted areas.
The project will reactivate sanitation facilities at communities, and schools to prevent water borne diseases and promote healthy environment in the areas of return in Tribal District Orakzai. HOPE will construct 2 separate single latrines at different locations at 1 schools and will provide 527 sanitation kit to most vulnerable families to support the construction of latrine to minimize the health risks associated with open defecation in communities. HOPE will distribute sanitation kits to vulnerable people for construction of latrine whereas community will construct latrine with their own resources.
HOPE will conduct a well-organized health and hygiene campaign on promotion of hygiene practices. For this purpose, Hygiene Promoters will conduct awareness raising sessions in government/privates schools, health facilities and communities while using CHAST and PHAST approaches. HOPE will conduct 205 health and hygiene promotion sessions through CHAST and PHAST approaches to cover 6,151 individuals (1,599 men1,661 women, 1,417 boys and 1,474 girls) and will targets children (girls and boys) community members (women and men), elderly and disables, needs to measure current knowledge and practices towards enhancement of hygienic condition of the target communities in a culturally appropriate and friendly manner. Prior to the health and hygiene session, HOPE will conduct Pre and Post KAP survey to know the knowledge, attitude and practices of the target group. The project will also provide 878 WASH NFIs to the most vulnerable families (covering 6151 people) to maintain health and hygiene practice of the returnees.

Needs

According to the Detail Needs Assessment (DNA) data analysis conducted by WASH cluster, the sources of drinking water available in Orakzai are open spring 38%), pipeline 15%, public tape 2%, protected hand pump 17%, unprotected hand pump 19%, protected dug well 3%, unprotected dug well 8%, protected rain catchment 1%, unprotected rain catchment 4% and surface water 12%. Similarly, the analysis further shows that 25% men, 79% adult women, 19% boys, 48% girls (Total 186, and 2% others, collect water from the sources. Water source distance from house to collection points are within 500 meters 25%, 500 meters to 1KM 51%, 1KM to 3KM 16%, and more than 3 KM 8%. In addition, time consumed in collection of water, less than 15 minutes 7%, 15-30 minutes 35%, 30 minutes- 1hours 44%, more than 1 hour 15%. Only 37 % respondents have access to latrine whereas 63% do not have access to latrine and they practice open defecation. 100% respondents were of the view that they construct latrine by themselves. These latrines are 8% Flush/Pour flash latrine, composting latrine 11%, pit latrine with slab 54%, latrine draining to canal/river/cracker 20%, latrine without slab 6% and other types 1%. 79% respondents were of the view that the reason behind not constructing the latrine is that it is very expensive. The analysis regarding hand washing shows that 63% wash hand with water, 33% with water and soap, 2% with sands/leaves, 1% water and ash. Only 17% received health and hygiene education whereas 83% were not sensitized on health and hygiene education. 91% using oral hygiene practices among them 77% with Meswak, 14 using fingers and only 9% using tooth brush. In order to validate the WASH Cluster Assessment, HOPE conducted Needs Assessment in Orakzai from April 27 to 30, 2018. WASH assessment covered three areas including education, health and communities. In education, in total of 15 schools visited, it was found that 57 % latrine were fully damaged whereas 27% were partially damaged. The condition of water supply in these visited schools were not encouraging as 40% Water supply sources were fully damaged and 30% were partially damaged whereas 47% of water storages were completely damaged and 37% were partially damaged. Similarly, in out of 15 assessed health facilities, 47% of the latrines were fully damaged and 39% were partially damaged. Furthermore, water supply and water storage facilities were 41% and 45% fully damaged and 35% and 33% were partially damaged respectively. Furthermore, HOPE Assessment Team found that 10 out of 13 visited drinking water supply were either completely or partially damaged. The damages of the schemes were of the scale ranging from minor repair to complete overhauling/installations. The Assessment Team also observed situation of sanitation especially needs of the latrine and reach on the consensus that most of the houses are without latrine which tends to open defecation and unhygienic practices.

Activities

In order to restore quality and affordable drinking water, HOPE will use multi-prong approaches including reactivation of existing water supply systems, Water quality monitoring and surveillance. The project will provide appropriate quality and quantity of safe drinking water through rehabilitating/repairing community water sources, as well as installing water tanks for water storages.All relevant stakeholders including line departments, local Administration, Public Health Department, Wash Cluster will be involved in the implementation of the project activities. The Technical Team will visit the schemes for detail assessment identified by PHED. On the basis of assessment, BoQ and design specification will be shared with UNOCHA for approval. Similarly, the project will provide water storage facilities through installation of water tank with the capacity of 500 Gallon where water storage facilities are not available. These tanks will be installed in various communal locations such as markets, cluster of houses (communities). The Water Quality Analyst will collect sample from different water sources at household’s level and will take water quality tests. If any of the water source is found unfit for drinking or contaminated, HOPE will take immediate actions and will bring this into the notice of the concerned government authorities such as PHED and local Administration. Water treatment such as chlorination of the water storages will be carried out on regular basis throughout the project period on household’s levels. While using community-led sanitation approaches for the construction of latrine, the project will provide 527 sanitation kits to most vulnerable families to support the construction of latrine to minimize the health risks associated with open defecation. Project will ensure community participation in the construction of latrine. Project will provide sanitation kits including shovel, pickaxe, p-trap, commode and PVC whereas community will construct the latrine with their own resource. Project will develop criteria for the identification of the most vulnerable people to be provided with sanitation kits for the construction of latrine. The criteria will prioritize Persons With Disabilities (PWD), widows, female/children headed household, orphans, elderly people and households having less than 20,000 rupees’ income per months. Hope will ensure provision of 10 % sanitation kits for construction of PWD/elderly latrine to the PWD and elderly people. The Indian commode will be replaced with English commode in sanitation kits. HOPE will construct 2 separate single latrines at different locations for male and female patients at 1 schools.Hygiene Promoters will conduct awareness sessions while targeting children (girls and boys) community members (women and men), elderly and disables, Members of the committees/organizations such as village Development Organizations (VDOs), Taleemi Islahi Jirgas (TIJs). While conducting these sessions with children, HOPE will use established Participatory Hygiene And Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) and Children’s Hygiene and Sanitation Training (CHAST) approaches. PHAST is a systematic participatory approach consisting of seven sequential steps that culminate in the development of an action plan for addressing the prevailing WASH problems in a given community. It employs Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA) tools to elicit meaningful and equitable participation of all community members. CHAST is an interactive approach for training children on hygiene and sanitation aspects. This is based on the PHAST approach and uses similar facilitation tools to enable young children to appreciate the messages. While using CHAST and PHAST approaches project will conduct multiple sessions with same target group to communicate messages properly. The project will also provide 878 WASH NFIs to the most vulnerable families (covering 6151 people) to maintain health and hygiene practice of the returnees.
