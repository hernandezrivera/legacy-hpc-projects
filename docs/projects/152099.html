Project Summary

Since 2017 REACH has implemented the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI), whereby REACH in cooperation with the cash and market working group assesses prices and availability of 32 basic food and non-food items (NFI) in 29 key urban areas on a monthly basis, recording and contextualizing month-to-month fluctuations and wider trends across markets. These activities have allowed for a deeper understanding of market dynamics in Libya, essential for market-based response planning. Their continuation is critical to ensure that up to date knowledge of the rapidly changing situation in the entire country is maintained and that the humanitarian response adapts accordingly. Through the proposed project REACH therefore aims to extend of the JMMI in cooperation with the Cash and Markets Working Group (CMWG).

Needs

Ongoing REACH JMMI and market assessment activities have highlighted the severe impact that crises and conflicts in Libya are having on markets. While markets have proven to be generally functional with most basic items generally available, the worsening liquidity crisis, trend of inflation and increasing prices, and fragile banking system place markets in a precarious situation. Continued monitoring of markets through the JMMI will provide the data needed for humanitarian actors to continually reassess the viability and impact of their market-based programming (including cash) as well as define response modalities. Markets are a crucial means for the survival of Libyan vulnerable populations. Access to basic services and commodities depend to a significant extent on the availability and functionality of markets as well as the ability of consumers to actually buy those commodities.

Activities

Continuation of the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI in partnership with the CMWG. The Markets Taskforce, which is a sub-group of the CMWG led by REACH and the CMWG Coordinator, have previously defined the methodology of the initiative in cooperation with REACH. Currently, through the JMMI REACH assesses prices and availability of 34 basic food and non-food items (NFI) in 29 key urban areas on a monthly basis, recording and contextualizing month-to-month fluctuations and wider trends across markets. Field coordinators familiar with the local market conditions have identified shops representative of the general price level in their respective locations (including supermarkets, bakeries, vegetable sellers and butchers, as well as central markets, which must be large enough to sell all (or most of) the items on the list and should be located in different areas within the respective assessed city). Per assessed item, at least four prices are collected within each location. REACH seeks to continue to expand its network of participating agencies (and deliver additional trainings in parallel) to achieve maximum coverage, while limiting the role of REACH to coordinating the JMMI and centralizing data. Factsheets summarizing price monitoring findings will be produced and disseminated on a monthly basis.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

The assessment activity is inherently designed to collect information on market prices from the conflict-affected population in Libya to improve accountability of the overall humanitarian response to the population. Furthermore, humanitarian partners, specifically through the framework of the CMWG Markets Taskforce will be involved from the very outset of the action - to jointly refine the approach, support data collection to ensure a strong ownership by other humanitarian actors, who are the direct beneficiaries of REACH research.
