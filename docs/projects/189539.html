Project Summary

This emergency WASH response is to support conflict affected and food insecure populations in 4 counties 3 of which are part of the 15 counties of the South Sudan WASH Severity Classification 4 (WSC 4) for 2022. The project aims to provide support for basic WASH services to households, healthcare centres and nutrition therapeutic centres, including COVID 19 prevention and mitigation support. This is a 9-month project that will have a strong component to support and mainstream GBV, AAP and disability inclusion into the project. The project is focused on supporting communities that have been affected by floods, intercommunal violence including displaced and host communities whose access to safe water has been constrained resulting in the high prevalence for poor WASH related diseases and high rates of malnutrition which is compounded by poor WASH services.
The project will mostly focus on the improvement of access to safe water through (1) rehabilitation of existing hand pumped boreholes (2) surface water treatment (3) household water treatment and provision of water handling and storage containers (4) construction of flood proof institutional latrine construction (5) hygiene promotion including the mainstreaming COVID 19 preparedness and prevention initiatives (6) distribution of hygiene kits.
The project will work to achieve its objectives through WASH Cluster Sectoral objective 1, to achieve the HRP Strategic Objective 1. The project will reach a total of 355,969 people with a budget of 713, 320 USDs. NCA will be directly responsible for the implement this project working in partnership with two national partners this approach will ensure the localisation of aid as well as capacity building of local entities.
NCA being an international ecumenical actor has commitments under the grand bargain to support local partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to a high quality and degree and this project will also ensure resilience of local communities and CBOs in WASH service provision by having components that will promote resilience through training water committees, supporting local government actors and line ministries in WASH service provision. NCA has a static presence in Budi, Kapoeta South, Gogrial East, Tonj North and Gumuruk in Pibor. NCA is also implementing activities in Kapoeta East, Tonj East and Aweil South counties.

1 - Needs

Assessments carried out in Tonj East and Kapoeta East in February 2022 indicate a high need for Emergency WASH support services in all these counties, all of which have intersecting multisectoral needs due to a combination of factors. As a result, these counties are also among the 15 counties on the high priority 2022 HRP and the WSC 4 for 2022.In Tonj East, for instance, assessment findings carried out by NCA in February 2022 show that many villages have a large number of internally displaced people following recent intercommunal conflicts, which also consequently places WASH services under severe strain.In Kapoeta East, following a poor harvest due to massive flooding in 2021, many households have been displaced to neighboring areas, such as Kapoeta North County, Boma, and Ethiopia. In addition, the low number of operational water points coupled with low capacity for maintaining the existing facilities means overcrowding, which further exacerbates the challenges of maintaining access to water, basic sanitation and hygiene needs, which are severely constrained. As a result, children are particularly affected, with an increased number of children suffering from diarrheal diseases increasing their vulnerability to the compounding effect of under-nutrition.Lack of access to safe water is a contributor to under 5 mortality and morbidity, which needs to be urgently addressed in Pibor and Grater Kubal area. Flooding and intercommunal violence in mid-2021 caused a large displacement of populations towards Pibor Town. This, coupled with the worst flooding in 30 years in the area, has also disrupted farming and destroyed crops. The fighting between communities from Bor, Upper Nile and Pibor area also resulted in WASH infrastructure to be targeted and destroyed. Over 75% of the functioning water facilities in the area were destroyed during the invasion of greater Kubal. The only functioning water facility in Gumuruk that was developed and run by NCA was vandalized and the communities in the area now have no access to the only source of water that previously served over 20,000 people in the area.

2 - Project activities

NCA and partners will provide timely and equitable and access to safe water through repair and rehabilitation of existing ones and installation of water treatment unit in areas where IDPs are settled in order to increase the number of water points and thus improve ease of access to safe water. We will also ensure access to clean water and sanitation facilities to PLW and children with SAM and GAM cases in the nutrition centers through connecting them to the water supply facilities and constructing sanitation facilities and handwashing facilities to facilitate nutrition programming and reduce the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission.We will also create communities to demand improved sanitation practice through contextually tailored hygiene promotion activities and messaging. The Hyegine promotion activity also will target to raise the community awareness on safe water handling at household level coupled with provision of household water treatment kits and Hygiene Kits, specially targeted households with SAM children and PLW will get the treatment chemicals for three months in order to reduce their vulnerability to diseases from contaminated water intake. NCA and partners will use CASH and Voucher approach to distribute NFIs where the market are functional while mitigating all the risks associated with the use of CASH with that specific context of the intervention area.Our WASH programmes have gender and AAP mainstreaming This will be achieved by carrying out a variety of activities not limited to the following; - • Rehabilitation of existing hand pumped boreholes • Surface water treatment Unit near to IDP sites• Distribution of household water treatment products and provision of water handling and storage containers.• Construction of flood proof institutional latrines and washrooms/bathing shelters at nutrition centers• Hygiene promotion focusing on safe food and water handling, including risk communication, community engagement and infodemic management on Covid-19. • Distribution of hygiene kits. • Consultation with stakeholders on WASH needs.• Setting up Complaints response systems for community feedback.• Training of hygiene promoters• Identify hygiene promotion volunteers.• Monitor sanitation improvement. • Identify people with special needs and provide targeted support. • Carrying out Knowledge awareness and practices Surveys • Water point inventories to identify water points needing repairs. • Carrying out water committee’s formation • Designing and Printing Vouchers• Identification of vulnerable populations and target groups • Carrying out monitoring of progress of implementation• Participating in Coordination mechanisms• Carrying out assessments of needs• Providing information and getting feedback from right holders and attending to complaints• Carrying out Safety Audit

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

NCA have been implemented CVA in Gumuruk to distribute Hygiene NFI using Redrose platform last year. The cash was distributed through E-voucher (Plastic card with electronic chips inside) to the targeted vulnerable households that could able them to access the items from the selected local vendors’ shops. the freedom of the beneficiaries to decide on their need what and when to get it. In addition, NCA have witnessed the impact of the intervention as a stimulant of the local economy and functionality of the market due to the new money ejected.The potential problem of price inflation has controlled as NCA will sign an agreement with the selected vendors including the maximum price of each items under the normal circumstances as it is revealed with the market price survey prior to the intervention starts. Again, on our discussions with Women and Girls has shown that there are no barriers to receiving hygiene materials from shops and that cash vouchers are generally safe. The distances and routes for right holders’ access to markets has undergone and In order to ensure a more resilient way to access sanitation materials as well as access to soap and other sanitary products, we have determined that a cash and voucher system is most ideal in this areas. Traders are happy to assure of a steady resupply of goods based on market demand and have a price range that does not fluctuate beyond a certain threshold. In Tonj East, we have similarly carried out an assessment and analysis of needs, including function of markets survey as well as a protection survey with right holders and we have determined that it is possible to provide soap, sanitary materials, hand washing facilities from local traders. The existence of money transfer services and accessibility of the area for monitoring shows that we would provide better services and ensure sustainability of interventions by using cash and vouchers. Overall, we have determined that using cash and vouchers will give right holders better value and choices and that we will be able to deliver our responses more quickly, with better value and more appropriately compared to in-kind assistance.NCA presented the successful CVA project to the national CWG in January 2022. NCA believe the same modality can be scaled up in Tonj East and Tonj North where there is reduced functional market in addition to Gumuruk market that we have operated CVA successfully in 2021.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

NCA has conducted market feasibility assessment in Pibor, and Tonj East between n October 2021 and January 2022, respectively.At the design stage, we considered issues of access particularly in wet season where costs of transportation of humanitarian materials becomes difficult due to bad roads. We considered the challenges related to costs for transport as well as costs and risks associated with in kind donations and also its effects and impact on local businesses. We considered the existing markets and their ability to supply some of the goods that we transport from big town e.g., Juba and Wau. We considered the importance of promoting local bussineses, the importance of not disrupting the function of markets by introducing external goods which are already available from the markets, We considered the need for sustainable access to WASH services as well as the need to build resilience of communities and markets as well as issues and factors for safety, market supplies and costs. We considered that markets get disrupted by road access constraint but also considered that local businesses are more resilient and more adaptive to shocks. We considered the impact of the economic crisis and how humanitarian responses can also play a role in supporting markets for delivering goods.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Our monitoring processes are undertaken regularly with monthly and quarterly field monitoring. While our evaluation programmes are undertaken annually. We also make our complaints response and feedback mechanism feedback part and parcel of our programme monitoring framework. We use participatory approaches and processes to ensure that the data collected has input from all sectors of the community we serve and also ensure that every individual opinion counts. We use a variety of tools to ensure that we have effective and structured community engagement so that we can obtain information that will help us to get an needs and concerns of women, girls, boys, displaced, hosts, returnees, elderly, disabled and other special interest groups but also representative. These include focused group discussions, Key informant interviews, and household surveys to engage community to provide us information on what is important to them and what they feel they need addressed or to provide feedback on how they feel our programme are going. Information obtained from right holders will be used to improve our delivery, ensure assistance is delivered in a way that the community feels benefits them. We will use the same information to reflect on our systems, approaches and strategies and adapt them to be more responsive to community needs and to make our programmes better and more targeted and of high quality.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We will hire volunteers and staff from within the project sites. This will help us to be able to communicate better with our beneficiaries. We will develop materials for information and dissemination. We will use our own security guidelines and SOPs to ensure adherence to agreed risk mitigation to prevent and void risk on staff, partners and community members. NCA has established diversified feedback and response mechanisms that are safe, confidential, culturally acceptable and accessible to all including persons with disabilities. NCA has established community protection committees, Water management committees, which the communities can use to complain about program quality and any sensitive feedback is channelled to our field safeguarding team and safely and confidentially escalated to the Juba safeguarding team for response. NCA often receives feedback from survivors during the counselling sessions and during monitoring visits by the M& E teams. NCA communicates the availability of these channels through visual aid such as posters, verbal announcements, and group discussion. sessions to capture diversified audience, including children, illiterate, and persons with disability. Messages are adopted to the local languages of the contexts.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Staff will be made aware about their expected conduct while working at the community level. All NCA and partners staff will be trained in different polices on code of conduct and safeguarding children, CHS and humanitarian principles. Our staff and also partner staff are made aware of these policies and commit to abide with them by signing a staff code of conduct upon employment or deployment and they also undergo training on the same. Any breaches to the code of conduct will be addressed in the manner prescribed in the HR and employment terms. Community members their leaders will be informed about their right to complain if any member of the staff or of partner staffs does not uphold their values, breach any policies according to the expectations of the community and according to the employment guidelines and policies. Community members and structures within the communities we serve will be provided with avenues for reporting any breaches and complaints of misconduct. NCA recognizes our responsibility to create a safe and abuse-free environment and in doing so, will treat anonymous complaints seriously because of the potential for future abuse and harm. Therefore, every possible effort will be made to investigate the allegation and satisfactorily address any evidence of abuse, misconduct or malpractice. All NCA employees are requested to report allegations, concerns and suspicion of breaches related to abuse, exploitation and fraud and corruption. Deliberate non-disclosure will lead to disciplinary action. Mechanisms have been established for whistle blowing as well as for Complaints response mechanism which will help us to monitor and act on any breaches

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NCA has a very deliberate policy of community engagement at every stage of our programme cycle and this includes at various stages. For instance, at the inception stage, we will hold community sensitization sessions with community members through a variety of approaches including focussed group discussions with key informants, leaders, religious leaders, representatives of minorities and people living with disabilities, women, youth and other gender groups. This approach will help us to understand the issues and needs and gaps within the community and among the various gender groups. We will use this information to carry out a participatory and focused assessment of needs and to map vulnerability which will help us to design our programme. The information from this process will be fed back to the community and their views incorporated. We also involve and consult community members. When we need volunteers or local staff to work on projects at the community, we as the community members to identify and provider names for volunteers to work on programmes. At the monitoring and evaluation stage, we try to ensure that in all these stages of the programme that all members of the community are consulted to provide input and feedback on how the project components has affected them positively or negatively. Our accountability to affected populations strategy is based on NCAs Complaints response management strategy. NCA system at the community level involves programme staffs and community members having dialogue at the initial stages to identify how community would like to provide feedback or receive it. Community members discuss a range of options that may include email & telephone, complaints register, suggestion box, face to face or community meetings or a mix of these options. We then set up meeting with community members to discuss ways of setting up the system at the community level once the most preferred system is outlined and discussing on how it will operate. In many of the community settings, electronic systems are not possible, and communities often choose face to face meetings and complaints boxes and complaints registers. The practicalities of the system in relation to local set up is discussed. The key issues discussed include what is a valid complaint and how to handle sensitive complaints, what are sensitive and non-sensitive complaints and how they are handled. The complaints and feedback mechanism is incorporated into the NCA monitoring framework. Issues Once system has been discussed and developed, NCA, Partners and the community agree on a monitoring framework for AAP and then integrated to the NCA CRM. All complaints (sensitive and non-sensitive are registered and forwarded to the NCA focal team on CRM. This teams ensure that each and every complaint is action on, addressed and feedback provided based on agreed feedback mechanisms. To receive complaints, the community will appoint a focal person to register the complaint. We use these engagements to also provide feedback and receive back from community and we use this information to address concerns but also to ensure that the community is at the centre of our response actions. Normal community meetings are also undertaken to engage community members during normal course of programme implementation. This helps to improve our engagement with community. Data collected during monitoring and evaluation sessions is also shared with community members, leaders and stakeholders as part of feedback and to also see how their input was reflected. Information we collect from our monitoring evaluation is analysed to inform us on whether we are on track and if needed to carry out adjustments and for learning. We also use evaluation feedback for learning
