Project Summary

The project aims to provide integrated services to enhance resilience and protection from fire risks, and protection risks and to improve the self-resilience and empowerment of IDPs for better governance/participatory management of camps and IDP settlements, especially for women, adolescent girls, the elderly, and people with disabilities in NW Syria, sub-districts of Dana, Maaret Tamsrin in Idleb and subdistrict of Azaz in Aleppo.
The project will target 60 IDP sites (also targeted by other shelter and protection SDI projects to ensure complementarity) with 11000 HHs, 70,000 individuals, primarily women and children, roughly disaggregated as 15500 men, 16500 women, 19000 boys, and 19000 girls.
Based on the needs assessment, the selection of sites will be made according to the needs and availability of HLP rights in coordination with SNFI and CCCM clusters, local authorities, and camp management. HLP due diligence will be conducted according to relevant cluster guidelines.
SDI will be keen to adhere to IASC standards and international principles about GBV, PSEA, and the inclusion and accessibility of people with disabilities. The project will ensure the formation of IDP committees in these sites, including the participation of women and PWDs. Coordination with stakeholders and committees will be maintained throughout the project cycles to ensure active participation and accountability. Camp management and different committees in the camps will receive awareness-raising on safety & security, complaint mechanism, GBV, and PSEA in addition to camp management and cross-cutting issues through sessions, training, and banners, which will be available to all camp residents. A multi-sectoral gap analysis will be conducted and submitted regularly to the CCCM cluster.
To ensure the safety of IDPs and enhance protection against fire incidents that might happen on a large or small scale. The intervention will focus on two aspects:
1. Increase the awareness of IDPs residing in those camps on safe practices against small to large-scale fire incidents, causes, and harmful effects; the targeted age/sex groups will include children and women.
2. Set up fire units within targeted camps and equip them with necessary tools (fire kits); in addition to selecting one out of every 75 IDPs (total of 934 IDPs) voluntarily to be trained on firefighting fire ranging from low to medium scale by a technical team from SCD (Syrian Civil Defense).
The project will also include the care and maintenance in camps, such as clearing drainage channels, replacing/refiling fire extinguishers, and repairing latrines, lighting, water tanks, fences...etc. It will form a committee responsible for these activities during and after the project. Cash for work CFW modality will be preferred for care and maintenance activities. Cash-for-work workers will be selected from the IDPs living in the camps who are willing to participate in care and maintenance works and have the needed skills or abilities, and they will receive training and simple tools. This will enhance the ownership of the project and self-monitoring among IDPs.
The project will be handed over to existing camp management/committees upon project closure after ensuring that the site management is acquainted with accountability and humanitarian principles, site management, care and maintenance in IDP sites, safety & security, and existing CCCM Syria Cross-Border cluster tools and guidelines and provide open channels with CCCM to keep regular reporting of needs, ISIMM, displacement figures and other essential information requested by CCCM through the training provided by SDI.
The SDI plan for the 2025 budget amounts to approximately the same amount as the 2024 budget. It aims to provide similar activities and target the same number of beneficiaries.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In NWS, 1.7 million people live in 1,526 IDP sites lacking adequate shelter, water, food, and health support. 40% of NWS IDPs require better camp management, and around 80% face critical population density issues, making them vulnerable to floods, fires, and high-speed winds. Most IDP sites lack proper management, infrastructure, and services, leaving them in inadequate shelters, and that increases the risk of diseases and GBV. HLP challenges persist, especially impacting women, children, and PWDs.SDI's project in Syria will involve coordination with stakeholders, partner organizations, and clusters to ensure comprehensive participation and assistance. Conduct HLP DD to protect owners' and IDPs' rights. IDP representative committees will be established with diverse membership to enhance protection and participation through awareness-raising and training. Fire safety systems will be implemented, including training fire wardens and distributing extinguishers. Care and maintenance activities will involve IDPs in infrastructure upkeep through CFW programs. Various CFM will ensure accountability and beneficiary satisfaction.HLP violations, hindrances to activities, or beneficiary eviction pose a moderate likelihood but high impact risk. Mitigation involves conducting HLP DD. Child labor risks have a high likelihood and impact, mitigated through zero tolerance policies and safety equipment provisions in contracts. Lack of participation and accountability to beneficiaries has a moderate likelihood and high effect risk, mitigated by involving representative committees, including women and PWDs, and implementing an effective complaint mechanism. GBV risks have a moderate likelihood and high impact, mitigated through beneficiary awareness and staff training on GBV referral pathways.There is a moderate likelihood but high impact risk of PSEA. Mitigation strategies include awareness to SDI staff, contractors, camp management, and committees about GBV, PSEA, and complaint mechanisms. A code of conduct, signed by all staff, includes zero-tolerance policies for GBV and PSEA. Large banners displaying PSEA information and complaint numbers will be visible in camps, along with zero-tolerance posters for staff. Feedback mechanisms will include hotlines, emails, and complaint boxes. Female staff will be present in various positions within the team to ensure comprehensive support and awareness of GBV referral pathways.Assumption: relies on the availability of materials, workshops, and skilled/non-skilled workers.R1 highlights potential unavailability hindering activities, with a very low likelihood but high impact, mitigated through assessments and wider procurement. R2 involves the risk of injuries during construction, mitigated through awareness, warning signs, and safety measures. R3 involves low-quality work, mitigated through close follow-up and quality checks.
