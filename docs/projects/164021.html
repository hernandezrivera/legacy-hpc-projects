Project Summary

The main objective of the proposed project is to protect the health of vulnerable and war affected people by providing WASH assistance to vulnerable IDPs, returnees, host communities and school aged children in areas with high rates of displacement and large-scale destruction. The consequences of the conﬂict have been particularly severe and have converted many parts of the governorates into hard-to- reach areas, where people suffer from extreme needs, due to the enormous destruction, the lack of public services and the inability to access local markets. To support displaced families and also focus on war affected children, the project will contribute to increase their access to suﬃcient quantities of portable water for people’s and student’s daily consumption, to ensure their access to safe and digniﬁed sanitation facilities, disseminate hygiene knowledge and create a clean environment. Therefore, the intervention will include the provision of water trucking and the rehabilitation of water and sewage network, the rehabilitation of existing WASH-facilities in schools, hygiene awareness trainings for girls and boys in schools and garbage collection and fumigation campaigns and rehabilitation of pump stations. Those measures will serve to cover the daily water consumption, improve sanitation habits and reduce the risk of waterborne and other diseases.

4 - Needs

After nine years of Syria crisis of conﬂict and violence have caused extreme suffering, devastated many cities and regions in the country and destroyed large parts of the basic infrastructure. Almost 70% of the population lives in extreme poverty. 13.1 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. The conflict in Syria has created and exacerbated large-scale humanitarian needs around water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) for the country’s population that around 3.7 million people are in acute need of WASH assistance in Syria. Up to 40% of the population is relying on unsafe water sources to meet daily water supply needs. Access to basic water supply remains very limited and drinking water is mainly operated through private water trucking, Reports have also been received of severe cases of bloody diarrhea in affected areas like Deir-ez-zor according to REACH 2019. Displaced families and returnees in Deir-ez-zor, Daraa, Hama and Ar-raqqa have limited access to safe and continuous water resources which makes it extremely hard for them to sustain basic hygiene practices. The needs for water supply are massive and require an emergency water trucking to protect the people’s health and reduce the risk of waterborne diseases. Lack of water supply in areas of conﬂict affected communities in hard to reach areas remain with no connection to public garbage disposal services, lack of official landfills and outbreak of random landfills, therefore garbage collection and fumigation campaigns are needed to create a clean environment and reduce the risk of epidemic outbreaks. destructed sewage networks and down pump stations affect the daily life of people specially for farmers. Large number of schools have suffered severe destruction and are unable to provide basic WASH-services to their students. Affected and displaced school-aged girls and boys are in need of adequate WASH in their schools to ensure access to minimal amount of water and maintain good hygiene routines.

5 - Implementation of activities

RSRP will secure a constant water supply through water trucking in communities with no access to public WASH-services, and to ensure access to safe water RSRP will rehabilitate water networks in addition to the rehabilitation of sewage networks to ensure its safe disposal and prevent waterborne diseases. Garbage collection and fumigation campaigns will be executed in areas with no access to public garbage-disposal services. Garbage containers will be provided and installed in the neighborhoods. Installation of pump stations to secure water for agricultural lands. Conducting capacity building for the team on hygiene promotion as community contribution to enhance sanitation and safe environment and the increase of good hygiene practices. RSRP will rehabilitate schools’ WASH facilities. The works will comprise the restoration of the roof, walls, windows and doors,toilets, sinks, water taps, and doors will be fixed or replaced. As students will receive a one-time hygiene awareness training of basics of a good hygiene routine and the correct use of hygiene products. RSRP will implement and monitor the Wash-interventions in coordination with the local community, local authorities and the WASH cluster, to identify needs, determine the appropriate implementation plan with respect to the local cultural norms and traditions and to avoid any duplications with other implementing partners. The accountability to the affected population will be guaranteed throughout the entire project cycle through the establishing of a complaint’s mechanism and the regular evaluation of feedback. The monitoring and evaluation process will signicantly contribute to the project’s compliance with the 4 core humanitarian principles. All the above-mentioned measures and processes will be translated in assessments, surveys, monitoring and evaluation reports and GPS embedded photos that will be shared with donors, partners and relevant clusters to participate in the humanitarian coordination process.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Reduced access for women and girls to the facilities (toilets represent GBV risk) and trainings due to culturally inappropriate design. Mitigation Measure 1: Ensure the alignment of all facilities to SPHERE standards. Install gender speciﬁc sanitation facilities; install doors and locks on each facility, and every toilet cabin for privacy. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Use of male and female hygiene oﬃcers to conduct the trainings. Protection Risk 2: Reduced access for disabled people/children to the assistance. Mitigation Measure 2: Identiﬁcation and inclusion of disabled persons and their speciﬁc needs into the project planning and implementation. Installation of handicapped accessible facilities. Protection Risk 3: Unequal distribution of services. Other vulnerable communities are excluded. Mitigation Measure 3: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Include community and authorities in targeting. Protection Risk 4: Misconduct by humanitarian workers while realizing the trainings. Mitigation Measure 4: Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection and develop strategies to address the risks of GBV during all project-stages. Selection of competent and professional hygiene oﬃcers and signing of a code of conduct by all humanitarian workers. Establishment of an effective complaint mechanism.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Access limitations could prevent the humanitarian actors to provide assistance to the affected population in a timely manner. – LOW Mitigation Measure 1: Constant monitoring of the security situation on the ground and capacity to mobilize all resources into another project area if required. Risk 2: Threat to the safety of RSRP ﬁeld-staff through confrontations between armed groups in the project region. – MED Mitigation Measure 2: Application of RSRP-internal (SOP) and daily evaluation of the security situation. Risk 3: Tension between included and excluded students and school-staff might lead to conﬂict between target groups and complaints against the humanitarian partner. – HIGH Mitigation measure 3: Clear and comprehensible communication of the selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneﬁciaries. Risk 4: Signiﬁcant population growth in the target areas leading to insuﬃcient assistance. – HIGH Mitigation measure 4: Consider feedback from monitoring reports and situational overviews of the area and coordinate regular coordination meetings with local authorities for further and more detailed up-dates.Risk 5: Delayed, denied or revoked GOS approvals which prevent delivering the assistance in a timely manner or prevent implementation of the project. Likelihood: High Mitigating Measures 5: Transparency with local authorities in the areas of intervention, in addition to strong communication and collaboration prior to project implementation with local partner. Risk 6: Unavailability of materials on the local markets due to closure of the border for commercial shipments or not renewal of SC resolution. - MEDIUM Mitigation Measure 6: Detailed market analysis in the area of intervention and development of contingency plan. Risk 7: Depletion of the market and inﬂation of the prices. - MEDIUM Mitigation Measure 7: Price monitoring and market assessments done by the RSRP ﬁnance and logistics department.
