Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the food gaps of households and reduce the chances of them from moving to unsustainable coping mechanisms occasioned by lack of livelihood opportunities. These activities will reduce food-deficit of target households thus reducing cases of malnutrition, negative coping mechanisms, and violence arising from competing for limited basic recourses. The project will ensure target beneficiaries have the basic resources to access food during the humanitarian phase of the project as part of its life-saving approach through cash assistance and IYCF activities.
ZOA practices both continuity and complementarity in its projects. This emergency cash project aims to continue serving the most vulnerable populations in Borno and will contribute to sustain efforts by ZOA
Project activities.
The cash programming will also be complimented with sessions for mothers and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) and dietary diversity to ensure the proper preparation and utilization of nutritious meals. Further, these IYCF and dietary diversity sessions will be highlighted with handwashing demonstrations and hygiene. The complementarity of targeted vulnerable populations in the same local government area allows for the synergy of the interventions. sensitization on hygiene prevents water borne illnesses in the larger population allowing food insecure targeted populations to proceed with household food strategies less susceptible to illness.
The key drivers of food insecurity have expanded beyond conflict over the years, affecting Local Government Areas (LGAs) differently. LGAs with chronic vulnerabilities and active conflict have been affected more severely by the socio-economic consequences, which disrupted supply chains and has also led to food price increases and has worsened tensions. Households deriving their income from unstable and precarious sources of income - such as day/wage laborers, small traders and those dependent on remittances or family support as well as farmers or breeders - reported greater reductions in revenue compared to other household categories with regular incomes. The erratic weather pattern has also resulted to flooding or droughts. This have affected agricultural activities and by extension food security.

The activities will contribute towards increasing household food security and laying a foundation for recovering means of income generation. • Activities will include humanitarian cash assistance, support for agriculture activities, and extension services, savings and credit opportunities through VSLA, empowerment activities through gender and protection initiatives, activities related to supporting IYCF and foundational support learners to attend school through food aid grants for learners.

2 - Activities

ZOA will provide monthly cash assistance to 16,600 individuals (2,767HH). The cash assistance is meant to provide resources during the lean season, as per the Food Security Sector recommendation for food for Biu, Monguno and Bama, ZOA proposes to support the families with 70% of MEB/ person/ month during the lean seasons in 2023 for the purchase of food and cooking energy. The regular practice adapted according to the Food Security Sector by humanitarian agencies in this area is to provide 70% of the MEB. ZOA, also aims to simultaneously start the early recovery initiative and support for food security/livelihood interventions for the same households. The money will provide households with resources during this period of food scarcity and high food prices. The lean season is the time most households report resorting to negative coping mechanisms. These mainly include reducing the number of meals and eating less nutritious food. ZOA will use the RedRose platform where principal cash recipients from households will be issued with biometric cards for offline cash disbursement. They will use the cards to redeem money from cash agents employed by ZOA. ZOA integrates Infant and Young Child Feeding activities as part of humanitarian assistance. The IYCF sessions provide beneficiaries with skills in dietary diversity, especially in breastfeeding and complementary feeding. These sessions have significantly reduced malnutrition cases among households with lactating mothers. These sessions with be facilitated by community nutrition animators. The sessions also present an opportunity to engage men with their children through the father groups. ZOA proposes early recovery activities to build the resilience of beneficiaries. The recovery activities will present an opportunity for more long-term engagements which are more sustainable beyond the scope of the project. The following activities will comprise recovery interventions:i. Assistance for micro gardensii. Assistance for Commercial agricultureiii. Assistance for Livestock packagesiv. Agriculture extension services v. Non-Agriculture (Business) start-up kitsvi. Business Skills Trainingvii. establishment of Village savings and loan groupsviii. Training on Financial literacy
