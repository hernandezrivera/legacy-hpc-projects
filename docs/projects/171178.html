Project Summary

Recent FSNAU assessments reveal that there is a dire need to gear the humanitarian efforts towards addressing the risk of malnutrition and similarly the projections done earlier in 2020 showed that malnutrition levels were above emergency threshold level in most of the parts of the country. correspondingly the national median GAM prevalence in children was reported to be around 13.8% and this translates to 0.96 million boys and girls. Out of this 162,000 are affected by life threatening severely acute malnutrition while around 800,000 are moderately acute malnourished and at the same time the programmatic data shows that of the total admissions, 54% are girls. In addition to children under five years of age, a further 571,159 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance. These vulnerable
population groups are more exposed to the risks of malnutrition due to the prevailing; COVID 19 pandemic, continued floods and desert locus. COVID 19 pandemic had an adverse effect on the service delivery whereby access of population of health and nutrition facilities was compromised. 2020 post GU results show favorable outcomes yet for nutrition it is estimated that in 2021 around 2.3 million people (1.14 million for treatment services and 1.16 million for preventive services) in Somalia will require both lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition support and this number includes children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women and girls as well. IDPs are prone to vulnerability due to being displaced either as a result of conflict and/or food shortages. Additionally, PLWs and children in IDP camps are more prone and therefore vulnerable to the consequences and impacts of the lack of access to basic livelihood and other social sector facilities such as primary health care services, access to safe drinking water and sanitation services.
In the proposed response for the year 2021, Juba foundation will work toward the Provision of equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases. The project will be engaged in active case identification of cases through screening by the health workers as well as the trained mothers who will be offering the mother MUAC identification and referrals to the health facilities.
JF through the other supplementary nutrition intervention; IYCF, micronutrient supplementation, iron folate and vitamin A will work to strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on improving nutritional status through provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions. JF will adhere to the response plan objective of ensuring that protection is provided to the targeted beneficiaries and no form of exploitation of the beneficiaries will be tolerated. JF has in place clear PSEA framework, stakeholders will be trained on the steps to follow incases of any violation. All the staff within the program will be trained on PSEA and the organization will have all beneficiaries sensitized on reporting cases.
JF will work towards strengthening referral pathways for both nutritional services especially on complicated SAM cases as well as on the cases of PSEA.
JF will actively work with other stakeholders in the targeted district, national nutrition cluster as well as the MOH to ensure that clear coordination is obtained. Regular meetings with the stakeholders will be carried out which JF representative will as well be present. JF has had active presence in the proposed regions with each one of these areas having a focal point representative and an office. We have actively participated in all the assessments done both in multi-agency as well as independent assessments.
