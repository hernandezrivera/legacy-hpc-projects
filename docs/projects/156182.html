Project Summary

The project will address the urgent health needs of both IDP and Host Communities in Idlep governorate & Aleppo governorate / Efrin district and Western Aleppo with an emphasis on continuity of current projects and ensure available medical services through providing equipment, medications, supplies and operational costs (salaries, fuel, etc.) for Two Trauma hospitals, Four maternity and child hospitals, 8 primary health care facilities, Four mobile clinics, One dialysis center and provision of immunization services through fixed and mobile teams in addition to strengthening health system by implementing referral system and providing capacity building trainings on IPC, NCD management and pharmacy management.

Needs

Programming proposed is based on the analysis of the following Assessments,-Multi sectoral Rapid Assessment Report for - South Idleb, December 2017-January 2018, by SRD-Protection and Health - need assessment, Syria - Idleb governorate, KAFR NOBOL - KAFR WEIDHAS, August 2017, by SRD-Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018, HNO 2018 report, HNO 2019 draft.-RNA 2018 OCHA-Multi sectoral Rapid Assessment Report for South Idleb, November 2018, by SRDwhich stated that the Key health needs and priorities are “Access to primary health care through an Essential Package of Primary Health Services–to cover essential health care needs of vulnerable populations, Maintenance of medicines’ supply chain for treatment of trauma, obstetric care, infectious diseases, and chronic conditions, Access to Reproductive health services and child health care.SRD has conducted Health and Protection Needs Assessment. The data was collected through Surveys, Key Informant Interviews, Clinic Assessments and Focus Group Discussions in Aleppo (Jebel Saman sub district ; Atareb community ) Idleb (Idleb sub district; Saraqeb community , AlMa'ra; Ma'arrat An Nu'man and Kafr Nobol communities and ARiha subdistrict;Ehsam community).The analysis showed that the most important health problems highlighted by the discussion group were Lack of medicines, Lack of financial and operational support to health centers, and the communicable diseases as the most critical factors contributing to poor health outcomes requiring interventions by the actors in Health Sector .According to -Afrin Rapid Needs Assessment May 2018, OCHA-SRD implementing partner-Sexual and Reproductive Health Priorities in Afrin District, Syria May 2018- UNFPA, Health cluster-Health indicators report - April 2018, WHO, Health clusterThe main results were Limited availability of health facilities is the major obstacle in access to health care, the lack of medicine being the second major issue followed by lack of health staff, lack of transportation and lack of specialized services. Women have additional constrains in accessing services, most likely due to restriction/danger of movement and lack services corresponding to their specific needs. Newly accessible areas require a higher number of equipped mobile clinics/ambulances and fixed PHCs, to improve access to primary health care services. A suboptimal referral system (case by case basis rather than a systematic approach) and limitations in reaching health facilities in Aleppo are resulting in lengthy clearance procedures for the referral of sick and injured patients.Referral system was identified by WHO and network partner as a gap and as an important factor to insure the continuity of care across the network and to provide integrated health services to affected Host Communities (HC) and Internally Displaced People (IDPs).SRD has prepared plans to improve the accessibility to the health services by operating preexisting health facilities in order to continue providing health services, and to improve the quality of the services utilizing the expertise of SRD teams in managing and operating many primary and secondary health care facilities, as well to link those health facilities to the concrete referral pathways between SRD supported health facilities in Idleb & Aleppo governorates.

Implementation of activities

SRD planned activities are in line with Health Sector Objectives which include: 1.Increase access to humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services for those most vulnerable and in need. 2.Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context.SRD will support existing health facilities in Idleb including 1 Trauma hospitals (Has), 3 Maternity and Child Hospitals (Alsayeda Mariam in Kafr Nobol, Harim, Saraqab), 5 PHCs (Maar Tahroma, Afs, Kafruma, Rakaya Sijneh and Azmarin), 2 Mobile clinics (Saraqab and Ehsem) and immunization services through 3 mobile teams and 4 fixed center (Saraqab, Kafr Nobol, Maar Tahroma and Azmarin).in Western Aleppo SRD will support 1 Trauma hospitals(Bagdad in Kafr Karmin), 1 Maternity and Child Hospitals (Alferdos Daret Azza), 3 PHCs (Kafr Taal, Hur and Abin Samaan), 2 Mobile clinics (Kafr Hamra and Alatareb), 1 Dialysis center in Daret Azza and immunization services through 3 mobile teams and 2 fixed centers (Daret Azza and Hur).in Afrin SRD will support 1 maternity and child hospital in Afrin city and 2 PHCs (Jandairis and Kafr safra) and immunization services through 1 mobile team and 1 fixed center in Jandairis.the planned support for all above HFs including salaries, running costs, trainings and other needs for the provision of primary and secondary health services including minor & major surgeries, deliveries, c-section, ANC, PNC, as well as in patient services. A set of medical equipment is available at the health facilities.SRD will continue referral system in coordination with WHO, and Idleb HD and other operating NGOs. SRD will maintain current referral 4 field teams. The teams will continue capacity building and supportive supervision visits for 125 HFs on referral system tools.Referral team will conduct 3 days training for new HFs' focal points joining referral network and regular refresher training on referral system tools including forms, registers, SOPs and flowcharts, Teams will maintain the coordination mechanisms between primary and secondary HFs within referral network as well as authorities and partner NGOs.SRD will provide all HFs with needed forms. To ensure access and continuity of care within referral system, SRD will operate 20 Ambulances for emergency patients' transportation and 20 non-emergency patient transportation vehicles with specific schedule of movement between referral HFs.SRD will continue to strengthen health systems in coordination with WHO office in Gaziantep by providing capacity building activities regarding infection control, NCD management, pharmacy management and first aid training of paramedics.Part of mentioned activities are funded during 2019 including: 1 hospital, 2 PHCs, Referral System in Idleb and Efrin until Nov 2019. 1 hospital, 2 PHCs and 3 MCs until 15 May 2019. 1 PHC and 1 MC until 15 Apr 2019. SRD is seeking fund opportunities for all activities. SRD has good relationships with Hatay HD and local HDs. SRD field offices will monitor activities on the ground. SRD has mapped out planned services in accordance with existing collaborative sites that have been in place for months. This information has crosschecked with the OCHA Health Cluster along with other peer agencies.SRD planned activities will meet specific needs to enable access for people with disabilities to health services in accordance to their specific situation. SRD will modify its HFs to ensure access for all beneficiaries with disability including ramp entrance with handles, WCs for disabled people and trained its staff to provide services to patients with disabilities.From GBV perspective, SRD will ensure the integration of GBV in Health services as there will be Women Waiting area within SRD HFs, ensure that SRD HFs has available Females staff and training HFs staff on GBV principles. in addition to that, provide CMR services when needed.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declined significantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas, with a long term effect hampering the scale up effort in newly accessible areas that witnessed recent change of control. Mitigating measures:- Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG), and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. - Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase.Gender Based Violence GBV:Health services are often the first, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical that health providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Mitigating measures:- Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package.- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behavior for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff.- Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programs it is implementing and what they intend to deliver.- Design and implement appropriate programs based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security in Idleb and western Aleppo could affect access in implementing area, in case of bombing by Syrian armed forces and its alliances or by internal conflict of opposition’s armed groups. In case of skirmishes and limited clashes, the possibility of occurrence is moderate and the impact is low then temporary and limited activities suspension is expected. In case of bombing and airstrikes, the possibility of occurrence is low and the impact is low, and in this regard temporary and limited activities suspension is expected. That will be at low risk in Afrin district SRD is able to response immediately (time sensitive)
