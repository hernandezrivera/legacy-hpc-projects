Project Summary

This project aims to support vulnerable individuals overcome barriers to accessing healthcare in Lebanon, while also supporting Lebanon’s ailing health system against pressures linked to deteriorating economic conditions and against the continued challenges by different health outbreaks. The project will provide essential health services across the continuum of care. This will include secondary health care and advanced diagnostics, building the capacity of health care workers, community health support through community-based interventions, and support to POEs in response to health emergencies. All interventions will be closely coordinated with MOPH, WHO, the Health and Migrant sectors, and active partners. The project will support migrants’ safe and equitable access to secondary healthcare based on lifesaving, and limb saving criteria, by contracting two hospitals in migrant dense areas, where IOM will be fully covering the hospitalization bill of migrants. This activity will be done through an implementing partner, building on previous cooperation with SAMs, and will involve other local NGOs active in secondary healthcare support. Advanced diagnostic for migrants and vulnerable communities will be supported through the selection of PHCs providing these services and based on migrant dense areas. A network of community mobilizers selected from the catchment area of the PHCs, will be engaged in accordance with the sector guidance and equipped with the necessary trainings and materials to deliver quality, integrated health messaging and link beneficiaries to the available health services. IOM will work in close cooperation with a network of Migrant community leaders and engage them in trainings and awareness raising activities, in addition to, the translation of awareness materials to different migrant languages. Translation will help overcome language barriers to knowledge and accessibility of health services. Under the response to outbreak section, Building on IOM’s active work with the national international health regulation “IHR” focal point to assess the POE’s in regard to health emergency prevention and response measures and the gaps identified, IOM will support 5 key POEs: Masnaa, Qaa, Aboudiyeh, Aarida, Bokayaa. IOM will strengthen MoPH capacity at the said POEs through deployment of health staff where needed, in addition to the development and implementation of relevant emergency response plans and a training of trainers for non-health actors. IPC trainings for all frontliners are planned and supported under the C19RM successful application, that will be channeled through the Middle East Response funded by Global Fund which IOM is the primary recipient. Building on IOM’s successful experience and the request of multiple partners and noting that many partners are new to migrant programming, IOM aims to provide a cultural sensitization training to health care workers. The main objective is to render the services migrant friendly and accessible to this vulnerable group.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

IOM will support migrants secondary health care, based on life and limb saving criteria. IOM will contract two hospitals: with consideration on migrant dense areas and hospital’s operational capacity. Support will be via implementing partner, such as SAMs, and other local NGOs. IOM will cover full hospitalization without any patient share. IOM will deploy a network of community volunteers, equipped with the necessary trainings and materials to deliver quality, integrated health messaging. These will work closely with a network of migrant community leaders to support culturally sensitive approaches toward migrants and vulnerable communities. Furthermore, information, education, and communication materials will be translated into different migrant languages. Community mobilization and outreach activities to increase access to information regarding PHC services available and raise awareness on public health topics of common interest to the community (Vaccination, NCDs, TB etc.) IOM will support two selected POEs by deployment of two health staff responsible for screening and awareness activities at designated POEs. In direct coordination with MOPH IOM will provide TOT sessions for non- Health staff at POEs on infection prevention control (IPC) and RING (Recognize, Isolate, Notify and Give support) trainings for non-health staff at POEs. Medical equipment will be provided to five land crossings, IPC to government hospital staff, cultural sensitivity trainings for PHC staff.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

All IOM staff are PSEA-trained and obliged to follow code of conduct. PSEA awareness also delivered to target groups. Information on the complaint mechanism is shared with all beneficiaries through visuals posted in service delivery points, and through migrant community leaders. IOM health and protection workers collaborate in referring any protection risks among target individuals, such as hospitalized cases covered by IOM where additional support needed to provide a complementary service to the beneficiary. Protection mainstreaming will enhance the response and improve detection and referral. Activities under this project will be gender and culturally sensitive. IOM will use network of volunteers, community leaders, consulates, to mitigate language barriers. IOM staff also have mental health contacts, plus wellbeing counsellors. Regular workshops take place in IOM to provide support to staff.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IOM uses following AAP channels. These include:1) Face to face: All IOM staff receive complaints/feedback/suggestions from the affected population and channeling those to the assigned FP at head office.2) Comment box: Dedicated comment box in all of IOM offices and centers that ought to be opened at least on bi-weekly basis by the PSEA/AAP Officer. 3) Helpdesk (walk-ins): Part of walk-ins in Beirut office. Facilitated by two IOM staff trained and inducted on handling and referring complaints/feedback/suggestions to the right channels.4) Consultations with beneficiaries/migrant-community leaders: Important step to build the trust with migrants and ensure confidentiality of sensitive complaints, appropriate follow-up, and steps taken. It could be through the selection of representatives of key community members, community volunteers, community-led organizations. 5) Dedicated email address: lbecomplaint@iom.int6) We Are All In Platform
