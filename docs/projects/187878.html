Project Summary

IOM aims to contribute to effective coordination and the UN's localization efforts by building the capacity of implementing partners. As the humanitarian situation in northwest Syria remains grave with increasing hostilities, a deteriorating humanitarian situation, and new displacements, the capacity of humanitarian actors in northwest Syria is stretched thin after 10 years of conflict. It is also possible that in the event of non-renewal of the UNSCR, some UN agencies may be forced to scale down or adapt their cross-border activities. INGOs and NGOs would then be required to significantly scale up their operations in order to continue supporting affected communities. INGOs and NGOs will be expected to maintain the delivery of quality humanitarian response activities and ensure effective coordination. As such, it is paramount to continue building the capacity of INGO and NGO partners across technical and cross-cutting topics.
In 2021, IOM supported the capacity development of its partners and cluster partners through trainings, coaching, mentoring, learning exchange workshops, and improving the partner’s institutional process and procedures through reviewing, and developing guidance documents, SoPs and checklists. Topics varied from project cycle management, finance, procurement, humanitarian access negotiation, and Cash-Based Intervention (CBI). In addition to those training, IOM published COVID-19 AAP videos and COVID-19 vaccination videos to support the information sharing, awareness-raising effort towards COVID-19 by the COVID-19 task force and the Health cluster.
The need for building the organizational capacity of implementing partners remains critical. This has been reflected in the different joint capacity need assessments IOM regularly conducts with its partners. IPs consistently highlighted the need for more capacity-building sessions on administrative areas such as Governance, Human Resources, Compliance, Programme Development and Reporting to improve their institutional process and procedures in resource mobilization and management. Furthermore, IOM aims to build the capacity of implementing partners and Cluster partners on sector-specific topics and cross-cutting topics such as community engagement, humanitarian principle, access negotiation and disability inclusion.
Building on its capacity development activities in 2021, IOM will organize around 64 trainings, consultation and coaching sessions on humanitarian access negotiation, human resource management, governance, compliance, and trainings on WASH-related topics in 2022 and 2023. IOM will facilitate a Training of facilitators (ToF) and Advance Certificate training on humanitarian access negotiation under the auspices of Access Working Group and OCHA for the sake of reaching in a wider group. Based on a clear workplan, the participants from the ToF will conduct peer workshops on humanitarian access and negotiation in northwest Syria reaching field humanitarian staff. In addition to the above trainings, IOM will also support the partners’ development of organizational and operational policy and procedures. All activities will be coordinated with relevant Clusters, Technical Working Groups, and other coordination fora.

4 - Needs

Based on the participatory Organizational Capacity Need Assessment (OCND) that IOM conducted with its implementing partners in mid-2021, IPs requested more training and coaching sessions on topics such as governance, compliance, human resource management, project development and reporting for the IPs to maintain the highest standard of humanitarian response in project management. IOM also identified the need for additional capacity in principled access negotiation based on feedback from IPs and the Access Working Group partners. Currently, the coordination on humanitarian access/negotiation is limited, especially on the field level, with IPs individually engaging with local authorities. A shared understanding of red lines and humanitarian negotiation will lead to enhanced principled humanitarian access. According to the capacity need assessment conducted by the WASH cluster to the cluster’s partners in 2021, the cluster identified priority topics such as standardizing the design of WASH facilities, WASH in Health Facilities and quarantine centers, rain harvest and groundwater recovery, wastewater treatment. IOM proposes to support the cluster by organizing capacity development activities such as, trainings, workshops, and development of guidance on topics identified by the cluster.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM is proposing to develop humanitarian partners’ organizational capacity on topics such as governance, compliance, human resource management and project development. IOM will organize 34 coaching/consultation sessions targeting 20 implementing partners. To improve partners' humanitarian access negotiation skill, IOM proposes to facilitate 2 Training of facilitators (ToF) and 2 Advance Certificate training on humanitarian access negotiation targeting 100 humanitarian workers under the auspices of Access Working Group and OCHA . The participants from the ToF will conduct peer workshops on humanitarian access and negotiation in northwest Syria reaching 150 field humanitarian staff. IOM will also organize 4 trainings to build the technical capacity of WASH cluster partners on topics such as wastewater treatment, and WASH in Health Facilities and Quarantine centers targeting 80 WASH technical humanitarian staff from different organizations. In addition to the trainings, IOM will also develop a guidance document on the two topics. To mitigate against the spread of COVID-19, IOM will continue to deliver virtual trainings, only hosting face-to-face sessions when it is feasible to do so. IOM has years of in-house expertise from working in the cross-border context and will recruit technical experts to deliver specific trainings when needed. IOM will continue to liaise with the relevant clusters and working groups to ensure proper coordination.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

IOM will ensure that protection is mainstreamed in its capacity development efforts. Protection risks in capacity development activities include the limited participation and representation of females in the trainings conducted and hence limited inputs on protection mainstreaming from participants. To overcome this limitation and increase the meaningful participation of women, protection topics will be integrated in the training curriculums across all trainings and coaching sessions. IOM will use in-house women experts to deliver capacity development activities. In the event IOM uses service providers and consultants to deliver capacity development activities, IOM will encourage female applicants to apply and will ensure that service providers and consultants are trained on PSEA. During the revision of policies and procedures of the implementing partners, IOM will closely coordinate with the PSEA network to ensure the integration of PSEA in the policies, SOPs, and procedures. Each training aims to have gender-balanced participants, with 50% females and 50% males. IOM will also gather feedback from participants on women's participation in order to improve the participation of women in future capacity development activities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IOM ensures that risk mitigation and prevention of SEA is mainstreamed in its capacity development activities, and that PSEA and other related concerns are mainstreamed throughout the capacity development activities. In order to ensure that sexual exploitation and abuse risks are properly mitigated, the capacity development team, consultants and trainers will be trained on PSEA prior to delivering trainings and consultation to implementing partner staff. IOM ensures that participants are aware of the internal reporting mechanisms to safely and confidently report complaints related to sexual and other misconduct. To mitigate PSEA and other protection risks, and whenever possible, IOM will use female in-house experts and consultants to deliver capacity development activities. The team will ensure service providers, consultants and contractors have understood and signed the humanitarian code of conduct (CoC) prior to delivering capacity development services.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

To ensure that priority needs are being met, IOM, in coordination with the NGO forum and the relevant clusters, will continue to assess the capacity development needs of IPs through regular needs assessments and will prioritize training based on the highest impact. To avoid duplication efforts from different organizations, IOM will liaise with the PWG and the relevant Clusters to capitalize on existing resources and promote localization. The identification and recruitment of qualified service providers for capacity development needs can be challenging. However, IOM, will ensure that the most qualified candidates are selected to deliver capacity-building activities. To mitigate against rapid changes in security which may impact the ability to deliver capacity development activities inside NWS, IOM will ensure that trainings are organized within a sufficiently flexible timeframe so that activities can be conducted when there are relatively stable conditions. IOM will strive for 50/50 involvement of both male and female participants in all capacity development activities and encourages qualified female trainers to deliver capacity development activities. IOM will continue web-based capacity-building activities using online platforms, videos, and e-learning courses to reduce the risk of spreading COVID-19. Whenever safe and feasible to do so, in-person training and support will be considered in line with national regulations, WHO and Health Cluster’s COVID-19 guidance.
