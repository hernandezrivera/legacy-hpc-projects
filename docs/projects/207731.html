Project Summary

The North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon are affected by a complex humanitarian crisis that has resulted in a system-wide breakdown of infrastructure and services in the most affected areas, especially in the health sector. This crisis has resulted in the displacement of over 715000 people within Cameroon and Nigeria, especially women and children (1). The Multisectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) of August 2022, revealed that 202 (18%) out of 1115 health centers in the North-West region and the South-West region were no longer functional. Most of the functional health facilities are not functional to full capacity. Attacks on healthcare are a common feature of this crisis and remain a major threat to the availability of healthcare in the North-West and the South-West regions (2). There are limited specialized services to take care of victims of physical and mental trauma in these regions as the number of cases has been on the rise because of the crisis. This situation has been worsened by the numerous outbreaks (Cholera, Measles, Yellow Fever and Monkey Pox) that have increased the burden on a health system that has already been fragilized by the humanitarian crisis. At least 18 health Districts in the NWSW were declared to have measles epidemics this year while cholera has affected 3 health districts in the South-Westregion this year.
In some of the most affected areas, health services are not available while less affected areas have health services of poor quality. This project aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of internally displaced populations (IDP) and affected host communities in the North-West and South-West regions and Support Early Recovery and Resilience in the North-West and South-West regions through the restoration and improvement of the health system’s components and core public health functions, in alignment with the principles of building back better and sustainable development of Cameroon. The project will target 373,069 IDPs persons (51% of this target are women, 16% are children under 5, and 11% are people over 60 years old). The areas of intervention will be Fako, Meme, Lebialem, Ndian, Manyu and Kupe - Muanenguba divisions in the South-West region and Mezam, Momo, Bui, Ngo-ketunjia, Mechum, Donga Mantung and Boyo in the North-West region. The project will emphasize on timely access to equitable healthcare through mobile clinics in areas with no functional health facilities and the reimbursement of medical bills in areas with functional Health Facilities. The project will also lay emphasis on supporting non-functional health facilities to restart while strengthening the capacities of some affected health facilities to improve the service offer.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The situation in the North-West region and South-West regions has remained volatile and unpredictable for the past 7 years since the onset of the crisis. The combined effect of the numerous outbreaks, and the crisis has had a negative impact on an already fragile health system. Access to essential health services remains a challenge to most of the affected communities due to the complete or partial shutdown of health facilities, the distance to the closest functional health facility, the limited service packaged offered by the health facilities, and the inability to pay for the cost of health care. The Multisectoral Needs Assessment carried out by IOM in August 2022 showed that 18% of health facilities are closed in both regions and over 368,742 people were still displaced from their homes. The persistence of insecurity is causing additional displacements in these regions. The displaced persons are at a higher risk due to poor living conditions, and the lack of access to basic services especially women and children. There is an urgent need to improve epidemiological surveillance in these regions as the ongoing outbreaks like Cholera, Measles, yellow fever and Monkey Pox might spread to other areas. It is important to strengthen the capacity of the Health Districts and Health facilities to carry out response activities during outbreaks and respond to the emergency health needs of the affected communities. Persons living with disabilities, pregnant women, and teenagers are the most vulnerable and hence they need assistance to access quality health services. Given that most of the affected populations are living under poor conditions, they are exposed to many risk factors that might increase the likelihood of illnesses. The brutal nature of the crisis has resulted in an increase in the incidence of trauma cases as a result of violence. Also, a good number of people are traumatized mentally and need mental health assistance. The project will have a mental health component to take care of the mental health needs of the affected populations.
