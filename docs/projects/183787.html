Project Summary

The proposed intervention is in line with, and contributes to 2022 HRP Strategic Objectives to provide lifesaving NFI/shelter assistance to crisis-affected population and to enable these crisis-affected populations to access adequate shelter solutions that provides protection, safety, security and space to live in a dignified manner.

Project operations in Gedaref, West and South Kordofan, will support vulnerable households affected by both natural disasters and man-made crises. Targets shall include vulnerable communities in need of adequate shelter solutions and life-saving Non-Food Items. Rains in July and September of this year (2021) has brought grave negative effects to the population in the above mentioned states (with specific regard to Kordofan areas).

Providing environmentally friendly, durable and locally sourced shelter materials and solutions will constitute the main focus of the proposed plan, working closely with key partners (i.e., INGOs, NNGOs, government entities, federal and state HAC, localities, ES/NFI cluster coordinator and partners at both federal and state levels), particularly for returnees and/or IDP’s. Sustainability of the interventions will be fostered with the aim to enable a shift from emergency settings and operational modalities, once most urgent needs are addressed. Wherever feasible and cost-effective, the plan shall explore possible options of cash provisions and/or using a voucher system to facilitate access to local shelter materials

In West Darfur, the project will continue supporting long-term IDPs which have been affected by most recent and longstanding violence outbreaks due to tribal conflicts. In West and South Kordofan, and Gadaref states, the focus will be on providing shelter and NFI support to flood victims, as well as supporting NFI/sheltering needs of host communities made vulnerable by the overall contextual insecurity.
The poor shelter conditions in temporary convergence points/locations will be targeted with emergency shelter rehabilitation kits, to be complemented by NFI support to ensure safety/security and restore dignity of beneficiaries/programme participants.

The provision of shelter and NFI materials will help vulnerable population restore their dignity and reduce their exposure to protection and health hazards. Highly vulnerable population with specific regard to (PLW) women (as well as single woman lead HHs), girls/boys, elderly and people living with disabilities constitute the primary focus of the plan. Protection will be mainstreamed across all interventions to ensure that rights of vulnerable populations are understood and secured at all levels (e.g., CRM, PDM, segregated spaces, disability/femininity friendly spaces, dignity kits among NFIs, etc.).

Concern will procure and store shelter and NFI materials as much as practically possible in the basis close or within the intervention areas. Minimum standards for Shelter and NFI kit packages will meet Sphere standards and will be harmonized with the ES/NFI cluster at both federal and state levels as per previous interventions. Synergizing activities with sectors WASH, FSL, H&N is standard in Concern operations.

1 - Needs

Insecurity continues affecting the physical safety and protection of people, particularly in West Darfur state where 105,249 people have been affected by a series of insecurity between January and September 2021 (OCHA intercommunal armed attacks and conflicts between January and September 2021). 5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The assessment, planning and implementation of shelter and NFI activities will factor the environmental impact of the intervention, and will aim at mitigating unintended harms by ensuring synergies with forestation activities and selecting durable/locally available shelter/NFI materials with low environmental footprint. The materials and construction methodology will be defined, selected and adapted to prevent over-exploitation, pollution and degradation of environmental conditions. The shelter materials are generally locally available, are easy to use for self construction, could be dismantled/disposed easily over time, and will not cause any environmental hazards.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all reasonable feasible steps to protect programme participants. Concern’s approach to protection is based on key principles of analysis and risk management such as: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups identified within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create, with the imperative mandate to reduce vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied to this intervention. Principles of protection mainstreaming will be exercised, including: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of communities about the best way to deliver services and consideration for vulnerable groups, and accountability mechanisms. Concern and partner will empower communities by training and capacity building and awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision-making process, identifying and training gender balanced project staff and volunteers. “Do No Harm” analysis will be conducted for each activity not to pose threats to beneficiaries especially women, girls and children. All project activities will be analysed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure beneficiaries are protected and services are easily accessible and safely utilized by all. Protection / (S)GBV risks faced by women and girls will be core focus of capacity building for staff and volunteers to ensure they are able to detect cases of abuse, recognize and refer to protection-mandated entities. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm, and they are able to respond safely, and appropriately, to protection concerns whenever they are reported using a confidential and survivor-centered approach to disclosure of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaint and ensuring that measures are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding project objectives (e.g., access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds, information sharing about project implementation, achievements, and selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously. Concern will coordinate with protection partners for GBV and child protection services to ensure that beneficiaries with (S)GBV / protection complaints are referred appropriately. All NFI kits will contain housing and hygiene materials which can be used by both male and female beneficiaries, yet take specifically into account hygiene needs of the latter group (e.g., sanitary pads and detergent). Community latrines and shower units will be segregated by sexes and carefully protected e.g., locks, lights and privacy.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Concern WW is a Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) member and this intervention will be implemented based on CHS principles. The targeted communities will be part of the entire project cycle through constant meetings, dialogues, focus group discussions (FGDs) and monitoring. At the beginning of the project, beneficiaries will be informed about the project objectives, deliverables and their expected contribution and entitlements. They will also be part of the assessment process and their recommendations will be recorded and considered for the project implementation plan. All ES / NFI distribution rounds will be rolled out according to the criteria which will be agreed upon and developed with target groups within local communities. Verification of intended beneficiaries will be conducted jointly with the afore-mentioned community members. Distribution committees will be set up in each community where the project will intervene, in order to ensure full accountability and understanding of selection criteria and procedures. Moreover, community members will actively participate in the construction of emergency latrines and bathing units as a mean to foster accountability and enhance their sense of ownership of the project deliverables. Volunteers and skilled labors will be selected among targeted communities, in order to increase beneficiaries’ buy-in and acceptance of the proposed intervention. As per ES / NFI distribution rounds, selection criteria will be agreed upon and developed with previously appointed distribution committees who will be responsible of the distribution of construction materials, overall coordination and supervision with the technical support of project staffContinuous information sharing through e.g. meeting with field staff, monthly coordination meeting with key leaders in the targeted communities, and partners, will be part of the information management during the project implementation. M&E activities include participants from the targeted activities to ensure appropriate feedback for improvement by way of HH visits and interviews and community FGDs. Beneficiary targeting will be guided by needs observed on the ground, humanitarian and “Do No Harm” principles, international methodologies and Concern’s specific policies targeting the poorest among affected population.Concern has an active Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRM) in place that will be utilized for the project. As per standard practice, a CRM table will be established during all distributions; moreover, one female and one male focal point will be appointed for each community in order to supervise the whole process and handle any potential complaint in a confidential and culture sensitive manner. There will be various tools applied for getting information e.g., CRM hotlines, feedback boxes, focus group discussions, one-to-one discussions to record the community/individual feedback coordinated by MEAL team.
