Project Summary

This proposed project is strategically designed to advance the protection of women and girls in Monguno LGA by increasing access to well-coordinated and quality integrated Gender-Based Violence (GBV) services for survivors and proactively safeguard those at risk through local communities. The primary objective is to strengthen the resilience of communities with a focus on specific outcomes. Firstly, the core focus is on enhancing access to locally coordinated and quality-integrated GBV prevention and response services. This targeted effort aims to provide crucial support to survivors of GBV and other vulnerable individuals within the project communities, ensuring a comprehensive and survivor-centric approach. Secondly, the project will elevate access to education within a safe and protective learning environment, prioritizing emergency-affected girls and boys. This initiative not only promotes educational continuity but also serves as a key protective measure for vulnerable girls. Finally, the project is dedicated to ensuring heightened safety and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water, improved sanitation facilities, and hygiene awareness specifically tailored to protect the well-being of women and girls among IDPs and the Host Communities. Overall, these outcomes will significantly contribute to the overarching goal of protecting and empowering women and girls in Monguno LGA, aligning seamlessly with the priorities of the allocation. Christian Aid (CAID) will implement this project with two sub-implementing partners namely; Rehabilitation Empowerment and Better Health Initiative (REBHI) and Borno Aid Humanitarian Foundation (BOAID)

1 - Needs

The HNO for 2023 highlights the alarming levels of Non-State Armed Group (NSAG) violence against civilians, especially in Borno State, with mass abductions and killings due to heightened suspicion of collaboration with government forces. The 2 million IDPs in BAY (Borno, Adamawa, Yola) states, particularly the vulnerable in Monguno LGA, face severe protection concerns (HNO 2023).The HRP estimates that 1.4 million individuals, including IDPs, returnees, and host communities, will need GBV services in the coming year. Similarly, Christian Aid and partner conducted an assessment in Monguno LGA, identifying significant gaps in dignity items and GBV services.The crisis has denied children in Borno their right to education, exacerbated by displacement, flooding, and poverty. Teacher qualifications, and vandalism of facilities pose additional challenges (JENA, 2021). The WASH sector faces issues of facility theft and water scarcity, impacting negatively on safe water access, sanitation, and hygiene. Key needs include supporting out-of-school children, providing hygiene kits for schoolgirls, renovating classrooms, and strengthening school-based management committees. New arrivals in over-congested areas require non-food items and basic needs to reduce vulnerability and prevent harmful coping mechanisms (OCHA SITREP, Nov. 2023). Addressing these issues holistically in Protection, WASH, and Education is crucial to the well-being and empowerment of affected communities in Borno State.
