Project Summary

Medair proposes to implement an integrated WASH response in partnership with four local NGO Organisations; DAWA, SAACID, SomaliAid and Zamzam. These operations will be carried out in Mogadishu (Karaan, Hawl Wadag and Daynille districts), Cadale, Afgooye, Balcad and Kismaayo and Badhadhe districts targeting 19,785 beneficiaries.
Open defecation rates in Somalia stand at 60 per cent in rural areas and at 39 per cent on a national level. The practice of hand washing is often focused on religious rituals rather than effective hygiene to reduce disease transmission, thus diminishing its effectiveness; only 37.2% of the population supported by Medair had access to a hand washing facility with soap. Open defecation, combined with very poor hand washing practice has resulted in high prevalence of diarrhea (35.4%) among children under five years of age.
In Afgooye and Balcad Districts, the non-functional water system and the recurrent drying of the River Shabelle which is the main source of water for drinking, domestic and irrigation purposes has left the communities vulnerable to AWD and other water-borne diseases.. There are very few to no latrines in most of the villages in the district. Open defection is common in the community and poses a great health risk. Sanitation issues in the community and villages must be urgently addressed; since this involves behavior change, this will be addressed through a community-led approach (for
The proposed WASH action is a continuation and extension of an ongoing project intervention that currently supports WASH infrastructure in supported primary healthcare facilities, hygiene promotion at household and community levels.
Hygiene promotion
Effective hygiene promotion is widely accepted to be one of the most valuable tools to reduce the burden of diarrheal diseases, by creating a series of barriers to infection, hygienic behaviors have a critical influence on the transmission of water- and sanitation-related diseases.
Hygiene promotion is a key component of Medair interventions in Somalia and currently supports over 1200 community workers who are extensively trained on behavior change communication approaches using the care group model approach. Information is first collected from the community about cultural ideas around hygiene to identify the behavioral aspects that will require to be positively changed Each community worker is assigned 15 immediate neighborhood houses where he will be visiting regularly promoting appropriate, hygiene and practices
The training will focus on; the importance of hand washing with soap or ash at five key moments, the trainings involve practical examples of the hygiene risks and how behavior change can prevent sickness, with particular attention for people's capacities to make these changes.
Sanitation (WASH in Health facilities)
Medair will ensure that all the health facilities have functioning sanitation facilities. Repair and maintenance of these facilities will be supported. The facilities will include, patients’ toilets/latrines, incinerators for hospital waste disposal, ash pits, support replacement of color coded waste disposal bins, safety boxes for sharps, hand washing stations for health workers and for patients, pits for sharps and placenta disposal pits. Over the years Medair has developed accountability mechanisms that reach across all stakeholders, including but not limited to actual beneficiaries. Complaints, compliments and other propositions are routinely received, reviewed and carefully addressed. Medair is an active cluster partner at local (sub-cluster), and national level. Medair has developed an M&E system that will improve and facilitate the process of data collection and analysis from health facilities, from community workers and from the Medair supervisors. Data will be dis-aggregate by age and sex to allow for more careful analysis of how the program engages these groups. Community feedback will also be provided via toll free phone

Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance - Medair is committed to ensuring that members of groups who are internally displaced OR minorities OR marginalized by any means are routinely involved at all levels of project cycle from inception to closure. They will also be engaged individually at their households and community level to better understand their unique needs and how best to respond to them. Dialogue meetings with local leaders will be enhanced to promote local ownership of project activities. Health workers will be trained and support-supervised to ensure they practice humanitarian core principles. Health services will be offered round the clock to all including host and displaced persons at no cost. Hot line number, which is toll free will be provided to community members so they discretely provide feedback on any form of exclusion or marginalization to services delivered. Community leaders will be encouraged to advocate for inclusion of minority in decision making roles Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance - Medair has zero tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse and has strict policies to prevent and rotection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Medair will continue to provide staff training on physical sexual exploitation and abuse mainstreaming and ensure they sign code of conducts on physical sexual exploitation and abuse, all employees have signed a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards including the rights of beneficiaries to access humanitarian services without undue exploitation. Medair has recruited a safe guarding office at all bases in Somalia to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow up on any allegation or even rumors about sexual exploitation and abuse to the beneficiaries. The Care group volunteers and health/nutrition workers are also trained to identify passive cases in the community and counsel them to seek professional care at supported facilities. where these incidences have happened, trauma counselling and first aid will be provided to the survivors. A clear referral pathways will be established in coordination with other actors with capacity for long-term psychological and legal support for affected personsBarriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms- Transparency will be observed in sharing all the information related to the project deliverable including targeted beneficiaries and their entitlement. Service charter in local language posters will be published, staffs will create awareness on the rights to safety, access, dignity, privacy and confidentiality Medair has an efficient and effective feedback mechanism in place for suggestions and complaint that is accessible, confidential and well understood to beneficiaries with a view to feedback to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Timely responses are provided to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. Ongoing awareness and sensitization sessions at the facility through the monitoring and evaluation officer/nutrition facility workers and the community through the care group volunteers and community health workers where they are informed on the mechanism in place and how it works. All Medair and the partners’ staffs/nutrition staffs, and community volunteers they will be trained on Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients.Exit interviews will be conducted on the targeted beneficiaries to understand their need and concerns on the quality of services they received

Please provide further explanation

The project is designed with environmental conservation in mind, it’s not expected to contribute to harm through actions and omissions. The structures of the facilities are sustainable for long term operations; the joint implementation of projects with local NGO actors ensures sustainability beyond one cycle. The large network of well-trained community hygiene workers ensures project activities continue. Medair integrated approach to projects alongside working in priority areas of Shabelle, lower Juba and Banadir portends well with national guidelines. Relevant line ministries are routinely consulted at all phases of project implementation, the same applies to community leaders and gate keepers. Displaced populations form a majority of the beneficiaries in this proposal, Banadir and along the Afgooye corridor. Medair has a standby emergency response team ready to respond in a timely manner and provide emergency support when the displacements happen, these include emergency hygiene kits.To promote and feasible exit, Medair works with partners who are trusted and reliable local NGOs who share a similar vision, building their capacity to ensure continuity and sustainability of current program results. Medair has developed an exit and expansion strategy linked to its middle- and long-term vision. It aims at building and monitoring capacities in Medair’s local partner agencies. This strategy sets indicators and minimum capacity criteria that local partners, health facilities and locations must meet in order for Medair to exit/ transition and move to other vulnerable locations. This strategy also defines partner agency selection with matrices that are based on organizational values for accountability. For this project, a strong emphasis will be placed on developing local capacity as an exit strategy from the start. Medair aims to increase the capacity of the local authority in terms of regulation, control and setting up accountability mechanisms
