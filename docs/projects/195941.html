Project Summary

The project aims to provide basic health service for host community and IDP camp and rehabilitation services to people with disability, with particular regard to the children. The project will support Usratuna PHCC and Rehabilitation service and 2 PHCU near IDP camp (Waystation and Mangateen) with the distribution of drugs, providing incentives, capacity building and a good referral pathway. The project will provide rehabilitation services (including physiotherapist, speech therapy and occupational therapy) and will improve the knowledge of health workers and community workers on disability and gender based violence.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2022 identified 8.9 M people in need (24% women, 53% children and 15% disables), the total population in need of health is around 5.5 M of people. In 2022, South Sudan the population has a very low level of accessibility to primary health care services, thirty-four counties have less than one primary health care unit per 15,000 people while 23 counties have less than one primary health care centre per 50,000. The provision of health services is challenged by conflicts, natural disaster and the low Government’s budget allocation for the Health Sector (increased from 1.9 percent in 2020/2021 to 7.9 percent in 2021/202), reflecting in lack of accessible health facilities and health workers/person, lack of health workers capacities, lack of medical and consumable equipment, lack of specialized services.The recent drop of crucial humanitarian actors funding a great percentage of health facilities disproportionately affected the ability of civilians, particularly those with chronic medical issues, older persons, persons with disabilities and pregnant women to access life-saving health assistance though reasonably close health facilities services (PHHC/U).The lack of health services and primary health care is strongly related to high mortality rate and spread of common diseases, significantly contributing to increase the percentage of persons with disabilities in South Sudan.In Central Equatorial there is a severe deficiency with regards to basic health services (i.e. primary Health Care Unit and Centre – PHCU/C and services for persons with disabilities) the HNO 2023 identified the needs to cover the health services for 717.9K in the state. The health services provision in Juba in 2023 will be challenged by 22 PHCC/U being dropped by humanitarian actors, with the Saba Hospital to be dropped in 2023. The pressure of IDP keeps flowing towards the capital city, it is an exacerbating factor challenging the health services provision in the area. Among the IDP camps, the settlements not recognized as IDP camps are the most severely affected by the lack of humanitarian assistance related to health services. Within Juba, Mangateen PHCU and Way Station are positioned close to IDP camp not recognised. In 2021 conducted 17213 and 12778 visits to persons in need. The two facilities are under the CES/MoH and provide free health services to IDPs. The facilities are not actually supported by the MoH, indeed are supported through humanitarian assistance. It is crucial to keep supporting the facilities, which are currently the only health services accessible for the population of Mangateen and Way Station IDP camps. The lack of access to health services significantly increase the mortality rate and increase the risk of disability provoked by common diseases, poor antenatal and postnatal care. The most common diseases are: Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity and accounted for 62,720 cases (64.4% of OPD cases) in the country, TB, diarrhea, pneumonia, intestinal parasitosis, HIV, syphilis, cholera (Outbreak of Cholera in Rubkona and Bentiu)South Sudan’s maternal mortality ratios are estimated at 800 deaths per 100,000 live births and an equally high neonatal mortality ratio at 37 per 1,000 live births based Further studies have shown that only one in four (25%) of mothers had given birth at a health facility or received care from a skilled birth attendant at the time of delivery.For people with disabilities, stigma can be a major barrier to participation, they are the most vulnerable people, excluded from key opportunities and services. Females, young girls in particular, with disability are doubly disadvantaged, due to the stigma associated with gender as well as with disability. For this reason capacity building about GBV and inclusion is one of the priorities for the health workers and community workers.

2 - Project activities

1.1 Providing primary health care services (including antenatal care) through Usratuna PHCC, Waystation PHCU and Mangateen PHCU to patients.1.2 Providing the target facilities with essential medical and consumable equipment and drugs and health personnel incentives.1.3 Providing capacity building to health workers on "Prevention and early detection of disabilities" in order to provide instruments to proper detection, first action and orientation in the referral system for disability cases.1.4 Reinforcing the horizontal referral pathway from Waystation PHCU, Mangateen PHCU to Usartuna PHCC and rehabilitation Centre and other health facilities in Juba for advanced management of Antenatal Care (through ultrasound), rehabilitation services for children with disabilities and other specific health services. 2.1 Providing rehabilitation services for children with disability at Usratuna Rehabilitation Centre (Cerebral Palsy, Quadriplegia, Diplegia, Hemiplegia, Ataxia, Motor/psychomotor delay, Microcephalus, Hydrocephalus, Spina bifida, Monoplegia, Erb's palsy, PIP, Guillain Barre Syndrome, Down's syndrome, Muscular Dystrophy).2.2 Managing cases in need of speech therapy services and occupational therapy at Usratuna Rehabilitation Centre Activity (Speech disorder, Speech delay, Dysarthria, Hearing loss, Cleft lip/Cleft lip and palate, Cognitive impairment, Swallowing disorder, Dysphagia, Down's syndrome, Behaviour disorder, Autism, Children with physical impairment).2.3 Training health workers and community workers on Gender Based Violence.2.4 Performing follow up home visits through specialized community workers, assessing and monitoring the patient and the family conditions and promoting disability inclusion, accessibility to health services and IPC through sensitization hygiene kits distribution for prevention of disabilities and diseases.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The partnership with Organizations of Persons with disabilities (DPOs) and an appointed social worker will facilitate the relationship with the most vulnerable beneficiaries.Sign language interpreters and specific materials will be used by the staff for effective communication.Also, Staff of different ages will be involved in order to guarantee a different approach and way of communicating.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

OVCI promotes information sharing, transparency, participation and learning with project participants. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are planned and based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. OVCI uses different tools, including suggestion boxes, surveys, mobile phone calls or text messaging, focus group discussions, one-to-one interviews, and community meetings.Complaints can be shared formally or informally. Feedback and complaints can help the evaluation process since they can be related to a specific theme or systemic underlying problem. The quality improvement team will be in charge of this process and mechanism.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

OVCI has a PSEA policy and an appointed social worker for the dissemination of the relevant information.The policy ensures a professional approach and attitude by OVCI staff and collaborators and complaint mechanism too. OVCI will provide specific awareness sessions for beneficiaries (and relatives of beneficiaries).OVCI staff will receive specific training in order to be periodically updated about this issue.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback can be used to adapt project assistance when it supports actions that improve elements of a broader response.The data will be verified and analyzed. OVCI will commit to provide timely, appropriate and fair responses. The feedback mechanism is run by the staff, with the relevant competences and attitudes. If necessary, consultation and outsider’s opinion will be asked to make necessary adjustments to the activities, while liaison with Organizations of Persons with disabilities (DPOs) through appointed social workers will facilitate the relationship with the most vulnerable beneficiaries.Sign language interpreters and specific materials will be used by the staff for an effective communication.Staff with different ages will be involved in order to guarantee a different approach and way of communicating. This will elad to a continuous adaptation of the intervention towards beneficiaries in a volatile context.
