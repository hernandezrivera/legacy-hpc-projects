Project Summary

The water infrastructure in Syria has been significantly compromised since the beginning of the conflict. Effective water supply has fallen by more than 80 percent since 2011 as attacks on pipelines and water stations dramatically reduced the capacity of the water distribution network. Power cuts, shortages of the fuel to operate pumps, and damage to irrigation canals and pumping stations has also contributed to water shortages. Due to the difficulty and cost associated with maintenance, many water stations are no longer operational. Many residents of Raqqa city and the surrounding countryside have no access to drinking water, forcing them to rely on wells, untreated water drawn directly from rivers, or expensive water tankers.
This project aims to address this issue through the rehabilitation of parts of the rural water network in Ar-Raqqa Governorate. The project will restore three damaged water stations and install or repair 13 water pipelines serving 34 communities and 13 camps in rural Ar-Raqqa. The restoration of water services will be accompanied by a comprehensive evaluation of damage to the water network in Ar-Raqqa Governorate, public health campaigning and a programme of training and capacity building for water station staff.
Through these engineering works the project will benefit:
Direct Beneficiaries – Around 55,000 residents of villages and camps will be directly supplied from the rehabilitated water stations.
Indirect Beneficiaries - 707,000 people from Ar-Raqqa governorate who will benefit from improved capacity of water station managers.
This programme will provide an essential service intervention through the provision of clean water, allowing beneficiaries to resume their livelihoods by removing the financial and physical burden of water collection and health risk of dirty water consumption. The project will augment humanitarian efforts in Ar-Raqqa by providing a secure, clean and reliable source of water to IDP and host communities, reducing competition for this scarce resource and improving livelihoods throughout the targeted communities.
CAID, together with our implementing partner Nawat, will:
Repair 13 pipelines and rehabilitate 3 water stations to provide a supply of clean, safe drinking water to 39,310 residents and 15,410 IDPs in rural areas of Ar-Raqqa governorate.
Deliver a public health campaign that reinforces smart water usage.
Provide training and capacity building for water station staff focused on sustaining service delivery beyond the life of the project.
Undertake a comprehensive evaluation of damage to the water network in the governorate.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of increased insecurity in the programme delivery areas Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Operations will not focus on areas of Raqqa Governate with resurgence of unrest; Contingency planning in the event of a deteriorating security situation; Security manager recruited to support operational field team; Ongoing, proactive monitoring of security and conflict status. Risk of operational delays (Weather, extended curfew, security checks) Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Selection of contingency sites to offset access or operational constraints; Ongoing contingency planning and operational reviews; Scrutiny of operational scheduling by project manager; Early reporting of potential or actual delays; Evaluation of quality of available machinery and materials and tools required for the project prior to contracting.
