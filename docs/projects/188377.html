Project Summary

UOSSM seeks to contribute to achieving the following objectives:
Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need
The proposed project is to keep running the acute mental health unit in Sarmada/Idleb with the two mental health mobile units. The main goal of the project is to promote specialized mental health services to address more severe mental health disorders.
Upon the need of the affected people to specialized MH services which are not sufficiently available in the area, UOSSM contributed to the response by establishing an acute MH inpatient and outpatient unit where services are provided for the most urgent presentations of severe mental health disorders, like schizophrenia (acute psychotic episodes), bipolar disorder (manic episode), severe depression, suicidal risk, severe substance misuse disorder and severe behavioral disorder related to mental health problems which cannot be managed and addressed in the community, in addition to providing multidisciplinary mental health service in the community (outpatient psychiatric units, psychiatric supervision, follow up and case management, individual, couple and group psychotherapy, and the-mental health supervision), and receiving referrals of more severe and complicated psychiatric beneficiaries from other agencies and health providers.
Also, to cover as many areas as possible, the acute MH unit is linked with two MH mobile clinics, where MH interventions are provided by doctors trained on MHGAP program for beneficiaries of children and adults with signs and symptoms of mild and moderate mental health disorders, in addition to psychosocial support activities for children, which are: self-recovery techniques, coping skills with different situation skills, emotional expression techniques, PFA and PFA for children. Also, to facilitate of awareness campaigns on mental health and protection concerns, such as (detection of signs and symptoms of mental disorders, early marriage, GBV prevention. etc.). Not only the detection and referral of the serious and severe beneficiaries to the acute MH unit in Sarmada, but also the referral of beneficiaries or families to community resources to help in recovery from mental or physical illnesses, and to facilitate access to services provided by other humanitarian actors.
Objectives of the Project:
•To respond to the most urgent acute and serious mental health problems of the affected populations in the area
•To facilitate access of the affected populations to long-term specialized MH services
•To enhance and promote the affected communities’ healthy coping mechanisms for both locals and IDPs
•To facilitate access for the affected populations to the available services in the area through an active referral pathway

4 - Needs

The total population there is about 1.6M; out of them about 1.2M IDPs.According to HeRAMS, Mental Health Outpatient services are fully available provided through only 26 health facilities; while inpatient capacity is available only through one facility in Sarmada. BioMed Central based in the UK has published in Jan 2021 a cross-sectional study that included people who lived in Syria in different governorates in NWS. The study included 1951 participants 527 of them (27.0%) were males and 1538 (78.8%) between the age of 19 and 25. Among participants, 44% had a likely severe mental disorder, 27% had both likely severe mental disorders and full PTSD symptoms, 36.9% had full PTSD symptoms, and only 10.8% had neither positive PTSD symptoms nor mental disorders. Around 23% had low overall support. Half of the responders were internally displaced, and 27.6% were forced to change places of living three times or more due to war. Around 86.6% of the responders believed that the war was the main reason for their mental distress.( IRC ) while preparing for the protection monitoring report which included conducting (Key informant Interviews) KIIs during June 2021 in MWS. 30 different KIs male 21 (70%), female 9 (30%) were conducted during June by IRC, aiming to screen the suicide situation in NWS, 37% (11) reported that suicide is more common among the (19 to 25 years old). 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery Mitigating : the wards of the beneficiaries in the acute unit will be separated, part for men and apart for women. In addition, baths and toilets will be also isolated and separated for men and women and also for the staff. The MH unit will be also provided by a complaint box reached by beneficiaries, all the staff members will sign a code of conduct. the role of M&E will be activated during the project cycle. Also, there will be a client satisfaction survey conducted every three months by the PSWs for beneficiaries. 2- Neglecting the most vulnerableMitigating Taking into consideration the context and the environment roles, traditions and customs, the MH unit staff will be mixed of the two genders, males and females. Also, the working hours in the outpatient unit will be during daylight which enable women to reach the services available. In addition, the building will be equipped with facilities for disabled beneficiaries such as a ramp, wheelchair, and modified toilets. Above all, the outreach PSWs will facilitate PSS activities targeting children and adolescences in schools and community centers in the area on a weekly or biweekly basis. 3- Discrimination in service deliveryMitigating All the services provided will be based on humanitarian perspectives, many things will be taken into accounts; the location and place of the MH


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual Harassment: among employees, volunteers and in work environment contextLevel: LowMitigation : All staff have been trained on PSEA and above all has signed a TOR and aware of the internal policy of PSEA which has been monitored by both the HR & MEAL teamsRisk2:Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: between service delivery personnel, towards beneficiariesLevel: LowMitigation : All staff (employees and volunteers) signed on a code of conduct that includes of refers to the commitment to PSEA, that is issued by the UN Secretary General Bulletin for the PSEA procedure. In addition, UOSSM has a PSEA focal point in addition to a dedicated hotline to receive complaints from beneficiaries in case of PSEA incidents. Risk3:Gender Based Violence: targeting the vulnerable groups with communities (i.e., women, elderly, children, special needs …)level: Low Mitigation : The project design is built on robust need assessment and is also targeting the most vulnerable groups in the community without discrimination or exclusion. Above, the project is a multi-sectoral one where all sectors are aware and acknowledged of detection and referral of GBV either internally to the protection department or externally to other service providers.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- UOSSM will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention.- Location remains accessible for the team and the beneficiaries.- Inflation and currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned deliverable quality and quantity.- Local qualified staff required for project activities are available and adequate.Risk:- The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities.- The close of borders prevents the shipments of supplies and medications to access facilities.Mitigation:- Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas.- Capacity building plan for the staff.
