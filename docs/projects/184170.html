Project Summary

Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 7.7 million women, men and children require humanitarian assistance in 2022 in Somalia. Conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of people to flee their homes in 2021 and remains key drivers of displacement in 2022. Poverty is pervasive in rural areas where access remains a challenge. It’s estimated that 71 per cent of the population living below the poverty line will remain at similar levels for 2022-23.
Globally, Somalia places fourth highest on the gender inequality index while women and girls constitute 50% of the country’s population. Gender inequality is exacerbated by pov-erty, disability, social class and harmful practices that violate the rights of women and girls. Women are either excluded from formal decision making and asset ownership or operate through a patriarchal filter. They lack access to fundamental tools for success, such as edu-cation, healthcare, credit and more. Somalia has the highest rate of genital mutilation, with 98% of girls subjected to it before they turn 13 years old.
Adult married women, adolescent girls, widows, divorced women, female headed house-holds, pregnant and lactating women, women and girls living with disabilities are subject to various forms of violence and discrimination emanating from social status and gendered social norms. Female heads of households, divorced women and women living with disabili-ties are particularly disadvantaged due to lack of male protection and provision, difficulty to report/escape violence or access services. Intimate Partner Violence, rape, forced abortion and revenge killings; sexual exploitation, harassment and abuse are rife due to poor living conditions; destruction of homes, water points, latrines, streetlights, farmlands, livelihoods and schools; family separation for children and open defecation. Lack of community and male protection due to male migration; segregation of toilets, travel to markets and service sites; poor lighting of IDP camps represent major GBV risks for women and adolescent girls. Men and boys are also targeted for rape, forceful recruitment and kidnapping and psy-chological abuse and trauma. Loss of livelihoods and food insecurity due to multiple dis-placements forced young boys to step into the roles of provision thereby increasing levels of school dropouts. Men’s declining ability to provide for their families adds to low self-esteem and the use of intimate partner violence to reclaim some of the historically privi-leged position of household head.
Despite the growing demand, GBV service provision remains significantly low as compared to the needs and geographical landscape response. Limited specialized services such CMR, case management, psycho-social support and higher levels of mental health care for trauma-tized women and girls are major hindrances to expanding provision of timely, confidential and quality GBV services. Limited GBV specialized service provider continues to impact both quality and reach of services.
Its against this background that NCA proposes to fill GBV service gaps in Dollow and Nugaal by building on current GBV response and prevention activities through HRP mechanisms to improve accessibility to GBV services in remote areas. NCA implements integrated programs in WASH, economic empowerment, peace building, education and GBV. NCA focuses on GBV in emergencies activities through Adolescent Girls Safe Spaces (AGSS) programming and long-term GBV interventions through MFA and NORAD funds. In line with NCA’s localization strategy, over 60 per cent of programs are implemented through local partners due to their good contextual understanding, acceptance by local communities and access to insecure locations. KAALO is NCA’s GBV partner in Nugaal while SEDHURO and CeRID are partners in Dollow. NCA and Save the Children implements an FGM joint program which is in its third phase. NCA will implement proposed activities through local partners.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.2 - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistanceMitigation: NCA will put in place appropriate policies, procedures and practices to ensure that staff, partners and programmes DO NO HARM to the beneficiaries. It includes protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), and protection of vulnerable adults, children and people with diverse genders, sexualities and bodies while providing aid. These policies include: the code of conduct, safeguarding, PSEA and anti-corruption among others. The same will be cascaded to partners, consultants, suppliers and contractors and enforcement of these policies will be audited routinely. NCA will sensitize beneficiaries on their rights and entitlements and on the importance of reporting any violations. A robust, safe, accessible and confidential feedback mechanism for reporting sensitive and non-sensitive issues will be used to receive complaints from beneficiaries to improve program quality and promote community participation. Having a system in place to receive and respond to complaints is central to accountability, impact and learning. It is a formal way of recognizing and addressing the power imbalance between staff and the communities we work with, and for addressing human rights abuses as well as any actual or potential adverse human rights impacts. 4.4 - Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors.Mitigation: NCA has a fully dedicated security focal person for Somalia program and will oversee all security matters in the country program. He will make regular visits to the project locations to assess security situation, conduct risk assessments and update security plans for each project location periodically. Movement of staff in and out of the project locations will be monitored and approved by the security advisor as appropriate. Security protocols will be revised from time to time to reflect the changing dynamics in the operating context. In addition, this project will be implemented through local partners who enjoys easy access to locations perceived unsafe for INGOs due to the presence of rebel groups. NCA will continue investing in and maintaining good working relationship with the government, partners and local community to enhance security of staff and assets and for information gathering on security matters.
