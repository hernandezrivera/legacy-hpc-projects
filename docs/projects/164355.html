Project Summary

The 2020 Syria HNO reported that "In addition to physical destruction, the protracted and multiple cycles of displacement have contributed to a steady erosion of coping/protection mechanisms of individuals and communities, generated high levels of psychological distress, disrupted the availability of support services to the most vulnerable populations, and put a major strain on community support structures". After nine years of conflict, the toxic environment has had a significant impact on the psychosocial wellbeing of both girls and boys. This project will assist children in overcoming these impacts of trauma, through the establishment of 15 Child-Friendly Spaces across NES. Structured and unstructured PSS support activities will be delivered through trained Facilitators to 1500 children aged 5-17. In recognizing the additional vulnerabilities of adolescent girls in Syria, this project will have specific PSS and life skills sessions aimed at this demographic, with particular support for girls who are married, pregnant or mothers. To ensure program sustainability, activities will be delivered by trained facilitators from both the IDP and host community populations, whilst drawing on the technical guidance of senior protection staff. Project locations will be prioritized in informal settlements, where the communities currently have very limited access to services including formal schools or community based PSS programming.

4 - Needs

The 2020: Syria HNO reported that “Children continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concer. This toxic environment leaves many girls and boys exposed to psychosocial risks (e.g. sense of security, purpose, control and significance, exercising social agency) and experiencing profound distress”. Children continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern with countless children killed and injured through persistent use of explosive weapons in civilian areas, recruitment and use of children, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access, particularly to children living in UN-declared besieged areas. This toxic environment leaves many girls and boys exposed to psychosocial risks (e.g. sense of security, purpose, control and significance, exercising social agency) and experiencing profound distress. This organisation identified in recent CFS intake assessments of urban and camp based locations in Ar-Raqqa Government; a high rate of women headed households and children participating in the workforce. These vulnerable categories are exposed to a range of different forms of harm, from forced recruitment into armed groups, to SGBV, from conflict related trauma to early marriage. Women and children have the least access to basic psychosocial support services, psychological counselling and child friendly spaces. Other issues which compound these difficulties are security concerns, distance, and restrictions on the movement of IDPs into areas controlled by the self-administration, which is why a tailored and targeted response directly into the most conflict affected area and recently IS liberated areas is necessary to reach the most vulnerable.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will work with 1500 Children through 15 Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) across north East Syria. Trained Facilitators will implement structured and unstructured PSS activities at each site based on the organizations agreed curriculum. These activities will be used by the facilitators to enhance the children’s development and learning. In addition, these spaces offer children protection, empower them, so that they become more resilient, and build their own positive coping mechanisms. CFS spaces will be prioritiesed for underserved areas, including informal settlements where there is no access to formal education or community based PSS support. To reinforce child protection and well-being, all children enrolled in the CFS will participate in age appropriate sessions on mine risk awareness, on the importance of education or on basic hygiene and health information sessions to build their life skills. Awareness-raising sessions will also be conducted with 600 caregivers and are aimed at increasing each caregiver’s capacity to protect their child’s rights and physical and psychosocial well-being. Given the serious presence of mines in the project’s districts, the initial session will also focus on mine risk awareness. Caregivers are taught how to identify different types of unexploded ordnances (UXOs) and improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and advised on different ways to communicate the hazards of these devices to their children. Other sessions given to caregivers are focused on protection issues and children’s rights to minimize exploitation risks, gender-based violence, raising awareness on the consequences of child labor and discussing the importance of education. All staff will be trained on the organizations Code of Conduct and Participant Policy, referral pathways and protection mainstreaming. Improvements in children's levels of resiliency, knowledge on protection risks and well being will be tracked through CFS baseline and end line monitoring.

Organization staff, as well as program participants, will receive mine awareness training with specific focus on high-risk activities such as rehabilitating irrigation channels, tilling soil, or conducting other activities involving digging. This project will consult with local authorities and with mine clearance organizations before starting work. Those staff who have been trained to give mine risk awareness trainings are responsible to share this training with all program staff and project participants. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk - There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS, TAF and FSA, especially in border districts such as Manbij, Kobane and Tabqa. In 2019, peaking in October, the Turkish Military incursion resulted in the establishment of a “Buffer zone” from Talabied to Ras Al Ain. Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioration in the security situation in the project area as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict. Assumptions The security situation will remain permissive to humanitarian accessThere will be no shifts in administrative control over key locations west and south of the Euphrates, specifically This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly Mitigating Measures this organization has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict this organizations response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localized insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations, This organization would likely suspend operations in these areas and shift its resources to its nearest other field office.
