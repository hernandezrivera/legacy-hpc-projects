Project Summary

The proposed project intends to contribute to the reduction of excess morbidity and mortality caused by common communicable diseases and injuries sustained from violent conflicts through ensuring access to quality essential Lifesaving mobile emergency health care services to women, men, girls and boys, and gender based violence services in conflict affected population and host community (Marrow, Labarab, Kessingor, Dourein and Thangojon) in Pibor county and the needy People( Otallo1 and 2, Daktek, Chaam, Nyium,Ajwara, and Omila Payams) in Pochalla County. RHS found that the primary heath facilities in the both proposed Counties have been reaching only the nearby population, just like at Gumuruk PHCU, Lekwangole PHCU, Jebel Boma PHCC, Pibor PHCC, and Pochalla PHCC, leaving other population without essential lifesaving primary health care services, unprotected against bad practices, sexual exploitation and abuses and more vulnerable and exposed to increased morbidity and mortality. This has been augmented by vulnerability of the communities due to the common infections and long distances to reach Pibor, Pochalla, Gumurk, Lekwangole and Jebel Boma and some go beyond walking for several days to Dimma Refugee camp in Ethiopia to access medical services.
RHS plans to open 1 static PHCU at Thangajon in Pibor County and establish 2 mobile clinics; 1 in Pibor County(Maruwo) to reach IDPs and host communities in Maruwo, Labarab and Kessingor areas. To save lives by providing timely and integrated assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in those places, and 1 mobile clinic to be in Pochalla North for the needy people of Otallo 1&2, Daktek Chaam, Omila, Ajwara and Nyium. The 2 mobile clinics will do integrated services of curative consultations, ANC, Vaccination of children under 5 years of age screening and treatment of MAM and transfer of SAM cases to the nearest PHCC, disease surveillance and health promotion and education.
The RHS will also train the key project workers in both at the static PHCU and at the 2 mobile clinics in preparedness and response to epidemic prone disease outbreaks with the focus on cholera, Measles, Ebola & Rift Valley Fever. as well as Malaria, meningitis, and Hepatitis E.
Inline with the health cluster objective 1 Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys, RHS intends to ensure adequate provision of essential and emergency healthcare services , including addressing the major causes of Morbidity and mortality among children under 5 (Malaria, diarrhea and Pneumonia), basic emergency obstetric and Neonatal care, (BEMONC) services, SGBV services, Clinical Management of Rape Cases (CMR) services to women and girls in Pibor and Pochalla Counties, Jongolei State, South Sudan. This will be further achieved through the continuity of curative consultations care services at Pibor and Pochalla. Other activities will include not limited to conducting clean, safe and hygienic deliveries by skilled birth attendants, minor surgeries, BEMONC including blood transfusion services, ANC to pregnant mothers including provision of HIV services, IPT, LLITNs and micro nutrient supplementation, growth monitoring and nutrition assessment to under 5 Males and Females, provision of screening and supporting treatment for MAM/SAM.
This project is designed to directly benefit a total of 10,000 individuals over the entire period segregated by age and gender.

Needs

Pochalla County is located in the eastern part of South Sudan bordering Ethiopia. It is administratively found in Jonglei State. It is one of the poorest areas of South Sudan with very few functional static health facilities. Jebel Boma Payam is located in Pibor County of Jonglei State in the Republic of South Sudan its where Maruwo, Labarab,and Kessingor bomas are located. The area was affected by internal conflicts between 2012 and 2015 plus a spillover of sporadic skirmishes from local confrontations between neighboring communities and other conflicts in the county. As such there are more than 3,000 IDPs mostly at Labarab and Dorein in addition to estimated 24,089 returnees and host populations.The area has a number of primary healthcare units which are currently not functional. It is reported that only the Primary Healthcare Centre at Itti (lower Jebel Boma) is currently managed by LiveWell South Sudan Organization. A May 2018 report by REACH indicates the leading causes of death and morbidity as malaria (65% prevalence among children less than 5 years), pneumonia and acute watery diarrhea and other water borne diseases. Prevalence of malnutrition is also indicated by 6% SAM, 5.9% MAM and 12.9% acute malnutrition cases among the assessed children aged 6 to 59 months while 50.6% of pregnant and lactating mothers were found to be acutely malnourished during the assessment.

Activities

This project will target 2,133 IDPs in maruwo, Labarab and Kessingor ( Jebel Boma Payam) of Pibor County and 7,867 individuals found in Kayiwa, Gogo,and Thangajon of Pibor County, Otallo1&2, Ajwara, Daktek, Omila, and Nyium areas of Pochalla County and are the people in need of integrated mobile clinic services. RHS will carry out screening of under 5 for malnutrition with MUAC, screening of pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition, rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women, screening for Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy, screening for vaccination needs to all under 5, screening for TB, management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity through OPT and SFP, outpatient consultation for common diseases, MCH (ante natal care, uncomplicated/normal delivery), routine EPI and Mass vaccination, outpatient for TB, application of the Minimum Initial response for HIV, basic dressing for war-wounded, provision of psycho-social support and basic Management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment STI and wound). The project will also include basic preventive services like Folic Acid distribution to pregnant women, Vit A supplementation for under 5 every 6 months, RDT and Malaria prophylaxis for pregnant women, deworming treatment for under 5 every 6 months, condom distribution, training CHW on HIV and minimum Initial response for HIV, routine immunization against all national target diseases and adequate cold chain in place, Antenatal care and Family planning.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

RHS has a plan in place for handling complaints from the beneficiaries. The entire project is designed to provide information on health best practices among the host communities and IDPs. RHS also works through community leaders to listen to community needs and addresses them through consensus and confidence building mechanisms.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes indeed messages are tailored to situations as they emerge. What is important is the specific respect given to each beneficiary to take part in the decision making process. Our information giving approach is gender sensitive; women are employed to handle sensitive information from women and girls and men handle issues related to their farewell. We also take special precautions to protect children especially unaccompanied minors in the IDP Communities.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

RHS code of conduct prohibits the engagement of staff in sexual exploitation and abuse and all employees are trained on before contracting. This project will involve communities and households in particular. Therefore, it has been designed to protect the vulnerable household members especially women, girls and children from sexual exploitation and abuse.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

During the rapid needs assessment, the community gatekeepers (the chiefs, youth and women leaders and local government officials) were involved but the direct beneficiaries were also involved. For example, women were directly consulted at the water collection points, at the residences, and churches. All were given opportunities to explain about the situation in any language each knows.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

This project is designed to include a rigorous monitoring and evaluation mechanisms using RHS M & E framework. Complaints that will not reach through the normal channels i.e. from the supervisors and project officers shall be received directly by the M & E Field visitors. After proper investigation of the alleged issues, the head office shall provide feedback to both the beneficiaries and partners including the corrective measures taken where applicable.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project involves the community in M & E through their leaders and selected group representatives. This includes direct stories from women, men, girls and boys on their take on the project. In fact for the sake of the continuity of the best practices, our plan is to train community leaders on continued monitoringand evaluation of such practices among different groups within theircommunities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on a protection risk analysis because it pays specific attention to the needs of the different groups in a gender sensitive manner. For example counselling spaces for SGBV cases in the health facilities for the IDPs populations will be designed after consultations with the beneficiaries.The other cultural sensitivities and variations will be put in much consideration during implementation of the project.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project does not identify any serious barriers to its implementation among the targeted women, men, boys and girls. The key assumption is that the security situation continues to be stable in the implementation areas and the country throughout the year.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The inequalities or protection threats are addressed through equal involvement of the beneficiaries in decision making, giving them opportunity to employment and empowering them to avoid and report violence against other more vulnerable groups ( women, girls, boys and disable groups) within the community.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

This project monitoring process measures access of beneficiaries through direct observation, random interviews and discussion with community leaders and then quantifying this information to arrive at numbers and percentages who have had access to project activities

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring will measure safety of beneficiaries through comparing the number of gender related incidences before and after the completion of the project.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring will measure dignity through the number of complaints received from beneficiaries from random direct interviews with the beneficiaries.
