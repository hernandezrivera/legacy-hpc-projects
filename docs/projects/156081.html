Project Summary

The project is designed to fill the education gap in Idleb that has witnessed lately influx of IDPs from Eastern Ghouta mainly. It will provide formal and non-formal education to 3793 host community and internally displaced children aged 6-18. The number of formal education children is 3313(1674 boys and 1639 girls) and 480 non-formal children(255 boys and 225 girls) in nine schools in rural and urban Idleb. The project will also target 198 education personnel(98 males and 100 females). Such an intervention can enhance resilience and a better integration as host communities are most often the front-line responders to populations most in need of help. Violet will coordinate with Idleb Education Directorate for avoiding any duplication as the Directorate has all the data needed for schools and teachers. MoUs will be signed with the Education Directorate for defining the commitments of both parties.
The project will ensure improved access and quality education for formal and non-formal education for both girls and boys(IDP or HC) equally without any discrimination. To better ensure a protective learning environment, Violet selected nine schools previously repaired under other projects. Each school will have: teachers, a head teacher, a supervisor, a caseworker, a cleaner and a guard who will be recruited based on hiring criteria and a gender balance approach where males and females have the right to apply and go through competitive procedures in a fair way.

Needs

According to HRP 2019,''The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year, has forced 2.15 million, over one-third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Teachers are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 percent of whom are in acute and immediate need.''Violet has conducted 18 need assessments in 9 areas in Idleb governorate and prioritized 9 schools in Idleb district and in Ariha district based the severity scale which showed that scale was 4-5 for the selected locations and the education need that the assessments showed. The 9 schools are hosting the majority of IDP children who either took refuge recently with their families, or earlier, when the conflict erupted. Most of them came from all over Syria (Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Idleb, Deir Ez Zor, Lattakia, , Hama, Homs, and Raqqa) and being out of school few years and at risk of dropping out for host community children. The assessment revealed that there is an urgent need to support the schools in IDP-host communities through providing formal and non-formal education The schools are in tremendous need of school supplies and education kits, incentives for teachers, training on non-formal education, and operational expenses. Existing classrooms and WASH facilities for the target schools are in a good condition because they were rehabilitated and provided with school furniture under projects with UNICEF and ECW. The assessment also revealed that there is a need for raising awareness efforts to reconnect school-age children who are out of school to get back to school. Such essential efforts will encourage out of school children to be back to learn. In addition, it will assist households who consider education a lower priority, to keep their children at school. This, of course, will minimize the risk of dropout, child labor, recruitment, early marriage, domestic violence, and neglect. The need assessment finished by Violet team will be attached as a document. The need assessment conducted by Violet revealed that most of the targeted schools by this assessment are lacking the full support, where in some cases Chemonics and the Syrian government are providing low incentives to some of the education personnel.

Implementation of activities

Violet will coordinate closely with Education Directorates in Idleb for high-level topics such as MoUs; for medium-level coordination, Violet will coordinate with education assemblies, local councils for getting their help for facilitating the implementation of the project activities. The project will target 3313 children in formal education who will use the GIS curriculum, in addition to 480 children in the non-formal education who will catch-up, remedial, and literacy, and numeracy sessions. For bringing back the children to schools and advocating for the importance of learning, Violet will conduct 9 Back to Learning Campaigns. Children, education authorities, and community leaders will be invited to participate in the campaigns. The project will rehabilitate 126 classrooms for improving the access and the learning environment. WASH facilities in the target schools are planned to be repaired to be accessible for both girls and boys.BoQs needed will be prepared by the engineers in coordination with the project manager. All 3793 students in the formal and non-formal education will receive student kits, supplementary materials, and recreational kits. On the other hand, 153 teachers will receive teacher kits that help them to better deliver their lessons and develop their teaching methods. For building their capacities that contribute to better teaching and learning, the 153 teachers will receive training in child-centered methodologies in the 9 target schools. Violet will contract with professional training agencies based on strict logistic or HR procedures. .The 198 Education personnel will be paid monthly incentives based on the actual working days. They sign on the timesheets prepared by the HR after calculating their actual attendance, which is submitted then to the finance for preparing the payrolls and paying the incentives. All the 3793 children in the formal and non-formal schools will receive PSS session with a dense focus on the children who have severe psychological traumas who will be referred to specialized centers. The project plans to distribute non-formal text books to 480 children either by printing them or purchasing them from the local market in Syria. For engaging the community in the project activities, the project will form 9 Parent-Teachers Association for taking part in solving the issues related to the children and contributing to developing the education process at the target 9 schools. The PTA will include teachers, principals, parents, and children who hold monthly meetings and provide their feedback to the project management for taking actions and finding solutions. Also,the students will form a Student Council in each school for discussing their matters and suggesting the solution and raising complaints.The head teachers will be responsible for setting the annual plans and timetables for the school staff in each school. Teachers will be supervised by the head teachers for ensuring high-quality teaching. They are required to plan the lessons and prepare the plans under the supervision and support of the 3 education supervisors in each school. The 3 education supervisors will also conduct classroom observation regularly and provide teachers with feedback. Teachers also will form Teachers Learning Circles for exchanging the experiences and learn new teaching methodologies. Plan B will be applied by teachers in the open air, houses, etc in case of any risk. For ensuring the quality of the training delivered to the teachers, The project coordinator receives all the reports and updates from the supervisors and then report them to the project manager after reviewing them. The project manager prepares all the purchase requests and follows up the logistic process until the final stage. The MEAL officer is responsible for monitoring all the distributions, back to learning campaigns, training, and meetings then submit an update to the project manager and to the department MEAL.

Protection Risk Analysis

-The risk: Bullying of children especially IDPs-Mitigating measures: establishment of complaint mechanisms in school, awareness to parents and classmates,-The risk: Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures.-Mitigating measures: members of the community and will always seek prior agreement and active involvement of the traditional leadership structure, activities which engage women will be endorsed by the community and religious leaders to gain support from male community members to reduce the likelihood of resistance to these activities-The risk: access to learning (e.g. responsibilities at home that may prevent girls from going to school; child and/or forced marriage; pregnancy; lack of menstrual hygiene supplies;gender-inequitable attitudes about girls attending school; stigma faced by certain groups; etc.)-Mitigating measures: awareness of all education staff on Codes of Conduct and basic issues related to gender, GBV, women’s/human rights, social exclusion and sexuality
