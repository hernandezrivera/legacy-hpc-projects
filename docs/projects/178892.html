Project Summary

In 2020 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including WASH, reaching a total of 35,081 beneficiaries, of which 90% benefited from our different WASH projects. In 2020 ZOA was able to secure $4,439,160 in funding, of which $1,331,000 was SHF funding. In 2021 ZOA has to date secured $3,888,000 with around $1 million secured for WASH interventions. Through the proposed project ZOA is estimating to target 94,940 beneficiaries, the cost per beneficiary being $21.5.
ZOA is currently distributing water tanks and hygiene kits to vulnerable households, as well as rehabilitating boreholes and sewage networks. Through this proposal ZOA is aiming at expanding these interventions to reach communities most in need of WASH in underserved conflict affected areas in Rural Damascus, Aleppo and Quneitra. All selected sub-districts fall under 3-4 on the severity scale, according to the PiN assessment document shared by the sector. ZOA’s activities focus on the maintenance and/or light rehabilitations of existing WASH systems, specifically prioritizing activities supporting existing water and sanitation systems, including water quality assurance and light rehabilitation of infrastructure. Interventions also include solid waste management activities. The overall object is to provide support to water and sanitation/sewage systems to ensure regular services for vulnerable households in areas with high severity of needs.
ZOA is proposing the following broad activities:
Support to water systems and services: Rehabilitation of 4 water wells will ensure sufficient quantity and quality water for target beneficiaries.Target locations are Markaz Darayaa and Nashabiyeh in Rural Damascus.
Other activities are the installation of household water tanks according to needs and the distribution of hygiene kits in response to community needs in light of COVID-19. Other target locations are Quneitra governorate.
Support to sanitation/sewage networks: Rehabilitation of damaged sewage pipes in Babella and Nashabiyeh Rural Damascus to prevent groundwater infiltration as well as sewage leaks which can contaminate the environment.
Support to solid waste management systems:
- Community debris and garbage clean-up in target communities - The solid waste management activities are especially important in light of the COVID-19 pandemic as breathing difficulties can arise from the fungi that develop from garbage that has not been safely disposed of (WHO, Technical Notes On Drinking-Water, Sanitation And Hygiene In Emergencies). Solid Waste Management helps to prevent the transmission of bacterial infections at a community level.
- Provision of communal garbage bins so target communities can safely dispose of their household waste.
- Provision of operating and maintenance equipment for garbage compressors to enable municipalities to systematically collect garbage.
- Cash for work activities.
- Capacity building of municipalities for operation and maintenance of infrastructure.
Target locations are Aleppo –Tall Ed-daman sub-district and Rural Damascus.
The project activities are aligned with the WASH sector: Objective 1: Support water, sanitation / sewerage, and solid waste management systems to ensure regular service for affected people in Syria.
The ongoing activities, water tanks installations & HK distribution, are based on the needs that ZOA team came across during the activities implemented during 2020 and on PDMs and FGDs done after the activities. ZOA takes the participation of local population and authorities very seriously, therefore, before the final design of any project ZOA will consult the concerned ministry, local authorities (governor, mayor, moukhtars) and organises FGDs with different age groups, so that the activities really serve the needs of the communities. In parallel, ZOA carries out a quick market assessment or search for a recent assessment done by partners, to define the proper modality that works better in the targeted areas.

4 - Needs

In Syria, basic WASH systems have suffered from damage owing to years of functioning at high capacity, with limited or no maintenance and destruction. ZOA’s project is aimed at supporting water and sanitation/sewage systems to ensure regular services. The 2021 HNO shows high needs for WASH assistances, as 12.2 M, 59% of the population in Syria, are in need for WASH support. Access to sufficient quantities of safe water remains limited, with increased water quality assurances and support to water systems rehabilitation, operation and maintenance unconditionally needed. As a result of the crisis at least 70 percent of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, exposing communities to significant health risks. There is a high need for WASH infrastructure repairs and capacity building. With 36% of the general population relying on alternatives to piped water and often unsafe water supply modalities to meet or complement their water needs, at least 70% of sewage being discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems not functional, and about 29% of garbage inappropriately disposed, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria requires significant repair and operational support (incl. capacity building for operation and maintenance of infrastructure ) (HNO 2021). The COVID-19 pandemic, coupled with the economic situation and power shortage in damaged WASH infrastructure are all whistleblowers to be prepared and to plan well for WASH interventions.

5 - Implementation of activities

For all activities: 1 Obtaining access & implementation authorisations from relevant local authorities. 2 Carry out needs assessments, consult with the concerned ministries, WASH sector, local authorities & communities. 3 Generation of BoQs for to ensure that the implemented work will result in the desired outcomes for PWDs, women, men, girls & boys. 4 ZOA will follow its approved procurement process to openly advertise and gather bids from interested contractors. The bids (technical and financial) will be reviewed by a committee and the most suitable contractor will be selected based on an evaluation of cost, quality, experience, and timing. Wells and sewage pipes: 5 Suppliers will be checked for capacity, experience, and compliance to sanctions. Work will be carried out under the supervision of a ZOA WASH engineer responsible for monitoring the quality of work and efficiency. The wells will be diagnosed for technical feasibility taking into account the history of the well, prior rehabilitation procedures/result and capacity. A scale and a water analysis, checking for potential chemical and biological well plugging problems will also be carried out. Work includes excavation, concrete, mechanical and electrical work. 6 Upon completion, ZOA will hand over the completed verified work. 7 Instalment of water tanks. 8 Hygiene Kits distribution. 9 COVID-19 awareness raising. 10 Cash for work. Manager of Program Quality supervises MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework- indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and for demonstrating impact.
