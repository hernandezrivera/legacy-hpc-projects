Project Summary

PCDCR will activate the five emergency response teams-ERTs; each consists of a field coordinator, two counselors and a case manager. The ERTs will be ready to respond to protection, and psychosocial needs of GMR injured individuals (adults and children) and their families. The teams will conduct regular home visits along the project period to respond to the needs of 3500 injured individuals.
The teams will provide different psychosocial and protection services as follows: Structured psychosocial support services will be provided for 4507 injured individuals and affected families including: 1) psychosocial support groups for 525 injured children and their parents (488 boys, 37 girls) through 35 groups, each of 15 children and continues for 6 sessions; 2) psychosocial group counseling for 852 injured individuals (200 boys, 16 girls, 590 males, 46 females) through 71 groups, each of 12 persons and continues for 9 sessions; 3) individual counseling for 715 injured persons (165 boys, 10 girls, 500 males, 40 females); 4) group therapy sessions for 14 injured male adults and 7 injured boys with disabilities, each group of 7 persons and continues for 10 sessions; 5) life skills groups for 2124 injured individuals (660 boys, 48 girls, 1320 men, 96 women) through 177 groups, each group of 12 persons and continues for 6 sessions; 6) stress management groups for 270 women (mothers and wives of injured individuals) each group of 15 women and continues for 4 sessions; 7) sharing days for 2600 injured individuals (1000 boys, 100 girls, 1200 men, 300 women). 8)Helping the helpers including 18 debriefing sessions and 4 sharing days for 40 members of the project team (field, technical and administrative staff).
Additionally, case management and referral services will be provided for 140 injured persons (32 boys, 3 girls, 98 males, 7 females).
Moreover, awareness raising sessions on risks of engagement in GMR will be implemented targeting 7000 individuals (1000 boys, 2000 girls, 1000 men, 3000 women) through 350 groups, each of 20 persons and continues for 2 sessions, and reintegration of injured individuals within their families and community through 120 groups of awareness sessions for 1800 families (600 men, 1200 women), each group involves in 4 sessions.
PCDCR is committed to the five levels of citizen participation and engagement in the different stages of the project management cycle. When identifying the targeted communities, PCDCR took in consideration the statistics of MOH about injured individuals and their families and coordinated with protection cluster to avoid overlapping with other service providers. Through assessing the needs and priorities of the targeted groups (injured individuals and affected families), PCDCR applied the first and second levels of citizen participation “to inform” and “to consult” through conducting interviews and focus groups with injured individuals. Also, PCDCR applied the third level “to involve” through enabling the target groups and other CBOs in the targeted communities to participate in the design of the project activities and interventions based on their identified needs. Additionally, PCDCR will apply the fourth and fifth levels of "to collaborate, and to empower" in the implementation of the project through forming 5 local follow up and support committees from 50 community members including women, men, youth and elderly people. These committees will participate effectively in implementing and following up the project activities, and they will have influential role in monitoring and evaluating the activities. They will share their feedback, comments and opinions with the project staff and partner CBOs to improve the quality of the project.
To enhance accountability, PCDCR will conduct two meetings with beneficiaries in the targeted communities to enable them to provide their feedback and comments about PCDCR performance and staff behavior in the project.

1 - Needs

Gaza Strip is witnessing the most deteriorated humanitarian situation at all levels (economic, psychosocial and political levels). This deteriorated situation resulted from Israel's ongoing occupation, the 12-year-old continuing internal Palestinian division, the successive Israeli offensives on the GS and the blockade that has been imposed on GS since 2007. Consequently, Gazans has started the "Great March of Return" demonstrations since 30 March 2018 where they demonstrated at the perimeter fence separating Gaza from Israel to demand the end of the Israeli blockade and the right of return for refugees. According to MoH, from 30 Mar 2018 until 7 Sep 2019, 312 people were killed, of whom 96.2% were male and 3.8% were female, and 19.6% (61) were children. The total no. of injured people was 34,282 where 54.4% attended hospital for treatment, 45.6% were treated at field medical points. Among the total no. of injuries, 6.6% (1222) women were injured, 21.8% (4067) children were injured. The total number of amputations was 158, including 29 children. In Mar 2019, PCDCR finished a study that identifying the needs of the GMR injured persons in GS. After asking the targeted sample about their most important 5 needs, the results were as following: 1- Regular provision of necessary food (69%), 2- Provision of adequate health care (61.8%), 3- The ability to maintain personal security and safety (57.5%), 4- Job opportunities and income sources (57%) and 5- An effective solution to the physical and logistical constraints affecting movement (mobility) within the community (53.5%). 81.1% of injured persons targeted in the study stated that their lives had been significantly affected in a negative way, and 60% of them fear incoherence among their families or the inability to create one in the future. Additionally, 39% of the target sample confirmed that they were harassed, verbally 89.1%, physically 10.3% or sexually 0.6% after their injuries. 47.5% of injured women, 43.8% of injured children, and 36.7% of amputees confirmed the harassment. The study has shown that 33.3% of those injured persons were harassed by the community, 30.8% by a relative, 24.4% by a friend, and 11.5% by a neighbor. Furthermore, 96.3% of targeted persons stated that the family is considered the primary incubator provides them with needed support. The study has also shown that 33.8% of the injured persons participated in psychosocial support activities and 89.6% of them said that these sessions helped them overcome their difficult time with their injuries. 65% of injured women said they did not participate in psychosocial support sessions after the injury; 30% of them were aged 18-25, 7.5% of them were aged 26-32, and 27.5% above the age of 33. On the other hand, 53.8% of injured children, and 46.7% of amputees said they did not participate in psychosocial support sessions after the injury. PCDCR implemented two HF projects targeting injured persons & their families in Gaza, Khan Younis, & North Governorates. However, after approx. one and half years of starting GMR protests, the no. of injured individuals in Rafah and Middle area is increased where 3027 injured persons are in Middle Area and 2142 injured persons are in Rafah, and they became in dire need to different protection and psychosocial interventions. Additionally, as reported by the neighborhood committees which consists of CBOs, community activists and leaders, established by PCDCR in the Middle Area and Rafah, there are a large no. of injured persons who are in need to PSS services and there is a lack in providing these services for both adults and children. Moreover, 30% of the calls that the current project team receives are from injured persons in Middle Area and Rafah asking for PSS services, while the case managers received many cases of injured persons in these two areas referred by other injured persons who already receive these services in PCDCR.

Activity 1: conduct regular home visits for 3500 injured children and adults based on individual needs of each patient to provide services (family counseling, individual counseling, and case management). Activity 2: provide psychosocial support groups for 525 injured children and their parents (488 boys, 37 girls) through 35 groups, each of 15 children and continues for 6 sessions. Activity 3: provide psychosocial group counseling for 852 injured individuals (200 boys, 16 girls, 590 males, 46 females) through 71 groups, each of 12 persons and continues for 9 sessions.Activity 4: provide individual counseling for 715 injured persons (165 boys, 10 girls, 500 males, 40 females). Activity 5: provide group therapy for 14 injured male adults and 7 injured boys with disabilities through 3 groups, each group of 7 persons and continues for 10 sessions.Activity 6: provide life skills groups for 2124 injured individuals (660 boys, 48 girls, 1320 men, 96 women) through 177 groups, each group of 12 persons and continues for 6 sessions. Activity 7: provide stress management groups for 270 women (mothers and wives of injured individuals) each group of 15 women and continues for 4 sessions. Activity 8: conduct 20 sharing days for 2600 injured individuals (1000 boys, 100 girls, 1200 men, 300 women).Activity 9: Helping the helpers activities including 18 debriefing sessions and 4 sharing days for 40 members of field, technical and administrative project team. While children receive medical support, assistive devices, child protection, and education services. Activity 11: Provide supervision and coaching for the case management team. Activity 12: conduct awareness raising groups on risks of engagement in GMR targeting 7000 individuals (1000 boys, 2000 girls, 1000 men, 3000 women) through 350 groups, each of 20 persons and continues for 2 sessions.Activity 13: reintegration of injured individuals within their families and community through 120 groups of awareness sessions for 1800 families (600 men, 1200 women), each group involves in 4 sessions. Activity 14: Monitoring and Evaluation: The project team will establish an internal system of M&E based on systematic reporting, regular meetings, and follow up of implemented activities. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project management will prioritize the safety and dignity of project’s beneficiaries through providing services to vulnerable groups (injured children and adults) in full confidently and with very private manner in order to encourage those targeted groups to receive this kind of support without being stigmatized. Whereas, PCDCR will implement the PSS activities in partner CBOs in the targeted governorates, these CBOs will be selected according to their accessibility by the beneficiaries, adaptation to people with disabilities, their existence in a safe place, and the availability of a suitable space to implement the activities and keep the privacy of the beneficiaries. Additionally, each member of project staff will be committed to PCDCR child protection policies and women protection policies besides signing children code of conduct as well as each one of the implemented partners throughout the five governorates of GS will sign on code of conduct that adhere them to promote safety, dignity, and confidentiality of beneficiaries’ data. Further, all children’s caregivers should sign on a permission document to allow their children to participate in project activities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Consultation with beneficiaries (injured persons and affected families) will be carried out to identify adequate time for the activities taking in consideration other medical services the injured persons receive, and women responsibility in homemaking and childcaring. Furthermore, to guarantee the participation of injured individual with mobility problems, transportation is provided to beneficiaries and careful selection of CBOs where the activities will be implemented to assure that these CBOs adapted to PWDs.For monitoring purposes, all the indicators are disaggregated by age and gender, and five committees of beneficiaries will be activated to contribute in project implementation, beneficiaries access, providing feedback and monitoring and evaluation of the project.
