Project Summary

The project aims to support the WASH response of the most vulnerable displaced populations in Northwest Syria through implementing the following set of activities:
i) Provision of emergency WASH services to 50 IDP sites (informal camps, planned camps, and reception centres) in 2023, regularly supporting 100,000 individuals in 2023. Activities include the provision of water services, solid waste management, faecal sludge management, , care and maintenance of latrines, provision of water storage tanks, garbage containers, and WASH NFI kits, as well as hygiene promotion, capacity building and community engagement sessions. (Activity 2.1 and 2.2)
ii) Construction/rehabilitation of sewage networks, including the construction of septic tanks to provide an initial treatment of wastewater produced in informal camps. This specific intervention will target 15 informal IDP sites in 2023, supporting 30,000 individuals in 2023. (Activity 1.3)
iii) Enhanced water quality assurance, operation, and maintenance of water supply systems through the provision of solar energy in 10 IDP sites in 2023, supporting 22,000 individuals in 2023 (Activity 1.2)
iv) Provision of WASH services and rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 18 health facilities in 2023, supporting 100,000 individuals in 2023. (Activity 4.2)
Overall, the project will contribute to the following strategic objectives 1,2 and 4 set out by the WASH Cluster.

3 - Needs

Across NWS, the damage to and destruction of existing water supply systems, exacerbated by rising needs due to displacement, have impacted communities’ access to water. In NWS, 70 per cent of IDPs in camps and 33 per cent of people in non-camps settings residing with host communities continue to rely on water trucking, while nearly a million people are facing a gap in at least one lifesaving WASH service or supply (650,000 in communities and 220,000 IDPs in camps WASH Cluster, “Northwest Syria WASH Cluster Early Warning Alert”. Recent field monitoring and assessments show severe gaps in water supply and storage, an insufficient number of latrines and hygiene items. Furthermore, in NWS, there are now over 336 cholera cases being reported as of November 19th, 2022. Access to adequate WASH facilities is a critical measure in preventing the transmission of communicable diseases, including COVID-19, and outbreaks of water and vector-borne diseases such as cholera. Where WASH services are inadequate, public health risks are increased which adversely impacts all sectors including health, nutrition, education, livelihoods, and protection as people resort to increasingly negative coping mechanisms to meet their basic WASH needs “Northwest Syria WASH Cluster Early Warning Alert”. Moreover, during the winter, heavy rain and low temperatures leave people vulnerable to flooding and cold weather, particularly for thousands of displaced people living in tents in informal settlements.

4 - Implementation of activities

IOM will work with established partners and coordinate with the WASH Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure needs are met without duplication. For the emergency WASH services, IOM’s IPs will carry out a competitive bidding process to identify contractors for water supplying, Fecal sludge management, and solid waste management. IOM will continue providing the water trucking service in the areas where there is no other water supplying systems in place. A KAP Survey will be carried out by the end of the year to understand the quality of the WASH services the emergency WASH programming. For the rehabilitation and construction of connecting drainage and sewage networks, IOM’s IPs will carry out technical assessments for each site, including topography studies. For the operation and maintenance of water supply facilities, IOM will work with experienced IPs to procure and set up solar panels to maintain newly established water facilities in informal sites and planned camps. Lastly, for the WASH services and infrastructure in health facilities, IOM will work with experienced implementing partners who are currently running these facilities. Technical assessments and BoQs will be prepared and reviewed by IOM technical staff. For all infrastructure activities (sanitation and water facilities), IOM will pay attention to HLP dynamics and ensure land ownership is identified prior to any infrastructure works, and agreements are signed with landowners and local councils.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

For the provision of water trucking, there is a risk that the quality and quantity of water does not meet the SPHERE standards. To ensure the provision of safe water, regular water quality monitoring and testing will be carried out and water quantity tracking will take place to meet the requirements. For all in-camp activities, there is a potential risk of congesting the sites through the movement of water-trucking, desludging, solid waste management, and provision of solar energy for water facilities. This issue can be mitigated by consulting/engaging the community regarding the services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedules. Specifically, on the removal of solid waste from the sites, there are associated health risks for those handling the waste. This risk can be mitigated through the provision of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and ensuring the safety regulations are abided by. Similarly, for the construction/rehabilitation of sewage networks, PPE will be provided to ensure IP staff or contractors are not exposed to unsafe chemicals. For the distribution of WASH NFIs, there is a risk on distribution of expired items and also selling of the in-kind kits. IOM will mitigate the expiration of items through conducting detailed quality control checks to the supplier’s warehouse. As for the selling of items, IOM will conduct consultations with beneficiaries on the kits contents ahead of the distribution to ensure the IDPs require these items.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All IP staff are required to have undertaken trainings on PSEA and signed a PSEA Code of Conduct. Details of IOM’s AAP mechanism are communicated to participants, as well as the Interagency PSEA hotline where appropriate. Gender segregated lines and gender-mixed distribution teams are always used by IPs to further mitigate SEA risks. As IOM is the leading agency for PSEA, all programmes are required to follow the network’s recommendations and incorporate them into each project. For WASH infrastructure, IOM makes sure all IP field teams consist of male and female members, and each beneficiary will receive a poster with the PSEA hotline number to report any misconduct by the project staff.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There are several risks which are beyond the project’s control, these include: access restrictions due to the shifting of frontlines/active fighting, aid diversion by armed groups, loss of assets through looting or bombing, non-renewal of the UNSC resolution, inflation of market prices (particularly fuel). The project will take the following measures to mitigate the abovementioned risks: regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents of attempted interference or shifting security risks. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations in coordination with the WASH Cluster and project donors. On aid diversion and loss of assets, IOM will invest in sound security measures in warehouses inside Syria and train staff on how to handle attempted aid diversion. In cases of conflict-induced damages to partners’ warehouses/trucks, IOM will ensure IPs provide timely incident reports to its access unit and share the information with other actors on the ground, donors, and clusters. Lastly, on the inflation of prices, IOM will conduct regular market monitoring.
