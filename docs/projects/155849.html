Needs

Gender-based violence, particularly verbal harassment, domestic violence (including family violence against women and girls), child/early and forced marriages and the fear of sexual violence, continue to be part of the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. While the reach of UNFPA's GBV interventions have expanded to 322 communities across Syria (as of Nov 2018 4W data) there are still 6,478 communities with no GBV services. Efforts have been made to expand GBV services to north east syria. The lack of awareness of the need for GBV health services in some communities is still high, indicating a need to strengthen GBV awareness, outreach and prevention activities. Sudden displacements and shifting lines of control continue to put women and girls at risk especially in Idleb, Deir Ez Zor, Raqqa, Rural Damascus and Rural Aleppo are likely to require emergency interventions. The fear of sexual violence, often associated with abduction, is a concern raised by women and girls, contributing to psychosocial stress and further limitation of their movements. The restriction on freedom of movement of women and girls also inhibits their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately their rights. Women and girls also fear so called honour killing as a result of sexual violence and/or perceived sexual contact. Families arrange marriages for girls, believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden on the family and girls are reportedly being married at the age of 11 and 12. The socio-economic situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, and increased poverty is ultimately leading more women to resort to negative coping mechanisms such as survival sex. Risks of GBV continue to be identified in other sectoral interventions.

Implementation of activities

The project aims to increase by 50% availability of and access to specialized GBV services across Syria through static facilities and mobile teams. UNFPA will increase its support and services to female headed households and adolescent girls, widows, divorcees (as those identified as the most at risk of GBV in the HNO) and focus on empowering them through women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), community wellbeing centers and integrated RH/GBV outreach services. Special attention will be made to increase the inclusiveness of GBV services to people living with disabilities. UNFPA will continue to lead the GBV coordination mechanisms and enhance the quality of the GBV response through building the capacity of member organizations, strengthening and updating referral pathways and SOPs. Dignity kits will also be procured to respond to sudden displacements and have been tailored to meet the specific needs of women, men, adolescent boys and girls. 1.1 / 1.2 Provide lifesaving specialized quality GBV services including case management, psychosocial support and reinforce referral pathways throughout Syria. Where possible, legal advice will be provided from the Damascus hub with referrals to legal partners as needed. This will translate in an anticipated 394 number of community reached with specialised services (all response services captured in the 4Ws) and 1,175,791 GBV services provided. 1.3 The capacity of 2100 services providers will be built to ensure quality GBV services are provided in a confidential, safe, respectful and non-discriminatory manner. Services providers will be trained on CMR and case management (both of which includes specific provisions for men, boys and girls having experienced sexual violence), adolescent girl friendly programming and disabilities 2.1 UNFPA will engage in strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV. These include implementation of the WoS adolescent girls strategy through its 4 pillars 1- generating evidence on the differential needs of adolescent girls, ensuring that services (GBV, SRH and others) are adolescent girl friendly, 3. UNFPA aims to reach 644,350 beneficiaries (257,000 women, 193,000 men, 129,000 girls and 64,000 boys) 3.1 UNFPA will train up to 435 humanitarian actors in mainstreaming on GBV. Protection Risk Analysis

Providing GBV services are critical to ensuring survivors are able to recover and heal, if these are not confidential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. Communities may be resistant to engaging around topics of GBV and resist there being Women and girl safe spaces and ultimately prevent women and girls from attending. As a result, UNFPA carries out thorough risks analysis of its interventions and works with communities to reduce risks of GBV. UNFPA strives to ensure that all services are confidential by building the capacity of its partners on the guiding principles of working on GBV so that survivors of GBV will feel comfortable accessing them but also to make sure that there are no negative repercussions in accessing them. Communities are always engaged when WGSSs and community wellbeing centers are established to ensure their acceptance. UNFPA is leading efforts to mitigate the risks of GBV. As such UNFPA has organized and facilitated trainings for a variety of sectors to ensure that actions to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV are included in their responses. UNFPA has established strong M&E systems. In addition to UNFPA’s regular M&E standard reporting practices such as onsite visits, through third party facilitators, monthly and quarterly partner meetings, UNFPA conducts quarterly assessments using client feedback forms at health facilities and WGSSs, key information interviews with service providers, and FGDs at WGSSs. Third party monitors are used to verify program quality and implementation through its cross-border operations.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access, permissions and bureaucratic hurdles may slow the implementation however, UNFPA actively works with its national partners to over come these challenges. Assumption that communities will continue to engage in preventing GBV. While GBV remains a difficult subject in many communities, this can be overcome by actively engaging with communities. Another risk associated with the project is mainstreaming fatigue as various organisations aim to mainstream GBV, gender, disability and other protection issues. The GBV AoR assumes that this fatigue will occur and has prepared to provide tools, checklists and technical support (Skype calls, email, meetings) to facilitate technical support in developing and implementing mitigating measures across sectors. UNFPA as the lead of the GBV AoR, as a mitigating measure, will work with colleagues addressing gender, disability and protection risks in order to synthesize and streamline processes e.g. conduct a FGD that addresses the GBV risks associated with person(s) with disabilities or pilot an indicator that addresses GBV and disability for women and girls.
