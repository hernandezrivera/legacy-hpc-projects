Project Summary

GOALPrime Organization Nigeria (GPON) targets to reach 31,000 individuals, aiming to provide comprehensive aid across various sectors, 10% of the total target are persons living with disability. Among these, 10,000 survivors of sexual assault—80% girls and 20% boys—will receive specialized GBV case management, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), and vital psychosocial support services.
Through house-to-house sensitization efforts, 10,000 individuals (60% women/girls, 40% men/boys) will be educated on GBV prevention and strategies to address harmful social norms. Additionally, 10,000 women and girls will benefit from the distribution of Menstrual Hygiene and dignity kits. Another 5,000 individuals (60% women/girls, 40% men/boys) will partake in peer support, mentoring, skills building, and empowerment programs in various LGAs.
To enhance awareness, 20,000 Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials will be disseminated strategically and created collaboratively in Hausa, Kanuri, and English languages. These materials will educate and sensitize beneficiaries while being distributed across all engagement locations. Radio jingles/messages on gender-based violence will be aired consistently across Mafa and Monguno, Damboa, and Kala-Balge LGAs. Child-friendly reporting mechanisms and zero GBV clubs will be established in schools and camps, complemented by phone booth services for case management support. GPON's quarterly capacity-building sessions will empower 50 women and girls in each engagement location, enabling them to respond effectively within their communities. GBV workers will receive field-level capacity building on proper case management.
The project aims to identify and support 300 girls facing livelihood challenges, linking them with livelihood support partners. Strengthening GBV referral pathways and quarterly coordination among project actors in each site will also be key initiatives. Targeting 650 individuals (80% girls, 20% boys), the project seeks to provide continuous community-based Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). In schools, the children will be reached through structured recreational activities and creative events such as Interclub and House competitions, GBV prevention and response awareness, and peer support sessions organized in school clubs and other club activities.
GOALPrime prioritizes COVID-19 prevention measures, integrating child-focused risk communication and protection into its activities. The organization ensures environmental sensitivity and the safety of beneficiaries. Collaborating with NHF-supported Humanity Studio, GOALPrime will create resource materials, and support GBV referral pathways, newsletters, advocacy notes, and other relevant resource materials. GOALPrime will be sensitive to the protection of the environment and ensure that our approaches and tools do not harm the environment and the beneficiaries. GOALPrime will also work with partners of other sectors across target locations and provide them with advice on how to mainstream protection into their interventions.

1 - Needs

Over 3 million people across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states need interventions for the prevention of and response to gender-based violence (GBV). The underlying drivers of the conflict and conditions of displacement continue to exacerbate the occurrence of GBV incidents. Half of this population in need consists of children (below 18 years) with the majority being adolescent girls. Sexual violence - including rape, sexual exploitation, forced and early marriage, survival sex and domestic violence, physical assault, denial of resources, and psychological and emotional abuse - are among the most frequently reported violations, recurrently perpetrated by intimate or former partners. Minors, particularly adolescent girls, are also at risk of GBV, which occurs mainly in the context of child sexual abuse and early marriage. Also, an estimated 2.7 million children and adolescents (1.5 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in north-east Nigeria require protection and cross-sectoral services. Threats and risks to the well-being of girls and boys include family separation, explosive remnants of war, gender-based violence including child marriage and conflict-related sexual violence, recruitment and use of children, abduction, and other grave child rights violations. At least 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent of girls) are estimated to have been recruited, abducted, killed, or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Of those, approximately 50 per cent are survivors of SGBV. The crisis also has caused severe psychological distress among children. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but has also negatively affected the ability of parents and communities to appropriately care for their children.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

By integrating every strategy with the protection principles, GOALPrime will mainstream protection (GBV & PSEA) throughout the execution of this project and advance meaningful access, safety, and dignity of beneficiaries, employers, and volunteers. Safety, decency, and avoiding any kind of harm to both beneficiaries and employees are priorities for GOALPrime. Additionally, GOALPrime will also make sure that the target population has equitable access to the service, free from impediments and prejudice to their needs. GOALPrime will pay attention to people and groups who could be especially vulnerable or have a hard time obtaining help and resources, including those with disabilities and GBV survivors who have been identified. GOALPrime will integrate GBV Risk Mitigation efforts to reduce or decrease GBV risk to beneficiaries. GOALPrime will work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. Also, GOALPrime will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). All staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project will be given PSEA orientation. They will be familiarized with GOALPrime's policy on PSEA, and they MUST express their unwavering commitment by signing GOALPrime's Zero Tolerance to SEA Code of Conduct. Also, GOALPrime will lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately, they occur. GOALPrime will also ensure that the identity of the reporter(s) is kept very confidential. When convenient, GOALPrime will encourage reports to be made on anonymous grounds. Also, being guided by the Inter-Agency mechanism for reporting SEA, GOALPrime will ensure a systematized engagement with affected populations including confidential complaints and investigations procedures at both the system and individual agency levels. As recommended by the IASC Task Force on PSEA which identified inter-agency cooperation in the creation and maintenance of community-based complaints mechanisms (CBCMs) as a key component in the prevention and response to SEA. GOALPrime will continue to raise awareness to the target population that humanitarian assistance is free and never conditioned on sexual favours as well as to be informed on how to access the appropriate complaints mechanisms, and support to set up in the community if SEA occurs. Furthermore, GOALPrime will ensure that established CBCMs in target locations are effective that there is inter-agency coordination to ensure consistent messaging and that access to the mechanism is as broad and straightforward as possible for potential complainants. GOALPrime will also be intentional about the environment and safety of the communities where we implement by ensuring that our approaches and tools do not contribute to the degradation of the environment. In addition, GOALPrime will also integrate climate change and provide routine sensitization of the project beneficiaries on climate change prevention.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Through this project, GOALPrime will strengthen the community stakeholders' and project beneficiaries' participation in the decision-making and execution phases of the project by consolidating its existing community-based project accountability framework. Considering the lessons, we acquired from our past implementation in the target LGAs, this initiative was designed in collaboration with children and community stakeholders. To facilitate decision-making for high-quality project implementation, GPON employs a people-centered feedback process to collect comments and grievances from community members and project beneficiaries. Lessons from the strategy, however, include creating a project committee at the LGA level with representatives from all community groups and stakeholders to increase the impacted community's involvement in the project's implementation. Additionally, GPON has provided SMSs, direct messaging, anonymous complaint/feedback boxes, SMSs, and direct calls to the Compliance and Accountability team. GPON project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the established community-based project committee to discuss the project deliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. Also, the LGA project committee will work with the project team to define project beneficiaries’ vulnerability based on the context of the LGA, agree on vulnerability criteria, and provide a recommended approach on how the project can be implemented to better reach and serve the project targeted groups. Also, the project team will conduct quarterly review meetings with the community-based project committee at the LGA level to inform how each quarter can consolidate with the previous quarter's achievement to improve the quality of services rendered.
