Project Summary

This intervention links to the strategic objectives outlined in the 2023 revised HRP by providing a life-saving humanitarian intervention through an integrated health, nutrition and WASH package for displaced population, their host communities, returnees and other vulnerable groups in West Darfur, South and West Kordofan States. The total direct target for integrated services under all sectors is 800,000 beneficiaries across West Darfur, South and West Kordofan States. The project will focus on addressing protracted health, nutrition and WASH needs of most vulnerable target population, and enhancing basic health and nutrition statuses of target population through static and mobile outreach service in the targeted localities, alongside improving the institutional WASH component. Concern’s operations in the target localities are longstanding. The proposed operational and financial plans are based on projections through BHA, EU, ECHO, Irish Aid, UN and private funding. 87 facilities will be supported across target locations – namely 24 in West Darfur, and 63 in Kordofan. 9 health facilities have been added to the previous figure of 78 health facilities flagged for support in the previous version of the HRP. The intended revised target represents 55% coverage out of a total amount of 157 facilities across target localities. Increased access to essential lifesaving primary health care (PHC) quality health services, including provision of pharmaceuticals, consumables, and LLINs, maternal, reproductive health, and child health and community education for vulnerable populations (pregnant and lactating women, and children under 5) will be provided. In line with the revised objectives for the health sector, Concern will focus on the list of previously identified facilities as accessible areas for humanitarian interventions including strengthening existing emergency obstetric and new-born care facilities, covering operational costs, fuel and WASH support in health facilities. In addition, reactivating the disease surveillance system and reporting in both government and partner’s supported facilities and supplying the static vaccination centres with the needed supplies will be a priority. The need for increased access to essential, lifesaving, and quality nutrition services and community sensitization for the management of moderate to severe acute malnutrition of the vulnerable population (pregnant and lactating women, and children under 5) will also be addressed. In line with the revised objectives for the nutrition sector, and in the same target areas as per health interventions (to ensure greater integration), Concern will scale up maternal and young child nutrition, IYCF practices, early detection and treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-five children, pregnant and lactating women, preventive nutrition interventions, and micronutrient supplementation among children under age 5 and PLW to prevent further deterioration of nutrition situation. Finally, improvement and maintenance of WASH services within health facilities, and hygiene-related community sensitization for vulnerable population (pregnant and lactating women, and children under 5), including distribution of essential dignity kits, will be guaranteed. In line with the revised HRP and at targeted facility levels, Concern will support provision or rehab of latrines, incinerators, and handwashing points depending on needs assessments; water trucking; distribution of essential WASH supplies and NFI emergency sanitation; and extensive hygiene promotion especially for those reliant on surface/river water. Partnerships with national NGOs are foreseen.

1 - Needs

Since the beginning of the current conflict, health service provision has been heavily impacted across the country in Khartoum and country-wide. Due to heavily centralized health system and reliance on the country’s capital for medical supplies distribution, performing laboratory tests, funding, and other aspects of the health provision across the country, all states were negatively affected by the violence that erupted in Khartoum. The bulk of the strategic medical supplies stored in the medical supply fund fell under the control of one of the parties to conflict impacting the distribution capacity to health facilities across the country. Six-month stock of malaria medicine is no longer accessible impacting treatments to over 3 million cases of malaria reported annually. The national vaccination programme has been disrupted amidst an ongoing measles outbreak reported in 15 states across the country before the beginning of the crisis. The main cold chain storage facility in Khartoum housing the bulk of the nations’ vaccines is no longer accessible. The disease surveillance system is currently inactive. Prior to 15 April, sexual and reproductive health coverage was low due to limited supplies, infrastructure and capacity. The situation was catastrophically impacted by the armed clashes, resulting in casualties including amongst pregnant women who are in need of pre and postnatal care in the midst of significant displacement from Khartoum, West Darfur and other affected states like the greater Kordofan.Conflict-induced displacement of households has resulted in increased vulnerability to malnutrition. The deterioration of the nutrition situation has led to an estimated 30% increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition in hotspot areas, a 15% increase in IDP hosting states and a 10% increase in other localities. Interruption of nutrition monitoring and reporting impacting on the understanding of the evolving nutrition situation in most parts of the country. The interruption of life-saving health and nutrition services in hotspot areas has led to thousands missing life-saving nutrition interventions. Thousands could not continue treatment due to the closure of nutrition facilities, insecurity and interruption of supplies and absence of nutrition service providers. Supplies are urgently needed due to the increase in acute malnutrition cases, as well as the loss of nutrition supplies through looting and direct damage and disruption of nutrition supplies replenishment chain system from national, state and partners warehouses to service delivery sites.WASH needs have increased significantly at health facility levels in both urban and rural settings. The bulk water treatment and supply systems have been compromised. Existing displaced persons, including the most vulnerable residents, and an increased number of displaced people in new locations, including international borders, are a matter of significant concern. The already limited water sources are under extreme pressure due to increased population concentration in new IDP concentration points, skyrocketing cost of fuel has resulted in a 300 to 400% increase in the operation of fuel-reliant water supply systems as well water trucking. The rainy season will further heighten the risk of outbreaks in the coming months. Water-borne diseases and epidemics are a threat due to the deteriorating WASH situation, including no waste disposal services, water safety, poor hygiene, disruption of the supply chain, and access concerns.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During the project design and implementation, climate change adaptation approaches will be considered particularly for the renovation and management of health/nutrition facilities/water sources. During the technical assessment and design of the renovation work, the reduction of water waste will be one of the most critical elements to consider, as well as solar systems. Nutrition and health activities will also consider the environmental footprint by using environmentally manageable materials for building renovation. Waste management (debris) will be organized during and after the renovation. Capacity building will conducted on waste management (particularly medical waste management, by means of appropriate storing and incinerating). Natural and locally available resources will be considered as much as possible to reduce the environmental footprint.

Inorder to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groupsand backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing aboutproject implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be providedcontinuously through a selected focal person in each group.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by two central documents, notably the organisation’s Programme Participant Protection Policy and Code of Conduct; and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Standard in Accountability and Quality Management. Concern’s Programme Participant Protection Policy sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries – children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii)the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. As certified member of HAP, Concern is committed to implementing the HAP Standard related to different aspects of beneficiaries’ accountability. Three of these aspects are particularly relevant to conflict sensitivity and protection: beneficiaries’ access to information; beneficiaries’ participation in all the stages of the programme; and establishing a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM). As of August 2022, Concern Sudan’s Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) was updated and aligned with overall staff and communities’ expectations as well as ground realities through a consultative exercise undertaken with the aim to adjust Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Concern will incorporate beneficiaries’ feedback through two ways. Every 6 months Accountability team will conduct FGDs with programme participants, as well as interviews with other stakeholders related to effectiveness of CRM system. At the time of conclusion of a complaint, each complainant will be asked for their feedback using a Satisfaction and Consultation form. Based on data received from these two sources, Concern will regularly evaluate and re-design the CRM system to improve its accessibility, responsiveness, efficiency and anonymity. Accountability towards affected populations as well as awareness raising is ensured through information materials as brochures, posters, business cards in Arabic, as well as through an informative video developed for the process. All posters, brochures, and business cards will be distributed to programme participants and will be posted/available wherever Concern Sudan work to ensure transparent information sharing.
