Project Summary

The project ensures the continuity of shelter and Non-Food Items (NFIs) provision for the crisis affected population in Diffa. It will focus on maintaining the shelter and NFI working group coordination mechanism and providing assistance to vulnerable households with integrated transitional shelters, NFI kits and income generating activities all with the aim of allowing them to meet their most basic needs hence improving their resilience to cope with their situation while maintaining a contingency stock of emergency shelters for new displacements as the security situation remains volatile

Needs

In Diffa, given the persistence of the crisis 5 years on, the shelter and NFI working group is adapting to the New Way of Working vision, proposing more sustainable solutions in shelter to the affected population. The evaluation conducted by REACH in the months of August and September 2018 highlighted that there were large needs in both shelter and NFI assistance. Shelter needs; 248,993 persons (35,570 households) largely out-weighing the NFI needs; 155,381 persons (22,200 households). Given the prolonged nature of the crisis and uncertainty of the crisis ending in the near future, the needs in shelter (35,570 households) have to be adapted to give a more sustainable longer-term solution hence provision of transitional shelters (t-shelter) to replace emergency shelters (e-shelter) has been adopted by the shelter and NFI working group as the strategy for response in shelter. Granted that t-shelters cannot be implemented in all the areas affected by the crisis because of the persistent and continuous insecurity in those areas, more stable areas like Chetimari, Mainé-Soroa and Diffa communes with a combined population in need of shelter assistance of 154,421 persons (22,060 households) can benefit from t-shelters hence improving on the resilience and protection of the assisted population. 51% of affected population being female and 55% of the total dsiplaced population being under 18 years of age, NFI kit are adapted to respond to the needs of women and children with hygiene kits included for women and clothers for children.It needs to be noted that some areas in the commune of Bosso are still inaccessible to humanitarian actors hence the evaluation carried out did not include needs for some communes in the department of Bosso which could lead to an overall increase in needs for both shelters and NFI. In line with its Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises (GBViC Framework), IOM will integrate measures to mitigate risks of GBV (GBV mainstreaming) throughout the project/in its interventions through the use of mixed gender teams for delivery of assistance and also inclusion of beneficiaries in decision making about the project implementation, hence ensuring that services are delivered in a safe and ethical way, and that they do not cause or put the affected population at risk of any further harm.
