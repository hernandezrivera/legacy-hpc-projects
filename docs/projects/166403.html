Project Summary

This project comprises the Common Service for Community Engagement and Accountability - which will provide a wide range of reactive and responsive capacity strengthening support, communication materials, feedback analysis and knowledge products to agencies and sectors working on the Covid-19 response.

Needs

Since the Covid-19 epidemic entered the awareness of Rohingya people, the situation regarding information availability and participation has shifted, although this is a rapidly evolving area and fully robust data is difficult to collect. Early results of recent surveys suggest that most people (93%) already have at least some awareness of hygiene and distancing precautions related to Covid-19, although the level of knowledge varied substantially. When asked, people highlighted the need for information about how they could access treatment if they or their family became sick; and how they could continue to access food and other relief during the current situation. People also highlighted information needs related to preventing Covid-19 and access to work or money, but these were less prevalent in the sample. These findings resonate with qualitative research which has found that, while basic awareness messages have been disseminated, the Rohingya community identify a need for more regular and consistent news and updates. Beyond health-related information about Covid-19, there is a strong need to ensure that refugees and host communities have access to information about changes in service provision and other non-health effects of the epidemic. Restrictions and response to Covid-19 is likely to also result in an increased need for CwC interventions in other areas - for example work to prevent gender-based violence - including attitudinal and social norms interventions - and work to address stigma, social conflict and fear and anxiety. In both Rohingya and host communities, men knew more about coronavirus than women. Both the communities were aware that coronavirus was spreading in many countries worldwide. People had heard many things about coronavirus from a range of different sources on a regular basis. In the discussions, men mentioned about ‘coronavirus’. They had read news about it on the internet from Bangla and Burmese news sources. They specifically mentioned Facebook pages and YouTube channels developed by the Rohingya community based in Malaysia and Saudi Arabia. They mentioned R-vision, which is a popular YouTube channel among the Rohingya community in the camps. They said that they were also receiving information from Rohingya family and friends living in Malaysia and Saudi Arabia, who shared information they had received from Facebook pages and WhatsApp groups. Some Rohingya men said that they had learned about coronavirus from watching television in local tea stalls in the camps. Others had heard about it from Bangladeshi radio and television channels, probably Bangladesh Betar, or Myanmar radio channels. Men from the host community said that they had heard about coronavirus from a range of sources including television, newspapers and Facebook. They also said that they had discussed the information with others at tea stalls. Women from the host community said radio was one of the main sources where they received information and said that they had learned that the coronavirus was starting to spread through Bangladesh. People from both the host and Rohingya community expressed their concern about coronavirus and said they wanted to know more about how it was spread. Specifically, they wanted to know how to protect themselves and how the virus affects people. They said that they did not know where to go for information about how to stay safe, about the symptoms of the virus, and about what to do if they were infected with coronavirus. Although Covid 19 is one of the most discussed issues among the Rohingya community, older men and women do not have as much information about the topic as other people. Older people in the community say that they want more detailed information about the virus and the disease. In particular, older women in the Rohingya community have little access to information about Covid-19. Activities or outputs

The reactive element of the existing common service approach will be repurposed and expanded to meet demand related to Covid-19 CwC services. We will maintain capacity to support agencies, sectors and government where they come to us with requests for support (content, tools, training, analysis) and we will continue to prioritise requests that are broadly applicable, where the support we provide can benefit more than one agency. Principal activities in support of the Covid-19 response will be: Assessments, research and studies: - Using rapid research and analysis to publish immediate insights and guidance (related to both refugee and host communities) that support humanitarian organisations to plan community engagement, and other services, in a rapidly changing environment. Insights from this research – including inputs from women, older people and other underserved groups – will also guide the project’s own work in terms of defining priorities for communication resources and capacity development activities. Communication resources: - Supporting CwC WG and the risk communications TWG in their work linked to Covid-19, including providing expert advice and technical capacity in support of the health response, continued access to critical services and secondary effects of the epidemic. This will include in-depth support to the Cox’s Bazar RCCE strategy and the development of the JRP revisions. - Proactively identifying gaps and solutions to ensure the focus of these groups and other actors is in line with the urgency, access and changing information needs to respond to Covid-19. - Production of appropriate communication tools for wide use in both refugee and host communities, in response to sector and agency requests and designed for use on channels that remain open to communities. Materials will include tools that target specific groups who are underserved by current provision – including tools targeted specifically to female audiences and older people. Wherever possible given physical restrictions, communication materials will be tested with target audiences and adapted and iterated in response to both formal testing and informal feedback received through partners. Capacity development: - Responding to bilateral requests for technical guidance on programming and approaches, to cover both refugee and host communities. - Training, mentoring and consultancy to agencies and sectors to ensure effective and evidence-led delivery of community engagement, participation and accountability services for both refugee and host communities – with a focus on reaching women and other groups who currently have reduced access to information. Support modality will be remote where possible and maintain appropriate distancing and hygiene precautions where face-to-face training is unavoidable. - Providing regular briefings to sectors and to cross-sector bodies including the INGO team leaders forum, the NGO platform and ISCG. Together, these activities will support to engage communities by (1) ensuring that a wide range of agencies and sectors have access to high quality, effective and consistent communication tools which are appropriate for use in a movement restricted setting; (2) have access to training, mentoring and guidance to help improve the quality and effectiveness of their community engagement activities during the Covid-19 period; and (3) have access to high quality and timely insights and analysis that allow them to plan and adapt their Covid-19 programming in response to community perceptions, views, concerns and suggestions.
