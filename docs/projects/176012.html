Project Summary

South Sudan still suffers from effects of decades of war compounded with climatic shocks including floods, drought, sub-national and inter-communal conflicts and widespread insecurity which makes the population extremely vulnerable. Notwithstanding the Revitalized Peace Agreement signed in 2018, most of the communities still languish in absolute poverty due to little agricultural production and livelihood activities and the weakening of the South Sudanese Pound. The COVID situation has further made the fragile situation complex.
Safe water is still not accessible to most communities which is critical for especially children who are vulnerable to water related diseases. The hygienic standards and coverage of sanitation facilities in the locations targeted by GLI which include Kajo Keji, Morobo Bor South, Duk and Tonj North Counties is still very low despite the fact that various actors have undertaken various projects in the area. Practices such as open defecation which according to UNOCHA stands at 73% at national level is still rampant in most communities. Proper handwashing at critical times is still not being observed. The COVID situation has also given rise to increased health conditions as many actors suspended/stopped their interventions due to lockdowns and other government protocols. Thus, according to UNOCHA, the number of acute watery diarrhoea and acute bloody diarrhoea have severely increased in 2020 with 593,000 acute watery diarrhoea cases reported, a 60% increase from 2020. According to Wolfer 2014, inadequate sanitation and hygiene has a major impact on health and is the main cause of preventable diarrheal and other diseases including cholera, dysentery, typhoid and hepatitis A. These preventable diseases cause extreme poverty and is a serious health issue for the communities according to WHO/UNICEF, 2013.
GLI strives to increase access to clean water and adequate sanitation to 20,000 people (1,690 boys, 4,150 girls, 6,400 men and 7,800 women) in the five locations of Kajo Keji, Morobo, Bor South, Duk and Tonj East. This will help to reduce the incidences of illnesses, improve the health status of the communities and reduce the burden of women and children by reducing the distance to the water points thus reducing and/or mitigating protection issues, which according to UNOCHA, 16% of the water users reported protection related incidences at water points.
GLI will deliver the project through context specific and integrated approach by integrating with other sectors like FSL and Nutrition since adequate water leads to increased agricultural production thus better nutrition for the children. GLI will integrate gender equality and women empowerment to ensure prevention, reduction and mitigation of GBV risks during the project implementation. The interventions GLI will undertake will be in line with the UN Millennium Development Goals that seeks to ensure communities have sustainable access to safe water. The project will ensure that the services create a positive effect in the community by promoting behaviour change. The project will also ensure to reduce conflict and tensions amongst the community by increasing the availability of water sources and ensuring the choice of locations is appropriate to all the members of the community. The approaches to be used in this intervention will take into consideration the social-cultural practices of the beneficiaries, their taboos and beliefs and economic status.
GLI will encourage active participation of the local community including women and children in the implementation of the project. This will be through consultation of local authorities, women, men, boys and girls based on the identified needs. The community will be involved in the design of the community and institutional sanitation facilities considering the need for privacy for females for the case of latrines and bath room’s construction.

1 - Needs

The high prevalence of diseases in South Sudan is affecting up to 36% of children under 5. The causes of malnutrition are namely; lack of proper child care due to food insecurity, high rates of disease outbreaks due to lack or little access to clean water, poor hygiene and sanitation, which has contributed to counties with high GAM rates. According to a FSNMS done in 2019 it was reported that GAM among children under five was at 16.2% which is very high.The 2021 HNO reports that 8.3M people in South Sudan will be in need of humanitarian assistance, 24% being women, 54% children and 15% PLWD. The HNO reveals that 7.2 million are in IPC 3 with a further 2.5 million in IPC 4. The report states that 34% of households travel for more than 30 minutes to access water points which means increased risk for GBV incidences. It is estimated that 1.4 million children under five are likely to be affected by malnutrition in 2021 and 483,000 PLW are estimated to be malnourished. The report states that only 36% of households in the country have access to water points with the rest having difficulties in accessing clean water.Inter communal conflicts in Kajo Keji and Morobo led to destruction of basic infrastructure including WASH facilities and disruption of basic services like health and nutrition. The conflict and tensions have caused protection concerns including sexual abuse and child protection issues. The HNO reports that 137,400 people in Kajo Keji and 88,200 in Morobo are in need of humanitarian assistance. A IRNA conducted by UNOCHA in Kajo Keji in March 2020 indicates that the levels of malnutrition is low in the county with 5% of children being malnourished and 32 PLW screened for acute malnutrition where 6.3% of them found with the same. The areas bore the brunt of the recent conflicts since 2016 which made hundreds of people flee to seek safety in the neighbouring Uganda. These communities are are returning home since the signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement and will put a strain on the already scarce resources in the areas. Tonj North which is classified as IPC 5 affecting 13,000 people and 166,800 people are in need as per the 2021 HNO which constitutes one of the highest numbers of malnourished children under five and 50% of the population in IPC 4. The county is prone to seasonal flooding that always affect the farm lands and washes away villages. The county also has limited water points which leads to constant inter communal conflicts over water and grazing land.Bor South and Duk currently on IPC 4 with 298,200 and 174,800 people in need respectively have a history of inter communal conflict and extreme weather conditions leading to flooding destroying crop production and wash away entire villages. The two factors of flooding and conflict have caused of the basic infrastructure including water sources be limited. Duk County has also experienced a high number of returnees estimated to be above 40,000 since 2019 and who have similarly been displaced by the floods experienced in 2020. The COVID-19 pandemic where South Sudan currently has 6,583 has disrupted livelihoods and made the communities more vulnerable as humanitarian aid has been suspended and/or disrupted. Access to water for handwashing during this pandemic is still not available to most of the communities in South Sudan. In all the counties targeted by GLI, the prevailing conditions are pushing affected population to share their limited water sources and practice risky hygiene behaviours. Female headed households are more likely to opt for open defecation compared to male headed, presumably because of their inability to dig and build pit latrines. Such conditions are conducive for manifestation of diseases due to poor WASH conditions which compromise the dignity of women and girls, put them at risk of harassment, and expose people to life-threatening diseases especially to young children, sick and elderly people.

2 - Project activities

• Rehabilitate existing 50 hand pumps (10 Kajokeji, 10 in Morobo, 10 in Tonj North, 10 in Bor South and 10 in Duk Counties)• Establish and train sustainable 50 water management committees responsible for the operation and maintenance of water facilities with 50% comprised of women.• Mobilize women and men to participate in the location, design, and maintenance of water points.• Organize distribution of WASH/NFI to 5,000 households. GLI through core pipeline manager UNICEF will access water treatment products and water containers. The beneficiaries will be provided with education on how to use these WASH emergency supplies specially the proper storage of the water containers and application of water purifiers.• Construct of 12 gender segregated communal latrines in consultation of all groups in Tonj North, Duk and Bor South Counties.. Design communal latrines in consultation with women and girls to ensure that users have privacy. The selection process of communal latrines sites will be based on the needs of the community eg IDPs locations will be prioritized in this selection process.• Carry out concerted hygiene promotion campaigns on safe excreta disposal and hygiene practices and COVID awareness.• Mobilize and train 500 hygiene promoters in Kajo Keji, Morobo, Tonj North, Bor South and Duk. Hygiene promoters will sensitize the IDPS about the use of latrines and host communities, water sources, importance of cleaning water containers, solid waste management, the importance of hand washing at critical times and the importance of personal hygiene among the affected community.• Conduct daily hygiene campaign/awareness among the internally displaced persons in Kajo Keji, Morobo, Tonj North, Bor South and Duk. Ensure all users in particular women and girls participate in identification of risks hygiene practices and coordination.• GLI WASH activities will be integrated with SC/OTP through a referral system to nutrition partners running these centres.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

GLI will establish a system that is transparent and known to all the beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The organization will ensure that internal procedures for staff to report PSEA incidences are strengthened and clear to all staff. All staff will sign a code of conduct with PSEA policies clearly outlined. Staff will also be given refresher training throughout the project cycle on PSEA.The project will integrate PSEA awareness raising in the target areas, where the beneficiaries will be enlightened on their rights in terms of the services to be provided. The awareness raising will also educate the beneficiaries on what is sexual exploitation and abuse and their obligations. A clear reporting system and channels for CFRM for reporting will be established where the beneficiaries will report PSEA cases. GLI will appoint a focal person who will be in charge of PSEA and his/her contacts made known to all.GLI will procure awareness raising IEC materials that are tailored to fit in the context and also taking into consideration the cultural beliefs of the beneficiaries which will include posters and t-shirts with relevant PSEA/WASH messages. During stakeholders engagements with the community like monthly meetings, community dialogue or focus group discussions GLI will ensure to increase awareness on PSEA on the services rendered in order to increase protection of especially the vulnerable members of the community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

GLI will ensure that communication and messaging is timely, accurate,appropriate, effective and understood. Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, focus group discussions, verbal/audio and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as face to face meetings) will be used. With a foundation of diversity mainstreaming anchored in theproject design, GLI will also ensure that a gender-sensitive community feedback mechanism (for consultation, feedback and complaints) is established to inform and change programming, including the establishment of accountability groups based on community defined vulnerability criteria

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This project will ensure that the affected populations in the targeted areas have a genuine voice during activity design and decision-making process that inform the proposed project’s priorities and modalities. As part of their orientation training and continual development, project personnel will receive instruction on why information sharing is prioritized, how it is operationalized, and what their roles and responsibilities are in making it work. In each target area GLI will initiate an open dialogue with the community and key stakeholders. We will ensure that communication and messaging is timely, accurate, appropriate, effective and understood. Project parameters such as site and beneficiary selection will be openly discussed with beneficiaries using a highly transparent methodology that is grounded in community participation. Information sharing will also be a key feature of the proposed project to help in avoiding overlaps between different actors in the selected counties. Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

GLI will ensure that communication and messaging is timely, accurate, appropriate, effective and understood. Tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, focus group discussions, verbal/audio, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions; consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard), consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) will be used.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

GLI will conduct a risk analysis for the beneficiaries during the onset of the project. The project will take into consideration the risks that may arise that will affect the boys, girls, women and men and the most vulnerable members of the community and take appropriate measures to mitigate the risks. To this end, the organization will disaggregate data by disability, age and gender, identify specific bottlenecks faced. GLI will include the voice of the beneficiaries during the project cycle and involve them in decision making so as to understand and/or reduce protection issues in the community. The organization will strengthen its complaints and feedback mechanisms and also its PSEA policies and ensure all staff are conversant with CFRM and sign a code of conduct for PSEA. GLI will also coordinate with other partners in the project location to create synergy in order to have common objectives that will reduce bottlenecks that may create protection issues. The organization will undertake robust awareness campaigns in collaboration with other departments (Protection) in the organization and other stakeholder/partners in the locality.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Integrating WASH into protection services increases the quality of WASH programming. By understanding the problems of the communities, their responsibilities and engaging in open discussions with the communities on protection issues will raise awareness on issues related to GBV and sensitive cultural practices and taboos can be addressed. While poor WASH services may not necessarily be the root cause of violence, however it greatly contributes to vulnerability of women and girls to violence. Improving the provision of clean drinking water and hygiene and sanitation services, and integrating GBV risk mitigation in the project, the risk of GBV will be reduced within the targeted locations. Since vulnerability to violence affects access to essential services including waterHaving hygiene promotion activities that demystify cultural taboos like defecation and girls menstruation. Engaging the communities in location of sanitation facilities including latrines and bath shelters and water points, the project greatly reduces the risk of GBV including sexual assault and harassment and rape. Also engaging the community will reduce tensions and fights amongst the different communities.Therefore, GLI WASH programme will strive to increase gender equality in the community, by removing barriers that stereotype gender and including both male and female members of the community in the committees. GLI will ensure a gender and risk analysis is done to identify gender roles and responsibilities, cultural norms and beliefs will ensure to improve the quality of services offered to enable the beneficiaries strengthen their coping mechanisms.WASH is essential for infection prevention and control especially for health services in order to prevent disease outbreaks. WASH is therefore a precondition for quality health care.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

GLI will undertake a multi-sectorial programing to address the root causes offood insecurity in the target locations. The project will integrate with FSL partners in the target locations to ensure projects and activities undertaken will be able to undertake the shocks experienced by the communities and thereby build their resilience. The project will particularly target children under 5 and PLW to support them their families with seed and farm inputs. The project will also work with other actors including government agencies, partners and traders to ensure there is availability of food in the markets. With the constant displacement and extreme weather conditions in the country, where communities cannot undertake livelihood activities and lack of basic infrastructure, GLI will partner with other sectors including FSL to ensure the communities have alternative means of livelihoods to ensure food security.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

GLI will consider vulnerable groups including elderly, women and children and persons with disability in the design, monitoring and evaluation of this WASH intervention. Data will be disaggregated by age, sex and gender to identify specific bottlenecks faced and solutions to the bottlenecks. GLI will consider the above vulnerable groups in the design, monitoring and evaluation of this WASH intervention. As a way to address discrimination and stigma when providing WASH services, GLI will encourage the participation of all the vulnerable groups in all decision making processes and ensure WASH related information is in formats that people with different types of disabilities can understand.GLI will have all stakeholders of the project receive knowledge and training on the specific WASH requirements of vulnerable groups, and have their knowledge regarding their rights and participation strategies increased.GLI will raise awareness and talk about vulnerable groups on WASH as well as their specific needs when planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating WASH activities with government, sector partners and communities to promote positive attitude towards addressing the WASH needs of people with disability.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

By integrating WASH intervention and Nutrition, GLI will ensure to contribute to the decrease of diarrheal diseases in the community this decrease malnutrition. The integrated WASH/Nutrition project will contribute to influence positive behaviour change through coordination with Nutrition partners in the targeted locations and thus increase nutrition status of the communities. The project will use already established contact points including care givers at SFPs, OTPs, and home visits by CHPs to talk about WASH. The project will also harmonize messages with the Nutrition/health partner to promote positive hygiene practices and ensuring the children under 5 are vaccinated against measles and given vitamin A supplements which will reduce morbidity and mortality. GLI will also raise awareness on food preparation and increase of food consumption to increase minimum dietary diversity and minimum meal frequency for children upto 23 months as per FSNMS at state level. GLI will also integrate with nutrition partners to advocate for exclusive breastfeeding practices.
