Project Summary

The humanitarian conditions for people in north-west Syria have deteriorated after the recent escalations, causing massive displacement of 358,000 newly displaced people from Idleb Governorate, most of them women and children which created a huge need for shelter and basic needs. The displacement continues due to recent offensive escalation in southern Idleb. (latest update from HNAP as of 10th of Feb. stated that the number of new IDPs since 1st of Dec. 2019 in NW reached 689 K.
iMDAT organization is planning to conduct shelter rehabilitation ( focusing on unﬁnished buildings/apartments) in Afrin Sub-District, ranked with 5 shelter severity scale, and winter assistance in A’zaz sub-, ranked with 5 NFI severity scale. IMDAT is planning to reach approximately 7100 beneﬁciaries. The disaggregation of this number would be approximately; women 29% (2059), men 20% (1420), boys 21% (1491) and girls 30% (2130).

4 - Needs

According to the recent humanitarian update issued by OCHA on 28 Jan. 2020, the deteriorating humanitarian conditions follow increasing reports of violence in the area and heightened concerns for the safety and protection for the over 4 million civilians in north-west Syria, over half of whom are internally displaced and the greater protection risks for vulnerable groups such as children, women, older people, and people with disabilities.. Some 45,000 newly displaced people chose to move to areas in northern Aleppo Governorate, seeking safety and to reach services, including Afrin and A’zaz, the UN Oﬃce for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) reported on 15 January. According to the latest UN Northwest Syria Readiness and Response Plan as of 31 January 2020, the shelter cluster has the biggest gap in current humanitarian response including funding. Also, the SNFI HNO 2020 summary highlighted the shelter needs in Syria which are widespread and acute resulting in signiﬁcant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to5.65 million people.1 Of this, 3.8 million (69 per cent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. The prioritization of Afrin and A’zaz was based on the HNO 2020 and ﬁled assessment conducted by our ﬁeld team. Furthermore, the shelter assessment and analysis that shared by Shelter/NFI Cluster X-Border Operation - Turkey Hub shows that Afrin was ranked 3rd out of the top 5 sub-districts for HHs living in unﬁnished houses/apartments with a total of 3770 (19% of the population). 4 case studies were conducted in order to assess the unﬁnished and damaged buildings in addition to cost assessment for the needed rehabilitation per shelter. RFN/IMDAT has been actively provided winter support in Azaz and Afrin for the last two years which enabled them to understand needs

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In line with PRA that has been developed and shared by cluster, RFN/IMDAT identiﬁed the following protection risks. A. Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages/value of voucher across members B. Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. C. Housing, Land and property issues; loss of HLP documentation, forced eviction, secondary occupation and land grabbing. D. Not having an exit strategy might lead to transform a temporary shelter solution (CSs)/un ﬁnished buildings into a long term accommodation, with the risks connected to tension with the host community, owners and eventually among beneﬁciaries families themselves

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk.A. Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages/value of voucher across member. Mitigation.A.1. Use the Cluster guidance and CWG on minimum standards (agreed amount) Risk. B. Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. Mitigation.B.1. Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities. Mitigation. B.2. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them. Mitigation. B.3 Ensure gender balance in the team, having female members to ensure mixed demographics (old, young, male, female) during community visits. This will ensure access and communication with female headed households and other vulnerable female groups.Risk. C. Housing, Land and property issues; loss of HLP documentation, forced eviction, secondary occupation and land grabbing. Mitigation.C.1. for the private apartments, providing copy of ownership document by the tenant or the head of household as a prerequisite to be selected for rehabilitation. As for public apartments, the document will be obtained from the local council. Mitigation.C.2. excluding the properties associated with legal or military disputes. Risk.D. Not having an exit strategy might lead to transform a temporary shelter solution (CSs)/un ﬁnished buildings into a long term accommodation, with the risks connected to tension with the host community, owners and eventually among beneﬁciaries families themselves. Mitigation.D.1. Ensure an exit strategy in place, with time-bound agreement with the owners and tenants Mitigation.D.2. Keep the communication lines with the authorities open to inform them of the rationale of the interventions and the aim of the program.
