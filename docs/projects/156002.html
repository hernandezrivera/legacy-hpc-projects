Project Summary

The project will be conducting activities for both IYCF and CMAM in Afrin, Euphrates shield area and Ariha in Idleb.
The project will have a wide range of activities in both IYCF and CMAM programs, with no less than 6 mobile teams for outreach activities in the Euphrates shield area with 2 OTP centers, 8 mobile teams in Afrin district with 3 OTP centers and 1 SC center, and 4 mobile team in Ariha district with 1 OTP and 1 SC center.
Through these fixed and outreach activities, IYCF support program and CMAM, screening prevention and treatment will be implemented.
One of the project activities will be distributing (LNS MQ) with food basket distribution to about 4500 children as a Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program(BSFP), in Idlib governorate in Jisr-Ash-Shugur subdistrict, Ariha district and Saraqab subdistrict, this activity will target children between 6 months and 2 years.

Needs

Throughout 2018, Al-Ameen for Humanitarian Support continued to provide lifesaving support and services in north-west Syria to conflict-affected populations in Idlib and Aleppo governorates. Since late 2017, north-western Syria received several influxes of internally displaced people (IDPs) from different parts of the country, The continuous waves of displacement within and into the north-west of Syria have created additional pressure on health systems and further stretched the limited capacity to provide health and nutrition services "OCHA,2018". According to the health cluster, the total number of population is around 3.9 Million, that means according to the percentage shared by both nutrition cluster and SIG team, the number of children under 5 in Gaziantep accessible areas are about 663,000 children, and around 292,500 Pregnant and lactating women "PLWs". Global Acute Malnutrition among boys and girls under the age of 5 years remains within acceptable international benchmarks in most of the assessed areas during 2018. However, there are some pockets across different parts in Syria where SMART surveys, rapid assessments, and nutrition surveillance information show an increase in acute malnutrition rates. The increase in chronic malnutrition potentially reflects long term inadequate dietary intake, including inadequate micronutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life, and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency., Maternal and child malnutrition is impacted by deterioration in security in some areas such as Idlib and rural Aleppo, increase in needs among returnees and internally displaced people, as well as coverage of nutrition programs such as in underserved North-East Syria and northern Rural Aleppo, including Afrin "Nutrition cluster,2019".

Implementation of activities

Through the project, a comprehensive IYCF + CMAM program will be implemented. Screening for acute malnourished for children under 5 cases by both MUAC and Z-score, providing preventative supplies, refer cases to fixed facilities for either OTP or SC centers, provide needed treatment for both MAM and SAM cases within the OTP and SC centers and follow-up through outreach activities the identified cases Through IYCF program, there' will be one-to-one counseling, group support sessions, IEC materials distribution on awareness raising on IYCF-E and optimal maternal nutrition for pregnant and lactating women. In addition to screening for malnourished PLWs, and distribute of preventative and treatable nutrition supplies for them according to the screening results.

Protection Risk Analysis

Different Protection risks are available during through this project it can be summarized with as the following: 1). The risk from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA which is referred to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. 2). Gender-Based Violence GBV: health and nutrition outreach services are often the first and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, and refer the case to the right position, survivors must have safe access to facilities, and it is critical that health & Nutrition teams have the right knowledge to handle the case and deal with it.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The mitigation measures for protection risk are: -Effective community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.-Awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. -Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave.- adopting a flexible delivery modality with adjustable distribution schedules.- Specialized staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.- Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor.- Presence of female staff in outreach teams and fixed facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. If a risk other than protection risk arises like risks beyond the project's control like attacking a facility or team, then we will also have different mitigation measures like: - Set up centers in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries.- Relocate nutrition centers in relatively safe areas- Reporting on attacks against healthcare.
