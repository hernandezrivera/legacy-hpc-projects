Project Summary

Gender Based Violence is most prevalent in South Sudan. According to UNFPA, 2023 Report, South Sudan is ranked second in GBV prevalence in East Africa. According to the study, substantial proportions of women (aged 15-64) in South Sudan experience GBV either in form of physical (34.0%) or sexual (13.5%) violence in their lifetime. Given that Gender-based violence (GBV) is deeply entrenched in South Sudan, where women and girls continue to be at high risk both at public and private spaces. Gender based-violence is not only manifested in the physical and sexual violence, but also lack of equal opportunities for women and girls to lead a fulfilling life, high rate of illiteracy amongst girls and women, high rate of child marriage, high maternal mortality due to preventable causes, limited access to livelihood opportunities, are some of the indicators that highlight the dire need to address the gender inequality as the root causes to end gender based violence
This project aims at providing GBV Prevention and Response Services to 7,966 crisis affected communities (8,614 Host community; 1,999 IDPs and 779 Returnees) in Kapoeta East and Lafon Counties of Eastern Equatoria State, South Sudan. This will be achieved through GBV prevention, risk mitigation and scale-up of specialized GBV response services. The main activities to be undertaken include awareness creation, both in the community and institutions; provision of dignity kits which is aimed at boosting the self-esteem of women girls especially during menstrual days, thus ensuring un- interrupted school attendance; training's /capacity building at WGFS, where women and girls are supported with GBV risk mitigation interventions such as income generating activities and provision of fuel efficient stoves, thus reducing their vulnerability

1. Needs

Gender Based Violence is most prevalent in South Sudan. According to UNFPA, 2023 Report, South Sudan is ranked second in GBV prevalence in East Africa. According to the study, substantial proportions of women (aged 15-64) in South Sudan experience GBV either in form of physical (34.0%) or sexual (13.5%) violence in their lifetime. In addition, the country is affected by both conflicts and natural disasters such as floods, this harmful traditional practices (Gender Based Violence) puts women and girls at risk of other sufferings based on their gender. The cases of sexual and gender-based violence including rape and child marriage are alarming although not unique to South Sudan as the country is ranked second in GBV prevalence rate in East Africa, UNFPA Report, 2023. GBV and Gender Equality are not just about human rights, but equally important indicators of economic growth Given that Gender-based violence (GBV) is deeply entrenched in South Sudan, where women and girls continue to be at high risk both at public and private spaces. Gender-based violence can happen to anyone. However, it disproportionately affects women and girls. Those in crisis settings are at a double disadvantage due to their gender and their situation. Gender based-violence is not only manifested in the physical and sexual violence, but also lack of equal opportunities for women and girls to lead a fulfilling life, high rate of illiteracy amongst girls and women, high rate of child marriage, high maternal mortality due to preventable causes, limited access to livelihood opportunities, are some of the indicators that highlight the dire need to address the gender inequality as the root causes to end gender based violence The underlying root causes of GBV is the social imbalances of power relations between men and women, to control resources, denial to certain opportunities as well as decision making that results to the abuse, over domination and discrimination against both women and men on the basis of their gender. GBV is rooted in the patriarchal structure of the family, in which the man is the head of the household. However, men can also experience GBV! For example, young boys, just like young girls, become victims of child marriage. GBV is a social problem and affects everyone, regardless of gender. According to the 2023 HNO, women and girls are differently affected, and their vulnerability is exacerbated by various forms of GBV, including rape, physical assault, forced marriage and SEA Gender-based violence (GBV) is present in every society around the world and takes many forms. As a violation of human rights, we have a moral imperative to stop all forms of violence against women and girls. 2. Project activities

The activities to be undertaken by NSDO in order to achieve Cluster Objectives 2, 1 and 3 are highlighted below:In order to ensure that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, advancing centrality of protection among humanitarian actors to reduce protection risks, and addressing coordination gaps/issues through close dialogue with partners and effective protection monitoring to inform programming, advocacy, and response, CO2, NSDO will undertake the following activities:a) Awareness raising sessions conducted targeting 100 beneficiaries consisting women, men, boys and girls on GBV risks, available response services and timely reportingb) Undertake GBV prevention initiatives (e.g. SASA, EMAP, community cares programming) targeting 404 beneficiaries, including women, men, girls and boys c) Supporting 386 beneficiaries which include women, girls with GBV risk mitigation interventions (Income generating activities, fuel efficient stoves, intergradation of GBV by other clusters and humanitarian actors) To achieve CO1 (GBV emergency) of ensuring mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations, NSDO will undertake the following activities, a) Awareness -raising activities on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting targeting 1,530 beneficiaries consisting of women, men, girls and boys b) Procurement and distribution of dignity kits to 1,250 women and adolescent girls c) Supporting 1,530 beneficiaries consisting of women, men, boys and girls with specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, PSS & WGFS programming)d) Supporting 894 beneficiaries consisting of women, men, boys and girls with specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, legal aid, safe houses & WGFS programming)e) Supporting 4,707 women, men, boys and girls with individual and group Psychosocial support including reactional activitiesf) Procurement and distribution of dignity kits to 1,795 women and adolescent girls g) Ensuring functional GBV referral pathway in the Lafon county Finally, in order to promote nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities, CO3, NSDO will train 35 non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV integration in their sectoral response. Given that GBV is a social problem and affects everyone, regardless of gender there is need to intervene from different angles namely: awareness creation which is aimed at raising cognizance of prevention, reporting, and response to cases of SGBV, Provision of support services (dignity kits and livelihood and economic support and trainings /capacity building
