Project Summary

The former Jonglei State of South Sudan has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people, followed by former Upper Nile State with 845,000 people. According to Last FSSNMS both counties of Ayod and Pibor had 23.7% GAM respectively. Ayod has been classified as a crisis county and Pibor while Pibor faces an food security of emmergency proportion. Between January and April 2020, 14 counties are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Former Jonglei State is expected to have the highest number of people estimated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at 1.18 million, followed by former Upper Nile State, with 765,000(IPC August 2019).
RMF has strong presence in Pibor and Ayod counties of Jongolei state implementing an integrated nutrition program. These counties are characterized by cattle raiding, child abductions, revenge killings etc.
Such shocks plus inter tribal clashes always lead to disruption of nutrition and health services which make it impossible to implement a coordinated and integrated programming in these areas.
Furthermore with the onset of the raining seasons the population is always expected to be highly vulnerable to malaria and other related illnesses which could in turn worsen the nutrition situation for under five children as well as PLWs.
RMF will work in close collaboration with WASH, Health and FSL partners in order to implement minimum package currently recommended as an intervention package for partners working in Pibor and Ayod counties
This intervention will lead to improved management of SAM cases in both under 5 and PLWs .Over all 2950(1100 for Ayod and 1850 for Pibor) children 6-59 months are expected to be treated for SAM while 9000( 3400 in Ayod and 5600 for Pibor)children 6-59 months are expected to be treated for MAM. 7470PLWs3100 and 4370 for Ayod and Pibor respectively) will be treated for MAM. The intervention is also expected to champion preventive aspect of the nutrition program through an elaborate MYCN activities .Our presence on the ground will allow us to work together with other stake holders to continue to monitor, analyze and share the nutrition situation on a regular basis.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is characterized by multiple crises and threats that include conflict and instability, natural hazards (such as droughts and floods) and communicable diseases. These can occur as a single event, one can trigger another, or multiple events can converge and interact simultaneously with cascading and magnified effects. Former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people, followed by former Upper Nile State with 845,000 people. According to Last FSSNMS both counties of Ayod and Pibor had 23.7% GAM respectively. Ayod has been classified as a crisis county and Pibor while Pibor faces an food security of emmergency proportion. Between January and April 2020, 14 counties are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Former Jonglei State is expected to have the highest number of people estimated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at 1.18 million, followed by former Upper Nile State, with 765,000(IPC August 2019). In Ayod and Pibor counties sporadic inter communal conflicts ,compounded by diseases outbreaks, the deterioration of the food security situation and subsequent economic shocks have negatively affected households’ coping capacities and livelihoods and put so many people under severe stress in terms of food access and availability, access to markets and livelihoods,basic services, and collapse of the social mechanisms that the community used to rely on.A recent report indicated"Unusually heavy seasonal flooding is affecting large areas of South Sudan, with estimates that between 600,000 and 800,000 people have been affected, according to authorities. As of late October, Ayod, Maban, Mayom, Nyirol, Pibor and Uror counties in Greater Upper Nile were among the counties most heavily affected by the floods" (OCHA seasonal flood snap shots, October 2019 report). The impact of the ongoing disaster is yet to be determined but could further aggravate a spiraling food security situation and impact households’ coping capacities and livelihoods.

2 - Activities

This programme seeks to support host population, IDPs and returnees in both Ayod and Pibor counties by providing lifesaving nutrition interventions, preventive health/nutrition measures closer to the community as possible. The programme will strive to address the problem of under nutrition identified in the 2 counties (Ayod and Pibor ) during the next 1 year . Targeted deliverable areas will be in prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition which require integration of Health, WASH and FSL activities through testing and treatment of children in nutrition programs, provision of health and nutrition education messages and linking CMAM exits with others services like GFD inorder to contribute to reduction of critical levels of GAM currently experienced in conflict affected county of Ayod. UNICEF and WFP will provide core pipeline supplies, equipments as well as substantial supportive supervision in Nutrition response.RMF will open 4 new OTPs: 2 in Pibor and 2 in Ayod counties .RMF will partner with WASH cluster and partners in order to ensure coordinated and integrated response including use of customized WASH messages to the beneficiaries. All efforts will be made to work in close collaboration with FSL cluster and partners at all times especially as effort will be made to initiate cooking demonstration. We will work in collaboration with our health partners and are looking into the possibility of integrating health and nutrition services in all our operational areas. RMF will maintain and furnish existing facilities, Screen Boys and girls 6-59 months for SAM and MAM, Train and capacity build project staffs on CMAM/MIYCN guidelines ,establish new mother support groups while existing groups are maintained through ,ensuring Mothers/Care givers of children 0-23 months on MIYCN and all mothers/caregivers of children 6-59 attending receive counseling MIYCN counseling CMAM services will receive individual counseling on MICYN,ensure nutrition information sharing,train and orientate staffs on the GBV referral pathway so they are able to refer survivors for appropriate care.Ensure basic hand washing facility,water for appetite test,Provide health and nutrition education,Screening/testing and treatment of malaria and other illness (URTI, UTI) in OTPs and Conduct sensitization to make raise community awareness of systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

While our humanitarian programmes address the urgent needs of affected population in the short- and medium-term,there is growing needs to link this projects to development programmes so it can contribute to reducing their needs, vulnerabilities and risks in a sustainable and longer term manner all which will contribute to delivering Sustainable Development Goals for the world’s most disadvantaged poor.While transitioning from humanitarian Aid to development programming is a non-linear process there is need for a concerted multi sect oral approach all which can help in addressing root problems in tandem with offering relief, humanitarian aid can contribute to the alleviation of cyclical crises. The program will be intergrated with health systems which is in turn linked with community systems through a network of volunteers through its boma initiative programs.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Under nutrition prevention strategies through health and nutrition education, maternal nutrition, infant and young child feeding practices, micro nutrient supplementation and early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition are among the major national priority strategies used to combat causes of morbidity and mortality among children in the first 1000 days of life. The problems of under nutrition is a general burden to the health sector, economic situation of the country and as well a factor of low productivity. The national ministry of health and other line ministries such as agriculture have guidelines and policy documents in place that enforce the need to address the under nutrition problems in the country through the various avenues in different sectors. This programme seeks to support host population, IDPs and returnees in both Ayod and Pibor counties by providing lifesaving nutrition interventions, preventive health/nutrition measures closure to the community as possible. The programme will strive to address the problem of under nutrition identified in the 2 counties (Ayod and Pibor ) during the 1 year . Targeted deliverable areas will be in areas prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition as well as intergration of Health, WASH and FSL activities .In protracted crises such as in South Sudan, where the nutrition sector still requires external support, it is essential for the Cluster to adopt a needs-based approach to address gaps that may span beyond treatment of acute malnutrition only to multi-sector, nutrition-sensitive interventions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

There is a notable vulnerability of young women who are mothers at early ages as low as 15 years with limited knowledge and ability to even meet their own nutritional needs. This shortfall manifests in undernourishment of children under their care due to low or very limited support available. In Ayod and Pibor Theft and looting; extortion of supplies are still prevalent. Also inter generational violence is common. Our project design is based on well-designed eligibility criteria and targeting is based on context, community inputs, evidence, and objectives;All beneficiaries within Pibor and Ayod and Neighboring counties are able to access services. There will be ongoing sensitization of beneficiaries, Information sharing as well as post-distribution monitoring .RMF will work closely with many protection partners to ensure appropriate Gender and conflict analysis, power mapping. Post-distribution monitoring will include questions on social relations. RMF’s experience in the 2 counties shall be used as a basis to steer project activities – especially on enabling effective engagement and meaningful participation of women in relation to nutrition services, The programme shall target all genders since they have an over-arching responsibilities in ensuring prevention of the problem. Caretakers of children 0 – 59 months and PLWs with their spouses shall be targeted for services in order to have an inclusive responsibility in curbing the effects of under nutrition.RMF will establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community etc Provide training to the nutrition programme staff to understand how to serve best the beneficiaries using the QAAP approach. Involve the communities in design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation on the planned or on-going nutrition project

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Beneficiaries will be provided with information on where to access services,the time and days for distribution. Also information will be provided on their basic rights and entitlements. Beneficiaries will also participate in needs identification. This will be provided through face to face discussions/meetings, putting up of suggestion boxes(anecdotal evidence suggest this appropriate way of receiving feedback from younger beneficiaries). Sensitization will be done,including pictorials. All this will improve access to marginalized groups .All nutrition treatment sites shall be located within existing health facilities in order to reduce lost chances for care for children; such health facilities are located within the population’s settlements of a village within a Boma. All centres shall have a clean environment; latrines shall be made available and labelled female and male. All beneficiaries of the programme shall be treated with dignity and impartiality during service provision regardless of any affiliation. In an event of information gathering and data collection, informed consent shall be obtained and information gathered shall be treated with confidentiality. RMF will include vulnerable members of the host community to mitigate potential conflict among beneficiary populations. Women, youth and vulnerable members will be actively sought out and encouraged to participate, including through coordinating with relevant national and local-level groups (including traditional leadership structures, religious and business leaders, and women and youth groups).
