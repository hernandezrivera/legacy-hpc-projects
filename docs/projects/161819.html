Project Summary

The project will be implemented over a twelve (12) month period (January – December, 2020) and shall cover 4 LGAs in both Borno and Yobe states (Ngala & Konduga in Borno and Yunusari & Yusufari in Yobe respectively). The proposed project will be implemented in active collaboration with State & LGA Ministry of Health, as well as local community based structures and will contribute to the reduction of excessive maternal and newborn mortality among the IDPs, returnees and host populations in the targeted geographical areas. The outlined initiatives will seek to increase access and improve the availability of quality reproductive, maternal, and new born health services for the affected population. Likewise, it aims to address the underlying social and gender norms that hinder women and girls access to life-saving sexual and reproductive health care.
The world over, CARE is widely known for its capacity to responding to the emergency needs of vulnerable populations in times of crisis through the provision of relief aids. While this remains a core area of our work, CARE also strives very hard to help build resilience among crisis affected populations in order to make them stronger and better prepared to face the future. Towards this end, CARE prioritizes and significant invests in sexual and reproductive health care coverage, especially for most isolated underserved and hard-to-reach areas through the following: i) supporting strengthening of health systems ii) building capacity of frontline health care providers and local community based structures iii) promoting collaboration, partnerships and coordination with state and local-level institutions/ authorities iv) creating safe spaces for addressing underlying negative gender and social norms. Additionally, CARE builds local partner capacity, resilience building, and sustainability, ensuring local organizations can continue to provide quality services to the people in need. Through this project, CARE aims to contribute to the reduction of excessive maternal and newborn mortality in the Borno and Adamawa states, North East Nigeria. The proposed intervention will contribute to ongoing efforts by both the Government and other humanitarian actors towards restoring the functionality and quality of reproductive, maternal, and new born health services available. Furthermore, it will enhance timely prevention and response to Gender Based Violence by addressing deep-rooted discordant social and gender norms and practices that hinder women and girls access to life-saving care.

1 - Needs

Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.The escalating conflict in North East Nigeria has since 2009 created a vast, protracted crisis in which millions face risks to their safety and basic rights, and are struggling to survive. Within this context of conflict and fragility, millions of people in the crisis –affected North east Nigeria and Lake Chad regions are further affected by the high risk to famine and recurrent epidemics of a large magnitude such as cholera outbreaks. Broad restrictions on aid and essential goods are worsening the humanitarian crisis. The 2019 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) shows a bleak picture for populations in the North East Nigeria following ten years of escalated conflict. It is estimated that across the three affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY), 7.1 million people are estimated to need humanitarian assistance in 2019 out of the total population of 13.4 million. Over eighty per cent of internally displaced people are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis, and over sixty per cent are living in host communities, making it harder to access them with assistance and putting additional pressure on the already stretched resources of these communities. One in four of the internally displaced people are under five, and 80 per cent are women and children. However, some 1.6 million people have returned home since August 2015, indicating that conditions in some locations have improved. Humanitarian organisations are not able to meet all needs in the north-east; more than 800,000 people in Borno State are estimated to be in areas that are inaccessible to humanitarian organisations. Recent findings from a H2R assessment carried out in Borno state between August,2018 to April,2019 with a focus on Humanitarian Needs and Displacement Dynamics in Hard-to-Reach Areas revealed that 82% of assessed settlements reported that some population remains in the hard to reach areas. This consists largely of captive populations and vulnerable sub-groups unable to journey to LGA capitals; and mostly have limited to no information on the availability of humanitarian aid services in LGA capitals. Although access to health services has been identified as a priority by the health sector, as identified through rapid assessments and systematic community engagements, the State Ministries of Health (SMOH) have not been able to provide funding to cover operational costs and the pressure has increased on health sector partners to fill the enormous gaps in primary and secondary health care services. The most pressing gaps include access and availability of quality SRH services, continuation of public health programs, scale up of health service capacity, access to medicines and supplies, and covering operational costs. The majority of functioning health care facilities across the targeted states, particularly Borno State, are supported by health partners. 2 - Activities

Please describe the project activities that will address the needs identified and how they will be implementedRapid Assessment• Conduct a mapping exercise to obtain a landscape overview of the stakeholders in the community and all available service providers that the project can link. Then an assessment of the functioning of the health system will be conducted before the start of the project. This information will be used to select health facilities in the target areas. Conduct situational analysis to better understand the social norms, perspectives, and barriers associated with the RH services, which will guide our community engagement and mobilization plan. Capacity Building• Together with SMOHs, and selected health facilities, 40 nurses/ midwives will be provided a 12 -day competency based refresher training course in basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC), PAC and family Planning, Clinical management of Rape . In order to ensure women and girl receive respectful care, these midwives will be sensitized on Respectful Maternity Care including contraceptive technology especially on IUD insertion and removal. . The nurses/midwives will be sensitized on the implications of GBV, including intimate partner violence, on RH of women and girls with specific information on referral to other health facilities and organizations providing services.• Trained nurses/ midwives will be provided with a midwifery kit that meets WHO and UNFPA standards and includes: basic drugs, renewable medical supplies, basic sterilization equipment, and bag and mask for neonatal resuscitation. • Community health workers (CHWs) in Nigeria have received minimal formal training. To increase their ability to build awareness of RH, antenatal care, and safer birth services, CHWs in each target site (a total of 160) will be identified in close coordination with the LGA health offices and local communities and provided a training on key SRH & GBV messages, communication and referral to appropriate health services. Together with the SMOHs and local health authorities, CARE will provide competency based capacity building for 40 health care providers (nurses, and midwives – 2 per health facility) in the targeted facilities on implementing adolescent friendly health services to enable them to respond to adolescents more effectively and with greater sensitivity, for all health needs, but particularly adolescent SRH needs. Capacity building will include improving the interactions and building rapport between the healthcare provider and adolescent patient, supportive and non-judgmental communication, and discussion of sensitive issues. CARE will work with the health facility staff to assess the physical layout, human resources, available services and information, policies, and engagement of youth at the target facilities and provide recommendations for adolescent friendly facility improvements.• Health facility staff, trained midwives, and trained CHWs will be provided with clean delivery kits to distribute to 600 visibly pregnant women and girls to assist them in giving birth safely in the event they are unable to reach the health facility for delivery. Whilst stressing the importance of visiting health facility for safe services.• CARE’s SRHR team will coordinate supervision visits with representatives from CARE, local partner and functioning line departments. • CHWs will conduct 1,536 awareness raising sessions in the target communities focused on addressing patriarchal gender and social norms that hinder women and girls access to care and raise awareness on the prevention and mitigation of GBV; establish 20 community committees ;strengthen referrals; promote community based screening; Faciliate joint supportive supervisions and establish and support mobile outreach services to underserved and hard to reach areas and promote feedback/complaints mechanisms.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CARE will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming too. CARE will establish a feedback mechanism, which will allow women, girls men and boys to express any complaints. Project participants will be informed of the existence of such mechanism, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Through these, CARE will ensure that issues of concern to the community are brought to the attention of CARE and other partners for intervention. CARE will ensure orientation for staff, volunteers on core humanitarian principles and CARE core values and monitor compliance to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict.
