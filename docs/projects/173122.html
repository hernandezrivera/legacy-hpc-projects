1 - Needs

COVID 19, together with over one year of conflict and displacement in Libya, continue to present greater instability and negatively affect the mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of boys and girls, who are increasingly exposed to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect (displaced children living in camps, collective shelters or with host families/communities as well as returnee children and families living in EO-contaminated areas among others). Violations of international human rights and humanitarian law and violence against children continue to be prevalent throughout Libya. The intensified conflict in Tarhuna, Sirte, and southern Tripoli increased IDPs from from 373,709 IDPs to 425,714 IDPs due to the intensified conflict in western Libya. When boys and girls experience or are exposed to violence, including but not limited to loss of or separation from family members and friends, GBV, risk of injury from explosive ordnance, use and recruitment, and other child protection violations, their mental health and psychosocial wellbeing is affected in a diverse way , including but not limited to delayed cognitive development, poor school performances and school dropout, with long-term consequences. Risks include reduced access to basic services, particularly health and education, deterioration in living conditions, presence of armed groups etc. Moreover, the presence and control of militias within communities in which children live, increases risk of association with armed groups.Children who have been exposed to armed conflict and instability (including children on the move) are in critical need of psychosocial support, safe environment and spaces to play, learn and be supported.Limited supportive child protection and family welfare services within communities, including scale up of focused services for vulnerable children affected by armed conflict, continue to be a major gap and need. Parents/caregivers are also affected, with conflict and displacement hampering their capacity to provide a safe environment and nurturing care for their children. A substantial investment on the Children Affected by Armed Conflict (CAAC) monitoring work is significantly needed to strengthen the monitoring, reporting and response to grave child rights violations. There is also a need to strengthening inter-agency coordination, leadership and advocacy in the area of child protection, and undertake child protection needs assessment.

2 - Activities

1. Community and school-based child protection and mental health and psychosocial support services, including identification and referral of vulnerable children (children on the move, children with disabilities, as well as rehabilitation and reintegration of children associated with armed groups and other vulnerable conflict affected and displaced children)2. Specialized Child Protection services: Provision of specialized child protection services to survivors of all types of violence, including child survivors of GBV, and close collaboration with other sectors through established referral pathways and strengthening of child protection sub-sector coordination.3. Awareness raising and increased capacity of local actors and community members on key child protection issues including child recruitment, GBV, discrimination and violence against children, Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, access to services that prevent and respond to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect.4. Skills development and capacity strengthening of frontline workers, social workers and other child protection services providers5. Strengthening the monitoring, reporting and response to grave child rights violations.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Project will contribute to increasing access and awareness of vulnerable populations to protection services (including CP, GBV) as well as to increase awareness around CP and GBV Issues within communities. Additionally, project is planned around strong comunity-based approach as well as a feedback and complaint mechanism accessible to all vulnerable groups targeted through this project.IPC (Infection, prevention and control) measures will also be mainstreamed throughout the project to ensure continuation of program while ensuring safety of beneficiaries and staff.

6 - AAP/ Accountability mechanisms
6.1 - How is your project accountable to affected populations?

Common feedback mechanism

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNICEF will ensure commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring transparent feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into project proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings, partnerships et. This will ensure that the affected community accesses timely sufficient information, guidance, and support that enable the target population to understand and influence key decisions in the project to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices through active participation and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and its affected populations over information provision. the project will set up a complaint and feedback mechanism that will ensure that beneficiaries concerns are addressed. the CFM will be systematically communicated to all beneficiaries including children, caregivers, community leaders and members participating in awareness sessions. Complaints will be received via a dedicated phoneline as well as through boxes places where CP activities will take place.
