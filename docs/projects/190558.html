Project Summary

Agriculture plays a pivotal role in the lives and livelihoods of the people of Northern Ethiopia. The Northern regions have been susceptible to recurrent drought, flood, conflict and Desert Locust invasion. In Amhara and Afar regions in particular, the current conflict affected over 7.0 million people (Afar 323,494 and Amhara 6,688,352) dependent on agriculture for livelihoods, income and food security. The conflict in these regions has led to a humanitarian crisis characterized by alarmingly high levels of food insecurity, gender-based violence, widespread displacement, limited access to services, loss of harvesting and deterioration of pasture, livestock mortality and the destruction of basic infrastructures, among others.
The Guji and Borena Zonal DRM Taskforce (Multi-Agencies) has conducted need assessment in about 15 districts of the zones in October 2021. The report revealed that the onset of the Hagaya/Gana rainfall was below not normal, unevenly distributed, erratic with about one month of dry spell and ceased before a month. Overall, the assessment report indicated that there is about 20% of livestock losses due to the current drought in both zones.
The envisaged project will target conflict and drought affected districts of Afar, Amahra and Oromia regions to support vulnerable and severely affected households through food security and livelihoods interventions. In line with this, the major interventions are:
• Provision of improved crop, forage and vegetable seeds: Provision of crop and vegetable seeds will increase crop yields for household consumption and improve the nutritional diversity and status of the people, thereby positively influencing the rates of malnutrition amongst children.
• Multi-Purpose Cash Transfer: It is expected that the unconditional cash assistance will be mainly used for expenditure related to food and basic non-food items, whilst also contributing to the protection of household assets.
• Support vulnerable households but with able bodied members with conditional cash support through CfW of communal community asset/resource rehabilitation- pond and pasture enclosure/Kallo. This intervention will improve income for participant households, reduce livelihoods depletion-address HH cash need and create community resources and reduce future shocks.
• Restocking female headed vulnerable households with shoats and pullets: The restocking of shoats or pullets will improve livelihoods assets of female-headed households (the target beneficiaries of this activity), and through previous responses by PIE it has been shown to positively contribute to their status in an agro-pastoralist community in the long term.
• Support vulnerable households with veterinary drugs through voucher approaches and link with local vet facilities to get the required services.
• Capacity building training for government sectors relevant staffs, community structures and beneficiaries on livestock feeding, agronomic practices as well as DL prevention mechanisms through strengthening Early Warning and Preparedness:
Plan International will use such strategies as ensuring active participation of project stakeholders in project management and implementation starting from inception, monitoring and evaluation as well as ensure sustainability. Plan International will maintain quality of delivery of project inputs through evolving relevant professionals into the procurement processes of project inputs such as improved seeds, small ruminants and planned trainings. Procurement of improved seeds will be conducted from certified government seed supply enterprises of federal and regional while the agricultural sectors are responsible to ensure the quality of inputs in terms of adaptability to the local agro-ecology. The planed cash transfer will consider feasibility market/cash assessment and PDM to prevent potential negative consequences of GBV at household and community levelsand creation of inflation due to cash injection.

1. - Projects activities

The envisaged project will target conflict and drought affected districts of Afar, Amahra and Oromia regions to support vulnerable and severely affected households through food security and livelihoods interventions. In line with this, the major interventions are: • Provision of improved crop, forage and vegetable seeds: Provision of crop and vegetable seeds will increase crop yields for household consumption and improve the nutritional diversity and status of the people, thereby positively influencing the rates of malnutrition amongst children. There will also be an opportunity to generate income for households receiving seeds if they succeed in producing surplus crop; and for people receiving crop seeds, the sale of produce in local markets will also improve upon the availability of grains throughout the surrounding communities. Provision of fodder seeds will promote pasture production and its availability for the livestock consumption and improve households and children’s nutritional status. • Multi-Purpose Cash Transfer (no this specific activity mentioned in cluster/sector part and included under IGA support: It is expected that the unconditional cash assistance will be mainly used for expenditure related to food and basic non-food items, whilst also contributing to the protection of household assets. PIE has long years of experience in cash and voucher assistance in various part of its project intervention areas. PIE will also carry out various approaches such as consultation with government and community for acceptance of cash distribution in the area, feasibility study, market and appropriate delivery mechanism-FSP analysis, monitoring as well as PDM etc before cash distribution. • Support vulnerable households but with able bodied members with conditional cash support through CfW of communal community asset/resource rehabilitation (considered in cluster/sector section as IGA support)- pond and pasture enclosure/Kallo. This intervention will improve income for participant households, reduce livelihoods depletion-address HH cash need and create community resources and reduce future shocks. • Restocking female headed vulnerable households with shoats and pullets: The restocking of shoats or pullets will improve livelihoods assets of female-headed households (the target beneficiaries of this activity), and through previous responses by PIE it has been shown to positively contribute to their status in an agro-pastoralist community in the long term. In the short term it will reduce negative coping mechanisms. Many of these female headed households have already lost much or all of what they possess, have started selling trees or grasses, making charcoal, and/or engaged in daily work in town as servants in order to make ends meet. Further still, due to poverty and lack of awareness on the importance of dietary diversification, these activities will help address underlining contributions to rates of acute and chronic malnutrition. • Support vulnerable households with veterinary drugs through voucher approaches and link with local vet facilities to get the required services. • Capacity building training for government sectors relevant staffs, community structures and beneficiaries on livestock feeding, agronomic practices as well as DL prevention mechanisms through strengthening DL Early Warning and Preparedness: This will strengthen technical skill and service delivery of agricultural offices and DAs/Development Agents to the community and project beneficiaries on livestock management, crop management and livestock feeding. Moreover, the training will also provide the opportunity to introduce and build capacity of the community as well as the beneficiaries on livestock management and feeding, improved agronomic practice as well as DL prevention and control mechanisms.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Plan International’s response activities are shaped by global standards and engagement in the UN and sector cluster systems among other key stakeholders in both Amhara and Afar Regions to ensure complementary activities that avoid duplication and address gaps in service delivery. PLAN International regularly collaborates with the Ethiopia Nutrition Cluster Unit, FSL, WASH, CPiE, EiE, and health clusters. PIE plays an active role Agricultural Taskforce (ATF), DRM/Food Security Sector (FSS), and civil society fora/international networks of NGOs through its field team in the targeted regions and across Ethiopia, and is a key player in theHumanitarian International NGO Network (HINGO). These networks act as plat forms for policy dialogue and learning with relevant stakeholders. Plan International encourages transparency and ensures its engagement of humanitarian partners in designing and managing projects, particularly among governmental sectors, NGOs, and UN agencies. With strong operational presence in the target regions, plan International coordinates closely with the Disaster Risk Federal Commission of Ethiopia, FAO and OCHA and will continue collaborating with zonal, woreda, regional and national level actors to share project progresses, lessons learned and best practices, where Plan gains practical insight for intervention relevance and community voiced needs.Plan International will closely collaborate with local government and local committees to build capacities and leadership to ensure project relevance, reach, sustainability and community resilience. Plan will directly implement the planned project in partnership with key government sectors such as agriculture, livestock, WCAO, DRMC as well as grassroots level community structures. The respective sector will provide/assign relevant experts in planned areas of sectors to provide the required technical supports. In line with this, agricultural office will be involved in distribution of improved seeds and verify its adaptability to the local environment, WCAO along with DRMC will technically support cross cutting issues related to gender mainstreaming and CPiE activity implementation. Moreover, the respective sectors will facilitate the planned capacity building training to be provided to the project beneficiaries. The zonal and district sector representatives will participate in jointly supportive supervision on quarterly basis to monitor project progresses and provide technical back stopping and correction measures for identified challenges. The sectors will also carry out final evaluation and hand over the project and ensure its sustainability upon termination.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Plan International’s community based approach to development and emergency intervention ensures participation throughout the life of any given project. PIE has conducted participatory situational assessments in the target communities. During those assessments, children, adolescents, women, district officials and community representatives were consulted through key informant interviews and focus group discussions to identify needs and gaps as well as propose potential activities during the planning phase. The information collected has been used to design and inform the proposed response. Involvement of the local community structures in the beneficiary selection is also seen as a crucial factor for channeling the project support to those most in need. Community members will continue to play a significant role in the implementation of this proposed response, and participation will help ensure a sense of integral community ownership. The respective beneficiaries of the Food Security & Livelihoods interventions will be trained on the areas of improved agricultural practices, vegetable production, shoats/pullet husbandry and EWS. This will require full participation of beneficiaries and will build capacity and resilience. PIE will conduct regular meetings with representatives of the community (ensuring the most marginalized are also consulted, including women and children) to communicate the project's activities, appropriateness of beneficiary selection, quality of project inputs and timeliness of distributions as well as implementing agencies staff behavior etc. These meetings will also provide the venue to facilitate the project's feedback and complaint mechanisms. Following field monitoring visits, feedback will be provided for the emergency response team to respond to the complaints in a timely manner. PIE will regularly follow-up to ensure that the complaints identified are in fact addressed timely.
