Project Summary

South Sudan is currently facing a multitude of crises, including sub-national and inter-communal violence, unprecedented floods, an economic crisis, and widespread crime. These factors are the primary causes of the humanitarian needs in South Sudan, as identified in the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2023. The objective of the project is to analyse and develop strategies to enhance community resilience in South Sudan, specifically in the states and counties of Jonglei (Pibor Administrative Area), Western Bahr El Ghazal (Wau), and Central Equatoria (Terekeka). This will be achieved by addressing the challenges posed by food insecurity resulting from climatic conditions and inter-communal violence. The project is focused on addressing the needs of individuals who have returned to their home country, refugees, individuals who have been internally displaced, and the communities that are hosting them. Breakdown of beneficiaries. The intended recipients of the direct general targeting will consist of a total of 112,000 individuals. The overall count of returnees amounts to 33600 individuals. This includes 9576 adult females without disability, 9576 adult males without disability, 504 adult females with disability, 504 adult males with disability, 6384 girls without disability, 6384 boys without disability, 336 girls with disability, and 336 boys with disability. The total number of targeted refugees is 5600. This includes 1596 adult females, 1596 adult males, 84 Female with disability and 84 males with disability, 1064 Girls non-disabled, 1064 non-disabled boys, 56 girls with disabilities, and 56 boys with disabilities. The overall count of internally displaced persons (IDPs) is 16,800. Among them, there are 4788 adult females without disabilities, 4788 adult males without disabilities, 252 adult females with disabilities, 252 adult males with disabilities, 3192 girls without disabilities, 3192 boys without disabilities, 168 girls with disabilities, and 168 boys with disabilities. The overall target host community consists of 56000 individuals. Within this community, there are 15960 adult females without disabilities, 15960 adult males without disabilities, 840 adult females with disabilities, 840 adult males with disabilities, 10640 girls without disabilities, 10640 boys without disabilities, 560 girls with disabilities, and 560 boys with disabilities. Activities refer to a set of actions or tasks that are performed to achieve a specific goal or Deliver food aid to mitigate the risk of famine and enhance food intake, nutritional variety, and adaptive mechanisms for populations at high vulnerability levels (classified as IPC 4 and 5). A total of 95,000 individuals are recipients of food, non-food items, cash transfers, and vouchers. A total of 112,000 recipients are involved in improving and maintaining emergency food production by utilising supplementary inputs for vegetable and crop livelihoods, engaging in fishing activities, and raising livestock. A total of 112,000 households and individuals are beneficiaries of agricultural inputs, vegetables, and fishing kits. A total of 15 kitchen gardens have been established at various nutrition facilities, educational institutions, and residential households. Minimise reliance on food and agricultural inputs to enhance households' resilience in coping with shocks and adapting to changing circumstances. A total of 360 individuals are currently experiencing positive outcomes through the implementation of capacity building and training initiatives, enhanced livelihood productivity measures, market development efforts, and proactive anticipatory actions. A total of 180 female individuals underwent training in the areas of vegetable preservation, processing, and packaging, specifically tailored for groups of farmers involved in vegetable production.

1. Needs

The humanitarian needs in South Sudan continued to increase, driven by compounded shocks triggered by conflict, food insecurity, displacement, disease outbreaks, climate shock and economic decline. About 5.83 million people – almost half of South Sudan’s population (46 percent) – are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) between September to November 2023. An estimated 35,000 people are in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in the Duk (3,000) and Nyirol (3,000) counties of Jonglei State; and the Rubkona County (15,000) of Unity State, while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). A further 1.64 million people are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). The most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) are Jonglei State (61 percent), Unity State (58 percent), Upper Nile State (56 percent) and Lakes State (53 percent). In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2023 to March 2024, the situation is projected to improve marginally because of reduced intensity of climatic shocks. An estimated 5.78 million people (46 percent of the population) are projected to face IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), including 25,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) and 1.71 million people likely to be in Phase 4. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, the food security situation will deteriorate, and an estimated 7.10 million people (56 percent of the population) will be highly food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above). 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) while an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to be in Phase 4. The most food insecure populations are in locations that have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production, and a reduction in humanitarian assistance.Key cross-cutting issues, applicable to all transfer modalities, include prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), gender-based violence (GBV) as well as expected behaviours. HAC South Sudan and other humanitarian staff, volunteers, visitors, and suppliers will be oriented on ‘common topics from the registration to the distribution phases. All visitors and suppliers (e.g., transport services) will also be oriented on PSEA, expected behaviours and HAC South Sudan worldwide policies (gender and child protection) before being deployed to the field. The Gender and Child Protection Officer funded by this project will be responsible for ensuring that awareness sessions will be conducted and monitored for compliance. The feedback and complaints mechanisms (discussed later in this document) for reporting inappropriate behaviours from any source will be widely explained and publicized. Each site will undergo a ‘safety audit’ led by the Gender and Child Protection Officer. This is important to assess site compliance with the key security issues including its location security establishments (army barracks), gender participation, child protection and inclusiveness of access for all vulnerable groups. Concurrent monitoring for continuous compliance will also be done as part of the distribution accounting process. The beneficiary verification process will be conducted during and after the beneficiary’s selection and registration to determine the targeting efficiency, check inclusion and exclusion errors and identify “ghost” beneficiaries and inflated households’ sizes.

2. Project activities

1. Training of 24 groups on FFS (consisting of 600 farmers) Through the AEW, the project will form/revive and support 12 farmers groups consisting of 300 farmers applying the farmer field school method. Farmers will receive training on good agronomic practices and climate-smart agriculture and are supported with seeds/inputs.2. Establish seed multiplication and demonstration training plots (24): The project will promote the multiplication of locally adapted seeds by building the capacity of farmers on modern seed production and handling skills. 3. Training of 04 groups on quality seed and seedling production: Local farmers largely plant materials through their stock, social networks, and local markets. Using the FFS approach, farmers will learn skills and best practices of quality seed multiplication using seeds sourced through Seed producers, STASS, and FAO-accredited seed suppliers.4. Training 300 farmers in post-harvest handling: To enhance standards, HAC will organize training of AEAs as Training of Trainers (ToT) on USAID’s Post-Harvest Handling Training Manual for South Sudan before delivering training to participating farmers on timely harvesting, post-harvest handling practices like threshing, drying, sorting, grading and storage to minimize post-harvest losses while ensuring high-quality final products are available for sale. 5. Provision of farming tools: During the first and second years of the project, 300 farmers in all the locations will be supported with farming tools (sickles, hoes, pangas, etc.). The action will explore a market-oriented approach toward revitalizing local economies and promoting agro-input trades. 6. Strengthen the marketing information system and sharing through CMCs: Through the project, the capacity of CMCs will be strengthened to conduct routine agricultural market surveys and mapping to identify agricultural market opportunities and constraints, main actors, and interrelationships, current and expected demand for goods, as well as policy initiatives to strengthen the business environment. CMC representatives will disseminate information to fellow farmers at the group level to inform decision-making on production, sale, and product marketing.7. Train farmers/beneficiaries on agro-processing (produce mills, value addition, fish processing, and preservation) – based on roadmap/pathways for agro-processing opportunities for Western Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, Central Equatoria, and Jonglie, Agro-processing, and value addition have remained largely an untapped and unexplored value chain area. 8. Establish community drying yards/storage facilities: To ensure product quality and promote collective marketing committee initiatives, the project will work on a need basis (where production evidence can be provided) with producers to identify critical community needs and establish community storage and bulking facilities. 9. Promotion of non-agricultural livelihood activities10. Facilitate comprehensive livelihoods and market assessment.11. Training of farmers and extension workers refresher training: HAC shall offer additional refresher training to model farmers and extension workers. The refresher training shall be conducted once every year. 12. Food value addition: HAC has trained a group of 30 women in food value addition in the past year. HAC will train 300 more women in value addition. HAC will also build on its prior expertise in training women to preserve Okra and fish. 13. Distribution of Fish Gears, Seeds, and farming tools. The proposed activities will help farmers obtain crop seeds, vegetable seeds, and farming tools, making them more productive for populations severely hit by the health crisis. 14. Accountability to the affected person- AAP: collection of complaints, and feedback from the community using news tools developed by Help a Child Global.
