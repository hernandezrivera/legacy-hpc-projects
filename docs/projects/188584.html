Project Summary

WFP, as the lead agency for Logistics Sector, aims to oversee all the strategic objectives from the sector, namely: 1. provide logistics services (transhipment and warehousing) to humanitarian organisations responding to the Syria crisis; 2. maintain regional WoS inter-agency logistics coordination and information management in order to support the overall humanitarian response; 3. Continue the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) to provide safe, reliable and sustainable air access to beneficiaries for the humanitarian community, medical and security evacuations and facilitating the movement of light cargo where required.

4 - Needs

Based on a Gaps and Needs Analysis (GNA) undertaken in 2021 as well as feedback from regular consultations with partners, key needs identified for 2022 include the provision of access to common cross-border transhipment services. There is also a limited common warehousing needs inside Syria and the northeast, for 2022. in 2023, the response aims to phase-out all storage service provision. Responding actors have also showed a need for domestic passenger and cargo flights operating from Damascus to Qamishli, Aleppo as well as other regions in Syria. The cluster as whole maintains a flexible approach that allows it to adapt its services, taking into account the situational needs, and augment or reduce capacities as required. These will be reinforced by a robust logistics coordination and information management platform with regular coordination meetings at various hub and field levels which is needed to ensure a predictable and secure logistics supply chain for responding organisations. The GNA also found a need to strengthen partnership with government counterparts and development-focused stakeholders, establish locally led technical working groups to address identified technical supply-chain related challenges, and encourage partners to use local available capacities and to share assets and pool resources.

5 - Implementation of activities

The WFP-led Logistics Cluster will maintain common logistics storage services by ensuring that WFP’s warehouse contracts for different locations are in place for 2022-23 in order to maintain sufficient storage capacity inside Syria. The cluster aims to phase-out storage services for 2023. It will also coordinate cross-border transhipment operations by maintaining the contract with the hub owner and taking all the necessary security and COVID-19 precautions required for ensuring operations remain uninterrupted. Meetings in Damascus, Gaziantep, and other field locations will continue to be organised to better respond to the different needs of organisations and provide partners with operational updates and relevant logistics information on regular basis. Capacity strengthening of local actors will also be key to localise the overall sectoral response over 2022-23. This will enable a slow transfer of coordination and information management mechanisms to local entities. UNHAS will continue passenger and light cargo air services for the humanitarian community using medium size jet aircraft CRJ200 in compliance with UNAVSTADS. UNHAS Steering Committee will provide overall governance on the smooth running of UNHAS and the User Group will promote interest of users in the movement of passengers and cargo in order to fulfil the humanitarian needs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WFP ensures that the service providers contracted abide by labour rules and regulations in-country. WFP will maintain the already implemented reporting mechanism for harassment cases in Logistics Cluster warehouses, and furthermore, organisations will be encouraged to nominate female candidates to attend trainings organized. Gender sensitive and gender appropriate facilities will also be made available during a training. Finally, agency GBV focal points will be invited to meetings with contracted service providers and humanitarian organisations to give guidance on issues related to harassment, abuse and gender-based violence.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The Logistics Sector is not involved in recruiting nor contracting directly staff and logistics service providers. WFP - as Cluster Lead Agency of the Logistics Cluster – is contracting logistics providers and recruiting staff that will support the implementation of the sectorial strategy.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Logistics coordination and information management remain pivotal in ensuring needs continue to be met while simultaneously maintaining capacity and resources to address any emerging issues. Loss of access is the main risk faced. In order to mitigate that risk, the sector will continue to advocate for better access through OCHA, and explore different routes and available options to reach different locations. In addition, WFP as provider of last resort has long term agreements and contracts with different transport companies which allow flexibility and adaptability in case of any sudden change in the situation on the ground. Safety and security risks that may affect implementation of UNHAS operations will be continuously monitored and relevant mitigation measures put in place to ensure implementation of safe operations. In addition, a lack of fuel availability will severely impact the running of logistics operations. WFP is in close discussion with the Ministry of Petroleum and SADCOP to ensure a minimum supply for humanitarian logistics operations. Finally, a lack of sufficient funds is also an important risk as they need to be made available to adequately respond to emergency developments, while also ensuring the continued implementation of other ongoing sector activities. In this case, WFP will work in consultation with partners and HCT to ensure the most critical actvities are prioritised to meet the most vital needs.
