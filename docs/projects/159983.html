Project Summary

Natural and man-made factors have contributed to the protracted humanitarian crisis that affects Somalia. While the drought crisis and floods are subsiding, their effects are still felt across the country and a large number of residents remain internally displaced. Latest displacement estimates place the number of IDPs in Somalia at 2.6 million people. Of these, around 1.8 million have settled in almost 2,000 IDP sites across Somalia, most of which are informal, small and scattered settlements in urban or peri-urban areas. Living in private land often means that IDPs are at constant risk of eviction, which further stresses their already vulnerable situation. Protracted conflict-driven displacement aggravated by natural disasters, limited livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services and limited access to durable solutions contribute to the continued increase in the number and vulnerability of IDPs.
Given the acute humanitarian needs present in Somalia, CCCM plays a key role in monitoring service provision at site level, advancing accountability to affected populations, improving community participation and supporting the improvement of living conditions in sites. The work of CCCM actors at site level also requires appropriate coordination among them as well as with government officials and other humanitarian and development actors; the regular compilation, analyses and dissemination of information about displacement patterns and outstanding needs of IDPs; and capacity building of relevant stakeholders in CCCM tools and standards.

Project Activities

IOM will provide 1 national cluster coordinator, 1 national cluster information manager and 3 sub national focal points (Baidoa, Dollow, Kismayo) in order to provide coordination for the CCCM cluster This staff will represent CCCM in relevant coordination forums including the ICCG or RICCG. They will facilitate monthly cluster meetings at district level and quarterly cluster meetings at national level. They will ensure relevant responders are mobilized towards providing sectorial assistance to those identiﬁed as very vulnerable. To build the capacity of local authorities, the cluster team will train partners and government counterparts on CCCM principles. To support opportunities for durable solutions, they will advocate with local authorities for public land for displaced people. They will ensure representation in the Somalia Durable Solutions Initiative and other similar coordination mechanisms. They will engage government authorities and community governance structures to ensure that the affected population are informed about their options on durable solutions. The information manager will support partners with baseline data on displaced populations, visualize data in the form of fact sheets and maps and provide normal cluster information management functions.

Mitigation Measures

Lense 2: The cluster will ensure that partners develop and support diverse community governance systems to ensure the participation of all groups in decision making. The cluster will work closely with the protection cluster to develop strategies to mitigate exclusion in service delivery. The cluster will ensure that partners develop monitoring and complaint and feedback mechanisms to mitigate exclusion based on ethnicity, age, disability or gender.Lense 3: The cluster will advocate for community cohesion projects that support host communities to ensure that humanitarian responses do not cause tensions between displaced and host communities.The cluster will continue to work with the GBV AOR to ensure that CCCM programs mitigate the risk of GBV and that populations have safe access to services in IDP sites. Lense 4: The cluster will work closely with the HLP AOR to ensure that land tenure is ensured in sites of intervention and that populations are referred to relevant actors for HLP support. The cluster will seek to mitigate evictions by working with protection partners, local authorities, landlords and communities to find alternative solutions. The cluster will advocate for sustained humanitarian responses for populations displaced by conﬂict in the instances that durable solutions cannot be found.

Please provide further explanation

The CCCM cluster recognizes the need for coordination and strategic complementarity between humanitarian partners, local authorities and development stakeholders to ensure that humanitarian programming links relief, recovery and development. IOM, as CCCM cluster co lead, will continue working closely with development actors at both national and ﬁeld level to ensure that emergency projects support longer term solutions for both displaced and host communities. In particular, the cluster will create synergies with programming related to the Somalia Durable Solutions Initiative, as well as related consortiums, that aim to complement the humanitarian response with resilience programmes and longer-term actions that target IDPs as well as host communities. The cluster will work closely with relevant authorities and community governance structures to ensure that affected populations are informed about options for durable solutions and receive support to attain their plans.
