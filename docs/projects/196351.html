Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 420,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2023. Many among them struggle to secure basic shelter and NFI need, and will be supported by UNRWA through this project as part of the 2023 Humanitarian response plan, while adhering to the Shelter Sector technical guidelines. This intervention has three components: 1-Cash assistance for winterization: UNRWA will provide 149,500 PalRefs (59,100 families) with cash assistance for winterization (one-off distribution of $ 118.6 for each family to purchase winter NFIs) in all locations (Damascus, Rural Damascus, Homs, and Hama, Lattakia, Tartous, Aleppo and Deraa governorates / urban and rural areas/. This is a direct disbursement of cash transfers through 02 financial service providers. 2-Cash assistance for minor shelter repair (small scale): UNRWA will target 300 families (1020 beneficiaries) in two camps (Ain Al Tal, and Deraa). The transfer value per family is ranged from $900 to $ 4,000 depending on the needs of each shelter- damage level- and to be distributed through 02 service providers. 3-Inkind distribution of NFIs to 500 families / 1690 beneficiaries of Special hardship cases (. At this stage, the locations are not identified and the eligibility of the beneficiaries to this intervention depends on their hardship and the needs assessments prepared to be conducted by UNRWA social workers. The unit cost of NFI kit is $ 73.16 /beneficiary (blankets, mattresses, kitchen sets, jerry cans Mats and Tarpaulin).

3 - Needs

According to UNRWA assessments, 420,000 Palestine refugees (96%) are in need of humanitarian assistance and 149,500 Palestine refugees (35.59%) are categorized as most vulnerable (female-headed households, families with members with disabilities, families headed by an older person, and unaccompanied minors/orphans). According to UNRWA crisis monitoring report on the Socio-economic Situation of Palestine Refugees in Syria (May 2021), 82 % of Palestine refugees live with less than USD 1.9 per day (at the rate of USD 1= SYP 3,250) after receiving UNRWA cash assistance. A total of 54 per cent of families reported having taken debts within the last 3 months. Around 40% of Palestine refugees remain in protracted displacement. As per HNO 2023, adequate shelter conditions are vital for maintaining good physical health, mental well-being, and to sustain learning and educational opportunities. Such issues, when combined with overcrowding, increase the possibility of exposure to protection risks and promote the spread of respiratory and epidemic prone diseases. In a PDM conducted by UNRWA in August 2022, 48% of assessed Palestine refugees reported being able at least half of their basic needs as they define and prioritize them while the remaining 52% were not able to meet their needs. Only 1% reported that they are able to meet all their basic needs. Cash for winterization aims to cover all most vulnerable PalRefs registered by UNRWA and NFIs distribution aims to cover emerging SHCs.

4 - Implementation of activities

1-Cash assistance for winterization: UNRWA will provide 95,100 most vulnerable PalRefs HH ( 149,500 beneficiaries) with cash assistance for winterization ( one-off distribution of$ 118.6 for each family for winter NFIs ) in all locations ( Damascus, Rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Lattakia, Tartous, Aleppo and Deraa governorates/ urban and rural areas/.This is a direct disbursement of cash transfers through 02 financial service providers and UNRWA distribution center.2- Cash assistance for minor shelter repairs (on a small scale): UNRWA will target 300 families (1,020 beneficiaries) whose homes have been damaged and are in urgent need of repair in two camps (Ain al-Tal and Daraa). The transfer value per family is ranged from $ 900 to $4,000 based on the needs of each shelter, and it will be distributed through 02 service providers and an UNRWA distribution center. This intervention will include: electrical work, plumbing work, doors, windows, Block walling work, Ground slab work and plastering works. 3-Inkind distribution of NFIs to 500 SHC families / 1690 beneficiaries. At this stage, the locations are not identified and the eligibility of the beneficiaries to this intervention depends on their hardship. The unit cost of NFI kit is $73.16/beneficiary (blankets, mattresses, kitchen sets, jerry cans Mats and Tarpaulin). In kind distribution will be performed by UNRWA staff in UNRWA distribution centers. PDM is planned to monitor the implementation as well as CFM in place.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Abuse and exploitation: the beneficiaries face the risk of abuse an exploitation when travelling to and from NFI distribution points or when withdrawing their cash assistance entitlement from FSP outlets. –mitigation measure: UNRWA ensures the proximity of cash distribution to the beneficiaries considering (1) a big network of FSPs outlets and (2) UNRWA gives the opportunity/flexibility to the beneficiaries to collect cash assistance from the nearest FSP outlet even though they are invited to attend another outlet. In addition, UNRWA assigns a staff to each FSP outlet to assist the beneficiaries withdrawing the relevant cash assistance amount and guide them through the disbursement process. –Monitoring: a complaint and feedback mechanism is in place to address the concerns of the beneficiaries on timely manner.Risk 2: Exclusion (request of documentation): beneficiaries who do not have official IDs are not able to collect cash from FSPs outlets. Mitigation measure: UNRWA established a distribution center to exclusively distribute cash to the beneficiaries who are not able to collect cash assistance from FSPs outlets due to lack of official IDs. Risk 3: community tension: beneficiaries who are excluded from the assistance may create tension in the community. Mitigation measure: UNRWA ensures the community engagement in all its interventions including the discussion of the eligibility criteria with the local communities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: SEA in the context of NFI distributions and/or shelter repair (identification of beneficiaries and/or implementation)  likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigation Measures: 1. UNRWA staff Prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. 2. UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. 3. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. 4. All UNRWA staff signs the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is provided.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS:1.Humanitarian needs of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2.Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially. 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RISK 1 UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Mitigation: 1.continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2.Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5 Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. RISK 2: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. RISK 3:COVID-19pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1.UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2.PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at distribution centres.Risk4 Inflation: prices are monitored by UNRWA and transfer values will be reviewed.
