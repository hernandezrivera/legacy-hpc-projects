Project Summary

As of October 2024, over 880,000 people remained displaced across 124 formal and informal sites in South Sudan, facing severe vulnerabilities due to limited alternatives. Unplanned, self-settled IDP sites were particularly at risk of being excluded from essential services. In 2024, more than 320,000 individuals in these sites lacked CCCM (Camp Coordination and Camp Management) support due to resource constraints, which hindered their access to coordinated services, community support, and vital information.
According to internal reports by the Coalition for Humanity based on returnee and protection monitoring in Leer, Mayendit, and Panyijiar Counties, poor living conditions, limited access to basic services, and depleted coping capacities—such as the lack of financial resources—caused IDPs in camps and camp-like settings to adopt increasingly negative coping mechanisms. Overcrowded conditions in these sites significantly contributed to hygiene challenges and exacerbated issues related to insecurity, education, health, nutrition, food security, shelter, and non-food items (NFIs).
This project aims to support 20,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, and Koch Counties. Coalition for Humanity (CH) will coordinate with service providers and affected populations while monitoring the movement of IDPs and returnees. The organization’s key activities include:
1. Evaluating Needs and Coordinating Services
Assess the dynamic needs of IDPs and returnees and identify gaps in settlement sites and camp settings. CH will coordinate with service providers, partners, and community-based structures, including Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) committees and camp management committees, to ensure effective interventions and service delivery.
2. Monitoring Safety and Raising Awareness
Monitor safety and protection issues affecting IDPs and returnees and facilitate appropriate responses or referrals. CH will also raise awareness about general protection issues to mitigate existing risks and ensure that vulnerable populations understand the available response mechanisms.
3. Providing Support to Vulnerable Groups
Distribute Non-Food Items (NFIs) and implement Cash-Based Interventions for the most vulnerable groups, including individuals with special needs, women-led households, persons with disabilities, and unaccompanied children.
4. Promoting Community-Based Management
Encourage community-led initiatives for camp management and site rehabilitation by fostering community-based management practices within settlement sites and camp settings.
5. Building and Supporting Camp Management Structures
Establish and strengthen camp management structures, including camp management committees. CH will support these structures and work collaboratively with them to deliver camp management services and coordinate the provision of aid to the most affected populations.
