Project Summary

The founding members of the Syrian Society for Social Development have come together from varied geographical, cultural and professional backgrounds to provide an effective response to the needs and challenges of Syrian youth, unified by a passion to make youth a priority with a special focus on the marginalized. SSSD is a non- profit NGO registered with the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor (MOSAL), for work in the whole of Syria. Working in 13 of the 14 Governorates, lately SSSD expanded to Latakia (juveniles), to be in a total of 14 out of the 14 governorates of Syria.
The SSSD team is working in 200+ locations in Syria such as community centers, schools, communal shelters, unfinished buildings, as well as in the protection residential centers (such as Juveniles centers, centers for people with special needs, and centre for unaccompanied children). Community Centers: 33, Mobile teams: 67, Women and Girl Safe Spaces: 7, Youth Centers: 2, Satellite Centers:9, Health Points: 10, Juvenile Centers: 4, Schools: 104
SSSD has intervened in all major responses (East Ghouta, Afrin responses, and Idlib), in addition to the COVID-19 response.
In rural areas of Syria, Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) and KIs indicated that post-conflict changes in HH dynamics and women's roles have increased their exposure to Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV), including child marriage, domestic violence, sexual violence, and harassment at schools, distribution points, in public and camps. SGBV triggers and risk factors include the large IDP influx, overcrowding, increased mixing between sexes, insecure transportation, unemployment, poverty, patriarchal structures, weapons’ spread, and an overall lack of rule, order, and legal redress mechanisms. Poor hygiene and environmental sanitation seriously threaten public health, with KIs reporting lice, scabies, diarrhea, and gynecological diseases.
Humanitarian interventions by the SSSD were focused on addressing people’s WASH needs holistically, rather than simply delivering a product or a cash subsidy. The information on needs and gaps from the Sector has been further triangulated by key informant interviews with representatives from the Salkhad, AS-Sweida City, Izra’, Sheikh Miskine, As-Sanamayn, Abul Thohur, Farqalas, AL-Qusayr, al-Rastan, Hawash, Al-Hasakah, Abu Kamal, Al-Mayadin, Hadher, Jebel Saman and Maskana, Sheikh Badr, Hama City, As-Suqaylabiyah, Lattakia City, Hanadi and Rural Damascus. Furthermore, SSSD beneficiaries in 2018 were over 1.8 million from the various sectors, i.e. Protection (including Child Protection and Gender Based Violence, mine action), Health (Medical, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support), Education (focusing on dropouts and at risk youth), Shelter (focusing on damaged houses for returnees), Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Promotion; Early Recovery / Livelihood (including Vocational training, Small business grants, tool kits), agriculture , Nutrition and food.
Another 700,000 are beneficiaries of SSSD’s public services (provision of a bakery, street lighting…) reaching a total of 2.5 million beneficiaries for 2021.In 2022 the number is 2 million. In 2023 the number till Dec 30th is about 1.5 million.
Between January and February 2024, the Whole of Syria WASH Sector, together with the Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP), conducted a countrywide survey of households (HHs) across communities and based on the WASH SPHERE guidelines.
After thirteen years of crisis, WASH infrastructure and service provision require significant investments, repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damage owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, destruction, displacement of technical staff and poor water resource management. At least half of the sewerage systems are not functional and 26% of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The findings of the assessment in targeted locations reveal high needs and gaps in sufficient water, hygiene promotion, hygiene kits, and sanitation. The areas are also characterized by a significant number of IDPs and a high number of returnees- people have access to less than 15 litres of water per day (HNO WASH). SSSD will respond to WASH needs with various interventions in many different locations. This proposal draws upon data from the assessment particularly for; 1) The provision of household water tanks in: Abu Kamal, Al Mayadin, Izra' and lattakia.2) The provision of garbage bins in; farqalas, Abu Kamal, Al mayadin, Ar-Rastan, Lattakia, Hanadi.3) The distribution of household hygiene kits in: Salkhad, Sheikh Miskine, Izra', Qanniyeh, As-Suqaylabiyah, Hama, Abu Kamal, Al Mayadin, Maskana and Deir Salman.4) The provision of hygiene awareness sessions in: Salkhad, Qanniyeh, Sheikh Miskine, Izra', As-Suqaylabiyah, Abu Kamal, Al Mayadin, Maskana, Hama and Deir Salman.5) The Rehabilitation and constructions of WASH facilities in schools in: Al-Qusayr, Abtin, Sheikh Miskine, Hama and Al Mayadin.6) The Rehabilitation and repair of Water Networks in: Nashabiyeh, Salkhad, Qanniyeh, Sheikh Miskine, Izra', As-Suqaylabiyah, Abu Kamal, Al Mayadin, Maskana, Hama, Hisn, Lattakia and Dier Salman.7) The Mentenance of Sewage networks in: As-Sweida, Abul Thohur, Farqalas, Al-Hasakeh, Hadher, Sheikh Badr and Deir Salman. SSSD’s activities are closely aligned with the WASH Sector’s 2024 HRP strategy as the following objectives; - Save lives and reduce Protection risks among displaced population and host communities by providing essential WASH supplies and services and enhance hygienic practices.- Support and restore water, sanitation and solid waste management systems to ensure continuity of services for most vulnerable affected people in Syria.The project prioritizes a location that ranks high on the severity scale based on an in-depth analysis of household-level needs in accordance with the sector severity analysis in which 6 key WASH parameters are considered: 1) Water quality, 2) Water sufficiency, 3) Availability and affordability of hygiene items, 4) Solid waste disposal, 5) Issues related to sanitation, and 6) Water affordability (2024 Syria HRP). SSSD will achieve all mentioned interventions with local authorities and Water Supply Authority, focusing on genuine community needs. The chosen locations will be agreed upon through consultation, ensuring efficient maintenance systems. To promote sustainable behavior change, SSSD will conduct an in-depth assessment of community knowledge and hygiene practices. This will be followed by participatory implementation, involving the entire community for stronger sustainability through adaptable approaches.
