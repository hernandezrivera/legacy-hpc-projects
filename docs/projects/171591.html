Project Summary

The global outbreak of COVID-19 is putting further pressure to the lives of people in Gaza who have already been facing years of humanitarian crisis under the on-going blockade, coupled with the impact of recurrent hostilities and deteriorating socio-economic situation in Gaza. The restrictions on daily life activities as a result of the measures enforced by the local authority to prevent the spread of the virus are having severe impacts on lives and livelihoods, leading to exacerbated pre-existing protection risks and negative coping mechanisms. In particular, these risks are intertwined with heightened MHPSS needs, the potential for an increase in GBV and SEA, and a lack of access to legal support by vulnerable women amidst sporadic closures of the courts.
In order to mitigate these risks, UNRWA proposes to:
a) conduct protection training to 200 staff members focusing on child protection, protection of people with disabilities, and protection of the survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) to ensure the protection mainstreaming incorporating protection principles and promoting meaning access, safety and dignity in all UNRWA services.
b) organize a campaign to better inform the available services and access to UNRWA’s internal MHPSS services as well as external specialized MHPSS services for 8,000 Palestine refugees
c) organize a multifaceted campaign to employ the recommendations outlined in the IASC’s Interim Technical Note on the prevention of SEA amidst COVID-19 targeting 150 staff for ToT and 1,000 people in the community. These include the reduction of risks, prevention, strengthening safe and accessible reporting channels, coordination and providing support and protection.
d) support the provision of legal services as well as other social interventions to approximately 1,000 Palestine refugee women in partnership with community based Women Programme Centres (WPCs) located across the Gaza Strip
e) engage in a minimum of 25 advocacy initiatives to the international community.

1 - Needs

In a highly volatile political, security and socioeconomic context, people in Gaza endure a humanitarian and economic crises driven by the Israeli land, air and sea blockade which entered its fourteenth year in June 2020. This has been coupled with cycles of violence and political instability, and the COVID-19 emergency added further pressures to the lives of the people in Gaza. The Protection Cluster identified the needs to focus on increased reported incidences of negative coping mechanisms including domestic violence, child labor and child marriage, as well as anxiety and other mental health disorders associated with increasing risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors among adolescents (HNO 2021). The heightened household tension and spike in various forms of violence including domestic violence are recorded according to GBV service data. The Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics study: “Violence Survey in the Palestine Society” shows that 38 per cent of the currently married or ever married women in Gaza experienced “at least once” a form of violence such as psychological, physical, sexual, social or economic violence by their husband, and 64 per cent of the same group experienced “at least once” psychological violence by their husbands throughout the 12 months preceding the interviews (PCBS, 2019). These rates are expected to be higher in the context of the COVID-19 emergency. The impact of lockdown on the women is further studied by the NGO SAWA (2020), confirming the 38 per cent increase in domestic violence by husbands for females since the beginning of the lockdown. Through the “Impact of the COVID-19 Outbreak and Lockdown on Family Dynamics and Domestic Violence in Palestine – June 2020” research, UNFPA found that 19.5 per cent indicated that domestic violence increased in their neighborhood during the lockdown, and 70.8 per cent believed that domestic violence would continue to increase with the extension of the lockdown (UNFPA, 2020). Anecdotal evidence from UNRWA’s COVID-19 response confirms that pre-existing protection concerns linked closely to the blockade and decades of conflict are being exacerbated amidst the pandemic. Increased social isolation, food and financial insecurity are combining to create a mental health crisis. Disruptions and related mental health stressors are exposing children to increased risks, including anxiety, trauma, psychosocial relapse and violence. UNRWA has noted an increase in the anxiety, depression and mental health concerns of refugees amidst COVID-19, particularly amongst elderly, children and women at risk of or in the midst of experiencing gender based violence. A record shows a sharp rise in attempted suicide rates in Gaza with 44 children in 2020 so far compared to 8 children in 2019 prior to COVID-19 emergency (HNO 2021). UNRWA continued to provide MHPSS services remotely since the COVID-19 outbreak to respond to the increasing needs. The UN Secretary-General’s policy brief on the impact of COVID-19 on children makes clear the increased risk of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), that existing support for GBV survivors may break down, and increased tension in the household may all lead to increased GBV and child abuse at a time when women and girls are further isolated and unable to access support. The IASC’s Interim Technical Note on Protection from SEA amidst COVID-19 (2020) notes that “[A]s seen in previous public health emergencies, when the humanitarian response scales up, the risk of SEA increases. Women and children in particular face heightened protection risks.” While the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 49.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2020, women face even higher rate of 66.8 per cent (PCBS, Q2 -2020), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Children are at particular risk of potential harm where school closures interrupt school-based services and interventions for at-risk children. Greater difficulties in accessing health services, as well as increased burdens and separation from caregivers (due to quarantines, or severe illness/death), may lead to SEA against children, in particular girls, including child/forced marriage or transactional sex.” The same technical note explains that limited movement can exacerbate SEA risks for already-vulnerable populations. Scarcity of resources, in particular access to health services, may result in an imbalance of power relations, increasing the risk of exploitation. A lack of access to food, created by the health emergency, can also increase vulnerabilities and lead to negative coping strategies, thereby increasing risk of SEA. Furthermore, the Protection Cluster warns that the imposed restriction to contain the further spread of the virus negatively affected the accessibility and availability of support services for people at risk and survivors of GBV, as well as disruption of life-saving GBV services and state protection and law enforcement (HNO 2021). UN Women has highlighted that when the Gaza Strip shut down in March 2020, “the suspension of the courts left many women with no legal backing to see their children. Many ex-husbands took advantage of this legal paralysis to prevent mothers from seeing their children or to stop sending alimony.” Ensuring access to child custody rights, divorce and alimony rights by women have been consistently noted as key issues negatively impacted by court closures. These negative scenarios underscore an already coercive and unequal legal environment in Gaza that Human Rights Watch highlights as discriminatory and biased towards the rights of men in its submission of the State of Palestine’s compliance with the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (2018).

2 - Activities

With 13,000 staff and 300 installations, UNRWA is the largest UN organization in the Gaza Strip and the only UN Agency that implements services directly to beneficiaries. UNRWA has been and continues to be in a unique position to directly deliver assistance to 75% of the population of the Gaza Strip in the areas of Education, Health, Relief and Social Services that beneficiaries are referred internally or externally depending on their needs. Refugees International notes that the Agency and its partners play an essential role in providing food, water, shelter, healthcare, education, and even jobs for a largely destitute population. Considering the scale of services provided by UNRWA, most of activities are conducted within UNRWA, and the services that UNRWA is not able to provide will be referred to external partners for assistance. a) Protection MainstreamingTo mitigate and respond to the mounting protection risks and impacts, UNRWA will reinforce its protection capacity and processes within programmes and services and strengthen inter-programme coordination, cooperation and referral system. Protection training to 200 staff members focusing on child protection, protection of people with disabilities, and protection of the survivors of gender-based violence (GBV). This training will support to ensure that the protection is mainstreamed in all UNRWA services. b) MHPSSTo address the increased MHPSS concerns amongst refugees arising from the pandemic and the gaps in disseminating information about available services during COVID-19 lockdown, UNRWA will conduct a series of awareness raising activities targeting 275,000 Palestine refugees through media campaigns. The expected result is better understanding of available services and an increase in reported protection cases. It is also expected to increase the service referral and timely MHPSS response to high risk and critical cases (suicidal attempt/ideation, severe depression, cases of women with life threatening situations). It will further coordinate with external actors specialized in MHPSS services to reach 8,000 Palestine refugees experiencing or at risk of GBV, abuse, neglect or child labor identified through UNRWA’s internal referral pathways as well as further developed external referral pathways. The expected result is targeted service provision to address specific needs and further coordination with external actors. c) SEAUNRWA proposes to mitigate the risks of SEA by engaging in a multifaceted campaign in line with the recommendations outlined in the IASC’s Interim Technical Note on the prevention of SEA amidst COVID-19. These include the reduction of risks, prevention, strengthening safe and accessible reporting channels, coordination and providing support and protection. More specifically, UNRWA GFO will undertake but not be limited to implementing the following recommendations: 1. Examine and mitigate potential SEA risks in healthcare delivery settings, including through comprehensive training of healthcare personnel; the introduction or reinforcement of PSEA Codes of Conduct for all healthcare providers; and by ensuring access to safe and appropriate complaints and feedback mechanisms in healthcare settings. 2. Ensure regular safeguards are maintained during recruitment procedures (in particular of health personnel). In light of expedited recruitment procedures, it is important that core safeguards (background checks, criminal record checks) are maintained to ensure previous SEA offenders are not re-recruited. The same applies to volunteers. 3. Adapt, translate and disseminate key messages on PSEA through radio, TV, social media, print and other mediums. Ensure that key messages are included in public health messaging. 4. Circulate PSEA Codes of Conduct (CoC) and other safeguarding measures and remind staff of their obligation in this respect. Make sure that staff and contractors are trained and aware of their responsibilities and obligations as it relates to the CoC 4. Collaborate with in-country PSEA Network to share information on high-risk areas and communication campaigns. The expected results include increased awareness of prevention strategies against SEA, increased awareness of reporting channels, a strengthened prevention framework, increased accountability mechanisms and an increase in cases detected and reported due to higher awareness. d) Legal Support to GBV SurvivorsIn order to assist vulnerable women in need of legal aid services, including survivors of GBV, UNRWA will support the provision of legal support as well as other social interventions to approximately 1,000 Palestine refugee women in partnership with community based Women Programme Centres (WPCs) located across the Gaza Strip (Daraj, Beach, Nuseirat, Maghazi, Deir Balah, Bureij, and Rafah). The lawyers at WPCs will provide individual legal consultations and conduct legal awareness sessions at WPCs. They will also act as mediators for conducting children watching sessions for divorced women in accordance to the local official systems and laws. Women who are in need of intervention can seek for support at WPCs, while some cases reach them through external referrals, including the referrals from UNRWA’s case management system. In addition, WPCs will be supported to raise awareness and involve Palestine refugee communities in the protection of women against GBV, to enhance both response and prevention. WPCs participate international events to mark International Women Day and 16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence campaign annually. The expected results include improved access to legal service and by proxy to justice and an increase in the empowerment of vulnerable women at risk. e) Advocacy To raise awareness about the various protection risks outlined above, UNRWA will engage in a minimum of 25 advocacy initiatives to the international community.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff. On a biannual basis, all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. The most recent protection audit was completed in 2019, and a number of recommendations were developed, which are now being followed up on by the Protection Team. UNRWA has a well-established process during its monitoring and documentation procedures of obtaining informed consent (and assent in the case of minors) for recording and use of information that affects them. This consent process was recently enhanced with the introduction of additional guidance to field workers and training. In the context of the COVID19 response, additional steps were taken to ensure UNRWA's activities prioritise the safety and dignity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychological consultation hotline available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. Finally, UNRWA developed guidelines for remote documentation and monitoring and has also established guidelines to prioritise field work in the context of COVID19 risks.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA as a service provider is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery, and takes responsibility in this regard very seriously. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. UNRWA takes measures to ensure that its assistance under this project is available to all persons, and takes dedicated action to ensure that individuals with specific needs are reached. UNRWA takes actions to ensure that persons with disability, with movement and access restrictions, the elderly, women and children are targeted during outreach and communication, and also are key stakeholders in all community interactions and in accountability processes. Services delivered are tailored to the needs of individuals, including for those with specific needs, and the case management services delivered are developed in coordination with the affected persons and are guided by their specific needs. Specific needs according to age, gender and disability are identified at the initial assessment stage of case management, for those affected by damages to residential property, and those exposed to displacement. The individual case plans are tailored to respond to these needs, for example, provision of services linked to mobility issues amongst the elderly. In terms of the work in advocacy, UNRWA ensures that in its documentation and monitoring dynamics including gender, age and disability factored into needs and trends analysis. UNRWA conducts a number of thematic briefings per year to focus on the specific needs of vulnerable groups. UNRWA has also developed a remote documentation procedure and guidance, conducting documentation and verification of incidents remotely, including over the telephone, using additional sources and community contacts. UNRWA has demonstrated an ability to readjust its operations to effectively face the operational and movement restriction-related challenges arising from the COVID-19 emergency. Finally, the COVID-19 emergency has increased demands on UNRWA and its services and presented challenges to establishing appropriate communication with communities, as well as to accountability mechanisms in the context of COVID-19 restrictions. The Protection Department has led the establishment of dedicated ‘do no harm’ lens to the COVID-19 response. UNRWA provides support to all registered Palestinian refugees in need of humanitarian assistance. This project will strengthen the capacity of staff in protection and raise awareness in the community that is expected to provide holistic support to protection cases. Women Programme Centres are community based organizations that are well-known within the refugee community. The centres are located inside the camps across the Gaza Strip. These multi-purpose centres are often used for social, cultural and recreational activities that people experiencing GBV or any other sensitive issues would feel ease accessing the centres.
