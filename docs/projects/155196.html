Project Summary

Agrajattra is a women headed National/local NGO founded in 1998 formed in CXB and registered with the NGO Affairs Bureau since 2006. The organization having activities in different dist of the country and a regional office in Cox’sBazar since 2000. At Cox’sBazar mainly focus on Humanitarian Responds, Rohingya refugee Crisis, Shelter support, Women Empowerment (IGA) & livelihood, WASH, DRR project.
Since 25th August 2017 Violence in Rakhine State had been driven and Rohingya people crossed over the border (Myanmar - Bangladesh) through pushing which made a large scale and escalating humanitarian crisis. Many NGOs were responding to reach more people in need of life-saving assistance. Hereafter, they are settled in different camps under Ukiah & Tehnaf sub districts under Cox’sBazar, Bangladesh.
The influxes created an invisible crisis due to enormous number of Rohingya entrance. Market balance collapsed its normal characteristics, excessive relief received by Rohingya which causes to create ‘Middle man’ in marketing supply chain & management, Daily goods selling ratio is very low & environmentally destroyed the whole Ukhiya & Technaf area. It is important to realize the actual scenario of the host community as well as the local entrepreneurs that are now facing amalgamation in livelihood standard.
Since 2000 Agrajattra working with host community of Cox’sBazar dist. Including Most affected Host community of Ukiya & Tehnaf sub dist. where host people are affected due to the influx of Rohingya. Having sufficient Expertise (Agriculturist, livelihood experts), skilled staffs, large number of Volunteers where it maintains an excellent connection with local administration, Elites, Community based organization (CBO), youth club of Volunteers and Community Agriculture Volunteers (CAV) whose will play a vital role along with different stakeholders for sustainability of the poor & extreme poor (PEP) group of host community as well as Rohingya refugees as peaceful co-existence. The following intervention will be selected to achieve the project goal & objectives.
Agriculture : Field crops & vegetable Cultivation,
Comprehensive Homestead Development (CHD) -- Vegetable gardening, plantation & livestock,
Income generating Activities (IGA) for small business to improve livelihood status and Increase market access & resource mobilization,
Skill development, Vocational Training for small Business & Entrepreneur.
On & Off Farm based IGA,
Private Sector Engagement (PSE) as Partnership,
Rural development through Cash For Work (CFW),
Disaster Risk Reduction & Management (DRRM).

Needs

Rohingya influx, as well as the surge of humanitarian services, has had significant consequences for the host communities and local markets in Cox’s Bazar. Prices of everyday goods like vegetables have increased significantly, along with the prices for transportation, which in some areas have more than doubled recently. According to a market survey by AGRAJATTRA, the price of almost all daily consumed food items including rice and vegetables has increased up to 120%. All retailers claimed about their business reduction by 60%-70% after the influx occurred. Access to more income-generating activities, especially to strengthen local food supply chain, and also to skills training and market linkages are required to promote social cohesion among the host community and Rohingya community. Any intervention to increase food availability through vegetable cultivation in camps and host community and accessibility through cash for work support will improve the food security and livelihood of both communities. AGRAJATTRA developed the livelihood strategy in light of the recent livelihoods assessments. We conducted market survey assessment in different areas of most affected Ukiah & Tehnaf Upazilas bazaars and it’s was part of a joint livelihood assessment with FSS last year 2018. At broader level, all the assessments indicates that livelihoods intervention through different skills training, cash for work, and social engagement initiatives are needed to provide longer-term solutions. From our field experience, and other internal and external assessments, a few preferences and patterns have emerged, including: • Host communities became vulnerable a decrease in few major traditional employment areas, due to this Rohingya influx: Restriction on fishing in NAF River limited opportunities for the fisherman from Cox’s Bazar Dist.  Conventional irrigation channels were disrupted in the surrounding villages of different makeshift settlements, resulting into a decrease in agriculture production. • Increased small businesses opportunities for the host community due to the demand from the Rohingya community. • High levels of interest among Rohingya for skills training and cash for work. • Women are interested in receiving training on tailoring, embroidery, beauty parlor etc.• Men are interested in receiving training on mobile repairing, masonry, and trading etc• Interest in building/constructing different facilities and provide cash for work support. • The host community labor force in Tehnaf and Ukiah has limited technical skills; the majority of respondents state that their cheap livelihood is fishing (66%) with wage laboring second (19%). Other findings included:• Men are interested in and want to work on agriculture, masonry, carpentry, and tailoring• Women are interested in and want to work on bamboo/cane work and stitching nakshikatha. • School dropped out girls and boys are interested to receive skills training at market place on tailoring, mobile phone servicing, carpentry, computer training etc.• Provide financial assistance to local businesses and to people who are undeserved by other mainstream financial institutions.

Activities or outputs

The following key needs are req for Rohingya community & Host Community as per assessment : ● Opportunity to learn new skills that can lead to income generating activities. ● Interest in livelihood/self-reliance support and activities can serve as an entry point for other services where demand is latent e.g. psycho-social counseling, nutritional support, child friendly space, etc. ● Desire to transition from relief recipients to active earners.● Increased local market access to create more income-generating activities. ● Increased access to relevant supplies of materials/capital/assets and financial support for active involvement in the self-reliance activities. For host community : ● Increase the support to income-generating activities among the host communities. Especially, in the areas of agriculture and food production.● Opportunities for the host community members to engage economically, especially with displaced people, directly or through other stakeholders.● Increased financial and technical assistance to the local community on different sectors to both improve the livelihood of the local people and promote local economic growth.
