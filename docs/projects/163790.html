Project Summary

The proposed project in Sebha and Wadi al Shati will support vulnerable children, youth and families, IDPs, Host communities and migrants, through a child and youth focused intervention in the sector of education and protection.
17 schools within the municipalities of Sebha, Temanhint and Brak al Shati will be supported in providing qualitative and protective education to 1500 IDPs, host community and migrant children, through school rehabilitation, equipment and trainings.
At the same time, and 9 CBOs, within the same areas, will be equipped and trained to provide social, health and protection services to the families of the targeted areas.

1 - Needs

The conflicts have damaged private and public infrastructure and seriously jeopardised public service provision, consistently affecting social cohesion mechanisms, deepening the divide between the different ethic and tribal groups and between these and the migrants, leading to further discrimination and abuse. MIGRANTS: Regardless of the erratic and sometimes conflicting migration policy followed by the Libyan government from 2000 onward, Sabha ad the entire Fezzan region have always been the main gateway to Libya from Niger and Chad, but also southern to entire western and central African region. In Sabha, migrants settled in established informal settlements, which they refer to as ‘ghettos’, dormitories owned by fellow nationals who arrived before them. The IOM DTMs analyses migrations in Sebha region since 2016 and it reported that from a benchmark of 10,000 to 20,000 in 2016, the number of migrants started to increase since mid-2017 reaching a total number of migrants in August 2018 of 38,815. PEOPLE WITH UNDETERMINED LEGAL STATUS: A significant number of people in Libya – estimated between 150,000 and 200,000 – are caught in a legal impasse: not officially recognized as citizens and cannot access documentation. Without the rights to work or study, these people are more likely to participate in informal or illegal economic activities, such as smuggling and trafficking. they largely do not benefit from healthcare, education and legal employment, and do not have the right to vote, barred from the ballot since 2014. Marginalization has forced communities to entrench themselves behind tribal and city-based identities, this has created a breeding ground for social unrest, crime, and radicalization, with ex-combatants and unemployed youth particularly at risk. IDPs: the conflicts led to a significant displacement of the population all across the southern region, with people moving from one municipality to another, trapped in inter tribe violence, in search for shelter and fearing for their lives. Sebha district ranks second in terms of displacement, just right after Benghazi. The displacement in this area is multi-layered, it occurred in several waves over the last 10 years, although the highest number of IDPs have been coming to these areas in 2018 and 2019, with the latest wave of 4,475 IDPs coming from Murzuq between August and September 2019 (DTM). While the arrival of such a high number of IDPs has created a situation of widespread overconcentration, overcrowding was most severe in Al Jadeed, the mahalla that has absorbed the most IDPs.IDPs face life in a region heavily affected by the resurgence of the conflict around Tripoli in April 2019. The South is historically dependent on the inflow of food and non-food items from the Tripoli area and from April 2019 onward. CHILDREN: Both Libyan and non-Libyan children are disproportionally affected by the ongoing conflict, at risk of domestic violence, sexual and gender-based violence and other forms of ill-treatment. Boys and girls suffer additional protection risks due to the conflict-related actions of their caregivers and/or community and the displacement affecting IDPs communities add another layer of burden to the already difficult living conditions. Within the continuous conflictual context of the South, youth are also at great risk of recruitment by armed forces; in the same way all children on the move are at heightened risk of forced conscription, being co-opted into brigades as young mercenaries or being trafficked for that purpose. Children, including UASC, are at risk of arrest and indefinite detention in Libya, where they do not receive preferential treatment and share cells with adult detainees, increasing their vulnerability and the risk of abuse. While few public schools are present albeit overcrowded, public childcare below 6 years old is completely absent and has been reported as one of the main needs.

2 - Activities

1.1 Physical rehabilitation of selected schoolsThe selected schools will undergo renovations and equipment, each one based on an individual work plan developed with the school staff as well as with the community and the families with children attending the schools. Based on the assessment conducted, small renovations are needed across the school, mostly concerning the toilets and the wells which are to be rehabilitated and water tested in 10 out of the 17 schools identified.1.2 Capacity building of schools and local CBOs (education)INTERSOS will conduct training for the staff of the selected 17 schools and of the 9 CBOs.While all schools and CBOs will undergo a preliminary Capacity and Knowledge assessment, which will be the base of their own individual capacity building plan, the following general topic will be mainstreamed into the skill building plan:1.3 Provision of Non formal education activities within Baity CentreThe activity will support all identified children in need of integrative and supportive educational activities. Children and youth in need will be welcomed benefiting from remedial and catch-up classes. Activities will focus on three main subjects: - Arabic, - English - Math.2.1 Case ManagementVulnerable girls and boys, and their parents/caregivers, will be supported through the provision of comprehensive case management including, in addition to immediate care and psychological first aid (PFA), best interest’s assessments, distribution of tailored material assistance and referrals. 2.2 Provision of Psychosocial support services to children and adultsBasic PSS activities for children: PSS activities for children will be done through the Bayti, for all the children who will be referred there and will attend sessions there, through the CBOs and within the schools.2.3 Capacity building of schools and local CBOs (protection)Building upon the curriculum already developed for the capacity building plan 2020

4 - Protection risk analysis

Risk 1: Restriction of movement of agencies, personnel, or goods to Libya preventing Beneficiaries to access the servicesRisk 2: Restriction of movement of agencies, personnel or goods within Libya Risk 3: Military operations and ongoing hostilities impeding operations for humanitarians and beneficiariesRisk 4: Violence against personnel, assets and facilities endangering beneficiaries' protection Risk 5: Major change in attitude of authorities and local authorities toward the planned interventions R6: Diversion of fund from staff and contractors. INTERSOS ensures directly the monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Performance of staff and contractors will be monitored by INTERSOS staff: any breach will result in disciplinary actions. R7: Authorities are not willing to participate in activities and prevent beneficiaries of some tribal group to attend the sessions.

5 - Mitigation measures

Mitigation measure and Contingency Measures (M)M1: INTERSOS is registered in Libya and access to Libya is granted. INTERSOS can count on local staff with solid management experience. DO NO HARM and protection checklist are done before the activities to take place.M2: The program would not stop in case of restricted access to Libya as most of the works, goods and services are purchased locally and local staff is trained and used to remote management. INTERSOS is working in Sebha and movements are done currently.Movements will be constantly monitored by a security agency. The support of Municipalities and ease the negotiation with the authorities to grant the access. INTERSO is an active UNICEF partner and the UN agency can help in negotiation. Negotiation with the authorities allows INTERSOS to dialogue with all tribes. M3: The conflict situation is closely monitored by a security agency. Security alert system in place prevents risk and different routes or timing are adopted to assure the safely conduct of operations.Should outbreak of violence affect again the security situation of the targeted areas, activities would be put in stand by and timely communication released.M4: INTERSOS ensures a continuous coordination with partners at local level and international specialized agencies (security agency) to monitor the situation.INTERSOS adopted a security plan and SOP in case of any major accident. M5: INTERSOS ensures good relationship with authorities in the area, informing them of the planned activities and coordinating with them in terms of access and security.M6: ensures a transparent and accountable way of working. Plans of intervention are shared and discussed to increase acceptance.M7: Multiple representatives from each local authority are engaged in the Action, to ensure that successful delivery is not dependent on key individuals.
