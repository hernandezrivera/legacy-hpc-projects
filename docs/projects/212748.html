Project Summary

HIHFAD seeks to continue integrating nutrition-related outreach activities and into the healthcare facilities it is currently operating through screening all children under 5 years and PLW who visit the clinics for nutrition services or other services (RH, or IMCI clinics, etc.), select beneficiaries through MUAC screening and/or Weight for Height/ Weight for Length (Z-score), provide SAM treatment for children with complicated SAM using therapeutic milk(for use exclusively in hospitalized patients) f75, F100, and RTUF beside clinical management of common complications, provide SAM treatment for children with uncomplicated SAM using Plumpy nut and MAM treatment for children and PLW with MAM using Plumpy sup, provide micronutrient supplementation to pregnant women and children, Provide one-on-one, and one to group counseling on WHO-recommended (IYCF)and maternal nutrition practices, disseminate IYCF messages.
- As well as HIHFAD will conduct training to build the capacity of frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19 and Cholera outbreaks. HIHFAD will receive the needed nutrition supplies for this project from UNICEF,WFP ,and through private donations to cover the needs. Around 2 million IDPs are spread over 1,500 IDP sites (80% of IDPs are women and children), the Combined Global Acute Malnutrition (cGAM) is reflecting of the burden of Wasting that has increased; from 5% in 2022 to 5.6% in 2023 (6.45% increment in Boys, 4,9% increment in Girls) in NWS based on SMART survey results from 2022 and 2023. Prevalence of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM)also increased from 2.4% to 3.4% (4.95% and 4.2% increment in Boys, Girls respectively). The prevalence of Anaemia in Adolescent girls was notably high; 59% in Aleppo and 41% Idleb, while and the prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) has decreased slightly from 0.9% in 2022 to 0.6% in 2023.Compared with 2022, The prevalence of stunting rate remains at the same level at 22.4% in 2023 with a very slight increase compared with 2022 at 22.3%, but it is still over 20% which is considered as a Critical/ Extreme according to the GNC (Global Nutrition Cluster) severity scale, the prevalence of underweight has been increased from 8.5 4 % in 2022 to 10.45% in 2023. Project data is collected on a monthly basis from the field, and reviewed by technical and administrative staff to ensure quality control, and adherence to the project proposal and sent as 4Ws for Nutrition Cluster on monthly basis, also HIHFAD follows the financial and administrative guidelines for financial control. HIHFAD's M&E team will monitor these project activities based on the agreed log-frame, A detailed monitoring plan will be prepared according to the final version of the log-frame, HiHFAD will ensure its MEAL plan to be implemented fully compliant with ‘Do No Harm ‘principles. The MEAL team will be guided and equipped with protection-sensitive interview skills and approaches in order not to damage the dignity of the beneficiaries. The Meal plan will apply the stratified sampling method in order to obtain sex and gender-segregated data, ensuring the equal participation of women and youth who are quite often under-represented in Syria response. Gender and age-appropriate survey tools and methods will be developed and the MEAL team will ensure appropriate gender balance among its staff, ensuring its access to both gender groups. The monitoring and evaluation team will implement the following plan:1- Field visits: to observe the overall progress of the project and compare the work plan with exact implementation.2- Monthly reports: To check programmatic/financial progress, identify obstacles3- Patient Exit Interviews via patients, parents, and companion feedback. (Monthly)4. Receiving and reviewing all complaints with the project focal point through WhatsApp complaint line and the complaints box

0 - Project Summary (extended)

in more detail, HIHFAD will manage the nutrition response through 16 RRTs,8 OTPs ( integrated primary care centers ), and 3 SC ( integrated in 3 maternity and children hospitals the activities will be done through the teams will be as follows: - Conduct training for Nutrition staff on the following topics on (CMAM, IYCF,PSEA, CP,PF, and PSS) - Conduct screening activities for children aged 6-59 months and PLWs to identify cases of malnutrition. - Admit children 6-59 months and PLWs with MAM and SAM without medical complications into the CMAM program. - Refer children aged 0-59 with SAM and medical complications to specialized services SC.-Provide preventative nutrtion supplies to children 6-59 months (not identified with malnutrition) to prevent anemia and malnutrition Conduct daily one to one counseling and group sessions on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) to PLWs and caregivers of children under 5 years .Provide IYCF Kits to Lactating women under with children under 6 months through OTPs Select and register beneficiaries to enroll in the CVA program based on the selection criteria. Conduct verification for beneficiaries that will be targeted by cash assistance. Distribute four rounds of cash to selected beneficiaries.Conduct post-distribution monitoring for Cash rounds .All project staff will receive PSEA sensitization training sessions before the activity's implementation, and staff will work to share information with the beneficiaries during the project activities implementation about PSEA and reporting channels/mechanism, the awareness materials include child-friendly materials will be provided.HIHFAD Developed PSEA IEC materials to be used in health facilitates, In addition to dissemination of the reporting channels as posters with all distributed items in the project, in addition to integrated Complaints against PSEA in complaint leaflets, a hotline number specific to PSEA cases, where this number is held with the Female PSEA Focal points. In implementing Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) within our sector, HiHFAD recognizes the critical importance of addressing child nutrition during crises. Children's dependency and physical fragility heighten their vulnerability, emphasizing the need to protect their development, particularly during the crucial first 1000 days of life. Gender disparities in food access further exacerbate these vulnerabilities, especially in times of instability. HiHFAD's mitigation strategies involve embedding risk prevention measures into our nutrition initiatives, prioritizing the safety of affected populations. Through mother-to-mother nutrition programs, we create spaces for discussions on protection concerns, including psychosocial support and gender-based violence (GBV). Collaborating closely with the protection sector, we prioritize the most vulnerable, facilitating smooth referral pathways between nutrition services and GBV/child protection facilities.
