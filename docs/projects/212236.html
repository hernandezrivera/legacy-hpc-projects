Project Summary

UNRWA will provide PSS services to 11,360 Palestine refugees to address trauma caused by years of conflict and deteriorating socio-economic conditions. Almost two in three families in Syria are psychologically distressed over their living conditions. Signs of psychological distress in children are reported by more than 27 per cent of households and are highest in female-headed households or when the head of household is living with a disability.
The prohibitive cost of rented accommodation outside refugee camps has forced the return of people to their houses in areas heavily damaged by the conflict, in particular in Yarmouk, Ein el Tal and Dera’a camps. Given the limited infrastructure and support services available, as well as the risks of Explosive Ordnance, these returns are out of necessity rather than choice. It is estimated that 30 per cent of populated areas in Syria are contaminated by ERW, including Palestine refugee camps. This threat remains a major protection concern in Syria, particularly for children. UNRWA will provide explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) for at least 15,000 people including UNRWA students, community members and staff, with a particular focus on areas of higher risk, including areas of return.
In line with UNRWA’s commitment to humanitarian principles, the Agency will mainstream protection into service provision, including by increasing staff capacity to identify and address intersecting factors affecting individual vulnerabilities. UNRWA will train 1,000 personnel on protection mainstreaming, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), disability inclusion, case identification and referral, GBV, and Child Protection. GBV preventive and responsive services including GBV case management by UNRWA social workers, economic empowerment and life skills at UNRWA’s 13 community centres with women’s safe spaces, and PSS etc will be provided to address GBV risks including lack of safe access to basic needs, risk of school dropout for girls, sexual and intimate partner violence, gender-inequitable distribution of household income etc.
Legal assistance will be provided to some 4,000 refugees to support them in obtaining civil documentation through UNRWA's legal aid program running through six offices in the country.
UNRWA will also continue to offer assistance to vulnerable refugees through protection activities, awareness raising sessions, and the provision of vocational training. Many of the suggested activities will be delivered with community volunteers. In an effort to expand its community-based protection mechanisms, the Agency plans to increase the awareness and involvement of the community through Area Protection Committees.
Furthermore, 1,500 people will be provided with needed assistive devices.
Protection monitoring, reporting and advocacy will be prioritized. UNRWA will continue to (i) participate in global protection mechanisms; (ii) engage, as appropriate, with the International Human Rights System (IHRS) in line with the Agency’s IHRS framework; and (iii) interact with relevant stakeholders, including the protection coordination structures of the Humanitarian Country Team at country and area levels. UNRWA will continue to monitor and advocate for, and respond to, particularly vulnerable groups within the Palestine refugee community. This will include individuals who remain in protracted displacement and Palestine refugees who choose to return to their place of habitual residence from within Syria or abroad, noting that those returning to newly accessible camps with limited services are often the most economically vulnerable.
The Agency will also strengthen adherence to humanitarian principles, including by conducting integrated assessments of all its over 120 premises to ensure they are compliant with protection standards and UN values and that they are safe, accessible and inclusive spaces.

Advocacy for rubble removal around UNRWA installations.PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN ClearCheck recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations.
