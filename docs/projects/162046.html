Project Summary

The project is designed to contribute to a reduction in WASH related public health risk through improved access for refugees and affected host communities in the area of Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Promotion. Infrastructure construction and service delivery would mainstream community engagement within AGD/Protection lens for better project outcomes and sustainability.

Objectives

1. To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs.2. To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable toilets and bathing spaces to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times.3. To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviours through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities. 4. To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected host communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable access to safe, sustainable and effective solid waste collection close to their place of residence.

Needs

In 2019, UNHCR and partners have routinely updated the comprehensive and detailed infrastructure sweep and review the WASH Knowledge, Attitude, and Practices (KAP) survey by the end of 2018. These update and review have contributed to better understand the WASH related needs of refugees in order to inform programme planning. UNHCR is the area focal agency for 14 camps (1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, Kutupalong RC, 3, 4, 4 Extension, 5, 17, Chakmarkul, Nayapara RC, 26 and 27) and Kutupalong Transit Center covering 40.4% of refugees in Ukhia and Teknaf Districts. There is also a need to address the needs of the host communities where they have shared interests with the refugee community. Sanitation coverage remains an issue within the camps. Communal latrine usage averages 23 individuals/latrine in Kutupalong (ranging from 12-38)-above the post-emergency target of 20. Additionally, bathing area usage averages 33 individuals/area—above the post-emergency target of 20. Lack of full coverage places particular strain on women and girls, who report being unable to use bathing spaces and latrines, and an increase in construction of ad hoc bathing areas and latrines inside shelters, causing contamination of surface drainage with faecal coliform. The large and increasing stock of latrines require frequent desludging, posing health risks and increased costs, as sludge must be pumped, transported, and treated. Despite major developments in 2018/2019, treatment capacity remains below necessary rates. Surveys indicate low levels of refugee hygiene awareness, with high rates of open defecation, misuse of toilets, and contamination of water after collection. Contamination of stored water at household levels also remains high. Testing of grey water shows heavy contamination with faecal coliform, often running into the open environment. WASH kits and hygiene kits have not been fully or systematically distributed, with gaps remaining. Few solid waste management systems are functional, with plastics build up becoming an issue, particularly with blocked drainage systems. The high refugee population and lack of sufficient land are contributing factors to the above, leading to lack of space for construction of facilities/access for desludging, as well as challenging environmental conditions in hilly terrain and flood/landslide prone areas. Hygiene interventions would include: personal hygiene including MHM, environmental hygiene, as well as water and food hygiene. Communities including user groups, religious leaders, women and children need to continue to be engaged in Hygiene promotion to enhance their holistic understanding of disease transmission routes and key practices to break transmission routes. WASH facilities and infrastructure for girl/women would be designed and built to be disability and MHM friendly. Market hygiene sessions and advocacy with government camp authorities would be intensified for improved food hygiene by commercial food vendors. The main source of domestic water for Rohingya refugees varies between the two main camp areas, Kutupalong and Teknaf. In Kutupalong, the main water source is groundwater accessed by hand pumps, now supplemented by a system of solar powered chlorinated water networks to be developed throughout 2019. A total of 4,051 tube wells in the 10 Kutupalong UNHCR-managed camps supply a population of 274,303 people, resulting in 68 persons per pump—far below the 250 person/pump UNHCR standard and a great success. UNHCR’s recent Knowledge and Practice (KAP) survey indicates that all camps except one receive the minimum 15 l/c/d, with only two receiving above the post-emergency levels of 20 l/c/d. For camps in Teknaf, the main water source is surface water collected in small reservoirs, treated and distributed through a network with tap-stands. The three UNHCR-managed camps receive only 12 l/c/d at present.

Activities or outputs

UNHCR shall continue to play a leading role in WASH sector coordination as co- chair of the Sanitation working group ensuring the following: - Multi-year investment for a sustainable sanitation infrastructure to reduce high future operational costs, aiming to target 42% of refugees in 2020; - Conduct basic maintenance of existing adequate sanitation infrastructure, as well as improvement of below-standard latrines to increase storage capacity alongside decentralized treatment to reduce desludging, piloting of potential sustainable sanitation solution technologies and improved facilities for women, children and persons with disability. - Scale up and prioritize hygiene promotion, including expanded distribution of hygiene consumables; such as female hygiene kits, soaps, jerrycans, aquatabs and WASH full and top up kits as context dictates.- Improve connecting drainage (WASH facility to tertiary) infrastructure for proper drainage and treatment of greywater; - Develop integrated household latrines and bathing areas to be adopted as standard in all new transitional shelter design for 13,000 Household; - Improvement of facilities for women, children, and persons with disability and align communal latrine coverage to UNHCR standards; - Transition to steel frame latrines and bathing area that are more cyclone/weather resistant, safer and cost efficient. - Increase faecal sludge treatment with a mix of large off-site units and increased use of small-;bore sewerage and appropriate cost effective on-site disposal; - Increase solid waste management systems and infrastructure; - Continue the multi-year investment to develop more appropriate water infrastructure in order to sustain the water supply for 369,000 refugees in UNHCR-managed camps and to reduce long-term operation and maintenance costs and maintenance of water infrastructure for ongoing operations. - Continue its support the WASH sector for the coordination, design, implementation, and management of 140 piped water networks in Kutupalong. - As lead of the Water technical working group support the Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE) and the WASH Sector and partners to develop a water management plan for Kutupalong through capacity support towards planning, designing, coordinating, monitoring and managing water facilities . -Sanitation targets three activities - fecal sludge management, solid waste management and sanitation facilities. For Fecal sludge management systems will be constructed or extended in Uhkia and Teknaf to cover all of UNHCR camps. Existing decentralised or semi-centralised systems will be incorporated or decommissioned and alongside manual desludging will be phased out- Solid waste management processing facilities including composting and recycling will be constructed at camp or multi-camp level. Segregated communal pits and HH bins will be installed and distributed. Further behaviour change and community cleaning campaigns will increase bin usage and waste segregation practices.- Sanitation facilities shall be upgraded with a gender and PwSN lens. Where gaps exist new facilities will be constructed and complemented by Women's Hygiene Centers. -The existing chlorinated Solar Water Powered Systems will be upgraded and extended to ensure continuity of safe water access all over the year and coverage of tap stands will be increased. interim solutions such as reservoirs will dug to cover needs until recurrent water shortage in Teknaf is addressed comprehensively for both host and refugee communities.
