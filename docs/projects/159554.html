Project Summary

DRC-DDG aims to implement this legal aid and capacity building project to (a) strengthen protection for conflict-affected people, including IDPs through the provision of needs based legal assistance; (b) to ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to high quality legal services. The action will address the legal needs of the most vulnerable displaced and conflict-affected populations in eastern Ukraine through a legal assistance intervention. It will also improve the resilience of conflict-affected population, including IDPs, and strengthen local response capacity through the FSLAC to ensure their access to quality legal services. The project will improve access to legal assistance and legal remedies by targeting persons who reside within GCA (0-5 km) area as well as NGCA (0-20 km) and cross the line of contact (LoC) through the five designated Entry-Exit Check Points (EECP). Beneficiaries will be identified and prioritised (through referral mechanisms when necessary) in cooperation with FSLAC lawyers, working at the EECPs under the existing ECHO project that is being implemented by DRC-DDG. R2P Eastern Ukraine Checkpoint Monitoring statistics for 9 months demonstrate that 94% of people, who crossed the contact line, were NGCA residents with 15% of them living in NGCA 0-20 km. area. According to the State Border Service, almost 15 mln. cross the contact-line at five EECPs annually. It means that on average 119 people cross the contact line each hour at all EECPs in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts (working hours in the fall). Alignment of these two highly credible data sources and their analysis demonstrates that each day of the project implementation may improve access to legal services for 130 NGCA residents living within 0-20 km. area. As there are 250 working days in a year (Ukraine, 2019), the approximate number of NGCA residents (0-20 km.) who would have better access to legal assistance during the project implementation is 32,500. The proposed project builds on DRC-DDG’s strengths and experience in legal aid provision, partnership network, as well as DRC-DDG’s resources and expertise in legal analysis. The proposed Action will be implemented through the five satellite centres to be operated by the Local Centre For Free Secondary Legal Aid Provision (FSLAC). DRC-DDG already has a partnership arrangement with FSLAC under which FSLAC has been providing primary and secondary legal assistance at three EECPs (Mayorsk, Novotroitske, and Marynka) in the past year. The proposed Action will fund these three existing EECPs as well as operationalise the other two EECPS at Hnutove and Stanytsia Luhanska. Full operationalisation of Hnutove and Stanytsia Luhanska EECP is subject to approval of local authorities, security clearance, availability of movable workstations, and access to services including access to utility. As of the date of writing this Action, DRC-DDG and FSLAC is confident of availability of such facilities in Stanytsia Luhanska. DRC-DDG and FSLAC will make best efforts to extract cooperation from all relevant stakeholders to install permanent facilities in Hnutove. In the event that a permanent station in Hnutove is not viable, the Action will cover beneficiaries at Hnutove EECPs through daily mobile clinics.

1 - Needs

Dates of assessments:- June 2018: REACH, Humanitarian Trend Analysis Fact Sheet. Government-controlled areas of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts- December 2018: HCT, Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019, Ukraine- 2019: UNHCR Participatory Assessment Report for refugees, asylum seekers, and internally displaced persons in Ukraine.- January-March 2019: Eastern Report Checkpoint Monitoring- April 2019 Statistics of the work of Donbass SOS Hotline For this project, DRC-DDG conducted a desk review of surveys, reports and needs assessments conducted by other humanitarian and civil society actors in Ukraine including the Protection Cluster, UNHCR, R2P, REACH, and the Donbas SOS. According to the Eastern Ukraine Checkpoint Monitoring data for June 2019, conducted by R2P at all five EECPs with 2,119 people, 70.4% of respondents reported the need to reinstate their pension payments as the main reason for crossing. What is more, reinstatement of pension payments was named as the main reason for crossing for 92.5% of respondents aged 60 and more, which shows elderly people’s dependence on legal services. Another 10.2% of the respondents crossed the EECPs due to the issues with documents, including ID cards, marriage certificates, and birth certificates. 67.2% of these respondents were women. Eastern Ukraine Checkpoint Monitoring data for April-June 2019 shows that 9% out of 6,539 respondents need information on where to apply for legal aid while 18% need contact information to reach out to with specific questions, complaints, and suggestions. Moreover, 10% of respondents require information on services available at EECPs. 97% of respondents (April-June 2019) are either NGCA residents or returnees. FSLAC statistics from its legal aid activities at EECPs under DRC-DDG's current ECHO project shows the following legal aid trends: restoration of pension payments (41%), passing physical identification at Oshchadbank (9%), procedures for crossing the contact line (19%), establishing facts of death and birth (7%), and other social benefits (5%). The April 2019 survey by NGO Donbas SoS, based on 2,409 hotline phone calls, shows that more than 80% of callers had one or more legal issues to solve. Those issues related to civil documentation, obtaining or renewing pension and social benefits, procedures for crossing the contact line, and employment matters. Lack of a simplified administrative process for civil documentation resulted in unaddressed civil documentation needs of hundreds of thousands of NGCA residents. The “reintegration law” of 2018 is yet to make any positive impact on the civil documentation situation of NGCA residents, as the simplified administrative procedure foreseen by the law has not yet been rolled out. The Unified State Register of Court Decisions shows 2,667 decisions on birth certificates and 7,214 decisions on death certificates for NGCA residents since the adoption of the reintegration law on 24 February 2018. These numbers are not significantly different than what the courts have issued in previous years. DRC-DDG estimates that the courts are addressing less than 20% of the civil documentation needs created in a year in NGCA. The remaining 80% are being added to the backlog of legal issues, which is likely to create a massive documentation crisis and could trigger significant additional humanitarian needs over time.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Activity 1.1 FSLAC lawyers will provide primary legal assistance through satellite centres in Mayorsk, Novotroitske, Marynka, Hnutove, and Stanytsia Luhanska EECPs. Activity 1.2FSLAC lawyers will provide secondary legal assistance, administrative, documentation, and court support through satellite centres in Mayorsk, Novotroitske, Marynka, Hnutove, and Stanytsia Luhanska EECPs. Activity 1.3FSLAC lawyers will disseminate legal assistance information and awareness-raising products. Activity 1.4DRC-DDG staff will conduct trainings to FSLAC lawyers working at EECPs. Outcome 1.1 2,800 conflict affected persons receiving primary legal aid. Outcome 1.2.500 conflict-affected persons receiving secondary legal aid. Outcome 1.3.3,300 conflict-affected persons with increased/appropriate information on relevant rights and/or entitlements. Outcome 1.4Fifteen (15) lawyers working at EECP will receive training from DRC-DDG. Throughout the activities listed above, measures will be taken to comply with the standards of CHS and AAP mechanisms, namely: a) Feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into the country strategy, programme proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, partnership agreements, and highlighted in reporting. b) DRC-DDG will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices. c) DRC-DDG will actively seek the views of the affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming. The feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction.
