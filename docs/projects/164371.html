Project Summary

This project started in late 2017 and, in its efforts to deliver safe and sound reproductive health services to war affected population in urban/rural areas and camps proved successful. However, service provision remains challenged by fighting and displacement (most recently due to Peace Spring Operation), and it emerges more important than ever giving continuity to strategic activities until full stabilization of the situation. To ensure a coordinated and lifesaving response UPP continues to support a work focused on improving standards of Reproductive Health through supporting and building the capacity of network of health staff that have been working with high results for more than two years. The intervention is multifaceted: on the one hand, mobile delivery units are particularly relevant in the current context of changing environment (as a piece of the health system and associated with a strong referral system), especially to provide integrated SRH/GBV services for displaced communities in remote villages, informal settlements and in collective centres recently established. However, secondary care remains a main concern, especially in populous urban areas that have experienced considerable influxes of IDPs from conflict zones. Management of intrapartum complications and performing C-sections in a safe environment constitute crucial needs in context where
surgical interventions are operated almost exclusively by private facilities at prices absolutely unaffordable for the majority of people. The key activities will include provision of essential SRH services, including Basic and Comprehensive EmONC, to 4 National hospitals, 1 PHC, and 2 mobile delivery unit. Furthermore, UPP will support the provision of essential RH equipment, medicine and supplies. UPP will also aim to strengthen existing referral mechanisms at health district level and coordinate standardization of services through continued development and validation of protocols and guidelines.
The project extends across 4 governorates and targets 50,000 beneficiaries with service
delivery during a year:
45,000 Women
5,000 Girls (<18)
Selection criteria for beneficiaries will include who lives in the catchment area. Considering the nature of SRH services, UPP focuses on women and girls of reproductive age who represent about 25% of the population, and are considered among the most vulnerable by the health sector in Syria HNO 2019. SRH services are open to all women and girls, men and boys who are in need. Through EmONC, the essential neonatal services will also contribute to save the lives of newborns. Health care providers will be direct beneficiaries for most trainings, including basic and comprehensive EmONC trainings for midwives gradually conducted in UPP supported facilities across the 4 governorates of North East Syria. The action is tailored to maximize delivery of SRH services to the beneficiaries living in the targeted governorates, who have limited resources, experience barriers to access health facilities due to security and cultural issues, and experience protection risks identified in the needs assessment. The action will also focus on responding to newly displaced people and returnees. Therefore, the
importance of flexible response and coordination of humanitarian actors on the ground to reach the population is essential.

4 - Needs

Syria is facing a multi-faceted humanitarian crisis, resulting from almost 8 years of conflict on multiple fronts that have taken a huge toll on the population. The consequent waves of displaced populations affect the entire country. The draft of the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020 reports 12 million people in need in Syria, of which 43% with severity 4. Moreover, 64% (13.2 M) of the population lives below minimum standards for healthcare. Health personnel shortages are particularly acute in areas affected by recent hostilities and displacement. In rural and remote areas, accessibility and cost of transport also impede access to services for a vast portion of the population, and especially persons with disability. In Ar Raqqah and in Deir Ez Zor Governorates around 70% and 60% of the population respectively is in need of health services. In Aleppo and Hassake Governorates still more than half of health facilities are destroyed and the majority in place are not-functioning or running at very low capacity. Among the people in need, 1.43 million children under age 5;3.22 million women of reproductive age (15-49) including 483,000 women who are expected to become. The need of basic services, such as health care, WASH, shelter, and protection services deeply affects the most vulnerable groups, particularly pregnant women and children. In a post-war context, as it happens in Hassakeh, Ar Raqqah, Deir Ez Zor and Aleppo governorates, health risk increases within most vulnerable groups. Inadequate nutrition of newborns and children under five and thespreading of infectious diseases due to poor sanitation and scant water sources are seen by health observers as factors that put at severe risk pregnant women and infants. Thissituation affects more intensively villages in remote areas and communities of internallydisplaced population. This worrisome situation needs to be confronted urgently with acomprehensive program of high quality reproductive health services delivery.

5 - Implementation of activities

In 2018, UPP activated 3 BEmONC mobile delivery units and 3 CEmONC facilities, training more than 100 local staff, now specialized in maternal and young child care. The hospitals supported are now able to attend pregnant women with CEmONC capacity, some of the very few public health facilities equipped to offer such services. At present, these facilities attend an average 6,000 consultation per month, allowing an average of more than 1000 deliveries attended by skilled health personnel per month. In 2019, UPP maintained these facilities, extending its support to the National Hospital of Hassakeh. In 2020, UPP will continue its support focusing mainly on secondary health care in hospitals, but still supporting existing RH mobile services in rural areas and camps. Activities will include the strengthening of the newly supported CEmONC facility, with the activation of a fully equipped Neonatal Intensive Care Unit. To shape the strategy with sustainable approach and to boost quality of care 100 health staff will be gradually trained by UPP medical advisors on antenatal, postnatal and perinatal care. To tackle the issue of anemia among women of reproductive age and pregnant and lactating mothers, ToTs on nutrition will be carried out with FPs in each facility. Staff will be capacitated to conduct screening for anaemic women during ANC visits (lab). Provision of iron and Folic Acid Supplements to all Anaemic PLWs and ensure that CHWs are able to stress the importance of including healthy nutritive iron-containing food in the diet. Monitoring: 1. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:- Further escalation between SDF and GoT, extended to territories beyond the agreement reached, and increased waves of population displacement (medium/high risk);- Attacks to health facilities in case of escalation of conflict between GoT and GoS (high risk)- ISIS resurgence and regained control over areas (low risk) or infiltrates carrying out attacks (medium risk);- GoS extends militarily and administratively further into existing SDF territory either through force or negotiation (high risk);- Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies for IP; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (high risk);- Cross-border INGOs evacuate expat staff and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (high risk);Mitigation1. Flexible response: Implementing partner can rapidly shift its support to facilities most in need or use mobile response to follow population needs. Preparedness plan, including contingency stock of pharma, is in place. 2. Contingency stock: particular attention will be given to emergency pharma storage. To ensure the safety of a contingency stock, a second warehouse was opened in Nov. ’19 in Hassakeh 3. Office/Facilities Sanitation: zero-paper policy in supported facilities. Every one or two weeks UPP staff brings to KRI official and sensitive documentation. 4. Acceptance: most of the staff recruited comes from the communities where they work. This ensures a high degree of ownership. KRC, through its own Charter of Principles, adheres to ICRC CoC, despite not officially being member. A joint commission of UPP together with KRC board is established to ensure a common decision making process that implies a strong commitment to humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence. KRC fully abides to ICRC CoC, addressing the needs of the vulnerable groups without distinction of ethnicity, religion, gender.
