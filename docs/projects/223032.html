Project Summary

The project aims to assist refugees in acquiring transferable and portable skills relevant to the labour market needs in Myanmar and the camp context. This is with the objective of supporting refugees' sustainable return and integration in Myanmar when conditions permit, as well as enabling refugees to maintain hope for the future and lead dignified and productive lives in the camps, including through engagement in multi-sectoral camp-based activities, minor livelihood activities and community works, through the utilisation of acquired competencies. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees, pending durable solutions. UNHCR will also enhance the engagement of refugee women in productive activities inside the camps, with participants producing female hygiene materials for distribution in the camps. This will be achieved by strengthening final product quality, including improving production processes and upgrading current production centres. The project will develop occupation-based skills with globally recognised competency standards (level 1) for 4,000 refugees aged 18-35 years that include all gender groups and persons with disabilities (will be assessed during enrolment by using Washington Group Short Set Questions). The project will implement 7 competency standards-based training for refugees inside the camps. The project will generate income/self-employment by creating a small enterprise as a comprehensive outcome of market need assessment for the skills-training graduates, covering 3,000 graduates who completed the training. The production of female hygiene materials and jute bags for distribution in the camps will also be facilitated through production activities, which will engage around 900 semi-skilled/skilled volunteers on a payment basis. In 2025, UNHCR will continue implementing Accelerated Adult Literacy for Rohingya adolescents and adults aged 18-35 in the Rohingya refugee camps. Under the AAL component, UNHCR will reach 10,000 refugees aged between 18 and 35 across the camps. The course will cover basic literacy, numeracy, and functional literacy. In 2025, UNHCR will continue an International English Language Testing System (IELTS) training. Administered by the British Council, co-owned and franchised by IDP Global, and assessed and validated by Cambridge, the IELTS is a globally recognized standardized test designed to assess the English language proficiency of non-native English speakers. The training will target 150 refugee graduates who intend to apply for the complementary pathways.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Skills Needs Assessment in 2022 highlights that 6.07% of Rohingya youth had participated in vocational training before they arrived in Bangladesh. Around 23.72% of refugee youths received non-standardised skills training inside the camps since arriving in Bangladesh, while 30.9% of Rohingya youth had received education while living in the camps. ISNA 2024 highlights significant positive change in formal vocational skills training; 14.6% of households reported access to livelihood skills training, and among the training receivers, 47.5% reported they received vocational skills training. In 2022, the figure was only 6.07%. The need analysis also provides insights on accessing non-formal technical training, which is 51.5%. This distribution indicates a nearly equal preference for non-formal technical and competency-based vocational training among the respondents. The need analysis suggests that while there is a significant uptake of technical training programs, there is also a substantial interest in vocational training that focuses on developing specific competencies. Various reasons for not attending training include the lack of nearby facilities (45.9%), lack of interest (18.9%), and long waiting times (16.2%). Women often face additional challenges, such as care responsibilities, which 10.2% of respondents mentioned as a reason for not attending training. Gender-related constraints specifically affected 2.3% of women, further restricting their access. Other notable reasons included the training facility being too far away (7.2%), lack of awareness about training opportunities (6.7%), and limited education opportunities (5.0%). Additionally, 1.1% mentioned insufficient literacy levels as a barrier. These findings highlight the diverse challenges that must be addressed to improve access to livelihood skills training. Post-training, 36.5% of respondents engaged in income-generating activities, while 63.5% did not, indicating that many face barriers in translating skills into income opportunities. This indicates that while some of the trained individuals could apply their new skills to generate income, a majority faced barriers that prevented them from doing so. These barriers could include lacking resources, market access, or additional support to translate skills into viable income opportunities. These findings highlight the need for inclusive training programs and comprehensive support systems that specifically address the barriers women face to ensure that training leads to tangible economic benefits for them.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project’s primary focus is to address the protection needs of the refugees, ensure their minimum rights, and make them self-reliant. In doing so, the project will emphasise refugee participation throughout the project cycle. The project will ensure equal participation of diverse groups, including persons with disabilities and vulnerable individuals, and adhere to the "Do No Harm" principle.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will primarily target refugees aged between 18 and 35, given that paid volunteering opportunities are available after successfully completing the training. The project is obliged to maintain the children's rights endorsed by international laws. Among the targeted participants, at least 30% will comprise women, while 2% will comprise persons with disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will capitalise on the established CFM mechanism of UNHCR and use the CFM Desks and Protection Hotline to capture the community's voices. The project will adopt a community-based approach which incorporates the principles of age, gender and diversity mainstreaming in its work with refugees to ensure accountability to affected populations; in other words, through consultation and participation throughout the programme cycle, refugee communities in each camp should be engaged meaningfully and substantively in all aspects of programmes that affect them and play a leading role in catalysing change and enhancing community resilience through the implementation of vocational skills training, which they can utilise when potentially repatriating or resettling to other countries.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNCHR will ensure that all its partners are abiding by the policies related to PSEA and deliver training to the staff and volunteers who are directly engaged with the target population to reduce the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) at all levels of implementation.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The Project will reduce the ecological footprint through economic empowerment, especially for women to cope with environmental and climatic shocks. The project will integrate the guiding principles of UNHCR’s Environmental Guidelines in alignment with the national legal framework (The Forest Act, 1927; Wildlife (Conservation and Security) Act, 2012) and Energy and Environment Working Group guidelines. All environmental and climate action activities will be implemented under the umbrella of UNHCR within or beyond the project scope using a comprehensive complementary approach. The focus will be to embed the principle of ecological restoration and reduce the risk of climatic hazards. The skills training will also integrate green skills concepts for cutting carbon emissions by running the centres with solar power and improving human skills in innovative green skilling.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project will have several skills training and livelihood production centres across the camps. These facilities will serve as a community space during evacuation and emergency response. All the centres mentioned will be equipped with emergency supplies and basic minimum resources for emergency response.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project's primary target group is those aged between 18 and 35. More than 50% of the population reached will be between 18 and 24. The skills programme will target the primary 18-24 age group and will also apply to Accelerated Adult Literacy (AAL).

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The goal of the project is to make the target population self-reliant and offset the dependency on humanitarian aid. The skills programme will help them to prepare for taking economic opportunities and productive engagement like paid volunteerism. It will also widen their windows to take complementary pathways. It also has Livelihood diversification activities that will create paid volunteer engagement provisions by running Livelihood Production centres.
