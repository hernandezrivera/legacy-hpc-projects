Project Summary

The purpose of the project to contribute to improving the health and socioeconomic well-being of East and South Darfur communities by preventing & reducing WASH related diseases and supporting IDPs, returnees, extremely vulnerable women and children as well as PWSNs, through provision of ES/NFI. Timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance is essential to reduce mortality and morbidity and sufferings of crisis-affected people.
To achieve above purpose Alight will do:
-Installation of Emergency water supply & sanitation services
-Increasing access to safe water and sanitation services, by supporting operation and maintenance of the water supply systems, rehabilitating existing nonfunctioning water schemes, providing emergency water supply & sanitation services, ensuring water quality by treatment and WQ testing
- Reduced prevalence of WASH-related diseases by increasing awareness on good hygiene and safe sanitation & environmental practices, promoting participation and communicating risk and prevention methods of COVID-19.
-ES/NFIs provided that reduce exposure to physical hazard, ensure safety and honor dignity of people.
Integrated WASH & ES/NFI and/or standalone ES/NFI services will be provided to affected communities, targets will be selected through conventional identification & verification processes. Activities that contribute to the overall national and state level humanitarian assistance initiatives are carefully selected. Alight's 14 years WASH & 4 years ES/NFI responses experience in targeted areas will be used to support 72,000 people (about 12,000HHs) in WASH & 24,000 individuals (about 4,000 HH) by ES/NFIs. Adding ES/NFI on ongoing WASH, health, nutrition, and protection projects will help affected and vulnerable people to get holistic humanitarian assistance.
Alight as part of state level humanitarian response agency, we will respond to the immediate need of life-saving services (both for WASH and emergency shelter/ non-food items). The response will be provided if existing humanitarian situations worsen or new humanitarian conditions emerge such as displacement of people due to conflict or extreme hazard (drought/flood) affect vulnerable people based.
As per our experience, the project will be carried out jointly with local authorities, WES, COR, HAC, UNHCR and other key partners in the targeted localities. During implementation, Alight will work with the communities and local administrations preparing hotspot areas and identify vulnerable communities, and raise awareness of the communities on hazards. Alight has contact of potential suppliers and transporters for WASH, ES/NFI materials, which will help to respond immediately. However, incase NFI gaps occur in the market, Alight will utilize the Core Pipeline to respond to emergencies.
During implementation Alight will use a standardized NFI package and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) developed by the ES/NFI cluster. Wherever necessary Alight will combine WASH and protection responses with the ES/NFI distribution. We will use environmental friendly emergency and culturally appropriate shelter materials. Most of the procurements will be locally sourced, which will help to activate local economies. Alight also provide on-the-job training of building techniques of shelters that will be useful for developing self-reliance and resilience.
Distributions of ES/NFIs to newly displaced populations in the targeted localities will take place after proper verifications, and will be coordinated with Alight, local governments and UNHCR. To improve accountability, transparency in programming, and adherence to “Do no harm principles,” Alight will strengthen the capacity of staff (men, women, and youth) and local national NGOs through training in the complete package of PSET for emergency response. Additionally, Alight will implement the project by considering protection and accountability to the community and to the donor.

1 - Needs

According to the WASH sector HNO 2021, Sudan's protracted crisis continues to create water, hygiene, and sanitation needs for vulnerable people. Key drivers are deteriorating economy, conflict, food insecurity, malnutrition, floods, and disease, including COVID-19. About three-quarters of the population have access to basic drinking water, 39 per cent have access to limited sanitation services, and 13 per cent have access to basic hand washing facilities. The people in need of WASH is 9.1 million people across Sudan, including 1.05 million refugees. Sudan is facing a number of protracted crises that is exacerbating the existing humanitarian needs and impacting the lives of all people in Sudan, including the deteriorating economy, conflict, civil unrest, floods, disease outbreaks, and the COVID-19 pandemic. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, there are 13.4 M people in need, out of which 9.2 M are vulnerable residents, 2.5 M are IDPs, 1.07 Refugees, and 0.17 M are Returnees. In South Darfur state an estimated 1.91 M people are in need for humanitarian assistance out of which, 1.06 are vulnerable residents, 0.79 M are IDPs, 0.08 M are refugees and 0.03 M are returnees. According to the PIN data for HNO and HRP 2022, identified that in South and East Darfur that, 26 localities out of 30 locality has IDPs and with 5 to 4 severity ranking in dire needs to humanitarian assistance. while 670,736 are people in needs to humanitarian assistance, out of them 171627 people in needs to water supply, 427,144 people needs sanitation services, 670732 people needs hygiene promotion intervention. Based on the HNO 2021 IDPs, refugees, and vulnerable residents will need continued WASH support. In some locations, people only access around 7- 8 liters per person per day, especially during the dry season. The rapidly deteriorating economic situation further affected access to WASH services, particularly drinking water. In the proposed areas of intervention based on the recent assessment conducted by ALIGHT WASH sector in April 2021, out of the 52 motorized schemes assessed, 15 were malfunction. The assessment also looked into the performance of water points/tap stands in IDP camps and host communities. 48 water points were assessed, and 16 of the water points were found to be not functioning. In Katayla, Dimso, Adilla, Yassin Abujabra, and El-Salam localities the water coverage estimated at 5-10 L/p/d. Furthermore, referring to gaps identified above, women, girls, the elderly, and people with disabilities were mentioned as the most vulnerable and affected groups to the shortage of WASH services. Women and girls who have been considered as main water collectors have to wait more than 45 minutes to collect water and walk an of average 1-5 Km to water distribution points. Although no obvious abuse cases reported due to distance location to water sources and sanitation facilities, still the program will put an effort into providing appropriate services to the communities and will improve access in terms of distance and waiting time through rehabilitation of water sources.In 2021 flooding affected several villages in South Darfur state, Kalma IDP camps and host communities lost houses, livestock, farmland, infrastructure, and services. As a result, the need for shelter and NFIs remained significant, especially for those living in IDP camps and in low-land areas. Drainage structures are not yet maintained posing a threat to property loss & displacement. Since 2020, serious inter-communal clashes have been occurring in East & South Darfur states, these issues has not yet resolved and has resulted in the displacement of a significant number of people in South Darfur in 2021, These threats are still on ground. The latest International Organization for Migration Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM DTM) Emergency Event Tracking (EET) estimates a total number of 6473 individuals.

2 - Activities/Outputs

ALIGHT will continue provide lifesaving activities for conflict affected and vulnerable people in IDPs camps and rural host community of target locations. ALIGHT will rehabilitate nonfunctional water yards in high density population areas of 5 water stations (3 in SD and 2 in ED) that served more than 45000 people suffering from lack of adequate water supply in these rural community, additionally will continue providing the operation and maintenance services for 24 water solar water systems in IDPs camps (18 in SD and 6 in ED), these water system need solar pump replacement, water network rehabilitation and expansion, which Alight plan to do it in this plan. Also 20 broken hand pump will be repaired and we will maintain 15 nonfunctioning water points. We will conduct regular water quality monitoring in all target water sources. Also, we planned to improve water storage condition in 5 sites. Our WASH services target to serving more than 72,000 people in East and South Darfur. In sanitation ALIGHT planning to construct 1200 HH emergency shared latrines and rehabilitate 800HH emergency latrines in IDPs camps and construction of two new institutional VIP latrine and support operation and maintenance of existing instructional latrine in the PHCC. Also the environmental sanitation and vector control activities that include drainage maintenance to convoy the runoff outside of people living in IDP camp, environment cleaning campaigns and improvement of solid waste collection and safe disposal at final dedicated sites. We will continue community sensitization on environmental sanitation and improve hygiene practices and change the unhygienic behavior. Distribution of WASH NFIs including jerrycan and Soap, as well as improve menstrual hygiene management for women and girls of reproductive age will be conducted. Where new IDPs emerge dignity kits for women and adolescent girls will be distributed. Also the project will implement COVID19 IPC awareness raising through community health workers(CHWs) in camp and rural areas. We will print and distribute MOH developed IEC materials and intensify the health session campaign in public places and by house to house visits. The Shelter and NFIs intervention aimed at support 27,000 people (about 4,500HHs) affected by conflict and disaster, including new IDPs and protracted IDPs in need of support and extremely vulnerable host communities. Displaced people will receive emergency shelter and non-food items (NFIs) timely. Regular information collection and humanitarian condition assessment will be conducted with other agencies. Any intervention will be decided by state level sector coordination, HAC & UN agencies and identification and verifications mechanisms will be applied using conventional methods. ALIGHT will use multipronged approach; direct distribution of Non-food items for the targeted community, provide direct emergency shelter materials or provide cash assistance for shelter construction materials. The method of response will be determined by assessment of communities abilities and market conditions and sector coordination decisions. In all actions Alight will participate the affected and host community, relevant local government authorities, UN agencies. Our MEAL department will conduct post distribution monitoring and transparency of intervention will be ensured by routinely collecting feedback and complaints and correcting based on findings.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ALIGHT will use environmental friendly approaches such as use of solar energy instead of fossil fuels to power its health facilities, water supply systems, and conventional and locally available materials for construction and maintenance activities. ALIGHT uses local culturally appropriate materials for shelter construction such as grass, bamboo sticks and these are easily renewable and has less environmental impact. Alight also include sensitization activities on environmental degradation and climate change impact. Communities will get awareness through trained community volunteers and encouraged to plant more indigenous trees. At start of rainy season to reduce the impact of malaria, Alight will conduct dedicated malaria prevention campaigns and environmental interventions such as drainage improvement and collection and safe disposal of solid wastes and filling potholes and draining pools. These activities will be conducted by community participation.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The WASH and ES/NFI response activities are designed based on safe programming criteria. For example latrine construction designs will take into consideration protection needs of different segments of users based on Gender, Age and Diversity (GAD) as it affects the Safety, Access and Dignity (SAD) of users. Designs will ensure privacy, dignity and security of women, girls and children, and give easy access for pregnant women, the elderly and people with disability. WASH facilities will be constructed at safe places with the consultation of the community. Latrines will be near to homes so that they can be accessed safely at all times of day and night. Institutional latrines will be gender segregated and ensure privacy and dignity, will have features such as lockable doors and solar lights where possible. The siting of latrines will take into consideration water sources to avoid possible contamination. To ensure safe access, new water points site will be selected in consultation with community. As much as possible water points will be placed at public places (open space) and be close to the living places which will reduce distance as well as prevent incidence of GBV. Furthermore we construct conventional emergency shelter with lockable door and ventilation and equipped with light during night that provide good protection in use for people with special need (PSN) in IDPs camp and for others with extreme vulnerability.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In regard to AAP; Alight will employ the AGD policy that will be implemented through community based, participatory approaches that are already well established in the IDPs camp and host community areas . The Project will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies and plans all through the project cycle. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. This is aimed at ensuring meaningful participation, enhanced communication and transparency, improved feedback and response. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boys, girls, women and men in all project activities with relative 60% female and 40% male. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, as the project activities are tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities and basing on relevant parameters appropriate in reverence to heightened protection risks of the persons of concern. The Project will ensure at all persons of concern have equal and non- discriminatory access to protection, assistance and solutions, this will build on their resilience and recovery. The project will engage much better with boys, girls, men and women while in their safe spaces like the women & girls centers, children and adolescents friendly spaces, youth centers. Activities like awareness raising, dialogues, will be conducted in those spaces and in the communities, provision of specialized MHPSS, comprehensive case management processes, specific needs and vulnerabilities identification and culturally sensitive coffee corners and Art Galleries'. Alight will maintain effective feedback systems ( including comments, suggestions and complaints) using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non- sensitive feedback e.g. use of complaints boxes, toll free hot lines. In regard to PSEA; All Alight staffs have committed to and signed PSEA policy, Child Protection policy, Code of Conduct as well as the engaged community based networks Alight is a member of National and State levels PSEA task force and regularly coordinates with other partners in relevant PSEA forums. Under the Project, we will conduct Safeguarding and SEA risk assessments to mitigate any potential or identified risks of SEA. Alight has contributed to Inter Agency Action Plan for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and will ensure Zero Tolerance - No Excuse to SEA in all its operations and project. Jointly with the PSEA task force, we will develop relevant SEA IEC materials and distribute them in communities, raise awareness on SEA, strengthen community based complaints mechanisms, enhance reporting and ensure appropriate measures are undertaken for investigations.
