Project Summary

Essential shelter and safe accommodation: The project will provide access to adequate and safe shelters for vulnerable Lebanese and stranded migrant workers living in Bourj Hammoud through the provision of cash for shelter assistance (to cover rental costs for 3 months) to be determined along with transfer rates and currency in coordination with and under guidance from the inter-agency Emergency Cash Task Force which sits under the Shelter Sector. The project will also support and complement on-going rapid needs assessments (Multi-sectorial Needs Assessment_ MSNA) in coordination through the Assessment and Analysis Group, as well as provide information on the impact of the Beirut Port Blast on migrant worker populations through [DTM].The project will engage local CSOs providing direct assistance to migrant workers, embassies and consulates, municipalities, and national response mechanisms.
Risk Communication and Community Engagement: through awareness raising campaigns on COVID-19, Safe Access to Isolation Centres, and Referrals Mechanisms using the IOM/ILO MW Coordination Group network. Printing of IEC materials which will be translated and localized into the languages of migrant worker populations (Bengali, Ki-swahili, Tagalog, Sinhala/Tamil), engaging embassies and consulates, and raising awareness related to COVID-19, how to seek care, and a brief directory on available services and assistance. IOM will ensure that relevant Information, Education and Communication materials will be translated into the most needed languages.

1 - Needs

On 4 August 2020, a massive explosion at the Port of Beirut (PoB) destroyed much of the port and severely damaged the dense surrounding residential and commercial areas. The explosion caused the deaths of 178 individuals with more than 6,000 injured. It is estimated that 300,000 people living in the affected area have been impacted, whilst the devastating damage and structural effects of the blast have been felt up to 10km away. Based on IOM’s Migrant Worker Vulnerability Assessment carried out in Beirut from May- July 2020, IOM estimates that 24,600 migrant workers have been directly affected by the blast. Prior to the explosion, migrant workers in Beirut were an already marginalised population group. Between May and July 2020, IOM initiated a Migrant Worker Vulnerability Assessment to understand the impact of the economic crisis and COVID-19 on the socio-economic and physical needs of migrant workers in Lebanon. Between May and July 2020 IOM undertook a Migrant Workers Vulnerability assessment. Needs reported by migrants to embassies and partners working in the affected areas following the blast remain similar prioritising food, shelter, cash for rent, medical aid, mental health, psychosocial support, and voluntary returns. An initial rapid assessment indicates that migrant workers, mainly from Ethiopia, Bangladesh, Kenya and the Philippines represent 8 per cent or 24,600 of the 300,000 people affected in Greater Beirut. IOM has confirmed that there are over 150 injured migrant workers and 15 deaths since the explosion on 4 August. A large portion of those living within the damage radius of the explosion (including Achrafiyeh, Bourj Hammoud and Ras El Nabaa) have lost their homes or suffered significant damage to their shelters. IOMs proposed response is in line with the priorities of the shelter sector Lebanese response crisis plan (LCRP).

2 - Activities

1. Data collection including mobility tracking, thematic assessments on migrant workers needs in disaster affected areas by IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix. DTM will be used to identify the most vulnerable according to defined vulnerability criteria and to assist with post distribution monitoring of cash for rent. 2. Provide conditional cash for rent to 1100 disaster affected and displaced individuals including 200 Lebanese HHs and 300 migrant worker HHs. IOM will determine HHs for targeting against inter-agency criteria including UN-HABITAT’s Operational Zone Scoring which includes socio-economic mapping; the World Bank’s Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment (RDNA) with preference given to HHs who fall into the severe and partially damaged categories. IOM is a member of the temporary cash for rent technical working group and the emergency cash assistance technical working. IOM will use existing tools and resources and ensure that assessments, analysis, and response are aligned with sector requirements. 3. Referrals for shelter support, transitional housing and related support to partner organizations- CSOs/NGOs will be made under the Migrant Worker Coordination Group. Additional services including healthcare, MHPSS, legal counselling, GBV and other forms of support may also be available from MWCG constituent agencies.
