Project Summary

DRC proposes to undertake a life-saving and sustainable assistance to Disaster Affected Communities (DACs) through provision of emergency shelter kits (ESK), non-food items (NFIs), transitional shelter and durable shelter solutions. DRC defines DACs as people affected by displacement internally and those that are returning seeking for reintegration. Therefore, DRC targets new IDPs, the IDPs in protracted situation, returnees and host communities. DRC understands that the primary displacements in Somalia is as a result of natural disasters and man-made conflict; but also, secondary displacements are now a common reality due to military offensive. These displacements are the key driver of humanitarian shelter needs in Somalia. The IDPs are currently residing in urban and Peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. They remain in need of Shelter and NFIs support for protection from harsh climatic conditions, overall improvements to their living conditions and improvement to their chances to access livelihoods and durable solutions. On the other hand, DRC targets Host Communities - to ensure peaceful coexistence between the local population and target groups and also because many of them are equally vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. For IDPs in protracted situation, DRC offers transitional shelter assistance. This also includes negotiating for extension of land lease/tenure to allow for a longer stay until full re-integration is sought. Returnees are also targeted with durable housing solutions in which case DRC proposes permanent shelter. For permanent shelter, DRC will ensure an integrated approach where other needs - WASH, shelter, livelihood, protection and education are included in the settlement planning. To enhance livelihoods, DRC will offer life-skills to DACs to enable them enhance their socioeconomic well-being. Therefore, DRC will target newly displaced populations with emergency shelter and NFIs. For IDPs in protracted situation and returnees, DRC will seek transitional shelter and durable solutions respectively. Where cash based intervention or voucher is sought, DRC will undertake registration based on AGD and vulnerability criteria through an electronic system. Then DRC will undertake a verification exercise whereby beneficiaries will be screened to see if they meet set criteria. The list of beneficiaries selected shall be shared with a mobile money transfer company operated by telephone service providers. The cash shall be released in two tranches of 70% and 30% respectively for both NFIs and ESKs. This shall minimize risk of cash being used for other activities not related with shelter but also to make sure the cash goes to the right beneficiaries.

Project Activities

Based on the needs, DRC will address both life-saving, transitional shelter and durable solution needs facing DACs. DRC targets a total of 69,300 beneficiaries as follows (63900 people with NFIs which is 10650 households; 36000 people with ESKs which is 6000 households; 3600 people with TS which is 600 households and 1800 people with durable shelter which is 300 households; 5400 people with community infrastructure which is 900 households of both TS and PS activities). Beneficiaries under TS and PS shall not considered for NFIs but only for community infrastructure. For the case of newly displaced IDPs, DRC will assist with emergency shelter kits (ESKs) non-food items (NFI). The standard emergency NFI package will include plastic sheeting, blankets, jerry cans, sleeping mats, kitchen set, mosquito net, solar lamp and Jerry cans. Where possible, DRC will give the NFIs through cash transfers or vouchers so that beneficiaries can decide based on their needs. DRC will also assist with ESK. The standard ESK contains: Local available stick for vertical post (Philipian Tree); Local available sticks for trusss, door and windows (Philipian Tree) or White wood (8cm x 4cm size each); Local available sticks (Philipian Tree) for horizontal frames & roofing or White wood (5cm x 2.5cm size each); Local available sticks (Philipian Tree) at the base of roof truss or White wood (10cm x 2.5cm size each); Nails No. 5 (5 inch); Nails No. 6 (6 inch); Drum door with frame; Hinges for door; Local plastic sheet (size: 4m x 7m); and outer wall clothing Buul layer. This provides the most basic shelter which can offer protection from extreme weather. The ESK helps the displaced household to put up by a 4m x 4m shelter structure within the first few days of displacement. For IDPs in protracted situation, DRC seeks to improve their living conditions by assisting them with transitional shelter and NFIs by cash provision. For returnees and IDPs with secure land tenure, DRC seeks to improve their housing and living conditions by providing permanent/durable shelters that includes - a two-rooms house with veranda and toilet. Where possible, and through referrals, DRC will do a full settlement plan to include improved access to basic services such as roads, clean water, electricity, school, market, health clinic, police post and other community infrastructure. As this provides an avenue for durable solutions - DRC seeks to engage in promoting public private partnership (PPP) in provision of services to ensure sustainability. This will be a continuation of DRC's ongoing engagement in Dollow and Baidoa where DRC has started partnerships for water service provision. For instance (where there is integrated response), for water provision, DRC will ensure that a water utility company is established that will provide the water services and a reasonable tariff set to enable returnees, host community and institutions afford the services. This will also be the case for solar generation, health, education and other services. DRC will develop a selection criteria based on AGD as well as vulnerability to ensure that assistance goes to the ones who deserve it most. Female-headed households, families with many children and the aged (65+ year-olds) will be given priority. DRC will also ensure that, persons living with disabilities (PLDs) or families with such persons are also targeted. DRC will train government on DACs rights ensuring that they assume their role as duty bearers. To ensure that IDPs and returnees enjoy their rights, DRC will advocate for housing, land and property (HLP) rights.

External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services - DRC will ensure that they take services close to the beneficiaries by carrying out distributions near their settlements. Where cash/voucher are used, this is not seen as a risk and DRC proposes to use CASH or vouchers where possible and only in-kind distribution when unavoidable. 3d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources - DRC criteria specific to targeting IDPs, Refugee Returnees and vulnerable Host Community, a Do No Harm approach that is seen to promote peaceful co-existence of Displacement-Affected Communities (DACs). DRC will ensure the selection/targeting criteria is shared with camp/IDP management committees. The committees must be selected based on AGD criteria to ensure all affected groups are involved in the intervention. 4a. Protection mainstreaming: Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas - DRC will ensure that land occupied by IDPs is allocated by government or that the landowners agree to accomodate them for a reasonable time to remedy any forced evictions. For protracted IDPs seeking durable solutions, DRC will ensure that they have land allocated by government or they have land ownership documents like title deeds that guarantees them reasoable lease of 5+years before any shelter is constructed.4b. Protection mainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution - DRC will ensure that there are IDP committees who organize people in order to reduce chances of scrambling during distribution. Please provide further explanation

DRC is cognizant of the fact that, there are four (4) categories of people in need of either humanitarian assistance or durable solutions depending on their situation. In the context of Somalia, DRC targets Returnees and IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons) as well as host communities. The targeting of latter group (Host Communities) is done on two fronts - one is to ensure peaceful coexistence between the local population and target groups; and two is that many of the host communities are as vulnerable as the displaced population. DRC is aware that most of the areas where IDPs settle are very temporal and will therefore not warrant durable solutions programming. However, in specific locations DRC and other humanitarian partners have advocated for permanent shelter and the respective regional governments or through federal government have reached an agreement to get land for resettlement. DRC is also keen to partner with other shelter agencies like UNHCR to improve the housing and living conditions of returnees by creating permanent shelters where land has been provided. For such permanent housing solutions, DRC will seeks to provide a two-unit housing with verandas and toilets as well improved access to basic services such as roads, clean water, electricity, school, market, health clinic, and police post where possible. As this provides an avenue for durable solutions - DRC seeks to engage in promoting public private partnership (PPP) in provision of services to ensure sustainability. For instance, for water provision, DRC will ensure that a water utility company is established that will provide the water services and a reasonable tariff set to enable returnees, host community and institutions afford the services. This will also be the case for solar generation, health, education and other services. DRC will also look into Housing, Land and Property (HLP) Rights. In Somalia there are three categories HLP disputes - landowner disputes; landlord-tenant disputes; and IDP internal disputes. DRC will get into advocacy with government and through clan leadership, ensure that IDPs are able to access the HLP rights without any form of discrimination.
