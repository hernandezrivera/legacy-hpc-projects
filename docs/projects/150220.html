Project Summary

Médicos del Mundo Spain (MdM-S) will contribute through this project to the achievement of Strategic Objective No. 2 of the Protection Cluster Strategy, i.e. (2) “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence”. In particular, this project will tackle protection for the mitigation the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence, risk of forcible transfer in the West Bank, impact of blockade in Gaza, settler violence, etc. through an increased access to quality enhanced public Mental Health services and MHPSS outreach services at the community level. The project will specifically target population, and in particular children, affected by the conflict in the West Bank and Gaza. This will be done incorporating the IASC MHPSS core principles: human rights and equity, participation, do no harm, integrated support to systems and building on available resources and capacities; and the enhancement of a coordinated and effective response to critical incidents.
MdM intends to pursue the following activities between 2019 and 2020:
•Provision of Child Protection response, with special emphasis on safe spaces for responsive interventions in emergencies, including case management and PSS for children anf familias, as well as Early Stimulation Programs.
•Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial services (MHPSS) for adults and youth, with special emphasis on gender equality.
•Capacity building of public Mental Health Services to address children and adolescents needs following appropriate guidelines and standards.
•Advocacy initiatives among the international community that aim to address protection concerns; prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHRL and IHL by all duty bearers.
• Contribute to coordination and participation in the Protection Cluster, CPMHPSS subcluster and progressively increasing contributions to GBV subcluster.

Needs

MdM-S has focused its needs analysis on the most vulnerable people living under occupation in Area C (Jericho and Ramallah Governorates) and E1 Area, and under blockade in the Gaza Strip.In the last year, several assessments have been conducted in the framework of current actions, identifying humanitarian needs and response gaps, in coordination with MoH, MoSD, MoEHE representatives, first responders (school counselors, community mental health professionals, primary healthcare professionals, and CBO mobile clinics health staff), as well as focal group discussions with target population. In addition, this project is aligned with 2018 HNO needs identification and the Main humanitarian trends and analysis 2019, and 2019 HRP Protection Cluster Strategy, and takes into consideration the increase in settler violence since the beginning of 2018 and the deterioration of Gaza’s humanitarian situation in 2018. Based on these assessments, the main humanitarian needs identified are: *Since cutting funding for UNRWA’s emergency programs (including mobile health clinics and MH assistance for Bedouin communities), over 220,000 West Bank Palestinians lost access to permanent clinics. They will face critical challenges in accessing primary health and MHPSS services, according to OCHA, access to healthcare could be hampered by expensive or no transport. *Need to increase in Gaza and Area C/E1 access to MHPSS services (especially outreach), primary health care and GbV survivors tailored services due of a shortage in service provision. *Need to increase the quality of MHPSS services and its integration into general health services and connection with the community services. *In the oPt the successive conflicts and the blockade/occupation created high levels of psychosocial distress and eroded infrastructures, negatively impacting children and their families, therefore making it essential to include Early Stimulation Programmes (ESP) in emergencies to foster the wellbeing of children exposed to conflict related violence. Moreover, the Mental Health (MH) services are insufficiently child-friendly. *Conflict related violence, risk of forcible transfer, settler violence and the impact of blockade and restrictions of movement makes it necessary to strengthen first responders self protection and safety, according to the Inter Agency Steering Committee (IASC) MHPSS Guidelines, including the provision of tools to mainstream gender perspective. * Need to tackle duty bearers according to International Humanitarian Law, through advocacy with International community, to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL. *Need to tackle Responsibility bearers and first responders to sensitize about the different aspects of the Right to health, and in particular, non discrimination, stigma (MH/GBV), and confidentiality.

Activities

•• IMPROVED ACCESS OF MHPSS SERVICES • 1. Improved coordination, referral and complementarity of MHPSS and social services in Gaza, through mapping of public and private resources for children and youth in mental health• 2. Provision of MHPSS Services for people in need at group and individual level, including family counselling with gender perspective, in the West Bank• 3. Implementation of early stimulation programmes (ESP) for children (0 – 6 years) in the West Bank, with attention to children with special needs.• 4. Provision of psychoeducation sessions (i.e. parenting skills, communication skills, etc.) for families (women and men) of children attending ESP sessions, ensuring gender inequalities are identified and addressed adequately.• 5. Improvement of integration of healthcare and MHPSS services in the West Bank and Gaza, including identification, first clinical and psychosocial response and referral. •• IMPROVED QUALITY OF SERVICES • 1. Capacity building to develop professional skills to improve detection, first management and referral of MH cases, and self care for professionals of MHPSS, PHC, Education, CBOs and others. The trainings will be provided with a gender approach both in Gaza and the West Bank, and will follow appropriate international guidelines and standards• 2. Design and adapt a specific facility suitable for MH first response for children and adolescents in the Gaza Strip, affected by conflict related violence.• 3 . Mid-term and final need assessments to update the information on psychological impact of conflict related violence in the area of intervention, both in Gaza and in the West Bank, and to verify changes of needs/objectives, and assess the security situation. Methodology will include gender balanced and gender specific focal group discussions, with community approach and both responsibility bearers and right holders participation.•• ADVOCACY AND SENSITIZATION • 1. Design and implementation of a Communication and Advocacy Campaign amongst the international community and relevant duty and responsibility bearers to advocate for the reduction of the conflict related violence suffered by the population in the Gaza Strip, the West Bank and East Jerusalem. • 2. Design and implementation of a Communication and Advocacy Campaign among the local community and responsibility bearers against stigma and discrimination of mental health affected people and their families. • 3. Field Visit to the areas of intervention with relevant duty and responsibility bearer to witness IHL violations. •• CROSS-CUTTING AND COORDINATION• MdM will participate in the Clusters system and other coordination mechanisms to ensure coordination, alignment, efficiency, and to avoid overlapping, as well as to contribute an participate in Advocacy task forces, and in particular in the Protection Cluster, Child Protection and MHPSS subcluster, and GVB subclusters, as well as the Health Cluster.MdM is also directly active in advocacy campaigns in Spain and is part of international platforms, including the international network of MdM associations. In the oPt MdM Spain, France and Swiss are present and coordinated in advocacy iniciatives. MdM will compile a lessons learned document with quali/quantitative data on impact of intervention, with attention to needs of women and girls, and gender dynamics.

The overall objective of the project is to improve protection mechanisms, both responsive and remedial, for most vulnerable groups in the West Bank and Gaza through increased access and quality of mental health and psychosocial support services with comprehensive and gender sensitive approach.The action is targeting the most vulnerable communities in Area C and E1 affected by or under the threat of forced displacement, and Deir al Balah in the Gaza strip.All targeted Bedouin communities fall within the 46 Bedouin Communities at Risk of Forcible Transfer in the Central West Bank.••Specific indicators: • 3,775 children affected by conflict related violence benefiting from structured psychosocial services and child protection interventions • 100 children affected by conflict related violence benefiting from specialised individual case management • 625 vulnerable men and women affected by conflict related violence benefiting from structured psychosocial services • 1 duty bearer’s field visit carried out to the area of intervention to witness IHL violations, during the project implementation (12- month). • 2 articles published on the media and social networks, during the project implementation (12- month).• 378 children aged 0 – 6 years in the West Bank (200 boys and 178 girls) have accessed to an Early Stimulation Program, during the project implementation (12- month). • 60 child protection workers (women and men) trained on guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support • 130 families in the West Bank have participated in psychosocial ‑ education sessions (90% women and 10% men) to strengthen Parent-Child Relationship during the project implementation (12- month). • 8 focus group and need assessments (4 in the West Bank and 4 in Middle Area and Gaza Governorates) with at least 6 people each (approx. 40% women and 60% men) carried out during the project implementation (12- month). • 400 victims of conflict related violence (approx. 60% women and 40 % men in the West Bank) benefit from Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) during the project implementation (12- month) • 1 report about public and private organizations in the area of intervention in the Gaza Strip that work with children and youth in the field of mental health; will be digitally distributed among the different child and youth mental health stakeholders in Gaza. • 3 group working sessions developed to identify, needs and challenges; and lessons learned of MHSPSS interventions with children and youth in emergencies carried out during the project implementation (12- month) with a participation of approximately 50% men and 50% women • 60 service providers (primary care, specialized care, hospitals, NGOs and educational centres, at least 50% women) both in Gaza and the West Bank have been trained to provide an adequate response to the situations caused by conflict related violence during the project implementation (12- month). • 100% of the interventions documented and reported to the relevant UN Agencies and Cluster system, during the project implementation (12- month). •• The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing with Goals 3 and 5.
