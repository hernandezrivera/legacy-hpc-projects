Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to improve nutrition security among the children under five, pregnant and lactating women and people living with disability or HIV in the face of drought. The intervention will address life-threatening acute malnutrition then ensure resilience among the communities affected by the drought in line with the National Nutrition Strategy (NNS) and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Zimbabwe is facing a multitude of humanitarian challenges as a result of climate and economic shocks. The impact of 2018/2019 drought combined with continuing macroeconomic challenges have resulted in high-level of food insecurity. Of this population, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis points to 3.58 million people – or 38 per cent of the rural population – projected to be in need of urgent humanitarian action between the period October to December 2019. The project will target centres of 2019 IPC classification four and five which are Chimanimani, Tsholotsho, Umguza, Mbire, Bulilima, Gokwe North, Zvishavane and Zvishavane Mine. All districts will work in addressing the Living Standards and Well-being of their communities. Each district has health and nutrition structures that go down to community level however the functionality of these has been hampered by inadequate resources and incapacitation of staff to sustain-ably drive nutrition-relevant actions. In the face of drought and difficult economic conditions, the burden has increased. The Ministry of Health and Child Care (MOHCC) through its structures will overall be in charge of implementation and monitoring of the intervention at the district level and community level. ADRA will complement MOHCC efforts through technical support and resources for enhanced capacity building, effective coordination, and mentoring, leveraging on its global and local technical expertise on health, food and nutrition. ADRA physical presence already in the target districts will work to strengthen local structures and integrate the management of malnutrition to existing programs on health, education and livelihoods. The project’s initial phase will seek to ensure that the severity of acute malnutrition is minimized through early identification and treatment. At a later stage, activities to reduce likelihood and morbidity to acute malnutrition will be carried through linking the vulnerable to behavioural change programs and other livelihood strategies for sustainability and ownership.
