Project Summary

There is ongoing armed conflict in Ukraine, and daily violations of the ceasefire agreement. Examples of these localized flare-ups include the fighting in Avdiivka in late January 2017 and the shelling of Novoluhanskoe in December 2017. These ceasefire violations have a serious impact on civilians’ lives and property. They damage or destroy housing and infrastructure, necessitating an emergency response in terms of shelter and basic needs, especially for most vulnerable and persons with specific needs. To provide displaced and conflict-affected persons with specific needs in a timely manner UNHCR will preposition emergency NFI kits to be distributed to families at risk of displacement or newly displaced, as part of emergency response. In GCA, the emergency response is generally led by local authorities, and UNHCR’s contribution will complement their efforts. Some 1,250 kits composed of basic and domestic items will be prepositioned closed to the line of contact for households living in or returning to individual or multi-story buildings damaged during the conflict and/or newly displaced or evacuated UNHCR’s emergency response will be coordinated closely with local authorities and complementary to their efforts to respond. Winterization activities are not in UNHCR’s prioritized plan, but could be implemented if there are critical needs and pending availability of donor funds as a part of the comprehensive plan. Should funds be available, UNHCR would provide cash assistance (in GCA) or coal (in NGCA) to households who live along the contact line and have no access to the supply of fuel for heating

Needs

Along the line of contact, there is extensive damage to homes. Between 2014-2017, humanitarian actors have conducted almost 35,000 shelter interventions, mainly light and medium repairs, and families themselves have repaired many homes. In GCA, shelter cluster partners continue to conduct light and medium repairs to eligible houses; there is a gap in terms of covering the need for heavy repair and reconstruction. The shelter cluster is updating information in the damage database about outstanding humanitarian shelter needs in GCA. A preliminary estimate suggests that there are at least 7000 households in need of humanitarian shelter interventions. Within GCA, an estimated 1,500 houses were newly damaged in 2017 due to the ongoing hostilities, and it is likely that at least a similar number was damaged in NGCA. UNHCR and partners must have a capacity to provide emergency shelter kits and repairs. If hostilities were to subside, more people would return to areas of GCA along the line of contact. Some returning families have damaged homes that had not been eligible for repair because the owner was absent, so returns could lead to a new demand for shelter interventions. In NGCA, 22,000-25,000 houses were damaged by the conflict and in need of light, medium or heavy repair. Because of access restrictions, the shelter cluster does not have accurate information about how many of these houses have already been repaired and how many have outstanding needs. It also does not have reliable estimates of the number of new damages from shelling. There are few actors implementing humanitarian shelter projects in NGCA. Since the outstanding needs in NGCA are considerably higher, and the capacity of humanitarian actors is lower, UNHCR will plan for a strong shelter component in NGCA in 2019. Since humanitarian needs in NGCA are rising due to economic isolation and few actors able to assist, UNHCR may have to fill critical gaps in basic needs in 2019.

Activities & outcomes

1. Shelter and infrastructure established, improved and maintained Outcome: Long term/ permanent shelter provided and sustainedUNHCR will provide sustainable long term shelter assistance to families whose houses/ apartments were damaged during the conflict. Priority will be given to those living close to the line of contact and NGCA parts of Luhansk and Donetsk oblast. In addition, those who are returning to the place of their previous residence and whose residencies are damaged will be also supported through shelter programme. Those whose houses are damaged and live in the high risk security areas will receive assistance in emergency shelter kit that they could use by themselves.Outcome: Sectoral cash grants or vouchers provided2. Population has sufficient basic and domestic items. Outcome: Core relief items providedBased on the vulnerability assessment, and taking into account age and gender diversity approach, UNHCR will provide necessary non food items to the families affected by conflict. The damage or destruction of housing and infrastructure caused by the conflict, necessitates an emergency response in terms of shelter and basic needs, especially for most vulnerable and persons with specific needs. NFI kits will also be prepositioned to be distributed to families at risk of displacement, newly displaced or returnees, as a part of emergency response. In GCA, the emergency response is generally led by local authorities, and UNHCR’s contribution will complement their efforts. Winterization activities are not in UNHCR’s prioritized plan, but could be implemented if there are critical needs and pending availability of donor funds as a part of the comprehensive plan. Should funds be available, UNHCR would provide cash assistance (in GCA) or coal (in NGCA) to households who live along the contact line and have no access to the supply of fuel for heating. 3. Logistics and supply optimized to serve operational needs Output: Warehousing provided, repaired and maintained During the year UNHCR will maintain sufficient purchasing, warehousing and transportation capacities in order to support unhindered implementation of shelter project as well as distribution of core relief items. UNHCR’s operation in Ukraine requires complex supply arrangements involving large-scale and well-coordinated procurement, storage, delivery and distributions. Its humanitarian supply chain includes suppliers and logistics services providers, the government, de facto authorities, donors and beneficiaries.
