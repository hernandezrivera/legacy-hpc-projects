Project Summary

Seven years of protracted armed conflict in South Sudan coupled with inadequate access to medical care, diseases, food insecurity, inappropriate hygiene and sanitation, and harmful cultural practices and beliefs are exacerbating malnutrition rates across the country and in particular among vulnerable groups. Specifically, women, children, elders and people affected by disability continue to bear the burden of malnutrition whereby the general low capacity to meet their basic needs is being difficult to address in a context where malnutrition is complex and linked to many different triggers.
The proposed intervention focuses on Tonj South and Tonj East counties, Marial Lou Hospital in Tonj North and Mother Theresa Hospital in Twic. The total population in the targeted areas is estimated at 781,055 people. The main purpose of the initiative is to ensure safe and equitable access to critical integrated basic services to enable 31,376 prioritized target people to meet their basic needs through improved coverage and quality of nutrition services and the capacity building of local institution in their coordination.
In line with the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020, the project will contribute to the Nutrition Cluster Objective 1, this will be ensured by addressing at least 70% of SAM cases in girls and boys 0-50 months and at least 75% of MAM cases in girls and boys aged 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women, older people and other vulnerable groups. CCM's experience in the targeted area as well as the close cooperation with the CHD will facilitate the integration of nutrition services and linkages with other sectors. Further, the proposed initiative will also contribute to the nutrition cluster objective 2, for at least 60% PLW, 90% Vit A coverage for children under five, BSFP for 30% U5 and 40% PLW. Finally, the Nutrition Cluster objective 3 will be supported through the already existing health and nutrition system and the promotion of a multi sector coordination network involving local institutions, international NGOs and local community groups in information sharing, context need analysis and synergic response plan that prevent overlapping in services provision and give the highest value to the actions of the involved networks members. CCM will target 31,376 women and children in need in Tonj East, Tonj South, Tonj North and Twic counties. This includes 8,652 children under five years in need of treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM). In addition, 13,345 children with vitamin A and 155,705 children with deworming tablet will be targeted. The interventions will includes counselling 37,321 pregnant and caretakers of children 0-23 months. Out of the total beneficiaries, 39% are woman accessing both preventing and curative care and health and nutrition promotion activities. It is widely recognized that women can influence child survival through appropriate caring practices, such as breastfeeding, adequate complementary feeding, hygiene and health seeking behaviours, including early identification of common diseases. 66% of the target beneficiaries are children U5 suffering from the consequences of household food insecurity, inadequate care and feeding practices, unhealthy household environment and lack of health services more than other vulnerable people. Community involvement will ensure better targeting of beneficiaries in need and the identification of the “best” nutrition practices, consistent with local culture, capabilities and the physical environment. Nutrition services will be equally accessible for people with disabilities and minorities, even if the project does not specifically target such categories. Finally, CCM will complement and strengthen his program through a holistic approach, which focuses on expanding and integrating current nutrition services with the Early Childhood Development (ECD) component, in order to create a supportive environment and caring practices for children.

1 - Needs

For the last two years, Warrap state has experienced an increasingly worsening nutritional status. Estimates from the 2018 DHIS indicate an annual curative consultation utilization rate of 0.99, with the highest rates registered in Tonj South (1.57). Malaria, diarrhoea, respiratory infections and malnutrition remain the most common reasons of OPD-U5 consultation at the facility level. According to national figures, the whole Warrap state is classified at critical nutrition level. Nearly half of the population in the area is facing food consumption gaps or only meets basic food needs through unsustainable coping mechanisms; specifically, 58.2% of its total population is estimated to be in crisis and emergency levels (IPC, August 2019). A recent SMART Survey, shown Acute Malnutrition result by domain of a GAM rate of 9.3% and a SAM rate 2.0%, MAM rate of 7.3% in TE (2018); GAM rate of 24.1% and SAM rate 5.5%, MAM rate of 18.7% in Twic (2018); GAM rate of 13.1% and a SAM rate 3.9%, MAM rate of 10.8% TS (2017).Among the key drivers of high levels of malnutrition across the targeted counties, there are: inter-communal conflict; displacement and poor access to health services; disease outbreak (particularly Cholera and Measles, Extremely poor diet (in terms of both quality and quantity); Low coverage of sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices; Further, the population is vulnerable to a range of risks and shocks, which derive from factors over which they have little or no control, such as volatile food prices, human conflict, social status/discrimination, lack of political and social power.Moreover, among the key drivers of high levels of malnutrition across Warrap state high prevalence of acute malnutrition is attributed mainly to poor food consumption, poor maternal and Infant and Young Children Feeding (IYCF) practices, morbidity, poor water, sanitation and hygiene facilities and practices, and constrained health and nutrition service delivery. Particularly, child-feeding practices such as untimely introduction of complementary foods or poor quality and inadequate quantity of these foods contribute substantially to the high levels of malnutrition. Additionally, low exclusive breastfeeding practices are a key contributing factor to the nutrition situation in these areas. Low access to PHC services for mothers and children, mostly due to movement constraints, poverty and limited awareness on health and nutrition risks, contribute to the extremely alarming levels of child mortality in the targeted counties. Severe shortages of health workers and functional facilities, socio-economic barriers, inadequate mechanisms to reach pastoralist communities and displaced populations, and the under financing of the health system make universal access to health services very difficult. Most health infrastructure are dilapidated, essential medical and surgical equipment outdated or lacking. This limited service provision capacity disproportionately affects women and girls. All the above factors clearly indicate an underlying nutritional vulnerability of the population in the targeted area.In line with the 2020 Nutrition Cluster objectives, the proposed intervention aims at decreasing the suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations in Warrap state, by addressing above identified needs through:- Increased access and quality of medical care through a good coverage of the health facilities and their services acceptability by the communities with a specific focus on nutrition interventions; - Integrated responses and components (i.e. WASH/Health/protection) to maximize impact on the target beneficiaries (such as hygiene promotion along with the provision of WASH kits, appropriate feeding practices for adequately processing, cooking and conservation of food, nutrition sites equipped with child play material, etc) and to address key drivers of malnutrition.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Gender and Age considerations will be also included in the programming. The proposed interventions will be built on gender mainstreaming as a priority approach to guide the overall implementation of the program at different levels. The intention is to ensure that County Health Plans are responsive to gender and social exclusion barriers, and that related issues are promptly addressed. The project will further reinforce the commitment of the South Sudan government to give preference to those in the society who are most vulnerable with limited or no source of income living in remote and poverty-stricken communities. In line with the Health Cluster approach, vulnerable groups will be the priority target of most planned activities. Priority to gender and inclusion across the program will be given at different levels: community – HF and county level aiming at the same time to increase awareness on gender equality and GBV related issues and improve the quality of the related services offered, including training of health facility staff on GBV related issues and clinical management of GBV cases. During the project implementation, the mental health component will be developed aimed at strengthening the health system creating mental health awareness at the community level and reinforcing the capacity of the health workers. The same approach will be adopted as far as disability is concerned, focusing on awareness-raising and demand creation. CCM will work with the aim to promote disabilities identification at community and HF level, in order to improve the current service package delivered.In order to mitigate the risk of population movements seeking for access to health services, the established system of outreaches activities allows to reach the people in need in the most disadvantage and remote areas of the Counties targeted.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to the affected population will be streamlined into CCM strategies and project/monitoring activities in order to enable improved participation, information provision, feedback and complaints handling. CCM will adopt systems to monitor, collect feedbacks and evaluate its action, ensuring a transparent and fair management and promoting better quality of services provided. Specifically, CCM has developed patients and community feedback mechanism that foresees the organization of ‘exit interviews’, through carried out in sample HFs on a monthly basis. Literate patients receive the questionnaire to fill in and return to the HF staff, while illiterate beneficiaries are supported by the staff in their compilation. Data collected in the sample facilities is then passed on to M&E Officers and analysed into a report. CCM will engage the affected populations in all processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices by routinely providing accessible and timely information to the affected population about their rights and entitlements. Communities will be strongly and effectively involved throughout the project timeline, promoting their active engagement in the development of the CHDs. MtMSGs, HHPs and Bomas members will be involved regularly for defaulters tracing, promotion of correct nutrition practices and development of community responses to nutrition needs. This mechanism is aimed at accessing beneficiary’s satisfaction and to put in place strategies to address identified specific needs of the most vulnerable community members. CCM is producing info graphics summarizing the Whistleblowing policy and reporting mechanism; they will be posted in the HFs together with a ‘complaining box’. Moreover, CCM developed a SOP to assure that disclosures are collected from the boxes respecting confidentiality policies and that they are properly referred as per mechanism.
