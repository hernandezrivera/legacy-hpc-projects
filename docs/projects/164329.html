Project Summary

The Darna Al Aman project aims to support the safe return of the civilian population of Raqqa through the provision of integrated services of protection and primary healthcare. The protection component includes the enhancement of the space for women and girls already situated in Raqqa Al Hilal Maternity Hospital. Moreover, it includes the setting up of a child-friendly space in the same location, thus allowing better access to integrated case management services. A key element of the action will be the integrated training plan for local medical and non-medical staff through modules on the protection of minors, protection of women, gender-based violence and mental health, integrated with health services already provided.
Although, the project also plans to strengthen the decentralized health system of the area, providing basic health services in the surrounding area of the city of Raqqa, through the opening of two Primary Health Posts. In addition to that, the project will continue supporting a Mobile Delivery Unit, together with a mobile medical team, which will strengthen the connection between the different health centres of Raqqa. Finally, the project plans to develop integrated awareness campaigns on health and protection issues with a community-based approach.

4 - Needs

The needs identified in the Health Sector are in line with the Whole of Syria plan. As reported in the face of the return of 220,000 people to Raqqa, public health facilities remain insufficient despite the improvements underway thanks also to the joint efforts of INGOs including UPP. Needs remain those of informing the population about health risks and working with prevention campaigns. Referral services to other health facilities are also minimal, apart from the ambulance service and mobile units of the Kurdish Red Crescent and the Health Committee. According to the WoS consolidated Erams (HNO, 2019), in Raqqa subdistrict the population/PHC facilities ratio is still too low. Moreover, as recognized by the Health Sector in the HNO 2019, children under age 5 and women of reproductive age (15-49) are among the most vulnerable groups. The maternal-child sector, on which UPP already operates extensively in the city of Raqqa, needs further efforts to connect the suburbs of the town with civilian health structures through a network of mobile units. In addition to that, the activation of peripheral structures remains a priority, according to consultation undertaken with local authorities and stakeholders, in the aim to fill the gap in the areas where no health actors are providing a stable intervention, and no referral pathways are established. Each sectoral response must always take into consideration that in the area there is a very limited number of gynaecologists and that the local health staff, made up of doctors and paramedics is in a context of urgent and priority clinical emergencies, and with an extremely vulnerable civilian population. Therefore, each intervention must provide an adequate level of training for local staff and sustainable solutions in the medium to long term, despite the emergencies and health problems that emerge daily.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activity is designed accordingly with the WHO guidelines for Northern Syria, called the Essential Care Package. The purpose of the action is to strengthen the decentralised system and the provision of primary health services in the rural area of Raqqa through two Primary Health Posts in coordination with Al Hilal maternity hospital, and a Primary Health Center in Raqqa urban area. Within the LEARN Consortium of which UPP is part, the INGO PIN has proposed to carry out the rehabilitation of the two proposed structures of Kobash and Hamrit Ghanam, located in the north-west and east area of the city. Each PHP will be operating by the presence of a nurse and midwife, an assistant nurse, a person in charge of a security guard and two CHWs. Through a mobile team and the MDU, the presence of paediatricians and gynaecologists will be guaranteed for two days a week in each centre. Besides, the mobile units will also serve to maximise access to ultrasound and other diagnostic tools. The centres will be continuously connected to the Raqqa urban area where clinical cases can be referred when necessary. The PHPs will mainly serve to decentralise activities such as Primary health consultations, Pre and postpartum visits, Child visit, Emergency stabilization, Community Health, Follow up for discharged patients. UPP M&E, will constantly monitor the activities, drafting monthly report of outpatient consultations and morbidity reports, supported by the Team Leaders of the clinics. UPP Protection Coordinator will ensure protection principles (Do No harm, Meaningful Access, Accountability and Participation) are mainstreamed through project activities. Health staff will be trained in GBV and CP core principles and cases identification in cooperation with the WSS and CFS in Al Hilal Maternity Hospital. UPP will coordinate his activities through the HWG and the NES forum to avoid duplication, provide a harmonised response and conduct referrals, including malnutrition cases.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

According to the Syria HNO ‘19 report, 501,503 people are in need of protection services in Raqqa governorate. According to the same report, gender-based violence (GBV) and in particular sexual harassment, early and forced marriage, domestic violence and IPV continue to negatively impact the lives of women and girls in Syria. The lack of psychosocial support services and specialized GBV life-saving services continues to represent the major gap in Syria and in particular in Raqqa. The analysis of the Whole of Syria GBV Area of Responsibility (2019, UNFPA and Gender AoR) confirms that the country faces "new realities in which gender dynamics have been significantly altered" and that GBV has a disproportionate impact on women and girls. Also according to Syria HNO 2019, 222,310 children need assistance in the Raqqa Governorate. These are at risk of violence, alcohol and drug abuse, school drop out, child labour or military recruitment, with potentially negative long-term effects. In Raqqa, out-of-school children and unaccompanied and separated children are more vulnerable to violence and the most common protection risks for them are early/forced marriage and child labour. Furthermore, there are limited child-friendly spaces and alternative care facilities. The conflict then caused a high number of child and female-headed households as well as the deterioration of social support networks.Finally, data collected by UPP in Raqqa between Dec. ‘18 and Apr. ‘19 confirmed low awareness of health and protection risks among returnees. The risks identified are linked both to external factors such as land mines, ERW and IEDs, food and water contamination, high crime rate and generalized violence, GBV and domestic violence, low health awareness during pregnancy. UPP will implement a CFM in the facilities to ensure a regular collection of feedback and complaints, ensuring a community-driven implementation and providing timely closure of feedback loop.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

During 2018 and 2019, security alarms continued in the city of Raqqa, and if the security conditions worsened, activities could be suspended. UPP has established adequate security procedures for its offices in Iraq and Syria in coordination with the INSO operator safety information system. Furthermore, information exchange systems have been activated with other NGOs and the UN agency and a specific hibernation and evacuation protocol. Also, for operations in both Syria and Iraq, UPP refers to the internally adapted manual "Security Risk Management NGO Approach".The territory of the NES falls into the red level and is therefore not accessible to Italian operators. All the operators were then trained with ad hoc courses for security in crisis contexts and are equipped with satellite phones and GPS. Risk assessments are carried out regularly for each mission to conducted out in risk areas. UPP security department is in charge of monitoring activities and ensures that information are regularly shared with the competent authorities and the International Coalition. All staff receive regular training courses on UXO/Mine.In the event of deterioration of security in Raqqa, expatriate project staff will not have access to the town. Management will take place from UPP's Amuda and Dohuk offices. In normal conditions, the project staff in will be able to move to Raqqa only during the day. Exceptional evacuation plans have been studied in case of military escalation conducted by any of the multiple actors present on the field. The IP on his own Charter of Principles, adheres to ICRC CoC, despite not officially being member. A joint commission of UPP together with the IP board is established to ensure a common decision-making process with strong commitment to humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence. The IP fully abides by ICRC CoC, addressing the needs of the vulnerable groups without distinction of ethnicity, religion, gender.
