Project Summary

The 2024 humanitarian intervention in the field of health and nutrition led by IMC is part of the extension of the 2023 project supported by the UNHCR. It aims to provide health assistance to the refugees living in the Minawao camp and the host communities residing in the villages surrounding the camp. The refugees in these camps are entirely dependent on humanitarian aid, as their ability to generate income and become self-sufficient remains very low. Therefore, the main objective of the project is to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality of Nigerian refugees in Minawao camp and local communities living in the villages surrounding the camp. This is based on three specific objectives:
- The improvement of the health status of Nigerian refugees in Minawao camp and the local communities surrounding the camp;
- To provide optimal access to reproductive health and HIV services to Nigerian refugees in Minawao camp and the local population living in the surrounding area of the camp;
- To improve the nutritional well-being of refugee children and the local population living in Minawao."
As in 2023, refugees in Minawao still depend on humanitarian aid to access basic services including health care, nutrition, education and water, hygiene and sanitation services. Considering this total dependency of Nigerian refugees in Minawao camp on humanitarian assistance which is related to their inability to generate income and become self-reliant, the main objective of the 2024 interventions will be the provision of free access to comprehensive health and nutrition services to refugees in the Minawao Camp and strengthen the capacity of the local health care system while collaborating with partners in charge of livelihood activities in the camp to ensure refugees become autonomous by 2026 when they will be required to be fully responsible for these free services.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

As in 2022, refugees in Minawao still depend on humanitarian aid to access basic services including health care, nutrition, education and water, hygiene and sanitation services. Considering this total dependency of Nigerian refugees in Minawao camp on humanitarian assistance which is related to their inability to generate income and become self-reliant, the main objective of the 2023 interventions will be the provision of free access to comprehensive health and nutrition services to refugees in the Minawao Camp and strengthen the capacity of the local health care system while collaborating with partners in charge of livelihood activities in the camp to ensure refugees become autonomous by 2026 when they will be required to be fully responsible for these free services.According to International Medical Corps’ field report on curative consultations in 2022, a total of 105,870 new curative consultations (95,283 refugees) were realised compared to 65,000 as planned by UNHCR which represents 1.67 new consultations/refugee/year (UNHCR standard 1.0 to 4.0 new visits / refugee / year). Children less than 5 represented 36.9% of the total new consultations. The main pathologies encountered were respiratory tract infections (38.3%), malaria (20.1%), diarrhoeal infections (5.3%), intestinal worms (4.6%) and NCDs (4.5%). During this period, a crude mortality rate (CMR) of 0.23/1000inhabitants/month (norm: <0.20/1000inhabitants/month) and an under five mortality rate (U5MR) of 0.46/1000inhabitants/month (norm: <0.50/1000 inhabitants/month). All these rates are at the limit of the normal values. IMC UK invested in capacity building activities, coaching and the organization of death reviews to increase the possibilities of preventing those deaths based on lessons learnt. Only 17.4% of these deaths occurred in the Minawao health posts 1 and 2, while 57.1% occurred in referral hospitals and 25.5% in the community. Concerning the immunization of children less than 5 with the measles vaccine, an achievement of 105% was made with respect to the annual target fixed. A total of 1,046 referrals were made for specialized care. It is important to note that the targets for consultations and vaccination were fixed by UNHCR for the year 2022 taking into account a potential voluntary repatriation in the month of February which did not take place but rather an influx of new arrivals was observed. Some major challenges were encountered for general consultation and curative services. These included: insufficient drugs for the management of chronic diseases and mental health conditions, the lack of qualified staff for the mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, frequent stock outs of essential drugs and some laboratory reagents and rapid diagnostic tests, the high cost for the management of patients in secondary care institutions and the absence of some important medical equipment which if present could facilitate medical and laboratory interventions. As for the sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and HIV/AIDS services, much was achieved in the past year as a result of multiple efforts by IMC UK through its supported district volunteer staff and the network of community workers. IMC UK recorded 99.4% of all deliveries in the camp realised by skilled health personnel, admitted 411 new users for modern contraceptives and provided comprehensive antenatal care (ANC) services to 5,451 pregnant women including 2285 for ANC1 and 1587 for ANC2). A nutrition SMART survey was conducted by the Ministry of Public Health with technical and financial support from UNICEF and UNHCR between the 16 of February and 14 March 2021 in 4 vulnerable regions of the country (Far North, North, Adamawa, and East) at the same time as the SENS survey in the Nigerian refugee camp of Minawao and among Central African refugees living in camps and in the host communities.
