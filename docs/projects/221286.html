Project Summary


The implementation will use MSNA results to identify the households and communities that are most in need using community engagement in decision-making and participation to build resilience and ensure sustainability. This will consider mapping stakeholders (community leaders-traditional and religious, volunteers, persons with disabilities, women, youths, etc.) to identify key actors that will enhance a smooth implementation during the implementation phase. Communities will be briefed on the project deliverables and expectations from the community and PSEA and AAP sessions. Finally, the communities will be given the room to select and prioritize needs and activities, how they will be carried out, joint monitoring and frequency. Project staff, and volunteer/community capacities will be built on humanitarian principles, access negotiation, security protocols, AAP, PSEA, Gender, Age, Disability, and Protection mainstreaming and jointly build complaints and feedback mechanisms.
Volunteers will be trained in all components (Health/WASH/Nutrition/Child/GBV Protection). This comprehensive approach will provide a holistic approach, save time and cost, and promote community engagement and sustainability as volunteers will be recruited from their communities.
Protection and GBV activities
1. Provide inclusive holistic care to GBV survivors and other forms of abuse.
2. Build capacities of GBV service providers in case management including MHPSS.
3. Strengthen GBV prevention activities at community-level
4. Strengthen and enhance the process of issuance of civil documentation
5. Conduct protection monitoring and sensitization on peacebuilding, and peaceful coexistence between IDPs, returnees, and host communities.
6. Establish and support community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms
7. Provide MHPSS to children and their caregivers through the creation of child-friendly spaces, training animators to provide MHPSS to children in the spaces and referral for other services, and provision of WASH services in the spaces.
8. Provide comprehensive Case Management to vulnerable children by providing alternative care and/or family reunification to UASC and case management and referral of GBV survivors.
9. Build the capacity of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms
10. Provide support to issue birth certificates to conflict-affected children and sensitize communities on birth registration.
WASH activities
1. Improve access to safe drinking water by construction and/or rehabilitation
2. Construct gender-sensitive latrines
3. Distribute WASH and dignity kits
4. Build the capacity of WASH actors
5. Promote safe hygiene and sanitation practices
6. Conduct emergency WASH needs assessments to inform WASH response.
Nutrition Activities
1. Provide IYCF to caregivers of children 0-23 months, and pregnant women.
2. Support community engagement for SBC at all levels
3. Screen and manage SAM cases using standardized guidelines from the Ministry of Health and WHO at facility and local levels.
4. Strengthening nutritional surveillance mechanisms by carrying out SMART surveys.
Health Activities
1. Provide medical services/mobile clinics, MHPSS, and refer critical cases
2. Support emergency preparedness through training of locals/communities
3. Providing safe pregnancy and delivery services by supporting vulnerable pregnant women for ANC and delivery
4. Provide holistic emergency care to GBV survivors within 72hours

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The situation in the North-West and South-West regions has remained tense as the populations continue to be affected by insecurity and the consequences of continued armed violence, including targeted attacks, abductions, kidnappings, killings, unlawful arrests, and destruction of property, including schools and health centres, and inter-communal clashes. This crisis, which has now entered its eighth year, has led to considerable internal displacements with almost 100% of IDPs displaced due to the crisis and the related violence. As of August 2023, the crisis had displaced almost 670,000 people internally within Cameroon and across the borders to Nigeria. Many displacements are mostly pendular within the North-West and South-West regions as people move back and forth between their homes and their places of refuge. In 57% of cases, it is the entire household which is displaced with a group of people (the elderly and persons with disabilities often times left behind. Some 3.4 million people living in Cameroon need humanitarian assistance and protection because of conflict and violence, climate shocks, and disease outbreaks. Most of the divisions in terms of intersectoral severity are at level 4 and according to the MSNA round 10, most priority needs are access to food, education, drinking water, livelihood, health, shelter and sanitation. This project seeks to address, the Health, Nutrition, WASH and Protection needs: 43% of affected populations have problems accessing humanitarian assistance, 34% have been affected by safety or security restrictions, 19% have been affected a safety security incident or protection incident.- The most severe health care needs is limited access to health care services- The most affected are the male heads of households and single males, the female head of households, adolescent boys and girls, unaccompanied and separated children. Additional violence and abuse have increased, coupled with lack of civil documentation and mental health stressors.- The Nutrition needs: no screening and management of children for malnutrition within communities.- The most severe wash need is drinking water and sanitation. - There is poor accountability to the affected population as over 53% of the population reported that they were not complaints and feedback mechanisms put in place by the humanitarian responders in their communities. This project seeks to address these needs by providing a multisectoral response (Health, WASH, Nutrition and Protection) to boys, girls, boys and girls with disabilities, men, women and men and women with disabilities and the elderly.
