Project Summary

Health Action Africa (HAA) has designed this HRP 2025 proposal to complement the continuity of already ongoing Health in emergency projects, supported by USAID /BHA, RRF/IoM, UNFPA, IMC, GAC, AAHI & AVSI, MSF-Spain and the LRPF. The current projects are multi-year programs implemented in the Counties; Panyikang, Fashoda, Canal Pigi, Pochalla and Tonj North. The Project activities are linked and aligned to the Health Cluster Strategic Objectives; 1, 2 & 3.
Under CLHEA/CO1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations;
The project will provide the following services;
• Support the rehabilitation of static Health facilities; Primary Health Care Centers (PHCCs) and Primary Health Care Units (PHCUs), as well as support the establishment of mobile clinics in the hard to reach areas. These facilities will be provided with essential health supplies including furniture, medical equipment, skilled human resource for health and essential health supplies.
• Conduct Outpatient consultations (OPD) and treatment for common diseases both communicable and non-communicable diseases in all the facilities to manage the malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Respiratory Tract Infections (RTIs), Skin infestations, congenital birth defects other epidemic prone diseases etc.
• Provide PENTA 3 Coverage; Conduct routine vaccination with all the antigens to children. This will be complemented by Vitamin A supplementation to children (6-59) Months and deworming in areas with high worm load. participate in the planning and implementation of the oral cholera vaccines (OCV). Conduct effective case management incase outbreaks are detected for epidemic-prone diseases (Meningitis, Measles, polio and subsequent referral
• Conduct skilled deliveries; provide the Ante- and postnatal care services /check-ups, Vaccination services. HAA will conduct skilled deliveries; provide the Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC) and the safe motherhood services.
• Provide the Clinical management of rape (CMR) services, Expanded Management of sexual a Gender Based violence (SGBV) survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care, emergency contraceptive pills, prevention care for tetanus, Hepatitis B & E, clinical counselling and referrals) and Application of the initial response for HIV/AIDS patients.
• Mental Health services; provide first line management of mental health services, trauma management, psycho-social support therapy and subsequent referrals
CLHEA/CO2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases. HAA will facilitate
• Mobilization and coordination of essential medical supplies /Commodities from core pipe managers for the Health facilities. IEHK Kits from WHO
• Surveillance: HAA will Support the sentinel sites; Early Warning, Alert reporting (EWARS) and Integrated Disease Surveillance Reporting (IDSR).
• Infection Prevention and Control (IP&C). assess the health facilities for IP&C and ensure compliance during project implementation, Risk communication and community engagement.
• Provide community feedback mechanism using the social tools such as the community score cards.
CLHEA/CO3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms
• Facilitate Community consultation during project designs, implementation monitoring and reporting and feedback.
• Conduct effective coordination with the relevant stakeholders; SMoH at state level, CHD at county level, local authorities, health cluster and other humanitarian agencies

1. Needs

In October, the humanitarian situation continued to deteriorate in South Sudan, driven by high food prices, a severe economic crisis, conflict, floods, disease outbreaks and cross-border movements from Sudan; Ocha South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot 2024. In Upper Nile State, protracted conflict and persistent flash floods that lasted for four (4) consecutive years caused breakdown of the social infrastructures and Installations. The recurrent floods in Panyikang County triggered mass displacements. According to Inter Agency Needs Assessment (IRNA) October 23rd, 2024, the floods displaced 3,212 Households (HH) 23,299 Individuals. These populations have settled in Uwachi, Nyiluak, Pakwa, Tonga and Panyiduay highlands with the county. The State Ministry of Health (SMoH) Upper Nile State working in Collaboration with Health Action Africa (HAA) and MSF-Spain through support from WHO confirmed Cholera Outbreak and Malaria Upsurge in the floods affected Population in November 2024. There is a dire need of critical Health care services across Panyikang County1Fashoda County in Upper Nile equally experienced the drastic floods that displaced over ¼ of the Population into the highlands. While major installations, households and farmlands were submerged in the floods water, the extremely limited access to food among the IDPs heightens the risk of severe outcomes with particularly acute consequences on the health and wellbeing of young children2. In Both Panyikang and Fashoda Counties, the major infrastructures such as the Static Health Facilities, schools, Market shades, water Points were devastated. The remaining facilities continue to experience frequent stock out of essential medical supplies. The reports indicated Disease upsurge especially acute watery diarrhea (AWD), Dysentery, Malaria and measles. In Jonglei State, The September 2022 Inter-Agency Assessment report (IRNA) Pochalla County recorded an alarming natural catastrophe caused by flash floods that are currently devastating communities in 75 villages located in the 5 Payams of Pochalla County, Jonglei State. The floods have resulted from the overflow of both the Akobo and Oboth Rivers saturated with water volumes from the Ethiopian highlands and the Sudd Basin. The worst affected areas were the Human settlements and croplands along the riverbanks and downstream areasThe IRNA records indicate that the Population of Pochalla is 75,274 Populations and 31,326 Individuals from 5,221 HouseHolds were displaced into new settlements. The Floods have affected 42 % of the County Population. Five (5) primary health care units (PHCUs) Ajwara, Ajope, Otello, Obudi and Ojangbai. were submerged and are currently dysfunctional. The rain pattern researched by Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) in Oct 2024 indicated that the water logging in Jonglei would remain high due to the saturated Sudd surface and the continuous flow of high volumes of water from the Ethiopian Highlands. Canal Pigi County is one of the worst counties affected by this disaster therefore, the flood-affected population will remain in the new settlement in the highlands, with limited access to social services including Health care. In the remote hard to reach Payams of Kurwai, Deil and Khorfulus, the static health facilities are dysfunctional. The 15th Sept 2024 IRNA report continue to reaffirm the needs of the vulnerable populations identified in previous IRNA reports in Tonj North County, Warrap State. These needs are; Health services, clean water, food and shelter. The Persistent wide spread inter-communal violence in the County has weakened the social fabrics, destroyed social infrastructures that includes; health facilities, water Points, and Shelters/huts.This project is designed to provide the needed critical lifesaving Health Care services to 35,833 Populations sex disaggregated Women 11,825 Men 11108 while Girls 6808 and Boys 6092

2. Project activities

Incompliance to the Health Cluster Strategic Objective CLHEA/CO1; Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations;This project will continue to provide Outpatient consultations (OPD) and treatment for common diseases. There will be OPD consultations for both communicable and non-communicable diseases in all the health facilities including mobile clinics for common diseases; malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Respiratory Tract Infections (RTIs), Skin infestations, congenital birth defects other epidemic prone diseases in the selected counties. Conduct skilled deliveries; prior to assisted deliveries, mothers will be provided with Ante- and postnatal care services /check-ups, Vaccination against Tetanus for pregnant women (if not received). HAA will conduct skilled deliveries in the health care centers; Maternal & newborn health; Clean and safe normal delivery, Essential newborn care: basic newborn resuscitation + warmth + eye, prophylaxis; Basic emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC), manual vacuum aspiration (MVA), post abortion care (PAC).Reproductive Health services; provision of prenatal and postnatal education sessions services, administer contraceptives, safe motherhood and other family planning services. The project will provide routine and Emergence Expanded Program on immunization (EPI) services; PENTA 3 Coverage for under one year; Conduct routine vaccination with all the antigens to children according to the formulary guidelines Ministry of Health (MoH)-South Sudan. This will be complemented by Vitamin A supplementation to children (6-59) Months and deworming in areas with high worm load. HAA teams will participate in the planning and implementation of the oral cholera vaccines (OCV). Conduct effective case management incase outbreaks are detected for epidemic-prone diseases (Meningitis, Measles, polio and subsequent referral where necessary. Clinical management of rape (CMR); HAA will dispense services for Expanded Management of sexual a Gender Based violence (SGBV) survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care, emergency contraceptive pills, prevention care for tetanus, Hepatitis B & E, clinical counselling and referrals) and Application of the initial response for HIV/AIDS patients. Mental Health services; provide first line management of mental health services, trauma management, psycho-social support therapy and subsequent referrals Infection Prevention and Control (IP&C). HAA will assess the health facilities for IP&C and ensure compliance during project implementation. Implement the health promotion and disease promotion services according to the BPH&N modules and above all medical waste management. .Aligned to the CLHEA/CO2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases; The project will provide Surveillance services: HAA will establish sentinel sites ; Early Warning, Alert reporting (EWARS) and Integrated Disease Surveillance Reporting (IDSR) for monitoring and reporting the public health information at both community and facility level. Community level surveillance will also be supportedCLHEA/CO3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanismsThe project will conduct Community consultation during project designs, implementation monitoring and reporting. A local community feedback mechanism/ community score cards will be used for effective feedback communications. Conduct effective coordination with the relevant stakeholders; SMoH at state level, CHD at county level, local authorities, health cluster and other humanitarian agencies
