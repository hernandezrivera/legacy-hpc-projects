Project Summary

The frequent conflicts, aggravating climatic conditions and COVID-19 pandemic are adversely affecting most socio-economic infrastructures and private business enterprises in the North-East BAY States (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe). There are inadequate levels of access to livelihoods, economic opportunities and basic public services (MSNA 2019). As a result, the deprivation of resources and market price skyrocketing are further fuelling inter-communal tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities, prompting them to adopt negative coping mechanisms to meet their basic needs.
In light of the above, COOPI’s goal is to contribute to the economic and communal stabilization through synergic livelihoods activities and peace-building sessions. The project will target 25,630 beneficiaries distributed in 7 LGAs, notably Askira/Uba (Borno State), Bade, Damaturu, Geidam, Jakusko, Potiskum, Yunusari (Yobe State), which have been prioritized based on the levels of damages and service delivery burden with high numbers of returnees and IDPs in host communities. The project will put great emphasis on livelihoods rehabilitation of most vulnerable groups with a special attention to women and children who have been pre-identified through COOPI’s ongoing food assistance and CBT activities funded by other donors. In doing so, COOPI ensures to carry out its activities in close coordination with the ERL sector as well as in complementarity with the ongoing EU-funded ERL program (BRICC) in Yobe State. Furthermore, COOPI will cooperate with national/state authorities, most of which it has already developed MOUs with, providing capacity-building opportunities to government’s institutes, thus deeply contributing to the state system’s strengthening. Equally relevant, the proposed intervention will reflect the recommendations of the Yobe State Socio-Economic Reform Agenda (YOSERA), the Buhari Plan and the Borno State’s 25-year Development Framework.
COOPI aspires to converge the livelihood and economic recovery action with other sectors, including conflict-mitigation, restoration of local government administration and rehabilitation of basic infrastructures, to foster social cohesion and the transition from emergency to long-term development. COOPI is committed to concretely apply the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus approach, forecasting a strong potential in the proposed LGAs where COOPI boasts a consolidated experience since 2015. In alignment with the 2021 ERL Sector's Objectives, COOPI will provide an integrated multi-sectoral intervention, promoting the protection of the conflict-affected population through participatory community engagement, in order to strengthen local resilience to vulnerabilities and to promote social cohesion, safe durable solutions and overall peaceful co-existence among IDPs, returnees and host communities. As a proof, to achieve its goals, COOPI will most rely on working through local NGOs, thus strongly contributing to the paramount localization process. Last but not least, COOPI will mainstream COVID-19 contingency measures in all its interventions to ensure the sustainability of its action.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in the North-East BAY States (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe), most triggered by the non-state armed groups’ insurgency and the counter-insurgency operations by the governmental security forces, has left 7.9 million people (23% women, 57% children) in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 (HRP Nigeria 2020). According to the HRP 2020, an estimated 2.2 million people are in need of early recovery assistance. Of these, 31% are women, 28% are men, 41% are children and 15% are people with disability. More precisely, 0.9 million are returnees and 0.7 million are host communities in the BAY States. The 33rd round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM – August 2020), estimated the number of IDPs and returnees to be 2,118,550 (78% women and children) and 1,714,682 (82% women and children) respectively. The number of displaced people increased by 30,426 individuals (2%) and the number of returnees recorded an increment of 9,115 (1%) as against the last assessment (DTM R32, June 2020). Out of the total number of displaced people, 1,566,011 IDPs are currently in Borno, 213,467 in Adamawa and 142,425 in Yobe, while 708,527 returnees are in Borno, 819,148 in Adamawa and 187,007 in Yobe (DTM R33).In 2019, COOPI conducted a Livelihood Market Assessment in Yobe State which, by assessing all the retail, wholesale, consumable products, agricultural produce, construction, vocational skills and financial related market systems, brought evidence about the alarming price inflation and huge market disruptions. In 2018, COOPI conducted a Rapid Assessment in Northern Yobe to examine the conflict’s impacts on livelihoods, mostly at the household level. Finding from the KIIs and FGDs showed their livelihood base has deteriorated and most vulnerable coping mechanisms (like borrowing, social security) have been negatively affected. Most of the population in Yobe State relies on daily labour and livestock production for subsistence and income, with more than 80% of citizens reportedly engaged in small-scale subsistence farming. Markets status varies from town to town and across LGAs: the main urban towns such as Damaturu, Potiskum, Gashua and Geidam currently have functional markets, and Potiskum Local Government Area (LGA) is home to one of the largest cattle markets in West Africa. The insurgency damaged and destroyed market infrastructures and water pumps, leaving the majority of households in Yobe without access to agricultural storage infrastructures, increasing the risk of postharvest losses. As a result, this context has fuelled competition for scarce natural resources and intercommunity clashes. Further deteriorating the situation, as confirmed by COOPI’s Protection Risk and AAP Assessment Report (Yobe State, November 2020), households’ struggle to meet their basic needs is aggravated by movements restrictions imposed by the government to curve the spread of Covid-19 as well as by more frequent flood disasters, such as the most recent one in Jakusko and other LGAs in Yobe State. Deforestation, due to burning by insurgents and displaced populations’ need for cooking fuel, will have lasting economic and environmental impacts if not effectively managed in the short-term. Crop pests such as Fall Armyworm have also impacted yields in Northeast Nigeria and the supply of fertilizers and pesticides dropped to about half of pre-conflict levels. Input suppliers lack rural outreach to distribute their produces ‘over the last mile’ and into the hands of rural farmers, and sometimes only sell one product, thus forcing farmers to visit multiple input dealers to access all demanded inputs. In light of all the above, the WFP-Rapid Livelihoods Assessment in Damaturu, Bade and Jakusko (April 2018) and results from COOPI’s recent community consultations, FGDs and KIIs bring strong evidence to the urgency of supporting local households through livelihoods and other conditional assistance through communities’ engagement.

2 - Activities

In light of the above, COOPI’s intervention aims to carry out the following activities: Specific Objective 1: To support livelihood stabilization and income recovery.Activity 1.1: Conduction of baseline and end-line; Activity 1.2: Identification of beneficiaries and CBPP verification; Activity 1.3: Community-mobilisation and sensitisation; Activity 1.4: Defining modalities of cash transfers to establish/scale up small scale business enterprises; Activity 1.5: Provision of conditional cash transfers – cash for work to vulnerable households to support generating employment opportunities; Activity 1.6: Provision of in-kind materials for Income-generating activities (IGAs); Activity 1.7: Identification of improved seed vendors in target LGAs; Activity 1.8: Creation/strengthening of farmers/livestock associations, also providing improved agricultural seeds and livestock/poultry; Activity 1.9: Skills-training on climate-resilient agricultural techniques and effective post-harvest storage to farmers (50% female, 20% youth aged 18 -24); Activity 1.10: Market-aligned and skills vocational trainings, including start-up kits and packages, to male and female youth aged 18-24; Activity 1.11: Creation/strengthening and capacity-building of Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs). Specific Objective 2: To restore local community governance services and enhance social cohesion among IDPs, returnees and host communities. Activity 2.1: Consultation meetings and sensitization sessions with line ministry staff; Activity 2.2: Conduction of capacity building trainings for LGA departments staff and communities, including provision of basic service materials (ex. office supplies and utilities); Activity 2.3 Implementation of awareness-rising campaigns on peaceful co-existence and dialogue sessions on community reconciliation and conflict-mitigation in areas of return; Activity 2.4: Establishment of Community Resilience Groups (CRG), with diverse representation, to develop community resilience plans and conflict prevention forums. Specific Objective 3: To increase access to basic social services through rehabilitation of public infrastructures.Activity 3.1: Rehabilitation/Reconstruction of agrifarms, livestock facilities, critical community infrastructures, water access points in markets, schools and health centres through Cash for Work; Activity 3.2: Capacity-building trainings on infrastructures management services to communities’ members and social workers; Activity 3.3: Conduction of awareness-rising campaigns on environment clean-up, hygiene and Covid-19 prevention related issues. COOPI aspires to converge the livelihood and economic recovery action with other sectors, including conflict-mitigation, restoration of local government administration and rehabilitation of basic public infrastructures, to foster social cohesion and the transition from emergency to long-term recovery and development. In alignment with the 2021 ERL Sector's Objectives, COOPI will provide an integrated multi-sectoral intervention, promoting the protection of the conflict-affected population through strong participatory community engagement, in order to strengthen local resilience to vulnerabilities and to promote social cohesion, safe durable solutions and overall peaceful co-existence among IDPs, returnees and host communities.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

In alignment with the HDP Nexus, COOPI is proposing a project with a cross-cutting and intersectoral perspective which synergically combines life-saving humanitarian assistance with long-term development activities and peace-building dialogue sessions at community level. This is based on the premise that to achieve long-lasting peaceful coexistence between IDPs, returnees and host communities, it is paramount to address immediate basic needs through a development lens which does not only ensure early recovery but aims to strengthen beneficiaries' resilience to conflict-driven and climate stressors in the long-run. As a proof, besides promoting the recovery of the main livelihoods and the rehabilitation of basic public infrastructures, COOPI simultaneously aims to provide skills-training for youth and capacity-building sessions for LGAs departments as well as to carry out peace-mitigation and conflict-management dialogue sessions at community level. By doing so, COOPI's goal complimentarily encompasses a strong contribution to social cohesion, economic development and self-reliant livelihoods through a localization approach and state system's strengthening: both of the them ensuring the sustainability of the action and thus an enduring peace consolidation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

COOPI places the community at the heart of its intervention. The community is regarded as the recipientof the action but also as a driver of change to improve living conditions and increase local resilience. For this reason COOPI organizes in a structured way awareness raising campaigns, and promotes local partnerships and capacity building activities to ensure ownership of the program by the community itself. COOPI considers that mobilization and community engagement are essentialto ensure that wherever possible, the population is encouraged to take charge and to be the key element for promoting sustainability. Furthermore, COOPI sets up from the start of the programs a clear strategy for mobilization to enable communities to respond appropriately to their own protection needs, to strengthen their coping mechanisms and to manage positively conflicts, especially in the future. COOPI’s main approach consists of facilitating the re-establishment of community structures and mechanisms, especially governance and representation structures, or organizations of womenand young people so as to reconstruct, to the extent possible, previous community groups.Gender and age will be taken into strong consideration in the selection of the beneficiaries and in the whole implementation and monitoring of the project. Youth will be provided skills-vocational trainings to create/scale up small-scale business enterprises. The Community Resilience Groups will have a strong women representation, fostering a more important role in the decision making among the households. The strong presence of women and youth in the project will indulge COOPI to ensure that the project respects the safety, the dignity and the integrity of the women, men, girls and boys receiving the assistance. Prioritization on queues and easy access to distribution sites, as well as possible home delivery for heavily pregnant women and beneficiaries with disabilities will be ensured. In addition, COOPI will commit not to engage children in the management of the distribution sites. COOPI monitoring team will ensure that the rights of all beneficiaries are fully respected and prevent gender and age oppressive stereotypes.Furthermore, COOPI has a Code of Ethics, Protection Policy and PSEA Policy whose core pillars and mechanisms will be instructed to both beneficiaries and implementing staff/partners through specific in-depth training sessions. Last but not least, Protection Officers and CFM assistants will be available to gather and answer to the questions of the members of the communities. They will fill a complaint book which will record all the complaints. A feedback and complaint desk will also be set up at each distribution site for those people who would like to remain anonymous.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI will put into place systems/mechanisms whereby beneficiaries can channel or express their grievances and seek redress. The system will also create an opportunity for beneficiaries to share feedbacks and recommendations on the implementation of the project. COOPI will have dedicated Complaints Reporting Mechanism (CRM) Officers whose responsibility will be collecting and responding to complaints and feedbacks. Where the nature or the subject of the complaint exceeds his/her jurisdiction, the complaint will be referred to a designated member of COOPI’s Senior Management Team (SMT) to manage the complaint. COOPI will institute two methods for complaints and feedbacks: A) Complaint Desk: COOPI will set up a complaint desk in the community during crucial activities as well as during payment of beneficiaries, to receive complaints and feedbacks from beneficiaries and concerned community members; B) Hotline Telephone contacts of the focal person (CRM Officer) will be made available to community members and posted in each community, to enable community present complaints and/or feedbacks. All reported cases as well as solutions shall be recorded for future learning.
