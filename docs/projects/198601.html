Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in North-east Nigeria is now in its eleventh year with no end in sight. Incessant attacks from non-state armed groups (NSAG), coupled with counter-insurgency operations by the military has led to a never-ending cycle of violence and displacements and creating new emergencies in what is already a prolonged emergency situation. Despite a significant scale-up of humanitarian response since 2016, the crisis continues, and significant gaps exist. In 2019, the humanitarian community adopted a three-year (2019 – 2021) Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRS), a multi-year approach to the humanitarian crisis in recognition of its protracted nature.
Salient Humanitarian Organization prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm, we will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention process as we equally ensure meaningful access by arranging for people’s access to assistance and services. The organization will make sure that the capacity for self-protection is provided through capacity building and assistance to claim rights, including but not exclusive to health, food, shelter, WASH and education
The HRS puts the number of people in need of protection and assistance in the three most affected states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) at 7.1 million - including 1.8 million IDPs – and to 3 million the number of people at risk of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) in the three states. The project will consider the following; door-to-door sensitization on GBV, SGBV and SEAH prevention and reporting within IDP camps and host communities of target locations, community GBV volunteers will be trained on SGBV/GBV core concepts, navigating social norms affecting GBV, GBV referral pathways and prevention of sexual abuse amongst women and girls of reproductive age have increased access to high quality standardized dignity kits and GBV/SGBV services, women and girls will also have improved access to case management and other GBV services.

1 - Needs

According to the Global Protection Cluster, the conflict between the Government of Nigeria (GoN) and NonState Armed Groups (NSAGs) in North-East Nigeria enters its 12th year, the people in the states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (referred to as the BAY states) continue to face widespread insecurity and ongoing military operations, damaged infrastructure, tattered local economies, loss of livelihoods and gross violations of their human rights by all parties to the conflict. The COVID-19 pandemic and ever deteriorating global food security have exacerbated already precarious conditions, leaving a projected 8.4 million people across the BAY states in need of humanitarian assistance and protection in 2022. Among them, 4.2 million people live in Borno state alone with around 1.6 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) out of the 2.2 million persons displaced across the three North-Eastern BAY states. 1 Insecurity and resultant restrictions imposed by the Borno State Government (BSG) on movements make humanitarian operations both dangerous and difficult. Humanitarian actors cannot reach an estimated 1.1 million people in need who reside in inaccessible areas, while humanitarian operations mostly focus on the state capital, Maiduguri, and garrison towns in Local Government Areas (LGAs). As displaced, returning and host communities are all experiencing critical needs across all sectors, the inaccessible areas in Northern Borno, where food security levels are likely to have reached famine levels, are of highest concern, the camp closures in Maiduguri Metropolitan Centre (MMC) and Jere LGAs with enforced relocations and returns to various areas across Borno, food insecurity and restrictions related to humanitarian food distributions, as well as the curtailment of the freedom of movement. 2 - Activities

The mode of operation of SHO will allow monitoring within camps, in rural areas or in urban settings. While always based on the same international standards, we will also focus on certain aspects of any given situation. For example, within camps, our monitoring will examine distribution systems, the physical locations and layout of camps, health facilities, the situation of vulnerable groups, physical protection, including sexual and gender-based violence and abuse, and issues related to psychological assistance and social welfare. SHO will also cover the various phases of the displacement cycle and include persons already displaced, persons at risk of displacement, returnees, and affected host populations. Different monitoring approaches may be required to monitor these different types of populations. No one activity or tool for monitoring is appropriate for all situations; yet, the activity or tool used to gather specific pieces of information will be clearly recorded in each situation. That will make it easier to verify the data collected and to confirm that the tool used successfully captured the protection concerns specific to the situation. Salient Humanitarian Organization will be sure of the fact that staff members are independent and neutral, and conduct with respect for the do-no-harm concept. Monitors must respect the principle of confidentiality, protect their sources, be sensitive to the feelings of victims and witnesses, and not make promises they cannot fulfil. The field staff members will respect the proper functioning of national and local authorities and seek ways to support local capacity building.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SHO has embraced protection principles & standards and will apply them throughout all stages of project implementation. It’s essential to maintaining confidentiality of the complainant even within an organization to avoid retribution/ retaliation and negative repercussion towards the complainant, by staff, partners and suppliers. At the level of SHO, we will tag feedback as open or closed, and the date of closure of a complaint or feedback will be recorded. Staff members will be trained on how to log complaints and feedback, to ensure consistent categorization and logging between staff. After a complaint or feedback is received and recorded, they will be assigned to appropriate internal focal points for investigation and follow up. Only staff trained to conduct follow up and investigate will do so, if not possible, alternative reporting systems will be used. If the complaint or feedback does not relate to SHO’s programming, it will be referred with consent to the relevant actor for actionThe organization will in consultation with the groups in various communities set up complaints mechanism like help desk, toll free line, complaint box, e-mails and consistently carryout awareness about it like; "In making your complaint, provide all information that you believe is relevant. Anonymous complaints will be considered but it may be difficult to efficiently address your concern if we cannot contact you.Do not put yourself in danger by trying to do your own investigations. Reporting sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment is an obligation for all but will not result in you receiving any direct reward or special treatment''.
