Project Summary

The project aims to bolster the resilience of internally displaced persons (IDP) households forced to flee their homes due to escalating security threats. Additionally, it targets vulnerable host communities identified through specific criteria set by IYD. Over a span of 12 months, the project will provide essential support to 26,200 families residing in camps within Dana Sub-district of Idleb province. This assistance encompasses monthly food rations.
Moreover, multi-round (eight rounds) cash distributions totaling $65 will be provided to 10,000 households across Aleppo and Idleb, with specific allocations for various sub-districts to ensure equitable coverage. Additionally, 15,000 households in Idleb will receive value voucher cash distributions tailored to their specific needs.
The IDPs are entirely reliant on humanitarian assistance for their daily needs, exacerbating the strain on host communities in provinces like Aleppo and Idleb, which have limited financial and livelihood resources. To address this, the project will implement activities aimed at improving food security and livelihood opportunities.
Needs:
The severity of the humanitarian crisis in Syria remains significant, as a result of the continued hostilities in localized areas, protracted displacement, and harsh weather conditions. According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview Summary published on 21 December 2023, the number of people in need increased by 1.4 million reaching 16.7 million compared to 2023, 5.5 million of them are internally displaced people. Whilst there has been a decrease of hostilities in Idleb governorate over the current year, the number of people in need had increased significantly, the impact of hostilities on civilians remains the principal driver of humanitarian needs in Syria.
Implementation of Activities:
Implementation of the project activities will be comprehensive, involving registration and verification of beneficiaries, contracting of service providers, conducting market assessments, and coordinating with local authorities for distribution. Regular post-distribution monitoring will ensure adherence to project and donor policies, while contracting bakeries for bread provision will streamline food distribution in camps.
Protection Risk Analysis:
Several risks have been identified, including exclusion and discrimination in service provision and potential harm to vulnerable groups during project activities. Mitigation measures include clarifying selection criteria, establishing robust feedback mechanisms, and conducting constant assessments to ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries.
PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy:
IYD has adopted a comprehensive PSEA strategy, including adherence to the inter-agency Code of Conduct, active participation in PSEA networks, and establishment of internal reporting and investigative mechanisms. Completion of PSEA checklists and ongoing monitoring will ensure adherence to standards and prompt action against violations.
Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures:
Various risks, including institutional, operational, social, and security risks, have been identified. Mitigation measures involve maintaining strong collaboration with stakeholders, ensuring service provider commitment, addressing social concerns, and coordinating with local leaders and security teams to mitigate security risks. Regular monitoring and updates will be conducted to ensure the safety and security of project implementation.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to UNOCHA Situation Report December 21st, 2023, North-West of Syria has a catastrophic needs level, where 3.7 million people in Northwest of Syria are facing food insecurity, and 2.9 million people are categorized as IDPs, 2 million of them live in over 1,500 camps or self-settled sites with limited predictable access to heating, clean water or other necessities. According to the Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster, 87 % of IDPs live in sites that need camp management and 79 % live in sites with a critical level of overcrowding that puts their health at risk. 80 % of residents in IDP sites are women and children. In the northwestern regions of Syria, the preliminary Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024 underscores a considerable number of People in Need (PIN) in both Aleppo and Idleb governorates. The draft HNO 2024 outlines the PIN for Idleb at 2.6 million, while Aleppo is outlined with a segmented PIN of 1.1 million. It is noteworthy that Idleb encompasses 23 sub-districts categorized under severity levels 4 and 5, whereas Aleppo has 17 sub-districts sharing the same severity level. Food Security Key findings from IYD's recent needs assessment reports: Children (aged 0-17) make up the majority of the internally displaced population (IDP) in northwest Syria, accounting for 51%. Adults (aged 18-59) account for 46% of the IDP population. Elderly people (aged 60+) make up 3% of the IDP population. Males make up 52% of the total IDP population, while females make up 48%. People with disabilities constitute 20% of the IDP population in northwest Syria. A significant number of the IDP population in northwest Syria are facing severe and extreme food insecurity. Females account for 55% of the food insecure population, and 6.88% of food insecure females are pregnant or lactating. Children account for 56.8% of the food insecure population. Elderly people account for 1.7% of the food insecure population. People with disabilities account for 1.14% of the food insecure population
