Project Summary

The Rohingya refugee population shows high levels of vulnerability based on the REVA II and MSNA results. THE current COVID -19 situation makes them more vulnerable. The ‘Fill the Nutrient Gap’ analysis found that almost none of Rohingya HH could afford a nutritious diet. Under the existing conditions, Rohingya refugees remain 100% reliant on food distribution to sustain the daily minimum required kilocalorie in-take per individual. The REVA II results indicate that over 40% of the host community population is extremely vulnerable. As per the ‘Fill the Nutrient Gap’ analysis (WFP, October 2019) 1 out of 2 Bangladeshi in host communities cannot afford a nutritious diet.
To minimize the food insecurity problems in the host communities and Rohingya camps, BRAC will undertake a holistic humanitarian approach, in combination with other partners to facilitate improved economic status, food security, and dignity at the household level as well as greater social cohesion and inclusivity within and across communities. The responses towards food security of the host community and Rohingya will include different livelihood and self-reliance interventions like agriculture and environmental restoration, skill development, poverty reduction through self-reliance (ultra-poor graduation), general food distribution, and supplementary input support for different interventions and other income-generating activities, etc.
Under the COVID-19 response, total projected beneficiaries will be 21,008 to promote Skill Development opportunities for Rohingya and enhance the livelihood and resilience of host communities. Based on need assessment, BRAC will cover different camps like 1E, 2W, 4, 4 ext., 5, 6, 7, 8W, 8E, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 20, 20 ext., 21, 22 & 26, and host communities like Jaliapalong, Rajapalomng, Haldiapalong, Ratnapalong, and Palongkhali of Ukhiya sub-districts and Whykong, Nhila, Teknaf, Baharchara and Sabrang union of Teknaf sub-districts respectively. For the COVID-19 response, a total of 2.75M USD will be required for the successful implementation of food security and livelihood interventions.
Gender equity and equality is the core focus of BRAC's food security and self-reliance strategy. The project will target mainly female participants from vulnerable HHs for creating skill development opportunities to enhance Self-reliance and livelihood support. Cross-cutting issues like empowerment, inclusivity, sectoral integration (such as protection and health), decent work/safe environments, long-term well-being, climate change/natural disaster resilience, and portable skills will be the high priority areas in BRAC’s food security and self-reliance interventions.

Needs

The impacts of the pandemic on the refugee populations will be compounded by preexisting drivers of humanitarian needs, such as food insecurity, malnutrition, poor health, etc. Since the COVID-19 outbreak, the delivery of humanitarian response has been affected by the limited movement of goods, human workers, and beneficiaries. Although the transportation system has been disrupted, the humanitarian assistance remains imperative. To reduce the impact of COVID-19 on the Refugee population, the sector will distribute agro-inputs support and cash grant among the vulnerable refugee population. BRAC will reach out to 8,576 beneficiaries where 900 will be new beneficiaries and 7,676 will be old beneficiaries (people from JRP2020).The COVID-19 outbreak is limiting the movement of people as well as their access to commodities and basic goods. Restrictions on vehicle movement and home quarantine obstruct farmers’ access to markets, shortening their productive capacities, and hindering them from selling their produce. Immediately after the shutdown, the prices of non-perishable commodities (rice, pulse, onion, eggs, oil, and dry food) has been increased as many people have stock pilled essential items. A portion of people, especially in rural areas, are most at risk and most severely affected due to the interruptions in their regular incomes, access to health services, and inability of meeting their living expenses. A longer-term shutdown would limit the availability of daily commodities and other supplies in the rural areas. As a result, production would go down and create a food crisis. To minimize the impact of COVID-19 on food security and the sufferings of both host and refugee communities, the sector is considering a comprehensive intervention such as cash distribution, agro-inputs support, house repairing, and hygiene support targeting 12,432 people where 9,432 will be from old beneficiaries (people from JRP 2020) and 3,000 will be new beneficiaries. The sector will also continue with the approved project interventions regarding livelihood support, cash interventions and skill development to meet the basic needs of the community and ensure a conducive working environment.
