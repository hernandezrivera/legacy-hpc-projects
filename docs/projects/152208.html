Project Summary

The project seeks to fill the gaps of CCCM Sector in line with the centrality of protection strategy and will target 120,000 women, men, boys and girls IDPs living in the camps, camp-like settings, IDPs out of camps and host community that received the IDPs and dwelling in their houses. The project will cover 4 LGAs in Borno (Ngala, Munguno, Banki and Damasak), 7 LGAs in Adamawa (Fufore, Yola South, Yola North, Girei, Numan, Demsa and Mubi axis) and 3 LGAs in Yobe state (Damaturu, Gujba and Gulani). The breakdown of the target for this CCCM project is 97,298 individuals (19,460HH) for IDPs and 22,702 individuals (4,540 HH) for returnees distributed as follow: 46% (male), 54% (female), 60% (children), 36% (adult) and 4% (elders). The CCCM Project will be addressing the gaps of tracking movement of population (new arrivals and returnees) particularly the most vulnerable including unaccompanied and separated children, assessing the needs of the women, men, boys, girls, adolescents and older displaced persons in a participatory manner and based on age, gender and diversity mainstreaming, supporting the coordination of response of service providers, capacitating the communities through their effective participation and ownership of activities and developing the resilience and self-reliance when they are still in displacement. The project will also focus on the sensitization of IDPs especially youth, boys, girls, adolescents and women who are facing different problem of protection. The CCCM project will improve the quality of management of the women, men, boys and girls IDPs within the camp settings, monitor and assist those out of camps and some vulnerable persons in host community. The project will support systematic follow up of the IDPs and returnees new arrivals particularly vulnerable women and girls. The project seeks to provide quality services during the registration process and will ensure that reception facilities are equally accessible to women, men, boys and girls newly arrived. Camp managers will ensure proper relocation of new arrivals from the reception centres to the shelters constructed taking into account the needs of the most vulnerable. Through this project, CCCM interventions will also assess, analyse and report on all protection gaps and needs to be addressed by different sectors in order to improve the living conditions of women, men, girls and boys displaced. The CCCM project will promote the participation of the IDPs based on age, gender and diversity in the life cycle of camps particularly care and maintenance of infrastructures, small scale drainage, etc.

Needs

There are total of 269 camps in BAY states with Borno having highest number of camps and IDPS. The total population residing in these camps is around 760,000. There are still 129 camps without site facilitation in place. Even for the camps with site facilitation support, the number of site facilitator required for the camp is far below the SPHERE standard making it really challenging to have proper camp coordination and camp management in place. This often leads to huge gaps in supporting beneficiaries thereby leading beneficiaries to suffer heavily. There are around 1 million people in hard-to-reach areas who are of concern to CCCM. Based on pas trend, around 40% of people from those areas come to stay in camps which even increases the number of CCCM needs. Additionally, some of the refugee returnees coming from Niger, Chad and Cameroon also come to stay in camp as a secondary displaced people as they can’t go back to their area of origin. There is need of proper reception centre in different places as new arrivals needs to be received and taken care of before being sent to camp. Return areas like Damasak and Banki still do not have proper reception centre in place whereas the existing ones are also not able to cater to the needs of new arrivals. The current situation in the various IDP camps and sites is difficult. Most of the CCCM organizations on ground have not yet succeeded in having a detailed and accurate readability of the situation of the population on site. The needs are high but the presence of actors and support team is limited. There is a need for UNHCR in collaboration with NEMA, SEMA and the sector to ensure there is dedicated and proper site facilitation in its area of responsibility to promote the reinforcement of camp committees, support beneficiating in identifying needs and gaps, trading beneficiaries on the ground, and launch innovative approach to ensure that camp committees have necessary CCCM tools to identify gaps and refer them across sectors. The coordination mechanism is not yet working properly in all IDPs camps and the synergy between different actors remain challenging. There is a need to link where possible the camp activities with public services such as education, health and public’s works. The lack of feedback mechanisms in various camps and the lack of continuous training/coaching for site facilitators, camp managers and community members hampered the continuous improvement of the quality of the governance in the different camps. The absence of innovations and knowledge for IDPs to take their destiny in hands when the humanitarian assistance is reduced is a challenge. There is critical need to build their capacity in resilience and self-reliance and develop the ownership to manage the activities within the displacement sites. The level of congestion of most of the IDPs camp in Borno is high. This is mainly due to challenge of accessing secured land and inability to provide functional security systems to large camps. This require therefore a more structured site in order to ensure that internal security mechanism and various camp committee can function effectively in a more comprehensive way. The care and maintenance mechanisms interventions in the various camp also require review. The current environment clearly shows that IDPs are leaving various camps to either stay in new surroundings or return to their home of origin. The secondary displacement movement of IDPs is not clearly tracked and the reasons which force the IDPs to leave are not well understood. Increasing attacks on camps hosting IDPs (and returnees) leading to the loss of life and property; restrictions of movement have raised the urgent need to improve the protection and physical security measures in and around camps and sites to ensure safety and security of inhabitants.

Activities

1. Site facilitation support in the camp to ensure all the partners across the camps have harmonised and coordinated approach to avoid duplication, overlapping and proper intervention and response in the camps2. Support the tracking and the monitoring of displacement and information on needs gaps for follow up on displaced population and referral across sectors. 2. Camp management and camp coordination capacities reinforced 3. Support the maintenance, upgrade and care, decongestion of sites through site planning, improvements when required and coordinated assistance 4. Support relocation and return of IDPs 5. Community empowerment and Community mobilization for participation and ownership of activities within camps and camp-like settings 6. Community based capacity building on resilience and self-reliance 7. Provision of protection and physical protection measures in camps and camp- like settings 8. Regular monitoring and evaluation of activities carried out and response provided9. Construction of reception centre in return areas.10. UNHCR and the various stakeholders will therefore advocate where possible the provision of new secured plot with more standardized site planning or ensure the current site planning are reviewed. The community participation will be reinforced also through the coaching of the committee leaders. 11. UNHCR will support actors in tracking returns that will contribute to the development and implementation of a durable solutions strategy that will ensure that the humanitarian community address the return and the relocation process in more comprehensive and effective way.
