Project Summary

This intervention supports the pursuit of a favourable protection environment for refugees and asylum-seekers in Libya, including the monitoring of the protection context, individual intervention at disembarkation, detention monitoring and assistance, and provision of documentation and advice.

1 - Needs

Over 46,000 refugees and asylum-seekers are registered with UNHCR. Refugees and asylum seekers face discrimination and challenges in access to basic services. The decreasing levels of available resources, decreasing cash liquidity and sharp price increases pose additional challenges for refugees and asylum seekers due to the competition for scarce resources with the host communities and IDPs. Protection challenges for Libyans and non-Libyans are a grave concern, primarily driven by exposure, vulnerability and inability to cope with conflict and violence, human rights violations and abuses, breakdown of rule of law, and major challenges related to impediments to access critical services and essential goods and commodities. Migrants and refugees including women and girls are often held in unsafe environments where they suffer ill-treatment, often carried out by the predominantly male guard workforce. The absence of powerful central authorities, effective border control, lack of rule of law and weak institutions provide a fertile ground for international smuggling and trafficking networks in Libya. The increasing level of violence, including SGBV, arbitrary arrest and torture, and deteriorating protection environment inside of Libya continue to be pushing factors for refugees and asylum seekers to move towards Europe. The overall security situation in Libya is expected to remain unpredictable in 2020 if the current political dialogue and the reconciliation process doesn’t achieve any significant results. The security instability and deteriorating living conditions may continue to lead to forced displacement, and restrict the access of UNHCR’s persons of concern to protection and essential services, in particular affecting women, girls and persons with specific needs. Nevertheless, UNHCR is looking at expanding its footprint outside of Tripoli and implement protection activities in Benghazi, Misrata and Sebha. Libya continues to be the main transit and departure point from North Africa towards Europe, particularly from Sub-Saharan Africans from the Horn of Africa. Over 90% of arrivals to Italy are reported to have departed from Libya, where brutal smuggling and trafficking networks benefit from political and security turmoil. In 2020, it is expected that Libya will continue to be the main transit point from Africa to Europe, allowing the trafficking and smuggling network to still be active, with a possible change of routes, new embarkation points and a possible situation of migrants and refugees being stranded in Libya for longer periods of time. In any case, vulnerabilities will continue to increase. Advocacy to end detention will remain a priority while the implementation of the comprehensive solution strategy will be pursued.

2 - Activities

UNHCR will continue advocacy work for the authorities to respect principle of non-refoulement and through interagency joint work, UNHCR will call upon the authorities for alternatives to detention and closure of detention centres in Libya.In parallel, UNHCR will continue its protection interventions in detention centres, including life-saving assistance, registration, screening, provide counselling on asylum procedures and solutions. UNHCR will continue to monitor the conditions in the detention centres and advocate for the authorities to uphold the international standards with regards to treatment and services. UNHCR will continue invest on work done the Gathering Departure Facility (GDF) to enhance delivery of protection to vulnerable persons of concern where they will have access to solutions.UNHCR will continue enhancing and expanding protection interventions at disembarkation points jointly with Libyan Coastguards and IOM where rescue at sea operations continue to save those stranded including persons of concern.UNHCR will provide live saving assistance and identify and assist most vulnerable including victims of trafficking and smuggling.UNHCR will continue its capacity building activities targeting key officials from MOFA, MOI, DCIM.UNHCR will strengthen access to mandate asylum and access to solution for persons of concern through advocacy for the government to acknowledge the ratification of the OAU Convention its obligations. UNHCR will pursue the pervious initiative work for the authorities to adopt asylum law that has been pending due to the instability prevailing in the country.UNHCR will provide technical and legal assistance to the authorities to incorporate asylum procedures in the national system and would advocate for the asylum course to be included in the university curriculum.UNHCR will continue to advocate for the authorities to recognise UNHCR documentation.

4 - Protection risk analysis

As of January 2020, UNHCR has registered over 46,450 individuals of concern (refugees and asylum-seekers): 4,941 (52% men and 48% women) refugees and 41,509 (64% men and 36% women) asylum-seekers. 2,506 refugees/asylum-seekers (5%) are held in detention while 43,944 (95%) reside in urban settings. There are currently an estimated 204,579 migrants considered in need of protection assistance in Libya, with an estimated 3,200 migrants held in detention centres. The political instability and weak rule of law has created a conducive environment for the mass movements of migrants and refugees through Libya, either to remain in country or to attempt to cross the Mediterranean Sea. The protection risks for non-Libyan populations are directly linked to the lack of formal recognition from the government for such groups and the risk of arbitrary detention, which increases individuals’ exposure to human rights violations. Libya is not a signatory to the 1951 Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees nor its 1967 Additional Protocol. Although it is party to the 1969 OAU Convention Governing the Specific Aspects of Refugee Problems in Africa, the national legislation contravenes international refugee legal instruments due to the lack of a functional asylum system and restrictions and limitations on the ability for UNHCR to fill the gaps in the existing national framework. Libya also contravenes customary international law, international refugee law, and international human rights law by returning rescued refugees and migrants to their shores where they are unable to provide safe harbour, as well as the indefinite and arbitrary detention of refugees and migrants in detention centres that are rife with human rights abuses. Protection risks for refugees and migrants in Libya are further compounded by the interconnectedness of the smuggling and trafficking networks with governmental detention centres and the lack of sustainable solutions.

5 - Mitigation measures

2020 office structure and staffing planning is based on the assumption that political instability, security and economic challenges in the country will continue. At the time of reporting the current protracted armed conflict, which began in April 2019 remains and is assessed by security professionals of the UNSMS as progressing with a noticeable intensification in hostilities between the GNA affiliated forces and the LNA. Despite this forecast, it is expected that the majority of activities will be able to continue within this very dynamic and challenging context.The capacity building of staff remains high priority with particular attention given to training activities and learning events, as well as staff welfare sessions particularly geared towards front-line staff challenged with numerous stressful and tragic situation of the beneficiaries. Mandatory security trainings for all staff are kept at 100% compliance level at all times. The capacity building of staff in areas of PSEA and other mandatory trainings will continue to be pursued. Continued efforts are made by Protection team in raising awareness and building capacity of our staff in the area of international humanitarian laws. UNHCR will aim to ensure that vulnerable displaced and conflicted affected communities are supported to establish legal identity, including obtaining legal civil documentation, and claim their Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights. UNHCR will continue to lead the Shelter/NFI Sector, and the Cash Market Working Group. UNHCR through the Protection Sector’s response will aim to improve access, protection monitoring activities, quality and availability of such services for the identified Libyan and non-Libyan populations. UNHCR will support the Protection Sector in prioritizing capacity-building activities of concerned government institutions with the aim to enable better services provision, greater accountability to communities, and ultimately, improved social cohesion.
