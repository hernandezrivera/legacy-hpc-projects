Project Summary

WASH response in any humanitarian emergency forms part of life saving interventions and due to the dynamic nature of emergency in newly merged Districts (Former Fata), WASH interventions are expected to remain a priority area of response throughout 2019. The situation remains very volatile and complex. A significant population has been returned to their areas of origin since 2015. As per information from different sources including government and humanitarian agencies, the remaining IDPs have returned to their areas of origin in 2018.
SRSP-Field team during the implementation of other on-gong projects in District khyber has carried out baseline surveys through which WASH needs are identified. Apart from that SRSP monitoring team during field visit in District Khyber observed that the lack of proper water management has produced WASH related issues; people are practicing Open Defecation, you will find garbage everywhere around the market and villages having no proper disposal system neither awareness which also produces unpleasant smell around the market, water coming from springs is contaminating, people are naïve to the washroom concept etc.
Under this project SRSP will provide integrated water, sanitation and hygiene services while considering the settlement topography, available resources in District Khyber. The project will be undertaken in complete coordination with District Administration, concern stakeholders including PHED, Education, Health department & local communities etc. In District Khyber 6,422 individuals (5,138 water, 3,853 sanitation and 6,422 hygiene) will be facilitated under different WASH related interventions in 20 villages. 1 schools will be provided with WASH facilities. The project would endeavor to revive the sanitation facilities, improve health & hygiene conditions of the communities particularly women and children. Community will be sensitized through series health & hygiene sessions. They will also be supported to rehabilitate, rebuild infrastructure to improve the health & hygiene situations in the area. The project will keep a holistic approach and target local issues using customized approaches.
SRSP existing setup and established networks and linkages with communities in the area will help to run the WASH project smoothly without any hurdle.

Needs

WASH response in any humanitarian emergency forms part of life saving interventions and due to the dynamic nature of emergency in newly merged Districts (Former Fata), WASH interventions are expected to remain a priority area of response throughout 2019. The situation remains very volatile and complex. A significant population has been returned to their areas of origin since 2015. As per information from different sources including government and humanitarian agencies, the remaining IDPs have returned to their areas of origin in 2018.In newly merged districts of KP(Former FATA) 73.5% population has access to improved water sources that is 68.9% in rural areas and 93% in urban areas. There is no proper mechanism to address the water quality issues. 31.4% population uses surface water for drinking purposes, mainly sourced from canals, rivers, springs, ponds and streams. Piped water only accounts for 8.9% households’ drinking water. (Source: FATA Development Indicators Household Survey 2013-14).Sanitation is another key indicator that impacts on the well-being of the general population. Provision of clean and covered sanitation facilities is especially important for women and children: incidents have been reported in which women and children have faced abuse or harassment due to the non-availability of proper sanitation facilities, particularly enclosed toilets. Open defecation is a normal phenomenon in the area; both male and female practice open defecation due to unavailability of sanitation facilities and lack of awareness. Women and girls face particular risk of GBV when defecating in the open. In newly merged districts of KP(Former FATA) as per PSLM 2012-13, Open defecation is 62%, which is much higher than the national average of 21%. Within FATA, majority of latrines are unimproved pit latrines (51.7%), against the national average of 9% (PSLM 2012-13).The newly merged districts of KP(Former FATA) has been ranked as having poor hand washing practices. The provision of clean water and sanitation infrastructure will not have the desired impact as long as the mind-sets of end users do not change towards adapting more hygienic practices. According to EMIS of newly merged districts of KPK (Former FATA) 24% of the primary schools have sanitation coverage and 35% have water coverage.Under this project SRSP will provide integrated water, sanitation and hygiene services while considering the settlement topography, available resources in District Khyber. The project will be undertaken in complete coordination with District Administration, concern stakeholders including PHED, Education, Health department & local communities etc. In District Khyber 6,422 individuals (5,138 water, 3,853 sanitation & 6,422 hygiene) will be facilitated under different WASH related interventions in 20 villages. 1 school will be provided with WASH facilities. The project would endeavor to revive the sanitation facilities, improve health & hygiene conditions of the communities particularly women and children. Community will be sensitized through series health & hygiene sessions. They will also be supported to rehabilitate, rebuild infrastructure to improve the health & hygiene situations in the area. The project will keep a holistic approach and target local issues using customized approaches.
