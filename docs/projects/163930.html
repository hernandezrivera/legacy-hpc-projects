Project Summary

Starting from the needs of the affected people in Syria, the project will participate in providing assistance for vulnerable groups and people whom under or at risk to be under protection concerns.
Starting from the needs of children related to child labor, dropping out of schools, separation, and psycho-social needs, the project will provide child protection services as structured PSS, Awareness raising on child protection concerns and specialized child protection services including case management, and referrals based on service mapping in each targeted location, the project will support 30 informal schools by establishing 2 rooms to deliver structured PSS and case management activities, also will support 40 formal schools by providing recreational sessions along with supervision to identify children with special protection needs.
The high target of child protection component, is because of the concrete structure of Bonyan in education which clearly described through the project under code: HSYR20-EDU-163925-1
And after building the capacity of the field staff through conducting trainings on GBV Specialized services provision, Bonyan will implement specialized activities in GBV with close coordination with GBV sub-cluster in future during coming projects.
To expand protection services out of protection and education facilities, 12 mobile teams will provide integrated protection services (CP, RE), in child protection topic mobile teams will provide recreational PSS, awareness raising sessions on CP risks, along with providing Risk Education in camps and IDPs collective centers to mitigate the risks from mines and war remnants.
Legal awareness raising and assessments will be conducted to issue advocacy messages regarding CSD issues, and the implementation will target students at schools and caregivers since the lost documentation will cause problems regarding students’ registration at schools.
Awareness raising sessions will be conducted along with distributing brochures to deliver the message clearly.
Specialized protection services will be delivered to the most vulnerable BNFs at risk of CP, GBV, General Protection concerns, the services are referrals to services providers, and direct support provision, based on service mapping will be implemented in the early beginning of the project.
the needs and risks should be measured during the project
Since the situation on the field level is rapidly changes, the needs assessment should be implemented on monthly basis, thus Bonyan is a partner in Child Protection Situation Monitoring, will implement CPSM survey in the project locations to measure the changes of CP issues severity during the project’s duration.
Regarding the capacity building component, all protection staff will be targeted with capacity building program focusing on the following topics:
CPiE 2 days, CPMS 1 day, Case management 1 day, GBV 3 days, Risk Education 1 day.
Each trainee will attend 8 days training program to ensure the knowledge and quality of service which will be delivered
Total trainees will be (65 mobile teams’ members, 180 CFSs protection staff (30 schools will be supported to include CFSs), 80 protection staff from 40 formal schools, 3 senior field management staff). In total of 340 trainees
The progress of the project will be monitored by a team of field monitoring officers, these officers will conduct periodic visits to the schools and learning centers in order to follow-up data through direct observation, interview the educational staff and make rapid surveys.

4 - Needs

According to the protection HNO 2020 assessments 5.7 million children out of 13.6M need to protection services in Syria. Increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to prevail across Syria, resulting from a variety of situations ranging from direct exposure to hostilities, multiple cycles of displacement, targeted and arbitrary detention, lack/loss of civil documentation, and constraints on freedom of movement.Multiple and complex child protection risks continue to shape the lives of girls and boys throughout Syria. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. Repeated and prolonged displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty, the disintegration of social support structures, and the persistent challenges in access and availability to services continue to take a huge toll on children. Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. In the first nine months of 2019, the UN verified 1,792 such violations against children in 12 of 14 governorates of Syria, illustrating persistent trends of violence though not the full scope of protection concerns affecting children.GBV continues to dis proportionally affect the lives of women and girls in Syria, both inside and outside their homes. New and recurring GBV trends confirm that women, adolescent girls, widowed/divorced women and girls, women and girls with disabilities and older women are exposed to a multitude of GBV risks, exacerbating their vulnerability and resulting in very few places where they feel safe.

5 - Implementation of activities

Each step of the project will be focusing on integration of the BNFs, by effective feedback and complaints mechanisms
