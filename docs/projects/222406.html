Project Summary

South Sudan faces significant WASH challenges such as low levels of access to basic water supply and sanitation services, poor infrastructure, environmental concerns, and water use conflicts. Across the country, 59% of the population lacks access to safe water (JMP, 2023). Families are forced to depend on surface water, increasing risk of waterborne diseases such as cholera and other diarrhea diseases, which remain the leading cause of death among children under 5years and elderly persons. Water resources are not evenly distributed across the country and inter-seasonal variability is high, leading to extreme flooding and dry season shortages. Water quality monitoring is generally limited and there are significant information gaps in the understanding of groundwater quality. According to the interagency Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA-2024) report, there is a need for improvements in access to water through rehabilitation of non-functional hand pumps. The current availability of water is at a critically low level with most returnees, host and displaced communities forced to use unprotected water sources for domestic use. Access to sanitation facilities is also extremely low, only approximately 10% of the population has access to basic sanitation (JMP, 2023). The situation is further aggravated by poor hygiene practices such as the practice of open defecation. Hygiene materials are inadequate with limited markets, and high prices of basic hygiene items at local markets, exacerbated by the prolonged floods cutting off access to alternative markets as well as increasing humanitarian operational costs. The risk of exposure to protection incidents and gender-based violence is of significant concern. Women and girls are primarily responsible for fetching water and are often forced to walk long distances putting them at greater risk of gender-based violence. An estimated 2.6 million people in South Sudan were at risk of gender-based violence in 2022 and nearly 40% of women and girls have reported having to avoid essential public places like water points, washrooms, and markets out of fear of being attacked, raped, or sexually exploited (UNFPA Report). The WASH situation has been worsened by the influx of returnees since April 2023 due to the Sudan conflict. A growing trend of preventative and communicable diseases is registered with cholera cases confirmed in 5 locations in the country so far. High number of acute watery diarrhea cases are also being reported due to high vulnerability and poor living conditions and the health system is severely overstretched.
The intervention will focus on geographically prioritized 13 counties (Fangak, Panyikang, Canal Pigi, Melut, Cueibet, Aweil East, Leer, Luakpiny/Nasir, Rubkona, Pibor, Ayod, Akobo, Fashoda) with high WASH vulnerabilities and acute malnutrition rates and areas with high protection concerns. Through this HNRP allocation, UNICEF aims to fill these critical WASH gaps by increasing life-saving emergency WASH support to the vulnerable communities through provision of safe water, improving access to basic sanitation, hygiene awareness and distribution of WASH NFIs. UNICEF will leverage its working relationship with Government and CSOs to deliver the program. Where feasible, UNICEF will explore the use of private sector and support to strengthen the local market for basic WASH supplies. Priority will be accorded to CBOs fostering localization, enhancing ownership of the intervention and sustainability. Capacity gaps with the partners will be identifies and UNICEF will organize and undertake tailored trainings to close these gaps. UNICEF will continue to engage beneficiaries meaningfully and adequately in the identification and prioritizations of their needs. Community Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms will be put in place and UNICEF will continue to enforce its commitment to PSEA and prevention of GBV through mainstreaming activities that prevent and mitigate such risks.

1. Needs

Needs:Vast WASH needs have been identified in the 13 priority 1 counties (Fangak, Panyikang, Canal Pigi, Melut, Cueibet, Aweil East, Leer, Luakpiny/Nasir, Rubkona, Pibor, Ayod, Akobo, Fashoda) faced with intertwining crisis drivers of conflict, floods, food insecurity, a cute malnutrition, disease outbreak, amongst others. Overall, 743,302 people are in critical need of WASH services in these locations. Floods particularly submerged WASH facilities and caused massive displacement of people to be congested in locations characterized with limited or no WASH facilities. Conflict in Sudan has displaced multitude of returnees over 800,000 people to South Sudan through Upper Nile and Unity borders burdening severely the scarce WASH facilities, increasing conflict amongst communities as they scramble for these scarce resources. Out break of Cholera in Renk spreading shiftily to rest of the country is threatening lives and causing panic amongst populations. Ongoing crisis in Sudan, Gaza, Ukraine, etc. has also shifted donor priorities and significant shrinkage has been noted in funding to support these at-risk populations in South Sudan/. This is further aggravated with the worsening economic situation impacting on the cost of delivering humanitarian interventions. UNICEF aims to strengthening WASH services in these counties ensuring continuity of WASH services provision through provision of safe water, safe and dignified sanitation, and adequate hygiene services and WASH NFIs distribution to 743020 most vulnerable, conflict and flood affected people in the named counties. UNICEF and partners in coordination with host government and WASH cluster will address critical WASH needs ensuring that efforts are not duplicated, and resources are efficiently used to reach more crisis impacted communities. UNICEF and its partners will explore working modalities to contain current cholera outbreak and put in place measures to mitigate occurrence of such outbreaks of public concerns. This will be attained through intensive Risk Communication and Community Engagement, intersectoral coordination and collaboration, and strengthening community resilience approaches. UNICEF will strengthen existing community-based WASH structures for operation and maintenance and sustainability of the intervention.

2. Project activities

The intervention will be undertaken through provision of safe water, basic sanitation, and hygiene services. Specific activities will include rehabilitation of boreholes and Solar Water Yards (SWY); set up and operationalization of Surface Water Treatment Systems (SWATs); rehabilitation of existing SWATs; upgrade of handpumps to SWYs; regular water quality monitoring and residential chlorination; batch chlorination of water, distribution of household water treatment chemicals; installation of additional safe water storage and supply facilities; establishment and training of community based WASH structures for operation and maintenance and sustainability. Sanitation activities will entail construction of semi-permanent communal latrines; latrine desludging services, disinfection of potentially contaminated areas; environmental cleanup. Communities’ awareness raising will also be intensified through the integrates Risk Communication and community Engagement structures in place complemented with radio jingles and radio talk show to alert communities on the risks related to unsafe sanitation and hygienic practices and buy their will and commitment in safeguarding their own health. Of focus in the awareness raising is messaging on cholera, Hepatis E prevention and mitigation. Critical lifesaving WASH NFIs will be distributed to populations in critical need at onset of crisis in line with WASH cluster prioritization and guidelines. This will significantly curb the blooming risk of death to diarrheal and other waterborne disease. WASH NFIs will be secured from the WASH Core Pipeline, and the Cluster guidelines will be adhered to in the distribution and reporting of WASH NFIs distributed. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted for supplies distributed to ensure supplies reach the last milage. The feedback gathered from the PDM and overall intervention will be documented and used to adjust the response and inform future programing. Capacity Building of local community structures, CBOs will continue to be emphasized. Enhancing capacity of the local market for WASH supplies will be explored to the extent feasible.
