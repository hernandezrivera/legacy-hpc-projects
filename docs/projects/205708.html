Project Summary

During 2024, the integration of health and preventative nutrition services for 54,891 (16,349 M, 38,349 F) in the 3 target counties (i.e. Abyie, Fangak and Pochalla), will provide protection centric interventions aimed at reducing morbidity and mortality, while building local capacity to withstand the impact of local shocks (HRP 2024 strategic objectives).
Across 6 service points (static and mobile), at a cost of $600,000; HEALTH interventions for 31,771 (13,979 M, 17,792 F) will contribute to the 3 cluster objectives i.e. #1, #2, #3 and work alongside the County Health, local communities and partners to ensure equitable access to lifesaving services.
Towards combating wasting in children U5 and PLW, preventive nutrition interventions targets displaced and underserved population pockets of Fangak, Pochalla and Abyei, at cost of $600,000 interventions for 23,120 (2,370 M, 20,750 F) will contribute to the 3 cluster objectives i.e. #1, #3, #4; Through interlinked filed work plans, Hold the Child will work with existing nutrition partners in to ensure community screening and referrals, MIYCN promotion, support cash (food component) transfers to discharged SAM/MAM cases and inter-linkages with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection service deliver as complements towards addressing malnutrition.

1. Needs

Over 2.2 million people displaced within the country since 2013 violence (HNO 2023) and 332,979 returnees (24th Oct, UNHCR/IOM) fleeing violence in Sudan living situations of compromised dignity with limited capacity to cope with shocks e.g. floods and rebuild their livelihoods. This reality is further compounded by inflation under the global economic melt-down on top of the already underdeveloped service health infrastructure at 15.05% (WHO report 2021); while some health facilities are cut-off/submerged during floods, others vandalised during violent episodes, supply pipelines broken and professionals scared away. Episodes of measles outbreak, acute malnutrition illustrate the brunt of crisis impact on Women and Children most especially in IDP pockets, high return and hard to reach areas.HEALTH: With over 75% deaths attributed to preventable diseases i.e. Malaria, Diarrhoea, and Pneumonia. In 2023, the Health cluster classified 69% of counties (including Fangak) to be at high risk of measles outbreaks, a trend attribute to low routine coverage and low demand for immunisation services among population pockets usually spread 20+Km away from the currently functional health facilities; the DHIS-2 2022 shows Penta 3 coverage at 67% in Pochalla and 30% Abyei way below the national 20% target. In additional to health service availability (nationally estimated at 30.4%); low awareness, supply breaks and limited qualified health cadres, distances, social barriers including care for siblings are known to be key drivers for low ANC4+ uptake at 28% in Fangak, 21% Abyei, 8% Pochalla and Skilled birth attendance at 4% in Pochalla and 2% AbyeiNUTRITION: Over 60 % of the population expected to be food insecure into 2024 (extrapolated from FEWS NET Jun 2023 bulletin), with 2023 below average rainfall harvesting households may only yield a few months of stocks only worsen the already dire situation. With only 4% children getting the minimum acceptable diet for them to grow and develop well, the Nutrition cluster estimates 2.5 million children and women to be at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024. Whereas the IPC classification (April-July 2023) ranked Fangak at phase 4, Pochalla at phase 3 and Abyei was unknown; the Nutrition cluster multiple deviations evaluation ranks both Fangak at GAM 22.2% and Abyei at GAM 19.6% at level 4 part of the highly prioritised counties, and Pochalla at GAM 11.8% falls under level 3. Food insecurity (limited supplies), poor food intake (diets and care practices), high morbidity rates and poor treatment seeking, poor WASH conditions, high GBV concerns, high illiteracy levels especially among women (primary care givers) are cited to be key drivers of malnutrition crisis. Nationally, 50% of the population lacking access to safe water or sanitation; in Fangak most homes drink from the river, open defecation is a common practice across the 3 counties visible at river banks in Fangak no surprise to annual hepatitis E virus episodes; the cycle should urgently be broken. Protracted tensions, IDP and returnee situations in Abyie, IDP and return influx in Fangak, Pochalla violent episode are known recipes for (a) heightened levels of gender based violence especially for women and girls, the primary care providers with direct effect on child nutrition and health status, (b) and psychosocial distress among children.Upon this background, 2024 child survival and maternal health indicators among project counties look even more scaring, it is therefore urgent that hard to reach pockets are reached with integrated efforts to improve food intake, home based care, early treatment seeking, expand access mother-child health services coupled with WASH promotion, MHPSS and prevention of GBV.

2. Project activities

HEALTH: Health Cluster Objective 1: (1) Support 8 health care facilities (4 PHCUs and 4 Mobile Clinics) to provide integrated lifesaving health care services, to underserved population complaint with WHO IPC requirements(2) Conduct tailored training for 52 health care workers; 6 clinicians and 20 nurses in IMCI and 20 midwives on safe motherhood, 6 Data Clerks(3) Conduct routine immunisation at target health facilities, and outreaches (out-post) within catchment Bomas to optimise Penta 3 coverage, PSEAH will be integrated (4) Integrated SGBV awareness at service points, and provide MHPSS services (5) Provide curative consultations at 8 health facilities to reach estimated 31,771 beneficiaries within catchments. Outreaches, and local referrals working with existing BHTs will reduce social barriers to gender, age and disability. (5) Provide routine Maternal health services i.e. ANC, Assisted deliveries and Post natal care, BEmONC Clinical management of Rape & referrals for CEmONC,Health Cluster Objective 2: (1) Conduct disease surveillance in 3 Counties; Weekly IDSR reporting for all the supported health facilities and EWARS for outreach sites.(2) Ensure emergency health supplies (IEHK) at supported facilities. (3) Conduct cluster determined reactive/mop-ups measles exercises when the demand arises. (4) Institute feedback mechanisms at supported units among human centred designs to advance service equity for the most vulnerable. (5) Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care in the selected counties and report any vandalization of health care infrastructure, theft of equipment, essential medicines and supplies.(6) Integrate WASH promotion at all supported health units and incorporate key messages in community based health education sessions.Health Cluster Objective 3: (1) Conduct regular consultation and supportive supervisions with CHDs.(2) Conduct community level consultative meeting to profile local needs and better articulate context appropriate inclusive response interventions. (3) Contribute to inter-agency rapid needs assessment in project counties, when the need arises. NUTRITION Nutrition Cluster Objective 1: Conduct (1) Train Lead Mothers and Champion fathers on MIYCN facilitating techniques, and facilitate the formation of Mother to Mother Support Groups of Catchment lactating women with children 0-23 months(2) Conduct optimal MIYCN promotion ie early initiation of breastfeeding within one hour of birth, exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months of life and optimal breastfeeding up to 2 years, and timely introduction of nutritious complementary feeding recipes (from local food baskets) including WASH key messages; groups counselling, interpersonal communication and faith groups (3) Support to 2 rounds of national campaigns of Vit A supplementation to children from 6-59 months, deworming to children from 12-59 months and screening of wasting to children from 6-59 monthsNutrition Cluster Objective 2: (1) Conduct MYCN Barrier Analysis across targeted Payams Nutrition Cluster Objective 4: (1) Conditioned CVA (food component) caregivers discharged from OT/TSF programs upon attendance of Mother-Mother support group activities and consolidate food in-take gains and household resilience. (2) Contribute to community level screening and referrals for Wasting in U5s in collaboration with Nutrition partners (4) Enhance psychosocial stimulation of children attending nutrition and health services. Work with OT/TSF and health programs to provide drop-in childcare services for facility contact children during when care givers are attending health education/counselling sessions. (5) Liaise with FSL partners to engage nutrition beneficiaries for home/kitchen gardening activities, distribution of farm tools and seed to enhance the community diversity and improve their feeding habits. Work with existing OT/TSF and supported Mother-mother support groups to establish demonstration gardening
