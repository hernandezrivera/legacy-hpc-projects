Project Summary

The primary goal of this project is to protect vulnerable populations from disease outbreaks by rapidly detecting and responding to Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) outbreaks. The project will focus on provinces previously affected by climatic disasters such as cyclones and tropical storms, as well as areas experiencing an increase in AWD and cholera outbreaks. Particular attention will be given to conflict-affected districts, where health systems are significantly disrupted, and populations face compounded vulnerabilities due to limited access to basic services.
This will be achieved through collaboration with Provincial Health Services (SPS) and Provincial Directorates of Health (DPS) to strengthen the coordination and leadership for AWD and cholera response. The project will guide the development and implementation of comprehensive multi-sectoral response plans at the provincial level, ensuring they are tailored to the unique challenges of conflict-affected areas. A key focus will be enhancing community health

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The objective of this project will be accomplished through collaboration with community health workers to engage with communities comprised of individuals with disabilities, girls, boys, women, men, adolescents, the elderly, child and female-headed households, and other vulnerable groups in order to promptly detect and respond to AWD. It is scheduled for implementation in provinces previously affected by climatic disasters, with particular focus on the conflict affected Cabo Delgado province.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will prioritize activities in Cabo Delgado Province and other disaster-affected areas. Efforts will focus on collaboration with WASH, health logistics, and RCCE sectors to facilitate the deployment of integrated multisectoral rapid response teams. These teams will validate, investigate, and respond to reported cases of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and cholera. The specific activities include:Training: Provide training for surveillance and response personnel at the provincial and district levels. Training will cover case identification and reporting methods, community-based case detection, sample collection and transportation, and emergency WASH operations.Community Engagement: Orient community health workers to identify and report potential outbreaks within communities.Data Management: Support SPS/DPS teams in systematically gathering, organizing, analyzing, and disseminating case data daily via epidemiological bulletins, situation reports, and other informational materials.Line-Listing: Assist SPS/DPS teams in compiling and sharing anonymized daily line-listing data from field-level sources.Protocol and Resource Updates: Review, update, disseminate, and share protocols, case definitions, and technical resources for AWD and cholera surveillance.Laboratory Needs Assessment: Conduct a needs assessment to evaluate laboratory capacity, focusing on gaps in human resources and training requirements.Lab Supplies: Provide laboratory reagents and supplies necessary for diagnosing pathogens responsible for AWD and cholera.Sample Logistics: Allocate funding to support the collection and transportation of samples to central laboratories, following standard operating procedures.Capacity Building: Deliver training to field staff on the use of Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs) and sample collection methods. Share best practices for the timely detection, prevention, and management of AWD outbreaks.These activities aim to enhance surveillance, reporting, and response capacities to control and prevent AWD and cholera outbreaks effectively.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will identify protection risks for women, girls, boys, and men through targeted focus group discussions with each group, ensuring their specific concerns and vulnerabilities are addressed. All integrated or joint mission assessments conducted by partners will incorporate the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) approach to ensure inclusive and participatory risk identification.WHO, through the Health Cluster, will work closely with the Protection Cluster to gain a comprehensive understanding of security issues highlighted during monthly field visits. These findings will be analyzed and used to inform the project's design and implementation, ensuring inclusive participation of all groups in project activities.Additionally, all deployed personnel will be trained on WHO’s non-discriminatory policy and the core humanitarian principles to ensure that actions remain impartial, respectful, and aligned with the "do no harm" principle. This approach will promote safe, equitable, and ethical project implementation.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will be implemented in line with the UN’s fundamental principles of equity, transparency, justice, and accountability, ensuring inclusive participation of all affected groups. Field officers involved in the project will be trained and sensitized on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). They will be guided to ensure that no one is discriminated against when accessing training or essential health services in any project area.To address the needs of all age groups, the project will actively gather input through focus group discussions involving diverse populations, including women, elderly persons, adolescent girls, and children. Findings from Community Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) will be systematically used to monitor perceptions and attitudes, with identified gaps promptly addressed. Special attention will be given to the inclusion and empowerment of women and girls, ensuring their voices are heard and their participation prioritized.The project will adhere to the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women and Girls (GEEWG) Policy and its accountability framework. It will follow the guidance in the IASC Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action to integrate gender-sensitive programming into all activities. This includes:• Ensuring accessible, inclusive, and transparent communication to empower affected communities with information for decision-making.• Utilizing feedback mechanisms to enhance accountability and inform adaptive responses.• Creating substantial opportunities for meaningful community engagement, with a focus on women and elderly persons.The project will leverage existing community feedback platforms, such as the Linha Verde hotline managed by WFP, as an inter-agency channel for lodging complaints and providing feedback, including sensitive issues. It will also collaborate with the Mozambique PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Network to integrate PSEA practices into all aspects of the response. This network will provide technical support to clusters, agencies, and partners, ensuring robust protection measures are in place and consistently applied.By fostering inclusive participation and empowering affected individuals, particularly marginalized groups, the project will strengthen meaningful access to assistance and ensure that no one is left behind.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Responders will be taught on how to evaluate people with disabilities, categorize them based on age and gender, and record their information in the proper registries. Community-based APEs will focus on assessing people with disabilities for enrollment in targeted health interventions, including during the risk communication forums for AWD. People with disabilities will take part in the planned assessments.Responders will be trained to identify and assess individuals with disabilities, categorize them by age and gender, and ensure their information is accurately recorded in appropriate registries. Community-based Health Workers (CHWs) will focus on identifying individuals with disabilities for inclusion in targeted health interventions, including those conducted during risk communication forums for Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD). Furthermore, people with disabilities will actively participate in planned assessments to ensure their specific needs and challenges are addressed effectively, enabling equitable access to assistance.
