Project Summary

IRC will deliver Integrated Protection services directly and through partnerships with LNGOs, reaching 239,257 vulnerable conflict-affected individuals in Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir ez Zor governorates.
Providing a comprehensive response to the protection needs of all community members who have been harmed or otherwise exposed to violence to recover and/or connect individuals to services that protect them to promote their healing and reduce their vulnerability.
Interventions will include establishing Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) and Safe Healing and
Learning Spaces (SHLS) combined with outreach teams to provide specialized case management services for GBV survivors and children who have been harmed by abuse, neglect, and exploitation, including supporting CAAFAG children with CPCM and reintegration in the community, providing cash assistance to meet immediate needs, structured and unstructured PSS services, parenting skills training for caregivers, and life skills education for boys and girls.
IRC will regularly monitor the violation of rights and the protection situation in NES to understand the changes over time, to screen and improve access to primary and specialized services and advocate on behalf of vulnerable populations to improve the protection situation in their communities and inform response planning.
Regular assessments and legal analysis on civil housing and property rights will inform legal support, programming, and advocacy. Protection mainstreaming will be within protection activities and other IRC sectors’ activities to ensure safety, access, participation, and accountability. Particular attention
will be given to accessibility and full inclusion for people with disabilities through accessible spaces, inclusive delivery, such as the use of sign language interpreter or having consent forms and other materials in Braille.
IRC additionally delivers capacity building and training about GBV core concept, safe referral, and mainstreaming, CP, PSEA, protection, and humanitarian principles will be delivered to different stakeholders including INGOs staff and LNGOs.
Finally, IRC uses cash for protection in combination with other protection activities especially case management to respond to the most accute and urgent needs.
A Protection Cash program is where:
• Cash has been identified as an appropriate modality to address specific protection threats and has been considered alongside or in combination with other modalities (protection services, case management, etc.)
• The program targets or identifies individuals based on protection-based vulnerability
• The use of cash is designed with the purpose of achieving protection outcomes, and the program monitors and reports on protection outcomes
Cash for protection programming does not include:
• Beneficiaries of a protection program who are unable to meet basic needs are referred to economic recovery and development ERD to receive cash to meet their basic needs, or to other livelihoods activities
• When meeting basic needs (such as food security) is the primary objective of the program, but the program also includes secondary objectives related to meeting protection outcomes to which the CVA is not explicitly linked
• Beneficiaries are no longer included in the protection activities or measured on protection outcomes
While providing our services to people who require hearing aid/sign language support, which is a dwindling small number, these persons bring along family members or acquaintances who help with the communication to address services and needs.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, 14.6 million people require protection services across Syria. In NES significant displacements remain a major issue, as of July 2021, the region’s population of 3 million includes 600,000 IDPs, 84% of whom are experiencing years of prolonged displacement. Civil status documentation lost through multiple displacements is a significant barrier to housing, property rights, and access to humanitarian assistance and puts people at risk of abuse or exploitation. Fears of violence are high among the population of NES, with recurring violence between the Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) and Turkish-backed groups as well as occasional disputes between SDF and Government of Syria (GoS) entities.Sexual violence continues to affect women and girls within and outside their homes, having a distinct impact on their sense of safety and capacity to move freely. Forced and early marriage remains a threat for girls as young as 10, linked to the economic situation and caregivers’ wishes to protect girls from sexual violence by marrying them off. COVID-19 and the economic crisis caused a stark increase in IPV and family violence while 92% of communities still lack specialized GBV services. According to Voices from Syria Women/girls widowed divorced or otherwise, HHs considered particularly a risk of sexual harassment, due to their reliance on paid work.Children comprise almost half of the affected population and grave child rights violations remain a significant concern.

4 - Implementation of activities

CP activities include case management (And CAAFAG), referrals to specialized services, PSS, Social and Emotional learning, parental training, awareness sessions, & CP Committees.GBV activities include case management, referrals to specialized services, individual support sessions, structured and unstructured PSS, awareness sessions, GBV Risk Mitigation activities, support to women-led committees.General Protection activities include case management for Persons with Specific Needs, referrals to specialized services, info desk on available services, PSS for men and boys, and caregivers of PWDs, protection monitoring and protection monitoring reports, legal awareness, and legal consultations.Cash for protection will be integrated to provide cash support to cases identified through all protection streams.Activities are delivered through multiple modalities: Community-based interventions, static programming for sustained delivery of protection services, and mobile teams to flexibly respond to different contexts/environments and adapt to emerging needs.IRC reports on 4W's and use MEAL systems' regular collection, analysis, and reporting of data with quality control and accountability. Outcomes for individual interventions (Case management) will be tracked with satisfaction surveys, and for groups (PSS sessions) via pre/post tests and feedback sessionsClient Responsiveness Mechanisms and data triangulation are activated to collect concerns, needs, or feedback on programs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Community stigmatization and discrimination of children, survivors receiving specialized services such as case management services.Mitigation: Use community acceptance strategy, including through the delivery of non-specialized services as entry-point and trust-building with the community.Ensure specialized services are properly messaged to the community.Embed specialized services in broader service-provision to de-stigmatization, using confidential spaces.Risk: Community tensions resulting from expectations of service delivery following protection monitoring and by asking sensitive questions, also placing NGO and its staff at risk.Mitigation: Train staff on communication and protection monitoring.Ensure information from protection monitoring is disseminated with relevant stakeholders so that any information collected is adequately acted upon.Review protection monitoring questions and adapt to context.Risk: Barriers to beneficiaries' access to protection services due to physical impediments/location as well as normative restrictions to access.Mitigation: Conduct inclusion analysis, deploy community outreach teams to support referrals of vulnerable individuals, and integrate protection service delivery across other sector responses.Safety and security risks to staff and persons of concern; mitigated through regular security mapping and updates and adjusting movement plans.Covid 19 related risks and infection; by observing Covid 19 SOPs and protective measures.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment of women and girls clients by staff and volunteers. Likelihood: Medium/Low Impact: Very HighMitigation: IEC materials for beneficiaries explaining reporting channels for beneficiaries to report PSEA and ASH. IRC adheres to a strong internal code of conduct related to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding beneficiaries, especially children, as well as a safe environment forreporting that allows for anonymity. Each year, all staff attend a full-day IRC Way Code of Conduct Training that includes discussions on NGO’s commitments to beneficiaries and NGO’s zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. IRC will work with partners utilizing their existing beneficiary mechanisms to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and ensure protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and accountability to beneficiaries and other stakeholders. This will include capacity-building activities with partners to reach these standards as necessary.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Change of context and limited access to the supported sites.Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: Assess the situation, maintain a link with local stakeholders and facilitate negotiations through the IRC Humanitarian Access team.The IRC recruits local staff from targeted communities who will be able to run facilities and provide essential life-saving services.Risk: Security conditions and COVID-19 disrupt activities and limit client access to sites.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigation: Project activities allow for adaptive management in line with internal contingency plans related to each operational area. Ongoing security planning during the project will ensure the activities can continue to adapt to the security environment and access context, with Humanitarian Access Teams providing regular security assessments. During periodic access constraints, IRC will strengthen implementation through community-based focal points. Remote service modalities will also be used as needed, building from our experience in 2021.Risk: Cash assistance can increase Protection risks or exploitation.Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: IRC only provides cash for protection along protection services especially case management. Wherever exploitation or increased tension/Violence in the household due to cash assistance is detected, it should not be provided to adhere to the do no harm principle. A larger plan should be put in place to support the family long-term.
