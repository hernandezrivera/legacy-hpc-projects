Project Summary

5 years after the signing of revitalized peace agreement South Sudan,Unity State, Mayendit county continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions attributed to public health hitches like measles & cholera outbreaks, climatic shocks, endemic violence, conflicts, access constraints & operational interference that have pessimistic impact to people’s livelihoods hampering access to education & water, sanitation & hygiene & health services thus negatively impacting the nutrition situation with more children acutely malnourished. The influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan further compounded the humanitarian needs.
2023 FSNMS & SMART survey’s findings, notes national GAM levels remained above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.5 million children & women at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024 & will need urgent treatment with 46 out of 79 counties at GAM above 15% (Cluster 2024 HPC (draft)).
2023 HNO projected 9.4 million people in need including 1.9 million IDPS, I.4 returnees, 5.8 million host & 337,00 refugees. Among them 2.2 million women & 4.9 million girls & 2.5 boys equivalent to 15% of the total population.
According to November 2023 IPC, April – July 2024, lean season projects the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 7.1 million people 56.3% of the population at IPC Phase 3 or above. 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) while an estimated 2.34 million people likely to be in Phase 4 (Emergency) with 8 counties of Unity state at IPC AMN phase4.
July 2023- June 2024 IPC Projections notes, an estimated 1.65 million children 6 – 59 months (SAM,0.48 million &MAM 1.16 million, & 870,000 PLW) are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and are in urgent need of nutrition services.
Through 2024 HRP, UNIDOR seeks to ensure equitable access and utilization of timely, qualitative & quantitative lifesaving services to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women & people with disabilities through provision of integrated nutrition services reaching at least 80% of SAM, 75% MAM and 60% PLW in mayendit county targeting total of 34,173 beneficiaries. Through management and treatment of 2,417 boys and girls with SAM 5,907 boys &girls with MAM and 2,898 PLW through OPT/TSFP static sites and outreaches per the cluster objective two.
In line with the cluster objective one, 10,417 will be reached with MIYCN counselling (6,981 caregivers of children 0-23 months and 3,490 PLW) integrated with preventive package.14,824 & 12,217 boys & girls will receive Vitamin A and Deworming respectively while 3,525 children 6-59months and 2,938 will be reached with BSFP in prevention of malnutrition with an estimated budget of $1,469,847.
UNIDOR will enhance the component of surveillance through continuous program monitoring that will include Mass MUAC screening, SMART surveys & supporting in FNSMS rounds in line with cluster objective3.
Also, UNIDOR will strengthen integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening, EPI &GMP distribution of LLITN), WASH (provision of safe water, soap, education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL on kitchen gardening & cooking demonstration, Education though provision of age appropriate toys and child friendly spaces. Protection through referral pathways and GBV safety audits and feedback mechanism through establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complaint desks, and community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback in line cluster objective 4

1. Needs

Communities in Mayendit continue to face public health challenges like measles and cholera outbreaks climatic shocks with rainfall above the normal ranges consecutively for the 4 years since 2019 with recurrent shocks of intensified violent conflict. The situation deteriorated further in the lean season April – July 2023 with heavy rainfalls resulting in flooding with 353 villages out of 365 affected by floods. County officials estimate 33305 (M:13594,F:19711) people (7369 HH) been affected by flooding since last year 2022, and some of them are now facing a 2nd displacement. Influx of returnees from Sudan after the war in April 2023 with an estimated 3,077 individuals received in mayendit further caused distress to the already affected households hit by the intra-communal conflict in March & April 2022 (August 2023 IRNA).REACH/IRC March 2023 SMART survey notes GAM of 20.5% (16.8-24.7, 95% CI) above the WHO emergency threshold while 2024 PIN cites GAM prevalence of 21.2% (SAM 4.4%,MAM,16.8% ,PLW-AMN19.6) and at severity 4. April – July 2023 IPC projection analysis affirms, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with 43,000 people likely to face IPC 5 levels of acute food insecurity, & 7,000 people likely remain in IPC 5 catastrophe. Btwn July-Sept, the situation expected to further get worse with mayendit among the 9 counties of Unity state at IPC AMN 4 (Critical). In the worst scenario, Mayardit might fall into IPC AMN 5 (Extremely Critical) with populations in Catastrophe (IPC 5). The most at-risk being women, children & the elderly faced with large food gaps and likely starvation if humanitarian food assistance is not provided. June 2022 Rapid Needs Assessment by IRC noted high food insecurity, with only 39% of households having ‘Acceptable’ Food Consumption Score, 18% borderline and the majority 43% are having ‘Poor’ FSC. Similarly, due to food insecurity, the coping strategies among households varied; 61% of households borrowed food, 53% reduced the number of meals eaten in a day, 42% skipped a whole day without eating, 44% restricted consumptions by adults in favor of children, 33% limited portions at meals times, 25% gathered wild food, 14% purchased food on credit and 10% consumed seed stock held for the season among other negative coping mechanisms with households in depending highly on humanitarian assistance. The floods have affected the crop harvest, and subsequently resulted in a high cereal deficit for the 2022/2023 consumption year.In addition, the current nutrition situation has multi-dimensional aggravating factors, such as severe food insecurity, high child morbidity, low health services, the occurrence of concurrent malnutrition aggravating factors as increasing food insecurity, poor access to WASH & health services & climatic shocks such as recurrent flooding are likely to impact negatively nutrition outcomes. Its worthy noting that, persistent poor macroeconomic conditions, currency devaluation, reduced market access and functionality played a role in the deterioration of food security situation leading to increased malnutrition status of the most vulnerable children U5 boys & girls, adolescents & adult & PLWs with women carrying out many roles, affecting negatively the amount of time they are able to spend feeding and caring for their children, which consequently increases children’s risk of malnutrition. UNIDOR in collaboration with other Humanitarian actors seek to ensure continued nutrition support through 12 OTP/TSFP sites,4 outreaches and 1 SC to the affected populations factoring in promotion of equity & access in participation of men, women, boys and girls in need assessment. UNIDOR is Education, FSL, WASH, CP & Peace partner with a well-established base hence the advantage to deliver multi-system approach to reduce all foams of malnutrition.

behaviors change messaging to mothers/caregivers of children 0-59 months and pregnant women through mother support groups, individual and group counselling at the community and facility• Provide diarrhea cases in collaboration with health. • Capacity strengthening, Job training and knowledge sharing to nutrition and health workers and the CHD staff on CMAM, MIYCN integrated with the preventive package and reporting. • Provision of preventive packages (Soaps, water purifiers & treated mosquito nets • Integration of ECD in the OTP/TSFP sites for stimulation by ensuring availability of Child friendly spaces with age-appropriate toys• Set up kitchen demonstration gardens and conduct cooking demonstrations to impact on dietary diversification with school feeding programs to improve on optimal nutrition services. • Collaboration with Protection to ensure mainstreaming of GBV referral pathways and support on GBV safety audits at the nutrition facilities. • Strengthen
