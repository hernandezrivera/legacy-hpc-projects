Project Summary

An estimated 5,573,193 South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2020. GBI seeks to provide a 12 months Integrated lifesaving & Resilience WASH services and Improved access to WASH facilities for vulnerable and crisis affected populations in South Sudan (Northern Bahr el Ghazal: Aweil East and Aweil North, Jonglei: Duk, Twic East, Bor South, Ayod & Pibor, Central Equatoria: Terekeka and Juba and Eastern Equatoria: Kapoeta North and South, Ikotos and Lakes: Awerial and Yirol East counties.
In line with the call by the WASH cluster and 2020 HRP WASH cluster objectives, GBI is proposing to scale up an integrated lifesaving and resilience WASH response in these locations through supporting functional nutrition sites, health centers and schools’ sanitation, hygiene and water facilities as well as rehabilitation of dysfunctional water points and disseminating key WASH messages. The project will support a total of 40,000 beneﬁciaries (men, 10%, women, 50%, boys, 15% and girls, 25%) targeted directly with integrated lifesaving WASH, GBV, Health and Nutrition interventions, along with sanitation and hygiene promotion activities at household level. The project will target IDPs, Host communities, Returnees and other vulnerable members of the community that may not fall under these categories, broken down as (10800, 16000, 11022 and 2178 respectively).

The overall objective of this project is to contribute to the reduction of mortality, morbidity and impacts of acute malnutrition due to poor sanitation and hygiene amongst children under ﬁve and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) through management of acute malnutrition as well as building their resilience. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with the current food insecurity and poor WASH conditions in the proposed Counties, and it also will complement existing WASH, Health, GBV and Nutrition integrated activities that implementing partners and GBI are doing in the proposed Counties. Increase access to Water, Sanitation and Hygiene for all, paying attention to the needs of malnourished children, GBV protection for women, adolescent girls and those in vulnerable situation. The project will require $ 800,000 to provide the intended services and activities throughout the intervention period.

1 - Needs

Water is a necessity, and an important resource for sustaining life. The decline in water quality endangers the health of humans as well as the ecosystem. Clean drinking water, hygiene, and sanitation play an important part in maintaining health. Contaminated water causes many waterborne infections like diarrhea and serves as a carrier for vectors such as mosquitoes spreading epidemics. Open defecation means no sanitation. It fouls the environment, and spreads diseases. The protracted conflict and economic crisis experienced in the country have left millions of people without sufficient public health and WASH services and made them more susceptible to diseases and malnutrition. One primary health center serves some 50,000 people and 60 per cent of nutrition sites do not have access to clean water (WHO March, 2019). According to the IPC for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) scale, all counties in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Jonglei (except Pibor), Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Lafon in Eastern Equatoria State, Awerial and Yirol West, Lakes were classified as (IPC AMN Phase 4). Malnutrition is driven by poor access to public health, poor access to and availability of food due to dysfunctional markets and reduced farm production, and poor caregiving practices on food utilization. Without sufficient access to health services, emergency nutrition support and clean water, infectious diseases spread in food emergencies (WHO March, 2019). The protracted conflict that began in December 2013 continues to affect the already poor rate of access to safe water in South Sudan, placing children at increased risk of waterborne diseases such as diarrhea and cholera (UNICEF March, 2019). According to the UNICEF/WHO 2017 Joint Monitoring Programme, over 61 per cent of the population of South Sudan practice open defecation, and the few existing sanitation facilities are mostly damaged. WASH assessment conducted by GBI in June this year indicated that 71% HHs practice open defecation while 76% didn’t own latrines. Another assessment in Aweil East in Northern el Ghazal indicated a worrying WASH situation with 85% and 86% practicing open defecation and no latrines respectively. GBI is currently implementing Integrated WASH and Nutrition project targeting 1,600 SAM children and over 17,000 individuals in communities with safe water and sanitation in Twic East County, Jonglei state and an integrated FSL providing assistance to 6,667 households in 2019/2020 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal. In line with the call for proposals and 2020 HRP WASH Cluster objectives, GBI is planning to scale up in greater Jonglei state, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central and Eastern Equatoria where we have operational presence. Activities integrating WASH, FSL, Health, Protection and Nutrition programming at households, institutions and community level such GBV mitigation, reduction of malnutrition in children U5, outbreak control as well as provision of timely, equitable access to water hygiene and sanitation will be implemented throughout the response period.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Through project design and community mobilization beneficiaries will be contacted to give their inputs and a group representing them is always established at boma level. The project will be implemented with the help of local authority who makes the organization accountable to the beneficiaries. The beneficiaries have the right to their entitlements as they appear in the project, they have right to choose distribution locations, right to receive right information about the project and the right take part in key decision-making process.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will target IDPs alongside host communities and returnees. IDPs are always part of intervention and considered the most vulnerable people by the virtue of being displaced from their homes and having lost their belongings. The project allocates 27% to IDPs as a contingency plan and also target the already existing displaced populations in the target locations. In 2020, the intervention will focus on IDPs affected by floods since most locations selected here have been gravely affected by flooding as many are displaced and their livelihoods destroyed.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Government is key stakeholder in the humanitarian intervention in South Sudan. The project will work with local community through chiefs, local authority through payam administrators, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), Local government departments such as ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation, ministry of labor and human resources development, Community established AAP committees etc. Before project kick off, entry meetings are held with each available structure to share project information with them and relevant authorities so as to guard the beneficiaries and ensure accountability to affected populations. Throughout the implementation, the government is part of distribution, monitoring of progress and challenges as well as project close out.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The activities in the project are aligned with South Sudan WASH Cluster Strategic Operational Framework (SOF) which involves enhancement of AAP and quality insurance, prioritization of urban and rural WASH needs, capacity building, integrated approach by improving needs based and protection sensitive access to WASH services for IDPs and GBV mitigation. The project will target IDPs 27% and 27.6 % returnees as well as provision of social protection to vulnerable households by considering age groups and different gender categories.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Whilst overall access has improved in the context of the recently revitalized comprehensive peace agreement, insecurity in some areas still impedes humanitarian organizations from reaching people in need. Though skeptical, humanitarian community in South Sudan is optimistic that increased dialogue between warring parties will help enhance access and thus enable the humanitarian organizations to reach the most vulnerable population in the country in 2020. Lack or limited infrastructure may result in inadequate, irregular or delayed assistance due to limited access to people in need. The project will also observe the humanitarian principle of “Do No Harm” in the project implementation by not causing drastic changes to the existing community setup as a result of the project activities. Beneficiaries and staff safety will be prioritized and strictly observed.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure the issue of sustainability and handover of activities to the communities, the project will prioritize capacity building, engage community structures and local government at all stages of the project life cycle. At all stages of the project implementation the communities are required to provide their contribution to cancel out over dependency on aid by providing inputs at their disposal for project implementation and takeover the ownership of the project outputs at close out. The project will train communities on management of created community assets e.g. Water User Committees, pump mechanics, capacity building to Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) on management of acute malnutrition, training women in crop production on small vegetable kitchen gardens and caregiving practices. The project will also form and train school hygiene clubs on practices of safe hygiene and sanitation practices. All rehabilitated or drilled borehole is handed over to the management of community to do maintenance after the humanitarian partner is gone.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Male and female, women and men or boys and girls are differently affected by humanitarian crisis, situation or disaster requiring an intervention. Community mobilization brings together all people of different sex, age group and status to decide for themselves as far as humanitarian assistance is concerned. The project will prioritize the needs of women and girls by involving them in key decision-making processes and by being part of AAP so as to mitigate the risk of being left out on grounds of cultural practices that don’t allow women and girls to participate in community work. Forced taxation of Humanitarian Assistance is mitigated by seeking letter of clearance from Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Juba for humanitarian items moving from Juba to field locations. The risk is also transferred to third party by giving transportation work to logistics companies which take the responsibility of safe delivery of items. While delivering within the locality security reports or situations that might arise to forced taxation are analyzed before authorizing delivery. Movement of staff are planned to take place between 9:00am – 4pm when security is guaranteed – no travels allowed. Normally, the local authority and local leaders try to corrupt the process of identification of beneficiaries by bringing their close relatives instead of vulnerable people in need of assistance or even having themselves registered. To mitigate targeting of wrong beneficiaries GBI conducts community mobilization before identification of beneficiaries to inform them about the selection criteria. We also work with Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Committees established at Boma level to help in identification of beneficiaries. GBI also works in close coordination with relevant government ministries who are aware of the humanitarian work.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Before distribution, beneficiaries are informed about their entitlements, rights and contribution towards the project. The AAP committees formed are used as channel through which beneficiaries who can't present their own complaints can speak to the organization. Rapid Post Distribution Monitoring (RPDM), end user surveys, exit interviews are conducted for beneficiaries to express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the project and through one on one or face-to-face discussion with beneficiaries. Beneficiaries are also provided with suggestion boxes through which they can share their complaints or feedback i.e. for those who know how to read and write. GBI has so far existing AAP committees formed in Aweil East and Twic East representing over 60000 beneficiaries being targeted with food security and livelihoods and WASH'Nutrition activities. They are picked from bomas and each village is represented by a member to ensure inclusiveness of population in decision making processes. Gender issues are also addressed through AAP committees by ensuring gender equality in selection of members.
