Project Summary

IBC has been operating health clinic No. 1 in Al-Bab since September 2019. Our needs analyses within the Soran region between 3 October and 24 October show that displaced people (IDPs) continue to be extremely vulnerable due to health clinics unable to actively operate in the region, and their numbers continue to increase. Thousands of internally displaced people receive barely health aid. The city of Soran is located north of the city of Aleppo, 23 km from the city of Azaz, and is directly administered by the local council of the city of Soran.
The total population: 28000 residents in the city and 86000 in the camps and villages surrounding the city (Al-Bel 1, Al-Bel 2, the villages of Al-Adiya village and Al-Daheriya village...).
In the city center, there are two clinics, one run by an IDA organization. We have a close relationship with IDA. The second center is a primary health care center in the city supported by the Turkish Health Directorate, and it is the center that needs support. Most of the city's residents are refugees who suffer from very poor living conditions.
Most of the poor people cannot go to the neighboring areas Because going to areas that have a health service far away, such as Azaz or Marea. Since there are no other health clinics nearby, it is very crucial to support the Soran clinic.
In the last three months, the number of patients has decreased due to a shortage of doctors, a shortage of nurses, the resignation of the midwife and the lack of medicines. There is a dental clinic, which is the living clinic in the city that provides the free service.
There are 5,500 beneficiaries per month of the health center services when the service is available.
The equipment's are available in the PHC. According to the needs analysis conducted by IBC, if the health clinic is supported regularly, the capacity of the clinic will be increased and the number of beneficiaries will increase by 35%. The people living in the region will have regular access to health facilities.
Energy sources: a local electricity network and there is a backup generator.
The local council, the clinic administration and the Turkish Health Directorate are very cooperative.

Launching of vaccinations for around 20,000 children under this district for polio and other basic epidemics provision of reproductive health services. IBC will start process of project implementation as soon as possible after approval of project, and its planned to finish in 2 months to contract health centers in Soran town. Health Clinic will start to give service for the beneficiaries. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Most targeted households have female or child under 18 years of old head of household because the males in the household had to go fight or look for better economic opportunities elsewhere. In the village camps especially there are a number of persons living with disabilities and many cases of social violence & further protection risks due to the high number of orphans in the camps and their exploitation by political actors, competition for resources sometimes leads to distributed items getting taken from those in need and for sale in another area. To mitigate these protection risks, IBC makes analyzes in the field. All records of support provided within the scope of the protocol we signed with the local council and the Syrian Task Force department will be shared transparently with the donor. In addition to the information obtained, we will ensure that the support offered by the project reaches the target beneficiaries through periodic analyzes and household visits in the field. We are also one of the few non-governmental organizations in the region that use the guidance mechanism effectively. We will make sure that the support offered in the project reaches all people in need mostly through snowball effect & raising protection risks among community. Otherwise, we will make the necessary interventions and inform the project stakeholders at every step. We'll hire female employees in the field & ensure that all employees receive training in GBV and health referrals.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Over half the population in Soran is female (55%), and 30% of the total population is children under 18 years old, with many children and female heads of household especially in the village camps. Child and early forced marriage, as well as abuse from unequal power balance between ''givers'' and ''receivers'' of project support. To combat these unequal balance of power, IBC follows its own PSEA, whistleblower, and complaints and response mechanism SOPs which were created in line with IASC standards. In the project planning stage, IBC consults with community members and stakeholders on best modes of communication in order to effectively articulate our commitment to PSEA and what our strategy is based on for the targeted communities. After consultation, IBC will incorporate the input of beneficiaries and stakeholders when starting the project, and ensure that everyone involved with the project is knowledgeable about IBC code of conduct and other relevant SOPs- with information freely available, as well as how the CRM system will work to be as inclusive as possible.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security and access are the two main issues that may be beyond the project's control. These can be affected by violence, political shocks, or epidemics. Öncüpınar gate will be used for transportation to the health clinic. MoU has been signed with local authorities for the easy implementation of this project. If emergencies and violence arise / breaks out, the steps to be followed by all employees will be implemented in accordance with the IBC safety/security procedures booklet. The safety of all employees will be considered a priority issue. Our health clinic will continue to actively use the referral mechanism for emergency cases to reach hospitals.
