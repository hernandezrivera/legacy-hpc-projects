Project Summary

Using its rapid response mechanism, SCSD proposes a 12-month action uses a flexible integrated programming approach that includes emergency and regular food distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind and food voucher assistance modalities.
This program is a planned action, submitted to a Donor agency for funding, contributes to the first sector objective of the FSL cluster which is "Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance" and aims to address the immediate food security needs of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and affected host communities with acute needs, living in the sub districts of Afrin, Atareb, Jebel Saman and Daret Azza (Aleppo Governorate), and the sub districts of Bennsh, Idleb, Salqin, Armanaz and Ariha (Idleb Governorate) in NW Syria, in both the short to medium term.
In the short-term, SCSD is planning to reach 3,000 HHs/18,000 individuals with 6,000 in-kind RTERs based on their needs and reported situation. This designed to be one-off distribution for exceptional cases in which beneficiaries do not have access to cooking facilities.
Equally, to meet the food security needs in the medium-term, SCSD aims to assist 4,500 vulnerable internally displaced, returnees and affected host communities’ HHs (27,000 individuals) that meet the Food Security Program beneficiary eligibility criteria with regular monthly food assistance through the most appropriate modalities. This will include provide 2,000 HHs/12,000 individuals, living in areas where markets are inaccessible, or food is unavailable or unaffordable with in-kind food baskets for eight consecutive months, and restricted food vouchers to a further 2,500 HHs/15,000 individuals with access to functioning markets in 8 distribution rounds, as recommended by the FSL Cluster and WFP.
In the framework of this project, SCSD will identify households who do not have the financial means to purchase food to meet their survival basic needs, priority will be given to newly IDP households from southern Idleb governorate, households headed by single or widowed women, unaccompanied minors, persons with disability and elderly persons. Indirect beneficiaries of this proposed project will be SCSD selected shopkeepers, vendors and suppliers that will benefit from a more stable income throughout the life of the program, as well as improve their capacity to assess and meet local market demand.
Throughout this program, SCSD will identify the unique needs of men, women, girls and boys and seek to maximize equality of treatment and access to services for all of these groups. Feedback collected through community assessments and monitoring and evaluation will regularly inform SCSD program design and implementation.
The proposed targeted districts are currently facing a funding gap in Food Security Program, leaving many people in need without assistance.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 45,000 individuals (18,000 individuals provided with one-time emergency food assistance and 27,000 individuals supported with regular monthly food assistance).

4 - Needs

Nine years into the crisis, according to the FFS HNO 2020, the number of food insecure people has increased from 6.5M (2019) to 7.9M (2020), approximately 20% increase from last year. At least 37% of the food insecure population are in NW Syria where approximately 2M people rely on monthly food assistance due to persist conflict in Idleb since April 2019. Of the total PiN, the ratio of total male to female is 51 to 49, with a substantial number of children (at least 42%) while adults and elders represent 54 and 4% of the PiN respectively. The national average price of a standard reference food basket at USD 77 based on formal exchange rates is more than 8 times higher than pre-crisis levels. By December 2019, overall the Syrian pound has depreciated by more than 1700% since the crisis began, highlighting the overall impact on market prices & reducing purchasing power as food prices remain high.Moreover, according to CCCM Snapshot of IDPs in NW Syria 2/2/2020, since the 1st of December 2019, more than 586 K have been displaced mainly from the southern Idleb governorate. The displaced HHs were basically from Ma’arrat An Nu’man & Saraqeb Sub Distrıcts moving further north away from the hostilities to Dana, Afrin, A'zaz, Atreb, Idleb, and Maaret Tamisrin Sub Districts. This is raising the total displaced people in NW Syria to 2.5M (887,887 in Aleppo & 1,618,965 in Idleb).At household level, the Food Consumption Score (FCS) has shown that 20% of the beneficiary HHs had poor FCS and 29% had borderline FCS despite receiving food assistance. The food consumption-based reduced Coping Strategies Index (rCSI) has been found at 15.4 in Idleb and 16 in Aleppo. The most frequently cited coping strategies are: relying on less preferred, less expensive food, borrowing food or relying on help from friends and relatives; reducing the number of meals eaten per day and or the portions size; and reduction in the quantities consumed by adults/ mothers in order to feed young children.

5 - Implementation of activities

1. SCSD is planning to reach 3,000 newly displaced HHs/18,000 individuals with RTERs within 72 hours of displacement, to cover the needs of a 6- members’ HHs for one month. RTERs will be designed following the SPHERE standards and FSL cluster recommendations and aims to meet 2100 kcal/person/day for 5 days. 2. Provide 2,000 HHs/12,000 individuals with in-kind food baskets for eight consecutive months, and restricted food vouchers with value 60 USD per HH per month based on SMEB estimates & FSL cluster recommendations to a further 2,500 HHs/15,000 individuals in 8 distribution rounds. Regardless the modality that will be employed, the ration (food basket) will be designed following the SPHERE standards and FSL cluster recommendations of 70% or 1550 of the 2,100 kcal/person/day, with 12% of total energy provided by protein, 29% provided by fat and 59% provided by carbohydrates with inclusion of required minerals.To ensure the most vulnerable HHs are selected, SCSD conducts a socio-economic vulnerability assessment scoring exercise based on aggregated vulnerability scores. the modality of response is determined by market assessments. Specifically, the market assessment will look into aspects related to shops, capacity, competition, accessibility, prices and supply chain. If markets are functional, SCSD prefers to use food voucher modalities which has consistently been preferred by SCSD beneficiaries in distribution and post-distribution monitoring activities and has shown positive impacts on the local markets and vendors. In cases where markets are not functional, inaccessible or unaffordable, SCSD selects in-kind support. The most marginalized beneficiaries will be eligible for additional support (such as home delivery). Each distribution will be followed by PDM. SCSD will coordinate with the other FSL respective partners to minimize gap and avoid duplication in the response, and report its activities to the FSL cluster on monthly basis using the 4Ws matrix formats.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women & girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts & Address any SEA complaints. Risk2: Tension between host communities, IDPs & returnees. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status, Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed, engage with the communities to sensitize the population, Make effort to target a % of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs, Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be and Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. Risk3: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or cancelled, Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding, Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions and crowded management, Application of checklist at distribution sites, Ensure that food/vouchers distribution are completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight, Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, Communicate with national and local authorities, stakeholders and community leaders, Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women and Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs. Risk4: For cash and vouchers there is the risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Strengthen over-sight mechanism, develop a range of measures and internal procedures to minimize the risk of corruption.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: SCSD has full access to the targeted communities and the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the program. Risk: the escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in targeted area affecting the SCSD’s ability to operate and access it. Mitigation: SCSD will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities, Hiring of field staff from the local community of the targeted areas, Purchase of materials from the local market with guarantee the Do No Harm principles, Communicate, coordinate and consultation intensively with LCs, stakeholders and other NGOs operating in the areas, Re-direction of programming into other areas. Assumption2: Aid delivery continues to be accepted and possible in Syria despite of the presence of various armed groups. Risk: Implementation of the durable activities is prevented/stopped by regular clashes, violence, or an upsurge in criminality including theft or kidnapping. Mitigation: SCSD will monitor the security developments and reported criminal instances/violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation. If an increase in criminality/violence is noted, alternative routes will be used and additional warehouses will be rented to mitigate against the possibility of an armed group seizing control of all of SCSD stock inside Syria, or consider alternative areas/activities under the planned intervention. Assumption3: Markets are functioning properly in the areas identified. Risk: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/or supplies. Mitigation: SCSD will conduct a Rapid Assessment for Markets in the initial stage of the program and situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. In case the situation would change, SCSD would consider relocating the area of intervention where the required conditions are met.
