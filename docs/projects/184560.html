Project Summary

Somalia is facing a growing number of threats to its food security. The ongoing desert locust invasion, socio-economic effects of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic and recurrent climate shocks, including frequent flooding and drought, continue to severely impact the lives and livelihoods of the vulnerable population. With this multifaceted crises, 7.7 Million people across Somalia need in emergency food assistance. With humanitarian needs expected to further increase in 2022, it is critical to enhance immediate food access, protect and restore livelihoods and strengthen the resilience of vulnerable households. Sustained humanitarian assistance is required to prevent the further deterioration of food security and nutrition.
Through this project, Plan International and Partners will effectively provide emergency assistance for food and promote dignity and choices among project participants by increasing purchasing power with short term injection of unconditional cash transfers for 1182 HH (7092 beneficiaries; 3546F, 3546M) for six months. Through field monitoring, government reports, and sector coordination, Plan and Partners will target households in areas identified as IPC3 or worse, risk of drought, and/or IDP communities, including those recently evicted by Somaliland government. These families have insufficient income to cover rising cost of food particularly during periods of drought and other shocks due to COVID19, instability and climate change, and will qualify per criteria, e.g. female headed household, household with person with disability, etc. Partners will ensure the crises affected families receive messages on Infant, Young Child Feeding and Hygiene and have access to sufficient, safe and nutritious foods to meet their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life. Partners have complementary programs running in these areas to enable consultant and selection within communities. By targeting vulnerable families particularly with children and delivering this intervention in a gender sensitive manner, Plan strives for secondary outcomes of improved the protection environment with reduced risks to women and girls.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

For Plan International whenever appropriate and possible, cash or voucher assistance should be the preferred form of aid over in-kind. In markets contexts where supply is adequate or prices are not dramatically inflated, providing cash or vouchers will save lives. Distribution team must investigate the gender considerations in order to ensure decisions made for CTP prioritize improving wellbeing of women and girls. From our 2020 response, we found multipurpose cash assistance to be good practice and an effective conduit to assist people in IPDs in Somaliland, and it boosted the economy, protecting the dignity while reducing social gatherings.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

In consultation with the Cash Working Group and other sectors in Hargeisa, Plan will ensure sufficient assessment of market functionality. This will determine whether target population would be able to procure and meet basic needs with what is locally available or if there are supply gaps, issues of inflation, or another reason to provide in-kind.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

As interventions will be only in Somaliland, not Somalia - highest risks are anticipated as following with planned mitigation: - Plan will train on child safeguarding policy and ensure staff, partner staff, vendors, frontline workers, etc sign and commit to PSEA policy - People with disabilities given special consideration for design/selection - Careful design and proper planning of needs, time, location – assess unique risks and barriers to participation - Include community members in project design and implementation - Include community members in selection criteria and process - Prioritize activities specifically for children with disabilities - Spot check at construction sites to ensure no risks to children or community - Ensure clear reporting/complaint process is displayed on site - Increased conflict sensitivity in programming - Careful selection and proper planning based on time and location - assess risks prior to activity, particularly distributions to ensure appropriateness of location and other considerations - Plan will also take environmental impact into consideration across project implementation with mitigation measures - collection of waste post distribution, long-term durable or recycleable materials as possible
