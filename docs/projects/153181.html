Project Summary

PAH will employ integrated emergency response strategy to meet the educational needs of the learners in the affected locations, focus will be on providing lifesaving safe and dignified learning facilities and services to learners.
In order to provide quality education interventions to the targeted population PAH will focus on implementation with community involvement through the approach described below:
• Recruitment and training or refresher of teachers/facilitators to ensure teachers are knowledgeable of the content and methodologies to deliver lessons. In this appeal PAH will recruit, train, and support teachers to provide learning opportunities to children and young people in the identified areas.
• PTA members will be mobilized and engaged to encourage a sense of ownership within the communities. Teachers and PTA members will be trained on school management and other cross cutting issues including protection issues, corporal, inclusion of people with disabilities, this is to promote a safe and enabling learning environment for all children attending lessons.
• This appeal will also aim to provide scholastic resources and materials to facilitate learning and recovery among learners and increase the level of enrollment and prevent school dropouts due to lack of materials and facilities. This will include materials such as textbooks, notepads, pencils, uniforms, bags, and schools in a box for the learners. To strengthen the education system teachers will also be supported with necessary tools needed so as to do their job such as teaching materials (books, chalks, teaching learning aids etc). This is to promote effective delivery of lessons to the learners.
• To promote a safe and favourable learning environment this project will also focus on improvement of temporary learning spaces. This will be especially for schools were there are reported high influx of IDPs or in areas affected by conflict whose school infrastructures have been destroyed, much attention will also focus on those areas where learners attend lessons in the open. This will be connected with Conditional General Food Distribution project as part of the integration with food security project.
• PAH shall also ensure through this appeal addressing key issues affecting schools/learning especially water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs which if not addressed will affect the enrolment and continuity of learners in school. As lack of basic things such as water, sanitation and poor hygiene can lead to diseases outbreak that pose the children and risk of such. PAH will work to renovate and rehabilitate WASH facilities in the schools, this will involve training of teachers on basic knowledge on how to address WASH issues in the school. Hygiene clubs shall also be established in the school to promote good hygiene among the learners to keep them safe from water borne diseases.
• Focus will also be on menstrual hygiene management, (MHM) PAH will also conduct MHM awareness sessions and training in the schools and provide access to sanitary pads and products to help girls of menstruating age meet there needs. This is to prevent girls from missing lessons and or drop out of school.
• PAH education project will focus on integration with other sectors within PAH, including WASH, Protection, FSL, Nutrition and with other partners where applicable. Including cash component to supplement school materials will be considered where markets are available.

Needs

Conflict in South Sudan continues to impact many parts of the country, which is affecting all programmes involving schools in terms of safe access, quality and safe living environment. At the national level the primary net enrolment stands at 44 % due to multiple factors which are exacerbated by the protracted conflict. Needs assessments conducted by education cluster partners indicated that the root causes of school dropout include limited access and total lack of scholastic facilities. This includes lack of trained and qualified teachers, lack of learning and teaching materials. Education quality is very poor, as teachers are underpaid, meaning that the most qualified teachers seek employment elsewhere. The current status of the teachers comprises of untrained or under qualified government or volunteer teachers, many of whom may have not completed secondary or primary education, many do not have sufficient knowledge of the language of instruction. Subject knowledge is very poor and the lack of textbooks and other teaching materials coupled with the poor learning environment, hostilities and economic hardships makes it much more difficult. Out of 5,883 schools in the country, 1,066 are reported as not functioning. This puts children aged 3 18 years (47.75 % of total population displaced) at risk of losing out in accessing education opportunities. 400,000 children estimated to have dropped out of school due to the conflict, millions unable to access basic education and high numbers of children forced into destructive labour and recruited into armed groups. Many children stay idle, particularly in conflict affected states and counties and displacement sites. They become at risk of turning to vices such as drinking alcohol and looting, some become street children. Denied the choice of schooling, such children are also vulnerable to being recruited by the militias groups. Girls and young women on the move and living in displacement sites are at increased risk of SGBV, and girls across the country are more likely to drop out of formal education to take up caring duties in the home or to marry early and begin a family.Children and adolescents experience high levels of trauma and stress but without education, the affected children cannot access any psycho social social support and life skills training opportunities. Schools also lack sanitary facilities and clean and safe water, which exposes the children to the risk of diseases due to poor hygiene and sanitation. The main water sources in some of the learning facilities are the river and swamps with few boreholes within the communities. There is also high risk of girls dropping out of school due to lack of good menstrual hygiene management facilities or spaces to help girls manage their period during menstruation. Many of the girls of school age cannot afford menstrual hygiene products, there are also a lot of myths attached to menstruation across different communities in South Sudan. In addition, lack of awareness/sensitization and the fact that it is not being talked about openly exposes the girls to enormous challenges in accessing learning.

Activities

• Teacher professional development including training on EiE (child centered approaches to pedagogy, classroom management, lesson planning and cross cutting issues), child well-being (physical protection and PSS) and life skills; this will also include in classroom coaching. Training of PTA and SMCs on school management and other cross cutting issues including protection issues, psycho-social support, inclusion of people with disabilities.• Paying teacher incentives, payment of these incentives for volunteer teachers not on the government payroll in IO areas and displacement sites, where the population is captive (this would be considered in Koch County if feasible to connect with other projects).• Provision of textbooks, learning and teaching materials.• Providing school materials support through cash based intervention, where feasible.• Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces and/or construction of TLSs where there is influx of new IDPs. Rehabilitation and/or construction of school fences, roofs, doors and/or windows locks. • School feeding program (in Bor South, under WFP project).• Recreational activities, establishing sports activities for children in school; training teachers on coaching sports activities.• Health and hygiene promotion, including Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). Establishment of youth friendly spaces, and recreational activities for adolescents and youth. • Establishment or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, including latrines (gender segregated), water points and provision of soap for hand-washing, provision of dry chlorine.• Chlorination, hygiene messaging on cholera symptoms, modes of transmission and prevention by school-based cholera response teams (PTAs/SMCs, teachers and adolescents/youth).• Creating school hygiene clubs to support messaging, provide awareness on cleaning and safe use of latrines.• Post-intervention (PIM) monitoring will be done across the project done by independent PAH M&E team. PIMs gives clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve overall quality of PAH’s projects.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Meeting with authorities, school directors, community members, teachers and students at early stage of project design and implementation to discuss project scope. Focus group discussions with various groups to collect feedback on the needs which is related to providing information on activities carried by PAH. Complaint/feedback system allows beneficiaries to approach PAH team member in case clarification or follow up is needed. This includes establishing help desk, supported by both men and women responsible for providing information to the community members, school authorities, teachers, students. Interaction sessions during campaigns, demonstrations and trainings. Using IEC materials for the promotion of key messages (related to WASH, nutrition, health, education, etc.)

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Some messages are general for all population, depends on items provided. Specific sessions for female teachers and female students will be taking place to ensure safety and security during the activities and to mitigate the risks related to accessing school. Needs analysis will focus on ensuring teachers, school authorities, PTA’s trainings are relevant to different groups, based on their scope of commitment needed. Women and girls are directed with key messages on menstrual hygiene, though boys and men are sensitized to break the stigma related to menstruation.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PAH has internal regulations on PSEA, with all employees signed commitment. PAH is also a member of the protection cluster and understands the modalities to ensure protection is mainstreamed within our activities. Key staff employed on field level (enumerators, teachers, casual workers, and other dealing directly with beneficiaries) needs to sign the safety code of conduct. Training is provided with clarification on child’s rights, child’s safety, abuse, reporting abuses, confidentiality and risk mitigation

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Focus group discussions with male and female teachers, girl and boy students, PTA’s and consultations on specific needs related to gender, age group, disability. Individual interviews on needs, protection concerns related to access to education will be conducted. Community needs to be inclusive of a wide representation of people in an intervention. Equitable Representation must include: Men, Women, Elderly, Boys and Girls, Youth, Disabled. In practice this is challenging with, for example, while women, girls only discussions are often monitored by community leaders, male teachers, etc. Protection issues that may arise are reviewed with protection team supporting project implementation at all levels.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

All PAH’s stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the PAH program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers. Complaints are collected through paper based system on the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within PAH mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed after complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a timeframe for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making; Referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project measure access of beneficiaries to assistance provided through complaint mechanism, tailored focus group discussions with youth groups, school pupils, teachers, PTAs, children with disability, and other community members. Protection specific data collection tools are used by the monitoring team to gather valid information for lessons learnt and future recommendations.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. Monitoring team has protection related questionnaires, developed by protection partners, approved by protection cluster. The tools gather key feedback from beneficiaries on safety related to assistance delivery.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries is measured through focus group discussions with the beneficiaries in which the beneficiaries will express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction of the process. Complain desks will be established during activities to address complains in a dignified way. Meanwhile throughout the project period beneficiary feedback, complaints and means of addressing will be documented in a well-designed form and analysed by the independent monitoring and evaluation team.
