Project Summary

The intervention is part of a project aiming at securing the basic needs and providing integrated community-based protection activities in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati . In these two areas, Caritas Germany (CG) seeks to provide integrated community-based protection services, implementing through its partner organization Caritas Syria (CS), to high at risk groups such as women, adolescent girls, children and elderly. In Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra, Caritas Syria has been implementing Basic Assistance activities since the beginning of 2017. CS has also been distributing relief items for SARC, UNHCR and WFP in the area. In 2018, Caritas Syria established a community center in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra, implementing informal education activities for school-age children as well as PSS for women, children and elderly. The concept of first establishing a relationship of trust with the local community through distributing relief items before opening up a community center has proven successful and shall be replicated in Karm Jazmati. Karm Jazmati will be a new project area for Caritas Syria. After consulting with regional coordination sectors UNOCHA, UNHCR, WFP as well as with organisations operating in neighbouring areas, Karm Jazmati has been identified as in high need for protection activites and therefore selected as second area of implementation under the proposed project.
The intervention aims at reducing the protection risks for at high risk groups through a community-based approach. The intervention will respond to the protection needs of vulnerable women, adolescent girls, children and elderly in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati. The intervention will respond to protection needs in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati with four pillars of activities: non-specialized PSS and awareness sessions for women, children and elderly; literacy courses for adolescent girls and women as protection measures against GBV risks; informal education for school-aged children aiming at reducing risk of child protection risk factors as well as a capacity building component aiming at strengthening the responding capacity of Caritas Syria staff on a national level.

Needs

Multiple displacements, lack of basic infrastructures and services have left a legacy of psychological distress in the communities Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati, two districts in the Eastern part of Aleppo city. According to the latest mapping conducted by Caritas Syria, there are currently 2.221 HH living in Jabal Badro/Ard Harmra (CS Mapping January 2019). In Karm Jazmati, 1.709 HH reside (Al Ishan Foundation, December 2018). The residents in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and in Karm Jazmati are mainly returnees (85% in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra, 87% in Karm Jazmati). Both areas are still projected return areas. In each of the areas, 13% of the population are IDPs. Only 3% in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and 0% in Karm Jazmati are members of the host community. CS has been operating in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra since 2017. Karm Jazmati has been identified as gap area lacking appropriate provision of protection services in close collaboration with the regional protection sector in Aleppo and other NGOs active in neighboring areas. In January 2019, CG and CS conducted a needs assessment in both locations. The assessment results reflect the complex and interlinked protection needs in these communities. Besides the loss and trauma experienced in the active hostilities in Aleppo, protection risks also stem from loss of livelihoods, property and assets. In 39% of HH in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and 34% of HH in Karm Jazmati none of the HH members is working and thus completely depending on external assistance. Having been considered as economically poor areas before, further increased level of poverties in the project areas led to marginalization and the neglect of groups with specific needs such as the elderly and people with disabilities. The assessment showed that in 34% of HH in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati, there is at least one person 60 years or older. In 14% of HH, there is at least one person with a physical or mental disability. Lack and loss of civil status documentation continue to emerge as a key need for all affected people. In Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati, 11% of all HH face at least one of the following legal issues: no official marriage documentation; no official divorce documentation; no personal official documentation; no family book; children not enrolled in the family book. 75% of the HH facing at least one of the above mentioned legal issues stated that they would consider it useful having or renewing the respective legal documents. All of these HH reported needing external assistance for obtaining/renewing their documentation to some degree; 83% in need of some external assistance and 17% fully depending on external assistance in the obtaining/renewing process. Furthermore, 55% of the interviewed persons stated being illiterate. 21% of HH with one or several daughters 15-17 yo reported that one or all of their adolescent girls were illiterate. From all respondents reported being illiterate, 88% stated that it had negative effects on their daily life. Illiteracy does not only deprive people of their ability to know and exercise their rights but also affects the chances of entering the job market and thus contributes to lack of livelihood opportunities. Illiteracy can therefore be considered increasing the protection concerns of already high at risk groups such as adolescent girls and women in multiple ways. Girls and boys continue to endure multiple protection risks on a daily basis; children who dropped-out of school to an even larger extend. Out of school children lack a structured daily routine which they need provably to feel secure. Also, drop-outs of school are deemed more prone to protection risks such as child labor. In Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati 22% of girls aged 7-14 yo do currently not attend school. In 5% of these cases, the reason for non-attendance is child labor. 37% of boys aged 7-14 yo, do not attend school. The main reason for non-attendance is child labor (45%).

Implementation of activities

Objective 1: Protection risks of vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children and elderly) in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati are reduced through integrated community based approaches, including awareness raising, psychosocial assistance targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms. Activity 1.1: Provide awareness session to high at risk groups of women, adolescent girls, children and elderly in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati Topics of the awareness sessions will be decided upon in a participatory way to ensure the needs of the different target groups will be met; therefore improving protection of the groups most prone to protection risks. At total of 810 persons will benefit from the sessions. Activity 1.2 Provide psychosocial assistance for women, children, elderly prone to protection risks Psychosocial assistance will be provided for peer groups of women, adolescent girls, children and elderly that face high protection risks. The non-specialized group sessions, held by CS social workers, will be conducted in safe spaces easily and safely accessible. The sessions will contribute to strengthen resilience of individuals and support healthy, functioning community structures. Upon identification of persons with specific needs, CS will ensure appropriate external referral. A total of 540 beneficiaries will benefit from PSS sessions. Objective 2: Caritas Syria staff has increased capacity to respond to protection needs and provide high quality of services through specific trainings to assess, analyse, prevent and address protection risks and needs 2.1. Conduct capacity building activities targeting Caritas Syria staff to improve quality provision of protection services During the project implementation period, trainings for 150 staff members on Protection (PSS, CP and GBV) are conducted through Caritas Germany consultants or external trainers. Trainings are selected according to need and interest of CS staff. The aim is to strengthen CS outreach activities, referrals, protection assessment and analysis through, i.e. training/ToT in identification of MH cases and respective (external) referral mechanisms, on PSEA (generally and Caritas Internationalis Code of Conduct specifically). Where appropriate, CB will be conducted on CS national level, targeting staff from all 7 regional CS offices. Where appropriate, links and synergies with internal and external programming will be sought. An officially recognized literacy organization will deliver a ToT to staff members of CS. In total, a number of 150 CS staff members will benefit from trainings. Objective 3: Risks of GBV for high at risk women and adolescent girls in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati are decreasedActivity 3.1: Provide literacy courses for illiterate adolescent girls 15-17 yo and women in Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati Alphabetization courses will be provided to adolescent girls and women. In the first project phase, literacy courses will be provided through an external service provider, with CS staff present to ensure safeguarding of beneficiaries. A total of 120 women and adolescent girls will benefit from the literacy courses. Objective 4: Protection risks for school-aged girls and boys are decreased Jabal Badro/Ard Hamra and Karm Jazmati Activity 4.1 Informal education courses (tutoring) will be provided to school aged children dropped out of school. In line with the “No Lost Generation” Framework, out of school children 7-14 years old will be provided safe access to tutoring classes in three basic subjects (mathematics, Arabic, and English). Thus, children will regain a structured daily routine and an increased sense of security which is important for their psychological wellbeing as well as increasing their chances for reintegration in formal education. At total of 240 children will benefit from this activity.
