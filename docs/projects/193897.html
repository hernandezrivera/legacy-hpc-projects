Project Summary

The project aims to provide multisectoral protection services to women and girls survivors or at risk of GBV, including PWDs, in the Southern governorates of the Gaza Strip through Al Awda Community Centre- Rafah and Al Awda Community Center- Khanyounis - Bani Suhila.
To provide the project services with the required quality and maximum protection for the target women and girls, the project staff of 10 persons of both sexes, (8 females and 2 males) will be involved in training courses to provide them with the necessary knowledge and skills, where 3 training courses will be carried out on: (i) Disability Inclusion (ii) GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response (iii) AWDA policies related to PSEA, Safeguarding and Code of Conduct.
Through the project, multisectoral services will be provided including: (i) structured psychosocial support services (collective and individual) (ii) legal consultations (iii) case management and referral to more specialized services such as health, Mental Health and court representation, this besides providing emergency needs for each individual case such as medicine, clothes, non-food items, assistive devices..etc. (iv) within the framework of protecting GBV cases cash assistance will be provided (v) to empower GBV cases, they will received grants to enable them to set up small projects after receiving vocational and management trainings that can guarantee a permanent income that contributes to provide their needs and those of their families with dignity. (vi) This in addition to distributing dignity kits to GBV cases upon specific criteria.
At the community level and to promote the concept of prevention, mitigating risk and response among men, women, girls and boys, including PWDs, awareness sessions will be carried out where 2 sessions will be allocated to include PWDs of both sexes, provided that a sign language interpreter service will be provided during the implementation of these sessions to ensure that persons with hearing disabilities benefit from the sessions information. This in addition to the publication of a motion graphic with a sign language interpretation, which will include awareness messages about PSEA.
The project will also build the capacity of a group of non-specialized GBV service providers from AWDA and CBOs staff on prevention, risk mitigation and response.
On the other hand, and in order to improve the staff's psychological status, self-care days will be organized to provide psychological rest and alleviate the psychological pressures they are exposed to.
To enhance Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), the project target group will be involved at all the stages of the project. And in order for them to access information about the project's activities and locations, adding to AWDA Feedback, Complaint and Response Mechanisms (FCRM), Facebook advertisement and Radio Spot will be published.
Noting that the project's services will be provided to the target groups, taking into account the gender needs, where the majority of the staff will be females. Furthermore, impartiality and independently will be taken into account during the provision of the project services with preserving the beneficiaries’ dignity and without any discrimination on the basis of sex, color, religion or political affiliation.
It is worth mentioning that the idea of the project was presented to the GBV Sub-Working Group Coordinator, who in turns supported the idea and confirmed its importance. Moreover, and in order to avoid duplication of service provision, coordination has been made with the Wefaq in Rafah and CFTA in KhanYonis.

1 - Needs

The blockade of Gaza and the repeated military interventions impact rates of GBV. Electricity and fuel crises have worsened living conditions, exacerbating tensions in the home and increasing the likelihood of violence. In the Gaza Strip: 38 per cent of ever-married women experienced some form of violence (psychological, physical, sexual, social or economic) by their husbands at least once. 64 per cent of ever-married women experienced psychological violence by their husbands at least once. 11 per cent of ever-married women experienced sexual violence by their husbands at least once. 26 per cent of ever-married women experienced physical violence by their husbands at least once. (Secondary Data Review Report Sept. 2022).Women and girls in the Gaza Strip face a number of risks, including discrimination and various forms of GBV such as early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, sexual harassment, rape, incest, denial of resources, psychological abuse and sexual exploitation and abuse. The Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistic (PCBS) survey on violence in the Palestinian Society, which published in June, 2021, reported that 29.4 per cent of (married or previously married) women between the ages of 18 and 64 experienced violence in the 12 months preceding the survey (37.5 per cent in Gaza and 24.3 per cent in the West Bank), of those, 56.6 per cent experienced psychological violence, 17.8 per cent physical violence, 8.8 per cent sexual violence, 32.5 per cent social violence, 41.1 per cent economic violence, while eight per cent experienced the emerging issue of cyber violence. On the other hand, and according to GBV Organizations in Gaza, in 2021, 23 per cent of all registered marriages involved girls under the age of 18. A survey focused on married girls shows that 45 per cent of the girls reported experiencing violence in the marital home. 92 per cent reported being subjected to verbal violence and 46 per cent reported physical violence. (OCHA 2021). MHPSS needs continue to rise across the OPT as a result of conflict-related violence, the COVID-19 pandemic, and decades of Israeli occupation and other related policies and measures. MSNA survey responses show that in 27% of households, at least one family member reports signs of psychosocial distress. (Secondary Data Review Report Sept. 2022). As part of the community's involvement in identifying their most important problems, needs and priorities, 2 focused groups meetings were organized in Rafah and Khanyunis. These groups included 10 members of women and girls, including PWDs. The results of these meetings showed that there are significant challenges for women and girls to continue their daily activities without exposure to various forms of violence. The participants also highlighted the importance and need to implement activities to protect women and girls on the one hand and to empower them economically on the other, as well as to focus on awareness of members of society on the issues of protecting women and girls and strengthening their role in the society.

2 - Activities

The project activities are designed to response to the protection needs of survivors or at risk GBV cases through the provision of multisectoral protection services at AWDA’s Community centers in Rafah and Khanyounis, as follows: 1. Publishing Radio Spot & Facebook Advs about the project's activities and locations adding to AWDA complaint system including the complaint channels for SEA where it is expected that 120,000 persons (10,000 boys, 10,000 girls, 50,000 men, 50,000 women) will benefit from these advs. 2. Building the capacity of project staff (10 persons) (2 men, 8 women) through the implementation of 3 training courses as follows: (i) 24 hour training course in disability inclusion aiming at strengthening protection mainstreaming throughout the humanitarian response. (ii) 24 hour training course in GBV prevention and/or risk mitigation and/or response. (iii) 12 hour training course in safeguarding policy, PSEA policy and Code of Conduct to guarantee the highest protection of the beneficiaries during receiving the services. 3. Providing multi-sectoral services, as follows: (i) Legal assistance to 160 GBV cases (140 women - 20 girls), including 4 PWDs. (ii) Providing Structured psychosocial support services through collective and individual sessions as follows: conducting collective psychological support sessions for 360 beneficiaries (240 women - 120 girls), including 8 PWD, (36 groups, 10 participants/group) (10 sessions/group). Providing individual psychosocial support services for 80 beneficiaries (60 women - 20 girls), including 2 PWD. (with average 8 sessions per case) (iii) Case management services to 120 GBV cases (100 women - 20 girls), including 3 PWDs. The case management services will include covering the cases needs such as medicine, non-food items, assistive devices…ect. (iv) Referring 20 cases (16 women - 4 girls including 1 PWDs) to more specialized organizations in mental health and legal services. 4. Distributing dignity kits to 300 women and girls (260 women - 40 girls), including 6 PWDs, based on specific criteria and in coordination with the Ministry of Social Development. 5. Building the capacity of 15 non-specialized GBV service providers (5 men 10 women) (from CBOs and AWDA staff) on GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response through a 24 hour training course (4 days, 6hours/day). 6. Providing CVA for (16) GBV cases (including 2 PWDs) based on specific criteria, for a period of 3 months (345$/month) to cover their basic needs. 7. As an exit strategy, the same women who benefited from the CVA will received vocational and admin training on how to manage small projects, where after small grants will be given to them (with an average 1000$/case) to establish income-generating projects that provide their needs. Note that AWDA pays attention to providing the basic needs of these women through cash assistance that was provided in advance for a period of 3 months to meet their basic needs first, and to ensure that the small grants will not be used to meet these needs and is allocated only to the income-generating projects. To ensure the impact of this support to the GBV cases, a consultant will be hired, who will conduct a field visit to each case and will prepare a business plan for each project, adding to conducting 2 sessions with each case during the implementation phase of their small projects. 8. Raising the awareness of different groups of the community on issues related to GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response including PSEA, through the implementation of 200 awareness sessions for 3000 beneficiaries (2000 women - 400 girls - 400 boys - 200 men) including 30 PWD, where 2 sessions will be allocated to include PWD of both sexes, provided that a sign language interpreter service will be provided during the implementation of these sessions to ensure that persons with hearing disabilities benefit from the sessions information. 9. In order to involve different groups of society, including PWD, in information about PSEA, a motion graphic video with a sign language interpretation will be prepared and published on Facebook with a sponsored advertisement & will be displayed on screens in the waiting areas for the beneficiaries in all AWDA’s facilities. It is estimated that the motion graphic will reach 100,000 persons (50,000 women, 30,000 men, 10,000 girls, 10,000 boys including 2000 PWDs). 10. Organizing 3 self-care days to the project staff (2 men, 8 women).

AWDA project coordinator will coordinate with the donor for issues related to the implementation of the project. AWDA MEAL officer will fill the Post Distribution Monitoring Tool (PDM), with the CVA beneficiaries. This tool will be used in the middle & end of the activity period to measure the impact of cash on the women's lives & if it met their basic needs & the extent to which it helped in protecting them. The feedback will be used in the improvement of the intervention & for drawing future interventions as well. Moreover, monitoring and follow up will be done by the MEAL officer to the small projects that will be established by the beneficiaries of the small grants. AWDA has safeguarding & PSEA policies & code of conduct, where the project staff will be trained on & sign these policies to ensure their compliance with them. The MEAL Officer will follow up on the implementation of these policies through field visits to work sites and holding sessions with a group of beneficiaries to ensure the extent the project staff’s commitment to the polices. In the context of involving the local community in the project monitoring, 4 focus groups meetings will be held with the project beneficiaries from both sexes and from different age groups including PWDs in the middle of the project duration with the aim of discovering any deviations in the implementation of activities and to work on modifying them. At the end of the project duration, satisfaction measurement questionnaires will be filled by beneficiaries to determine the percentage of their satisfaction with the project's services and to benefit from their suggestions for future work strategies.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The results of HNO 2023 pointed the need of multisectoral services for women and girls exposed to gender-based violence. The Project activities were designed to respond to these needs, as the AWDA owns permanent centers in all Gaza Strip governorates, including Rafah and Khnyounis Governorates (where the project will be implemented), where multisectoral protection services are provided in these centers for women and girls, including PWDs, and this proposal will contribute effectively to expanding the range of services provided, on the one hand, and increasing the number of beneficiaries of the services on the other hand. Noting that the psychosocial, legal and health services are provided in the same center, and this make it easier for women to access multisectoral services in an accessible manner while ensuring that they are protected from social stigma while receiving the service, as the health center is considered as entry point for the community services. The experience of the AWDA in the field of providing multi-sectoral services has accumulated over 15 years, as it has implemented dozens of projects in the field of GBV and protection, and the protection program is a vital program in AWDA strategic plan 2020-2024. It should be noted here that AWDA has acquired cumulative experience in the field of cash support and management of income-generating projects, as it has implemented several projects in this field in partnership with APS and TDH, in addition to its experience with UNFPA in providing small grants, therefore, through this project GBV cases will receive cash assistance according to specific criteria to provide their basic needs, while they will be involved in vocational and small projects management training courses to give them the skills of managing income-generating projects, and then granting them small grants to establish income generating projects to ensure that their needs and the needs of their families are met to live in dignity.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

AWDA set several mechanisms to receive feedback & complaints while ensuring that they can be accessible to all population groups, including PWDs (whether hearing, visual or physical disabilities) as the means of submitting the complaint varied in terms of written complaints directly to the complaint box or voice complaints through the use of the Toll-free No. (which is a free No. specified for complaints 1800180181), this in addition to electronic complaints via a generic e-mail and WhatsApp, besides receiving complaints face to face or by filling out questionnaires. AWDA developed a Feedback, Complain and Response Mechanism (FCRM) which is active and includes the following classifications: • Sensitive complaint. • Complaint & dissatisfaction. • Inquire about existing services. • Request for individual support & assistance. • Thanks & appreciation. Where the call center employee, through the electronic form, fills in all the details of the complaint and forwarded it to the MEAL manager, except for sensitive complaints that are forwarded to the General Manager directly. As for the complaints on the General Manager himself, they are forwarded to the Chairman. In order to response to gender needs, there are call center workers of both sexes. All the above-mentioned complaints mechanisms are announced to our beneficiaries through social media and radio advertisements.Where all types of complaints are received, including those related to SEA using AWDA complaint channels. While the target group will be informed about the mechanisms for submitting SEA complaints, including calling the number 121, which is managed by the SAWA organization.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The idea of the project, its services and implementation mechanisms stem from respecting the protection principles stipulated in the Humanitarian Charter, including the right to live in dignity, the right to assistance and the right to protection. Moreover, these mechanisms are part of the values of the AWDA within its strategic plan 2020-2024. Through this project and to avoid exposing the beneficiaries to harm the following pillars will be taken into consideration: - The AWDA will provide the project services through its 2 health and community centers in Rafa and Khanyounis. The geographical locations of these facilities are distinguished by their presence in populated places, making it safety and easy to be accessed and are known to the public since their establishment. - In order to prevent SEA while providing the project services, the project staff will attend training in PSEA. In addition, their contracts will include a special clause on PSEA in accordance with the IASC Principles and Guidelines. - In order not to expose the beneficiaries to further harm, they will be educated about PSEA and the AWDA complaints mechanisms. - To ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed while receiving services on the basis of dignity and protection, the project staff will participate in 3 training courses related to: (i) disability inclusion (ii) prevention, risk mitigation and response (iii) AWDA’s policies related to PSEA, safeguarding and code of conduct. - Insurance of work accidents and health insurance for the project staff will be provided. - Providing health insurance for the project staff in the AWDA health facilities. - Cases consultations and documentation are private and confidential. - Beneficiaries will be provided with all the necessary information about their intervention plan. - Gender needs will be considered by having female staff among the work teams to meet the gender needs of women and girls.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AWDA adopts the principle of equality and impartiality in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups who need humanitarian interventions to improve their protection and humanitarian situation. AWDA provides its services without any discrimination based on age, gender, religion, race, or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project and to achieve the principle of impartiality in providing services, AWDA will provide services to the targeted women and girls on the basis that each case has her own individual characteristics and special needs, especially PWDs as the most vulnerable in society and who need special care and protection while maintaining dignity, confidentiality and nondiscrimination. The project services will be easily accessible by the target groups, as they will be provided through 2 health and community centers affiliated to AWDA in Rafa and Khanyounis governorates that are well known to the beneficiaries, knowing that all the project services will be provided to all the project beneficiaries totally free of charge. To ensure that the targeted women have access to the services provided under the project, especially women who do not have the money needed for transportation, they will be provided with transportation fees.
