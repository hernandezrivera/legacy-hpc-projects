Project Summary

As the lead agency for the Shelter Sector, UNHCR is coordinating the shelter responses from
inside Syria as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey). Through this project, UNHCR will support
the communities of IDPs and returnees in targeted areas through emergency response
and long-term shelter solutions from inside Syria as well as from Gaziantep (Turkey).
The project is in line with both the priorities of the HRP and 2023 Sector Strategy and the
project will be completed in line with Sector guidance. Project indicator targets represent a
Sum of Syria and Gaziantep hub targets, outlined below:
The shelter program aims to improve housing and related community/public
infrastructure addressing the shelter needs of 7,500 vulnerable households/37,500
individuals through basic house repairs. Returnee communities will be supported with
debris removal, common area repair, and installation of solar streetlights to support 200,000 individuals/40,000 returnee families, and host community.
Through emergency response, 30,000 individuals/6,000 households will receive with
emergency shelter packages and 247,750 individuals/89,550 households will bene􀂦t from tent distribution, camp construction, and collective shelter rehabilitation.
UNHCR’s cross-border shelter program aims to provide emergency shelter (tents) to
15,000 households/75,000 individuals and address the shelter needs of 15,000 vulnerable
households/75,000 individuals through tents distribution, and roads repair and construction inside IDPs camps
The role and responsibility of the local and national authorities will continue in close
partnership with MoLAE
and partners (NGOs/INGOs) in the implementation of shelter and
infrastructure works. Technical training will be provided to 1,200 engineers from local
government.

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 Needs, It is estimated that 5.5 million people in Syria will require shelter support in 2023. In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34 percent of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelters, of which 19 percent reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues. Lack of access to electricity/lighting. The range of shelter needs is diverse across different groups, and an increasing number of IDPs and refugees are returning to their original houses/apartments, especially in Homs, Eastern Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Dar’a, Deir-ez-Zour, north Lattakia, Hama and Quneitra Governorates. Experience to date in engaging with return communities has demonstrated that a multisector, area-based approach is the most effective way to ensure the viability of returns. Shelter and support for the rehabilitation of basic services and community infrastructure have been particularly relevant to anchor return. UNHCR will continue its current response to address the need to voluntarily return IDPs and refugees in their initial reintegration, through shelter rehabilitation and community infrastructure repair, in partnership with other humanitarian and recovery actors as well as with the local authorities. Vulnerable groups, including children, youth, women, disabled and older people, will be given consideration in the shelter response

4 - Implementation of activities

The emergency shelter will be required for newly displaced people in NE and NW Syria. Shelter support will also be required for people who return to their houses. Shelter activities include the collective shelters, and shelter materials for minor shelter repairs in coordination with local authorities, landlords, and shelter sector. In camps, UNHCR provides maintenance in twelve camps in NES, including installing solar streetlights, roads, upgrading, and constructing communal kitchens. In 2023, UNHCR will provide 40,000 tents in camps and spontaneous settlements. Also, rehabilitate 175 rooms in collective shelters. UNHCR plans to repair 7,500 damaged houses and rehabilitate the common areas of 80 buildings. UNHCR will provide 2,000 solar streetlights and provide safe access to shelter through the repair and removal of the debris for about 10 kilometers. 5,000 houses will receive shelter materials such as doors and windows, WASH, electrical, and solar packages for minor repairs. UNHCR has access to the locations and will be able to deliver the planned activities. All shelter projects will follow the approved sequence for shelter projects, starting from technical assessment, approval, tendering, and implementation, till official handing over while monitoring throughout the project lifecycle will be conducted through UNHCR teams in the field, partners, as well as third-party monitors. UNHCR will report to the sector while mainstreaming AAP and Gender throughout the response.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 2: Returnees are exposed to risk of eviction and/or exploitation with major protection implications, while there are also risks of rights violations, either of owners or occupants, and future disputes due to the difficulties in demonstrating ownership documentation (HLP). Mitigating measures: Gather available information locally through a structured due diligence assessment that would help to identify and prevent/mitigate HLP risks specific to each intervention. Risk 3: Interference with beneficiary selection may lead to potential fraud and aid diversion resulting in financial or reputational risks. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Advocate with relevant authorities and partners to apply shelters sector criteria implementation in beneficiary selection process. Emphasize importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory training for its own and partner staff. since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners along with the establishment of complaining mechanisms to ensure neutral assistance according to shelter sector guidelines and vulnerability criteria. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the checklist will not be able to continue their partnership with UNHCR in the following year. 1.
