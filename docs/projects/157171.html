Project Summary

This project aims to support the WASH response and the immediate needs of most vulnerable displaced populations in the northwest of Syria by providing emergency WASH assistance in camps and improving the basic WASH infrastructure and WASH conditions in IDP sites in northwest Syria through:
i) Improving access to drinking water by rehabilitating 13 water systems and installing connections to IDP camps.
ii) Rehabilitation and construction of 21 drainage systems in informal IDP sites.
iii) Provision and distribution of 12,000 WASH NFI kits.
iv) Provision of temporary water supply to newly displaced and/or highly vulnerable populations living in 34 IDP camps.
v) Provision and construction of 70 communal latrine units in IDP sites.
The project will contribute to the two objectives of the WASH cluster which are:
• Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria
• Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behaviour and practices of most vulnerable people
Overall, the project aims to support 155,144 beneficiaries with humanitarian WASH assistance and improvements to basic WASH infrastructure in informal IDP sites in NW Syria. Project locations are Harim, Dana, Ma’arat An Nu’man and Azaz.

Needs

IDPs living in IDP sites are one of the most vulnerable population groups in Syria. According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 6.2 million people are internally displaced in Syria and there are an estimated 871,150 IDPs living in last resort camps, informal settlements, transit/reception centres, and collective centres. Of these, almost half (420,103 IDPs) are in the northwest of the county, the majority of whom are located in Idleb Governorate. According to the CCCM Cluster, throughout the IDP sites in northwest Syria, children make up 54 per cent of the population or about 226,856 individuals. The cluster has also tracked 1,401 female-headed and 466 child-headed households, 1,620 elderly persons, 76 separated children in those IDP sites. Furthermore, among the estimated populations of 6,174,977 individuals in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, 15.1% are young children (<5), 35.5% are children (5-17), 45.5% are adult (18-59), and 3.9% are over 59 years old (Elderly). Out of those living in IDP sites, approximately 89% are residing in informal settlements (IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM), October 2018). Compared to formal camps, collective centres, and reception centres (which do receive some form of relief assistance), informal settlements have intermittent or non-existent basic service provision, as well as poorer standards of sanitation and housing infrastructure. Furthermore, the majority of informal settlements have not been integrated into ongoing joint humanitarian response planning due to access issues and a lack of reliable information. As explained in the 2019 HNO, the scale of displacements to IDP sites In the northwest since the beginning of 2018 has contributed to a deterioration of already overused WASH facilities and increased the need to scale-up emergency operations while seeking for more sustainable solutions (connection to existing water networks and/or establishment of simplified water and sewer networks). The very high levels of displacement combined with the increased number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites has presented significant challenges to WASH partners to deliver assistant in IDP sites because of the increased cost and complexity. Especially, sanitation needs are considerably higher among displaced people living in informal settlements and camps compared to the rest of the population, with over-crowding, cleanliness and protection concerns linked with substandard conditions of sanitation facilities. Idleb and north rural Aleppo have become extremely crowded. Increased pressure on already overstretched WASH services has resulted in increasingly depending on alternative and often unsafe water sources, which significantly increase the risk of waterborne diseases. In addition, the continuous drilling of wells in search for water has negatively affected groundwater resources, with a significant depletion of water aquifers observed especially around camps near the Turkish border. Furthermore, heavy rains and serious floods throughout December 2018 and January 2019 have affected a large number of these IDP sites. An inter-sector rapid needs assessment (collaboration involving clusters, OCHA, HNAP and REACH) on the impact of the floods on IDP sites in NW Syria indicates that an estimated 81,805 people were impacted by the flood. According to the assessment, the flooding impact has exacerbated pre-existing conditions and needs and the report emphasizes a critical need for repair and rehabilitation of infrastructure and social services impacted by the flood. Therefore, there are urgent needs of providing emergency WASH assistance to displaced populations in IDP sites as well as activities to maintain and rehabilitate existing WASH facilities in the IDP sites, especially informal settlements, in northwest Syria.

Implementation of activities

The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with WASH Cluster Coordinator, by the IOM Turkey cross-border team based in Gaziantep with technical and administrative support from IOM sub-office in Gaziantep as well as the main IOM office in Ankara. IOM will work in partnership with well-experienced NGOs in each specific activity in camps and camp-like settings as implementing partners, selected from active members of the WASH and CCCM Clusters. IOM will provide technical support and monitor the project implementation on a daily basis through direct communication with the implementing partners. For provision and distribution of WASH NFI kits, IOM will procure, warehouse, and transship the items to IPs' warehouse in Syria. IOM, with coordination with the WASH Cluster, will keep 6,000 WASH NFI kits as contingency stock in case of massive displacements. IPs will provide needs assessment and detailed distribution plan for their targeted locations and will collect beneficiary lists and signatures from each household after distributing the kits. For the rehabilitation and construction of drainage systems, rehabilitation of water systems and provision of mobile latrines activities, implementing partners will be asked to conduct topography study for each IDP site, provide detailed needs assessment, site plans, implementation/ distribution plans, bill of quantities and scope of work. IOM technical staff will review and revise the project proposals and the above-mentioned documents and ensure the project idea and design meet the set objectives. IOM will also pay attention to Housing land and properties (HLP) dynamics and will mitigate possible tenure risks and disputes, by asking implementing partners to go through land ownership identification process and sign agreements with landowners / local councils. Each agreement should include physical and construction works to be completed, the purpose of the intervention and approval from the landowners / local councils. Third-party monitoring mechanisms will be utilized to monitor and evaluate project activities. Warehouse verification, distribution and post-distribution monitoring activities will be conducted by third parties for at least 30 per cent of the overall distributions and activities. The disaggregated number of beneficiaries will be verified through regular reporting by cluster members through the 4W reporting format to the WASH cluster and through IOM weekly progress reports. Exit interviews with men and women beneficiaries will be also conducted at the project sites. IOM will also engage all different groups of target community through regular meetings, transparent information management and throughout implementation. IOM will apply the IOM Accountability to Affected Populations frameworks, including community feedback mechanism, focus group discussion and joint community-IOM post service monitoring, to all project activities and will work to build the capacity of project partners in AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations) throughout the project lifespan. Based on feedback from the affected population, the project is modified as much as possible to better address the needs of camp residents. Gender and age are key vulnerability criteria (e.g. female-headed household, households with 2 or more children less than 5 years old) for beneficiary selection of some activities. Even for projects which use a blanket approach for all camp residents, such as the distribution of WASH NFI kits, gender and age information will be used to tailor the modality of assistance. Moreover, targets indicators are disaggregated by sex and age. For example, the number of IDPs living in IDP sites provided with life-saving assistance will be counted by men, women, boys, girls. Men and women largely receive similar benefits, which will be verified by post-distribution satisfaction surveys.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks:1. Inequality in WASH NFI kits distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV.2. Risk of theft of NFIs and chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be attractive to armed groups inside Syria.3. Movements of water trucks in congested, camp-like settings.4. Time of water delivery not appropriate causing safety risk for beneficiaries or creating pulling factor for school-age children.5. Latrines represent GBV and/or health, safety risk; Access to toilet is not available to all 24/7; Access to toilets not available for all.6. Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities.7. Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to chemicals and sewage.8. Children below 18 years old conduct work on behalf of the humanitarian organisation, its partners or contractors. Mitigation measures:1. Promote Establishment of an adequate management plan; Promote involvement communities in distribution planning and feedback on package for improvement.2. Agencies provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to users; Work closely with all partners on the ground; Promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock).3. Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that uses wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators.4. Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding water delivery schedule; Establish a clear schedule for water trucking.5. Ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up. Lessons from previous experience are shared and learned.6. MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. Promote cost recovery systems for longer-term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. 7. Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with chemicals wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors.8. Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ labourers; add zero tolerance disclosure on child labour in the contracts; check and register civil documentation if available

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Staff mobility and access restrictions to affected population: Active fighting, shifting of the conflict lines, and potential closure of the border might prevent delivery of humanitarian assistance. o Partners will elaborate different plans for deliveries allowing for flexible dispatching of goods when certain areas are inaccessible.o The security assessment will be undertaken before the planned transportation and distribution of items as well as appropriate risk mitigating measures will be put in place. o No UN staff will cross into Syria, which will lower the profile of the activities and ensures the safety of both UN and IP staff. o ‘Duty of care’ strategy in place for local partners. • Aid diversion by armed groups. o IPs are encouraged to report any incidents of aid diversion and interference incidents by the armed groups.o IOM utilizes third-party monitoring mechanisms to conduct warehouse verification, distribution and post-monitoring distribution activities to mitigate the risk.o Targeting of most vulnerable beneficiaries will also be a key under this action. Recruitment of female staff within distribution teams will be considered. • HLP disputes on project sites.o HLP Due Diligence Guidelines has been/will be used to minimize the risks associated with land ownership.o All partners will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate ownership of land, and discuss with owners and available site management. o For sites in which the landowner refuses heavy physical works, IOM may only focus on light works. • Changes in the areas of control may impact the continuation of WASH service provision.o Partners will develop a business continuity plan and will be equipped with strategic locations of warehouses and offices and partnership with local councils. • Risks mitigation measures specific for contingency stock of hygiene kits. o Hygiene kits will be released to trusted NGOs who have a proven track-record inside northern Syria. o One criteria to determine which community hygiene kits will be released for is a question of access and security threat in a particular area of community.o IPs are requested to distribute within two weeks of the delivery to their warehouses inside Syria to minimize threat of looting of warehouses.o IOM has rigorous monitoring and evaluation tools in place to ensure dispatch, transportation, and delivery of hygiene kits to partners inside Syria.
