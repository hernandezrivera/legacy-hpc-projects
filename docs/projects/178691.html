Project Summary

This project is aligned with the priority of protection of population affected by the crisis is
improved through community-level approach and targeted protection
interventions, and through advocacy with duty bearers, relates to strategic objectives
Project contributes to prevent and response Child Protection (CP) concerns through
implementing Community-level child protection activities as the following:
The first main objective of the project is to strengthen community-level child
protection services.
total BNs number: 20250 for CP activities *boys 8000 girls 7250 men 2000 women 3000
needs will differ depending of age group and gender, CP activities such as PSS sessions topics or type of activities, CM, parenting sessions topics vary in line with age group.

The main activities that will be:
1- Case management (survivors or at risk of UASC, CAAFAG, Child labor & child marriage,
abuse)
2- Referral pathway
3- Psycho-social Support structured.
4- Establishing for CP community committees, supports Community-level child protection
5- Awareness rasinng sessions for both adults and children
6- Ensuring the safe access for PSEA for both adults and children
The second main objective of this project to strength the protection mechanisms in the community through implementing the following activities:
1-providing awareness raising through mobile teams within targeted location on different protection concerns.
2-conducting periodical community-based activities and vocational training for both F/M., all trainings will be related to real need to the adults and youth such as languages and computer skills.
3-Provision of legal advice on civil status documentation /registration.
4-conducting protection monitoring and protection needs / risks.
SOP will be developed for project aligned with child protection sub-cluster guidance, ToRs
and agreed criteria. While, BDC will determining “vulnerability” and “vulnerable groups”
typically according to multiple factors based on age, gender, diversity, displacement status, and other characteristics, both youth and CWD will be considered and included in this project targets.
the project will target mainly: IDPs living in last resort camps/sites/collective centers where freedom of movement and/or access to services is limited.
and Newly-displaced populations (during first three months of internal displacement): IDPs fleeing active hostilities often face immediate and acute needs during their initial months of displacement.
over-burdened host communities: communities hosting a large concentration of IDPs and/or spontaneous returnees - in relation to their host/pre-returnee population - also face significant needs. This particularly relates to availability of services, competition for livelihoods, and possible tensions over access to services/economic opportunities resulting from the influx of population and overburdened absorption capacity. These communities may be targeted for community-based and/or individualized assistance for the hosts.
humanitarian principles and protection principles will be applies along the project life: Do no harm principle and code of conduct, safe guarding policy, PSEA policy will be introduced well to the staff and CPN members.

4 - Needs

Children through NW Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks.child protection related risks and vulnerabilities have been exacerbated during the COVID-19 pandemic which has increased risk factors that drive the intensity and frequency of violence against children. Restrictive measures in some locations in Syria are isolating children in homes that are not safe, exacerbating the risk of emotional, physical and sexual violence. (HNO,2021)Referring to child protection situation monitoring report 2020 more than 79.9% of KIs reported that children are in need for focused non specialized services, targeting both host communities and IDPs,also, almost half of assessed communities reported for a need of specialized services including case management, focused PSS, and IPA, 85.64% of the KIs reported that children are engaged in some types of work and that prevents them from going to school, the percentage of children involved in begging increased during 2020, in addition to the high risk of collecting COVID-19 infection to the children working in begging,the largest percentage of the needs reported within the protection was psychosocial support services, and many KIs mentioned the urgent need to support the separated children institutes and specialized services for children engaged in labor.According to the Protection Monitoring Task Force reports, security concerns continue to affect freedom of movement for boys and girls. Lack of Civil Status Documentation continues to be a barrier to accessing humanitarian assistance.according to MSNA findings negative coping mechanisms are increasingly being reported,boys are usually facing child labour / begging and girls are facing early marriage.

5 - Implementation of activities

this project will be implemented by providing the following activities taking into consideration all protective measures which is related to COVID 19 pandemic:1.1 providing awareness raising through mobile teams within targeted location on different protection concerns 1.2 conducting periodical community-based activities and vocational training for both F/M between 14 and 24 years old such as language courses and computer skills.1.3 Provision of legal advice on civil status documentation /registration.1.4 conducting protection monitoring and protection needs / risks.1.5 providing awareness-raising and community events to prevent and respond to child protection issues1.6 providing structured PSS within CFSs and by mobile teams for both boys and girls in the targeted areas using I DEAL curricula for children between 6 and 12 years old 1.7 providing specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys are in prioritized locations BDC will work with CPNs to identify entry points in accessing survivors or at-risk children and to refer them to different service providers 1.8 support child protection groups/committees to ensure the community’s active participation to prevent and respond to child protection issuesall project activities will be monitored including CM services in line with CP and GP clusters guidelines which related to monitoring protection services, MEAL team will develop the M&E plan and make sure that activities will be monitored and evaluated through means of verification to achieve the best impact of the protection interventions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.For awareness raising: expectations of response/services in locations where services may in fact be limited/non-existent, mitigation:receive feedback on the quality and usefulness of the information being provided.2.For community centers: physical security risk due to conflict activity; barriers for certain groups in access centers (limitations on movement); sustainability of centers given current funding environment, mitigation:Develop clear communication to manage expectations and advocate with donors to fund the centers to ensure sustainability of the project3.Risk that by providing information about documentation which may not be recognized is perceived as endorsement of said documents/legal systems, mitigation: ensure humanitarian actors in northern Syria are informed of the potential risks in civil documentation.4.Girls, boys, women and men's physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information,mitigation: Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community and ensure mobilisation of key persons in the community.5.Risk of increasing violence in the home and community for girls and boys receiving specialised services, mitigation: Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.6.Risk to the wellbeing of child protection service providers due to exposure to violence (e.g. secondary or vicarious trauma). mitigation: Self-care strategies as part of programmes and organisational HR policies.
