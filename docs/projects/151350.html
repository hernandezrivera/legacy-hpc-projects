Project Summary

The project intends to conduct facility and community-based screening for severe acute malnutrition for children aged 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and provide treatment of acutely malnourished children 6- 59 months (1632 girls and 1568 boys) and pregnant and lactating women 1440) at the outpatient therapeutic centre attached to the health facility to curb malnutrition in the targeted districts.
Further, micro-nutrient supplementation to PLW and children (6-59 months) for treatment and prevention of malnutrition, capacity building of health facility and community workers (boys, girls, men and women) on appropriate IYCF practices to provide support and counselling services to the beneficiaries and training of facility based nutrition workers and community nutrition workers (10 Male and 10 Female) on integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition (IMAM) Guidelines and creation of awareness/community mobilization on IMAM programming will be done.

Needs

Malnutrition is a critical risk in Somalia and good nutrition and access to adequate food remains a challenge to child survival. According to FSNAU/FEWSNET September 2018 Post Gu Technical Release, approximately 1.5 million people in Somalia are still facing acute food security crisis or worse outcomes despite improvements, 294,000 children are acutely malnourished and face an increased risk of morbidity and death with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence considered Very Critical (>30%) and Critical (≥4.0-5.6%) respectively. SAM prevalence is Critical (≥4-5.6) particularly among Mogadishu Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) settlements. Results from 30 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners between June and July 2018 indicate that Urgent treatment and nutrition support is needed for approximately 294 200 acutely malnourished children, including 55 200 who severely malnourished between now and December 2018. Integrated support interventions should be sustained to maintain recent improvements as well as prevent further deterioration in the nutrition situation across Somalia. Over 1.5 million people facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support between now and December 2018. Livelihood support is also required for 3.1 million people who are stressed (IPC Phase 2)The proposed Aid Vision nutrition intervention will go a long way in reducing maternal and child health in Garasbaley of Kaxda district in Banadir region, Warsheikh, Raga Celle and Balcad district of Middle Shabelle that have no or little access to basic nutrition care services. Aid Vision has proposed scale up of Garasbaley MCH along Afgooye corridor, establishment of primary health care facility in those targeted districts and provision of this will be closely integrated.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Aid Vision will ensure that all segments of the affected population in the IDPs including pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months have equal access to Nutrition assistance. The minority and marginalized communities in the IDP camps who are at risk of being neglected/ excluded are also given quality nutrition care services like the host community.
