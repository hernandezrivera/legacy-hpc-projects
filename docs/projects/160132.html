Project Summary

Ceel Barde and Tayeeglow districts of Bakool region like other parts of Somalia are plagued by malnutrition and high level of food insecurity driven by drought conditions from late 2018 and below average rains in 2019 as per FSNAU post GU report for 2019. The median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (13.8% in 2019 Gu, 12.6% in 2018 Deyr and 14.0% in 2018 Gu). A high level of acute malnutrition persists due to a combination of factors - food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin A supplementation coverage, and poor care practices. Notably, a prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was observed in Bakool and related areas of Southern Inland Pastoral livelihood zone. FSNAU projects the GAM in Bakool district at 15-29.9% weight-for-height Z-score and Middle upper Arm circumference below 125mm of > 10%.
The purpose of this program is to improve the nutritional status of children under the age of 5 and PLW (pregnant and lactating mothers) through an integrated treatment approach that goes beyond the treatment phase of malnutrition. It combines therapeutic support to nutrition promotion activities that prevent malnutrition as well as support optimal nutrition behavior as a long term goal. Through this approach the program will focus on a) nutritional treatment services b) basic nutrition prevention services and c) monitoring and surveillance that links information to early action
This comprehensive approach will contribute and enhance early detection and treatment of malnutrition reducing the case fatality rate, done through community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) in line with Somalia BNSP (Basic Nutrition Service Package). Based on the nutritional and clinical needs of the child, SOLO will provide inpatient therapeutic program for SAM with complications, outpatient therapeutic programs for SAM without complication, community mobilization to enhance, screening, active case finding, defaulter tracing and follow up of cases, Vitamin A supplementation and Immunization. These services will be provided through static sites utilizing existing community structure and mobile sites to enhance access and utilization of services by IDPs, rural and scattered communities.
Some of the context-specific immediate courses of malnutrition will be identified and addressed through a social behavior change action to the individual, household and relevant community members that will increase appropriate knowledge, attitude and practices regarding Infant and Young child feeding practices, and Maternal nutrition. In addition, nutrition education and hygiene promotion will be carried out at the household level using the TIPs or other similar approaches, starting with families of children in the CMAM program. At the facility level, nutrition education and hygiene promotion will be provided to caretakers of malnourished children. At the community level, different forums will be used. CBW (Community based Workers), Teachers, TBAs (Traditional birth attendance) and religious leaders will be included into the program to stimulate discussions on how they can support messages and gain support for the activities.
Lastly, the program will utilize the existing monitoring and surveillance systems to collect, analyze, interpret and report information on the nutrition status of the targeted community and to inform of an appropriate response, highlight on an evolving crisis, identify malnutrition children, and areas with greater needs.
The direct beneficiaries will include 2040 children 6-59 months of age, 2600 pregnant women at their 2nd and 3rd trimester, and lactating women with babies from 0 to 24 month of age and women of reproductive age.
The programme will also target community health workers/volunteers, Community health, committee groups and relevant Ministry of Health staff who will benefit from different components of BNSP

Mitigation Measures

Although the situation in CeelBarde, Rab Dhuure and Tayeglow districts is relatively safe, feared attacks from insurgents outside the town is still a potential risk in some villages that hampers the delivery of aid. SOLO takes both preventative and corrective measures, even before the risk materialize, by working with the community leaders who provide the organization with the security information that assists us in decision making and preparedness. In addition, SOLO gives priority and recruits local staff to reduce some potential risks but it also requires that project risks be regularly monitored, reviewed and risk matrix updated accordingly to ensure timely mitigation of any potential risk arising to the project, staff, and beneficiaries.SOLO has a policy of remaining impartial in local politics and requires all staff to adhere to this. Diversion of project’s resources is a potential risk. While UNICEF will provide supplies, SOLO has logistical management policy and procedures in place to ensure security and proper handling of any supplies to avoid loss or diversion. On the other hand, internal financial controls and reporting mechanisms including segregation of duties are meant to safeguard against fraud. SOLO has invested in community structures through which it reaches the communities, facilitates participation and decision making in regard to the target communities. This project will benefit from SOLO’s approaches of working with and through beneficiary communities and community structures such as community project committees. SOLO also enjoys very good working relations with the administration at local, regional and national levels. The deep knowledge and relations also help SOLO to effectively address core issues of gender, inclusion of minorities and protection of vulnerable groups such as women and girls. The organization is currently implementing protection and youth/women empowerment projects with UNFPA and GIZ respectively which will complement this program. This program has protection built in and mainstreamed. Women and girls accessing nutrition support may be exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse. During mobilization and subsequent sensitization sessions, SOLO will include clear messages especially to women and girls that services are free and no sexual favours should be given in order to benefit. SOLO will ensure that all staff are trained in eliminating protection risks to girls and women. OTP sites will be strategically located and mobile sites added to ensure the vulnerable groups do not cover long distances unnecessarily and increase exposure. Where possible, household visits will be done.

Please provide further explanation

The programme will ensure all RUTF sachets distributed to beneficiaries are brought back and properly destroyed. Proper disposal and management of medical equipment and supplies will be upheld. Furthermore we shall encourage caretakers to use basket/permanent bags for collection of the nutrition rations. SOLO will work closely with MOH and relevant line ministries, targeted communities and other actors to ensure linkage coordination and collaboration of the services across the district. The proposed intervention is in line with the Somalia National Development Plan, 2017-2019 goal of improving access to quality services for the prevention and management of malnutrition with a focus on children under-five and women of reproductive age. Whilst sustainability of any program in the context of South Central Somalia is often questionable, the approach SOLO has taken in terms of program implementation through recruitment of local competent staff to ensure a high probability and likelihood of continued long-term benefits for society as a whole. In addition, the intensity and frequency of learning increase the probability that the trained individuals will retain the knowledge and skills over a longer period of time can be continually applied even if the program itself does not continue. SOLO will strive to provide equal participation and inclusivity to all the beneficiaries regards of gender, class, religion, clan affiliation and social status will be involved in the project cycle and program as a whole. Although this is a primary Integrated nutrition program, its design, and implementation incorporate equality, which has been recognized to be one of the critical factors contributing to the success of programs for SOLO. Beneficiaries entitlement will partly recognized in terms of the contract agreement between the state actors, implementing partner and donor. Although this will be done through a legal binding document between the donor and the implementing partner, at the beneficiaries level and MOU between the state actors and the implementing actor will utilised as mean of legal document in ensuring service and utilities are provided for the intended beneficiaries.
