Project Summary

The objective of this project is to provide humanitarian assistance to refugees and hosting communities under all three HRP 2021 Strategic
Objectives:
O1: Provide timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity;
O2: Contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic services;
O3: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through quality and principled humanitarian action. Lifesaving assistance for new arrivals/vulnerable groups is prioritized to respond to urgent humanitarian and protection needs of anticipated new arrivals.
IOM will provide multi-sectorial intervention in Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), improved Emergency Shelters (ES)/ and Non Food Items (NFIs), livelihoods activities to response to urgent needs of South Sudanese Refugees (SSRs) and hosting communities. IOM will focus on six localities and this includes El Radoom, South Darfur, Bahar El Arab, El Ferdous, Assalaya, East Darfur, Keliak, West Kordofan and Gedaref to respond to the Ethiopian refugee influx. High rates of SSRs displacement has been reported in the six localities mentione, whereas as of 9 Novemeber 2020, over 40,000 Ethiopian refugees have crossed the border into Sudan, in Gedaref State, from Ethiopia. WASH activities will increase access to safe drinking water, adequate quality sanitation facilities and improve personal and public hygiene practices. Shelter and non-food items (NFI) interventions will be used to improve living conditions for vulnerable refugees and host communities receiving high rates of displacement and offer protection from the harsh elements. Both refugees and hosting communities will have improved access to livelihood opportunities in an effort to both reduce protection risks (for example family splitting for employment opportunities or negative coping strategies) and improve purchasing power. IOM's project will focus on targeting both refugees and hosting communities in an effort to mitigate the risks of tensions over scarce resources, increase the capacity of host support hosting communities to absorb population influxes, and promote social cohesion. Under Non-SSRs response plan, IOM will focus on Um Dafug locality, South Darfur where has been hosting refugees from the Central African Republic (CAR). Health interventions delivering a basic package of primary health services (BPPHS) will be implemented in Um Dafug locality, along with WASH, ES/NFI and livelihoods interventions. The COVID-19 response and mitigation measures will be integrated within the planned activities with emphasis on infection prevention and control, case management, and risk communication and community engagement (RCCE). In Gedaref, IOM's response will primarily focus on ensuring access to essential WASH and health services in and out of the camp sites, addressing protection risks, providing emergency shelter and logistical support for the delivery of services. The planned activities and response plan have been planned on an initial target figure of 100,000 refugees.
All distribution and hygiene promotion activities will follow national guidelines on COVID-19 preventive measures, such as physical distancing, use of PEE and no large gatherings will be organized.

1 - Needs

Since the outbreak of the conflict in South Sudan in December 2013; a total of 729,557 South Sudanese refugees have fled to Sudan as of October 2020. South Sudanese refugees have crossed the border at different sites into Abyei, South and West Kordofan, White Nile and East and South Darfur due to continued instability in South Sudan. While there is a major humanitarian presence in these states, significant gaps remain in East Darfur, South Darfur and West Kordofan, where humanitarian interventions have been more limited. At the same time, localities like Um Dafoug in South Darfur, have witnessed the influx of over 14,000 refugees from CAR due to the clash between Sara and Kara tribal groups that broke out in CAR in June 2019, and access to basic services and shelter is very limited. Multi-sector needs of the population are high both in and out of camps. Many refugees are living among host-communities that already have very limited access to basic services; for example in Keliak, West Kordofan, approximately 4 Liters of water are available per person per day (l/p/d), which is below the Sphere standard of 15 l/p/d and the influx of South Sudanese continues to create tensions between the community and the refugees. A lack of proper water management, sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices have also been observed among these communities’ waste management is limited, and open defecation is widely practiced. Therefore, there is a great need to support these communities with construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of water structures and safe sanitation facilities; as well as facilitating hygiene awareness campaigns and waste management campaigns. The health and nutritional status of South Sudanese and CAR refugees entering Sudan is also dire as they were already facing serve health issues and acute malnutrition before they arrived in the country. Livelihood opportunities for both refugees and host communities will also be improved to increase food security and purchasing power. IOM's refugee response will focus on providing support to both refugees (75%) and host communities (25%) to promote social cohesion and decrease the likelihood of tensions or conflict between the two communities.As of 2 December 2020, and as per the figures released by UNHCR and the Commission of Refugees (COR), 46,7128 Ethiopian refugees (57 per cent men, 43 per cent women) have fled the Tigray region and crossed into Sudan since the crisis began on 9 November 2020. Influxes of refugees are arriving through the primary border points, including 32,165 individuals in Hamdayet in Kassala state, 702 in Algazira, Yagolo, and Yabisher in Blue Nile state, and 12,399 in Lugdi and 862 in Abderafi in Gedaref state, which also act as a transit point for transportation and relocation to camp sites and settlements. As tensions continue in the Tigray region, it is expected that within the next six months, 100,000 displaced persons from Ethiopia will cross into Sudan’s formal and informal eastern borders. UNHCR is currently monitoring entry points and registering refugees as they arrive. Needs remain vast and there is an urgent need for interventions across all sectors, especially WASH, health, shelter and protection.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will have a neutral impact on the environment with mitigation and enhancement - IOM will employ environmentally friendly solutions for the implementation of the activities, for example the use of solar panels for the rehabilitation or construction of new water sources. New water sources will only be constructed after a geophysical study is conducted and ground water levels are monitored. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Sector and the targeted communities to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Hygiene and garbage collection campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper/ responsible garbage disposal methods and systems. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; IOM will focus on finding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, scalable, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Experience has shown that solar powered systems reduce running costs by two thirds unlike motorized pumps, which are also noisy, polluting (emitting greenhouse gases which contribute to climate change) and are highly susceptible to fluctuating fuel prices and availability. Solar powered water sources will be prioritized where possible. Furthermore, improved livelihood opportunities to mitigate the negative coping mechanisms of vulnerable communities (ex. cutting down of trees for firewood). Through agricultural training sessions, IOM will also aim to improve the knowledge of local farmers on how to absorb and respond to climatic shocks. Primary health facilities will have proper medical waste management according to WHO and MOH standards. IOM will also provide health programs that raise awareness among refugees and host community about environmental health risks. The awareness campaigns will focus on issues like preventing the spread of infectious disease through vector control, proper waste disposal and personal hygiene.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project activities will be designed in a way to support women, children and people with disabilities. Special attention will be paid to women, lactating mothers or pregnant women. The majority of the displaced refugee women have little to no assets/household goods and have no means to engage in income generating activities as they did before the displacement, therefore, there is a need for star-up kits and training especially designed to cater the specific needs of women. The project will aim to empower women through specifically designed interventions informed by a combination of targeted assessments and consultations, by enhancing their knowledge and skills on asset-based contribution and small businesses management. In addition, as described previously, women will be involved in all decision-making processes and will be consulted from the very start of the project, in the design of the activities and when determining the type of priority activities to be implemented. It will be important to ensure that protection risks and concerns are fully considered in the selection of activities. For ES, protection will cater to the needs of women, children and disabled persons. Locations for ES will be chosen through community consultations to decrease walking distances to key services to reduce associated risks of gender-based violence for women and children. IOM will also ensure to prioritize safety and security issues ensuring that shelters are built in areas where there is access to other basic services and possible land tenure / ownership issues are discussed between local authorities and among the community members before shelters are constructed and to ensure that vulnerable women, children, persons with disabilities or other special needs are prioritized to receive transitional shelters based on needs assessments. For the WASH activities, consultative participatory approaches will also be employed to allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability, especially for the distribution of the Menstrual Hygiene Management kits. IOM will undertake participatory assessments/exercise to identify gender- and age-specific needs and context-specific risks of different groups, especially women and girls, related to access to and safety around service provision. This inclusive participatory approach assured the inclusion of ideas from vulnerable women and girls, and people with disabilities, who are often excluded from community decisions or under represented during decision making processes, especially with regard to selecting the location for the construction/ re- construction of the latrines and rehabilitation of water points with the use of the spare parts to ensure that their needs are not only addressed but protection risks are addressed and mitigated.
