Project Summary

The idea of the project is based on sustainable development and a participatory approach to society by harnessing the resources and resources needed by young people, women, and other stakeholders to support the fish farms and marketing outlets of this product.
The project aims to contribute to efforts to combat poverty and to improve the livelihoods of vulnerable populations to provide food for themselves, their families and communities by providing a cheap source of fish protein by providing support to farmers in the region and providing job opportunities for unemployed youth and marketing support for this product, which suffers from great difficulties due to the weakness of the possibilities available.
The idea of the project will be implemented in stages:
1. The first phase: the establishment of the fish farms according to the distribution of the target areas (10 groups of 10 farmers per group with a total of 100 farmers).
2. Phase 2: Establishment of fish farms or rehabilitation of existing farms at the level of each region and group. Accompanied by the operation of cash-for-work workers in construction, rehabilitation, and processing.
3. Phase 3: Distribution of production inputs (fish, feed, veterinary materials) to support and operate fish for the groups to be targeted in the project, which will be accompanied by raising training capacities in the field of modern techniques in fish farms.
The health aspect is the most important. A mobile veterinary clinic with a range of preventive and curative materials will be provided, in addition to a qualified veterinary team, which will tour the beneficiaries and provide the necessary veterinary services, in addition to providing technical support and guidance to the beneficiaries throughout the year.

4 - Needs

After several meetings that included (focused discussion groups - designer thought sessions - face-to-face meetings) it was found that the most important problems experienced by fish farmers are:1. Adopting traditional methods of fish farming.2. The farmer needs large quantities of chicks, where he is grown in cubic meters of water. 4 Chicks of the comb If the weight of the chick is 40 grams, Dan needs 160 kg of fry at a cost of 112,000 l, equivalent to approximately $ 245.3. Production of large quantities of chicks during the fattening season. This is what the farmers do not want because of the price of a kilogram of the chick at the end of the fattening season and before the winter 150 SYP ($ 0.35) and the cost of production on farms 660 SYP ($ 1.4).4. Fishes fish in mating, which leads to fast food, where the female fasting for food 10 days to embrace eggs and chicks in her mouth. This process is repeated 3 to 5 times during the fattening season, delaying fish maturity and reducing production rates.5. The high cost of rehabilitating the ponds and the lack of dredging mechanisms, since the fish pond needs to be rehabilitated every 2-3 years.6. High feed prices in the market (the price of one kilo of mixed fodder 165 l equivalent to $ 0.35)7. A weakness of veterinary services in the field of fish and semi-absence of their own medicines.8. There is no specialized market for selling fish, where sales are done by sellers on specific days of the week (bazaar).Depending on the problem and the existing needs, we will rely on supporting fish breeders through inputs that will be providedThe project's work strategy, based on the principle of sustainability, provides continuity and continuity of work in the project and the continued availability of the project products in the local markets at competitive prices and the start of successive production cycles with the possibility of increasing quantities of production.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Fish are exposed to disease: Specialized veterinary clinics and integrated pest management through the extension activity of training courses. 2- Lack of Marketing: Targeting cities with a large number of populations.3- Price fluctuations: The team will conduct market assessments to measure the needs of the domestic market and direct the surplus to other markets.4- The inability of the market to absorb production:Because of the decline in purchasing power, people from target communities may face problems in obtaining production inputs. Therefore, it is important that the market assessment determines the extent of local needs and where the remaining product can be redirected.
