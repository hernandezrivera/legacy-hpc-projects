Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to address the life-saving needs of 2600 highly vulnerable conflict-affected IDP, returnee, and host community households, in the Western (1100HHS), Eastern (1100HHS), and Southern (400 HHS) regions of Libya, and improve their access to essential goods and services of their choice at their own discretion and with dignity, but also to enable them to diversify their expenditures and ensure their protection needs. This would be through the delivery of three installments (580 LYD each) unconditional multi-purpose cash-based assistance and through the provision of one-off multi-purpose cash assistance for the emergency response in Tripoli.

Needs

As a matter of fact, according to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), in terms of severity of needs in conflict-affected area, Tripoli, Ejdabia and Sebha fell under severity category ‘Severe problem’ as a level four catastrophic problem, while Benghazi and Derna fell under ‘Critical problem’ as a level five catastrophic problem. As of September 2018, IDPs were identified in the Eastern, Western and Southern part of Libya with 31%, 43% and 26% respectively, according to Round 21 of International Organization for Migration’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM DTM), with 26,865 individuals in Benghazi and 23,555 individuals in Sebha. Within this context, efforts should continuously be put in place in order to support those in dire need, namely Internally Displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities who were similarly impacted by the crisis, to access to basic household goods and commodities including food, safe drinking water, and essential non-food items, which remain among the key humanitarian needs in Libya. Indeed, the situation of IDPs, returnees and host communities alike have been slowly but steadily degrading. The economic crisis combined with general public lack of confidence in the banking system and the shortage of foreign currency has resulted in a major liquidity crisis. The Libyan Central Bank has restricted the distribution of cash in the country, meaning Libyan bank account holders can only access fraction of their salary and savings. The liquidity crisis is having a severe impact on vulnerable people in Libya who have limited access to cash, in what is a cash-based economy. Consequently, an increase in the cost of living, limited access to cash for citizens, and an inability of employers to pay salaries have been observed, which has increasingly hindered access to basic services and goods, especially for IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable households (HHs). According to REACH JMMI January-June 2018, from February onwards, food prices rose steadily to finish in June 2018 at 93.2% of their January level .

Activities

The purpose of this project is to address the life-saving needs of 2600 highly vulnerable conflict-affected IDP, returnee, and host community households, in the Western (1100HHS) (Tripoli, Aljfara), Eastern (1100HHS) (Benghazi and Ejdabyia, with the possibility of a pilot action in Derna), and Southern (400 HHS) (Sebha, Wadi Ashshati) regions of Libya, and improve their access to essential goods and services of their choice at their own discretion and with dignity. This will mitigate the burden caused by liquidity crisis and the conflict by directly providing cash assistance for vulnerable HHs to purchase items and services and enabling them to diversify their expenditures and ensure their protection needs. This would be through the delivery of three instalments (580 LYD each) of gender-sensitive unconditional multi-purpose cash-based assistance. ACTED will maintain a flexible approach, whereby the most feasible and suitable cash delivery mechanism will be selected according to the context. Nonetheless, it is more likely that cash assistance will be mostly delivered to beneficiaries through electronic payment modality in the East, West, and South, if not then other modalities such as bank transfers or vouchers will be explored. ACTED will proactively incorporate its understanding of gender and age discrepancies in the local context at every stage of the project cycle to ensure proposed activities benefit all age groups and genders (women, girls, boys, men and the elderly) based on their specific needs, rights and risks. Considering that female headed households are among the most vulnerable and that the barriers are largely financial in nature; in order to increase the capacity of women to meet their basic needs, ACTED will prioritize female-headed HHs to receive multipurpose cash assistance and will emphasize the priority of that group for 500 HHS in the East and the West, by ensuring a tighter coordination with the protection referral mechanisms established in Benghazi and Tripoli to respond to their protection needs. Consequently, this approach will empower them financially and socially, this will also allow to avoid overlapping and maximize the impact of the intervention. Indeed, vulnerable HHs, identified during the selection phase, in need of specialized protection assistance will be referred to ACTED protection team that will provide them with information on the protection services available in Benghazi. ACTED will also include in the selection process protection cases referred by the ongoing protection monitoring activities and identified as beneficiaries in need of multipurpose cash assistance. While in Tripoli and considering that ACTED is not currently implementing Protection activities there, the beneficiaries identified in need of specialized protection assistance will be referred to the protection cluster that will inform the service providers working in Tripoli of the identified need. Furthermore, as a member of the CMWG Technical Guideline Taskforce and of the CMWG steering committee, ACTED will ensure a tight coordination with the CMWG as well as its members for a better response.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

1. Protection needs/Gender/age analysis: the proposal is gender-informed and ACTED commits to disaggregating all project data by sex, age, disability, household status, etc; including both program and Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) data. ACTED will proactively incorporate its understanding of gender and age discrepancies in the local context at every stage of the project cycle to ensure proposed activities benefit all age groups and genders (women, girls, boys, men and the elderly) based on their specific needs, rights and risks. 2. Cash and Targeting: Households ranking the highest in terms of vulnerability - based on the vulnerability scorecard endorsed by the cash and markets working group the protection sector under the lead of UNHCR will be selected. In addition, households which are female-headed, displaced, have numbers of disabled, critically ill, or pregnant or lactating women households (often more vulnerable) will be considered a priority to ensure that ACTED supports women as actors with economic agency. 3. Protection sensitive complaints mechanisms: Complaints response mechanism (CRM): The CRM allows beneficiaries and other community members to contact ACTED directly via phone or email in order to ask questions or express concerns. ACTED will ensure that beneficiaries are sensitized to the existence, the purpose and the processes of its CRM. AME staff operating the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) will have awareness on basic issues related to protection and gender and gender-based violence so that they can provide those affected with information on where they can obtain support in needed. 4. Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation (AME): in order to ensure the quality and timeliness of project deliveries, ACTED will put in place a robust system of monitoring and evaluation scheme. ACTED’s dedicated AME department will be responsible for: i) collecting data on the impact of the previous cash interventions and will ensure that the lessons learnt are taken in consideration for the development of new proposals, ii) monitoring all activities during the course of the implementation and providing feedback for quality assurance, iii) assessment of the effectiveness of all project components will be included such as, the implementation of the baseline and endline surveys with the beneficiaries of the project. Where baseline and endline surveys are not applicable, in-depth interviews on overall quality and impact of the project will be conducted. 5. ACTED adopts a zero tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse. ACTED is committed to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse both within the organization and within the framework of ACTED programmes and ACTED beneficiary populations. In this context, the aim of ACTED’s PSAE policy is to define expressly sexual exploitation and abuse, to set up a reporting process in the case of a violation of the rules and a specific disciplinary sanction procedure based on the seriousness of the acts committed.
