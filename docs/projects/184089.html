Project Summary

This project aims to provide life-saving support to contribute to the reduction of morbidity associated with malnutrition and prevent livelihood depletion of vulnerable communities through responding to immediate food security & livelihood and protection needs of the target population in Khartoum, Karari, Jabal Awlia and Umdurman and sharg alnile localities of Khartoum State, through improved access to agricultural and livestock inputs and provision of livelihoods support for 8000 floods-affected HHs (24000 beneficiaries) from destitute host communities. The intervention aims to address immediate FSL needs through direct provision of agricultural and livestock inputs including: provision of community-based veterinary services including vaccination and vouche treatment of 20,000 heads of livestock belonging to 8,000 HHs in the 4 localities, to contribute to the beneficiaries’ livestock assets protection and reduce morbidity and mortality rates. Increased animals movement due to large scale displacement of floods affected populations both animals and humans at risk of diseases, hence 40 CAHWs will be provided with refresher training on disease protection, outbreak prevention and control methods, CAHWs play a key role in treating animals and reporting on outbreaks, and other concerns regarding pastures, water resources and livestock problems in remote locations. In this activity: 100,000 doses of animal vaccines will be provided, including: sheep pox, PPR, HS, anthrax and BQ, CAHWs kits and veterinary drugs will be provided to CAHWs for animals’ treatment. Provision of quality assorted seeds mainly legumes including: chick pea, pigeon pea and vegetable seeds including: okra, watermelon and tomatoes to 6,000 vulnerable HHs, in addition to provision of hand tools including: hoes and rakes, this activity aims to enable beneficiaries ensure self-reliance, increase their dietary intake and diversification and will place special focus on women who are involved in farming activities in targeted areas. In addition to 300 farmers targeted by extension training in recommended agricultural practices. To enable the targeted communities to withstand current crises and improve their resilience; restorative livelihood activities and Income Generating Activities (IGAs) will be undertaken including: capacity building in small scale business management and provision of start-up packages whilst mainstreaming gender and environmental protection. IGAs target the vulnerable HHs to create self-employment and improve their income including: Provision of a small stock of goats to 4000 vulnerable female headed HHs with the necessary animal husbandry extension training required for the success of the intervention. To further contribute to women empowerment; 50women will be targeted by training on manufacturing of fuel efficient stoves (FES) and provision of start-up kits, FES decrease firewood consumption hence reduce the time spent by women collecting firewood and their exposure to abuse and SGBV in remote areas, in this activity also promotes environmental conservation as a result of reduced firewood extraction, and reduces health and safety risks associated with using traditional three stone fire. This in additional to dignity kits will be provided to the targeted beneficiaries along with COVID 19 response materials. All of the project activities will be conducted with technical support from line ministries at the state level. VSFG will incorporate best practices in humanitarian response including DRR, NRM and "Do No Harm" approaches in this project.
VSF-G has already implemented protection interventions in Blue Nile State south kordofan and west Darfur through GBV capacity building to government counterpart, stakeholder and capacity building for NNGOs as well as trained more than,6000 women and girls and build them capacity in GBV prevention and responses through the establishment and rehabilitation of more than 12 women and youth multipurpose center.

1 - Needs

According to the joined flood impact rapid assessment that conducted by the FSL sector and the government in last November 2020, coupled by the heavy rains and flood in 2021 the economic crisis, compounded by a set of complex and intersecting factors including the COVID-19 pandemic, climate-induced natural hazards, conflicts, and rapid and unpredictable soaring food prices have led to a deterioration of the food security and nutrition situation in the Sudan. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) for June–September 2020 estimates that 9.6 million people in the Sudan are in Crisis or worse levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) including 2.2 million people in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). This is the highest figure ever recorded in the history of the IPC in the country. Additionally, nearly 15.9 million people are estimated to be in Stress (IPC Phase 2). These numbers indicate that most people have been shifting to worse phases of food insecurity. The recent floods have exacerbated and intensified the food insecurity, malnutrition and livelihoods impoverishment of already vulnerable populations and further put them at risk of falling into more severe phases of food insecurity – such as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and Phase 5 (Famine), if no robust external support from resource partners is provided. Since July 2020, through mid-September, continued torrential rains and flooding combined with the historical overflow of the River Nile and its tributaries have affected all the states in the Sudan with the exception of South Darfur, causing devastating damage alongside riverbanks in the northern, central and eastern regions of the country. This has caused widespread damage in a range of sectors, including and most significantly, the agriculture sector. Floods and waterlogging due to intense rainfall heavily damaged crop and pasture lands in the Sudan. Flash floods also caused major losses of agricultural inputs, tools and pumps, as well as the destruction of agriculture and livestock service facilities. Limited movement and access to farms and inputs was also recorded, affecting the livelihoods and food security of thousands of already vulnerable communities across the country. Moreover, the wet environment constitutes favorable conditions for the spreading of weeds, plant pests and livestock diseases, posing additional risks to the agriculture and livestock sectors. The key findings of the assessment, revealed that 597 689 farming and pastoral HHs in the 80 assessed localities including Khartoum state have been affected by the floods and heavy rains. This number includes 527968 farming HHs in rain-fed areas 49 200 farming households in the irrigated sector and 20521 pastoral households. About 42 percent of the total affected households are female-headed. Approximately 2216 362 ha of cropland is reported to have been damaged in the 15 assessed states including Khartoum. In addition to the damages to crops, the livestock sector was also severely impacted with the loss of more than 108000 heads of livestock particularly sheep, goats, poultry, and cattle, belonging to about 20521 households. Additional findings of the assessment show that affected populations have begun to engage in distress coping mechanisms such as borrowing money and selling productive assets to access food. Levels of debt are escalating and affected populations will need to access cash as soon as possible to repay their debts. Restocking of sheep, goats and chickens should take place as soon as possible to avoid a sharp decrease in family income and animal protein intake. According to HRP2020 for GBV SS the Sharg Alnile locality at Khartoum state one of high risk level and its need for emergency intervention addressed that 28550people in need and still responses very week to GBV services the sector highlighting that still there is gap in dignity kits, and GBV services need to improve through strengthening referral system.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The proposed grant will enhance improved resource management and equal access to available resources. Furthermore, VSF G has considered seriously the ‘environmental marker across all the project designed activities. ’The project will protect and restore the natural resource base in the livelihoods systems of the poor and vulnerable households through redressing environmental degradation, increasing efficiency and promoting conservation of soil, water and biomass resources. Due to potential waste generation through medical supplies (vaccination and Treatment campaigns) VSF G will ensure mitigation measures are in place such as proper waste management of the disposable elements of animal vaccination and treatment. The waste will be incinerated safely and each training will include element of environment protection

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF-G adopts IASC standers for protection and gender mainstreaming the project will seek involvement of both men and women putting more emphasis on the participation of women in community-based committees. The project will make use of different approaches to equally reach both genders based on needs, roles and responsibilities. As well as VSF_G will take into account UNHCR principles as protection coordinator and UNFPA principles as GBV SS coordinator In this interventions, hence incorporates protection principles into its programming through promoting inclusive and meaningful access to services provided, considers the safety and dignity of the project beneficiaries and target communities in designing implementation modalities. VSF-G will ensure participation, consultation and inclusion of all community ethnic, age and gender groups to guarantee that unintended negative effects that might increase the vulnerability of targeted people are prevented and duly mitigated. Risks will be assessed at the beginning of this action, and then regularly to ensure access and participation of especially marginalized and or at-risk groups (i.e. elderly, disabled) is guaranteed. Through implementing accountability to the affected population with effective feedback and response mechanisms to measure the adequacy of the services provided and satisfaction of the target population and to effectively and timely address valid complaints. Empowering the communities through involving them in decision-making, promotion of participation and capacity building of the communities with regard to their rights and responsibilities. This will be ensured by establishing dialogue and orientation in community meetings.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Through implementing accountability to the affected population with effective feedback and response mechanisms to measure the adequacy of the services provided and satisfaction of the target population and to effectively and timely address valid complaints. Empowering the communities through involving them in decision-making, promotion of participation and capacity building of the communities with regard to their rights and responsibilities. VSF-G adopts Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. IASC commitments with regard to AAP are incorporated in this plan including governance and integration of feedback and response mechanisms into program design, maintaining transparency and dialogue between the organization and the affected population, participation, and involvement of the target population in project design, monitoring, and evaluation of this project. All the while mainstreaming protection and considering the safety and confidentiality of the target population. VSF-G focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and or marginalized groups. Community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness with regard to their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the project partners towards them. Participation will be promoted through the involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. The community committees will be created to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. Representatives from a cross-section (with respect to age, gender, and diversity) will be ensured to participate as community committee members and in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response.
