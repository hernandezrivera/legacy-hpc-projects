Project Summary

Needs: Mitigating protection risks remains a priority in NWS, with increased needs following the earthquake in February 2023. ERL needs remain high in NWS according to the HNO 2024. As job opportunities have decreased sharply in Syria with the ongoing conflict, small businesses represent one of the most feasible sources of income aside from agriculture. Activities: The action will target 3,574 individuals located in Jandairis, Afrin, Raju, Sharan, Azaz subdistricts, locations which are listed as underserved, based on identified needs. The project comprises two pillars: (1) provision of business training, soft skills and grants for small businesses to develop and grow; and (2) provision of community led protection services to reduce the protection risks through the reactivation of community-based mechanisms and initiation of community projects. Women will be prioritized by the small business grants. Participants to the training will be invited to submit business plans and will be mentored and coached to expand their businesses. PRA, incl. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

Extended needs description for project sectors: As job opportunities have decreased sharply in Syria with the ongoing conflict, small businesses represent one of the most feasible sources of income aside from agriculture. An ILO Technical assessment prepared in March 2023 notes that the earthquakes have severely affected micro and small enterprises, with the activity of an estimated 35,000 micro and small establishments disrupted. The loss of these businesses goes beyond the loss in incomes and encompasses the cost of the physical damage to their infrastructure, such as buildings, equipment, and inventory. Additionally, almost 144,000 people were disemployed in Idleb and Aleppo with an estimated 5.1 million USD loss per month as income. ILO recommendations focuses on supporting MSMEs to rebuild their assets and offer employment opportunities for local population. In terms of protection risks, the impact of the earthquakes has significantly increased unintentional family separations in NW, primarily because of the loss of lives and displacement. Family reunification processes have been difficult, with the loss of civil documentation worsening the situation. Overcrowding and a lack of privacy in collective centers and other types of shelters contributed to an increase in gender-based violence. Despite people with disabilities and older people being identified as some of the most at-risk groups, the response to their specific needs has been lacking or remains very limited. The loss of civil documentation, especially related to housing, land, and property and identity (such as birth certificates), prevents people from accessing services and recovering their belongings. Mental health and psychosocial support needs are extremely high and require a more specialized psychological response following the earthquakes’ impact on a population already exposed to significant levels of conflict related trauma. Additionally, the use of child labor, forced and early marriages, and substance abuse as coping strategies are on the rise. Eviction and forced displacement continue to be constant threats in an already unstable region.
