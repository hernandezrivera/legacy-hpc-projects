Project Summary

This project aims to promote food security and livelihood assistance through the support of farmers and animal husbandry among the extreme vulnerable returnees and local communities in west Anbar governorates of Iraq. The overall objective of this project is to contribute to decrease the vulnerability of the targeted returnees and local communities through the support of farmers, youth among males and females and women in the proposed targeted areas. 1000 HHs will be economically supported through livelihood intervention in the sectors of agriculture and livestock, 500 HHs will be supported through distribution of seeds, fertilizers, and fuel for agriculture farmers and 250 HHs will be supported with livestock inputs (2 female sheep and 1 Male sheep per family) and fodders for animal breeders.The project will mainly contribute to ensure that food security is promoted through the improvement of agriculture productions as well as improve income generation for the targeted families and promote the stability of living of these families in theri origins after a prolonged displacement as a result of conflict and the rapid return to their homes. This project will also ensure the alleviation of suffering of the targeted beneficiaries through the provision of qualitative assistance to the targeted families which will enable them to secure income that will help in supporting their family members and children to provide them with nutritional values and improve daily food intake of the family members and children.
The project will also form community committees among men and women in the targeted area to act as a link between the project and targeted beneficiaries, represent the beneficiaries, and be part of the service delivery monitoring process. The formed committees will be part of the rapid assessment to identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries among the returnees and local communities. The project also aims at introducing cash flow into the targeted communities through the rehabilitation of agriculture schemes through Cash for Work, Cash for work will be carried out by engaging unskilled and skilled labourers to rehabilitate and clear access roads, irrigation channels, bunds and irrigation structures. The cash for work will be implemented during the project life cycle. However, it will provide employment opportunities among skilled and unskilled local labours for as the proposed infrastructure is designed to be labour intended collective activity. The objective of CfW activity is to reintroduce cash flows into beneficiary communities, revitalize local markets and restore basic economic functions. The availability of short-term employment helps prevent the selling of fundamental assets such as livestock, equipment, land or even household furniture. Also, this will reduce the chances of indulgence in crime or security incidents.
IRW has a strong presence in the proposed targeted areas. IRW will coordinate with FSC, WFP, relevant UN agencies, Department of Agriculture, local authorities, Area Leaders (Mokhtar), and relevant working actors (I/NNGO). IRW has formed community committees among the targeted community member to who represent their areas and speak up for the beneficiaries needs and entitlement. The project team will coordinate with the working I/NNGOs about the project intervention to reduce the gap, avoid duplication and overlapping, and ensure all actors in the targeted areas are aware about the implemented intervention. In addition, Information sharing will be done on monthly basis through the cluster.

Needs

The agriculture sector is vital to Iraq’s economy and has been the second largest contributor to the country’s gross domestic product (GDP). The Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) has escalated armed activities in the summer of 2014 at a particularly crucial time in the agricultural calendar, leading to significant damage and loss to the sector. Harvested wheat, barley and vegetables were lost due to population displacements, looting and the burning of grain bins. Farmers were unable to plant for the next agricultural season. According to a research conducted in March 2016, Iraq has lost 40 percent of agricultural production since ISIL began occupying some of the most important agricultural areas in 2014, with damages continuing from the current armed conflict. In this context, the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) has conducted an agricultural damage and loss needs assessment in December 2017, across six of Iraq’s 18 governorates – Anbar, Babil, Diyala, Ninewa, Salah al-Din and Wassit. The assessment collected field data through community-level focus group discussions and key informant interviews.The need to revitalize agricultural activities and livelihoods across these areas is unquestionable – not only for the communities in these regions, but for Iraq as a whole, given the role that agriculture plays in the livelihoods of the rural population. Anbar Governorate is in western Iraq and shares borders with Syria, Jordan and Saudi Arabia. In early 2014 Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) took control of part of its territory, including Falluja. By the end of June, ISIL expanded their control to 70 percent of Anbar governorate. As of November 2015, to date, Iraqi Security Forces (ISF) managed to retake Ramadi, Falluja, Heet, Rutba and Ana districts; leaving only two main cities in western Anbar under ISIL control: Ru'ua and Ka'im.The latest conflict in Iraq started in 2014 when ISIS extended its control to the northern governorates of Iraq, Ninawa. Beside Ninawa, ISIS has controlled large parts of Anbar and Salah-El--Den governorates. ISIS occupied territories in Anbar since 2013 and extended to control Mosul and Ninawa governorate in June 2014.When the Iraqi government started its operations to retake the city of Mosul in October 2016, the country was already in continuous conflict since four years. According to the HRP 2019, estimated 6.5 million people are currently in need of assistance. In December 2017 the government announced that the Iraqi land is completely liberated from ISIS. Hence the government started encouraging the IDPs to go back to their origins in Anbar, Mosul, and SalahEl Den governorates. Many of the returning areas were destroyed through the struggle of the government to liberate it. Houses in these areas suffered of damage that vary between total damage, severe damage, major damage and minor damage according to classification of UNHCR Shelter Cluster.However the issue is still that many of the houses are either fully damaged or partially damaged which hinders the return of the families to their homes“Around 38% of internally displaced Iraqis have returned home since the beginning of the crisis in 2014. In a press release published on 13 December 2017, IOM announced that more than 1.84 million displaced Iraqis have returned to their areas of origin, mostly to Anbar and Ninewa governorates. However, more than 2.78 million people remain displaced. While IOM findings indicate that 90 per cent of displaced Iraqis are determined to return home, an ongoing lack of security is cited by displaced people as the main obstacle to return to the place of origin, whether due to ongoing conflict, presence of unexploded ordnances, landmines, or militias. Concerns over economic security also persist, with 80 per cent of displaced people and 63 per cent of returnees citing access to employment as one of their top needs. Meanwhile, residential and infrastructure damage remains widespread, with nearly one third of returnees reportedly returning to houses that have suffered significant damage” (According to UNHCR Flash Update – Iraq-27 December 2017).The poor economic situation of the country, in general, and for the returnees who suffered of years of displacement have hindered many Iraqi people from starting the reparation of their damaged houses.Although, cities like Ramadi, Falluja, Heet and others have been declared liberated since mid of 2015 and 2016, many houses are still not repaired due to the lack of its owners of financial resources.Currently, Iraq is moving towards a post-conflict context, the Iraqi government has announced that it expects IDPs to return to their homes by April 2018, with the demobilization of the Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) in disputed territories ideally resulting in a more secure and conducive environment for IDPs to return.

Harassment of Beneficiaries women during the project implementation and project input distribution 6. Project contract holders and supplier exploitation of child labour

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Mechanism: 1. To mitigate this risk, the project team will induct the targeted communities on the project beneficiaries selection criteria and ensure that the formed community committees are well oriented on the targeted beneficiaries selection criteria, so the community committees will be be involved to induct the targeted communities accordingly. In addition, this project will also target local communities in the targeted areas based on the specified criteria under this project. 2. To mitigate this risk, Islamic Relief Iraq will ensure well-reference mechanism is done when project staff are recruited. The hired staff under this project will be introduced to internal policies and procedures including harassment policies, safeguarding policy, sexual exploitation. 3. Regular security update will be maintained by the project team and security focal persons involving the clusters, INSO, and the local authorities. If security situation get deteriorated in the targeted project area due to any reason, Islamic Relief Iraq will avoid areas under conflict and will concentrate on comparatively safer areas for project activities implementation and might propose change the targeted area in case of inaccessibility.4. IRW inducts the current staff and the newly joint staff on code of conduct, safeguarding policy, child protection, and harassment policy 5. IRW usually plans their distribution to women in different days of men’s and where needed separate lines for men and women. In addition during the distribution of the project of inputs to women, the project team will select safe and reachable area for women and ensures the attendance to the women guardian, community members, and area leaders. The project team will induct the targeted communities on the right of the selected entitled women and the ownership of the provided inputs to men and women.6. IRW procurement unit and Program unit always inducts selected supplier on child labour policy and safeguarding policy. The signed contract of IRW with supplier consist of a clear article states that suppliers must not hire or mobilize children in the delivered work and addition to keeping the dignity of the targeted beneficiaries.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Islamic Relief is committed to ensure that its program interventions are appropriate and designed in a way which positively contributes in lives of children and communities. To abide by this commitment, Islamic Relief has a Monitoring, Evaluation and Accountability (MEA) system which aims at improving quality of our interventions and document key learning, at the same time MEA provide timely information to program staff and senior management to make critical and strategic decisions regarding our program interventions.The project Coordinator will take the lead role in conducting a series of community mobilization sessions at targeted locations in form of public gatherings where project goal, objectives and funded activities will clearly be outlined. The project M&E Coordinator and Project Coordinator will also be responsible for complains-feedback mechanisms whereby project beneficiaries, local authorities and other key community key people will freely contact them (through a shared and displaced phone number and complaint box ). All complains will be registered, classified (based on the seriousness of the complaint), relevant departments/sectors consulted and timely feedback relayed back to the complainant/community leaders. The project will also provide opportunities for the communities to engage directly in the project implementation thus stakeholders will be encouraged to raise their concerns.
