Project Summary

According to the LCRP (2017-2020), child mothers, early married girls, unaccompanied and separated boys and girls, women and girls with disabilities, older women, and female heads of households continue to be the most at risk. The most commonly reported type of GBV as reported in the GBVIMS and IMC data from its work across Lebanon involves physical violence, mainly linked to violence within the family, sexual violence (rape and sexual assault), as well as forced and early marriage. Protection risks for all targeted populations remains high, with women and girls facing significant risks of multiple forms of GBV. The economic insecurity combined with different levels of crisis affecting the country as well as risks related to legal status, add to these vulnerabilities.
GBV is a pervasive public health and human rights issue, affecting the physical and psychological health of survivors, as well as the health and well-being of families and communities. Throughout the health and economic crisis that Lebanon is going through, the frequency, severity and risk of GBV, especially affecting young women and girls is increasing. Preventing, mitigating, and responding to GBV in emergencies is a lifesaving priority and an essential component of humanitarian action, especially in a country with extreme structural gender inequalities. Lebanon ranks 145 out of 153 countries in the World Economic Forum Global Gender Gap Report.
The below proposed activities focus on survivor-centered care with case management teams working with local actors in order to meet the immediate, lifesaving needs of women and girls while laying the groundwork for survivors of GBV, their families and their communities to recover. Under Indicator 1.1, the project will be providing non-focused PSS for 375 participants, dignity kits to 3000 beneficiaries and awareness sessions to 1725 non-unique beneficiaries. There will be an overlap of beneficiaries for this activity where beneficiaries that attend non-focused PSS activities will also be attending awareness sessions (this also applies for the recipients of the dignity kits). Under Indicator 4.1, IMC will be providing case management and focused PSS for 260 beneficiaries. As part of GBV case management, emergency cash assistance will also be offered. IMC will provide the services in four safe spaces, 2 in Akkar, 1 in Beirut and 1 in the South. GBV department is well established in these areas with well-trained Case managers and Social workers.
Based on IMC’s safety audits previously conducted, focus group discussions implemented in the area and PDM reports, the objectives of the ERP directly address the needs of the communities especially vulnerable women and girls. IMC will organize empowerment activities for women and adolescent girls and will also reach men and boys in awareness raising activities. IMC will coordinate with local actors, authorities, and other sectors to strengthen GBV prevention and response and will continue working with communities to shift social norms that contribute to GBV and threaten the protection and wellbeing of women, girls and individuals at risk.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Specific measures and interventions to mitigate, respond to and prevent GBV must be undertaken from the onset of a crisis and continue through transition and recovery efforts. This intervention will also support in the following: - Ensure that quality case management services continue to be available. - Strengthen community protection mechanisms to ensure that vulnerable populations, especially persons that identify as LGBTIQ+, persons with disabilities, women head of households and elderly have access to required services, such as health care, livelihood and case management. - Strengthen the collaboration with the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support sector for the availability of mental health and psychosocial services to all persons. - Foster collaboration and promote joint initiatives with Child Protection actors to mitigate the risks of GBV against children including adolescent girls/boys and in particular against sexual abuse and exploitation. - Evaluate the effectiveness of access to remote activities for women, girls and marginalized groups and develop a plan to improve inclusiveness of remote activities. - Links will be made to the Internal Security Forces (ISF) who are responding to the domestic violence (1745) hotline. - Ensure the distribution of hygiene and dignity kits for vulnerable women and girls. - Provide emergency cash assistance to reduce the risks of protection issues among vulnerable women.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IMC operates using best practices of safeguarding and protection mainstreaming. All GBV activities are implemented while taking into account principles of safety and do no harm, meaningful access including to minorities and people with disability, participation of community members in project design and implementation (through existing women committees and outreach volunteers from the community), and accountability through established strong complaints and feedback mechanisms. IMC Lebanon has a built-in solid PSEA framework throughout all its operations. Staff are trained on PSEA and are aware of internal reporting mechanisms and dedicated PSEA trained focal points are mobilized across the different project locations. Through its existing projects, IMC monitors trends of protection and GBV risks by collecting feedback from program beneficiaries, community volunteers and community-based actors and adapts its project focus accordingly.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IMC Lebanon conducts needs assessments to identify areas of interventions where affected communities are critically in need. In addition to key informant interviews, Lebanon team conducts focus groups discussions with the affected communities in order to identify beneficiaries’ needs and areas of intervention. IMC Lebanon employs mechanisms and approaches to ensure the participation of the affected communities and enable them to play a role in taking decisions affecting their lives. In order to make sure that beneficiaries’ feedback is received on a regular basis, IMC Lebanon conducts yearly patient satisfaction surveys and post-distribution monitoring. IMC Lebanon implements a Community Based Feedback and Response Mechanism, a set of clear, transparent procedures that provide affected communities with access to safe, timely and confidential means of voicing complaints related to supported services and activities.
