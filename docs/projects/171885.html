Project Summary

Through its Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) activities, NRC Ukraine will ensure that its beneficiaries have increased knowledge of and access to rights related to legal documentation, pension payments, access to services, IDP registration, freedom of movement, and Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights and durable solutions. ICLA’s efforts will be organized with a key focuses on sustainability through local partnerships and providing services to hard-to-reach and contact-line communities as well as increasing the outreach to conflict-affected population via digital and remote means of communication.
NRC ICLA aims to support conflict-affected population in eastern Ukraine (CAP), broadly defined as individuals who have suffered harm, including physical or mental injury, emotional suffering, economic loss or substantial impairment of their human rights and fundamental freedoms as a direct result of the conflict in eastern Ukraine. CAP include: IDP (registered or not); people at risk of displacement and people who are unable to flee; returnees; host families or communities.
With the aim to address the humanitarian protection needs in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, NRC will implement a multi-faceted response. NRC will maintain a significant level of the direct implementation of protective legal aid to CAP, while strategically moving forward with a transition of service delivery to local partners via a series of activities aimed at transferring key skills and experience, enhancing relevant capacity and establishing a localized paralegal network.
NRC will maintain the current direct legal aid modalities like in-person reception of beneficiaries in Legal Aid Centres, field visits and hotline maintenance, while also improving the provision of legal aid via such digital means as email, Skype and messengers in order to maintain the former to tackle the challenges imposed by the COVID-19 Pandemic.
The network of paralegals – activists providing free legal aid services to member of their respective settlements, will be established in order to enable access to justice and free legal aid provision in the contact-line communities and build resilience of local communities.
As the current beneficiary caseload could not be fully catered by the national actors only, ICLA will embark on a holistic technical and institutional capacity enhancement initiative with the aim to enable sustainable and resilient transition process. Thus, the target group for such activities will include
duty-bearer beneficiaries - central, regional and local legislative, executive and judicial authorities responsible for various aspects of the CAP issues, and other stakeholder beneficiaries - professional national associations/institutions (Law Faculties and Legal Aid Clinics, Free Legal Aid Centres) and nation-wide, long-standing NGOs, as well as local civil society organisations / initiatives / associations representing interests of conflict-affected people.
NRC will tie the mentioned activities together through advocacy on key protection issues impacting Ukrainians affected by conflict through working groups, technical forums, and by supporting partners in strategic litigation.
NRC Ukraine will ensure that a strong emphasis on reaching the most vulnerable beneficiaries will be put, while also securing the sufficient level of gender mainstreaming efforts within all its activities via involvement of different age and gender groups into Programme response design. The vulnerable target groups will include (but not limited to), women/single headed households, especially with a large number of children, elderly (particularly living alone), people with disabilities or limited mobility, extremely poor and no-income families, people of pre-retirement age, people living in rural or remote areas. It is important to stress that this will include persons from GCA and NGCA, the latter being targeted through a range of modalities.

1 - Needs

On 14 April 2020, hostilities in eastern Ukraine entered their sixth year. The human toll of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine is critical, with 5.2 million people affected by the crisis, of whom 2.8 million require protection assistance (over 680,000 people living along within 20km from the contact line (GCA), 1.3 million in non-Government controlled areas and 810,000 in GCA 20+km from the contact-line). Civilians living in the vicinity of the 457-kilometers long contact line continue facing serious risks to their safety and disproportionate restrictions of the freedom of movement and access to basic necessities. More than 55,000 residential buildings have been damaged in the conflict on both sides of the contact line. The protracted hostilities and connected social, political and economic consequences have led to decrease in economic activity, widespread job loss, problems of salary arrears, suspension of pensions and social benefits and record-high prices. This, in turn, has caused increased food insecurity levels, loss of access to services and livelihoods, and the application of negative coping strategies from food reduction to depletion of savings to illegal or high-risk jobs for people living both in NGCA and in GCA. The Government of Ukraine (GoU) has registered almost 1.46 million internally displaced people (IDP), of which a half reside in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts, beyond the 20 km area along the contact line. Many IDPs continue to face bureaucratic impediments and discrimination due to various legislative acts and policies adopted since the beginning of the conflict such as the introduction of residence, identification and verification procedures, the imposition of payment of pensions and social benefits through one State bank only - and solely based on an IDP certificate. Internally displaced women are more vulnerable than men, often more financially dependent on other family members than men, more likely to be taking care of children (a 14.5 percent gender gap), less active on the labour market and in starting their own businesses, and much more frequently faced with insufficient funds and forced to economize than internally displaced men. Moreover, while the compensation procedure for destroyed housing was established, its correct and full implementation is yet to witnessed, which will require support from protection actors to streamline this process accordingly. In addition, local institutions and organizations still require technical and institutional capacity building support that will enable the latter to both cater the full caseload of free legal aid needs and provide holistic service package to CAP once INGO service providers phase out of Ukraine. The target groups for such capacity building activities include local courts, Free Legal Aid Centres, local NGOs, Legal University Clinics etc. The situation with access to justice and free legal aid was worsened by the COVID-19 Pandemic consequences, as evidenced in the Report on Human Rights Situation in Ukraine. The following could be mentioned among the issues arose as results of Pandemic: hindered physical access to free legal aid and/or governmental institutions; disruption of social services provision; complexity of in-person legal aid provision; delayed trials; lack of timely and sufficient legal information provision etc. The mentioned issues create additional request for remote and digital service provision, including timely information dissemination and awareness raising on most acute CAP-related legal issues. This is especially important for NGCA residents and those crossing EECPs, as with COVID-19 restrictions, their access to services and free legal aid is greatly hindered and lack of information and awareness of current procedures becomes critical. Thus, this indicates a great need in unified digital information platform to enable access to timely and reliable information on situation at EECPs and procedures for crossing them.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Legal aid provision remains one of the key protection and durable solutions interventions needed for conflict affected people in eastern Ukraine. -ICLA will continue to provide information, counselling and legal assistance in:-ICLA Legal Aid Centres (LACs); -During mobile (home) visits to the most vulnerable;-ICLA desks at local municipal, council or village premises; employment and community; -Partner’s offices,-At the EECPs along the contact line. -With the aim to tackle the issues of access to justice and free legal aid connected to the COVID-19 Pandemic, ICLA services will be also provided through a variety of remote and digital modalities, such as: -Telephone hotline;-Email;-Viber;-Skype.A special emphasis will be put in reaching out to NGCA beneficiaries and residents of contact-line hard-to-reach communities, as these two groups had their access capacities hindered the most and often have no means of receiving requested legal aid otherwise.In order to sufficiently enable the access to free legal aid, social services and assistance from other NGOs, a Paralegal Project will be continued. Local Paralegals, identified and trained by NRC together with respective stakeholders, will provide basic legal aid within their contact-line communities, while also allowing to refer complex cases to professional legal service providers and non-legal questions to respective institutions.Information provision and awareness raising will be also conducted through numerous modalities, including ICLA Facebook Page; dissemination of printed materials, group information sessions, TV and Radio and websites.Moreover, in pursuance of addressing the gap with information provision to NGCA residents and those crossing the contact line, NRC will support the initiate of the Ministry of Reintegration and provide technical and funding support in development of a Digital Information Platform. In addition, to mitigate risks and enable digital information provision, Webinar Information Sessions will be conducted.Capacity building activities (training, seminars, workshops etc.) will be conducted to enhance the skills and knowledge of respective stakeholders.In close coordination with NRC’s Advocacy team, ICLA will strengthen the use of research and advocacy resources to address systemic issues of concern for targeted CAP beneficiaries. In its turn, NRC Ukraine Advocacy will focus on:1) Improving the legal and policy frameworks on access to pensions, FoM, civil documentation, HLP rights, including compensation.2) Promoting the rights-based approach to DS for IDPs focusing on their local integration and supporting IDPs' participation in policy-making and their ability to advocate for their rights.3) Supporting the authorities in developing & implementing the evidence-based policies and provision of services to CAPs/IDPs, including the Ministry of Reintegration, the regional state administrations and local authorities.-NRC will continue to lead the HLP TWG and coordinate the efforts of humanitarian actors to tackle the HLP issues. NRC ensures participation of CAP and IDPs in its programming by inclusion of beneficiaries in all project cycle phases. This is achieved by conducting regular assessments, beneficiary satisfaction evaluations, pre-strategy surveys, focus group discussions, and key informant interviews with relevant stakeholders. The findings of the respective assessment actions are evaluated during the ICLA Strategy sessions and ICLA meetings on an ongoing basis with the aim to improve the response and identify new modalities that address needs of target groups.Accountability: NRC policy places a heavy emphasis on ‘rights based’ programming, in which accountability plays an important role. NRC will adhere to the Ukraine Accountability Framework as recommended by the STAIT as both HLP TWG lead and as a program implementer. NRC has a comprehensive Complaints and Feedback mechanism that is independent from programme operations.

3 - Indicators & targets

• 6 000 beneficiaries receive accurate, timely and relevant primary legal aid (information and counselling) on HLP, LCD, IDR, access to services and employment/livelihoods directly from NRC or via Partners.• 1 400 beneficiaries supported through the Paralegals Project (community-based protection initiative).• 500 beneficiaries receive secondary legal aid (legal assistance) on HLP, LCD, IDR, Access to Services and employment/livelihoods (secondary legal aid);• 200 000 of estimated beneficiaries benefit from receiving information on HLP, documentation, IDP residence registration, social benefits, legal assistance or entitlements through ICLA Remote modalities;• 300 persons referred to authorities or humanitarian partners for assistance in areas outside of ICLA or NRC Ukraine competences;• At least 1 Downstream Partner of NRC ICLA will conduct its activities with financial support of the latter;• 100 duty bearer and other stakeholders beneficiaries benefit from ICLA provided capacity-building activities, while 8 trainings are conducted.• 1 Digital Information Platform created to enable information provision to NGCA residents on EECP Crossing Points;• At least 17 contact-line communities benefit from presence of local paralegals, tasked with provision of basic legal aid and referrals to other organizations;• 12 HLP TWG/Task Forces meetings conducted aiming at searching for and developing solutions for pending HLP issues in Ukraine;• 25 advocacy interventions carried out at local, national and international level (meetings, events, round-tables, polling, coalition and network building; media outreach and partnerships; briefings /presentations; issuing analysis/research; strategic litigation; lobbying; policy proposal development). In 2021, the project might be amended according to needs assessment conducted/changes in ICLA Strategy.
