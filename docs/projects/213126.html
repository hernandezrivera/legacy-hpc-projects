Project Summary

The project will provide vocational skills training, apprenticeship support, and small business grants to selected 300 unemployed men, women, youth, and adolescents to support income generation and livelihood restoration across Al-Raqqa and Aleppo Governorates.
Vocational skill training for youth and adolescents (300) will be supported based on the Labour Market Assessment to provide market-demanded vocational skills for those without prior skills. This will be accompanied by two months of training (following the NGO’s standard curriculum). The selected youths will be linked with the relevant businesses in the local markets to provide apprenticeship opportunities leading to employment opportunities for the skilled youth and improving youth workforce participation. The trainees will be provided with a stipend during vocational skills and apprenticeships to cover transportation and incidental costs whereas selected youth with potential business interest and sound business plans on completion of the 4-8 weeks’ apprenticeships schemes will be provided with the business grants to support start-ups. An approx. 50 HHs (300 people) will be provided with small business grants based on competitive assessments of the business plans.
People with existing vocational skills and business experience (who no longer have operational businesses due to the ongoing conflict) will be supported through small business grants (600-800 USD each) to restart or restore businesses, supporting the self-reliance and self-sufficiency of the households. Before supporting such businesses, the NGO will provide comprehensive business management skills training to the selected participants, where business plans will be selected based on a market assessment to support businesses that are in line with the market needs and have opportunities for growth and sustainability. The business management skills training will also cover basic financial and numeracy skills training to improve participants’ financial skills.
The 100 households (600 people) will be selected from the NGO’s unconditional food or cash assistance, protection of education programme supporting multi-sectoral integration, and promoting socio-economic resilience of the most vulnerable households.
The application process will consist of a basic presentation of the business plan, cost analysis, marketing and pricing strategy, detailed budget, and income and expenditure forecasts. The applications will be evaluated based on specific criteria. The grant is designed to support the most promising and well-planned small businesses. The NGO will grant a maximum of 600-800 USD each to an estimated 100 applicants. The NGO’s livelihoods team will continue the follow-up with the grant recipients, provide additional coaching support, and verify the adequate use of the grant.

Protection Risk Analysis:The NGO will support small and micro-based businesses tailored to the skills and capacities which the participant may already have and which the person can utilize at home, especially for those with difficulty leaving the home due to childcare responsibilities (in the case of women and female-headed households) or disability. As with all the NGO's programming, this project will adhere to a “not harm” approach to ensure that any support provided will not put the participants at risk. Livelihoods staff will be trained on protection identify protection risks and make appropriate referrals to the NGO’s protection programme. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategyThe NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of the IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and training with support from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA network's developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measuresRisks; Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following:- Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and the Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. - IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with the extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas - Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict jeopardising access to affected project areas.- Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations Assumptions;- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no significant shifts in control over key locations in NES.- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme. - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally. Mitigating Measures:- Strengthen operational resilience - In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to the nearest unaffected base of operations.- Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and the availability of key goods.
