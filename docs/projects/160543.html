Project Summary

The project will reach the most vulnerable 195,000 people (70,200 girls, 46,800 women, 35,100 boys and 42,900 men) which corresponds to 32,500 HH (in Nassir 7000HH, Maiwut 6500HH, Akobo 7500HH, Leer 5000HH and Fangak 6500HH). We shall contribute to FSL cluster objective 1 ,2 and 3 by providing production inputs (crop kits, fast growing vegetable kits and fishing kits) and Capacity building, skills & knowledge development in line with cluster objective 2 and 3. This will improve food production and provision of fishing kits will contribute to better nutrition at the HH level and in other cases improve HH income due to the sale of excess fish, this will take place from January 2019 to December 2019. We shall also provide unconditional cash assistance in line with FSL cluster objective 1 lifesaving food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable population. This response plan is aimed at an effort to prevent an even more severe food shortage as well as strengthening the coping capacities of the vulnerable host community and IDPs. Nile Hope shall liaise with FAO to get in kind support for the crop fishing and vegetable kits for the emergency responses but will procure for the other ongoing developmental projects
The production inputs will be given to 27,500 HH (16,500 Female HH and 11,000 Male HH) 7500 in Akobo,5000 HH in Leer,5500HH in Fangak,4500 HH in Maiwut and 5000 HH in Nassir ,which corresponds to 165,000 beneficiaries vulnerable Host Communities and IDPs and unconditional cash assistance to 5000HH (2000 HH in Maiwut,2000HH in Nassir and 1000HH in Fangak) which corresponds to 30,000 beneficiaries. Special focus will be given to people with disability to make sure they are not left out. Capacity building training will be conducted on improved crop, vegetable and fishing practices and continuous practical sessions in the farms to selected recipient beneficiaries at the Payam level as TOTs to the rest of the Community for sustainability purposes targeting 6,500 people (3900 women and 2600 men). The overall project objective is improved food security and livelihoods at house hold level for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition

1 - Needs

The crisis in South Sudan is marked by alarming levels of hunger, 4.54 million people facing emergency levels of acute severe food insecurity, (WFP Situation report #255, 2.1 million acutely malnourished women and children (HNO) & 1.47million IDPs. Akobo has witnessed high influx of IDPs from the surrounding counties of Nyirol & Uror where insecurity & conflict pushed most people to flee while others moved in search for water and pasture for their livestock, it is classified in IPC 4 (IPC August, 2019) and likely to experience severe food shortage due to poor harvest caused by flooding & the high number of IDPs giving pressure to the host community. NH aims at preventing food shortage by providing production inputs (fishing, crop &vegetable kits), also with the ongoing support from EU shall continue building their resilience by supporting producer groups through capacity building training on improved production, post harvest loses, value addition & marketing. Leer is one of the most affected counties in Unity State due to protracted conflict in the area, August 2019 IPC putting it in IPC 3, it is at the centre of the conflict, 3 payams being controlled by government while all other payams controlled by the IO. The community resorted to seek safety in swampy areas commonly known as Toch . The swampy areas are controlled by the opposition. In the midst of all the challenges, fishing &vegetable production is a good opportunity. The County is affected by rising food insecurity mostly a result of the insecurity, deepening economic crisis & depleted food stocks from insufficient household production. NH will continue supporting vulnerable HH in Leer County in 2020 with fishing, vegetable & crop kits, this is aimed at enhancing food production at HH level. Fangak is classified in IPC 3 (IPC, Aug 2019). The community there is vulnerable &It is hosting IDPs fleeing fighting In Unity state &returnees from Sudan. There is also decreased access to food &disruptions in the supply chain due to the conflict between government & IO forces over control of the Tonga market which supplies Fangak. NH has an ongoing unconditional cash programing in partnership with DCA & in 2020 plans to continue supporting the vulnerable HH in Fangak with provision of unconditional cash assistance & provision of production inputs (crop, fishing & vegetable kits). Maiwut is classified in IPC 4 according to Aug IPC report, it is known to be the headquarters of IO. The community has experienced recent shock that caused mass displacement which left them extremely vulnerable as a result of the conflict between the warring parties over control of the area & is also hosting high number of returnees from Ethiopia. The population is in dire need of immediate humanitarian support. We have an ongoing Unconditional cash project in partnership with DCA & intends to continue with the same throughout 2020 to ensure that the vulnerable community has access to food &other basic needs. We will also provide production inputs (crop, vegetable& fishing kits) to enhance food production. Nasir County in IPC 4 remains a high priority location (IRNA report march 23rd-27th,2019) as a result of protracted conflict which has led to multiple displacement which prevents the people from fully engaging in food production coupled with poor harvest due to prolonged dry spell and then flooding due to heavy rains. The already vulnerable host community is extremely overwhelmed by the many returnees coming from Ethiopia & IDPs who previously ran from the conflict hot zones. The compounding effects of widespread violence and sustained economic decline that have further diminished the capacity of people to face threats to their safety and livelihoods. NH has ongoing unconditional cash assistance support with DCA & livelihood support SSHF project, we intend to continue supporting the area in 2020 through unconditional cash assistance and provision of production inputs (crop, vegetable and fishing kits)

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project acknowledges the beneficiaries are the right holders, and we shall ensure that we follow the legally recognized procedures to deliver the assistance. Their entitlement will be clearly explained from inception to them and to the duty bearers which includes the government.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, the internally displaced people have been allocated 40% in this project. This was informed by the assessments carried out in the target locations

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will strengthen the existing linkages with the relevant line ministries at the national level, state and the county level. Where possible capacity building sessions will be done in collaboration with the County Agriculture department at the County level

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to the national cluster objectives 1,2 and 3 which developed in consultation with the line Ministries,FAO and WFP who are lead agencies in the food security and livelihood cluster. All the proposed activities are contributing to improve food security and livelihoods at house hold level for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The target locations are opposition-controlled areas with some few Payams controlled by government. Nile Hope endeavors to ensure the safety of the beneficiaries first, we ensure we remain neutral and constantly consulting the stakeholders and the beneficiaries to ensure safe and accessible distribution sites are selected. In collaboration with Nile Hope’s protection and GBV sector we ensure we work within the already established referral pathways to refer any affected beneficiaries in all our target locationsThe project includes both humanitarian and developmental activities.The affected people will be linked to long term food production for HH consumption as well as for sale of surplus by establishing farmers/fishing groups and producer associations for sustainability purposes.They will also be focus on capacity building, skills & knowledge development and community engagement

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project will complement our other ongoing Cash and market project that supports Conditional and unconditional cash assistance, fish and vegetable producer groups, VSLA groups, trader support and skills enhancement. This will work well with the beneficiaries because we will be able to link them with the already established producer groups and the local traders to ensure that there is continuity and sustainability. Also linking the fishery beneficiaries with the already established fishing groups that are already doing fishing as an IGA. The project steering committees will also play a big role in terms of ensuring comprehensive involvement and participation of the local community which will contribute towards ownership and this will improve the sustainability. The community will also be encouraged to develop a culture of cultivation to ensure that there is continuous availability of food at the HH level. Also, through training the production skills will be enhanced to ensure they maximize on vegetable and fish farming which is possible throughout the year as the target County are strategically located along River. The training will focus on reducing the post-harvest losses, value addition and marketing

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Nile Hope has a feedback and complaint mechanism system which consists of suggestion box, stakeholder’s project review meetings, complaint desk at the field level and a dedicated staff focal point based at the field and Juba level. We also have 2 trained people (male and female) tasked at the project steering committees to handle any complaint that may come up at the community level and escalate to Nile Hope focal point at the field level, the ones that they are unable to handle. Also during distribution we always put up a complaint desk to handle any issue that may arise during the distribution.
