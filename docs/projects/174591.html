Project Summary

This project will undertake food assistance and provision of access to fuel efficient technologies, income generating activities and agricultural resilience support to affected persons in Jere, MMC, Ngala, Mobbar and Rann (Kala-Balge) in Borno State.
SHO is proposing to achieve these through the food distribution, delivery of cash-based emergency food assistance, support of income generating activities through cash grant and business skills training and development for livelihood, and improve agriculture production through seeds and other inputs, and transformation of skills for food and livelihoods to households.
This project will also include a Wet Feeding component as part of the food assistance which is going to provide emergency response and act as first responder to new arrivals and returnees in the selected LGAs in Borno State.

1 - Needs

The persistent and widespread humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria continues to cause repeated displacement, protection concerns and unrelenting food and nutrition insecurity. Millions of children, women and men bear the brunt of the conflict, which has decimated livelihoods, crops, livestock, infrastructure, education and basic services. According to the October 2019 Cadre Harmonise analysis (projection for June-August 2020), approximately 3.8 million people in the BAY states are projected to be food insecure and considered to be facing crisis or emergency situations. The October 2019 Cadre Harmonise confirmed a worsening of the food security situation compared to endings of the CH analysis for the same period in 2018. The marked deterioration in the food security situation is linked to the upsurge in insecurity as evident in renewed attacks by non-state armed groups which have led to a new wave of displacements, limited access to farming and grazing land essential for livelihood opportunities. This situation has stretched communal resources due to increased dependency from IDPs and returnees.Furthermore, across the BAY states, access to productive services such as farming has been severely hampered by a twin factor of COVID-19 and insurgency, due to lockdowns and movement restrictions imposed by governments. Production of food crops has remained at an all-time low level. The low level of supply of food commodities in markets has led to souring prices of stable food items threatening an already fragile food security situation in the region. Agriculture is the mainstay of 80% of the population in Nigeria and it accounts for 40% of the total gross domestic product (GDP). However, the insurgency has negatively impacted on the ability of conflict affected population to undertake agricultural activities. Limited access to land and the closure of trade routes, levies imposed by the insurgents and the military restrictions have negatively impacted farming, disrupted markets and other income generating opportunities. SHO aims at targeting about 16,000 households (equiv. to 80,000 people), in Jere, MMC, Mobbar, Ngala and Rann in Borno State. The intervention strategy will be based on specific needs, type and number of beneficiaries, access to protection and security. SHO will support agriculture and farming initiatives, including livestock production and empowerment through provision of agricultural inputs (seeds and vegetables and fertilizer) for planting, and provision of small ruminant livestock for rearing. SHO will also enhance self-employment through provision of grants for income generating activities; households with existing businesses (petty trade) will be identified and trained for proper business management. SHO also aims to provide unconditional Cash for food assistance to the most vulnerable Persons of Concern (PoC), and finally, promote vocational training, literacy, and entrepreneurship, for unemployed youths.

2 - Activities

SHO shall conduct assessment in Jere, MMC, Mobbar, Ngala and Rann prior to implementation of the program to screen vulnerable Persons of Concern households and deliver in-kind food assistance and provision of access to Fuel Efficient Technologies, income generating activities and Agricultural resilience support. Activity 1: Prior to implementation, SHO shall conduct a need assessment to screen vulnerabilities within IDPs’ households, returnees and host communities of the selected LGAs in Borno State. This is going to be conducted with the host community, IDPs, women and youth forming the committee and will be meant to achieve needs assessment, project design, proper delivering of assistance based on the needs assessed and review of projects. Activity 2: Provision of adequate/improved Wet feeding for New Arrivals for Five days. - Incorporate Protection Mainstreaming - At the field level, protection monitors are charged with ensuring that effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. - conducting of post distribution monitoring per feeding cycle - Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints, to ensure accountability to the affected population - Engaging the community volunteers in cooking their food with the supervision of cooks. - Providing mobility to the new arrivals from screening area to RC. - cooking demonstration on how to prepare CSB++ - On site ratio, provide 2100 kcals of energy per person per day. - To provide cooking fuel to new arrivals who are able to cook for themselves. The new arrivals targeted for this intervention is 37,000 based on the need’s assessment conducted. Activity 3: provision of fuel-efficient technologies (stoves) to vulnerable households in targeted LGAs in order to reduce reported cases of Gender Based Violence during collection of firewood in the nearest bushes. Targeted number of PoCs to benefit from this fuel-efficient technology is 33,000. Activity 4: Provision of Agro-based income generating activities in crop cultivation, livestock and aquaculture, 10,000 beneficiaries are target to benefit from this to reduce vulnerability and further increase IDPs dignity. Activity 5: Provision of accessible, effective and confidential complaint mechanisms that are well understood and respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can immediately be put in place. Staff the mechanism with both men and women and ensure it is accessible for children. - SHO will set up a rudimentary Help Desk and rely on PMC. Draft a report of complaints and feedback collected. - In other locations Help-desks will be made available and PMC consulted before and after a registration or distribution. - SHO will sensitise the PMC members, the community leaders and affected populations about the use and purpose of the Help Desk. - SHO will locate Help-Desks in safe locations at the sites under shades. SHO may also have to walk around collecting complaints and feedback or conduct a community outreach initiative with the PMC. Activity 6: Documentation of differences in dietary needs, preferences and restrictions and Monitoring whether women and men (boys/girls) receive fair and comparable benefits from interventions in food assistance, agriculture, livestock, fisheries, and/or livelihoods.Other Activities: Capacity building training, Provision of Cash Vouchers, Procurement of food for distribution and Monitoring and Evaluation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SHO shall collect feedback from the vulnerable members of the community through FGDs (Focus Group Discussions) and installation of suggestion boxes and help-desks, securely placed in camps, reception centres and host communities for reporting. To enhance relevance and quality of responses, it will further maximize the impact of the intervention, enhance the targeting of beneficiaries and avoid the harm potentially generated by the programme and increase the safety of the staff and beneficiaries during the intervention. Our complaints mechanism will be accessible, effective, confidential and well understood with a view to improving programming, assisting in understandingbeneficiary and community perceptions, promoting beneficiary empowerment and assisting in the early detection of problems such as targeting, misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse, food diversion and other forms of fraud. It will respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can immediately be put in place.
