Project Summary

South Sudan faces a dire health crisis due to the lack of adequate health services and infrastructure. The country depends on international humanitarian aid to provide basic health care, but this is not enough to address the multiple and complex health emergencies that the country faces. Floods, food insecurity, conflicts, and disease outbreaks have worsened the health situation and increased the vulnerability of the population, especially women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities.
Based on latest projections, in 2024, 544,012 people are in dire need of essential health care services in Tonj North, Wau, Kapoeta East, and Kapoeta North counties with health cluster targeting to reach 193,728 people in Tonj North (113,490); Wau (55,081); Kapoeta East (20,050); and Kapoeta North (5,107) counties. Out of the health cluster target, IRW will target 18,000 people in the four counties. To improve the access and quality of health care, there is a need to ensure coordination and integration between Health, Nutrition, Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), and food security and livelihoods (FSL) sectors. There is also a need to strengthen community health services through Basic Health Initiative (BHI) and community engagement.
To address current humanitarian needs in Wau, Tonj North, Kapoeta East, and Kapoeta North Counties, IRW response design focuses on providing lifesaving essential health services in line with national and international humanitarian standards, and is based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality, and trauma-related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness, response, and mitigation activities will be undertaken with special attention given to providing vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities. IRW in collaboration with the State Ministry of Health, County health department, and humanitarian partners will support different intervention modalities suited to the needs of affected people, including disease outbreak control and prevention interventions. IRW will undertake the Inter-cluster coordination mechanism at all levels and will work towards comprehensive integration and collaboration with other clusters, including FSL, WASH, Nutrition. Integration of FSL, WASH, Protection, Health and nutrition is the adopted approach IRW has been practicing in implementing both emergency and development projects for the last two years. Therefore, this project will be integrated.
Islamic Relief will continue prevention, preparedness, and response readiness activities addressing human rights for health. IRWSS will further support static and mobile health facilities, and community-based healthcare systems to save communities who do not have access to health care facilities and are affected by emergencies.

1. Needs

In 2024, 544,012 people are in dire need of essential health care services in Tonj North (340,471); Wau (75,922); Kapoeta East (84,208); and Kapoeta North (43,411) counties according to Health PIN. The health cluster is targeting to reach 193,728 people in need in Tonj North (113,490); Wau (55,081); Kapoeta East (20,050); and Kapoeta North (5,107) counties. Out of the health cluster target of 193,728, IRW will target 18,000 people (Men 4,765; women: 6,176; Boys: 2,647; Girls: 4,412; PWDs: 2,941) in the four counties of Wau, Tonj North, Kapoeta East, and Kapoeta North. IRW will focus on reaching following needs in particular: 1) Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. 2) Reduce excess in morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases 3) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. Islamic Relief aims to improve people’s access to and utilization of essential health care services to reduce the rate of morbidity and mortality. Disease outbreaks will be detected proactively, responded to in a timely manner and adequate surveillance systems will be in place through the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS). In coordination with the heath Cluster and its partners, Islamic Relief South Sudan will provide health services that are adapted to the emerging humanitarian crisis while continuing to provide essential lifesaving health care. Community and County Health System reliance capacity will be promoted through bringing synergies and optimizing use of limited resources among humanitarian, development and peace actors. Community consultation, feedback and partnership will be promoted.

2. Project activities

Islamic Relief South Sudan will implement the following activities in the target locations in the following seven static health facilities ((Baggari, Biringi, Taban, Gaitan, Geidi, Athiengpuol, and Lopua PHCUs including medical mobile outreach sites) 1. Curative consultations for common communicable diseases per age, sex, disability (18,000 (Men 4,765; women: 6,176; Boys: 2,647; Girls: 4,412; PWDs: 2,941)) 2. Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases for children less than one year with different vaccine antigens including coverage of measles reactive campaigns; (3,500 (Girls: 2,100; Boys: 1,400) 3. Provision of maternal healthcare services to pregnant women such as clean deliveries by trained health workers, antenatal and postnatal care, and TT immunization services (1,500 pregnant women 4. Conducting integrated health and nutrition mobile outreach clinic to hard to reach areas. 5. Provision of sexual reproductive health services such as contraceptives, CMR to survivors of SGBV (200 (120 F, 80 M)); 6. Conduct community health education and awareness on health seeking, AAP/CRM, SRHR, PSEA, and GBV related messages (18,000).
