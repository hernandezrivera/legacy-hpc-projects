Project Summary

TGH will directly contribute to Objectives 1, 2 & 3 for Syria through providing safe andequitable access to qualitative non-formal education (NFE) to OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out within public educational institutions (this project will be part of an Education, Protection, WASH and Shelter integrated programming under 2024/2025 HRP).
The response proposed by TGH is based on their 5-year experience in implementing ECHO, SHF and SDC funded education projects in Syria. TGH is intending to carry on its integrated approach and replicate its successes in sub-districts of Rural Damascus, Hama, Idleb, Aleppo and Dar’a. They were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) with all the localities being rated over 3 (66% of level 4, 34% of level 3), TGH 2023 & 2024 field assessments and coordination with municipalities SARC, DoE & other NGOs. In addition, these areas are all witnessing important returns and/or displacements movements thus increasing the level of needs. Through the proposed intervention, TGH will simultaneously address immediate needs and foster coherent services by providing/enabling rapid and sustainable access to formal and non-formal education (NFE) for the school year 2024. This will benefit 14,755 individuals (5,915 boys, 6,852 girls, 1,476 PwD, 12,009 individuals from host communities, 2,236 IDPs, 510 returnees). TGH will ensure access to education by rehabilitating schools in locations where facilities are partially destroyed. Children will be both encouraged to participate in the activities through BTL campaigns. A specific focus will be made on supporting the reintegration of OOSC, CwD, early married girls and child labour cases into the FE system. TGH will support the most needed youth in education and livelihood opportunities through TVET. In coordination with the Protection sector, TGH will provide PSS to students and teachers, campaign awareness to parents. In coordination with ERL sector and other partners, TGH will explore all the possibilities to support the economic situation of parents through livelihood opportunities in order to sustain the access and retention of children at school. The proposed intervention will include:
-Rehabilitate 13 schools, incl. gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities; and provide prefabricated schools; Provide the rehabilitated schools with solar power system when feasible; Repair damaged classroom furniture; Provide schools with school furniture, including winterization items such as heaters.
-Provide OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out with NFE; Provide students and teachers school based PSS; Conduct BTL campaigns; Provide students with school supplies; Provide students with hygiene items including sanitary pads for girls; along with health and hygiene campaign awareness; Provide students with winters clothes through CVA; Provide maintenance and running costs in schools; Provide cash for education given the priority to children at risk of dropping out; Provide professional development to teaching staff and education personnel; Provide teachers with teaching resources and transportation; Provide children with sup. learning materials; Provide transportation incentives for student in school and youth in TVET; Establish and support parents engagement initiatives.
All M&E activities are gathered in a MEAL workplan, monthly updated and shared to PMs to ensure the activities' completion in time and make sure the team is supported by the necessary tools that are developped with the HQ support.
