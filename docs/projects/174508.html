Project Summary

Sindh is the second poorest province of Pakistan; approximately half of its rural population live below the poverty line, 6.5 times higher than in urban areas – the highest urban-rural poverty split in Pakistan. The province is extremely vulnerable to multiple natural hazards; the most recent are the monsoon rains/flood 2020, Locust 2019-20, & drought 2018-19 entailing adverse effects on livelihoods & productive assets of the vulnerable households. Likewise, the COVID-19 crisis has also caused market disruptions, adversely affected purchasing power, income, & cash availability at the household level. Compounding effects of natural disasters, COVID, & other shocks have disproportionally affected the vulnerable households, including smallholder farmers, most of whom are resource deficient, experience severe food insecurity (IPC 3 or above), malnutrition (GAM rate above WHO threshold of 15%), & frequently resort to detrimental coping strategies. The smallholder farmers particularly have had trouble accessing markets to sell their produce, buy certified seeds, fertilizers, other essential inputs, or avail casual labor. The majority of the smallholder farmers practice subsistence farming, produce inadequate food, & heavily depend on markets to meet household food needs. Following recent exposure to a disaster (floods), lingering effects of past disasters (droughts), and COVID-19, vulnerable households are reportedly consuming less food at the household level owing to limited purchasing power & income losses caused by the limited availability of off-farm labor opportunities. Consequently, the humanitarian country team's recent joint analysis identifies the highest number of people in need of food security & livelihood assistance in Sindh (around 2.7 million out of total 5.8 million). The multiple shocks have a detrimental impact on households with malnourished pregnant and lactating women and/or children in their first 1,000 days, elderly and disabled people. The vulnerable families need support to enhance the household food production, restore livelihood for an improved food security, nutrition, & livelihood outcome. FAO, ACTED, Concern Worldwide, & HANDS, as the National Disaster Consortium, have jointly designed the proposed project. The project strategy is needs-based, gap filling, & informed by the recent assessments & situation analysis undertaken by the FAO & the consortium partners. The NDC partners will implement the project in the worst affected districts. The consortia partners have identified the project districts and interventions by considering the geographical prioritization of the Food Security Cluster, the severity of multiple shocks, prevailing food insecurity vulnerabilities, limited capacities of the households to restore their productive assets, or meet their food production & livelihood needs. The project envisages providing critical livelihood recovery & integrated household food production assistance to the vulnerable households to help them maintain & further enhance household food consumption, dietary diversification, livelihoods restoration, & access to cash at the household level to ensure immediate relief to them. The overall anticipated impact is "Enhanced food security and resilience of the most vulnerable households in targeted districts of Sindh province." Two outcomes will accomplish this anticipated impact. Output 1 "Multiple shock affected smallholder farming households supported with nutrition-sensitive integrated household food system initiatives to improve food production and utilization" will be implemented by FAO targeting 1520 women farmers/ households. Output 2 " Vulnerable households benefits from cash grants & cash for work activities to meet their livelihood & food needs" will target 8690 HHs by ACTED, Concern worldwide, & HANDS. Overall, the project will reach 10,210 vulnerable households (66,365 people: men: 22052, women: 21459, boys (11661, girls: 11193, and 2654 disabled people).

1- Project Activities

Output 1: Activity 1.1: Considering the issue of food insecurity and malnutrition in Badin and Tharparkar and the inability of the vulnerable households to produce and consume food in sufficient quantity and diversity, FAO will apply the nutrition-sensitive IHFS approach, already implemented in Balochistan with a positive bearing on household food production and utilization. The union councils will e selected in close consultation with the district authorities. FAO will coordinate with WFP/UNICEF/or their implementing partners/HANDS/ Sindh Government nutrition program to identify the households with pregnant and lactating women and/or children in their first 1,000 days by following nutrition-sensitive targeting criteria. The IHFS support constitutes a) nutrition-sensitive production support of kitchen gardening package (vegetable seeds and mesh for protection), poultry package (5 vaccinated birds: 4 hens and 1 cock, poultry feed, drinker and feeder, and cage) and dairy goat package (1 vaccinated goat - lactating/pregnant with feed, water feeding tub) to 1520 women. b) Multi-purpose community water facilities in selected communities that lack sufficient water for kitchen gardening. These water facilities will include water tanks at the ground level connected to the already existing water source and solar submersible pumps for pumping water. c) 12-sessions technical capacity development program using the farmer field schools (FFS) approach for women farmers in kitchen gardening, homestead poultry, and dairy production. d) 12-sessions experiential nutrition education program to complement the nutrition-sensitive food production at the household level, entailing a comprehensive package of localized nutrition behavior change activities to improve food safety, utility, hygiene, and nutrition education. The IHFS support will apply a cascade training and support model; one FAO FFS facilitator will support 76 trained women activists, and each activist will train and support 20 female farmers. Output 2:Activity 2.1: ACTED (Mirpurkhas, Umerkot), HANDS (Sajawal, Sanghar), Concern Worldwide (Tando Muhammad Khan, Dadu) will undertake communal infrastructure rehabilitation through CFW. This activity will create short-term employment opportunities for the affected households whose livelihoods were disrupted due to the 2020 floods and unable to meet their food and other basic needs. In total, 3190 HHs will be engaged in CFW infrastructure rehabilitation during the lean period (ACTED: 1190 HHs, Concern: 1000 HHs, HANDS: 1,000 HHs) for 3-cycles and receive 8000 PKR/ cycle based on the harmonized market rates, covering around 80% of the monthly food basket. The rehabilitation will focus on severely damaged agricultural infrastructures such as desilting of irrigation channels, agricultural plots, paths, repair of culverts or drainage channels, cleaning communal ponds, and roads. 319 CFW tool kits will be distributed for the planned 319 schemes. The three NGO partners will select the CFW schemes jointly with the target communities; technical staff will determine scheme feasibility, potential impact, and later monitor the quality and progress of the CFW. Activity 2.2: 5,500 extremely vulnerable flood-affected households (Concern 2,730 HHs, ACTED: 970, HANDS: 1,800 HH) who have lost their livelihood and productive assets during recent floods will receive a multi-purpose cash grant of 15,000 PKR /HH to restore both on-farm (for instance restocking of livestock assets or purchase of fodder) or off-farm (for instance small shops) for sustenance, food security, and livelihoods. Activity 2.3 100 Basic veterinary vaccination kits will be provided by HANDs to communities reaching around 8000 HHs; proposed due to the persistent gap of basic vaccination equipment with the livestock departments. The target communities will be mobilized to approach the government vaccinators for livestock vaccination.
