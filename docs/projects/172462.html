Project Summary

The project aims to support an estimated 31,300 pregnant women in 21 selected districts receive skilled delivery care, including management of obstetric complications. Health facilities will be capacitated with equipment and sundries to provide EmONC services. Health care providers and managers will also be capacitated in the minimum initial service package for SRH in emergencies or humanitarian situation.

1 - Activities

The current socio-economic challenges, COVID pandemic and drought have a direct and indirect impact on sexual reproductive health and rights (SRHR) especially for women and girls. Without adequate support to the existing system, SRHR indicators will worsen including rising maternal and perinatal mortality and morbidity, teenage pregnancy and rising incidences of sexual and gender based violence (SGBV). Food insecurity linked to droughts leads to malnutrition which increases vulnerability to anemia in pregnant women, increased rates of intrauterine growth retardation, low birth weight and increased perinatal mortality. Obstetric complications such as haemorrhage are exacerbated by anaemia. This project aims to mitigate against adverse pregnancy outcomes for women in 4 selected provinces through i) strengthening provision of emergency obstetric and neonatal care ii) capacitating health workers to ensure minimum initial service package for sexual and reproductive health is in place during emergencies and humanitarian situations (MISP) for continuity of essential SRH services. According to the latest VHMASS survey the selected provinces (Mat South, Midlands and Mash West) have low CEmONC coverage of 66%, 72% and 83% respectively against a target of 100% of their secondary level facilities. There are therefore likely to be most adversely impacted by drought or other emergency occurances. Though Matebeleland North provinces latest CEmONC coverage from the VHMASS survey is 100%, the coverage has been fluctuating from quarter to quarter. The project will be implemented within the COVID context. COVID has affected continuity of essential SRH services in Zimbabwe. During the peak of the outbreak in Zimbabwe; normal deliveries, casearian section and women seeking postnatal care (Day 3) declined by 6%, 27% and 16% respectively. During the same period institutional and community maternal deaths increased by 10% and 29% respectively. There is therefore need to strengthen EmONC, ensure commodity security and capacity to provide MISP within the health sector to mitigate against negative impact on pregnant women and ensure continuity of essential SRH services. The project targets an estimated 31,300 pregnant women in 21 selected districts in the 4 provinces. Procurement and distribution of medical RH kits and sundriesMedical equipment, RH kits and sundries to support provision of quality delivery care and management of obstetric and neonatal complications will be procured and distributed to all the selected 21 districts. Support referral of obstetric complications – Equip, Repair and fuel ambulances In view of the fact that some obstetric complications will need to be referred to the next level of care, support will be provided for equipping ambulances with life saving equipment for use during patient transfer. Ambulance repairs, servicing and fueling will also be supported. The districts/facilities to be supported will be determined after a rapid assessment on the ambulance status. Capacity buiding in MISP420, twenty from each district, health workers will be trained in MISP. The trained cadres will in turn sensitize other health workers in their respective districts. This will contribute to ensuring continuity of essential SRH service during emergencies or humanitarian situation. The MoHCC has just adapted the MISP guidelines. Technical support and supervision and M&EA rapid assessment will be conducted at project inception. Technical support and supervision visits will also be undertaken to the supported districts.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

In the supported rural districts the health centre committees, which comprises the community representatives and health facility staff will oversee implementation of the project. The committee will regularly review project implementation and challenges that will be faced in project implementation, including coming up with solutions for the challenges. Prior to delivery, a significant proportion of the pregnant mothers in the rural districts will be admitted to the maternity waiting homes – this setting provides a platform to engage women, offer health promotion/education as part of their antenatal care and address any concern that they may have related to service provision including delivery care at the health facility. Feedback mechanisms will include focus group discussions with the women and client feedback suggestion boxes.
