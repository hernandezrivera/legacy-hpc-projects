Project Summary

The project is responding to humanitarian needs of drought affected community in target locations of Puntland and Somaliland through cash injection to address the immediate food needs of the people. Cash transfer programming will be used to reach households that are in IPC 3 and above. While focusing on life-saving interventions, the project will also support recovery and resilience building of the people where possible.
Improving food availability through support to agricultural production in Awdal and strengthening existing resilience work under SomRep in Sool and Sanaag regions of Somalia is seen as an entry point to diversify pastoral livelihoods of those two regions. This plan involves also supporting communities’ recovery and livelihoods diversification interventions through creation of VSLA, livestock support with vaccination and treatment and Improving Agricultural Production/Food Security.
Livestock production was severely affected by the drought leading to substantially reduced herds; which is the country’s prime investment especially in rural communities. The mains reasons leading to the death of livestock were water shortages and lack of pasture. In view of this situation the project will explore fodder production techniques trained to women groups to ensure fodder is produced in those location but also conservation and preservation of fodder. Oxfam will prioritize livestock vaccination and treatment which will be closely coordinated with ministry of livestock and FAO. These include promotion of carrying capacity for sustained production. These actions will be coupled with Cash for Work activities which will be used for communal asset development linked to natural resource base rehabilitation aimed to reduce effect of climate change on NRM and promotion of land productivity.
In improving livelihoods/ food security, the project will use conservational agriculture model as a climate smart agriculture technology to facilitate regeneration of agricultural production among agro-pastoral households. The beneficiaries will be given seed to improve seed security for the coming farming season. Farmers will be trained in collaboration with the Ministry of Agriculture on agricultural techniques to obtain optimal yields from their fields. Upon harvesting farmers will be able to reserve part of the harvest as seed for next season.
In times of shock and more critical food insecurity, Oxfam will utilize multi-purpose cash transfers to meet the immediate emergency needs of at-risk communities. Oxfam will strengthen government capacity to anticipate and respond to slow onset crises through supporting mobile early warning data collection, dissemination back to community to enhance preparedness measures.

Project Indicators

1. 80% of MPCT provided to beneficiaries have reported to have used for food only while the remaining 20% was covered for household debt, medication and travel according to post distribution monitoring conducted by Oxfam 2. % of the target population with acceptable Coping Strategy Index (CSI): Coping Strategy Index (CSI) is a proxy of household food insecurity; it is based on list of behaviors (coping strategies) and combines the frequency of each strategy, (i.e. how many times the strategy was adopted?); and their severity, (i.e. how serious is each strategy?) for households reporting food consumption problems. A higher CSI indicates a severe food insecurity situation while a lower CSI score indicates slightly moderate food insecurity situation. 3. Mitigation Measures

Oxfam will conduct protection analysis in the operational locations to identify protection issues. The vulnerable community members most in need of emergency assistance will be identified through participatory mapping exercises at village level; specifically through well-being rankings, vulnerability mapping, food consumption score, and household purchasing power. Gender will be considered in household selection, to help reduce additional burden on women heading households. The criteria will be clearly explained to the selection committee/panel prior to selection, with clear communication on what beneficiary entitlements during the project, to avoid unrealistic expectations.The target beneficiaries and locations will be prioritized based on:- Socio-economic marginalization - those with the least resources and people marginalized due to clan affiliation- Impact of drought on individual households i.e. livestock mortality- Female-headed families, the elderly living without support, persons with disability, etc.- Families with malnourished children- Areas where households are affected and their assets are destroyed- Villages/IDP settlements with limited or no access to safe water and hygienic latrines- Locations without other humanitarian actors (aid gap)To assure fairness and transparency there will be a standard of inclusiveness in the selection panel - a diverse pool of stakeholders consisting of:- Oxfam staff- Local government representative/village head- Community members (women, youth, elders, people living with disability-PLWD)- Religious community leaders/elders (at least 1 representative per sub-clan)Oxfam will select beneficiaries based on the above factors targeting households showing high vulnerability. As per the selection criteria which is also in line with ECHO and Cash Alliance Working group guidelines, such households may include the elderly, female headed households, lactating women with children of less than 6 months, pregnant mothers and PLWD.
