Project Summary

The key overall focus of this project is to provide multidimensional WASH responses for vulnerable children, women, and men that are most affected by the prevailing conflict and crisis in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon.
First, this project aims to tackle the urgent need for clean drinking water, sanitation solutions, and hygiene amenities for 80,000 people targeted out of 1.2 million crisis-affected individuals. Due to ongoing conflicts and limited access to basic facilities, thousands of people are suffering from diseases that stem from unsafe water consumption and insufficient hygiene practices. To address these concerns, this project will implement sustainable interventions designed to ensure continued access to safe water sources and encourage proper sanitation habits, such as:
- The construction and rehabilitation of water points in the communities, schools, health facilities, TLSs, and CFSs.
- providing appropriate sanitation facilities (constructing latrines in schools, health facilities, child-friendly spaces, and temporal learning spaces (TLSs); household latrine construction for IDPs; distribution of WASH kits)
- sensitizing school children and community members on water treatment techniques and proper hygiene practices. 
All interventions will integrate strategies that mitigate the risk of gender-based violence and support feedback mechanisms to enable better accountability to affected populations and ensure the inclusion of persons living with disabilities.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Challenges around of accessing basic WASH services in schools, health facilities, and in community at household levels remain significant across the NWSW regions. As the MSNA Round 9, MSNA Round 10 confirmed the ongoing critical need for WASH services, maintaining it among the top three. Over 43% of the assessed population relies on unimproved water sources for drinking, cooking, and other personal uses, and 47% of households do not have sufficient water. Additionally, 83% of households do not treat their water before drinking. Most households spend between 31 to 60 minutes daily collecting water, with 79% of those responsible for fetching water being children. More than 46% of households use unimproved sanitation facilities, and 4% practice open defecation challenges include inadequacy, cleanliness, and lack of disability-friendly facilities. According to JENA assessment, over 50% of schools lack access to safe, drinking water and gender-sensitive functional latrines. JENA report also indicates a severe shortage of functioning latrines and toilets in schools, with an average of 130 enrolled children per functional facility, far exceeding the Sphere standard of one toilet for every 30 girls and one toilet for every 60 boys. The above needs identified requires The WASH Cluster is currently conducting a WASH Specific Needs Assessment in the NWSW regions to come up with more viable strategies to address WASH needs both at institutional and community level.
