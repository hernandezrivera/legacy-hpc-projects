Project Summary

It is estimated that approximately 2 million people will be affected by HLP issues in 2024 across the country and in the Abyei Administrative Area. The situation is particularly challenging for some 507,000 IDPs and 401,000 Returnees(SSD HNO 2023). However, due to the influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan Crisis, HLP issues may be on a rise.
Notwithstanding the guarantees in the Transitional Constitution of South Sudan of the right of women to land and property, South Sudanese women are denied ownership rights to land, and have weak access rights that are wholly dependent on their relationship with men. The important role of women as the main users of land for agricultural production, and the large number of women headed households makes it imperative that their land rights be strengthened and secured to ensure livelihood security, household food and nutrition security and the productive use of land for economic development. (South Sudan Draft National Land Policy 2023, p.41).
Factors contributing to these higher risk of HLP issues include but not limited to dynamics such as a history of displacement, influx of returnees from Sudan an Ethiopia, conflict, and secondary occupation, land grabbing, flooding, lack of widespread knowledge of HLP Rights among the communities, forced eviction, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure, the increase in population growth, PoC sites transition to IDPs Camps, increasing number and high influx of refugees and returnees affected by Sudan Crisis as well as refugees returning from the neighboring countries such as Uganda, Kenya, and Ethiopia (South Sudan HLPAoR HRP 2021 Strategy). Without appropriate interventions taken or immediate assistance provided, the HLP issues will definitely result into further displacement and conflicts which will have adverse effects on the families and children.
Therefore, this project will target 11,175 People (5588 Women, 5587 Men,) for provision of HLP Assistance including: HLP awareness raising activities, individualized legal counselling and aid, establish or strengthen dispute resolution mechanisms and train local authorities and relevant institution’s staff for them to be enabled to meaningfully participate in said mechanisms, establishment and strengthening of Information Assistance/Help Desks.
In all its implementation phases, CINA will strictly adhere to national laws and international norms on HLP, Code of Conduct, Community Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, uphold the Do No Harm Principle, promote centrality of protection principles and enhance accountability to affected populations before, during and after project implementation. CINA will implement this project with technical assistance from South Sudan HLP Technical Working Group Area of Responsibility (AoR), and in coordination with Protection Partners on the ground together with relevant authorities and will require approximately $250,000 to meet the HLP needs of IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, housing, land and property (HLP) issues are both a consequence and a cause of conflict. The Sudan Crisis, climate change impacts and intercommunal violence have exacerbated some of the risks associated with HLP issues in several areas of the country especially Ayod, Fangak, Panyikang, Fashoda (Tier 1), and Uror and Baliet (Tier 2) (HRP Locations 2024). This is evidenced by widespread destruction and looting of houses and properties, degradation of housing due to displacement, secondary occupation, forced eviction, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure which are all features of the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan (HLPAoR HRP 2023 Strategy). Spontaneous returns of both IDPs and refugees from Sudan have increased as the government moves forward with the implementation of the Revitalized Peace Agreement. This will result in a rise of HLP issues in areas of return especially illegal occupation and grabbing of houses and lands, which has potential to contribute to sub-national and localized violence along with conflict based on identity lines in some areas.There is also limited awareness of HLP rights among local authorities and affected populations. Displaced and returnee populations, in particular women and child headed households, also lack legal assistance to access the limited courts or alternative dispute resolution mechanisms to claim their rights. It is recently documented that access to Housing, Land, and Property (HLP), an important issue during the years of protracted conflict, is now critical as people are further displaced, the population grows, PoC sites start to transition and increasing number of South Sudanese refugees returning from the neighboring countries such as Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia and Sudan. Another pressing HLP issue is that, the formalization of land laws and the creation of mechanisms of their implementation remains a gap and creates an obstacle for people who want to return. Lack of widespread knowledge of HLP laws and insufficient legal ownership documentation made it difficult for many people to understand and effectively advocate for themselves. Worst of all, women, children and people with disabilities disproportionately struggle to assert rights to housing and land. Despite the legal provisions recognizing the equal rights of women to land, recognition and protection of those rights remain limited throughout South Sudan. Women’s land rights remain largely conditional. Dispossession of widows, daughters, and divorced women is common. HLP issues constitute a major challenge to creating conditions conducive to pursuing durable solutions for displaced populations and peace among the communities. In a context of increasing land-related disputes, and anticipation of returns, relocations, and local reintegration of IDPs and refugees, HLP issues will be a major priority need to be addressed in South Sudan from 2024 onwards.

2. Project activities

Activity 1. CINA will conduct HLP Awareness raising to community members on HLP rights. These people include IDPs, returnees and Host communities. The awareness will be centered on the issues affecting the community social cohesion and what can be done to avoid conflict in common places of social concerns. access to land and property is one of the main problems IDPs and returnees face in the area of return. To facilitate HLP awareness raising activities, CINA will establish a HLP desk in the community centre at each targeted locations (Ayod, Fangak, Uror, Panyikang, Fashoda and Baliet) managed by volunteers supervised by CINA HLP staff which will conduct awareness sessions on HLP and distribute the Information and Education Communication (IEC) material on HLP issues, as well as identify and respond to HLP cases. Particular focus will be given to women, who often do not have access to justice due to the lack of knowledge of their rights, availability of legal aid and associated payment fees. In addition, very few women are in a possession of land and even fewer who have documents confirming land ownership as highlighted in the National Draft Land Policy 2023. Activity 2. Provide legal aid to people of concern. Individual legal assistance, pro bono services/case management will be provided for specific HLP cases. Activity 3. Provide legal Counselling to people of concern. CINA will identify and prioritize HLP beneficiaries to receive HLP support. Depending on the severity of the concern, the individual may receive more specialized support which might include additional legal advice or referrals to the authorities, whereas individuals who may not require case management will be referred to the HLP Help Desk. Through this component, beneficiaries will be supported to access both formal and informal justice mechanisms (individual cases follow up with law enforcement, courts). Where beneficiaries require referrals to external services, CINA will conduct direct payments to these services to ensure the beneficiaries are able to access this critical support. Activity 4. CINA, will work with the community in IDP locations and areas of return to establish alternative dispute resolution mechanisms/committees representative of IDPs, returnees and host communities. The six HLP ADR mechanisms will be set up in each location with the composition made up of local leaders, representatives of minority groups and people who have knowledge of disputes related to HLP after attending the awareness sessions. Activity 5. Conduct Capacity Building trainings to Local authorities and relevant institution's staff on HLP, dispute resolution and ADR.
