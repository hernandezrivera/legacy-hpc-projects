Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to ensure the availability of, and safe access to, quality life-saving GBV response and preventive services, that are survivor-centered and in best interest of the child. The services will address the specific needs of different vulnerable groups, including: women, adolescent girls and boys, children, people with disabilities, people with alternative gender identities and sexual orientations, and minorities who are at a high risk of GBV, trafficking, exploitation and abuse in camps and host community.
With funds mobilized through the JRP 2020, UNICEF in partnership with 5 international and national NGO will establish/strengthen 19 Safe Spaces for Women and Girls (SSWG - 15 in camps and 4 in host community) to reach 17,550 women and adolescent girls in camps and host communities with safe, confidential and quality survivor-centered GBV services including: case management, structured psycho social support (PSS), and care for child survivors. 20,000 women and adolescent girls will be engaged in empowerment activities including skills and vocational training to address the heightened protection risks which expose them to negative/harmful coping mechanisms like child marriage.
UNICEF will build the capacity of 1,000 service provider staff, strengthen existing government structures such as hospitals, the Ministry of Children and Women’s Affairs (MOCWA) and police to provide quality survivor-centred GBV services to women, men, boys and girls in camps and host communities.
Furthermore, community mobilization will be conducted to reach 90,000 women, men, boys and girls with messages on GBV prevention and to mitigate GBV risks while 6,000 community members will be trained on proven strategies like Engaging Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP), to address the negative social norms that fuel GBV among the population.
To address the limited knowledge of sexual and reproductive health (SRH) among adolescents, adolescent SRH sessions will be integrated in SSWG and MPCs, to increase information on and access to ASRH.
To enhance social cohesion among refugees and members of host communities this project will reach 109,553 (76% in camps and 24% host community), women, men, boys, girls, people with disabilities, minorities and people with alternative gender identities and sexual orientations with GBV responses and prevention activities.
UNICEF has a Zero Tolerance Policy to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA); therefore, to enhance accountability to the affected population, UNICEF will continue to create environments free from SEA and strengthen PSEA reporting and response mechanisms for UNICEF and its partners.
To respond to the effects of natural disasters including monsoons and cyclone, UNICEF will ensure adequate preparedness including ensuring availability of contingency supplies such as dignity kits and skilled emergency response providers.
UNICEF will continue to ensure that partners engage the affected population through Information and Feedback Centres in the camps and host community to improve/implement interventions that meets the population needs.

Needs

As of September 2019, UNHCR estimated 914,998 Rohingya refugee women, men, girls and boys to be hosted in Cox’s Bazaar District, of which 55% are children, while 52% are women and girls. In 2020, over one million refugees and vulnerable Bangladeshis in host communities are estimated to require humanitarian assistance. The unstable security environment in and around camps in 2019 has continued to impact negatively on the physical and psychological well-being of refugees and host communities, particularly women, adolescent girls, persons with disabilities, female-headed households, minorities, people with alternative gender identities and sexual orientation, who are more vulnerable to various forms of GBV, including trafficking, forced/child marriage, forced prostitution and SEA, and face heightened protection risks and mobility restrictions due to social-cultural, gender identity and physical limitations (JRP 2020). Studies (WRC 2018) also revealed that Rohingya men and boys are subjected to different forms of sexual violence. In Rohingya refugee camps, GBV continues to be one of the major challenges to women and girls. According to the GBVIMS data (2019), the most commonly reported forms of GBV include rape, physical assault, sexual violence, forced marriage, denial of resources and psychological abuse. Domestic violence constitutes 76% of reported GBV cases. Women and girls in the host community are often exposed to GBV due to deep-rooted patriarchal social norms. The Violence Against Women Survey 2015, revealed that 73% of women have experienced violence by their current husband and 50% reported physical violence in their lifetime. According to MSNA 2019, overcrowded conditions, physical camp layout, and limited service delivery have exacerbated GBV risks among women and girls in the camps. Water points and latrines/bathing facilities were highlighted by women and girls in camps and host communities as areas where they feel unsafe and at risk of GBV and exploitation. Different studies have also shown that 5-10% of Rohingya households engage in child marriage and two-thirds consider it a normal practice, while approximately one-third use it as a coping strategy for the protection of girls and lessening economic burdens. Vulnerable host community families, due to limited space and access to basic services and livelihoods have resorted to negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage to meet their needs. Child marriage leads to early pregnancy and the girls become vulnerable to sexual and physical abuse from their husbands. There is a critical need to actively engage communities with an increased focus on men, boys, religious leaders and other key gatekeepers to address these negative social norms Due to stigma, threats of retaliation, fear of rejection by family and community, low awareness of services and deeply-rooted socio-cultural barriers, adolescent girls, with higher vulnerability to child marriage, trafficking, and sexual violence, face heightened obstacles to access the multi-sectoral GBV response services. A GBV SS, Service Audits 2019 report, highlighted gaps in the capacity of service providers including government hospitals, women and child police desks, legal aid and the justice system to provide quality GBV services. Gaps include; capacity in case management, mental health and PSS, caring for child and male survivors.A household assessment in camps showed that, while 57% of the refugees, were unable to identify GBV service points, 85% prefer to go to Majihis for referral of cases of sexual violence. It is imperative to scale up awareness raising on GBV services, safe referral points and intensifying programs to address socio-cultural barriers as well as strengthen the capacity of service providers and systems to provide quality GBV multi-sectoral response services for refugees and host communities.

Activities or outputs

1. Establish/strengthen 15 SSWGs in camps and 4 SSWGs in host communities to provide quality and confidential life-saving GBV response services including case management, care for child survivors, structured psycho-social support, counselling, psycho-social first aid (PFA) and referrals for health, legal and safety services for women, girls, children and people with disabilities in camps and host community 2. Strengthen the capacity of 1,000 service providers (law enforcement officers, caseworkers, social workers, health workers and child protection actors) on GBV, structured psycho social support, clinical management of rape, caring for child survivors, PFA, case management, GBVIMS and PSEA.3. Ensure the availability of emergency supplies and build the capacity of implementing partners to strengthen their skills for emergency preparedness and response;4. Strengthen existing health, child and gender support structures including MOWCA, the Ministry of Law and other legal, police and health personnel to effectively respond to the health, legal and social needs of GBV survivors in the Rohingya and host communities; 5. Support 20,000 women and adolescent girls in camps and host communities through structured empowerment programmes including skills training and resource-based interventions including vocational opportunities to mitigate GBV incidents;6. Support community engagement activities targeting 90,000 men, boys, women, girls and community and religious leaders in the camps and host communities to increase awareness on negative/harmful social norms and enhance GBV prevention and risk mitigation;7. Support the implementation of proven community GBV prevention strategies among 6,000 community members with a specific focus on men and boys through the EMAP approach to promote positive transformation of social and gender norms that fuel and normalize GBV in camps and host populations.8. Increase information on and access to ASRH and formation of peer support ASRH Groups for 8,000 adolescent girls in camps and host community9. Ensure availability and distribution of 20,000 dignity kits to support the dignity of women and adolescent girls during emergencies and periodically, to enhance their dignity and facilitate their mobility to access lifesaving services;10. Coordinate with other sectors including WASH, Health, Education and SRH to ensure a multi-sectoral approach for effective GBV response, prevention and risk mitigation 11. Strengthen prevention and response to SEA by ensuring availability of functional PSEA reporting and response mechanisms for UNICEF and its partners.12. Ensure appropriate information is provided to refugees and host community on Disaster Risk reduction and provide required supplies for re-enforcing the safe spaces for safety
