Project Summary


FSNAU and partners survey conducted September 2020 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices. At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence has remained Serious (10–14.9%) for the past four consecutive seasons (11.8 in Gu 2020, 13.1% in 2019/20 Deyr, 13.8% in 2019 Gu and 12.6 % in 2018/19 Deyr). High levels of acute malnutrition continue to persist driven by several factors, including high morbidity, below sphere immunization and Vitamin-A supplementation, poor childcare practices, inadequate access to clean/safe drinking water and acute food insecurity. Consequently, the country is facing a Triple Threat of Desert Locust, flooding’s and the global pandemic COVID-19 contributing towards the deterioration of production outputs, social economics increasing vulnerabilities of household. A total of 2.4 M under five children and PLW in need, while 1.14 M need treatment services and 1.16 M needs prevention services, despite the nutrition cluster effort in reaching 66% of the target in HRP 2020 still the needs on the ground is higher and needed urgent focus on the both treatment and prevention services to the vulnerable populations. However, Crude Death Rate and Under-Five Death Rate were relatively low in most areas. Urgent treatment and nutrition support is required for an estimated 1960,300 children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition through June 2021, including 341,679 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Integrated interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation. The proposed activities will improve equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services through systemic identification, referral, and treatment of acute malnourished case boys, girls and pregnant and lactating women. It will also provide holistic life-saving and life sustaining nutrition intervention, however, PDA been active nutrition and food security clusters where the organization is currently undertaken treatment of moderately malnourished children and pregnant and lactating women as well as providing cash based intervention through the funding of WFP and FAO intends to continue its current nutrition intervention in Middle Shabele and Mudug region through activating the static side and mobile outpatient therapeutic program sites hence provide active community case finding, screening, admission and treatment of severe acute malnutrition cases boys and girls, PLW including disable children and women.Through this PDA will ensure to coordinate its nutrition project with its protection program hence ensuring greater child protection as well as women and disable population in the target district ,PDA will also share ONA disggregated data by 5th of every end months through the nutrition cluster platform
PDA will also ensure to coordinates with the federal ministry of health member state and federal government as well as the other humanitarian actors in the proposed area of intervention hence ensuring coordinated response in the target population

Mitigation Measures

To ensure equity, OTP mobile/outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities as well as the IDPs and host communities, limited information and communication might hinder the understanding between PDA teams in the field and community leaders, however, to mitigate any such eventuality PDA will create feedback mechanism and reasonable compliance officer within its office as well as hotline mobile services to ensure all beneficiary complains are addressed timely. PDA intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of the vulnerable children boys, girls and pregnant and lactating women, and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Location, distance and access routes to the OTP sites and services might be a challenge of preventing factors limiting the access of the OTP sites PDA will enable its beneficiaries have accessible to all of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women PDA will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement.
