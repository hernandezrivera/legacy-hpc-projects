Objectives

Objective 1: Monitor and advocate for access to territory, prevention of refoulement, respect for refugees’ rights, whilst enhancing continuous registration and documentation for all refugee women, men, girls and boys, in order to contribute to sustainable solutionsObjective 2: Promote a community-based approach to the response, support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services for persons at heightened protection risk, including girls, boys, women and men of all ages and with diverse needs and vulnerabilities, with the ultimate aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks, strengthen the resilience of affected communities, and place communities at the center of the response in line with AAP principles.Objective 3: Support system strengthening, by supporting Government and local partners, promoting peaceful coexistence and enhancing social cohesion within and between refugee and host communities

Needs

Access to territory and asylum:Despite reduced arrivals in 2019,reports of alleged pushbacks and tightened border surveillance by authorities necessitates continued border monitoring.Continuous Civil,Identity Registration and Documentation: Data of the GoB-UNHCR Registration exercise completed in 2019 has to be continuously updated to facilitate access to assistance and protection. Refugee births,deaths,marriages and divorces are not officially registered.Marriage and divorce are conducted by traditional/religious leaders with little protection for children and women.No formal certification is allowed for those with access to education.Need to standardize civil registration-documentation and align them with the national systems.Fragile Protection Environment:Insecurity in the camps and rising tension/violence between refugees and host communities is a top protection concern.Armed criminal/radical elements cause fear/insecurity and engage in human trafficking,drug trade,abduction,robbery,restrictions on community volunteers,murders etc.Presence of security forces is inadequate and security incidents rise at night.Security efforts include informal curfews,day/night patrols by security forces and refugees.A wholesome approach is vital to ensure safety particularly for women and girls.Local media reports following criminal incidents and peaceful gathering of refugees in August 2019,led to restrictions on mobile phones and use of community volunteers,pressure on refugees to relocate to Bhasanchar and reduced protection space for humanitarian agencies.Access to Justice:Restrictions to refugees’ movement and unclear legal status renders them unable to access civil administration services and justice,puts them at risk of unlawful detention and leaves them vulnerable to exploitation and abuse.Unfamiliarity with the legal system,fear/suspicion of the police,fears of retaliation by criminal elements and lack of procedures for reporting,limit the scope for access to justice in the camps.Refugees are also reluctant to seek formal legal redress due to long court processes. Age,Gender and Diversity:Threats by alleged criminal groups/radical religious entities target refugee female volunteers and their families.Women and girls face cultural restrictions accessing services and resources.Issues of dowry,abandonment of women after marriage,custody of children after separation/divorce,are widely reported.These disputes are not justiciable for the Rohingya in courts,and require guidelines to protect women and children.UNHCR Refugee Stats indicate 3% elderly;31% families with at least one specific need,4% have disability and 2% are caregivers of elderly persons.PwSNs (including disability and the elderly) face barriers with access to services,communication and complaint mechanisms,inclusion in activities and decision making and negative stereotypes,.Unemployment of the youth places them at risk of involvement in crime or (perceived) radicalization. Refugee Representation and Community Structures:Monitoring reports indicate some level of corruption by “majhis”.Male dominated Majhis pose challenges for women participation in decision-making.The current policy climate is not conducive for refugee elections.There is need for structured framework for community-led mediation/dispute resolution.Access to information/accountability mechanisms:According to Ground Truth Solutions report (June 2019) 53% refugees feel informed of available services,58% feel they have adequate information on how to meet their needs and 78% feel aid providers consider their opinion when providing services.The June 2019 MSNA indicated that 94% households informed on feedback mechanisms still rely on Majhis to provide feedback/lodge complaints and women have less knowledge of existing complaints mechanisms.Face to face engagement is the preferred choice of communication given literacy and mobility barriers.

Activities or outputs

Objective 1: Border monitoring:200 border monitoring visits,advocate and engage authorities on access to territory for asylum seekers. 10 protection trainings for law enforcement agencies working in border areas. Receive new arrivals at Transit Center and relocate them to camps, prioritizing vulnerable cases. Protection Monitoring:Assess protection environment,access to basic rights,well-being,tensions with host communities and referral/case management of vulnerable refugees.Harmonized protection monitoring tools used.Refugee protection counsellors and paralegals support monitoring activities. Advocate for formal joint review of the protection environment (RRRC,humanitarian agencies,LEAs). Security/legal assistance:Advocate for increased freedom of movement for refugees. 30 prison visits,legal assistance and family visits to detained refugees.Advocate,train authorities on refugee protection.Provide legal assistance for all refugees.Refurbish police outposts and police patrols in camps.Human trafficking prevention and response.Co-chair Anti-Trafficking WG.Partner with IOM and UNDP on community policing.Registration:i)Support GoB to maintain 6 registration sites in new camps,the current structure in Registered camps and harmonize registration of both caseloads ii)Continuously register unregistered individuals, births,re-issuance of identity documents and update changes in family composition,personal status and protection needs iii)Avail ProGres to CiCs and other agencies iv)Align WFP SCOPE with ProGres. Civil Registration/Documentation:Align civil documents issued by CiCs with national systems.Support training,staffing and infrastructure for civil registration/documentation.Support CiCs to maintain marriage and divorce registers.Birth registration advocacy for children of mixed marriages.Support standardized birth notifications and certificates for all children. Objective 2: i)400 COMs and 15 Information Service Centres facilitate community feedback ii)Engage religious leaders and refugees in CwC messaging iii)Community Groups implement 1,500 projects iv)COMs engage 300,000 refugees on protection risks v)Profile and support 10,000 special needs refugees (disabled,elderly) vi)Advocate for/support elected camp committees.Objective 3: i)Refugees lead activities in 20 Community Centres ii)Youth engaged on community cohesion iii)Identify challenges,initiate community solutions between refugees and host communities iv)Support community-led dispute resolution.Protection Mainstreaming:Protection technical support/training to other sectors. Women’s needs/protection risks addressed during community participation.PSS spaces included in Community Centres. Participate in PWG, Child Protection and GBV sub-Sectors, PWG Task Team. UNHCR Protection Focal Points/PERU work with other sectors.Inclusion/accountability: FGDs/AGD participatory needs assessments conducted.Strengthen Feedback and Referral Systems.Participate in CwC Strategic Advisory Group;Accountability sub-Group;Technical WG. Gender Equality:Over 30% Camp Committee, Community Groups and COMs are women-UNHCR aspires to increase this but given current challenges with community empowerment/long-term efforts needed to address negative social norms,safe/meaningful participation will be prioritized.Engage religious leaders,men and boys on the women’s role in the community.Capacitate women volunteers/leaders.Natural disasters:COMs and Community Groups participate in emergency preparedness and response.UNHCR data guides relocation of refugees supported by Protection Focal Points,PERU and COMs.Co-chair CwC Emergency Communications WG.PSEA:HR policy requires staff background checks and complaints mechanisms (including community based);investigation,support and monitoring by IGO;Ethics Office and OIOS;dedicated UNHCR/partner PSEA focal points;provisions in staff and partner contracts;mandatory PSEA/CoC trainings;regular field monitoring. Strengthen SEA Victims Assistance Tracking.
