Project Summary

The objective of UNHCR's project is to increase resilience and livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable people affected by the Syrian crisis. This is to be achieved in collaboration with other UN agencies and local stakeholders through a range of livelihoods support interventions delivered in the underserved communities The project aims to assist a total of 102,630 IDPs, returnees and selected members of host communities across Syria. The project contributes to the Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) Sector outcomes as follows:
1. Strengthen access to livelihood by creating income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery.
2. Improve access to basic and social services and infrastructure.

4 - Needs

According to the draft 2020 HNO, around 9.9 million people are in need of multi-sectorial humanitarian assistance. An estimated 83% of Syrians live below the poverty line, becoming increasingly vulnerable due to the loss or lack of sustainable livelihoods. The HNO also confirms that people in Syria are lacking access to basic essential infrastructure and services. The existing social protection schemes, such as pensions and regular cash transfers for those who cannot provide for their livelihoods, are limited and those without such protection face significant risks of poverty and deprivation, often leading to social exclusion. The sanctions and fluctuating national currency, and soaring food and fuel prices have further aggravated the humanitarian situation. More than half of household income is now being spent on food (source: Crop and Food Security Report FAO/WFP, 2016 ). The situation is particularly serious for the most vulnerable in the society who may be compelled to resort to negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage, child labour, survival sex and temporary marriage. Many households are now headed by single females or the elderly who increasingly find themselves as the sole or main breadwinners. The project will take into account the recommendations on the improvement of the accessibility of the women and youth to the labour market, provided by several assessments carried out in 2019 including market assessments and Gender Barriers study. Where possible, socio-economic assessments will be undertaken to understand the socio-economic profile of target communities as well as the capacities and needs of targeted population in order to better identify livelihood opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The following four (3) main activities will be undertaken as part of the project:a) Rehabilitation of key local and community level economic and social infrastructure such as bakeries, market places, vocational training classes/workshops, warehouses, irrigation channels;b) Provision of business advisory services (including 'counselling') regarding business start up processes, business management and pursuing business opportunities;d) Conducting key socio-economic surveys, livelihoods analysis and other assessments (e.g. value chain analysis) necessary to understand vulnerability and relevant coping strategies and livelihoods opportunities;

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Protection risk: There is a risk of discrimination and the possibility of tension arising from selecting beneficiaries of livelihoods interventions, and risk of the most vulnerable being left out (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Mitigating measures for Risk 1: • Needs assessment will be conducted to gather information about the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees to be targeted. • Participatory assessments and community consultations will be applied in all stages of the programme cycle to enable beneficiaries to actively engage in the assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation of the project.• Awareness raising on equal opportunities and respect for human rights will be part of each intervention. Indicators of tension will be monitored throughout project implementation to intervene in a timely manner as necessary. • Capacity building of Partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD;• UNHCR will clearly inform community on beneficiary selection criteria and ensure that community leaders, local council, elderly people are engaged in community sensitization and beneficiary selection process. An active and informed complaint mechanism will be in place. • Enhance Partner post distribution monitoring 2. Protection risk: Existing labour market opportunities limit the participation of youth, women and persons with disabilities resulting in lower remuneration and return to negative coping mechanisms. Mitigating measures for Risk 2: • Analyze access to market opportunities with an age, gender and diversity lens. Compare this information with protection needs assessments.• Conduct market assessments and socio-economic field surveys to identify viable livelihoods interventions.• Provide livelihoods counselling and maintain referral system with protection partners.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk description: Lack of access hampering needs and impact assessments and protection monitoring of the programs results in continuous relying on estimations, 3rd party reports and information provided by partners for planning purpose, which may not accurately reflect needs and impact being assessed largely through proxy reporting. Mitigation measures: - Capacitate Partners to capitalize on information collected through livelihood staff and in the community centres and systematize methodologies used for focus group discussions in community centres;- Advocate with GoS on the need for systematic monitoring and continue the direct and indirect advocacy for access to safe areas;- Maintain and expand UNHCR field presence and monitoring capacity.
