Project Summary

Three decades of civil war and instability have weakened Somalia’s health system and contributed to it having some of the lowest health indicators in the world. The conflict-related fragility of Somalia over the past two and a half decades has resulted in the weakening of the health sector, its systems and its personnel, with a related focus on emergency response interventions to recurrent crises.
Countrywide, there is less than 1 health facility per 10,000 people, and many health posts do not operate fully due to human resource and infrastructure constraints.
Against this back drop, since 16 March 2020, 3588 laboratory-confirmed cases of COVID-19 including 99 associated deaths have been reported in Somalia. The large majority (83%) of cases are aged between 20 and 60 years, with a median age of 33 (ranging from 1 to 110 years) and 74% of the confirmed cases have been male. The weekly positivity rate has increased from 2.6 % last week to 5 % this reporting week (20-26th September 2020). The cumulative positivity rate, since the start of the outbreak, has declined gradually and as of this week, the rate stands at 12.2% while the cumulative case fatality rate stands at 2.7%. In Southwest State Somalia, suspected COVID-19 cases since March 2020 is 436 people and 14 people tested positive on week 20-26th of September 2020. Over 20,156 tests were carried out to date, and the testing capacity has improved in terms of geographic coverage though remains weak with gaps in coverage. (MoH COVID 19 Dashboard October 2020)
This project aims at providing quality essential lifesaving and life-sustaining health and nutrition services in Bay and Bakool area, with focus on women, children, and other vulnerable groups and strengthens the local capacity to deliver evidence-based and cost effective services based on the EPHS and Primary Health Care Approach. In addition, this will involve measles case management, immunization campaign, emergency preparedness, and response capacities, awareness and mobilization campaigns including COVID-19 messaging to prevent the spread of the virus.
In Baidoa, Berdale Hudur and Dinsoor, READO will support a total of 12 health facilities - 5 fixed health facilities (1 fixed health facility per district except for Baidoa which will have 2 fixed sites) and 7 mobile teams with 1 mobile team per district except for Baidoa district which will have 4 mobile teams with a network of CVs (IERT 1 Berdale, 4 Baidoa, 1 in Hudur and 1 in Diinsoor). The fixed facilities will act as a referral point for cases requiring observation and further referral for secondary health services. More so READO will engage 60 CVs in the four districts of implementation. Through this project, a total 55,000 individuals in Baidoa, Berdale, Diinsoor and Huddur will have access to a package of Emergency Primary Health Care (EPHC) including 18,000 children under 1 year of age in urgent need of Routine Immunization service containing measles Vaccination by the end of 2021. Vit A supplementation 18,000 children aged between six months to five years through mobile teams and fixed sites into the coverage area by the end of 2021. Deworming of 18,000 children aged between one to five years through mobile team and fixed sites with supplies the end of 2021.

This will involve risk communication and awareness campaigns (including debunking of myths, rumors and stigma) for a target of 10,000 women of reproductive age with a particular focus on vulnerable populations including IDPs, persons with disabilities and marginalized communities. Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion: READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. READO, will ensure that the lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. In addition, READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which included who has access to health care, who manages facilities, who is able to influence decisions on the provision of health services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identifyany barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. This information will be considered when determining locations for health interventions. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will provide the local/host community with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. In addition, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conict between the two communities. Flood or other emergencies:Target communities affected by flooding, AWD, influx or IDPs or forced IDP evictions etc., unforeseen by EWS. To mitigate this, READO will scale-up the beneficiaries targeted with health interventions, to provide emergency assistance to those worst affected. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age Preventing risks ofexclusion based on gender and age READO will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. This will be done through introducing special arrangements for persons unable to access health facilities eg.Mobile health services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks). In addition, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories andlayers of the affected population in identifying and responding to health needs.READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for health center location, design and services. Moreover, READO will engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.READO will also have a proportionate number of female health staff. READO will also establish complaints and response mechanism. READO will use information boards to display Complaint Response Mechanism phone number during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection and at health centers. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaint. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. In turn, the call center will be in charge of calling beneficiaries as part of the monitoring system. Protection of civilians: READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets. READO will not share information on the identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits)
