Project Summary

Bur Hakaba is the largest district amongst the five districts of the Bay region in the south-west, Somalia. It is located Baidoa to the west, Wanlawein to the east, Dinsor to the south-west, Jalalaqsi to the north-east and Qoryoley to the south. The main livelihoods in the district are southern inland pastoral (camel, sheep, and goats) and the agro-pastoral (camel, cattle, sorghum).
Bur Hakaba is estimated to approximate a number of 160,000 people living in urban and rural villages specifically 26 main villages and 387 sub-villages with varied inhabitants of decedent’s mostly indigenous marginalized and vulnerable community strongly dependents on rain fed crop farming and livestock raising. Inhabitants are mainly sorghum producers.
Burhakaba Districts with GAM prevalence of around 13.8% (FSNAU and FEWS NET report of May, 2020). The situation is attributed to impacts of Covid-19 pandemic and eventual containment measures, serious Gu 2020 flooding and desert locust invasion that not only aggravated shocks but also worsened malnutrition caseloads that weakened already fragile food insecurity and further aggravated morbidity due to incrased cases of water borne diseases and low immunization for vaccine preventable diseases. Bur Hakaba District experienced recurrent drought, insecurity and Flash floods which is resulted Water shortage, Food shortage, outbreak disease and Malnutrition, Over the past decade, Bur Hakaba has faced a series of small/large scale disasters, with thousands affected by disastrous floods, Droughts and displacement with frequent cholera outbreaks in rural and urban areas. The damage to WASH infrastructure is immense with the humanitarian consequences of the disaster. Poor Hygiene and Sanitation. Access to clean water and sanitation continues to be low in Bur Hakaba. The lack of access to safe water and sanitation contributes to the incidence of diarrheal disease, which has a detrimental impact on children’s nutritional status. About 11% of Bur Hakaba children have diarrhea in any 2-week period.
Amongst the most vulnerable communities, highly affected are the in both urban& rural areas. This dangerous situation could easily lead to the spread of water borne and Covid19 that require lifesaving humanitarian assistance. Failing to respond to the most acute needs will drastically increase the prevalence of diarrheal diseases and risk of AWD/Cholera/Covid19 outbreaks among population that have already suffered from drought conditions.

Mitigation Measures

1- Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and service: Mitigation Measure : Enhancing security at the project sites such as guards at the health facilities as well as those accompanying the mobile teams 2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. Mitigation Measure: Ensuring beneficiary feedback and complains mechanism in place in addition to all staff signing PSEA code of conduct 3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) Mitigation measure: Conducting awareness on protection assistance for survivors of protection incidences 4. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities Mitigation measure: BTSC supports the participation of both men and women as well as other vulnerable groups by providing safe spaces for these groups to provide feedback (e.g. women-only focus discussion groups). Accountability to affected population BTSC has a robust complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) that handles complaints and feedback from all beneficiaries and all stakeholders involved in the assistance process. The CFM not only aims at strengthening accountability to communities but also to identify a range of protection, fraud and diversion issues as well as other concerns related to delivery of BTSC relief programs. Complaints can be made through a well-established Somalia-based toll-free hotline, directly to BTSC monitoring staff and cooperating partner staff at programme implementation sites, through telephone calls and email or short messaging to BTSC offices, and through local authorities and local leaders.
