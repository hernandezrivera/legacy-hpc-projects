Project Summary

The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the South Sudan. The Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 3.9 million people, about 55 per cent of the 7.1 million peoples (excluding refugees), in dire need of Food Security and Livelihood life-saving assistance in 2024. PCO aims at targeting 2000 households 12, 000 individuals (Twic 4,000, Yirol West 4,000 and Pibor GPAA 4,000), includes people with special needs and youth amongst host, returnee and IDP communities for a period of 12 months. To enhance and sustain emergency food production will be through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support. In light of resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households (CO3), this will be realized through training of farmers, setting up of kitchen garden in the 9 targeted Nutrition facilities, establishment of demo sites, fisher folks, formation of VSLA groups (20 member each), and through local procurement of seeds through seed fairs. The training will cover good and climate-smart agronomic practices, countering post-harvest losses, sustainable income generation activities, Seed Fairs (SFs) for Crop Seed procurement will be done from seed producer groups/traders in Neighboring Counties of where seeds will be procured locally from these farmers and distributed to the community for farming purposes among vulnerable populations at risk of protection concerns through linkages of already instituted collaborative efforts with GBV, Nutrition, Education, Health and WASH partners, these will also be linked to create complementarities. Beneficiary targeting will be through the AAP committee with mainstreaming of gender, persons with disabilities, minority and marginalized groups.
PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead in WBeG and currently implementing School Feeding Program in Wau and Jur River counties funded by WFP, Currently implementing Education project targeting 5 schools under UNOCHA 2022 SA1 funding in Twic County being supported by SSHF 2022, The on-going Multi Year Response Education Program (MYRP) project targeting 40 schools in Gogrial West, Tonj East and Tonj North Counties funded by Education Cannot Wait (ECW) from June 2023 to May 2026. PCO is also implementing RALP FSL Project in Wau County from November 2022 to November 2023 being funded by World Bank and also implementing Camp and Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Project in Pibor GPAA of Jonglei from December 2022 to November 2023 UNOCHA 2022 SA1 funding, In addition is the CCCM Cluster Lead in Pibor GPAA. PCO is the FAO/FSL Cluster core pipeline manager in Pibor GPAA. PCO in partnership with NPA has a running FSL Project in Rumbek East County of Lake state supported by NORAD. PCO has maintained uninterrupted programmatic presence, strong network and linkages with the local authority in targeted counties since 2014 and proposed action will scale up FSL initiatives. The organization has a functional and experienced emergency response team that has been working in coordination with the states as well as National FSL, Protection, Education WASH and Nutrition Clusters and is better placed to respond to the proposal.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation is worsened by high levels of food insecurity, endemic violence, protracted conflict and flooding limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services, sub-national violence is expected to continue in many locations and abate in others until the peace process and governance becomes truly inclusive; a fifth year of a typical flooding is becoming the new ‘normal’ causing displacement, damage to homes and affecting agricultural livelihoods and households’ ability to produce their own food with the added effect of water logging and water borne disease on livestock and human health, access constraints and operational interference, persistent high food prices, periodic dry spells continue to threaten people’s food security. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)-2024 estimated that 7.1 million are people in need of emergency life-saving food security services in South Sudan. The on-going economic crisis has significantly reduced households’ purchasing power, prolonged dry spells at critical stages of crop growth, flooding, crop pests and diseases, Livestock have been looted, the recent influx of returnees and the returning of IDPs to their original residential areas as a result of the current conflict in neighbouring Sudan Country impeding humanitarian access. South Sudan is experiencing unprecedented levels of acute food insecurity, with an IPC Phase 3, 4&5 (Catastrophe) acute food insecurity indicators observed in Counties of Twic, Yirol West and Pibor GPAA. The high levels of food insecurity observed in Warrap State (Twic County, Jonglei state (Pibor GPAA) and Lakes State (Yirol West) could potentially serve as a trigger for continued organized violence, as hunger forces households to raid neighbouring communities for livestock and livelihood assets. The protracted nature of the crisis in South Sudan have resulted in significantly greater levels of food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, forcing many families to resort to negative coping strategies by reducing meals, going without food, surviving on wild fruits or selling their last livestock’s, transactional sex, sale of productive assets, early/ forced marriage and forms of child labour. These results to food consumption gaps, reduced dietary diversity and increased household hunger scores. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. This project aims at enabling the community increase food and livestock production to save life and reduce dependency on food and agriculture input to strengthen household ability to absorb shocks to conflict affected population in Counties of Twic, Yirol West and Pibor GPAA of South Sudan. PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead in WBeG and also the CCCM Cluster Coordinator in Pibor GPAA. This project is linked to the general strategy of saving lives and providing timely integrated multi- sectorial assistance and reinforcing protection, particularly connected to the 2024 FSL cluster strategy by Enhancing and sustaining emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock and Reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. PCO will collaborate with other clusters like WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Health and stakeholders to ensure coherent, coordinated and integrated responses driven by the assessed Food Security and Livelihood needs of affected populations.

2. Project activities

TThe overall objective of this proposed Humanitarian Response plan is to address acute food insecurity to the most conflict/ flood affected vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities for 12,000 individuals (2000HHs) through implementation of the following key activities; Farmers training through farmer field schools and demonstration plots to farmers, Training of fisher folks, Conducting Seed Fairs (SFs) for local Crop Seed procurement seed producer groups/traders, Formation and training VSLAs. Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits, Emergency food & livelihood support, fishing kits/ vegetable kits; rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed), Livelihood support to returnee households, Provision of timely food security information, Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives IRRM: food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds, Conditional cash transfers (social skills & safety nets)/ Conditionality: (investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees etc.), Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc.,Multi sectoral models: livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation, Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW & DL surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication, Reduction in post-harvest losses and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies. VSLAs shall be identified and engaged in the seed multiplication and seed saving training exercise. Establishment of demonstration plots, farmer-to-farmer learning, and on mass agricultural campaigns and conduct refresher training to County Agriculture Department Officers and CBAEWs. Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with a particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to the affected population, and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities.
