Project Summary

This project will focus on ensuring that crisis-affected populations in Aweil East and Aweil West counties are supported to meet their immediate food needs in the most efficient, cost-effective and impacting way through one time off food distribution, and through provision of early maturing crops and vegetable seeds and farm implements for the vulnerable households to be able to grow vegetables and other crops and provide for their immediate and long term food needs. The project will also focus on creating nutritional awareness campaigns to help reduce the impact of malnutrition. The project further focuses on GBV prevention and awareness to help reduce GBV cases and negative coping mechanism. In addition, Peace Winds Japan will also provide technical knowledge on modern vegetable and crop production methods, Climate Smart Agriculture practices, and soil and water conservation to maximize household production for food to meet the lean season food requirements.
The project will also aim at ensuring that the target population, ie the returnees, the flood affected victims, IDPs and host communities are empowered and their mindset changed from over reliance on emergency aid to more sustainable economic independent status. To ensure such aim is obtained beneficiaries’ active participation and engagement throughout the project cycle is imperative as this will promote acceptance, success, and sustainability of the actions. Similarly, coordination with other actors will promote information sharing, harmonized approach in implementation, agree on prioritization, joint planning, increase efficiency in resources mobilisation, design, methodologies and tools for ensuring effective and quality delivery of intended activities. Peace Winds Japan will closely coordinate with protection, education, WASH, food security and livelihood actors to increase synergies and complementarities of proposed activities. In addition, Peace Winds Japan aims to strengthen collaboration with community actors such as community leaderships, relevant line ministries and community-based organizations (CBOs) to plan and implement activities that reflect the interests of the community.
The project will ensure close collaboration and coordination with other stakeholders will play an important role in ensuring complementarity and efficient implementation of the project as duplication of activities is minimized.
Finally the project targets both the returnees, IDPs and the host communities who are the victims of conflict and natural disaster (floods). The target population for the two counties is estimated at 97,319 ( 28,217 boys, 20,101 girls, 15,816 men, 17517 women, 2,444 elderly men and 3,413 elderly women

1. Needs

South Sudan is facing an unprecedented humanitarian crisis driven by a combination of ongoing subnational violence, climate change, displacement, and an escalating cost of living. These factors have pushed millions of families into acute food insecurity, with nine million people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in 2024. The food security situation is dire, with the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) forecasting that 7.1 million people will experience severe food insecurity during the 6 month (April-September) lean season, including over 79,000 people in Northern Bahr el Ghazal who will face emergency and catastrophic levels of food insecurity. Among these vulnerable populations, over 1.6 million children and 870,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women are moderately or severely malnourished, surpassing the levels recorded in 2023. The influx of over 676,836 returnees and refugees from Sudan, fleeing deteriorating security conditions, has further strained the already limited resources in host communities. Many of these newcomers arrive without assets and with minimal coping mechanisms, worsening the existing vulnerabilities of local populations. Counties bordering Sudan, in particular, are experiencing IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) or worse levels of food insecurity, as these communities are overwhelmed by the additional burden of providing shelter, food, and basic services to the displaced.Agriculture, which constitutes 36 percent of South Sudan’s non-oil GDP, holds significant potential to improve food security and livelihoods in the country. However, the agricultural sector remains underdeveloped due to limited access to quality inputs, inadequate farming techniques, and poor infrastructure, such as the lack of all-weather roads. As a result, many households continue to face food shortages and income insecurity. These challenges are compounded by high inflation, currency devaluation, and competition for scarce resources, further exacerbating the vulnerability of both local and displaced populations.The combination of these ongoing challenges, alongside the disruption of supply chains for essential commodities, is severely affecting over 54 percent of the population already experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity. In addition to the immediate risks of hunger and malnutrition, the growing food insecurity threatens to have long-term social consequences, including the use of harmful coping strategies such as child labor, school dropouts, child marriage, neglect, marginalization, and an increased risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Furthermore, the recent devastating floods in South Sudan have exacerbated the situation, displacing over 271,000 people, destroying crops, livestock, and infrastructure, and rendering key supply routes impassable. With Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Unity states bearing the brunt of the floods, the damage to agricultural production and infrastructure is expected to undermine recovery efforts, leading to long-term challenges in food availability and economic resilience.Given the compounding humanitarian, economic, and environmental crises, there is an urgent need for a comprehensive food security and livelihoods programmes in South Sudan. Such a project would not only provide immediate relief to those suffering from hunger and malnutrition but also support the long-term recovery and resilience of vulnerable communities, particularly those hosting returnees and refugees. It is critical to address the root causes of food insecurity by enhancing agricultural productivity, improving access to markets, and strengthening livelihoods through sustainable interventions that promote economic self-reliance and reduce dependency on external aid. This project will contribute to addressing the immediate needs of food-insecure populations while building a foundation for longer-term recovery and resilience.

2. Project activities

The Lean Season Response in the counties of Aweil East and Aweil West (Priority 3) will be for 6 months (April to September) and the following are the activities;Objective 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations 1-1. Mapping of Beneficiaries: Peace Winds Japan will mobilize local stakeholders (officials, leaders, and communities) to identify and assess beneficiaries, ensuring the inclusion of vulnerable groups such as IDPs, returnees, and women-headed households. 1-2. Identification and training of Project Management Committees for General Food Distribution to ensure community-based project monitoring. 1-3. Procurement and distribution of assorted food items to the affected population.1-4. Nutrition sensitization will be run to teach participants about dietary diversity and nutrition. This will include the type and variety of foods to consume and grow and the cooking practices but also hygiene guidance due to the intrinsic link between hygiene and malnutrition.1-5. Establishment of complaints feedback mechanisms such as help desk, suggestion boxes and hotline call where possible at all project sites and raising awareness to communities to provide feedback and communication.Objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock2-1. Mapping of Beneficiaries: Peace Winds Japan will mobilize local stakeholders (officials, leaders, and communities) to identify and assess beneficiaries, ensuring the inclusion of vulnerable groups such as IDPs, returnees, and women-headed households.2-2. Enhancing Farmer Knowledge and Skills: Farmers will receive training in good agricultural practices (GAP), climate-smart agriculture (CSA), and sustainable farming techniques. The training will focus on crop selection, soil conservation, water efficiency, and post-harvest management to improve resilience to climate change.2-3. Selection and Training of Farmer Group Leaders: Farmers will form groups, with each group selecting a leader to be trained as a trainer of trainers (ToT). These leaders will provide ongoing agricultural advice to their groups.2-4. Procurement and Distribution of Farm Inputs: Farmers will receive agricultural inputs such as seeds, tools, and pesticides. Follow-up will be conducted to monitor the usage and effectiveness of the inputs during key stages of crop production.2-5. Establishment of Kitchen Gardens at Institutional and household: The gardens will be established with support from trained ToTs, Peace Winds Japan agricultural officers and government officers. At the institutions (schools and nutrition facilities), these gardens will serve as demonstration farms for agriculture students, and lactating mothers and provide food for the school's feeding program and also support mothers.Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes3-1. Community Infrastructure Support: Infrastructure projects such as soil water conservation, water harvesting systems, and boreholes/pumps for irrigation will be implemented to enhance agricultural productivity and resilience. Peace Winds Japan will oversee the design, planning, and construction.3-2. Training on Marketing and Post-Harvest Handling: Farmers will receive training on market research, sales techniques, storage, value addition, and post-harvest management to improve profitability and reduce losses.3-3. Procurement and Distribution of Post-Harvest Inputs: Farmers will receive post-harvest tools like sacks, wheelbarrows, and drying racks, followed by a post-distribution survey to assess their effectiveness.
