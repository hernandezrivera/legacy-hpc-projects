Project Summary

The project will reach those most at risk (IDPs, host communities, returnees) by supporting health facilities mainly located in areas where large number of IDPs are living including inclusive facilities easing access for people with disabilities (PWDs), dialysis centres providing services to the chronically ill, and facilities providing life-saving trauma and other medical services. Also, throughout this project, GBV survivors will be supported by more capable health facility staff and referral mechanisms at the supported health facilities.
The Project has following main components:
 4 Mobile Emergency Units will be supported by stipends, running costs, equipment, drugs, and disposables.
 6 PHC centres will receive stipends running costs, equipment, drugs and disposables.
 2 dialysis centres will receive stipends, running costs and dialysis sessions.
 1 cardiology centre will receive drugs, disposables, stipends and running costs
 30 PHC centres will receive equipment, drugs and disposables (without stipends)
 150 healthcare staff will be trained in data management, warehouse management, orientation on the complaint mechanism, GBV case management, protection, and inclusion.
 Maintenance of the healthcare facilities in the promotion of inclusion and the strengthening of established referral systems for GBV cases will be carried out to promote protection.
IR Syria Mission in Turkey is involved in various national and international coordination settings, i.e. as member of the NGO forum which includes most of the INGOs working to support communities inside Syria through Turkey. IR in Turkey takes part in UNOCHA coordination activities to coordinate with all other actors working inside Syria. Within the health sector, IR Syria Mission is an active member since its launch in Southern Turkey. IR Syria Mission team regularly attends the relevant clusters meetings conducted on monthly basis where they share the 4Ws in order to update on the progress of all ongoing projects. IR also reports on the UN-Financial Tracking System (FTS). IR Syria has also actively participated inside the country in regular coordination meetings and has good relations with the local councils, authorities and health directorates and coordinates with various community-based organizations in the areas in order to be able to update the data on access, beneficiary numbers and safety regulations frequently.
Moreover, IR coordinates with local communities of different areas inside Syria to share information about their needs and ongoing responses on the ground. IR coordinates with various community-based organizations and other local actors like the local councils and health directorates inside the areas of intervention in northern Syria in order to share updates about the accessible routes to guarantee that assistance reaches the target beneficiaries and to ensure the safe passage for project deliverables as well as staff.
The project aims to provide a complete service delivery package including trauma, non-communicable diseases and referral support. This includes but not limited to provision of drugs, disposables and equipment to the targeted health facilities to treat range of diseases such as respiratory illness, Dermatologic conditions, acute diarrhea, Chronic illnesses (diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, kidney) Anemia, antenatal care and surgical procedure.
IR has 2 MEAL officers and 2 MEAL assistants in Syria to provide progress monitoring. Field reports in the form of monthly progress reports as well as feedback received from the main stakeholders and from the targeted community will be shared through regular Skype meetings between field staff in Syria and management staff based in Turkey and actions will be taken accordingly. Also IR will ensure to have a robust complaint and feedback mechanism.

4 - Needs

Eight years of crisis have heavily disrupted the health system in Syria, including 65 attacks on health care reported between January and September 2019. 42 administrative districts – home to 14.5 million people – are below emergency standards for hospital beds. Up to 70 per cent of health workers have left Syria and many others have been injured or killed. 64 per cent (13.2 M) of the population lives below minimum standards for healthcare workers. Health personnel shortages are particularly acute in areas affected by recent hostilities and displacement. Distance, overcrowding and long waiting times are top barriers reported by patients to access health services. Accessibility and cost of transport also impede access to services for persons with disability. Availability of medicines and treatment costs are also leading determinants of child mortality in Syria. Among the 12 million people in need of health services, 1.43 million children under age 5; 3.22 million women of reproductive age (15-49) including 483 000 women who are expected to become pregnant in 2020; 336, 369 elderly (65+) and those with early-onset chronic disease; and displaced populations, including 5.54 million IDPs and returnees, are key population groups for the sector. Persons living with disabilities, an estimated 15 per cent of the population15 (3.07 million people), as well as those with functional difficulties16 require specialized services and access considerations.

5 - Implementation of activities

IRW will implememnt the project through the following:- Mobile Emergency units: 4 MEUs will be supported for 10 months with stipend for the health workers and adminsitrative staff, running cost and drugs and disposables. The Units conduct surgical procedures as well as treat chronic medical conditions and trauma injuries. -PHC center support: 6 PHC centers will be supported for 10 months with stipned, running cost, equipment, drugs and disposables. Also another 30 PHC will be supported with drugs, disposables and equipment. This is to address the need for medicine dispensing. The medicine dispensed free of charge will help the medical staff treat the main conditions affecting the population of the IDP camps as well as the other communities of NWS.-Dialysis centers support: 2 dialysis centers will receive support including dialysis kits, running cost and stipend for the health workers and centre adminsitrative staff. -This project will also provide trainings to healthcare workers in many different subjects depending on their specializations, the staff of the health sector will receive appropriate trainings in the following:a) Advanced data managementb) Warehouse management basicsc) Orientation on the complaint mechanismd) GBV case managemente) Protection and inclusionThe proposed project will improve communication between health actors to strengthen comprehensiveness and continuity of care, enabling coordination among health care networks. This will be achieved through active participation at Health cluster forum, monthly submission of 4W, and reporting the ﬁnancial contribution on UN Financial Tracking system (FTS). The project reﬂects the different needs of men, women and children in the health sector and aims to be sensitive and inclusive. The project will support qualiﬁed medical staff with stipends to ensure they are able to provide quality health services. While covering the special needs of women and children.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Some of the identiﬁed protection risks and mitigation measures that IR will apply include the following: 1. Data on attacks against healthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA) during the period of January - October 2019 documented 73 attacks affecting 52 health facilities. Despite extensive advocacy on the part of health actors, media and the UN, the attacks continue and violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas. Mitigation Measure: IR will ensure continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the humanitarian leadership (cluster) in their efforts to highlight protection challenges facing health workers. 2. Gender Based Violence: Health services are often the ﬁrst, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical that health providers are equipped to offer quality health services. Mitigation Measure: Identiﬁcation of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases, in a manner guarantees safety, conﬁdentiality, and privacy of the survivor. Provision of drugs and disposables required for the treatment of GBV cases. Facilitate Presence of female staff (considering female for stipend provision) in targeted health facilities to increase access of women and girls to health services. 3. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Mitigation Measure: Emplacement of effective complaint and feedback.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

some of the risks and their mitigation measures identiﬁed are as follows. 1. The closure of Bab al Hawa border crossing between Turkey and Syria. Mitigation measure: IR will use second border crossing i.e. Bab al Salamah. This will slightly increase the transport cost of the deliverables. 2. The interruption of the road between Manbej and Azaz and Idlib areas. Mitigation measure:The closure of this road will cause higher fuel prices as this road is the only source of fuel entry to the region and therefore will cause higher transportation and labor costs in general, but there are more expensive alternatives such as fuel imported from Turkey which is regularly present in the project area. 3. The escalation of hostilities and targeting of the medical facilities supported. Mitigation measure: IR will conduct a thorough security and safety analysis of the healthcare centre to be supported or location to host a mobile unit. Once supported, the facility will be reassessed for safety and security regularly in order to prepare contingency plans to deal with any emergency situation. These contingency plans will address the relocation of the health facilities (in case of the MEUs), the staff work schedule and emergency treatment of trauma patients including their referral destinations. 4. Non availability of some medical items delaying the procurement process. Mitigation measure: IR team can identify the alternatives of the medical items non available and can initiate to replace such items with those available. Since IR is working with multiple suppliers for procurement, thereby due to long standing relationships the suppliers can also facilitate this replacement of items quickly. 5. Qualified health staff leaving area. Mitigation measure: IR will ensure rigorous coordination with the health directorates in order to find alternatives for the health staff also stipend will be provided to encourage health workers to work with the designated health facility.
