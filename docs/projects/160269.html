Project Summary

ASIMO intervention; “Emergency WASH preparedness and response support for women, men, boys, and girls affected by conflict, disease outbreaks, natural hazard, or displacement in Raja County, WBeGs and Tonj North Warrap” This project aims to reduce levels of morbidity and mortality among vulnerable populations displaced to and within Raja North, South, Bormadina and Sopo payam under Raja County and Tonj North in Lurchuk, Pagol, Manloor and Manyang where critical WASH infrastructure has been destroyed or made inoperable by the conflict, or has become insufficient to address the needs of conflict-affected communities. While durable solutions for water provision are found through water site rehabilitation or enhancement, ASIMO proposes to bridge the gap in access to safe drinking water in Raja by assessing and rehabilitating 6 water points in Bormadina, Raja North, south and Sopo, payam and 7 boreholes in Manloor, Manyang, Lurchuk and Pagol in order to improve access to water in these areas and boost capacity of communities affected by conflict through focusing on establishing resilient water supply and sanitation facilities in strategic locations for health service provision. In line with the Health and WASH Cluster's recommendations, ASIMO will enhance water infrastructure resilience through rehabilitation of 2 boreholes in Health Centers (PHCU) under sopo, raja and Raja South and 4 boreholes in Tonj North. The direct target population of the ASIMO WASH intervention is the IDPs and returnees residing in the Makeshift Settlements of raja South, North, Sopo and Bormadina contains approximately 6, 000 IDPs, whilst the population of returnees is 10,300.in tonj North, the targeted are 2,300 IDPS hosting with communities in Manloor, Lurchuk, Manyang and Pagol and returnees are 8,180. Access to a safe water supply in returnees and IDPS Makeshift settlement remains challenging. Access to sanitation which meets sphere and national indicators is lacking across the entire target area. Within 12 months, complete coverage of WASH infrastructure in targeted health facilities and the communities Makeshift will be completed and maintained. Improved relations with settlement structures will contribute to better maintenance of WASH infrastructure and improved awareness amongst the population of the importance of maintaining WASH infrastructure and improving hygiene practices. In turn, this will contribute to better health outcomes in the makeshift settlement, reducing risk of waterborne diseases and maternal and neonatal deaths. The proposed lifesaving interventions will target IDPs and vulnerable host population and returnees who are hosting in Raja and Tonj North Counties. The proposed interventions will compliment nutrition interventions being undertaken, through improved hygiene and sanitation practices. This will result in reduced incidences of acute malnutrition and diarrhea among children aged 5 – 96 months and reduction of water, sanitation and hygiene related diseases among targeted populations. This intervention will create synergies and complement with other national NGOs and INGOs WASH partners operational in Raja and Tonj North in Warrap for clean water supply. In partnership with the protection cluster, the project will address protection related concerns linked to sanitation facilities to mitigate gender-based violence and to ensure minimum safety and privacy requirements are upheld. The key project interventions are; construction of latrines, waste management through establishment of garbage pits, menstrual hygiene management through distribution of hygiene and dignity kits to women and girls, hygiene promotion awareness support provided through awareness sessions to target population. The project targets 26,780(8,000 men and 18,780 women) conflict affected population (8,000 IDPs, 15,780 Returnees and 3,000 host communities)

1 - Needs

The South Sudan security situation is uncertain, unpredictable and fluid, meaning that IDPs and Returnees in Raja and Tonj North are constantly moving to escape the conflict and violence, which is reflected in the fluctuating numbers. The humanitarian crisis in has further exacerbated gender inequality, especially with regard to health risks and sexual and gender-based violence. For example, women and girls face risks when they leave their homes to conduct daily duties, such as collecting firewood or water. Young girls are at greater risk of being forced into early marriage to provide their families with money as coping mechanism during the crisis. Separated children, especially boys, face the risk of being recruited into the armed forces or being targeted by ethnic-based violence. In particular, recruitment of boys belong age of 18 years has been prevalent in since the onset of the crisis. Gender based violence, forced and early marriages and other forms of child abuse are rife with in Raja and there is need to intervene and reverse the situation that greatly impacts women and girls.There is dare need for sanitation facilities. The project will have separate sanitation facilities for female and male users, with visible demarcation erected, with latrines being located closer to homesteads at minimum distances, to ensure that women do not have to travel far to access services. And that these latrines are accessible by disabled and elderly men and women and physically disabled persons by construction of ramps to them. There are numerous consequences of poor WASH services in Nutrition/HCFs. Due to inadequate provision of WASH services, conflict affected populations are potentially at higher risk of developing health care associated infections (HCAIs). The risk of infection is particularly high in new-borns. Further, lack of adequate WASH services may discourage women from giving birth in HCFs or causing delays in care-seeking. Addressing the inadequate provision of WASH services in HCFs will not only improve the quality of care but also attract many people to seek care including delivery services to pregnant women and most importantly contribute in the prevention of HCAIs. There is need to prevent, prepare, increase awareness, and possibly respond to disease outbreaks in high-risk locations with WASH support. In addition, there is great need to avail as well as create awareness to target women on the use of dignity kit /menstrual hygiene management kit, in a discreet and dignified manner, in a women’s only forum. In order to meet these needs, ASIMO’s Emergency Response (ER) team will respond with water supply systems that might be required with set up and installation, consumables such as soap, PUR sachets, buckets, jerry cans, male hygiene kits, female hygiene and dignity kits, etc., that are required for regular provision to IDPs. Latrine construction materials such as latrine slabs, tarpaulins and digging tools are required to meet basic needs and minimum SPHERE standards.

2 - Activities

Activity (1): Conduct focused group discussions (FGDs) at targeted community settlements, to get feedback on design, implementation and monitoring of appropriateness of sanitation facilities. Activity (2): Construction of 100 household latrines to be shared between 2- 4 families through the implementation of CLTS approach, taking into account minimum safety and gender considerations, as well as accessible for disabled population, other approaches utilized based on appropriateness to the context Activity (3): Establishment of 20 garbage disposal pits in collaboration with households/community through awareness and monitoring. Activity (4): Identify and train 10 groups of community hygiene promoters and each has 10 members, 50% being female on hygiene awareness and promotion. Activity (5): Beneficiary registration and distribution of appropriate menstrual hygiene management kits targeting 1000 adolescent girls and women of child bearing age. Activity (6): Distribution and training on the use of household level treatment systems (boiling / PUR tablets, etc) Activity (7): Construction /rehabilitation of 6 institutional health facilities latrines in raja and Tonj North. Activity (8): Capacity building of 40 module mother approach members for sensitization of care givers and patience on proper hygiene and sanitation practices at health facilities Activity (9): Repairing and rehabilitation of existing 20 non-functional hand pumps in Raja and Tonj North Activity (10): Create awareness on menstrual hygiene management (MHM) to the Adolescent girl and women in targeted locations for WASH intervention targeting 2000 beneficiaries) Activity (11): Design and distribute culturally and gender sensitive IEC and training materials appropriate to the local culture. Activity (12): Create awareness on environmental hygiene (waste management systems and water safety handling targeting 2,000 beneficiaries) Activity (13): Training of 20 water users’ associations groups in targeted locations and each should have 5 members for returnees, host and IDPS sites Activity (14): 200 Water quality testing sampling, including monitoring of water quality at the household level Activity (15): Distribution of WASH hygiene NFI kits (dignity kits, water storage and transport devices, etc.) Activity (16): monitoring the progress made towards the implementation of WASH intervention in targeted locations in Raja and Tonj North Counties.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project takes a human rights approach to addressing the risks faced by the targeted conflict affected people. For instance it pays recognition to people with disabilities access to WASH as a human right. Resolution 64/292,of the United Nations General Assembly explicitly recognised the human right to water and sanitation and acknowledged that clean drinking water and sanitation are essential to the realisation of all human rights. Additionally, this resolution calls on international organisations to help provide safe, clean, accessible and affordable drinking water and sanitation for all. The UN Convention on the Rights of people with disabilities also mandates accessibility as a human rights’ issue and changing lives of people with disabilities through several articles that call for states to make public and private infrastructure accessible for people with disabilities. Additionally, the new Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) includes a proposed goal to ‘ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all’. (SDG Goal 6: Clean Water and Sanitation)The project is also guided by the Sphere Project Handbook (2011) that sets out minimum standards in disaster response and includes approaches for meeting the needs of people with disabilities. Disability is addressed as an issue cutting across all the main sectors, including water supply, sanitation, nutrition, food aid, shelter, and health services.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Community members were consulted through focused group discussions (FGDs), to get their preliminary contribution to overall project design, implementation and monitoring and appropriateness of WASH facilities/interventions. Through this broad consultation, protection is furthermore enhanced because the disaggregated risks/needs of the community are recognized and enable a more targeted response; the drivers of conflict are more likely to be seen, thereby reducing the risk of causing further HARM while, in contrast, enhancing protection by the community. Beneficiary and community participation at this point of consultation ensures project ownership and accountability is achieved through community contribution in the project implementation cycle.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project aims at ensuring durable solutions for water provision through water site rehabilitation or enhancement. Training of borehole pump mechanics and water management committees will provide the sustainability component beyond ASIMO intervention. The fact that beneficiaries are to play an instrumental role in repair/rehabilitation of WASH facilities through cash for work model through incentives payment to trainined water users associations. Beneficiary and community participation to ensure project ownership and accountability shall be achieved through community contribution in the project implementation cycle, for example, communities will contribute labor for excavation of latrine pits and garbage management pits, and construction of superstructure of latrines using local materials sourced by community under CLTS approach. The project from the start takes due consideration and steps towards sustainability and orderly exit strategy
