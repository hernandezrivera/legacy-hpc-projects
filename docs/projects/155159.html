Project Summary

In 2018, efforts by the Communication with Communities (CWC) Working Group members have seen a rise in access to life-saving information. However, significant gaps remain in the dissemination, use of these materials across sectors/members as well as community engagement, particularly for vulnerable people.
In host communities, ensuring equality of access to information throughout the response is one the major identified gaps. This information gap is creating various kinds of insecurity, resulting possibility of unrest situation between rohingya communities. Besides this info gap in lessening the access to host community therefore, in order to meet this gap BRAC-C4D design this activity.
1. 200 trained community volunteers disseminate life-saving messages on health, WASH, education, food security, environment, protection, skill development, GBV, DRR, social cohesion and other relevant issues conducting regular inter personal communication (IPC) with family groups (women, men, adolescent boys and girls etc.) sessions in host communities. 200 volunteer will be conducted 10 IPC session in 1 day. As a result, 4000,000 IPC sessions (200 volunteers X 10 IPC sessions per volunteer, per day X 200 days in one year) will be conducted to disseminate life-saving messages on health, WASH, education, food security, environment, protection, skill development, GBV, DRR and other relevant issues by the trained volunteer within 200 days in one year. Along with that, will contribute to meeting the information needs to the host community.
2. Interactive Popular Theatre (IPT) shows as edutainment to promote positive behaviours and practices.
3. Organize Consultation meetings in participation with community people from host and Rohingya community, service providers and leaders to establish service accountability to affected populations through effective community engagement and social cohesion.
4. Conduct advocacy meetings with community leaders in host communities to identify and act on gaps for ensuring social cohesion.
5. Conduct needs assessment to support CwC and sectors.
6. Feedback mechanism to ensure accountability to affected populations from host community.
This project will be focus on achieving gender equality in terms of having information. Moreover this project will incorporate disaster risk reduction issues as a key focus.

Needs

In 2018, efforts by the Communication with Communities (CWC) Working Group members have seen a rise in access to life-saving information. However, significant gaps remain in the dissemination, use of these materials across sectors/members as well as community engagement, particularly for vulnerable people. There is significantly a growing need for CwC intervention in host community. According to the Mid Term Review of JRP 2018, There continue to be critical gaps in how partners can communicate with refugees and the host community. It is also mentioned in the document that Communication between Host communities and Rohingya refugees is doubly important as host community needs and concerns continue to grow, and social cohesion remains limited between the refugee and host populations in certain areas.In host communities, ensuring equality of access to information throughout the response is one the major identified gaps. Marginalized people and those with limited mobility and lliteracy are heavily reliant on others for needed information and as such are at protection risk of denial, exploitation and other forms of abuse. A consistent, meaningful, acceptable and transparent feedback and remedial mechanisms to demonstrate accountability to affected population and service users (refugees and host community) is not yet concretely in place in all Sector and in several camps. Finally, there continues to be a need for minimum standards for data collection and data sharing mechanism across all sectors and partners to ensure a more streamlined approach to providing services and collecting feedback from affected populations (refugee and host). As per the information needs assessment conducted by Internews, alike the Rohingya community, almost 80% of the population of host community does not feel like they have enough information to make good decisions for themselves and their families. Almost 60% people from host community prefers to receive information from face-to face communication like, from friends and families, community and religious leaders etc.Considering the context BRAC C4D designed the project with CwC activities for host communities with the aim to meeting the need for more accessible, community preferred means of information. Based on the experience of the Interpersonal communication session BRAC C4D has been conducting successfully with the Rohingya communities from the last one year, the project will focus on the same IPC activity for host community visiting door-to-door with a focus to reach most vulnerable groups of people.Based on the need for coordination mentioned in JRP 2018 review, the project incorporated consultation meetings in participation with Rohingya and host communities and advocacy meetings with community and religious leaders. Also, there is a concrete plan for conducting needs assessment surveys in host community as BRAC C4D did for Rohingya community. This will ensure effective coordination with sectors and CwC WG. Besides, a common and more effective way of feedback mechanism of ISCG will be used for collecting and analyzing host community feedback.
