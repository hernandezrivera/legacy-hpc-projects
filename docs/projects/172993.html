Project Summary

This project aims to provide all the populations in need (Vulnerable host communities, IDPs and returnees) in the target high risk states and localities, with basic humanitarian services, through a comprehensive and holistic approach that integrates the sectors of Health, Nutrition, WASH, FSL and Education, to ensure all essential needs are covered and accessible.
The project will target the 12 states and 26 localities of: Abyei PCA, Blue Nile (Altadamon, Baw localities), East Darfur (Assalaya, Alfirdous and Yassin localities), Gedarif (Alfashaga and Alfao), Kassala (Reifi Hamashkoreb and North Detla), Red Sea (Haya and Sinkat), South Darfur (Dimso, Umdafoug, Elsalam and Alradoum), South Kordofan (Alburam, Al Leri and Umdurein), West Kordofan (Alnuhood and Lagawa), River Nile (Shendi), Northern State (Algolid) and White Nile state (Aljabalen and Alsalam).
The Health interventions include the provision of primary health care (PHC), reproductive health (RH) and strengthening of expanded program of immunization (EPI) through support health facilities in the target locations with needed medical supplies and equipment. The interventions will also focus on COVID19 response through provision of needed support to the health facilities (for case management) and the communities (for risk communication and infection prevention and control IPC). The nutrition interventions include provision of CMAM services for all the target populations and treatment of SAM cases, with or without complications for children under 5 and MAM cases for CU5 and PLWs. The project further intends to improve the public health of these targeted communities by providing WASH services for these target communities through promotion of hygiene, and construction/rehabilitation of communal and institutional latrines (in target HFs, OTPs, SCs and schools). In addition to improving the access of the target population to safe drinking water to minimize the risk of contracting infections. The Food Security and Livelihoods interventions will aim to lower the impact of the economic crisis and the COVID19 pandemic on the livelihoods, and consequently the food security, of the most vulnerable target beneficiaries, through provision of suitable vocational trainings, vouchers and cash and general food distributions. The education component in this project intends to contribute to the return to schools after a long period of schools shutting down (the civil unrest in 2018 followed shortly by the COVID19 lockdown) through rehabilitating/constructing and furnishing of schools and classes and restoring them to original status prior to the lockdown. The education interventions also include provision of all needed kits, supplies and uniforms to children in the target primary schools, and provide the teachers at the target schools with needed support.
Through these integrated interventions the vulnerable populations (vulnerable host communities, IDPs and returnees) will be targeted with the services and improve their accessibility to the services. All while ensuring that gender, protection and disability mainstreaming is an integral part of implementation, and working on strengthening the resilience and integrating the IDPs and returnees within the target communities. This project will also take into account the environmental factors and risks that may be influenced by the proposed activities and devise the appropriate mitigation methods to minimize the effects on the environment.

1 - Needs

The insufficiency of health and nutrition services has led the target localities to be ranked high in terms of severity (5 and 4) according to health and nutrition sectors 2021 HNO. According to the health 2021 HNO, EPI coverage is suboptimal; with 21 of the target localities having a percentage of less than 90% in DTC3, and 16 of the localities with coverage of less than 90% for the measles vaccine. This coupled with the recent outbreak of circulating vaccine-derived poliovirus type 2 (cVDPV2) emphasizes the importance of strengthening EPI. The number of functioning health facilities in comparison to the high numbers of populations in these target areas is quite low, a situation that is further made complex by the COVID19 pandemic, which has also affected the performance and the capacities of the already limited health staff in these health facilities. The RH services are also insufficient (low number of HFs providing bEMOC and skilled birth attendants) (HNO 2021).The target localities have a nutrition severity and stunting risks of 4 and 5 (HNO2021). According to the S3M 2019, most of these target localities have a high GAM rate, with some exceeding the emergency threshold (Haya 21.87, Sinkat 23.59). The nutrition situation is also greatly affected by the constrictions imposed as precautionary measures in response to the COVID19 pandemic, and the economic deterioration that significantly affected the food security of many households. The low coverage in immunization this year may also further exacerbate the deterioration in the nutritional health of children under 5. All target localities have a high WASH severity rank of 4 (North Delta, Kassala and Haya, Red Sea having a severity rank of 5). According to the 2021 HNO, out of the 25 target localities in this project almost 18 localities have a percentage of households with access to basic water services of less than 65%, with the remaining 8 localities having a percentage of less than 85% of households accessing the basic water services, as opposed to the standard of more than 85%. In terms of the percentage of households having access to limited sanitation services, 21 out of the 25 target localities have a percentage of less than 50%, and only 3 have a percentage of more than 50%, still far from the standard of more than 80%.As for the access to hand washing facilities with soap and water, 20 of the target localities have a percentage less than 7.5 in the number of HHs accessing these services. This has great impacts on the general health of the populations in these localities, and this is more emphasized after the emergence of the COVID19 pandemic.According to the latest IPC analysis, 17 of the target localities in this project face high levels of food insecurity (IPC phase3), and 8 of localities are fall under phase2. In Eastern states,the recent events of Ethiopian refugee influx adds more burden on the target localities, which are already considered under crisis (IPC phase3). The analysis also notes that the high inflation rates,the COVID19 outbreak, and the latest flooding events suffered by almost all of Sudan’s states has greatly strained the adaptation abilities of majority of the population.All these factors may decrease the availability and access to food and may strain their access to livelihoods.The year 2020 has posed a greater challenge for the education system,with the COVID19 pandemic leading to schools shutting down, affecting the maintenance of the already poor physical structures of the schools, with the great possibility of children dropping out and engaging in child labor to respond to the crushing economic effects. According to the Education cluster’s 2021 HNO, the target localities in this project are of great need in education interventions with a high number of people in need, especially in the localities of Delta North in Kassala (66,073), Shendi in River Nile state (40,685), Elfashaga in Gedarif (21,993), Lagawa in West Kordofan (27,164).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The health activities include rehabilitation of health facilities, as needed, provision of medicines, medical supplies and medical equipment to target HFs, strengthening EPI across the target localities, and provision of RH services. COVID19 response will be also addressed through strengthening of IPC, risk communication case management and testing and strengthening of the surveillance, early warning and reporting systems. Referral systems will be supported and strengthened. In disaster response preparedness rapid response teams will be trained to timely and efficiently respond to any disasters that may occur. The project will place special focus on building the capacities of the health staff through provision of specialized trainings. The nutrition services will be taking a community based form, through provision of treatment for SAM with and without medical complications, MAM children under 5 and PLWs through community outreach activities and rehabilitating and completing the existing nutrition facilities with needed medicines, supplies and equipment In addition to the promotion of IYCF within the target nutrition facilities and in the target communities. Formation of MSGs and supporting them with needed trainings in agriculture and farming and including them in FSL activities will be emphasized on to ensure a more sustainable approach to addressing malnutrition. For the WASH interventions, the water supply proposed activities include rehabilitating and/or drilling and installing of new hand pumps and water yards (including solarisation), rehabilitation and/or construction of new protected wells and hafirs, operating and maintaining water sources (includes water quality treatment and monitoring).Construction of gender segregated communal latrines, institutional latrines (in the target HFs, nutrition centers and schools). Rehabilitation of emergency HHs latrines and emergency communal latrines. In addition to operation and maintenance of existing latrines. The hygiene promotion activities will include the provision of soap and jerry cans, construction of hand washing facilities in communal set ups in schools, HFs and nutrition facilities. Provision of personal hygiene materials (reusable sanitary pads) in target schools for girls in 7th and 8th grade. Conduct hygiene messaging in HHs, OTPs, SCs and HFs, and hygiene campaigns in communal setups (the target schools and HFs). Conduct vector control activities such as spraying of insecticides and drainage of stagnant water. Disposal and management of solid waste (communal waste pits and medical waste). Distribution of water quality supplies (Aqua tabs/pur) at point of use, in addition to water treatment at source in flood prone and risky epidemic hotspots.In the target localities, the Education activities will include provision of secondary school with kits, teacher kits, uniforms and hygiene kits. Improvement of the learning conditions through construction/rehabilitation and furnishing of secondary school classrooms.Build the capacities of the teachers through providing trainings in EiE, and provision of teachers’ incentives. Formation and training of PTAs on child safeguarding and PSEA. The activities will also include provision of children ALP/catch-ups. The FSL activities will include general food distribution at full ration for the identified and selected target beneficiaries on the basis of vulnerability, provision of vouchers and cash under MPCA at full ration. The activities will also include vocational trainings on several topics such as (production of reusable sanitary pads and soap) to address the sanitation and hygiene gaps in the target localities. Distribution of CAHWs and kits.Support of agriculture and livestock will also be included, with aiming to include the mothers or caretakers of malnourished children in the targeted groups.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

To ensure the effective integration of environment preservation approaches in the proposed activities, the following approaches will be considered: More sustainable and environment friendly project designs and materials for the construction and rehabilitation activities. Use of solar power in the target facilities for lighting and for water pumping, and minimize the use of generators. Proper management and disposal of hazardous waste arising from health and nutrition activities (medical waste) and WASH activities (sludges from water treatment plants and expired chemicals) . management and disposal of household waste through composting or biogas production. GAH will also ensure the proper use of vector control chemicals in WASH interventions and proper disposal of them. GAH will further advocate for environmental preservation through mainstreaming of environmental concerns in all capacity building trainings in the different sectors with clear and simple messages of key environmental considerations. The messages will be conveyed in training of community health promoters CHPs, community nutrition volunteers CNVs and community health volunteers CHVs. Parent/teachers associations (PTAs) trainings will also include messages on importance of environmental preservation and encourage the dissemination of the messages between the school children. Vocational trainings will also address the topic of environmental preservation and the proper methods to minimize the environmental contamination when producing of their goods.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GAH is a protection mandated organization, thus protection mainstreaming is always paid extra attention during project design, and also following the “Do No Harm” principle and maintaining the dignity of all targeted beneficiaries. For mainstreaming protection in this project, GAH will ensure that during the construction/rehabilitation of schools, health facilities and nutrition facilities, that gender segregated and persons with special needs (PSN) friendly WASH facilities are constructed, GAH will also make sure that the design of the latrines includes locks for additional safety measures. The rehabilitation of schools and all health and nutrition facilities will also take into account the ease of access to PSN such as construction of wheelchair ramps. GAH will also incorporate topics of child safeguarding and PSEA in the trainings of parent/teachers associations PTAs, community health promoters CHPs, community nutrition volunteers CNVs and community health volunteers CHVs, and raise awareness of school children on sexual exploitation and abuse, and how/to whom can these violations be reported. Incorporation and linkage of the target facilities (schools, HFs and nutrition centers) and/or formulated groups and structures with protection services providers will be prioritized in this project, to enable survivors of violence and vulnerable age groups to receive the needed legal aid, psychosocial and/or medical care they need.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

GAH will ensure accountability to affected population (AAP) is fully in place from the inception phase of project by conducting focal group discussions with members and leaders in the target communities, to address their major highlighted needs in a context-suitable approach while ensuring equitable representation of women and girls in the assessment, design and monitoring of all project activities. Prior to implementation, GAH will address the target communities and explain the nature and expected results of the interventions, by different means such as meetings with community leaders, active community members and/or advertisement of the interventions through various methods (campaigns, radio broadcasting). GAH will clearly convey the message of what is to be expected from the interventions and the staff of GAH, and ensure that the target communities have a safe channel to express any violations (sexual exploitation and abuse) without any fear of repercussions. GAH will achieve that by collecting feedback through face to face meetings with individuals, group consultations and community leaders through aid workers. In addition to providing complaints and feedback boxes in the target institutions and explaining to the target communities importance of providing their feedback on quality of provided services and/or any abuse complaints, and how to use the boxes. GAH will regularly check the boxes to ensure the safe running of the project and interventions. GAH will conduct trainings on PSEA and child safeguarding to contracted vendors and all appointed staff in the target locations, and ensure their abiding to the code of conduct. If any complaints of SEA and abuse are reported through the various aforementioned methods, GAH will immediately take appropriate measures to hold the person reported accountable, and ensure that the act never to be repeated. GAH will provide a full report to the interagency on any violation cases, should they occur and the mitigating steps undertaken, in addition to AAP measures.
