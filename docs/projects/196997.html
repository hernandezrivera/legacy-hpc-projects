Project Summary

Blumont will work to improve communities’ food security through supplementary food assistance to vulnerable host communities and bread distribution to vulnerable people living in camps and their surrounding host communities in Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, and Deir ez-Zor governorates in NES. Blumont will continue to implement food security inventions supporting over 215,000Syrians living in camps and in host communities, including providing daily bread to all camp and informal settlement populations, supplementary food vouchers to provide fresh foods to WFP standard food basket recipients, and early recovery activities including agricultural support for smallholder farmers and households.

3 - Needs

In December 2021, the nationwide average price of the World Food Program (WFP) food basket reached Syrian Pound (SYP) 220,112 ($88), representing the highest recorded since 2013. The highest monthly increase was reported in Al-Hasakeh Governorate, where the price reached SYP 198,044 ($79). According to WFP, almost half of all households (HHs) reported inadequate food consumption in December, a 10 percent increase from last year. As the SYP continues to depreciate, rising food costs are straining HH purchasing power. This challenge forces families to adopt negative coping strategies, such as buying less-expensive food, eating fewer meals per day, prioritizing child food consumption needs, and engaging in child labor and early marriage while restricting adult food consumption. As of February 28, 2022, in NES, 135,507 individuals reside in five planned and seven informal camps, 25,328 individuals live in 143 collective centers (CCs), and 126,306 individuals in 166 informal settlements. This represents a 7% population increase from last year. They are among the most affected by the deteriorating situation, as they rely exclusively on humanitarian assistance for all their needs. Humanitarian Needs Overview. Syrian Arab Republic. 2022.WFP VAM Food Security Analysis. Market Price Watch Bulletin. December 2021.

4 - Implementation of activities

1) Agricultural Support: support 300 Smallholder farmers with agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, harvesting bags) and pre and post-harvesting training. 2) Homestead Vegetable Gardens: Provision of agricultural training and HVG kits that include locally procured, in-kind inputs (seeds and toolkit), and a drip irrigation system for 1,000 HHs. 3) Bakery Support: 18,000 MT of Local and/or regionally procured wheat flour and 63 MT of yeast to local bakeries. 4) Bread Distribution: Bakeries will produce bread for sale at subsidized DOC prices to an estimated 200,000 local host community population at pre-identified distribution points. Blumont will buy bread from the bakeries and distribute it free of charge to approximately 138,000 people in four formal camps (Al-Hol, Mahmoudly, Areesha and Newroz), five informal camps(Washokani, Al-Tuwayhinah, Tel al-Samen, Abu Khashab and Serkaniye), and 68 CCs in Al-Hasakah and Ar-Raqqa. 5) Supplementary Food Vouchers: To supplement standard WFPfood baskets, supplementary food vouchers will be provided to 3,000 food insecure HHs for at least eight months to purchase fresh foods

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Blumont will continue to work with local communities, LAs, and INGOs in target locations to mitigate protection risks to beneficiaries and any unintended consequences of program activities. Blumont’s field staff will consult with LAs and community leaders (including sharing beneficiary criteria) for assistance incommunicating program activities, beneficiary selection criteria, and conflict resolution. Blumont’s M&E team will also help ensure distributions do not create inter-community conflict and do not take place in areas of armed conflict. For bread distributions, Blumont will mitigate intra-community conflicts by making bread accessible to all participants. Supplemental food vouchers will also target food-insecure host community members. Vulnerable female, child, PwD, and elderly-headedHHs will receive selection priority, and bread distribution points will be located near underserved and hard-to-reach areas to ensure equitable coverage and reduce protection risks for vulnerable HHs. Camp distributions will be organized to ensure that lines for women, children, PwDs, and elderly-headed HHs are separate. The agricultural activities involve the provision of in-kind inputs specifically to reduce protection risks or mobility challenges for such HHs and to promote livelihoods for women and other vulnerable groups. Training for HVG and smallholder farmers will be gender-segregated as needed, and typically Blumont’s female staff will lead or support assessments for FHHs.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Blumont understands and accepts the responsibility to help ensure SEA does not take place through the proposed project. Blumont has a dedicated PSEA strategy that covers the following elements: recruitment policy; induction and training; performance management; whistleblowing; discipline and grievance/complaints; partnership agreements; policy implementation, monitoring, and review; and promotes an organizational culture concerned about these issues coupled with top-down leadership from its Board of Directors and Executive Management Team. Blumont also sensitizes vendors on its Code of Conduct and its intolerance for gender discrimination and sexual harassment or abuse.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Blumont has identified the following risks, assumptions and mitigating measures: 1)COVID-19: The pandemic continues into its third year and is still a threat to all staff and program beneficiaries, especially those still unvaccinated. Blumont will continue to monitor developments in relation to COVID-19 through advice being published by WHO and the U.S. Government. Blumont staff wear gloves and masks, use hand sanitizer frequently and practice physical distancing by maintaining two-meter distance between themselves and camp residents. 2) Civil unrest: Blumontcontinuously monitors for tensions within the camps to address with relevant stakeholders, monitors national news, media and social media, and maintains a low profile and avoids areas of demonstration or protest. 3) Physical Security & Crime: Program staff or objectives may be impacted through physical violence and theft of project materials. Blumont’s security team continuously monitors the on-the-ground context and Blumont Global Safety & Security Policy and Security SOPs are in place.
