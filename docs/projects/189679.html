Project Summary

Lulu Care proposes to continue supporting vulnerable people across South Sudan with essential life-saving assistance, whilst creating conducive environments for recovery and sustainable solutions to vulnerabilities. Lulu Care plans an integrated GBV strategy for 2022 focusing both but more on emergency GBV response including components of transition and recovery GBV programming. Lulu Care implementation strategies will be contributing to Protection Cluster Objective 1,2 and 3. We propose to strengthen GBV case management while establishing and updating 4 referral pathways in Tonj South and Bor counties. 3,300 women and girls will be targeted with survivor centered psychosocial support (PSS) focused on healing, empowerment and recovery through specialized counselling, group counselling and inter-generational gatherings/ activities that will include bead work, soap production, sandal making, embroidery lessons, here the adult women(big sisters) will mentor girls in crosscutting issues regarding health, domestic chores and early child marriages this activity will take into account the key precautions and observations of the Covid-19 measures. Lulu Care is targeting a total 3,300 of women and girls in provision of appropriate GBV case management services including persons living with disabilities. To ensure that women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns are supported Lulu care will engage other partners in establishing and strengthening the referral pathways in Tonj South, Juba and Bor respectively, this will be supportive in implementing the Lulu Care GBV toll free helpline to support remote communities in delivery of available GBV services through partners. The recovery and transition centered activities will basically focus on provision of support programming through establishment of 2 Women and Girls Friendly spaces(WGFS) in Bor and Tonj South (Manyang Ngok), strengthening 6 community-based protection monitoring committees with 10 members each totalling 60 persons (65% women and girls) in each location including PWDs, provide protection awareness campaigns and GBV prevention messaging targeting 10,000 men, women, boys and girls. To support the women on livelihood Lulu Care will provide vegetable seed kits to 1350 households as a protective socioeconomic support to vulnerable women and girls. It is believed that the women will have portion to sell and even supplement of the household diets to cut down malnutrition cases, The targeted women for protective socio-economic support are 1,200 women and the girls will be only those above the age of 18. Lulu Care will facilitate trainer of trainees (ToT) trainings on moulding and use of fuel eﬃcient cooking stoves, briquettes and charcoal lighters targeting 244 women and girls. Lulu Care believes that to achieve a mile in prevention and mitigation of GBV men and boys should be involved, therefor using the EMAP approach 3420 men, boys and youths will be mentored, including adolescent girls and young women on Comprehensive Sexuality Education (CSE) programming (mentoring youth as agents of change on risks mitigation against GBV). The need for sustainable peace in all communities is paramount therefore Lulu Care will engage all 800 community leaders and other community gatekeepers in peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence activities to strengthen inter- and inter-clan/community peaceful coexistence to ensure durable humanitarian access and programming. Lulu Care will provide information to humanitarian actors implementing FSL/WASH/Education/Nutrition and Health projects about the GBV toll free helpline (662), PSEA and conduct advocacy sessions for an increased participation and protection of women and girls in their activities. Lulu Care will take account of and include persons with disabilities in all project activities, by applying an age, gender and diversity lens and considering their specific needs, while prioritizing safety, dignity, and avoid doing harm.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022, prolonged conflict in South Sudan has contributed to an extended humanitarian crisis, which has been aggravated by unusually high levels of flooding and the COVID-19 pandemic. Although conflict has reduced between parties to the Revitalized-Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (RARCSS), violence has continued in many pockets of the country. GBV is a prevailing issue in South Sudan. In armed conflict, both women and men have been subjected to sexual violence, rape, murder and torture. According to UNFPA between Jan1 – Sept, 30, 2021, a total of 6,295 reported incidents of Gender-based Violence were recorded with statistics showing 97% of the reported GBV incident survivors being female while 3% were male. In South Sudan Conflict has driven changes in some of the traditional gender roles that people play. For example, where men are no longer present, women may be required to support and care for the family and their assets. Women tend to experience greater food insecurity given their cultural and social roles as caregivers of children and older persons especially in rural areas. According to the 2021 FSNMS+ analysis, cultural barriers reportedly include male control over decisions around income generating activities and over income earned. Perceptions that females without male support (such as those heading households and widows) should not engage in certain service sector activities has also been reported.The UNICEF-(MRM) points out that the risk of child marriage remains constant due to conflict and the country’s economic situation and harmful social norms . The quadrant of insecurities, loss of livelihood, changes in gender roles and lack of quality GBV services have culminated into increased incidences of GBV. Sexual violence remains high. A recent IOM study commissioned highlighted that 45.8 per cent of all household’s reports having experienced an incident of GBV directed against females in 2021. Lulu Care GBV helpline 2021 report(January-December) indicates that 42% of the received calls are domestic violence cases, fuelled by poverty and the outbreak of the Covid-19 and its related consequences, especially with the ongoing lock down. 34% of such women have sustained injuries due to the violence. Furthermore, women and girls continue to face extreme levels of GBV much of which goes under reported especially in remote locations, with limited availability of lifesaving GBV response services to survivors there has been loss of lives for instance in Bor South rape/defilement cases through child marriage are at an increase. The COVID-19 pandemic continues to pose enormous risks to children, women, girls including mental health and psychosocial distress, intimate partner violence, physical and emotional maltreatment, and risk of exploitation including PSEA. Majority of survivors of GBV are often not aware of available services and they are afraid of seeking help. In Tonj South the inter-communal fights have displaced communities, majority of whom are women, children and the elderly, including persons with specific needs. According to the FGDs conducted by Lulu Care in Bor, Tonj South and counties, there has been continued vulnerability of women to rape and harassment during the collecting of firewood in afar bushes. All women that responded stated that they do not report attacks and incidents inflicted. With dire need for supporting survivors there is need to strengthen remote responses to effect the remote GBV service delivery predominately emotional support and case management) over a technology platform (i.e. hotline) rather than in person to expand the geographic reach of services. PWDs continue to face multiple challenges including accessibility and attitudinal barriers that hinder their participation in social, economic and political life in addition to less access to education, poorer health and lower participation in the formal labour market.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Lulu Care listens and is responsive to beneficiaries feedback on the project activities. To ensure that accountability mechanisms are accessible, meaningful and effective the organization will be context-specific, and based on consultation with users, ensure inclusivity and accessibility to information, empower beneficiaries, consistently closing the loop with a commitment to transparency and accountability, enable collective responsibility for giving voice to those, be impartial, do no harm and be appropriately resourced. All in all the feedback will be used to shape the mishaps and challenges that may affect the implementation of the project

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Lulu Care will use numerous tools for beneficiaries to provide feedback, they will be made available from the project planning, implementation and evaluation phases, we shall ensure that they are in local languages and community mobilizers will be provided with skills to support the vulnerable persons including people with disabilities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Lulu Care has a developed PSEA policy and has inducted the staff who adhere to the values and principles outlined in the Code of Conduct. In accordance with these values, the organization operates and enforces policies in Beneficiary Protection from Exploitation and Abuse. The organization will conduct trainings and awareness with relevant leaders, develop information education and communication materials with key messages on PSEA in addition to awareness campaigns to the communities

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Lulu Care will institutionalize feedback mechanism to allow benefciaries , girls and boys, women and men including persons with disabilities to voice their successes, as well as complaints and concerns to stakeholders on quality and impact of the project as a means to measure accountability to affected population. Our feedback models on activities in the field includes beneciary scorecards about the services, complaint collection boxes; displaying activities and budget of this action in visible places, periodic joint review sessions of beneciaries and the local authorities further more Lulu Care utilizes the Joint stakeholder field monitoring visits, surveys, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), focus group discussions, one-to-one interviews, community meetings, notice boards, community monitoring of service delivery and participatory budgeting. Lulu Care operates a toll free helpline that is used to collect information and feedback from the beneficiaries.
