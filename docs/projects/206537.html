Project Summary

The project will strengthen protection systems, inclusive child protection, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and general protection through technical capacity building of communities, partners and the government to prevent and respond to violence against girls, boys and their families, empowering children with improved access to quality services, coordination, and advocacy on child rights monitoring mechanism and meaningful children's participation.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Six years into the conflict in northern Mozambique, more than eight hundred and fifty thousand people (850,599 people), of whom 50% are children, remain displaced across the northern provinces (IOM DTM 19). The conflict is a protection crisis. Girls and boys are increasingly exposed to child rights violations, gender-based violence and other protection risks found in situations of children in armed conflict. Additionally, women remain largely excluded from access to income and decision-making and face limited availability of services. While the rate of IDPs has decreased, a simultaneous increase in the number of returnees has been reported. As of 11th September 2023, 571,468 people had moved from Mueda, Montepuez, Metuge, and Pemba to Mocímboa da Praia and Palma (IOM DTM 19). Reasons for the return include the perception of an improved security situation and scale-up of development initiatives, insufficient or inadequate livelihood conditions in areas of settlement, as well as insufficient humanitarian assistance. However, upon return, many have found their homes destroyed and their belongings stolen or burned. And an end to the conflict remains currently out of sight. Additionally, Mozambique is one of the most climate-vulnerable countries and the impact of wide-ranging climate-induced disasters so far in 2023 has been far greater than most predictions. As part of the humanitarian response, Save the Children will coordinate with other humanitarian actors and the government to enhance the access of children to safe, quality, gender-responsive, and inclusive child protection services. Save the Children will provide case management and referrals for the most vulnerable girls and boys affected by ongoing violence and displacement in Cabo Delgado and Nampula, including family tracing and reunification (FTR) and protective interim/ foster care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). Save the Children will provide structured mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services to UASC, their families and communities through its signature technical approaches like healing and education through the arts (HEART), Team Up and child and youth resilience programme (CYRP) in safe child friendly spaces (CFS), mobile and home-based psychosocial support. Save the Children will also promote rolling out of its Safe Families Common Approach to enhance the capacity of the parents and caregivers to positive parenting in their daily lives to ensure a joyful and protective environment for children in their homes and communities. Through caring for child survivors of sexual abuse approach, and aligned to IASC standards for GBV case management, Save the Children will ensure survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) access to quality care. Save the Children will also strengthen community-based child protection mechanisms in cooperation with local structures and members of communities in collaboration with the Department of Social Welfare. Save the Children will put emphasis on advocacy to reduce violence against girls and boys including increasing trends of child marriage caused by conflicts in Cabo Delgado, including child rights monitoring on grave violations against children in targeted locations. As part of strategy, we will also build the capacity of civil society organizations (CSOs) and strengthen the interventions to address child early and forced marriage and unions (CEFMU) and empower adolescent girls and boys.The project aims to reach 62,050 people, IDPs, returnees and host communities (30,000 girls, 30,000 boys, 1,550 women and 500 men) with integrated protection and survival services for child survivors and children at risk with a focus on UASC, children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) and children with disabilities from IDPs, returnees and host communities in Cabo Delgado.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Save the Children will implement priority protection activities across districts in Cabo Delgado:Priority activities will include: -Provide child protection services including inclusive case management support, focusing on family tracing and reunification, and alternative care for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). -Provide child-centered services to child survivors of GBV; -Provide child-centered reintegration services to CAAFAG with their families and communities. -Conduct inclusive positive parenting sessions for parents/caregivers to ensure children are supported and empowered at home, communities and school, as per SC's Common Approach, Safe Families. -Mobilize communities to prevent violence, social and harmful practices, and discrimination, as well as work with CSOs and duty-bearers (social workers, health workers, police, and others) to strengthen their technical capacity on child-friendly procedures, gender-sensitive, climate change sensitive and inclusive child protection services. -Strategically collaborate with key child protection actors (including the government and UNICEF) in rolling out and using CPIMS+. -Train staff/partners/volunteers to implement structured MHPSS programmes in child-friendly spaces to build resilience, promote psychosocial well-being, and holistic development of children and adolescents. -Conduct advocacy campaigns on SGBV, child, early and forced marriage and unions (CEFMU), violence against children (VAC) etc. targeting the policy makers and duty bearers in the humanitarian context. -Conduct MHPSS activities through healing and education through Arts (HEART), child and youth resilience programme (CYRP), Team Up, I support my friend etc. technical approaches. -Advocacy for the strengthening of the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) on the six grave child rights violations in armed conflict. -Generate evidence on the impact of the Child Protection Technical Approaches e.g., Team Up, HEART, Safe Families, Steps 2 Protect (S2P) etc. and share with Cluster, AoR, TWG, Network etc. -Enhance the capacities of children in designing, implementing and monitoring programme interventions.-Provide support to children for civil documentation (birth registration). The activities will be implemented in Cabo Delgado province and in Macomia, Mocimboa Da Praia, Nangade, Palma and Quissanga districts.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will ensure that the most vulnerable boys and girls, women and men will be identified and reached. Guided by Save the Children’s Gender Equality Policy and leveraging the Global IASC standards for gender equality, the project will conduct gender assessments in the communities using the gender equality marker. Gender and safety audits will enable the identification of deep-rooted gender inequalities including harmful traditional practices to girls, boys, women and men. By using participatory community appraisals and risk mapping exercises, Save the Children will empower the community to recognize and do away with harmful traditional practices like child marriage, sex preference among others, that are a barrier to gender equality.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will work with relevant agencies including the UN, Protection Cluster, CPAoR, PSEA network and Coordination Office, and the government to build capacity of girls, women and the elderly through leadership and equality trainings, mentorship and empower programme. Additionally, Save the Children will also work with boys and men champions to create awareness on gender equality and its positive impact on families and the community. The project will develop and utilize standardized protection and PSEA tools, e.g. code of conduct, SOPs, checklists, and case referral pathways including information, education and communication materials (IEC) and behavioral change and communication (BCC) materials that will enable all sectors and programmes to identify and mitigate risks to women, children and the elderly in the project locations. Specifically, it will allow staff, partners and community-based protection structure to safeguard women and children from the risk of GBV, including the risk of SEA and other forms of abuse and harm, such as physical abuse, neglect and emotional harm. The protection tools will support safe programming and a safe environment for women and children, including those who have disabilities to speak out.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will ensure access to services is guided and based on a vulnerability assessment that is inclusive, and gender-sensitive considering age and ability. Assessments, registration and inclusion criteria will be guided by the Washington Group Questions ensuring persons with disability are consulted and included in the project. Save the Children will collaborate with DPOs including FAMOD, AIFO and UN family for strategic planning, advocacy and implementation of activities. Save the Children will work with DPOs to ensure the capacity strengthening of partners, communities and government on disability and inclusion standards, tools and approaches and general child protection. A mentorship and coaching approach will be employed for quality assurance, reaching higher numbers of children with disabilities (at least 5% of the total target) improving accessibility and responding to needs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Save the Children prioritizes the Centrality of Protection, and the proposed intervention considers context specific vulnerabilities, including those based on gender dynamics, and a need for conflict sensitivity approaches throughout the entire project lifecycle. To ensure environmental considerations, Save the Children will conduct conflict-sensitive and security assessments, with the aim to understand the context in which we operate, understand the interaction between our interventions and the context, and act upon this understanding to minimize negative impacts and maximize positive impacts of our organization on conflict factors. Save the Children will ensure risk mitigations and management at all levels from senior management to field level, ensuring operations and interventions are safe and adhere to both national and international environmental management standards and practices.
