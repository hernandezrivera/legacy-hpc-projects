Project Summary

The project’s main goal is to scale up safe and equitable access to education for crisis-affected children and youth, through the implementation of multiple activities that improve both the soft and hard components of the educational system in conﬂict affected areas, including rehabilitation of schools, provision of materials and supplies, conducting Non-formal education activities throw remedial classes and enhancing the capacity of teachers and education personnel.According to severity scale and coordination meetings with the local authorities total of 294,040 targeted beneficiaries in 30 targeted schools, will receive education assistance as following, including direct and indirect beneficiaries:
Total of students 288,790 (‬141,507‬ boys and 147,283 girls)
Total of teaching personnel 5250 (2678 female and 2573 males)
The original scope of this project was reduced due to the overall limitations of the Education Sector’s budget ceiling. The original budget was $ 3,562,739 with a target of 294,040 people

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has forced2.1 million children aged5-17 to be out of school,1.3million children are at risk of dropping out.Over 227,743teachers and 6.8million children are in need of education assistance inside Syria,61 per cent of them are in acute needThis situation has negatively affected the quality of education for all children in different areas across the country facing challenges in accessing education. Safety and security issues and limited access for humanitarian partners has prevented them from providing educational support to the people in need with extremely limited access to quality educational services. Public services are unable to support the children and adolescents in urgent need of education, school infrastructure is severely damaged, the number of returnee teachers and educational staff lack experience in teaching the affected students. Considering, the rehabilitation and improving of schooling services in conﬂict-affected areas in Syria is essential to the country’s recovery. However, Governorates in like Deir-ez-Zor,Daraa,Aleppo,Hama and Rural Damascus are facing multiple challenges, in particular in Education resulted in the extreme decrease in the number of adequate schools where functioning educational facilities do exist, the most commonly mentioned barriers to accessing education are poor conditions of buildings, early marriage and children labor.25% of children and adolescents aren’t attending classes due to the poor condition of educational facilities. The need for children to work and early marriage were the top barriers facing out-of-school children.The displacement of educational staff has led to severe lack of qualiﬁed educational personnel capable of teaching. The project will support the cooperation between humanitarian actors, donors and local authorities to support affected children guarantee their basic right of access to education and reduce the risk of children’s exposure to gender- based violence and sexual exploitatio

5 - Implementation of activities

RSRP's activities are in line with the education sector’s response strategy according to needs-based approach, identification as of 2020 HNO, down to sub district level and the following strategic objectives which have been set in coordination with local authorities , the communities and education cluster. RSRP will be focusing on: 1- Conducting a back to learning campaign for 180,000 students to raise awareness in communities about the necessity of education via dissemination of SMS, conducting puppet/street theatre, photo exhibition, C4D activities and door visit. 2- Improving education for 4800 children through remedial courses by specialized teachers that prevent them from risk of drop out. 3- Capacity building for 4800 teachers through trainings and orientation sessions (Basic PSS-social support Active learning, Life skills) 4- Improving the capacity of local staff involved in educational projects through trainings and orientation sessions. 5- Distributing educational supplies and materials such as (recreational kits and other kits that could improve awareness for 35,000 children and adolescents). 6- Minor and hard rehabilitation of 15 damaged schools to increase the access to schools where they will be provided by school furniture, gender sensitive and disability sensitive wash facilities in addition to school safety and security equipments. 7- provide 3500 of children with school transportation support through distributing fees to attend their ﬁnal exams mainly for the children of 12th and 9th grades who are living in hard to reach areas. 8- TVET program will be included in our education plan for 2020 by enrolling 5000 of youth and adolescents from 10- 24 years in formal and non-formal TVET. All that is above will be translated in assessments, surveys, M&E reports and photos with results shared with donors, partners and relevant clusters. As all activities are established and designed to involve the community through focus group discussions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Protection Risk: Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection (damaged infrastructure). Mitigation Measures: Learning facilities should be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected. This may include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electric, water, etc. School grounds should have fencing. All works should be supervised and monitored by technical personnel. RSRP should ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work start. 2- Protection Risk: Insufficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WASH facilities or a mixing of sexes and ages. Mitigation Measures: Sufficient water and sanitation facilities should be provided and maintained. WASH facilities should be sex and age segregated and accessible to people with disabilities. Soap and water should be available and facilities kept clean. Students should be trained on good health and hygiene practices and if needed be provided the items needed to practice what they learned. 3-Protection Risk: RSRP staff, volunteers or associated personnel are not able to make informed decisions due to security situation. Mitigation Measures: RSRP has clear Duty of Care policies that clarify the organization’s responsibilities and obligations to its staff, volunteers and associated personnel such as facilitators, teachers and other education personnel. 4-Protection Risk: Children in the targeted areas are over exposed to drop-out of school due to lack of learning facilities that lead to negative coping mechanisms, including child labor and child marriage. Mitigation Measures: Providing rehabilitation process of schools and maintenance of school furniture to reduce dropping-out students.

Likelihood: High Mitigating Measures: - Constant overview of situation reports and latest updates related to Syrian crisis, which leads to experience and knowledge in locating other areas of implementation if access to implement in the mentioned areas isn’t feasible. Risk 3: Delayed, denied or revoked GOS approvals, which prevent delivering the assistance in a timely manner or prevent implementation of the project. Likelihood: High Mitigating Measures: - Transparency with local authorities in the areas of intervention, in addition to strong communication and collaboration prior to project implementation with local partner. Risk 4 Major Fluctuation in prices. Likelihood: Medium Mitigating Measures: An experienced ﬁnancial department able to stay updated on the economic situation in the area, make the correct ﬁnancial assumptions and generate quality ﬁnancial reports for donor.
