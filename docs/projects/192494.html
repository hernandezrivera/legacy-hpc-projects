Project Summary

Since 2019, education has been disrupted due to protests against government, the Beirut port explosion, the Covid pandemic, & teachers' strikes as the economic crisis devalued their salaries. Over the last 2 years,an increasing number of obstacles have been preventing children from accessing education.
Many children dropped out of education, with some being forced to work due to the overwhelming financial pressures.Those who remained in schools reported a decline in learning quality.Students mainly attributed this to teachers struggling to effectively teach online, difficulty maneuvering the new technologies and programs used, &unsuitable learning environments at home. With insufficient access to inclusive distance learning tools, students have been deprived of education for almost 2 academic years.
Consequently, children in Lebanon have been displaying poor learning outcomes which carry serious implications for human capital development & economic growth. However,beyond the macro-repercussions for the country’s development, there are significant protection risks for each child drop-out. Safe learning spaces &schools not only support children to achieve academic learning outcomes but also play a critical role in children’s social, emotional &physical development. In 2021-22, public schools opened for only 34 days as of March 31st, including days when schools were “only half operational”.
Despite not requiring tuition fees, access to public education in Lebanon includes financial demands that make education out of reach for many children.These include costs for transportation, stationery, clothes &other indirect costs (Lebanon Education Sector, 2021). Approximately 45% of school drop-outs are students from difficult social &economic backgrounds (Ibid). With extreme poverty increasing from 10% to over 34%,the number of school drop-outs will likely rise(ESCWA, 2021).
In response to the current educational crisis, ISWA is proposing to carry out a contingent project to ensure that children in Lebanon can adequately return to school in the upcoming academic year. The remedial catchup summer program aims at enhancing the educational outcomes of 645 children in Saida (aged 6-14).
The city was chosen as it has huge poverty hotspots, including many refugee gatherings. The city's large population exhausts its limited public services.The program will be launched at the beginning of July 2022 &it'll include an outreach process to identify ®ister kids at risk of dropping out.
ISWA(A.D. No 574)has track record for implementing such programs through its educational subsidiary, Vision Academy. Partnering with numerous organization like UNESCO, Human Appeal, Islamic Relief and Save the Children and others, ISWA has been conducting remedial and PSS programs since 2018. For this project, ISWA will utilize 3 of its centers which contain 31 classrooms.
Through carrying out remedial &psychosocial support sessions (3days/week) along with extracurricular activities (once/week) over the period of two months, children's educational outcomes &wellbeing will be enhanced. Hence, improving their ability to concentrate &motivation to learn. The Summer catchup program will be carried out in 4 educational centers in Saida,where 37qualified teachers&10 PSS facilitators will be responsible to implement the remedial &PSS program. The selection criteria of the students will take into consideration vulnerability according to well-established criteria. Providing remedial classes to vulnerable children will allow them to bridge their learning gaps,decreasing their chances of dropping out. The students will be provided with age-appropriate stationery kits,transportation, &daily snacks, reducing some of the parents’ burdens. As for the PSS, it'll help the children cope with the struggles they have been facing due to their engagement in negative coping strategies. To further lift the children's spirits, fully-covered extracurricular activities will be conducted.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

This project is in line with the 2nd strategic objective of the education sector: “Improving Formal Education of most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children through schools” as the aim of the project is to improve the academic preparedness, enhance their resilience and well-being, and reduce dropout amongst vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinian and Syrian students in Saida. This is done by offering remedial support to 645 students 3times/ week over the course of 2months. Hence, decreasing the probability of them dropping out due to academic underperformance. Moreover, the project addresses key hurdles to students’ access to education, namely the increasing transportation and stationery costs which were reported as the two main reasons for not attending school (VASy2, 2022). As for the provision of snacks, it addresses the grim reality of food insecurity which reflects negatively on students’ academic performance, where each student will receive a daily snack during their attendance of the sessions. Moreover, this project is in line with the 2nd objective under the protection sector “Holistic and integrated Child Protection services are provided to boys and girls at risk and/or survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation.” As the students will be provided with PSS sessions 3 times/week over the course of 2month addressing the increasing rate of violence and aggression among children by teaching them positive coping mechanisms, hence improving their mental health and wellbeing.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ISWA has its own gender, protection, and safeguarding policies that abide by the core humanitarian standards. Moreover, ISWA staff are always trained on these policies and are entitled of spreading awareness among the beneficiaries which in return will encourage them to later talk about it. A complaint/Feeback mechanism is in place where any complaint, especially those with sensitive content will be tackled immediately.Among ISWA’s child protection policies, that we take to address gender, the inclusion of children with disability or critical medical conditions are the following:- Equal opportunities for accessing our services- Zero tolerance for any kind of bullying and discrimination, whether comingfrom staff or from students towards each other.- Creating an enabling environment by trying to eliminate the barriers (thecenters are student-friendly)- Awareness and integration of inclusion principles within the policies andimplemented activities

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ISWA adheres to the 9 commitments of the core humanitarian standards, ensures beneficiaries’ access to information, and allows their involvement in decisions that affect them. To ensure APP, ISWA will-Establish 2 channels for complaint/feedback (one is anonymous). Along with a complaint box, a hotline is established for collecting the caregivers' feedback throughout the project-Organize focus groups with the students to collect their feedback.-Hold staff surveys for educators to elicit their feedback on the program.-Organize teacher-parent conference meetings to inquire regarding their feedback on the project and following their children’s progress.-Outputs of feedback will be taken into consideration to identify areas of improvement- Ensuring beneficiaries access to information by providing them with adequate information regarding the program's outcomes-Monitor attendance so that any issues related to the drop in attendance are addressed in a timely manner
