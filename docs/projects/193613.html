Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to increase the resiliency and coping capacities of vulnerable populations in Area C of WB. For this intervention, MA’AN Development Center will support vulnerable households (including Bedouin and herding families) with dignified living conditions and increased capacity to deal with threats related to their most urgent protection concerns from natural and manmade hazards. Given their exacerbated vulnerability, MA’AN will prioritize targeting HHs that are women headed, elderly, and HHs with PWDs.
The response will achieve this through a tailored and integrated modality of assistance that aligns with HRP 2023 Strategic Objectives and responds to the Shelter Cluster’s Objectivesfor 2023 (Os 1, 2, & 3). The proposed intervention addresses the needs of 6 vulnerable communities living in inadequate shelters and facing challenging environments: 1. In Bethlehem governorate (Arab ar Rashayida al Barriya & Zatara al Kurshan), 2. Jerusalem governorate (Az Za'ayyem Bedouins & Za’atreh Az Za'ayyem Bedouins) 3. Hebron governorate (Deir Sa’eeda & Masafer Bani Naim).
The response will support 242 HHs (1736p: 437 boys, 417 girls, 392 men, 374 women, 116 elderly – including 34 PWDs) from these shelter and protection vulnerable community members in mitigating immediate harm [O1] through:
- Provision of protective DRR materials (first aid kits and fire extinguishers) to mitigate the harm of settler violence
The response also ensures vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities [O2] through:
-Rehabilitation of 196 substandard shelters to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards, including adaptation that meets the needs of PWDs, isolation spaces for gender privacy, and upgrading WASH units (including separating shared toilets and upgrading kitchen facilities). -Provision of winterization/summarization assistance for 46 shelter structures to vulnerable households at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards.
The response also increases target communities’ capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner [O3] through:
- Formation and training of Community-Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs) - Provision of gender segregated training of established CBPSCs as a protection task force in localities. Training will focus on safeguarding, Gender, Protection; the sessions will include topics related to sexual exploitation & abuse, privacy inside shelters, child protection, PWDs minimum living standards, how to safely report complaints – including SEA incidents.
-Capacity building of CBPSCs, local council representatives, and volunteers on emergency response, self-recovery, legal awareness, & protection measures to be able to support IDPs and affected people during and post emergencies - in coordination with legal aid actors.
The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as MA’AN adopts shelter response protection & risk mitigation measures that are guided by protection principles. MA’AN considers safety, dignity, privacy, meaningful access, participation &accountability in all stages of the project. The proposed intervention will ensure that the needs of marginalized groups including (women, children, elderly & PWDs) are accounted for in tailored individual HH level assistance. The proposed intervention aims to complement responses proposed by other aid actors amidst increased threats to steadfastness of Area C populations on their land and their living conditions. The intervention at the community level assistance will be coordinated with all relevant stakeholders to ensure an effective approach including, MoSD, Legal and protection aid actors, and community members. Coordination with other shelter aid actors including ACTED, ACF, and WW-GVC has been conducted to avoid overlap in designed assistance for the target groups.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2023 & MSNA, over 72000 Palestinians of those in need of shelter assistance live in WB including Area C EJ & the H2 area. Between Jan & Sep 2022, 214 residential structures were demolished or confiscated, rendering 722 people half of them are children, homeless & in need of urgent shelter assistance. At least 40476 individuals in Bedouin & herding communities (Area C) are living in extreme & severe conditions in basic shelters as being substandard & in need of urgent rehabilitation/ upgrading. Many communities in WB suffer from frequent settler attacks & need shelter protection measures. Of the 3925 of HHs interviewed for MSNA in the WB, 311 reported being at risk of eviction. The majority of these HHs (268) were in Area C, with 6.5% of HHs in Area C reporting risk of eviction. 3.0% of HHs reported living in inadequate shelters. MA’AN conducted an assessment which included desk research (including a review of the shelter vulnerability matrix) and field visits conducted in Aug-Sep where MA’AN met with local councils, men, women, youth, & PWDs in targeted localities & identified from the collective findings of the assessment 6 communities (all in Area C) that demonstrated med-severe shelter & protection vulnerability, which has been exacerbated by occupation annexations plans, discriminatory practices, settlement expansion & settler violence. All 6 communities are also part of the Community Protection Approach (CPA) initiated by the WB Protection Consortium. Targeted communities: 1.Az Za’ayyem Bedouins: HHs are continuously subjected to harassments & violations by settlers, IF & ICA aiming at forcing them to emigrate. Their shelters are made of unprotected wood, zinc & tent covers. Living conditions are very difficult for all residents especially for women, elderly & children. Women are most vulnerable to the consequences of overcrowding & lack of privacy while children are most vulnerable to the threats of harsh weather & threats of being attacked by snakes, & scorpions. Unprotected shelters & bad conditions below SPHERE standards are the main causes of feeling lack of safety & dignity among community members who emphasized that improving their living conditions by rehabilitation of their shelters will enhance their steadfastness. 2.Zaatreh Az Za’ayyem Bedouins: are located close to E1 scheme (Ma’ale Adumim settlement expansion area). The community is highly exposed to IF harassment. Most of shelters in the community are unprotected & in very bad conditions. The shelters are metallic shed covered by wood & plastic sheets. Overcrowding, lack of safety, & lack of privacy are major concerns expressed by women in the community. In winter the strong winds rupture the plastic covers of their shelters which causes water leakage. The shelters’ size is very small & overcrowded for a proper housing need; hence, the lack of privacy is another protection concern. 3.Arab ar Rashayida al Barriya: HHs are exposed to settler trespassing & intimidation. Community herders are also exposed to physical assault during their movement in the closed military area. Most community shelters are made of brick & non-insulated zinc roof. Community members are suffering from extreme heat in summer & freezing cold in winter, & water leakage through the ceiling. Some shelters are dilapidated tents that need rehabilitation. children are at high risk of being exposed to snake & scorpion bites especially during summer. Women expressed having no privacy due to overcrowding. Some have no WASH facilities & still defecate in open areas. 4.Zatara Al Kurshan: HHs are under threat of forcible displacement since a military training & closed area surrounds the community along the southern & eastern sides & they face frequent visits by the ICA officials to the community (1-5 times/month). All shelters are made of wood/metal sheds. They are weak & not protected against harsh climatic conditions & attacks of wild animals during summer. Families are suffering from the lack of adequate housing space & facilities, which are necessary to cope with the natural population growth among the expanded families. Lack of privacy is also a significant problem that mostly affecting women & elders. 5.Masafer Bani Naim: the proposed project compliments an HF project that MA'AN is implementing now in this location where 10 human shelters were rehabilitated and needs arised for more. HHs in of Masafer Bani N are close to PeneHever settlement, which extends on community lands from the western part. There is a settler outpost & Israeli military area near the community which affects herders’ activities. Many shelters in the community are either residential tents or made of zinc & wood. Most of these shelters need rehabilitation. Residents suffer from freezing temperatures, water leakage in winter & hot weather in summer. 6.Deir Saeeda: the proposed project compliments an HF project that MA'AN is implementing now there (rehabilitation of animal shelters). By being present in this community MA'AN saw the following human shelter needs: HHs are very close to the separation wall & the green line & fall within the surveyed state land, not far from Tene & Eshkolot settlements which makes them under the risk of forcible displacement for the purpose of settlement expansion. Shelters are made of cement bricks & wood/zinc surface, they are in bad conditions especially during winter & need rehabilitation to ensure minimum protection conditions for family members. Lack of space, lack of privacy & crowded spaces are major problems that the HHs suffer from. Women reported that family members (males & females) sleep in the same room. In general, the problem of most communities targeted is reflected in that shelters lack space and privacy, have poor structure materials, and are affected by extreme weather conditions in summer & winter. Shelters targeted are in a poor condition, not adequate or healthy for families (especially women, PWDs & children).

2 - Activities

MA’AN will continue to coordinate with relevant authorities at all phases of this project and will lead a coordination meeting with Shelter response relevant stakeholders, MoSD, its directorates, & the protection cluster to map out implementation plan in target locations & explore special regulatory considerations for program implementation in the 6 target communities. Further, Community Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs) will be formed in in each location, a known MA’AN approach when intervening in communities for increased community ownership, protection mainstreaming & accountability. CBPSCs help inform the needs of their communities & the different marginalized groups. The CBPSCs will serve an integral role in increasing accountability, information dissemination & transparency & report protection concerns throughout the response & will be comprised of representatives from (local council/municipality, schools, clinics, & community members including Women & PWDs). The project capitalizes on MA’AN’s extensive technical experience in providing shelter assistance and will also seek coordinated support from partner organizations for legal follow-up & specific protection concerns, taking into account socio-economic & protection vulnerabilities through responsive, remedial & preventive support. Through monitoring in the field, any arising case of HH in need of legal aid support will be referred to WBPC partners & sub-implementers (NRC, JLAC, St. Yves, etc.). Protection cases (ie GBV, child protection concerns) will be safely referred to relevant protection actors through the Protection cluster & existing referral pathways. Risk assessments will be conducted with communities in order to mitigate risks associated with provision of humanitarian assistance in Area C, including legal advice to partners & beneficiaries to ensure safe intervention. MA’AN will coordinate with legal stakeholders to conduct legal awareness sessions in target communities to inform beneficiaries of risks, legal avenues & resorts before, during & after the intervention. The proposed response includes the following activities:1.1Provision of protective shelter materials to 242 HHs to mitigate the harm of settler violence in the 6 identified communities. There has been a noticeable expansion of settlements in the surrounding areas, HHs ability to graze their lands has been hindered in fear of settler violence. The shelter material will be accompanied with DRR assets including first aid kit & fire extinguishers to better cope these HHs in dealing with violent incidents. 2.1Rehabilitation of 196 substandard shelters to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards, including adaptation that meets the needs of PWDs, isolation spaces for gender privacy, and upgrading WASH units (including separating shared toilets and upgrading kitchen facilities). Technical individual shelter assessments will be conducted with males, females & PWDs in HHs to ensure their shelter & protection needs are expressed & met. 2.2Provision of winterization/summarization assistance for 46 shelter structures to vulnerable households at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards. Technical individual shelter assessments will be conducted with males, females & PWDs in HHs to ensure their shelter & protection needs are expressed & met. 3.1Formation and training of Community-Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs): Orientation and selection meetings. Provision of gender segregated training of established CBPSCs as a protection task force in localities. Training will focus on safeguarding, Gender, Protection; the training sessions will include topics related to sexual exploitation and abuse, privacy inside shelters, child protection, PWDs minimum living standards, besides, they will learn how to be able to safely report complaints – including SEA incidents. 3.2Capacity building of CBPSCs, local council representatives, and volunteers, by training and tools for emergency response, self-recovery, legal awareness & mitigation (in coordination with legal aid actors), protection measures to be able to support IDPs and affected people during and post emergencies.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The project will be implemented through in-kind modality with equal assistance to target groups. The rehabilitations work will be conducted through contractors and laborers in order to ensure equal quality of shelter upgrades and utilization of same materials in accordance with BoQs which is difficult to monitor and achieve when using a cash modality for rehabilitation. Furthermore, through the in-kind modality, MA’AN will utilize its network and previous experience to obtain best prices and standards for HH DRR protective materials. This modality has been utilized by MA'AN for decades of similar works in Area C and has proven to be successful for such responses.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiary satisfaction is continuously monitored by MA’AN team, that regularly visits & interviews beneficiaries. MA’AN’s female MEAL Officer will oversee & ensure transparency in the complaint response mechanism (CRM), duly reported to MA’AN’s management when relevant, & then properly addressed. MA’AN establishes a CRM under each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints & give feedback in a confidential & secure way. As many communication means as relevant are made available to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context & culture, as well as project resources & beneficiaries’ vulnerabilities. The CBPSCs will be sensitized by project team on the CRM. Its information is clearly displayed at project sites. Complaints are recorded in an area-level register under restricted access. Under project supervision, Committees will inform & encourage project beneficiaries (special focus on women and PWDs) to share feedback on provided assistance, potential encountered issues &/or remaining needs to be addressed. Depending on the nature & severity of the cases received under CRM, different action pathways may be activated. All feedback received through CRM or collected through field visits will be taken seriously and dealt with according to MA’AN’s SoPs. MEAL Officer will also ensure that this feedback counts into the lessons learned and is used to adapt interventions to be more responsive to vulnerable groups’ needs (especially women & PWDs). MA’AN will also provide beneficiaries with PSEA hotline number and explain that it can be used for complaints involving inappropriate behavior, sexual harassment, exploitation or abuse - all correspondences will be addressed with the utmost confidentiality & seriousness. Any GBV cases reported to MA’AN will be dealt with in utmost sensitivity. MA’AN will consult the Protection Consortium & depending on the case, might refer the victim to a specialized org.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MA’AN’s Complaints Response Mechanism is easily accessible, takes into account the specificities of the local context & culture, as well as project resources & beneficiaries’ vulnerabilities. At the onset of the project, MA’AN staff will coordinate with all relevant stakeholders in the community in order to call for an orientation meeting and ensure that representatives from all community groups are attending. During the orientation meetings, MA’AN staff will introduce the organization, project objective, timeline & planned interventions - setting the stage for a smooth & accountable implementation to be executed. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Addressing the needs of full community clusters & involving community members as leaders throughout the project’s implementation, the proposed intervention seeks to minimize implementation risk & enforce protection mainstreaming and the “do no harm” policy. Community leaders will be involved throughout every phase of project implementation. The security situation is monitored closely on an ongoing basis by MA’AN to ensure timely advice & relevant security precautions can be implemented. Furthermore, MA’ANs local coordinators & continuous presence in the communities along with the CBPC members will report regularly on any risks to safety & security during implementation to mitigate time & effectiveness impact. When needed, MA’AN has depended on security & safety approaches to infrastructure work in Area C. To ensure safety & security of HH’s & beneficiaries during implementation & in line with the “Do No Harm” principles, MA’AN ensures that infrastructure work in all its forms in Area C do not affect the arial view of shelter dimensions in the community to reduce chances of future demolition orders. Further, during provision of materials, MA’AN ensures all material provided are hidden under existing structures to avoid confiscation. When needed, provision & rehabilitation work is done during night hours as a response to security & financial threats in the target Areas. The communities targeted by the project are very risky locations (surrounded by settlements or under ICA threat of demolition), therefore MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during weekends) in coordinated times with the community committee & beneficiary to assess the security situation of the community. MA’AN mobilizes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. When possible, in alignment with its procurement policy & guidelines, MA’AN procures from local sources. MA’AN has been working for many years in Area C, including for shelter projects, & has a good relationship with the communities, so it will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time. MA’AN will also remain in contact with various partners working on protection & legal cases & reporting incidents. Aligning with the “Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work is completed with project closeout. In instances where contractors and HHs are unable to effectively conduct the shelter rehabilitation, MA’ANs technical team will directly supervise the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households, persons with disabilities … etc). Furthermore, MA’AN staff follow strict health, hygiene, & safety measures (that were adopted during the spread of COVID-19) in order to mitigate the risk of harm being done to targeted beneficiaries and community members.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The response activities have been designed to address the tailored needs of community members. The criteria will focus on the socio-economic conditions of the households as well as on the housing conditions & the gender make-up of the household. As women are responsible for the household in Palestinian society, the number of females in the household will also be an important part of the selection process. Women headed households, seniors living alone, & PWDs are disproportionately affected by poor housing conditions, such as those in the selected villages thus these groups will be a prioritized during the selection of beneficiaries. Moreover, protective materials will be distributed to HHs meeting the criteria based on the rapid technical assessment of the communities. Shelter rehabilitation/ upgrade and winterization will be conducted for these groups by the projects technical teams including provision of material and conducting the rehabilitation work. The shelter technical needs for selected households will be evaluated individually and customized to meet the needs of the groups in terms of access, safety, protection and privacy rather than a standardized implementation for all shelters. Moreover, the CBPC will ensure representation of these marginalized groups and their needs will be accounted for in the customized protection of DRP trainings in communities.It is important to note that MA’AN adheres to the codes of conduct and standards that have been developed by the international humanitarian community. MA’AN has set standards of behavior in humanitarian settings seeking to maintain the high standards of independence, effectiveness, and impact. MA’AN recognizes its obligation to provide humanitarian assistance wherever it is needed. We strive to alleviate human suffering amongst those least able to withstand the stress caused by disaster. We base the provision of relief aid upon a thorough assessment of the needs of the disaster victims and the local capacities already in place to meet those needs. We endeavor to respect the culture, structures and customs of the communities we are working in. We do not tie the promise, delivery or distribution of assistance to the embracing or acceptance of a particular political or religious creed. Additionally, in order to protect our independence, we seek to avoid dependence upon a single funding source. We place a high priority on the proper co-ordination of our emergency responses, and we make sure to engage and build local capacities while doing so. Intended beneficiaries are always involved in the design, management and implementation of the assistance we provide. We also endeavor to minimize the negative impact of humanitarian assistance, seeking to avoid long-term beneficiary dependence upon external aid. Most importantly, we hold ourselves accountable to both those we seek to assist and those from whom we accept resources.
