Project Summary

Syria remains a protection crisis, Unaddressed widespread unexploded ordnance (UXO) impacts people’s livelihoods and movements with negative consequences on long-term perspectives for people in need, rendering the prospects of durable solutions bleak.
In 2024, the vulnerability of certain groups, including women, girls, people with disabilities and children, is expected to persist, with incidents of gender-based violence and diminishing social cohesion compounding existing challenges.
Throughout the year, Syria saw the continuation of aerial bombardment in Homs and Damascus Governorates, The presence of landmines and UXOs was regarded by humanitarian partners as a humanitarian access constraint in 146 sub-districts (54 per cent of all subdistricts).2 Children are particularly at risk.
The most affected areas included Aleppo Governorate where 4.2 million people were affected to varying degrees.
The February 2023 earthquake in north Syria has added agony on top of a catastrophic situation, increasing the strain on services, causing displacement, and inflicting widespread damage.
Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, resulting in millions of people unable to meet their basic needs. Recovery from the earthquakes will necessitate long-term funding and improved emergency preparedness.
Protracted displacement, economic decline, global inflation, and the aftermath of the earthquakes that struck northern Syria in February 2023 are some of the biggest challenges Syrians currently face.Evolution of People in Need ( Protection sector ) is expected to be 15.3 million people at 2024,,566,142 in Aleppo, 2,044,565 in Rural Damascus, 906,456 in Damascus, 981,729 in Homs, 674,415 in Lattakia
Evolution of People in Need ( NFI sector ) is expected to be 6.2 million people, (in increase about 500 thousands people from last year) 1,111,204 in Aleppo , 953,157 in Rural Damascus, 400,262 in Damascus, 404,956 in Homs, 255,166 in Lattakia.
Protection activities in Damascus will be implemented in AlMidan in Southern Damascus where a density of IDPs and overburdened host community exist.This project will support the already exist community center in Almidan to expand its activities due to the increased number of beneficiaries seeking for services.In Rural Damascus, the proposed project aims at adding a complementary activities to the already existing protection services, this means adding GBV and GP activities to areas where only CP activities are implemented, and adding CP, GP, RE to areas that GBV services are provided.
In Aleppo, activities will be a continuation of protection activities currently implemented under SHF fund that will end by April 2024.
Altamayoz has a strong data base that will be used to control distribution of DKs,NFIs and avoid duplication
In Homs and Latakia, all protection activities will be implemented through Altamayoz centers where no protection services have been provided yetز
Target under NFI sector is 6000 HHs (30000 individuals), Target under protection (CP, GP, GBV, MA) 78575 individuals
The proposed project mainstreams protection through considering the principles of Do No Harm. GBV issues will be considered through conducting awareness-raising sessions to mainstream GBV prevention.All staff members have to sign PSEA code of conduct. AlTamayoz has an organized database for M&E. NFIs will be procured as per procurement policy accredited by UN agencies. NFIs are planned to be distributed to most vulnerable as per clear selection criteria
PRA
During project planning phase, Altamayoz has analyzed protection risks related to this project in the targeted location taking into consideration the risks women and girls, older people, people with disabilities might be exposed to while receiving services, expected tension among communities (IDPs, Returnees, Host community) while delivering NFIs and cash assistance.
