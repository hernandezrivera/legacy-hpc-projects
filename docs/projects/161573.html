Project Summary

This project will undertake food assistance, livelihood projects and provision of access to SAFE to affected persons in Jere, MMC, Ngala and Rann (Kala-Balge) in Borno State. According to Food Security Sector 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan, Food access remains a challenge due to relapse of the security situation and fresh waves of displacement, and continued food assistance for conflict-affected Households is needed. Borno State in the North-East region is largely affected by insurgency and has left most vulnerable crises-affected people in need of access to timely and appropriate Food assistance, including fuel and energy-related support to meet their immediate Food needs and Livelihood support. Access to humanitarian assistance has improved throughout the State, though periodic access constraints have persisted notably in many parts of the State. Despite this improvement, Crisis and Emergency remain widespread, and an estimated 1.75 million affected persons are targeted for assistance and support in the year 2020.
Food assistance and livelihood/access to SAFE projects will contribute significantly to:
1. The prevention of common diseases: having healthy community that will have strong immune system to fight infections.
2. Children learning properly when they have food available either at home or in schools: once there is food in households, girls and boys are allowed to attend classes than when there is no food in the house.
This project will include a Wet Feeding component which is going to provide emergency response and act as first responder to new arrivals in the selected LGAs in Borno State through:
• Preparation and distribution of the meals to feed the new arrivals for 120 hours in the reception center.
• Provision of cooking fuel to new arrivals who are able to cook for themselves.
• Provision of follow ups and referrals for therapeutic feeding program.
The project will target an improvement in household food security, with the average number of meals consumed by both adults and children increasing from below 2 meals to nearly three meals (2.7). Household dietary diversity will also improve with households consuming a more varied diet.
At the field level, this project is going to have protection monitors to ensure effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. Identifying protection threats enables us to harmonize and fine-tune our food security activities in order to address, mitigate or reduce these threats. 5% of the total Budget for this project will be channeled towards protection mainstreaming.
At the programmes level, SHO will ensure Accountability, Participation and Empowerment by achieving the following:
• Making sure beneficiaries know they have a right to equitable and safe assistance, and where and how to obtain it.
• Consulting Men, Women, Boys, Girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities/specific needs to understand their needs and preferences for location, assessment, design, and methodology of assistance.
• Communities and beneficiaries will be involved in planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating of programmes so they can make informed decisions. SHO will be transparent in their objectives with beneficiaries and non-beneficiary communities, as well as with the government and other Stakeholders.
• A complaints feedback mechanism will be established with a view to improving programming, assisting in understanding beneficiary and community perceptions, promoting beneficiary empowerment and assisting in the early detection of problems such as targeting, misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse, food diversion and fraud.
• Ensuring that Food or livelihood committees are representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socio-economic group, etc.).
• Monitoring and Evaluation will be done to track implementation and outputs.

1 - Needs

The current situation which is caused by the insurgency that started in 2009 has created very complex Humanitarian situation in Borno State, with the Humanitarian situation deteriorating throughout the North-East. An estimated 1.75 million people are targeted for Food assistance access to SAFE, Agric-based entrepreneurship and other related food security assistance. Compared to the same period in 2019, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis or worse acute food insecurity. However, high levels of acute food insecurity still persist in few locations. The main drivers of this insecurity mainly are displacement and economic decline as a result of the insurgency. An Assessment was carried out by SHO in some LGAs, locations in Borno (MMC, Ngala, Jere and Kala-Balge in early October 2019, this revealed needs in food assistance (Wet ration), livelihoods opportunities and access to SAFE for vulnerable women and youth, access to farmland, livestock, and aquaculture. IDPs in these locations especially those in Ngala and Rann (Kala-Balge) do not have access to agriculture-based entrepreneurship, due to limitation of farmland. IDPs do get their firewood from nearest bushes which exposes them to sexual assault, insecurity, and abductions. The respondents also sell their items so as to purchase food, while men and women adopt coping mechanism. In fact, women and men of vulnerable households in camps and host communities revealed that they reduce their number of daily food consumption in order to meet the food needs of their children. Furthermore, the respondents in these locations expressed the need in accessing legal documentation (identity cards, births certificates) in order to access registration for food assistance. The result of the needs assessment carried out by SHO revealed that Number of food insecured people classified in CH Phase 3-5 in the selected location was 20,000, Number of people (new arrivals) in need of food assistance through in kind - wet rations was 37,000, majority of this number was from Ngala and Rann (Kala-Balge) where the new arrivals are much more.The number of IDPs who are in need of support with energy efficient stoves is 15,000. Number of IDPs who were assessed to be in need of Livelihood inputs and Agric-based entrepreneurship was 23,000 and 8,000 respectively.As of 15th of August 2019, the estimated number of the influx in the border towns was about 35,000 individuals. The new arrivals cannot afford Food, cooking utensils and access to energy to feed themselves before they could settle with the new environment. It takes time, while some are starved due to harsh conditions encountered on their way, which is why the contingency measure to receive the new arrivals especially In-Kind Food assistance (wet feeding) need to be taken into serious consideration. Through this project, SHO aims to expand its wet feeding program and also emergency response program to better serve the IDPs at the time of arrival and intend to expand to other liberated locations. Since Salient has been the service provider of last resort in wet feeding in Ngala.

2 - Activities

SHO shall conduct an assessment to MMC, Ngala, Jere and Kala-Balge prior to implementation of the program to screen vulnerable IDPs households and deliver in-kind food assistance, Livelihoods, SAFE and wet feeding.Activity 1: Prior to implementation, SHO shall conduct a need assessment to screen vulnerabilities within IDPs’ households, returnees and host communities of the selected LGA in Borno State. This going to be conducted with the host community, IDPs, women and youth forming the committee and will be meant to achieve needs assessment, project design, proper delivering of assistance based on the needs assessed and review of projects.Activity 2: Provision of adequate/improved Wet feeding for New Arrivals for Five days. - Incorporate Protection Mainstreaming - At the field level, protection monitors are charged with ensuring that effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. - conducting of post distribution monitoring per feeding cycle - Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. , to ensure accountability to the affected population - Engaging the community volunteers in cooking their food with the supervision of cooks. - Providing mobility to the new arrivals from screening area to RC. - cooking demonstration on how to prepare CSB++ - On site ratio,provide 2100 kcals of energy per person per day. - To provide cooking fuel to new arrivals who are able to cook for themselves.The new arrivals targeted for this intervention is 37,000 based on the needs assessment conducted. Activity 3: provision of fuel-efficient technologies (stoves) to vulnerable households in targeted LGAs in order to reduce reported cases of Gender-based violence during collection of firewood in the nearest bushes. Targeted number of IDPs to benefit from this fuel efficient technologies is 23,000.Activity 4: Provision of Agric-based entrepreneurship interventions in crop cultivation, livestock and aquaculture, 8,000 beneficiaries are target to benefit from this to reduce vulnerability and further increase IDPs dignity.Activity 5: Provision of accessible, effective and confidential complaint mechanisms that are well understood and respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can immediately be put in place. Staff the mechanism with both men and women and ensure it is accessible for children.- SHO will set up a rudimentary Help Desk and rely on PMC. Draft a report of complaints and feedback collected.- In other locations Help-desks will be made available and PMC consulted before and after a registration or distribution.- SHO will Inform the PMC members, the community leaders and affected populations about the use and purpose of the Help Desk.- SHO will Locate Help-Desks in safe places at the sites under shades. SHO may also have to walk around collecting complaints and feedback or conduct a community outreach initiative with the PMC.Activity 6: Documentation of differences in dietary needs, preferences and restrictions and Monitoring whether women and men (boys/girls) receive fair and comparable benefits from interventions in food assistance, agriculture, livestock, fisheries, and/or livelihoods.
