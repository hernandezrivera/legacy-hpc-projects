Project Summary

This project aims to ensure access to emergency integrated lifesaving mobile healthcare services to under-served IDPs, returnees and host populations in the hard-to-reach rural locations of in Pibor County in Jonglei State. Out of the county’s population estimated at 227,854 people, 127,598 are considered people in need of health services. RHS targets 6% of the people in need disaggregated as follows: 2022 children less than five years (1091 males and 931 females), 2,325 children aged more than 5 years but less than 18 years (1,098 males and 1,234 females), 2,926 adults aged 18-60 years (1,366 males and 1,560 females) and 678 elderly people (60 years and above) including 247 males plus 431 females. The project is also expected to reach at least 1,194 people with disabilities or 15% of the target population.
The intervention aims to establish and support 2 community-based mobile clinics at Manydakel and Teneth villages where response activities shall be coordinated. The key focus will be on provision of integrated essential life-saving primary healthcare services based on the basic package of health and nutrition services (BPHNS), the Boma Health Initiative Model, epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, and responsive activities to the people in need paying considerable attention to the needs of vulnerable groups (women, children and person with disabilities) shall be greatly prioritized.
Our interventions aim to contribute to the Cluster and HRP strategic objectives (CO 1, 2 & 3), to reduce excess morbidity and mortality caused by common communicable and epidemic prone diseases, Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to the vulnerable populations, increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, the disabled and mental health disorders thus, enhancing considerable community resilience to the compounding shocks and endemic precarious conditions.
Through the 2 supported facilities, RHS will provide basic healthcare services including: OPD curative consultations, antenatal care (ANC) and postnatal care (PNC), conducting clean, safe and hygienic deliveries by skilled birth attendants, Routine mass vaccination (EPI services) of U5 children, Screening, treatment of MAM and referral of SAM cases, conduct surveillance on epidemic prone diseases (integrated disease surveillance & response/early warning alert response system- IDSR/EWARS), health education and hygiene promotion, training of project staff on WASH-IPCs, preparedness, response and control of epidemic prone disease outbreaks focusing on, malaria, pneumonia, cholera, measles and COVID-19, management & referral of SGBV cases; clinical management of rape cases (CMR) and basic psycho-social support (counselling), Management of war wounded (injuries), provision of basic & minimal initial HIV/AIDS/TB services, RDT Screening services for most endemic illness on children and pregnant women, distribution of LLITNs and micro nutrient supplementation and deworming tablets, growth monitoring and nutrition assessment to under 5 children and PLW.
The proposed target population is 7,958 direct beneficiaries and about 2,500 indirect beneficiaries from the surrounding communities at a cost of 300,000 US dollars.

1 - Needs

South Sudan’s humanitarian situation is expected to remain dire in 2023. The September Humanitarian snapshot estimates that 8.9 people are in need as a result of extensive flooding, high levels of food insecurity, violence and disease outbreak. The snapshot further elaborates that 7.7 million people are acutely food insecure while 676 women and 1.4 million children are malnourished.The proposed project area (Pibor County) has been submerged by flooding since June 2020 with most of the people displaced and their livelihoods severely affected. As per the Health Sector severity classification, Pibor is at intersectional severity level 5. The flooding incident coupled with sporadic inter-ethnic violence have compromised people’s coping mechanisms and complicated the needs of women, children, girls, men and boys and people with special needs. The number of people in need of health services in the county is estimated to be 151,293, mainly women (24%), children (53%) and elderly persons (8%) being vulnerable affected residents, IDPs and returnee populations (Health Cluster PIN, 2022).The 2022 HNO further observes that the ongoing flooding, COVID-19 scourge and wanting and relentless inflations, economic crisis amid the limited health facility functionality, limited capacity and poor coverage (inaccessible), have made it difficult to meet the required health needs in the precarious conditions. Women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable to an array of illnesses and preventable conditions such as acute watery diarrhoea, malaria, upper respiratory tract infections among others.Another compounding reality is that in Jonglei State, only about 5% of health facilities are fully functional, implying inadequate access and unavailability of healthcare services including community health system to most communities in the State, a huge gap in deed! As a result, only 59% of the population have some access to health facilities in one hour’s time; 80% faces immense barriers in accessing health services. In addition, with low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage), children are more vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. Malaria, RTI (pneumonia) and AWDs (20 counties regarded cholera hotspots) are the leading causes of morbidity (52.6%) and mortality (58.2%) and significant outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases (measles) with highest maternal mortality (789 per 100,000 live births). Only 39% women are availing recommended 4 ANC services during pregnancy and institutionalized delivery (WHO, 2021). These realities on the ground in Pibor call for tailored planning for appropriate humanitarian response given that government spending on healthcare is still extremely low.

2 - Project activities

This is an emergency humanitarian project to the vulnerable communities affected by natural and man-made disasters notably conflicts, flooding and poor living conditions. RHS proposes to establish and support 2 mobile health facilities at Manydakel and Teneth villages in Pibor. The project will focus on provision of integrated essential life-saving primary healthcare services based on the basic package of health and nutrition services (BPHNS), the Boma Health Initiative Model, epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. The project will offer prevention, preparedness, and responsive activities to the people in need paying considerable attention to the needs of vulnerable groups (women, children and person with disabilities). The project aims to ensure and enhance the Cluster objectives (CO 1, 2 &3), to reduce excess morbidity and mortality caused by common communicable and epidemic prone diseases, Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to the vulnerable populations, increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, the disabled and mental health disorders thus, enhancing considerable community resilience to the compounding shocks and endemic precarious conditions.Through the 2 supported facilities, RHS will provide a wide range of healthcare services including: 1. OPD curative consultations, antenatal care (ANC) and postnatal care (PNC) 2. Conducting clean, safe and hygienic deliveries by skilled birth attendants, Routine mass vaccination (EPI services) of U5 children, Screening, treatment MAM and referral of SAM cases, Conduct surveillance on epidemic prone diseases (integrated disease surveillance & response/early warning alert response system- IDSR/EWARS), Health education and Hygiene promotion, Training of project staff on WASH-IPCs, preparedness, response and control of epidemic prone disease outbreaks focusing on, malaria, pneumonia, cholera, measles and COVID-19, Management & referral of SGBV cases; clinical management of rape cases (CMR) and basic psycho-social support (counselling), Management of war wounded (injuries), Provision of basic & minimal initial HIV/AIDS/TB services, RDT Screening services for most endemic illness on children and pregnant women, Distribution of LLITNs and micro nutrient supplementation and deworming tablets, Growth monitoring and nutrition assessment to under 5 children and PLW. RHS project activities shall serve a population of up to 10,458 people; 7,958 direct beneficiaries and about 2,500 indirect beneficiaries from the surrounding communities estimated to benefit from the proposed interventions at a projected budget of 300,000 USD.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Before the project commences, RHS will directly engage beneficiaries to first ascertain the needs of various vulnerable groups in a gender and age sensitive manner with considerable attention to persons with specific needs. In collaboration with relevant stakeholders our teams will directly incorporate and use the beneficiaries’ representatives (women, youth, persons with disabilities and others) and local authority leadership structures to facilitate and enhance a joint sensitization on the project detail including issues related to implementation, monitoring, evaluation and reporting. Suggestions boxes will be established in all the proposed facilities to ensure beneficiaries who feel threatened because of the information divulged are protected. This will mutually enhance effective and efficient complaints, feedback mechanisms about the project content, context and scope. Information obtained from beneficiaries will be kept with prudence, integrity and utmost confidentiality to ensure maximum protection in all aspects during and after project period. In addition, our community health committees (CHC) approach previously used in Manydakel and Teneth in 2019, remains a great testimony and reference success stories to widely adopted in every community-based project intervention. RHS shall continuously apply this concept everywhere for the similar community project interventions and ensures joint monitoring and evaluation as well as regular feedback in the course of project implementation

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This is an emergency humanitarian intervention. RHS team will meet with beneficiaries prior to the start and convene with relevant project stakeholders. Wherefore, all feedback from beneficiaries regarding the project, will be accorded special attention and respect. It will then be analysed and appropriated diligently. The responsive approaches will promptly address complaints especially those that will require wider community consultations and recommendations whence, handled, appropriated for informed-decision-making. As a result, stewardship and information sharing on aid effectiveness, and or ineffectiveness as well as general challenges shall be collectively accounted for thus, ensuring the project cost-effectiveness, reliability and sustainability in the areas.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RHS will engage all beneficiaries through their representatives; persons living with disabilities (PLWDs), pregnant & lactating women (PLW), girls and illiterate members of the community in the RHS proposed community-based health committees (CHCs). Through these people’s representatives, the project will ensure all voices are heard at all times and their complaints analysed and responded to in the most efficient and satisfying manner. RHS project will dis-aggregate beneficiary data by vulnerability, preferably, using the Washington Group Short Set of Questions (WGSS). The literates will be encouraged to utilize the proposed suggestion boxes to deposit any confidential information written for them by trusted persons as the illiterate groups relay their regards through the CHCs and their representatives.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RHS has a PSEA policy that guides the staff to ensure protection of beneficiaries against any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. It is principally meant to safe-guide all workers, collaborators and consultants in the course of delivering healthcare services. At the onst of the project RHS will orient all the recruited project staff and the CHCs’ members to be the guardians of the PSEA policy in the field so as to minimize and regularly report the PSEA incidents. The CHCs will be subsequently charged to facilitate the communities on the appropriate pathways and avenues for reporting all incidents as community watch-dogs and whistle-blowers. We shall keep abreast of all new knowledge and updates on the matter and this will be communicated effectively and timely to all stakeholders in the areas of operation. In addition, RHS will establish and support various women and girls’ friendly spaces to advocate for their rights and enhance access to relevant information regarding available care services with appropriate information exchange network and referral mechanisms. Suggestion boxes will be provided at all the 9 facilities to capture confidential information from those who may fear reprisals. All information will be analysed and responded to appropriately.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project pays speciﬁc attention to the needs of the different groups in a gender sensitive manner. Cultural sensitivities and variations will be put in much consideration before and during implementation of the project. Each beneﬁciary and project stakeholder shall be accorded special recognition and be proactively involved, to take part in the decision-making process. We also hinted necessary precautions to protect children especially unaccompanied minors among the IDPs. This project will involve communities and households in particular, because it has been designed to protect the vulnerable household members especially women, girls and children from sexual exploitation and abuse. Complaints that will not reach through the normal channels from the supervisors and project oﬃcers shall be received directly by the M & E Field visitors during project progressive evaluation meetings. After proper investigation of the alleged issues, the head oﬃce shall communicate appropriate feedback outcomes to both beneﬁciaries and partners including the corrective measures taken, equal involvement and participation of the beneﬁciaries in decision making, giving them opportunity to employment and empowering them to avoid and report violence against other more vulnerable groups (women, girls, boys and disable groups) within the community while keeping referral pathways (information exchange) so confidential and influential at informed-decision making. The project will identify, address and monitor environmental, attitudinal and institutional barriers and enablers to participation for people with disabilities and older persons, and will dedicate budget for removal of these identified barriers
