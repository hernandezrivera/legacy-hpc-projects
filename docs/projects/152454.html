Needs

In 2019, an estimated 5,712,000 South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019. The number of people requiring emergency WASH services in 2019 increased by 7 per cent from the previous year, explained by the expansion of WASH data with a higher reliability and more detailed analysis. Women and girls face increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water and using communal latrines, and access to menstrual hygiene products, and appropriate and dignified washing locations remains their key need. WASH needs are also high among IDPs in non-camp settings and among their already stretched host communities. Only 29 per cent of the population has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-minute distance without facing protection concerns in the whole country. The remaining two thirds of the population are thus required to take greater lengths to access water or are reliant on surface water or unprotected water sources, which increases the GBV related risks for women and girls fetching water. More than 90 per cent of the population practices open defecation, either because they do not have access to or are not accustomed to using a basic sanitation facility. Only 13 per cent of the population has access to WASH NFIs, such as jerry cans, soap and mosquito nets. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. For example, 75 per cent of the population reported households with members that had been self-diagnosed with a water-borne illness in the previous two-week period. The conflict has continued to drive people’s WASH needs, from blocking or destruction of WASH infrastructure to limiting WASH commodities in the market. Insecurity at times prevents humanitarian actors from accessing areas that have high WASH needs, while the presence of armed groups can prevent civilians from accessing existing WASH infrastructure. During 2018, though its different WaSH intervention in Upper Nile, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Central Equatoria where SI has static presence, SI has been able to see that WaSH needs are still important in those areas, especially in Upper Nile and Central Equatoria. In Fashoda County, where SI is currently operating, 100% of inhabitants reported an unimproved source or surface water as their main source of drinking water. In Aburoc, the situation is critical as people will have no access to water after November 2018. SI was also implementing an Emergency, Preparedness & Response (EP&R) project until March 2018 in which 137,494 beneficiairies have been reached in total. As the crisis is dynamic and the needs are still high, it is still paramout to have the capacity to deploy quickly and cover new identified urgent needs. SI already has experience of the EP&R approach and is willing to restart responding to the needs throughout the country.

Activities

• Provide timely equitable access to safe and sufficient water for vulnerable and affected population with setup of SWAT systems, borehole drilling and rehabilitation of wells and boreholes• Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among the affected population to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through hygiene promotion sessions• Provide vulnerable and affected population with accessible, secure and dignified gender appropriated sanitation services with the construction and rehabilitation of latrines, latrines desludging, bathing shelter constructions, solid waste disposal and drainage. SI will implement these activities through WaSH in communities, WaSH in Health, WaSH in nutrition and WaSH in schools through a gender based approach in order to ensure that water points and sanitation infrastructures are accessible, safe and dignified for vulnerable people such as disabled, elderlies, women and children. All these components will also include Cholera predparedness and response and Ebola preparedness and response as SI is currently active in Cholera and Ebola preparedness.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

SI will liaise with community leaders and elders to get information about the location of beneficiaries to reach and inform about the assistance provided. Furthermore, consultations will be held through focus group discussions with women, men, girls and boys. The focus is to understand their needs and to let them know about all the components or the projects and their expected involvement. Finally, SI will publish the lists of beneficiaries and will set up a complaint mechanism.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SI provides accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and affected populations over information provision. SI will always include the most vulnerable in its messages (i.e. women, children, elderlies and disabled). For instance, in hygiene promotion, SI will work with local population to ensure that the messages are properly transmitted in the local dialect.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

SI is determined to prevent the sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers and reaffirms its goal of achieving full implementation of the six core principles adopted in 2002 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The present PSEA Policy complements SI’s Code of Conduct and Protection Policy which both state that it is strictly forbidden for any employee to use its position to obtain any favor, including sexual favors, and to have sexual intercourse with minors. A Whistleblowing Policy and a feedback and complaints response mechanisms framework are also key pillars of SI’s policies package which allows any SI worker to report any misconduct at headquarters level.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

SI will allow affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence. SI ensures consultations are widely done while appreciating diversity among different population. For example, consultations are done with people with disabilities, women, girls, boys, men and religious groups when necessary. Moreover, SI will conduct gender segregated focus groups discussions and set up a complaint mechanism.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Along with its policy on accountability, SI will seek the views of affected populations to improve policies and practices in programming, therefore a feedback and complaints response mechanism will be streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. As an example, SI will set up a complaint desk during distributions.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Based on its broad monitoring and evaluation framework, SI ensures that monitoring and evaluation of the goals and objectives of programs involves the affected populations. This enables SI to improve its humanitarian assistance deliveries to be of quality and address the needs of the affected populations. For example, SI will consult the community leaders and conduct gender segregated focus group discussions.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In order to mainstream protection, SI strives to respect the Do No Harm principle. To that extent, SI initiates a protection risk analysis before and during the implementation of the project. The aim is to have a participatory methodology which ensures that the aid recipients (especially the most vulnerable i.e. disabled people, elderlies, women and children) are at the heart of the consultation process, and can voice their protection needs and risks. SI identifies the issues, their nature, their actors and their motivations in order to implement the project in the best way possible.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Assistance will be provided through meaningful access to impartial services to beneficiaries, while paying attention to the context and sociocultural norms. For example, consultations will be done with people with disabilities, elderlies, women and all these vulnerable groups will be represented in the distributions committees. These consultations will limit the risks created or heightened by aid. SI will work to prevent and minimize any unintended negative effects of the intervention (the Do No Harm principle), and work towards equality, impartiality, accountability, participation and empowerment. This will promote safety and dignity for the beneficiaries.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In its program design and activity selection, SI sought to minimize the protection risks during the implementation stage. To that extent, during the initial assessments, SI ensures protection aspects are assessed to make sure that its interventions will not endanger lives of the beneficiaries. Furthermore, the Complaints Response Mechanism that will be put in place during the implementation will help SI to readjust its practices during the intervention and functions as a real-time monitoring tool. Finally, when choosing beneficiaries, the vulnerability to threats of different population sub-groups such as people with disabilities, elderlies, women and youth – exposed to different types of threats – is considered, together with their needs.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

In order to ensure the access of beneficiaries, SI will make sure that they are protected during the distributions from their specific vulnerabilities. During its monitoring, SI seek to monitor the number or percentage of population declaring having challenges in accessing the humanitarian assistance. In addition, SI, in its monitoring ensures to understand the type of access challenges or barriers beneficiaries might be facing while trying to reach assistance. This data inform the modifications or strategies that SI can put in place in consultation with the beneficiaries to address the access challenges. To that extent, vulnerable people who can’t access water or sanitation infrastructures because of disabilities will have adapted ramps to allow them to use them. Furthermore, SI will always make sure that hygiene promotion sessions are done on sites where access is possible for any beneficiary. Post distribution monitoring’s and KAP surveys will measure the level of satisfaction on access related matters at different times of the project.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

SI ensures that its intervention will not put beneficiaries at risk. This is done by conducting risk analysis on WaSH infrastructures. When constructing boreholes or latrines, SI will always ensure that it will be done in a safe place for women. Furthermore, latrines blocks will be composed of different stances specifically for men and women. During NFI distributions, SI will consider the distance from the accommodation to the distribution site and ensure that beneficiaries reach their home before dark. Complaint desks on site will also allow SI to adjust its activities according to the beneficiaries’ feedback. Finally, post-distribution monitoring will measure the level of satisfaction on safety related matters at different times of the project.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

As SI always strives to respect the Do No Harm principle, this project will respect the dignity of beneficiaries. In order to do that, SI will always make sure that all negative effects of the intervention such as sexual assault or the distance of distribution points, water points and sanitation infrastructures from the accommodation of beneficiaries. Knowledge, aptitudes and practices surveys and satisfaction surveys will be conducted to help SI measuring the satisfaction of beneficiaries.
