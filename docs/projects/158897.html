Project Summary

The project aims at improving the access to safe and equitable WASH services and appropriate GBV response mechanisms during emergencies and shocks in accordance with the dignity of the underserved and most vulnerable communities of the Middle Area in the Gaza Strip. The project targets the most vulnerable communities of the Middle Area with particular focus on families whose children were identified to receive case management services (according to the national child protection referral system), families whose children affected by GMR related violence, and GBV survivors.
The objective of this action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the WASH Cluster 2020 HRP, objective 1: “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with the dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank". In addition, this action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the Protection Cluster 2020 HRP, objective 2:” Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence”. The project activities include two main components (1) WASH component, and (2) GBV component. Under the WASH component the following activities will be implemented: (1.1) Distribution of functional and safe Roof Storage Tanks (1000 liters’ capacity), (1.2) Distribution of functional and safe Potable water storage tanks (200 liters’ capacity), (1.3) Rehabilitate sanitation facilities for 300 households to meet SPHERE standards (age, gender, and culturally sensitive WASH facilities, and adequate to the needs of people with disabilities); (1.4) Hygiene promotion activities, (1.5) Hygiene kits distribution, (1.6) Maintenance/installation of sewer lines, and (1.7) installation of houses sewage connections. Under the GBV component the following activities will be implemented: (2.1) Provide case management and referral services, (2.2) Counselling Services (Individual and group counseling), (2.3) Distribution of dignity kits, (2.4) Awareness raising on GBV, (2.5) provide legal services, and (2.6) training of non-GBV specialist, WASH staff, on GBV detection and referral

1 - Needs

In April 2019, MA’AN started working in Wadi Al Salqa and the Middle Area addressing the gaps related to people's access to equitable and safe WASH services and people access to appropriate GBV response mechanisms. Significant gaps are still in place. In August 2019, MA’AN updated the assessment involving the Municipalities of the Middle Area and other different community-based child protection structures including the Child Protection Emergency Response Teams, and child protection community-based committees. A mapping exercise was conducted for the localities of the middle area where the poor access to WASH services imposing child protection threats. The mapping exercise resulted in identifying a set of prioritized interventions. The needs were validated in consultation with the Municipalities, the GVC (WASH Cluster Focal Point), and the CMWU. The main priorities included to (1) intervene at the community level addressing the poor access to sanitation services in Al QASTAL Area; and (2) provide maintenance for the in-house WASH facilities for the most vulnerable families in the targeted localities. The prioritized targeted groups are families whose children were identified to receive case management services (according to the national child protection referral system), families whose children affected by GMR related violence, and GBV survivors.A database is available for 200 cases of children receiving case management, and 198 GMR injured children (Supported by MA’AN in partnership with Unicef and SCI). The needs of the prioritized target group were assessed and validated through a survey outreached 101 families. Among the assessed families, 96.0 % are abject poor and other 4.0 % found poor. 13.9 % of households reported no income and a completed dependency on aid. The assessed population included 10.9 % female-headed households, 1.0 % of elderly headed households, 2.3 % people with disabilities, and 9.6 % pregnant/lactating women.The results showed significant concerns related to poor access to safe and equitable WASH services. 91.3% of the assessed families have poor and unsafe storage tanks for domestic water. 82.6% of the families do not have potable water storage tanks. 95.7% of the families have toilets in poor conditions that are neither healthy nor safe. 91.3% of families have kitchens in unhealthy/unsafe conditions. The results showed that 91.3% of the families are connected to the public sewage network, and 8.7% are using cesspits which are in poor and unsafe conditions. 60% of the surveyed families reported poor access to sanitary supplies and hygiene materials. 66% of the visited cases of people with disabilities reported the inadequacy of the WASH facilities to their particular needs. The poor access to adequate and WASH services have reflections on social relations and affects adversely the psychological well-being of women and increases the risks women face in the form of violence. The interviewed women showed high concerns related to the toilets and kitchen facilities which are unsafe and unhealthy with poor privacy. The majority of interviewed women reported that their poor access to adequate and safe WASH services has further intensified their responsibility for unpaid care and domestic work. The assessment addressed another type of protection concerns which is the GBV. 24.8% of the visited families reported the occurrence of at least one GBV incidents in the last six months. MA’AN partner, Women Affairs Center, had conducted an assessment to address the prevalence of GBV concerns and women's access to protection services. The assessment involved 137 women through interviews and focus group discussions. Several key informants were interviewed. The GBV assessment results revealed high risks of GBV. Women get exposed to various types of violence including physical, sexual, economic and denied inheritance. Poor access to GBV related services were recognized and reported by consulted women.

2 - Activities

The elaboration of the project activities had considered the conducted needs assessment and further consultations with the target beneficiaries regarding their priorities and the proper response mechanisms with particular focus on women and children. The project activities prioritized children affected by the GMR, children with child protection concerns, and GBV survivors.The implementation of the project activities will consider significantly the recommendations of MA’AN study which addresses the relationship between GBV and the poor access to equitable, safe, and adequate WASH services. The study has been under development.The project activities include two main components: WASH Component:- With equal participation of men and women, establishment of community-based WASH committees to ensure accountable, gender-sensitive, and environmentally friendly policies, practices, and decisions of duty bearers that are related to WASH issues.- Rehabilitate sanitation facilities for 300 households to meet SPHERE standards (age, gender, and culturally sensitive WASH facilities, and adequate to the needs of people with disabilities). - Distribution of 200 functional and safe Roof Storage Tanks (1000 liters’ capacity), and 200 functional and safe Potable water storage tanks (200 liters’ capacity)- Distribution of 700 hygiene kits. Sanitary supplies and hygiene materials will be appropriate to distribute to women and girls, especially related to menstruation. This will be accompanied by hygiene awareness activities. - Maintenance/installation of sewer lines (2200 meters). The target is Al QASTAL Area in Deir Al Balah. MA’AN is intended to elaborate an environment and social impact assessment (ESIA) that will be conducted in order to ascertain the possible environmental and social impacts of the infrastructural subcomponents- Installation of houses sewage connections (200 house connections). This should support families to be served by the installed sewer line in Al QASTAL Area. GBV Component:This component will be implemented in partnership with the Women Affairs Center. The main activities include:- Awareness-raising on GBV. 40 awareness-raising sessions will be conducted on women’s rights, early marriage, GBV risks, and protection mechanisms. 1000 women, girls, men, and boys will be outreached.- Provide case management and referral services. During the awareness raining activities and the home visits, cases of GBV will be identified (estimated at 100 cases). The case management process will be applied. The case management is a coordinated services delivery approach at the individual and household levels involving the identification of vulnerable children and women, assessment and planning, referral to services providers and follow up, in collaboration with the extended family, community and other service providers.- Counseling Services (Individual and group counseling). According to the case management process, cases in need of psychosocial counseling will be supported.- Distribution of dignity kits (100 kits). The kit content will be identified in consultation with women. The kit may include soap, lady slippers, underwear-bra, underwear for women, sanitary pads, absorbent cloth towel, toothbrush, toothpaste, shampoo, hair combs/brush, wipes, bath sponge, hand cream, socks, deodorant, and bag.- Provide legal services. Cases in need of legal services will be supported with the needed service using the project resources. About 50 cases will be supported to access appropriate legal services.- Training of non-GBV specialist, WASH Staff, on GBV detection and referral. 25 women and men will be targeted. This will be done in consultation with the GBV Sub Cluster. The project will ensure linkage to child protection referral networks for meeting the needs of girls in need of child protection interventions that have not been provided by MA’AN or WAC.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

An effective and reliable complaint mechanism will be applied that provides a chance for all beneficiaries especially women and people with disabilities to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities. The complaint and feedback mechanisms will be introduced to the stakeholders and target group through the orientation sessions at the beginning of the project activities. The beneficiaries will have the right to raise their concerns, feedback, and complaints in writing, verbally, via phone or SMS, directly to the responsible person/field monitors from MA'AN, and verbally and directly to MA’AN offices. MA’AN will document the received complaints to have them discussed among the complaint committee to take the proper action. The applied complaint mechanism has considered particular SoPs in case of gender-sensitive complaints.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention has been developed with comprehensive understanding of the context in which it is operating, and understanding of the interaction between the intervention and the context. The project design had considered protection risks and potential violations. The project includes effective use of referral pathways for specialist support, in areas related to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection and psychosocial support. Accountability to the affected population has been well mainstreamed in the project design, implementation, and monitoring. A set of mechanisms will be established to ensure accountable implementation of the project activities. One of the main components is to establish establish a Community-Based WASH Committee to support accountable, gender-sensitive, and environmentally friendly policies, practices, and decisions of duty bearers that are related to WASH issues. MA’AN in-house expertise will provide orientation for the committee members about the WASH minimum standards, the accountability standards, child protection policy, and the gender policy. The members of the committee will be selected based on their interest and commitment while ensuring the representation of the different community groups. Terms of reference addressing the roles and responsibilities of the committee will be developed in close coordination and consultation with the committee members and the municipality. The committee will play an essential role to ensure accountable implementation of the project activities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The prioritization for the target groups had considered the feedback of the involved community structures. The project targets the most vulnerable communities of the Middle Area with particular focus on families whose children were identified to receive case management services (according to the national child protection referral system), families whose children affected by GMR related violence, and GBV survivors. The beneficiaries' selection process will consider transparent and accountable processes including the feedback and complaint mechanism. This will ensure high involvement of the WASH Community Based Committee and the Child Protection Community Based Communities. The project activities will be announced clearly in the targeted communities. All of the relevant stakeholders will be involved including the MoSD, the Child Protection Working Groups, the GBV Working Group, and the WASH Cluster.
