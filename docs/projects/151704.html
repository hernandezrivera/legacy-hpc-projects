Project Summary

The primary goal of this project is to reduce preventable morbidity, mortality and disability as a result of infectious diseases through supporting existing health facilities in the geographical targets. The project founds an improved access of vulnerable people including migrants, displaced families, returnees and people with special needs to an integrated health care services at primary and secondary facilities with the focus on infectious disease management.
Under this project, WHO will strengthen the technical and operational capacities of the primary and secondary health facilities by providing health service providers with effective disease diagnosis and case management training opportunities. Equally the targeted health facilities will be supported with medical equipment and essential drugs for the timely provision of preventive and curative services to all communities.

Needs

According to the Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN), lower and upper Acute Respiratory Infection (ARI), watery and bloody diarrhea and leishmaniasis remain the leading causes of mortality in most of the districts of Libya. Similarly, spread of Tuberculosis (TB) among migrants and mobile populations with the identification of Multidrug Resistance (MDR) continue to be documented in overcrowded detention centers and displaced settlements. According to the National TB Program (NTP), 1,357 TB cases were recorded in 2017; of this 14.6 were migrants. The treatment options of TB are limited and expensive while recommended first and second line drugs are now always available.The spread of TB among migrants who are originally from countries with high prevalence rates of Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) raise another alarm for TB and HIV co-infections. TB infection with HIV is the most powerful known risk factor inclining for Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection and progression of HIV to active disease which increases the risk of latent TB reactivation 20-fold. Globally, TB is the most common cause of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndromes (AIDS) related death; thus TB bacteria and HIV act in synergy accelerating the decline of immunological functions and leading to subsequent death if untreated.

Activities

The following activities will be implemented in order to achieve the project objectives:1. Capacity building traininga. Train 250 health service providers from 24 primary and secondary health facilities on effective diseases diagnosis and case management focusing on upper and lower respiratory infection, leishmaniasis and other infectious diseases.b. Conduct a refresher training for 25 TB specialists on TB case management, diagnosis, and case reporting and drugs management.c. Support 10 cascade training courses for 240 health service providers from 24 TB centers in the main cities and districts focusing on case management, treatment and reporting.2. Procurement of laboratory supplies and consumablesa. Procure and distribute two consignments of laboratory reagents and consumables for effective disease diagnosis. b. Procure 3 GeneXpert machines for effective diagnosis and determine resistance to Rifampicin.c. Procure 3 mobile laboratories to screen the caseloads with other respiratory tract diseases.3. Procurement of essential drugsa. Procure and distribute three consignments of essential drugs for upper and lower respiratory infections and leishmaniasis.b. Procure and distribute three consignments of standard first and second line TB treatment on quarterly basis.c. Procure and distribute two consignments of rapid test kits for Sexually Transmitted Infections (STI) and HIV.d. Procure and distribute two consignments of Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) to main facilities.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

During the community consultation and needs assessment activities, the project identified the gender implications and gendered health determinants influencing health inequities between men, women, boys and girls. Vulnerabilities and strengths of men, women, girls and boys; older persons; persons with disabilities; persons belonging to minority groups and persons with mental disorders were also identified through joint assessments. The project will improve access of affected people to services by making services readily available round the clock. Gender equality in health service provision will be taken into consideration in order to warrant equity in health access and promote the Universal Health Coverage.WHO will organize a full orientation session on the accountability to affected people and protection for the service providers. A complaint mechanism will be established to receive and address concerns of beneficiaries.
