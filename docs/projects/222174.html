Project Summary

In South Sudan, acute food insecurity is driven by a combination of frequent and overlapping shocks, including climate-related challenges, insecurity, large-scale population displacement, and macroeconomic instability. Years of wealth depletion and limited access to essential services have significantly eroded many households' ability to cope with these adversities. The situation is further aggravated by the ongoing conflict in Sudan, which has placed immense strain on already resource-constrained villages along the South Sudan-Sudan border, forcing them to share scarce supplies with a continuously growing population of displaced individuals.
According to the IPC analysis for 2024, Unity State remains one of the most food-insecure regions in South Sudan. It is projected that 762,000 people (67.3% of the population) in Unity State will face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Among these, approximately 484,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), and 278,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity.
Climatic conditions have further deteriorated the situation. In 2023, the western parts of the state experienced drier-than-average conditions until mid-July, causing planting delays of up to six weeks. Conversely, Central Unity faced severe flooding, which disrupted household planting and crop production. Sub-national conflict in Central Unity has compounded these challenges, hindering most households from accessing agricultural fields. Disrupted access to critical inputs has also negatively impacted agricultural production across the State.
Currently, 6 out of 9 counties in Unity State are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, while the remaining three are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Counties such as Rubkona, Leer, and Mayendit are of particular concern, with over 75% of their population facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse conditions.
The majority of the population in Unity State relies on subsistence farming, yet they face significant barriers, including limited access to quality seeds, farm tools, and storage facilities. A general lack of knowledge about seed production, improved farming techniques, and post-harvest loss reduction further exacerbates the situation.
This project aims to build the capacity of 200 households (farmers) in Rubkona county, to produce seeds locally by supporting essential crops and vegetable production and productivity through the provision of vegetable seeds, RDAA shall purchase field crops seeds locally and give them for the FFS establishment and multiplication, field crops seed production techniques and multiplication and other agriculture inputs shall be addressed in this intervention.
RDAA wants to promote vegetables and fruit production as a means of diversifying household diet to address malnutrition which remains a major concern among children, and pregnant and lactating women as further detailed in the humanitarian context analysis, this will be realized through the empowerment of households and as well the IDPs. The provision of improved inputs like vegetable seeds and other tools will significantly contribute to the availability and accessibility of food from farmer’s production.
RDAA will fully engage the state ministry of agriculture and County Agriculture Department of the three counties and other relevant offices, community leaders, women’s groups, youth groups and other social segments throughout the life of the project.
The project will be integrated and enhance responses to the immediate needs of IDPs and the host community in Rubkona County and aid restoration of livelihoods and capacity building to pave the pathway for long-term recovery to strengthen communities’ ability to cope with and recover from ongoing lack of good seeds, malnutrition and poor diet

1. Needs

RDAA has completed an analysis to estimate the impact of floods based on information from multi-sector fast assessments conducted by clusters and other humanitarian groups, as well as information from the RRC. According to IPC ACUTE FOODINSECURITY AND MALNUTRITION ANALYSIS SEPTEMBER 2023 -JULY 2024, 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as some basic agriculture inputs then they can produce for the entire state and reduce the dependence syndrome on foreign importation of seeds. This project stands as a chance for them to get support so that, their one-time production capacity and interest may be enhanced through this intervention. The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe).The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. In the current analysis period (July to September 2024), 53 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 or above (Serious or worse).The number of severely food-insecure people during harvest periods has grown at an accelerating rate in Rubkona County due to poor seed accessibility Seasonal fluctuations in food security are becoming less stack and the overall food-insecure population has grown considerably Nevertheless, Rubkona County has the potential to progress in agricultural practices. Rubkona residents practice agro-pastoralism, augmenting their diets with fishing and foraging. This mix of livelihoods, however, differs depending on the payam. In 2021, an anticipated 20% of Rubkona County households will be farming, with a gross grain output of 0.5tonnes per hectare (FAO/WFP 2022), rising to 0.7 tones per hectare in 2022 (FAO/WFP 2023). The main crop grown is maize, with a smaller amount of groundnut sown as well. Sorghum, sesame, and vegetable farming are also practiced, but on a small-scale. This only needs some capacity building and supply of Equatoria. An effort that will extend RDAA’s service to Unity State and specifically to the people of Rubkona County where there is limit to the quantity of food available for consumption will help improve local seed supply and sustainable food production.
