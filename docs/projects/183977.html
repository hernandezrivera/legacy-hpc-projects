Project Summary

South Kordofan is suffering from multiple shocks which has created a dire humanitarian situation in the state. Mercy Corps (MC) considers the acute needs of the targeted localities and vulnerabilities of these people and will ensure increased household access to basic services and will help reduce vulnerability to diseases including the spread of COVID-19. Urgent humanitarian assistance is needed to support local people in South Kordofan to meet their basic needs. In response to this crisis, Mercy Corps plans to deliver a multi-sector response comprising of interventions in Health, Nutrition, WASH, FSL and Protection. These interventions will be targeted at the following localities: Habila, At Tadamin and Ar Rashad.
Multiple surveys point to extremely poor health indicators within the state especially in relation to morbidity and mortality among children under five and pregnant and lactating women. South Kordofan is also vulnerable to disease outbreaks such as Cholera, Malaria, COVID-19 and Chikungunya. The program will aim to support the SMoH to increase the availability of, and access to, the life-saving primary health care services the health centers provide. We will also support the SMoH to improve the availability of quality preventative and curative nutrition services at the targeted PHCCs. These interventions will be supported by awareness-raising activities and promotion of healthy behaviors within the target communities. MC will also support the targeted PHCCs to improve Infection Prevention Control ((IPC) measures at the facilities through a combination of training and the provision of supplies / equipment.
MC will also deliver a range of nutrition activities targeting children under 5 and pregnant / lactating women (PLW). We will support health facilities with outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) services whilst also training mothers support groups (MSG) to addresses the multi-sectoral causes of malnutrition, such as inadequate diet diversity, poor WASH behaviors, and unsafe food preparation and conservation methods. MC will deliver a range of nutrition activities targeting children under 5 and pregnant / lactating women (PLW). We will support health facilities with outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) services whilst also training mothers support groups (MSG) to addresses the multi-sectoral causes of malnutrition, such as inadequate diet diversity, poor WASH behaviors, and unsafe food preparation and conservation methods.
The protracted humanitarian situation within the state has resulted in multiple protection concern. GBV has been identified as a priority concern and MC will ensure the safety, health and dignity of women and girls by provision of quality multi-sectoral GBV response services with a survivor-centred approach and GBV risk mitigation measures. Activities proposed include support to Women and Girls Safe Spaces, train service providers on GBV especially health staff, conducting awareness-raising against GBV and PSEA and the provision of dignity kits for Women and Girls. Due to conflict & displacement, education in the region has suffered. MC will also establish early warning system within CBP.
MC will address WASH needs through rehabilitation of water sources at both community and institutions, formation and training of WASH committees to ensure improved access to clean and safe water. Mercy Corps will then complement the water supply activities through hygiene promotion and train community hygiene promoters who will carry out hygiene promotion awareness raising activities to enhance health and wellbeing of the targeted individuals. During the implementation, MC will ensure COVID-19 prevention
measures are intertied to reduce the risk of the spread.
Through this project, MC will provide ES/NFIs to affected populations inline with SPHERE standards. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) support will be provided during ES/NFI distributions especially in relation to fire and flooding.

1 - Needs

The latest Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that about 13.4 million people – 29 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance throughout 2021. This is 4.1 million people more compared to 2020 and an increase of 44 per cent in the year. Overall, the number of people in need in Sudan in 2021 is the highest reported in the past decade (OCHA, 2021 ‘Humanitarian Needs Overview’). GBV:South Kordofan state continues to be affected by conflict which has resulted in multiple GBV risks including gang rape, abduction, physical assault, harassment, and humiliation. Analysis shows that conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) is especially common in IDP camps and in the vicinity of camps. Refugee girls and young women in particular face a high risk of sexual violence. The collapse of traditional community support structures has increased the vulnerability in particular people with specific needs, including those living with disabilities. COVID-19 exacerbated GBV risks for girls and women with anecdotal reports suggesting an increase in domestic violence and sexual abuse. According to the 2021 HNO, there are 2.3 million people in need of GBV prevention, mitigation and response activities Health: The worsening economy and protracted health emergencies have resulted in an increase of the number of people who do not have access to basic health services, and a decrease in the health system capacity to deliver essential healthcare to people. The COVID-19 pandemic further strained the capacity of the health care system due to nationwide lockdowns, re-allocation of health resources, and disruption of global supply chains that impacted availability of medicines and medical supplies. During 2020, the cost of healthcare increased by 90 per cent due to inflation with availability of essential medicines ranging between 15 and 50 per cent. By the 15 of December 19,548 people had contracted COVID-19 including 1,344 associated deaths. Low access to safe drinking water, suboptimal vaccination coverage, and the risk of widespread seasonal flooding are existing hazards that contributed to the resurgence of vaccine preventable, water-borne, and vector borne disease outbreaks - including malaria and arthropod-borne viral diseases. By September 2020, 1.3 million cases of malaria were reported across Sudan with chronic lack of first line malaria medicines and mosquito nets. Nutrition:The nutrition situation in Sudan has been characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and under nutrition since the first S3M survey conducted in 2013. Malnutrition in all its forms is a serious public health challenge in Sudan. When nutritional needs are not met, it is impossible for an individual to achieve optimal physical and mental health. This creates limitations on educational and employment opportunities. The rate of malnutrition in Sudan has changed little in the last three decades, with an estimated 52 per cent of cases living in the nine non-conflict states. Drivers of malnutrition include high income poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. The nutrition statistics for Sudan are estimated as follows, GAM 13.6% at national level; SAM is over 600,000 and MAM is 2.2million based on the updated 2022 HNO analysis. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), over 117,000 people in South Kordofan are in need of nutrition support.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will provide vital humanitarian assistance to IDPs, Returnees, Refugees, HostCommunities in South Kordofan state. The proposed interventions will meet the needs ofthese groups in terms of WASH, FSL, Protection, ESNFI, Health and Nutrition. Protection monitoring activities through missions and remotely through regular communication with the community based protection networks to identify the most vulnerable individuals, protection concerns in the areas of physical safety, material safety, legal safety, freedom of movement and residence. Protection monitoring findings will be analysed, reflected in regular reports and shared with PWG South Kordofan, identified cases will be assisted through this project as well referrals to other services providers and partners. Mercy Corps will adopt a multi-sectoral approach to maximize the impact and added value of the proposed activities. The health, nutrition, protection, WASH, and food security sectors are key interventions that are likely to directly and significantly impact morbidity and mortality in emergency contexts. The protection cluster ensures linkages between the psychosocial services provided through the protection cluster partners and mental health services provided by the health cluster partners. Protection interventions promoting safety have clear links to reducing incidence of violence. Increasing access to health services for survivors of gender-based violence and relevant referrals between the protection and health clusters will ensure that the health consequences (both mental and physical) of gender-based violence are addressed. The health consequences of violence are mitigated through multisectoral service provision coordinated across the two sectors. The shelter and NFI clusters will impact health outcomes. Overcrowded settlements and camps can contribute to an increase in disease, while poor lighting and lack of safety considerations can contribute to risks of sexual violence, and poor site planning for hygiene and sanitation can lead to disease. Infrastructure investments in water and sanitation services play a critical role in multi-sectoral food security programming by addressing root causes of many humanitarian crises that stem from droughts, floods, and natural resource-driven conflict. Integrating WASH activities has many benefits in term of reducing the spread of water borne diseases which in turn has health and nutrition benefits. Mercy Corps will lever this multisectoral approach to enhance the outcomes and impacts of the proposed interventions. Target informationWithin the target area there are an estimated 62,039 People in Need (PIN) which represents approximately 28% of the population. The number of vulnerable residents is also increasing. In 2018, the number of vulnerable residents was recorded as 26,360 while the current estimate for 2021 is now estimated to be 40,686. Needs assessments estimate that the target localities have 32,924 people with disabilities and 14,390 elderly people (65+). GBV - the target localities are suffering from a range of GBV issues including child marriage, child labour, IPV, SGBV, rape, FGM and other harmful practices. An estimated 11,468 PiN are in need of GBV support and assistance within the target localities. Ar Rashad has the highest caseload with 5,407 PiN needing GBV assistance. Nutrition - the targeted communities suffer from a range of nutrition problems related to poor food security including high rates of SAM and MAM. An estimated 20,542 PiN are in need of Nutrition support Health - the state has poor indicators in terms of health. Within the target localities, the last nutrition survey in 2018 identified an estimated cases of GAM 17,699. An estimated 35,815 PiN are in need of Health services within the target localities. WASH - the targeted communities suffer from a range of WASH problems including the availability of water, water quality and poor hygiene / sanitation conditions.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, Mercy Corpswill abide by Do No Harm principles. MC will also strive for gender balance in therecruitment of staff at all levels and will train officers, responsible for deliveringservices and inputs to participants, on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches.Mercy Corps’ in-house gender expertise will provide a solid foundation for the program.Recognizing the key role of women in agriculture and household nutrition, a minimumof 40 percent of participants will be women. Specifically under WASH interventions, inthe target locations, water collection is typically the responsibility of women and girls.Limited access to water sources forces women and girls to walk long distances insearch of water, often exposing them to additional protection risks. This interventionwill help to reduce these risks through rehabilitating infrastructure which will increasethe availability of water and reduce the distance women and girls are forced to walk tocollect water. In addition, Mercy Corps will ensure that women and girls are representedin the community-level WASH committees – minimum 50 percent of WASH committeemembers. Mercy Corps plans to encourage the involvement of women and girls in allelements of the community-based operation and maintenance of the water sources.Ongoing discussions with some of the targeted communities have clearly shown thatwomen are more interested than men in taking key roles in the implementation andmanagement of the proposed user fee. As part of the user fee implementation strategy,Mercy Corps will form an advisory board with women taking key decision-makingpositions regarding rates, exemptions and managing collected money.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Mercy Corps places the issue of accountability at the core of this project design,implementation, monitoring and evaluation processes. Ensuring relevant, quality andtimely programming and interventions reflects this commitment. MC considers thetarget communities as leading the solutions for the humanitarian issues they face, andthus are regarded as key stakeholders in the design, implementation and monitoringand evaluation of the project. MC will work through existing community structurescomprising of a representative of women, youth and community leaders to representcommunities during project implementation. Due to the limitation of gatherings asresult of COVID-19, the information will mainly be shared through public media,posters, call centers, community microphone information broadcasts, and the use of apublic address system in the target areas. Information dissemination will be adaptedto the context of COVID-19 to ensure actions contribute to containment. Rather thangathering large groups, some activities will be done through a variety of approaches,including voice messaging, to cater to the vast majority who are illiterate. The projectwill use participatory, gender- and age-sensitive monitoring approaches to ensure girls,boys, women, and men, as well as CBOs and services providers, are involved centrallyin the process of project follow-up, provide their feedback and concerns on the projectperformance and are informed about decisions made in community meetings anddiscussions. MC will provide timely, relevant, and precise information about theorganization including feedback and complaints mechanisms. Communities andbeneficiaries will be kept up to date in changes in feedback mechanisms and will beprovided with information on how they can feedback remotely, such as via hotline,WhatsApp etc. Information sharing will be via different formats to ensure informationis available promptly while taking into account language barriers and literacy status –such as flyers and posters left in communities, health facilities, hospitals etc. withsimple and clear messages/standards and accountability assigned a number to allproject locations. Mercy Corps’ functional Complaints and Response Mechanism willcollect feedback where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, and a dedicated call-innumber – any meetings held (where still possible) will be in open air locations, inspaces large enough for distancing; and otherwise can be conducted remotely.Complaints/feedback will be captured, categorized, and shared with relevant staff forresolution and logged into the action plan trackers. Depending on the type of feedbackand complaint, some will immediately be closed through sharing the requiredinformation (different methods dependent on scale of response – face to face usingdistancing, telephone etc.). At the same time, for some, an investigation will beundertaken to verify the facts before a concrete decision is made.
