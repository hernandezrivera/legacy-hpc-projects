Project Summary

Across Sudan, GBV continues to be a life-threatening concern, exacerbated by a variety of factors including armed conflict, tribal clashes, forced displacement, and recurring disasters such as floods The situation in Sudan change since November 2022, on 15 of April the fight started between the SAF and RSF, hundreds of people has been killed, forced more than 218,000 to flee across borders and displaced over 730,000 people inside the country in the first four weeks of the conflict, hence 4.2 million People in are need of GBV services, out of which the GBV Sub Sector is targeting 1.3 million for GBV prevention, mitigation and response, focusing mainly on women and girls from localities that are affected by the recent conflict and tribal clashes, hosting refugees as well as disasters, with a high percentage of households engaged in negative coping mechanisms .. The severity of protection needs is very critical in 131 localities across 12 states in Sudan. UNFPA intends to target 100% the GBV subsector target of the PiN in severity ranking 5, 50% in severity ranking 4 and approximately 30% of PiN of the GBV SS target in severity ranking 3.
The project pursues and focus on the following 3 objectives in the coming 7 months:
Provide life- saving specialized quality GBV services, including CMR, case management and psychosocial support, and reinforce referral pathways. (With a focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls) (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 1), through the provision of different services to vulnerable women & girls including GBV survivors (Case management, PSS, referrals), assessments, community awareness-raising, and programming, provision of dignity kits, and capacity building of GBV frontline actors.
To promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 3) by training on GBV for Non GBV actors and to strengthen GBV coordination mechanism at national and sub-national level and strengthen data collection and information management

1 - Needs

Across Sudan GBV continues to be a life-threatening concern, exacerbated by a variety of factors including armed conflict, tribal clashes, forced displacement, and recurring disasters such as floods. It is estimated that there are 4.2 million people in need after the start of the clashes between SAF and RSF on the 15th of April for GBV prevention, mitigation and response, including mainly women and girls from localities that are affected by the conflict and tribal clashes, with a high percentage of IDPs engaged in negative coping mechanisms. Vulnerable women, girls and at-risk groups in Sudan face heightened threats of GBV driven by the ongoing conflict and subsequent displacement, as well as loss of income, non-availability of cash, skyrock¬eting prices for basic commodities, and limited access to basic services and support. IDPs and conflict-affected women and girls face increased risk of GBV, including sexual violence during displacement and in temporary accommodations, exacerbated by the increased presence of armed actors. Domestic violence risks have increased with limitations on mobility, increased stressors and decreased access to basic needs, as well as vulnerability to sexual and other forms of exploitation. Those are on the move and crossing borders are exposed to increased risks of GBV and exploitation. Unaccompanied adolescent girls and youth may be unable to cross the border due to a lack of documentation. New states hosting displaced people require urgent GBV response capacity. Additional needs are emerging in areas with pre-existing humanitarian crises and now hosting a significant number of displaced persons, People with special needs, survivors of sexual violence as well as sexual exploitation and abuse, and at-risk boys and men, may face additional challenges in accessing life-saving GBV services. Widespread gaps in access to life-saving essential services for GBV survivors have been severely exacerbated in conflict-affected states. Most of the hospitals and health facilities that provide the CMR are out of services, there is a critical shortage of supplies across Sudan, including Rape Treatment Kits and Dignity Kits including menstrual hygiene, due to ongoing hostil-ities in Khartoum, the main procurement hub in Sudan. The severity of protection needs is very critical in 130 localities across 12 states in Sudan (HNO 2023), these localities are needier now due to the effect of the recent conflict in allover Sudan even if they don’t receive additional IDPs. UNFPA will continue providing the GBV prevention and response services to all vulnerable women, adolescent girls, men and boys. Sudan has one of the highest prevalence in harmful practices globally. Among girls aged 20-24 years, 60.2 % were first married or in union before the age of 18, and 87% of women aged 15–49 years have been subjected to Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) (UNICEF, S3MⅡ 2018). Many of the drivers underpinning FGM and child marriage tend to exacerbate during crisis. In particular, faced with insecurity, breakdown of the rule of law, and disruptions in social networks and family routines, families and parents may see child marriage as a coping mechanism that deals with increased economic hardship, protects girls from sexual violence, preserves virginity, and protects the honor of the families, including management of rape. FGM is frequently seen as a preparation for marriage.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Provide life- saving specialized quality GBV services, including CMR, case management and psychosocial support, and reinforce referral pathways. (With a focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls) - Support civil society including women led organizations, and government actors to provide GBV specialized survivor-centered services including clinical management of rape and other health services; psychosocial support (PFA, individual & group PSS, specialized MH services); appropriate, quality case management services including coordinated care and support to navigate available services as well as referral services to connect GBV survivors to appropriate, quality, multi-sectoral services in a timely, safe and confidential manner. To overcome the accessibility challenges, especially in conflict-affected areas, assistance will be provided in different formats, including through an e-platforms (remote case management and Psychological Support Services), mobile and static service delivery points, and mobile teams for hard-to-reach communities and state level hotlines when feasible. Capacity building for service providers, community initiatives, and women groups, etc (remotely as necessary). 2. Establish and support Women and Girls Safe Spaces (temporal and static) to provide, accessible quality services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment - Support community based protection networks and strengthen capacity of community members / initiatives involved in GBV prevention and response ,skill building activities Community awareness on GBV and harmful practices and social and gender norms change communication to promote gender equality and reduce GBV risks 3. Promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response - Advocate for and support the integration of GBV risk mitigation and survivor support across humanitarian sectors and strengthen capacity of non-GBV service providers - Women and girls receive dignity kits to reduce GBV risk and promote safety and dignity 4. To strengthen GBV Coordination mechanism at national and sub-national level and strengthen data collection and information management - GBV Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and locality-level referral pathways operationalized - Coordinated GBV intervention strengthened at state level by strategic action plans and capacity building of implementing partners (remotely as necessary) - Conduct GBV assessments and strengthen mechanisms for safe and ethical data collection.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Designed interventions and activities considered the impact on the environment with mitigation and enhancement process, the planned women centers – if supported - will be rehabilitated using to the extent possible the no environmentally harmful materials. The promotion of fuel- -efficient stoves - if supported - will contribute to the improved livelihood of vulnerable households while reducing environmental impact as fewer trees are cut for cooking fuel. In addition, during the dignity kits distribution, women and girls will be sensitized to environmentally friendly ways of disposing of the used sanitary wear. Through the women and girls’ empowerment program, the GBV AoR partners will explore the production of reusable sanitary pads as a replacement for disposable ones. the movement to remote villages will always consider the seasonal changes as during autumn movement is difficult to a number of states and localities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is core in the UNFPA response and improved through continuous consultation with the affected population on implementation modalities as well as to their satisfaction with services provided. UNFPA is an active member of the national PSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team and together is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanisms are accessible to survivors of PSEA. UNFPA will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the PSEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. UNFPA’s partners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regard to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities. This is done by conducting a focus group discussion with women and girls in women centres. The project will support the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improving beneficiaries' daily lives through improved formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequality as the root cause of GBV
