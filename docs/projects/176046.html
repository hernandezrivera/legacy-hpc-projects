Project Summary

Despite the relative calm in the country since the signing of peace in September 2018 & the subsequent partial formation of the Revitalized governments in 2020, access to health services has never improved. Protracted communal conflicts, cattle raiding due to lack of rule of laws, economic hardship coupled with flooding & COVID-19 effects have continuously devastated the lives and wellbeing of people in 2020 & expected to worsen in 2021. Jonglei & Upper Nile states are among the states with the highest health access gaps and the already weak health care system infrastructures in the counties have additional waves of devastation by triple shocks in 2020 causing displacements, hence more hindrance on access & utilization of services. IPC analysis of Oct 2020-Jul 2021 based on the data from the nutrition monitoring system, SMART surveys, & the trend of admission in 2020, reported 1.4 Million under5 children expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021, the highest since the eruption of conflict in 2013. Multi-sectoral IRNA conducted by partners including CMD in Akobo West, July 2020 reported lack of static health partner. Data from the KIs interviews reported common morbidities in the area as Malaria, AWD, ARI & some communicable diseases such as HIV & TB plus the NTD as Kala-azar.
87% of locations this project targets are in IPC phase4 & 11,000 persons expected to be in IPC pahse5 in Akobo county alone. The continuous rise of water levels, lack of latrines in the displaced settlements (highlands) increases the risk of outbreaks of waterborne diseases as Acute Watery Diarrhea/Cholera, an increasing surge of malaria. The absence of EPI services increases the risk of outbreaks for vaccine-preventable childhood illnesses like measles & Poliomyelitis. COVID-19 preparedness & response requires scaling up of services to include the provision of sufficient spaces in the health facilities to ensure adherence to social distancing, provide PPEs to frontline health workers. WASH facilities as part of IPC. Multi-sectoral humanitarian assistances particularly the essential health care services therefore needed to save lives and avert more suffering in these counties in 2021.
CMD, an NNGO with over 15years of experience in providing humanitarian & developmental health services has vast expertise in the local context and a significant presence sub-nationally. CMD plans to implement this project in Akobo, Ayod, Bor South, Duk, Canal/Pigi, Ulang, Kapoeta North & Guit counties. This project aimed at directly targeting 37,500 host communities & IDPs including 19,125 women & girls, 6,750 under5 children, 5,625 persons with disabilities through static health care facilities, mobile outreaches & community-based case management with an estimated cost of 5 Million USD. Disease prevention, detection, surveillance and reporting, case management & BEmONCs services where Skilled Birth Attendants deliver 80% live births. 1,875 mothers receive ANC4 visits & post-natal services. 80% of SGBV survivors will receive Clinical Management of Rape, basic psychosocial support services & a strengthened referral system. Deployment of qualified health workers to maintain & continuously improve the quality of care to mothers of childbearing age, children & persons with disabilities. Beneficiaries, stakeholders’ involvement & capacity building of CHDs and health workers. Inter-sectoral synergies with WASH, Protection & GBV, Food Security & Livelihood & Nutrition will be incorporated through close coordination mechanism. Use EWARS and weekly IDSR system of reporting to monitor the morbidities and trends of outbreaks. Utilize the HMIS & other internally improvised tools to ensure the quality & consistency of data. Obtain beneficiaries` feedback by supporting the existing Health Facility Committees and establish committees in health facilities that do not currently have. Install suggestion boxes in the health facilities & CMD offices, organize FGD as part of AAP.

1 - Needs

Since independence in 2011, South Sudan never cease dependency on humanitarian aides. Conflicts, lack of basic infrastructure, lack of stable economy that supports investments on the basic services have constantly been subjecting the people to unending sufferings over the years. The formation of the R-TGNU started in February 2020 but still noted completed a year later. In 2020, South Sudan hit by triple shocks of protracted conflicts that include cattle raiding and revenge killings, the major flooding in years & COVID-19 pandemic. These & other factors severed access to optimal health services more especially in Jonglei and Upper Nile regions. Over 800,000 people reportedly displaced by flooding from July 2020. 1,500 civilians including humanitarian workers killed during the first half of 2020. 6,000 GBV incidents reported of which 97% of the survivors were females. First case of COVID-19 confirmed in April & 3,511 confirmed COVID-19 cases reported by week 52 of 2020 including 63 deaths & a case fatality rate of 2.0%. The country experienced a surge in COVID-19 cases in January 2021 & by the 3rd of February 2021 the National Task-force announced partial lockdown for a period of one month and encouraged people to observe prevention and control measures to curve the spread of the pandemic. Testing, isolation, active case finding, contact tracing & case management mostly happen in Juba while only the risk communication & community engagement are being applied at the sub-national levels though on a very limited scale as well due to lack of adequate resources at partners` disposal.HNO 2021 projected 8.3 Million people to be in need of humanitarian assistance, a 10% rise compared to 2020. 5.2 Million People will be in need of health services, an increase of over 30% compared to 2020. Women of childbearing age & children bear the brunt of this humanitarian suffering & are projected to make 24% and 54% respectively of the people in need in 2021. IPC analysis Oct 2020-Jul 2021 projected many counties in Jonglei & Upper Nile at IPC phase4 while Akobo & Pibor counties at IPC phase5. 1.4 Million Children & 483,383 PLW expected to suffer from acute malnutrition with proxy GAM prevalence above the 15% threshold in 47 counties.Globally South Sudan is among countries with worse health indicators. Maternal mortality rates of 789 deaths per 100,000 live births, under5 mortality rate of 90.7 deaths per 1,000 live birth according to UN Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation, 2019. One physician for every 65,574 people according to WHO report on access to health services. Humanitarian & developmental partners support 57% of the functioning health facilities and only 44% of the population live within 5 km of a health facility. There is a limited diagnostic capacity for communicable diseases such as HIV & TB. There are significant pockets of NTDs as kala azar in Jonglei & Upper Nile & several incidents of snakebites due to too much water in the area. The impact of COVID-19 prevention measures strains the provision of essential health services in 2020 & expected to result in higher health needs in 2021. Final health cluster PiN calculation in 2021 depicts 100% of the population in Canal/Pigi & Guit counties & between 50%-80% in Akobo & other counties. IRNA conducted in Bor South where CMD participated in August 2020 reported 91% of the population do not have latrines and therefore practice open defecation. 89% of the population never practice water treatment before use. 84% do not have washing facilities. Data collected through KI interviews with representatives from the IDPs reported a high prevalence of malaria & AWD amidst inadequate drugs, medical supplies & qualified health workers in the area. RRC and The partners who participated during this assessment recommended scaling up of health services delivery with a special focus on immunisation, health promotion, awareness & establishment of more access points in the county.

2 - Project activities

• Provide routine immunization services for common preventable childhood illness through static and mobile outreaches in 8counties • Conduct timely disease surveillance and outbreaks detecting, reporting and response within 72hours of occurrence • Conduct OPD curative consultations for common morbidities including management of non-communicable diseases in 14PHCCs, 26PHCUs and 10mobile units.• Support laboratory capacity to undertake testing, counseling and referral for communicable and Neglected Tropical diseases including inpatient case management in the PHCCs• Provide Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services, referral and necessary counselling to the SGBV survivors in 8counties • Support Infection Prevention and Control for COVID-19 through risk communication and community engagement, triaging, screening, case detection and isolation at the health facilities• Provide ANC, delivery by Skilled Birth Attendants and Post-natal services at the health facilities including BEmONC and CEmONC centers• Provide basic psychosocial support and mental health services at the health facilities • Support WASH in health facilities through rehabilitation/construction and installation of latrines, incinerators and washing facilities • Conduct MUAC screening and referral of malnourished children and Pregnant & Lactating Women to the nutrition care centers • Support the installation of boxes for feedback mechanism in the health facilities and the organization`s offices in the area• Enhance the capacity and knowledge of health workers and the County Department (CHD) officials through formal and in-services trainings • Procurement of drugs, medical supplies, laboratory reagents and other consumables • Support sub-national humanitarian coordination and reporting activities

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CMD has PSEA policies and operational guidelines to protect every beneficiary and hold any culprit liable of any attempt of sexual exploitation and abuse. The project staffs with support from the Senior management will organize orientation sessions to all employee in this regard before they engage with the beneficiaries. Organize awareness sessions for the beneficiaries, women leaders, community leaders, law enforcement agents and every stakeholder in the counties on what this project considers as sexual exploitation, channels of reporting and the available resources within the organization. Employees sign code of conduct and PSEA guidelines as part of their contractual obligation toward the organization and their beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project staffs will organize individual and group meetings but to avoid discussing any gender sensitive issue in a group of mixed sex. Also, considering the age so that beneficiaries share their views without any fear of repercussion. Identity of those who provide feedbacks from individuals and/or different groups of beneficiaries be kept confidential and considering the patriarchal nature of the society in South Sudan women and girls will have separate avenues to avoid intimation from men.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project staffs will support the existing Health Committees per each health facility and ensure to establish committees where they do not exist. Use the minutes of their meetings to track how the beneficiaries perceive the progress of the project implementation. Use the Focus Group Discussion and individual conversations as part of getting feedback from the beneficiaries and suggestion boxes stationed in the health facilities and CMD offices so that the beneficiaries who are able to read write use this means to share their feedback

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Any feedback from the beneficiaries will instantly be analyzed and accordingly incorporated into the project implementation modalities during subsequent project review meetings with the stakeholders. In case the feedback doesn`t fit into the existing resources, then the organization will use it as part of resources mobilization strategy and the beneficiaries will also be informed on what they have reported and what the organization is doing to address them.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project staffs will work closely with local authority and community leaders and request surveillance and escorting of the beneficiaries in roads considered risky for few people especially women and girls.The PSEA focal persons will continuously engage the project staffs and other stakeholders at the sites of project implementation to ensure their uninterrupted access to services. Regular feedback will also be sought from women, girls and persons with disability to understand from them barriers and risk associated with access to health care services and corrective actions be taken accordingly.The organization will conduct self-assessment and evaluation about the policies, Standard Operation Procedures, guidelines and existing mechanisms before the launch of the project implementation. Once gaps are identified, the organization will allocate to resources to filling those gaps as part of the preparatory phase of the project implementation.The organization will work closely with other humanitarian actors in the counties who have expertise in other fields to ensure a coordinated humanitarian assistance. A coordination platform under the leadership of County Health Department and other relevant sectors in the counties will be supported so that partners come together and plan on how to tackle programmes impediments.The project will put in place mitigation measures that include organizing separate engagement approaches with vulnerable groups of the beneficiaries and directly pick up their views on how they would expect the project to address their felt needs. The project staffs will be tasked to continuously meet these vulnerable groups individually and/or in groups to understand what they think the project is achieving and what can be adjusted to fit their needs. Awareness sessions on how and where beneficiaries share their concerns will be organized during the inception period of the project implementation and periodic evaluations on the progress will be done so that any barrier is addressed and rectified. Employees will receive comprehensive orientation sessions before they begin their engagement with the beneficiaries and the management through HR department will convey uncompromised directives in that regard. Supervisors will keep checking their subordinates and any breach on the right and dignity of the beneficiaries will be dealt with impartially in accordance with existing rules of engagement.A periodic review of the project and community needs will be organized with the involvement of the direct beneficiaries to ensure timely correction of any mismatch.The organization will involve every stakeholder and sensitize people to follow due process in accordance with the existing laws governing the land. Advocacy and awareness shall conducted to encouraging peaceful resolution and minimizing harm on individuals, groups and the community.The organization will support advocacy and awareness on the protection against explosive hazard and contamination and ensure that relevant stakeholders receive the information timely to remove and/or reduce harm on individuals, groups or community.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This project will incorporate expertise from different fields to ensure timely access to services. SGBV survivors will get clinical management of rape, necessary counseling, and basic psychosocial and mental health services. Resources will also be availed to support advocacy and community awareness against SGBV and access to justice for the survivors.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This project will prioritize the prevention and health promotion activities. Beneficiaries will be engaged on the importance of having latrines at the households, handwashing after using toilets and before eating or handling food to avoid exposure to diarrheal diseases that could further jeopardize their health and make them more vulnerable. In collaboration with relevant stakeholders, the beneficiaries will continuously be sensitized and encouraged to engage in activities that strengthen their coping mechanism and not relying entirely on humanitarian handouts.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The target beneficiaries for this project will be disaggregated by sex, age, persons with disability and any deviation from the planned target will rectified accordingly, and barriers for each group will continuously be evaluated using feedback mechanisms and addressed throughout the project implementation

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will ensure MUAC screening for under5 children who seek medical care at the health facilities and refer all malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites. Community Health Workers who are part of the Community based Case Management and the mobile team will do MUAC screen at the community level, refer and later trace and track any defaulter from the nutrition sites. Health awareness sessions on the dietary messaging and nutritious feeding of children be also organized as part of this project implementation strategy.
