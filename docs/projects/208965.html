Project Summary

Concern Worldwide is proposing to provide emergency response in conflict affected population in West Darfur, West and South Kordofan, and Red Sea state in 2024. The intervention links to the Emergency Shelter/NFI cluster response strategy by distributing NFIs and shelter kits mainly in violate hotspots locations (target: 3,500 NFI kits, 900 shelter kits) in West Darfur and the Kordofans, community shelters improvements in urban-centers (target: 6 centers), and cash-based assistance for locations where programme beneficiaries can adapt to the diverse housing needs (target:1000 HHs) in Kordofan, and Red Sea states.
Targeting will follow the cluster approach and will prioritize localities where needs critical, and response were limited in the last period. Overall, the response will focus on the cluster recommended localities and states, however there are some areas e.g. Red Sea State (Port Sudan locality) with existing needs and gaps, therefor response will be utilized in these locations, as well, subject to funding. Khartoum state is also our top priority for 2024, however due to the active war in the state, and no access for humanitarian response currently, we plan for a 'flexible approach' meaning once access is granted we will respond by providing NFIs however if the needs changes, we may change the response to Shelter or Community Shelter support. Currently we assume NFI is the best option for Khartoum, however this may change over the course of the year. We continue engaging with the cluster and the humanitarian community, as well as authorities and advocate for access in Khartoum and other isolated places in Kordofans.
Concern Worldwide will mainstream cross cutting issues such as S/GBV risks mitigation, conflict-sensitive targeting to minimize exploitation that are usually associated with low standards shelter provision, lack of privacy, and overcrowding. In order to reduce these risks, flexible programming e.g. using cash-based support instead of shelter kits, joint PDM for service deliver monitoring etc. will be applied. Based on Concern's current present in West Darfur, West and South Kordofan and Red Sea states, response will mobilize local networks, available social structures especially in places where IDPs settled with local/host community population.
Concern Worldwide emergency shelter/NFI response plan follows the cluster agreed unit values, although local prices and availability of resources will be considered, and may play an impact during roll out of the action.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies.Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protectprogramme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the keyconcept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communitieswe assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within thesecommunities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities asa consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child ProtectionPolicy that will be applied for this intervention. To ensure appropriate protectionmainstreaming, Concern will apply international and internal program qualitymonitoring standard procedures to target most vulnerable persons of concern with afocus on women, children, elderly, and persons living with disabilities. Protectionprinciples such as CRM tools which will be complemented by international guidelines,Sphere standards and Camp Management Tool kit to ensure accountability to, andparticipation of, affected population. Principles of protection mainstreaming will beexercised, including: meaningful access through consultation and involvement ofcommunities about the best way to deliver services and consideration for vulnerablegroups, and accountability mechanisms. Protection / (S)GBV risks faced by womenand girls will be core focus of capacity building for staff and volunteers to ensure theyare able to detect cases of abuse, recognize and refer to protection-mandatedentities. NFI kits will contain dignity items for women and girls; shelters will besegregated and well lit for protection, with women-lead households receivingassistance for construction. Post-Distribution Monitoring will be conducted, andtimely reports submitted to UNHCR in order to reconcile distributed kits and evaluatethe effectiveness/efficiency of the intervention, with specific regard to communityfeedback through complaint and response mechanisms (CRM)

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

CRM will be collected by staff (CRM officer) and will be shared with programme team (except sensitive complains) and each complain will be responded. If the complain will be sensitive, the CRM office will work with the Safeguarding and Accountability Coordinator in the organziation to address the issue in a separate protocol system to protect the individual. The programme team will use the feedback to adjust e.g. the contents of the NFI/Shelter kits, improve quality of the contents etc. After distribution of services, particularly the NFI/Shelter kits, there will be a PDM (post-distribution monitoring) for each activity to visit the beneficiaries (in community or HH level), and listen the feedbacks of the services, in-kind, cash-based support. Feedbacks from the project participants will help not only to address the particular complaint, feedback, but also to improve the quality of programme. Information (non-confidential) will be shared with cluster members to further improve the quality of services as well as adjust the programme to the eve-changing context of Sudan. During the PDM, sensitive information will be gathered following safeguarding and protection protocols, and response/referral of these cases will be separately managed.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by two central documents, notably the organisation’s Programme Participant Protection Policy and Code of Conduct; and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Standard in Accountabilityand Quality Management. Concern’s Programme Participant Protection Policy sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries –children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist e.g. gender based violence due to lack of safety shelter, stealing NFIs from beneficiaries ect.; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities such as harassments while walking to NFI distribution site, iii) the risks our programme may create such as distributing NFI/Shelter kits only for displaced people not to consider other vulnerable groups.As certified member of HAP, Concern is committed to implementing the HAP Standard related to different aspects of beneficiaries’ accountability. Three of these aspects are particularly relevant to conflict sensitivity and protection: beneficiaries’ access to information (e.g. how beneficiary selection procedure conducted) ; beneficiaries’ participation in all the stages of the programme (e.g. from objective of an intervention, selection procedure, implementation till evaluation and reporting); and establishing a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for each community/place where the grant will be implemented. In 2022, Concern Sudan’s Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) was updated and aligned with country specific needs and priorities, and communities’ expectations, resulted the adjustment of the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Concern will incorporate beneficiaries’ feedback through two ways: 1) PDM after (max.) one month by the accountability team mainly FGDs, and HH visits, and 2) interviews with other stakeholders, programme participants contributed to the activity e.g. NFI/Shelter kit distribution, cash for rent activities. Feedbacks and complaints feedbacks will use Satisfaction and Consultation form. Based on data received from these two sources, Concern will regularly evaluate and re-design the CRM system to improve accessibility, responsiveness, efficiency and protection of the participants. Sensitive complaints (GBV risk, SGBV risk) will be managed through a separate CRM system to ensure safeguarding of the individual. The Accountability and Safeguarding Coordinator will handle these cases through a separate, data protection system. CWW has done a protection services mapping in 2022 that is currently under update. This referral system will be used to provide support and response to S/GBV survivals, as per standard protocol.
