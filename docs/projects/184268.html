Project Summary

The social impacts of COVID-19 pandemic continue to ravage the fragile protection systems with the GBV AoR together with partners reporting increased negative coping mechanisms to the economic challenges faced by the communities. The heightened political tensions especially in the south together with forced evictions of households continues to exacerbate the dire living conditions of vulnerable conditions and worsening exposure to GBV cases. Access to GBV services to men, boys, women and girls and more so to the elderly and people living with disability is limited in rural communities. Despite the collaboration with health sector to strengthen medical services for GBV survivors, the high turnover of health staff presents a gap in clinical management of rape capacity. Assessments conducted by WVI show a gap in knowledge of the communities on available GBV services especially among IDPs and school going adolescents particularly the boys. The same assessments also indicate limited awareness on MHPSS services for survivors of sexual violence, FGM and other forms of GBV. The situation is worse for men and boys because of societal stigma attached to GBV. It is against this background that WVI is proposing to provide well-coordinated, age-appropriate and disability-sensitive quality GBV response services in 2022. This will be achieved under the broad themes of prevention, mitigation and response where WVI has physical presence and ongoing interventions in complementary sectors of Child Protection, Health, WASH and FSL. 1. - Projects activities

The overall objective of the project is that vulnerable women and girls (including those living with disabilities and from minority clans) from, IDP camps and host communities have access to safe, confidential, timely and quality coordinated GBV/FGM services through integrated community referrals. To achieve this, WVI will leverage on existing health facilities and human resource to strengthen the component of medical support to survivors of SGBV. The project has invested in building capacity of the community based GBV committees and faith leaders to create awareness and engage communities of GBV prevention, timely response and group PSS activities. Community meetings will be done with adherence to COVID-19 protocols. Through case management, individual direct support will be accorded to GBV survivors using survivor centered approaches. The GBV AoR updated service mapping will be used to disseminated through various mechanisms to promote timely access to services. In addition, the project will ensure services are available in close proximity to the community while taking consideration of barriers to uptake of services e.g. stigma associated with SGBV affecting men and boys. Capacity building of health worker will also include gender sensitivity and ensuring both male and female staff form part of the trainees. To reach the elderly and people living with disability, outreaches and home visits will be used to provide specialized support. In order to reach the young people and adolescents on GBV prevention, creative methods will be used to share messages including on SRH. 2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CTP will enable target beneficiaries to have greater choice and dignity by allowing them tohave purchasing power and ability to prioritize individual needs, while at the same time stimulating local economy and improving the market cash flow by promoting local businesses and providing a source of livelihoods which is a factor in prevention of GBV. WVI will prioritize cash transfers to channel material assistance to GBV survivors for procurement of supplies and unique gender needs as opposed to providing a standard in kind package.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

WVI is a member of the national cash working group and participates in market functionality assessments conducted by the FSL projects already existing in the proposed districts covered by this project.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. Risk of Minority clans/tribes excluded from humanitarians assistance and consultations This risk will be addressed by providing for multiple awareness raising mechanisms on available project services including factoring in mobile outreaches to far and hard to reach locations to ensure timely access to services. To understand the demographics within the districts, assessments will be conducted which will inform targeting and beneficiary selection criteria. A mapping of the availableGBV service providers alongside the telephone of their focal points will be displayed in public places accessible to all such as health facilities and other social amenities like schools in the Somali language. 2. Risk women and girls exposure to SEA in relation to accessing humanitarian services WVI already has an existing community response mechanism which will be integrated inthis project to increase access to feedback and complaints options existing in the project areas. The toll free number 364 will be displayed in all service delivery sites in the Somali language. Monitoring of the response to complaints in this system usually forms part of the senior leadership team monthly updates until the issue raised is resolved. Suggestion boxes will continue to be used in schools alongside the setting up of help desks where boys and girls share concerns about WVI service delivery including reporting SEA concerns. WVI also has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy that strongly communicated WVI's zero tolerance to SEA which all staff are trained on annually and sign a self declaration to adhere to the behaviour protocols which covers PSEA guidelines. Staff found culpable of SEA usually face termination of contract. The various means to reports all forms of staff misconduct is usually shared during community meetings which includes reporting to trained safeguarding focal points, and anonymous toll free line to WVI Global Safeguarding Unit. 3. Risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance and indiscriminate attacks from armed actors. WV staff has a fulltime security team (composed of a Security Advisor and field-based Security Officers)who continuously monitor the security context, liaising closely with International NGOSafety Organization (INSO) Somalia and the local security apparatus to keep abreastof the evolving context and make informed decisions. The project will also make use of staff weekly updates on the regional security situation to inform activity implementation. GBV risk assessments will also be conducted by the project teams prior to rolling out activities e.g. cash transfers to reduce these risks.
