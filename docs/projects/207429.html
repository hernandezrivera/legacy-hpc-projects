Project Summary

This project solely targets people with disabilities, including individuals with physical, visual, hearing, and psychosocial impairments. Individuals with disabilities will receive assistive devices, home, and shelter modifications, and benefit from accessible service centers in the community.
CBM and CDD’s current project takes place in the same geographical locations as the proposed project locations: camps 8W, 8E, 11, 13, 18, and 19 and Rajapalong, Ratanapalong, and Holdiapalong Unions in the host community. No other agencies provide the same/similar services in the proposed project locations.
Capacity building of Site Management and Site Development: CBMG and CDD provided technical support to IOM and its implementing partners CARE and DRC on capacity building on inclusive Camp Coordination and Camp Management. The capacity-building support will extend in the proposed six camps.
Home and community accessibility modifications: The project will provide assistive devices and home/shelter modifications for 1,000 people with disabilities and will promote service center accessibility in the community. Providing this support will improve health for people living in already difficult conditions, in makeshift shelters, on rough terrain, and with limited healthcare services. Without these services, people with disabilities are unable to participate in their community and are a burden on family members who have become carers. These services will improve their functional abilities and allow for greater independence.
Accessibility at the public and communal Facilities: To create access to different services for people with different types of disabilities, the project aims to conduct accessibility audits and install accessibility features (e.g., ramps with handrails, accessible WASH facility modifications) in three public offices, two health centers, six primary and secondary schools in the host community, and 12 communal facilities in the Rohingya Camps.
Assistive Devices: A rehabilitation team will assess the clients to determine the types of assistive devices required. The project then will procure height-adjustable devices (e.g., crutches, walkers, toilet chairs) available in the local market and produce customized assistive devices (e.g., wheelchair, special seat chair, tricycle, prosthetic limbs) at CDD’s workshop and distribute to the clients. The materials of the assistive devices will consider the durability, cultural context, availability in the community, and appropriateness for the terrain.
Ultimately, this project will facilitate clients' ability to move, see, and communicate, help reduce the risk of secondary complications, and provide require support to clients for overcome environmental barriers in certain situations to better engage in community life. Through the proposed interventions, people with disabilities in the camps and host community will have greater access to humanitarian and social protection services, improved quality of life, better functional independence in their daily regular and productive activities, and increased participation in the community.
Documentation: The project will develop some knowledge management documentation along with a video documentation of the accessibility modifications that will be produced to showcase the work and to be used in training and advocacy to present examples of good practice to other actors, who will improve facilities and services for more people with disabilities across the camps.
The project will be managed within CBMG and CDD’s current Inclusive Rohingya Response Program providing interventions in five camps and three Union Parishad in the host community. CDD’s office in Ukhia will monitor operations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

A historic forced displacement of the Rohingya; ethnic, linguistic, and religious minorities from the northern Rakhine state of Myanmar has been occurred in 1978, 1992, 2012, and 2016. Bangladesh has generously welcomed Rohingya refugees temporarily on every occasion. The Rohingya have returned to their ancestral home in Rakhine State most of the time previously. The largest forced displacement from Myanmar into Bangladesh began in August 2017. As of 30 April 2023, 960,128 Rohingya refugees – largely women and children — are residing in thirty-three camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of the Cox’s Bazar District, as well as on the island of Bhasan Char. Within the Rohingya refugee camps in Bangladesh, people with disabilities encountered significant challenges due to the camps' constrained infrastructure and resources. People with disabilities often had limited access to essential services such as shelter, healthcare, education, and livelihood opportunities. Many lacked proper rehabilitation care, which led to dependence on family members and restricted their participation in camp activities. While community awareness regarding the rights of people with disabilities in the camp has improved, there remains a need for inclusivity across most services. Regrettably, the engagement and active participation of individuals with disabilities remain restricted. The most recent Multi-Sectoral Need Assessment (MSNA) 2023 stated that a subset of 8%host and 15%of refugee households with at least one member with a disability. 56% of the host community and 59% of refugee households reported having improved their shelter in the 12 months prior to data collection. Of the 41% of refugee households not improving their shelters, the majority reported not improving them due to lack of humanitarian support (60%) or insufficient support (34%). Refugee households in camps 20 (59%), 18 (58%), 8W (56%), and 4 (55%) were some of the most commonly reported not improving their shelters for those reasons. Of the 44% of host community households not improving their shelters, the majority reported not improving it due to it not being needed (45%) or lacking funds (43%). For a more in-depth and representative analysis of challenges for persons with disabilities, REACH conducted an Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment in May 2021 (available here). The Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment (ADINA) published by REACH in May 2021 which involved 2,530 household interviews found the following:- High proportions of persons with disabilities face barriers moving inside shelters and around the camp.- The inaccessibility of meeting venues created a barrier for persons with disabilities to participate in community meetings and events.- Physical accessibility of WASH facilities creates barriers for persons with disabilities to use them independently. The rapid Assistive Technology Assessment conducted by CBM Global and REACH in May 2021 found that 51% of persons with disabilities have unmet needs for assistive technology. The overall prevalence of disability across the camps is 12%. 50% of persons with disabilities have unmet needs for assistive devices, and only 1% have received support with home modifications to support their challenging living conditions. Self Help Groups (SHGs) and Disability Support Committees (DSCs) have also identified inaccessibility (e.g., no ramps, handrails) as a barrier to accessing government and humanitarian facilities and services.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is well aligned with the rationalization principles, in that it adopts a multi-sectorial approach which will entail active involvement with the ISCG engaging Humanitarian agencies operational in both Refugee and Host communities as well as the Local Authorities to mainstream disability. Project activities which include on-site technical support and capacity building of SHGs/DSCs and brokering of relationships with mainstream agencies will be well coordinated with the agencies in the camp and Host communities. The capacity building of SHGs/DSCs to advocate for their rights to meaningful participation in the response will influence disability inclusion in the long-term creating structures and systems to ensure representation of people with disabilities in Local government committees and various project committees to promote their rights by themselves for accessing different services. CBMG-CDD will work to ensure continuity of mainstreaming, influencing, advocacy, and technical support interventions. Through the project guidance notes, tip sheets, training modules, and IEC material will be developed and shared with sectors. CBMG-CDD will participate in ISCG coordination, PWG meetings, and Local Authority CiC meetings to ensure smooth coordination of activities in the host and refugee communities. The project will develop a risk matrix to identify, analyze, and mitigate against likely risks. The project will share monthly monitoring and project update reports with the SCCCM sector.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection will be a cross-cutting theme throughout the project cycle. The project will adopt the four protection principles of prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment. The project will be designed and adapted to ensure that it is inclusive of and accessible to everyone, including persons with disabilities, older people, and gender-diverse populations. The project will also identify a Protection Focal Point to report protection issues in the project and ensure staff participate in protection training and adhere to protection guidelines.

3.3 - Age & Disability

At least 12% of the project’s participant target will be people with disabilities. CBMG-CDD conducted prevalence surveys using the Washington Group Short Set of Questions Enhanced (WGSS-E). The surveys recorded prevalence rates in their programs of between 6% and 13%. These results led the partners to decide on a conservative target for 2024, hoping that this target can be exceeded by leveraging existing, improving data collection using the correct tools, and proactively engaging persons with disabilities to create a snowball effect. A comprehensive GEDSI action plan will be developed in the inception phase. Across all outcomes, WGSS-E will be used to identify persons with disabilities. The Self-Help Groups (SHGs) in the host communities and Disability Support Committees (DSCs) in the camps, developed, will be strengthened; SHG/DSC members will be direct beneficiaries and will contain at least 50% persons with disabilities per group as per the Convention of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) requirements. These groups/committees will be enabled through regular meetings, training, and linkage building with GoB departments and with local Organizations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs), supported by CBMG/CDD. Implementing partners will receive training in disability inclusion, ensuring they can support and empower persons with disabilities to understand their rights and effectively participate in all project activities by making activities accessible and providing reasonable accommodations. All facilities will be modified/retrofitted to ensure the service is inclusive and accessible. Based on specific needs, persons with disabilities will be referred to CDD for rehabilitation and Assistive Technology, if required.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Upholding AAP principles ensures that the unique needs, concerns, and preferences of the affected population, particularly people with disabilities, are at the forefront of project planning and execution. This involves fostering transparent communication, engaging in inclusive decision-making processes, and continuously seeking feedback from the affected individuals. By prioritizing the AAP guidelines, the project aims to enhance accountability, and effectiveness, ultimately contributing to the creation of accessible and dignified living spaces for people with disabilities in the Rohingya context. CBMG-CDD will ensure accountability by establishing inclusive complaints, feedback, and response mechanisms that proactively capture systematic feedback from beneficiaries with disabilities and set strategies to remove barriers accordingly. The iFRM will follow the following five steps. Step 1: Embed inclusive feedback & and reporting (iFRM) mechanism in all new projects.Step 2: Analyze the community profile and local context.Step 3: Consult with communities.Step 4: Decide on the most appropriate feedback and reporting channels.Step 5: Develop a plan of action for handling feedback and training staff. The choices & requirements of the People with disabilities will be prioritized during installation of any accessibility features at their shelter. CBMG-CDD will ensure that women, men, girls, and boys receiving humanitarian assistance are the primary stakeholders of the project and that they have a basic right to participate in the decisions that affect their lives, receive the information they need to make informed decisions, and to complain if they feel the help, they receive is not adequate or has unwelcomed consequences.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will prioritize the utilization of eco-friendly materials and methods during implementation, including a reduction in paper and plastic usage. Additionally, materials for shelter modification will be selected to align with environmentally friendly standards, while ensuring proper waste management within offices and centers. Regular awareness sessions on Ecological Sustainability will be conducted for both staff and project participants. In doing so, we will ensure the conservation of the natural environment for the well-being of future generations. To prevent the risk of biological infection of staff and beneficiary populations CBMG and CDD will follow the advice of the health cluster and abide by the restrictions in place by the Government of Bangladesh such as the use of hand sanitizer and mask. Staff will be trained in personal protection and personal protective equipment. The project will influence the inclusive humanitarian policy environment in the humanitarian response, providing inputs into Humanitarian Response Plans, Preparedness Plans, and other policy documents to ensure Disability Inclusive Disaster Response Mechanisms are in place.
