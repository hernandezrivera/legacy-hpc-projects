Project Summary

Silk Road aims by implementing this project to contribute to strengthen education process in the affected and vulnerable areas and provide a safe educational environment for approximately 30188 students aged 5-17 years in 45 schools in Teftnaz, Qourqeena, Mare', Haritan, Ariha and Harim, Bulbul, Raju, Sheikh El-Hadid, Sharan, Ma'btali, Raju, Afrin, Maaret Tamsrin and Darkosh sub-districts through focusing on a response to needs that are the improvement of access to equitable education and learning opportunity as well as promoting quality and relevance of formal and non-formal education within a protective environment in the targeted schools.
This project is a humanitarian action for emergency education needs including WASH in schools and raise awareness of good hygiene practices a protracted crisis by using the existing school buildings that are under the authority of the local education authorities as well as the project will improve these structures so that they can provide a more protective learning environment for children beyond the project period moreover, the project will build up the capacity of the targeted educational centres and its personnel which will increase their influence in the community, increase the value of the schools in the community and increase education enrolment rates year after year.
Training teachers will support the sustainability of education beyond the life of the project since those teachers will commit to teach in Syria and will support their colleagues by sharing their learnings with them. The project team will also strive to support cross-learning between education actors by sharing resources, knowledge, reports, and lesson learned with the community served and education actors.
The project will work with the local education authorities to ensure local ownership of the project and the sustainability of the project's investments.
30188 Students will benefit from:
1. Back to Learning campaigns- raising awareness of parents on the importance of children’s education, particularly for young girls.
2.Provide schools with equipment and furniture (Whiteboards, pens etc)
3. Provide professional development opportunities (Continuous training).
4.Provision of incentives for teachers and education personnel
5.Provision of teachers with teaching materials.
6.Support teachers and educational actors with training on ASER assessment
7.Rehabilitation of classrooms and WASH facilities in addition to the provision of clean water for students.
8. Support hygiene promotion - Rising WASH awareness by providing accessible key messages (Hand Washing, Food Processing,)
9. Providing psychosocial support activities - Psychosocial support using the below curricula:
- For younger children (5-9 years) using PSS Palestine guide
- For older children (10-16 years) using the Children's Resilience Programme: Psychosocial support in and out of schools (Save the Children).
10. Referral pathways to specialized services such as child protection, gender-based violence and nutrition. Mapping of services and referral pathway
11. Roll out of Global standards and Guidance on Child Protection case management.
12. Recreational materials
13. Provide textbooks to all children.
The project team will work towards equally reaching girls and boys and women and men throughout the project. To ensure a do no harm approach and mitigate the risk of resources related conflict the project will target both IDP and host children.
The project team recognizes that the issues such as some families may be less likely to encourage their daughters to go to school; some families may need their sons to work, some boys may be forced to enrol in military activities are beyond the influence of the project team and the project. 4 - Needs

According to the 2020 HNO, there is a total of 7-million people in need of education support. 86% of these people in need have an acute and immediate need for humanitarian education assistance (severity of 4-5-6). Teachers, who are also affected by the conflict and maybe part of displacement and return flows, lack of training on teaching in crisis contexts, including how to manage overcrowded classrooms, positive discipline, ensuring a child/learner-centred approach and catering to children’s broader emotional and social needs. Limited income has forced many parents to have their children contribute to the household income.According to the need assessment conducted by Silk Road, the most common reason which prevents children aged 12-17 to attend school during the current year is 79% Lack of sufficient basic facilities, teachers, books, supplies, water and sanitation facilities, drinking water. 60% 70% No obstructions prevent children from education, 65% Children and teachers suffer from mental disorder, 88% Inability of students with disabilities to access as in the figure belowThe lack of fund and educational support have caused many schools to stop working in the targeted areas either because of lack of the capacity of the schools, displacement of populations, interruption of school support, unavailability of the basic services or other reasons.Schools need support in Furniture, fuel, Rehabilitation of damaged school/ facilities. Moreover, the students need some psychiatric care to be able to continue learning. Most of the school in this area are still working despite the situation of desks, chairs, tables, damaged facilities. The state of public facilities and bathrooms in the schools is bad for a medium where the majority of schools need rehabilitation of WASH facilities with different needs. The need to rehabilitate WASH facilities in the affected schools as well as providing schools with school equipment and running costs where no support for these schools.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will start through establishing safe learning spaces for 30188 students in formal settings through the main activities which will be started by rehabilitating 580 classrooms in 45 School including WASH facilities and installing special equipment for students with disabilities in addition to the furniture and safety equipment. BTL campaign will be launched at the beginning of July and will include the outreach activities will be carried out by 10 community outreach workers. Catch-up classes (For three months ) will be held in one of the classrooms in the targeted schools by the teachers in these schools. In parallel with the launch of catch up classes our team will start providing the targeted schools with equipment, also we will provide students with learning materials as well as recreational materials and the teachers will be provided with teaching materials and Silk Road will provide students in the targeted schools with 30188 sets of textbooks. Teachers and education personnel will be provided with incentives and continuous professional development including DO-NO HARM and child protection policy and needs and rights of children in crisis contexts.Children with disabilities will be provided with transportation in order to ensure that each student will have the opportunity to get into the school. 30188 students will be provided with psychosocial support through assign, child protection facilitator (one in each school) who will provide PSS activities for students, conduct recreational activities for students, provide referral pathways to specialized services such as child protection, gender-based violence and nutrition and conduct awareness sessions or other activities to students' parents and teachers. On the other hand, Silk Road will conduct pre- and post-assessment, in order to measure students’ education level before and after the programme beside continuous monitoring across the whole project to ensure the satisfaction of the beneficiaries.
