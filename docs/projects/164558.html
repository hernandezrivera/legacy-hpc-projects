Project Summary

The proposed action will respond to emergency, displacement S/NFI needs and protect the well-being and thermal comfort of conflict-affected highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in North West Syria (NWS). This will be achieved through an integrated programme of assistance focused on providing targeted, emergency S/NFI assistance to newly displaced households and winterization assistance to the most vulnerable households in areas with greatest severity of winter need. Whenever possible, complementary Food Security assistance will be provided to SNFI beneficiaries.
Funding is secured to provide immediate, emergency and / or winter assistance to 9,102 vulnerable households newly displaced by the latest conﬂict escalation, of which an estimated 26,760 male (14,745 boys) and 27,852 female (15,291 girls) as summarised below. Please note for activities implemented by the Lead Partner, our submission shows only the caseload and budget to be implemented by our organisation. The Lead Partner will input their planned activities to the HPC separately, to ensure no duplication in targets reported.
In partnership with INGO and funded by ECHO: Multi-Purpose Cash Grants (MPCGs) to reach 1,163 IDP households + in-kind NFI kits for a further 1,000 HHs. Based on S/NFI Cluster and Cash WG guidance, beneﬁciaries are anticipated to use 15% of MPCG assistance to meet NFI needs.

In partnership with INGO and funded by DfID: In-kind NFI kits to respond to the winter and displacement needs of total 2,639 households.
Fully funded under the SCHF: Winter cash to meet the thermal needs of 500 vulnerable households in Idleb City and Darret Ezza (completed Jan 2020).
Fully funded under the SCHF: Cash assistance to meet winter SNFI needs of 3,800 households.

4 - Needs

The draft 2020 HNO estimates that 11.06 MIL people will require humanitarian assistance in 2020, of which 4.65 MIL will be in acute need. An estimated 6.1 MIL people are displaced and more than 80% of the population live below the poverty line. Of these, approx. 26% of the population are experiencing abject poverty. While markets remain functional in most areas and essential NFIs remain available, the majority of affected HHs do not have the financial means to access NFIs without assistance. The Idleb Crisis has compounded an already desperate situation w/ humanitarian need reaching unprecedented levels. HNAP Mobility and Needs Monitoring overview for Dec 2019 reports 196067 newly displaced IDPs in Dec 2019. A HNAP IDP Snapshot dated 9Feb2020 reports main needs of newly IDPs include NFIs (100%), shelter (84%) & cash (57%). The population of people in communities surveyed by HNAP reporting SNFI among top three priority needs has been steadily increasing in Jan 2020, making this a critical need at the current time. These findings align with our Rapid Need Assessments (RNAs) completed with new arrivals in Dec2019 which found NFIs, cash & food to be priority reported needs. Our RNAs found that local markets were functional & accessible and equipped to respond to priority needs expressed by affected populations. Distribution Monitoring (DM) completed by our org with new arrivals in Nov & Dec 2019 highlight the vulnerability of many affected HHs. On avg, IDPs surveyed were displaced 2.25 times prior to the DM survey. 10% of respondents were female-headed HHs, 5% included HH members living with severe disabilities with the same % also reporting members living with chronic illness. While all IDP BNFs interviewed reported taking ID with them when they left, only 25% reported taking cash or cooking utensils and 40% winter blankets. The majority of affected HHs have neither the cash nor the essential NFIs required to meet HH needsor protect thermal comfort without assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

Under Act 1.1 ‘Provision of Core and Essential NFIs’ emergency S/NFI assistance will focus on areas hosting the highest # of newly disp HHs. This will include Afrin, A’zaz, Atareb and Jandairis (Aleppo) and Dana, Maarat Tamsrin & Idleb SDs (Idleb). Emergency response will benefit from well-established systems & monitoring processes designed to ensure targeted, timely assistance to populations affected by sudden shocks. Our experienced emergency response field teams will provide rapid, cash-based or in-kind assistance to newly displaced people (displaced within 30 days). SOPs include robust BNF verification to ensure HHs assisted were newly displaced and specific SOPs for cash assistance. Activities will seek to assist all new arrivals in targeted communities. Should need exceed the assistance available, we will coordinate with other partners to address gaps / apply weighted vulnerability criteria to target the most vulnerable. Weighting focuses on IDP HHs in greatest need of shelter assistance (e.g. living in collective centres or in the open) and at greater risk of protection concerns, including female-/child-headed HHs. 2019/20 winterization activities were developed in close coordination with the S/NFI cluster and members to address gaps and particular vulnerabilities and ensure a harmonized response. The same approach will be used for planning 2020/21 winterization activities to identify underserved areas with greatest winter severity ranking. Funding has been requested from ECHO to assist 2,100 HHs with winter cash. Based on our caseload for the 2019/20 winter season, funding is sought to assist a further 3000 HHs with winter cash to ensure the overall winter response can meet needs. Winter assistance will be a cash-based transfer to increase access to fuel and non-fuel winter NFIs prioritized by each HH. Highly targeted selection criteria will be applied to winterization assistance with complementary FS assistance sought to protect the winter package.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

We will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex & age disaggregation of info, participation, access & an understanding of how different needs will be met. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, BNFs will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible, inc., needs and resource identification, design, implementation, M&E. This project will work with female & male community leaders and members to ensure that distributions are equitable & gender sensitive, eg having separate lines for male & female BNFs at distribution points & ensuring female staff are present whenever possible. Our PDM also shows that female-headed HHs are more at risk of engaging in harmful coping strategies to meet basic needs. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity & non-discrimination so that all potential BNFs will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. We will engage with LCs, RCs and other actors to mitigate risks faced by women & children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To mitigate protection risks, we work closely with LCs & other admin bodies to ensure open & accountable feedback mechanisms for BNFs and non-BNFs inc. face-to-face interviews & a direct Whatsapp number. We require all staff to sign the org's code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or BNFs, including misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols against any sexual relations with minors or beneficiaries of any age. Additionally, we will ensure that assessments and BNF selection will assess women’s and girls’ unique needs in add.to other vulnerable groups.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real-time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapidadjustments to planned activities as necessary. This can include temporary suspension of field activities during times of extreme conflict. In line with well-established SOPs, distributions will be placed on temporary hold during periods of heightened risk to staff and beneficiaries. We employ multiple measures to maintain delivery assistance, which can include door-to-door distributions, or phased distribution times to reduce the risks of large numbers of people gathering. The use of cash-based assistance seeks to mitigate possible supply chain breaks or border closures. For in-kind NFIs, Framework Agreements are in place with NFI suppliers who have demonstrated reliability and ability to consistently deliver. Should supply chain breaks be experienced due to border closures or other, unanticipated events, we have the ability to immediately transition from in-kind assistance to conditional NFI vouchers or cash to ensure targeted beneficiaries can maintain access to essential SNFIs. We are continually updating our contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in NWS. These plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. All field staff are well trained and incidents trackers are maintained to identify trends and flashpoints. Funding is secured for FS operations in Afrin district, Aleppo. This will allow us to respond to need in these areas should significant incoming displacement be experienced as a result of further, possible conflict in Idleb and western Aleppo. Securing this access also allows us to re-programme planned S/NFI assistance to areas in northern Aleppo should security prevent ongoing operations in Idleb or western Aleppo.
