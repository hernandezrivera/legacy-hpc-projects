Project Summary

This project aims to establish and improve 10 informal IDP settlements infrastructure in Northwest Syria, particularly in northern Idleb, it targets the IDP sites that reported to be impacted by recent floods in winter 2018-2019 and had a miserable condition with damaged tents and cut of access to their site. The improvements and upgrades will contribute to the winterization plan of 2019-2020. Targeted IDP sites will be selected based on specific criteria that ensure which informal settlements are recommended for infrastructure improvement and upgrade. Speaking of which, the criteria of site selection will be based on the recommendations from a topographical information, HLP condition of the land, secondary data (CCCM online coordination tool for flood response) CCCM Cluster guidance. interventions are based on the main needs reported by CCCM during the recent flood response, Gravelling, tent leveling, tent inner and outer insulation and establishing drains to eliminate water from the site. The proposed activities will ensure the resilience and safety of IDPs in future flood and deteriorated weather. Additionally, awareness activity will be in place where Muzun will provide camp management and IDP committees brochures that include first actions to be taken in expected weather deterioration.

Needs

A large proportion of IDP sites in Syria are informal settlements, also known as “spontaneous” or “self-established” camps. These usually consist of groups of tents or other shelters that were set up by IDPs themselves or other actors. Crucially, these sites are not managed by anyone and often have no legal claim to the land on which they were established. In these sites, the delivery of dignified protection and multi-sectoral assistance cannot be guaranteed by humanitarian actors, a critical issue for vulnerable groups, and particularly for women- and child-headed households (HNO 2019, CCCM Cluster). For the past years in Syria context, floods and harsh weather have always been a dilemma for the IDPs residing in informal IDP settlements across the northwest of Syria. 215 IDP sites in Idleb and Aleppo were affected by recent floods and rainy weather, most of them are spontaneously and newly established, resulted in tent structure damage, losing of Non-food items, damage wash facilities, access restriction to/within the IDP site due to muddy land and roads and increase the number of health cases in informal settlements (mainly diarrhea and cold ache). Around 8,222 Families, around 53,443 individuals, were affected by the recent storm between 26//December/2018 and 10/January/2019 based on CCCM online reporting tool. Muzun through its field teams have assessed the needs in 88 informal settlements in Harim, Jabal Samaan and Jisr-Ash-Shugur districts, where 4032 tents reported to be damaged hosting an over of 4000 families, 25,750 individuals, in these sites, 13,262 of them are females. The priority need for families as first needed intervention was to eliminate flooding water, gravelling (which is not possible during that period) and insulation and/or replacement for their tents. New NFIs and Food were also priority for affected families besides nutrition for babies which was an essential need in Jisr-Ash-Shugur IDP sites. In addition, women and girls, 51% of the affected people, were the most affected since wash facilities were not functioning as well as absence of privacy in shelter, dignity kits and WASH kits reported by the KIs within several sites to be also a priority specially for those who lost their privacy because of the damaged shelter.

Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented in parallel with IDP committees in each informal settlement, 10 IDP committees should include males and females with various ages to ensure good and effective participation in project design, implementation of activities.As to commence with, the project will be implemented in 10 Informal settlements in Harim district northern Idleb Governorate where high concentration of IDP sites are located. The selection of the IDP sites will be based on criteria that includes: recommendations from a topographical information (land slope, soil condition…etc), HLP (land ownership easy verification), secondary data (CCCM online coordination tool for flood response) and space in m2. All applicable informal settlements that meet the criteria will be considered for the implementation of activities. improving the physical conditions in these already existed Informal settlements is the main outcome of these intervention and increase the resilience for those IDPs through establishing and improving infrastructure in informal IDP sites to meet the minimum standards. In order to meet the needs and protect the IDPs form potential floods in winter 2019-2020, several components considered to be cost effective that will prevent the IDP site from flooding, - Gravelling for main roads site entrance.- Tent leveling (ground insulation).- Tent inner and outer insulation.- Establish drains for water to pass (based on topographic engineer).- Awareness campaign for IDPs on how to protect themselves before and during floods.- Establish a WhatsApp number for emergency cases reporting from families. Through these components, flood prevention will be well planned for the coming winter, this will significantly increase the resilience of IDP families against harsh weather, reduce number of lost NFI, decrease number of damaged tents, protect the privacy of the families specially for women and girls, reduce health cases, keeping youth and young children’s friendly spaces.In awareness campaign, IDPs will receive brochures that include some points to prepare themselves if any weather deterioration is expected. For instance, it will include securing important items and documents, to put a heavy item on the roof of the tent, avoid using electrical devices during intense rain and digging around the tent, not to mention the follow up with managements and IDP committees of that informal settlements during flood since Muzun will keep its presence in the targeted IDP sites. Keeping in touch with the CCCM Cluster and other humanitarian actors for better guidance in winterization activities through regular reporting and follow up.

Protection Risk Analysis

- Construction Contractors could create some tension or harm based on their salary payment- Create pull-factor and expose IDPs to physical safety risks when/(if the camps and settlements are located in unsafe areas). High Impact, High Likelihood. As ISIMM tool captures only the camp that respect the above mentioned principles, ensure that only the locations ISIMM are supported by the CCCM clustermembers.- An increase in tensions within the camps, as space (a scarce resource) is occupied by these infrastructure and create differences between the camps in terms of services. High Likelihood, Medium impact. Mitigation: IDP Committees should be consulted before the construction of any infrastructure and be made aware of the risks that the current project was attempting to mitigate- Project staff may abuse beneficiaries in order to get benefit in return. mitigation: MHD will follow its PSEA policy to ensure staff do adhere humanitarian principle and respect the dignity of beneficiary.

High Impact, High Likelihood. Mitigation: Muzun to keep coordinating with local councils, camp managements and IDP committees to keep being independent in its cross border operations.-Bad weather may delay the infrastructure work. Medium Impact, Low likelihood.
