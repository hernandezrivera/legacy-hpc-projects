Project Summary

The project aims to provide life-saving WASH assistance to 80,000( 37000 male, 43000 female) IDPs, returnees, and non-displaced including 30,000 children in Algabal Elgarbi, Zwara, Derna, Misrata, Sebha and ejdabia affected by the conflict. Additionally, the project will preposition essential WASH supplies in order to respond to emerging humanitarian WASH needs across the country. UNICEF and its implementing partners will apply all the humanitarian principles including do no harm and accountability to the affected population.

Needs

In 2018, a total of 267,000 people including 106,000 children have been identified in need of humanitarian WASH assistance. Findings from the Multisector Needs Assessment (MSNA) recently conducted by REACH (August 2018) revealed that approximately 55 per cent of respondents accessed their household water from main networks. According to Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) (Round 20 – June 2018), 44 per cent of municipalities depend on water trucking, with a reduction in the volume of safe water attributable to a lack of electricity and poor maintenance of WASH systems. The wastewater treatment system is not functioning properly compared with the pre-conflict situation due to poor maintenance. IOM’s DTM also makes clear that only 17 per cent of municipal waste water is collected, treated and disposed of properly. DTM informs also that 33 per cent of garbage is not properly disposed and collected. Water in Libya is supplied from three main sources, the Man-Made River Project (60 percent of overall population), municipal wellfields managed by the General Company for Water and Wastewater (GCWW) (30 percent) and desalination plants (10 percent). These three main service providers have been left without essential operation and maintenance budgets which could lead to total breakdown of water services across the country and the risk of 6 million people not having access to sufficient water. Noteworthy to highlight that this big infrastructure is vulnerable to terrorist attacks and sabotage that have been taking place leaving millions of people without access to safe water.Schoolchildren are also affected by the protracted conflict with limited access to WASH facilities in schools. As outlined in the WASH assessment report undertaken by UNICEF and the National Centre for Disease Control in 2017, 67 per cent of children has limited access to water whereas the clear majority, 95.8 per cent, have limited sanitation services, while the remaining 4.2 per cent have no sanitation services. The survey revealed also that water in 10 per cent is contaminated with harmful bacteria.

Activities

1. Evidence generation through rapid and joint needs assessments. 2. Provision of safe drinking water and sanitation facilities through • Water trucking• Provision of chlorine and water treatment materials• Installation of emergency latrines• Light rehabilitation and repair of water and sanitation facilities in IDP camps, schools, health facilities • Support on solid waste collection and disposal system in the municipalities affected by the conflict and displacement • Distribution of soap, basic hygiene kits and awareness information 3. Provision of equipment and supplies (such as portable water quality testing kits, pumps, generators) to ensure operational municipal systems in affected municipalities4. Preposition emergency WASH essential supplies (including cholera kits - WASH components) 5. Capacity building for WASH sector partners including governmental partners6. Support the General Water Resources Authority GWRA with the development and implementation of WASH emergency and response plans.7. Support and strengthened coordination mechanisms with involvement of governmental leadership
