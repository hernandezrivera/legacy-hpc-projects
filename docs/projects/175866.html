Project Summary

It is estimated that 1.5 million South Sudanese will be affected by Housing, Land and Property (HLP) issues in 2021. In addition, 743,000 children are indirectly affected of HLP issues. According to Severity Mapping Duk and Pibor are among the four counties classified as catastrophic (Severity 5) with the highest HLP issues in the country which is really alarming (South Sudan HLPAoR HRP 2021 Strategy). In tandem with that statistics, areas with high number of IDPs and returnees were classified as high-risk due to the increased likelihood of limited availability of HLP for all groups, destroyed HLP et cetera.
Despite the legal provisions recognizing the equal rights of women to land, the knowledge, recognition and protection of those rights remains very limited throughout South Sudan. Women’s land rights remain largely conditional, derived through their marital or childbearing status and dispossession of widows, daughters, and divorced women of their HLP ownership is common. There is tension between competing notions that customary rules and practices should adapt to changing socio-economic circumstances and those who resist change, fearing its impact on tradition and cultural identity, cause a significant gap between the law and practice, particularly in rural areas (South Sudan draft Land Policy, p.4).
Factors contributing to these higher risk of HLP issues include but not limited to dynamics such as a history of displacement, conflict, and secondary occupation, land grabbing, flooding, lack of widespread knowledge of HLP Rights among the communities, forced eviction, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure, the increase in population growth, Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites transition to IDPs Camps, increasing number and high influx of South Sudanese refugees returning from the neighboring countries such as Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia and Sudan, and COVID-19 Pandemic (South Sudan HLPAoR HRP 2021 Strategy).Without appropriate interventions taken or immediate assistance provided, the HLP issues will definitely result into further displacement and conflicts which will have adverse effects on the families and children.
Therefore, this project will target 25000 People ( 13000 Women, 12000 Men, including 15% persons with disability) for provision of HLP Assistance including: HLP Case Management; Legal Assistance and Counselling; Provision of temporary shelters; HLP Awareness Raising and Advocacy; HLP Protection Monitoring; Establishment of Community-based Alternative Dispute Resolution Mechanism & Community Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM); Peace-building activities; Capacity Building for Local Governance Structures; Establishment and strengthening of Information Assistance/Help Desks.
In all its implementation phases, CINA will strictly adhere to national laws and international norms on HLP, Code of Conduct, Community Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, uphold the Do No Harm Principle, promote centrality of protection principles and enhance accountability to affected populations before, during and after project implementation.
CINA will implement this project with technical assistance from South Sudan HLP Technical Working Group Area of Responsibility (AoR), and in coordination with Protection Partners on the ground together with relevant authorities and will require approximately $325,000 to meet the HLP needs of IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities in areas with catastrophic HLP needs. CINA will use its existing protection presence in Jonglei to reach populations in need of HLP in Duk and Pibor and will also employ mobile protection response through field-based roving to access hard-to-reach areas in Pibor, Duk and surrounding areas.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, housing, land and property (HLP) issues are both a consequence and a cause of conflict. The COVID-19 pandemic, flooding and inter-communal violence have exacerbated some of the risks associated with HLP issues in several areas of the country especially with 34, 218 people from Duk and 40,106 people from Pibor in need of HLP assistance (HRP Locations 2021). This is evidenced by widespread destruction and looting of houses and properties, degradation of housing due to displacement, secondary occupation, forced eviction, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure which are all features of the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan. (HLPAoR HRP 2019 Strategy). It also is noted that HLP rights including reclamation, restitution and reconstruction continue to be a crucial issue in South Sudan. To achieve durable solutions for those affected by the conflict significant effort will be required to ensure that they are effectively addressed. (South Sudan HLP TWG: HLP Challenges in South Sudan January 2021). Spontaneous returns of both IDPs and refugees are expected to increase in 2021 as the government moves forward with the implementation of the R-ARCSS. This will result in a rise of HLP issues in areas of return especially illegal occupation and grabbing of houses and lands, which has potential to contribute to sub-national and localized violence along with conflict based on identity lines in some areas. There is also limited awareness of HLP rights among local authorities and affected populations. Displaced and returnee populations, in particular women and child headed households, also lack legal assistance to access the limited courts or alternative dispute resolution mechanisms to claim their rights. It is recently documented that access to Housing, Land, and Property (HLP), an important issue during the years of protracted conflict, is now critical as people are further displaced, the population grows, PoC sites start to transition and increasing number of South Sudanese refugees returning from the neighboring countries such as Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia and Sudan (South Sudan HLPAoR HRP 2021 Strategy). In a context of increasing land-related disputes, and anticipation of returns, relocations, and local reintegration of IDPs and refugees, HLP issues will be a major priority need to be addressed in South Sudan in 2021.In Duk and Pibor, HLP issues constitute a major challenge to creating conditions conducive to pursuing durable solutions for displaced populations and peace among the communities. The assessment on potential high-risk Areas of HLP issues by HLP TWG in June 2020 classified Duk as high risk due to high number of IDPs resulting from limited access for HLP for all groups, destroyed HLP or secondary occupation of HLP. In addition, there is high land-related disputes among IDPs and host community and lack of HLP services reported in Duk (Shelter/NFI Analysis for Duk October 2019). In Pibor, there is limited HLP due to destruction of houses, trees, flooding and ongoing conflicts (Pibor ICCG IRNA report March 2020). Another pressing HLP issue is that, the formalization of land laws and the creation of mechanisms of their implementation remains a gap and creates an obstacle for people who want to return. Lack of widespread knowledge of HLP laws and insufficient legal ownership documentation made it difficult for many people to understand and effectively advocate for themselves. Worst of all, women, children and people with disabilities disproportionately struggle to assert rights to housing and land. Despite the legal provisions recognizing the equal rights of women to land, recognition and protection of those rights remain limited throughout South Sudan. Women’s land rights remain largely conditional. Dispossession of widows, daughters, and divorced women is common.

2 - Project activities

Pursuant to South Sudan HLPAoR HRP 2021 Strategy, CINA in its intervention will; 1. Pursue HLP through mobile protection in hard-to-reach locations in Duk and Pibor or other surrounding areas. These activities will include mobile legal clinics, HLP information and awareness raising, protection monitoring and assessment of HLP needs. CINA will will also conduct static HLP programming in prioritized locations, including: 1. Provide HLP legal aid and case management to 60 (28 women, 32 men) beneficiaries with HLP cases. 2. Provide cash based assistance (CBI) to 60 most vulnerable HLP cases as part of case management support. The CBI modality will be restricted, using voucher and will be provided based on vulnerability criteria and in line with protection principles and will be coordinated with Cash Working Group and Protection Cluster. CINA HLP Caseworkers will identify vulnerable HLP affected persons who will be supported with Cash Based Interventions, Legal representation through community leadership structures, awareness raising sessions. 2. Provide legal Assistance and Counselling to 150 (70 women, 80 men) Beneficiaries. This will be done through individualized or group sessions. 3. Provide HLP Awareness and Advocacy to 25,000 people (13000 women, 12000 men). CINA will conduct awareness raising in the church, schools, community centres and food distribution sites. Considering COVID 19 measures, there will be door to door awareness raising, use of radio, clustering of participants into small groups, etc. 4. Conduct 6 HLP Protection Monitoring visits to identify violations of HLP rights and protection risks encountered by internally displaced persons (IDPs), Returnees and other affected populations with an immediate objective of assisting and guiding evidence-based intervention by relevant national and international actors to ensure the full respect of the rights of the affected populations. 5. Establish/strengthen 10 Community-Based Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) Mechanisms in the project areas. The ADR Committees will comprise 50 members (20 women, 30 men) for all the locations. 6. Conduct Capacity Building sessions to 60 (28 women, 32 men) Local Governance Structures’ members on HLP rights, peace-building and coexistence mechanisms. CINA HLP Staff will train 30 (14 women, 16 men) per each location. 7. Conduct Peace-building activities to 180 people (85 women, 95 men) through Dialogues, Focus Group Discussions and training. 8. Establish/strengthen 10 Information Assistance/Help Desks in Duk and Pibor that will be used to identify, respond and report HLP issues. 6 IADs/Help Desks in Duk and 4 in Pibor and each will comprise 5 members (2 women, 3 men) making total 50 people (20 women, 30 men) for all the locations. They will be based in Community Centres. 9. Provide temporary shelters to 50 HLP beneficiaries through distribution of Shelter kits or Cash Based Intervention to those identified and verified as vulnerable.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA has a PSEA Policy and Code of Conduct, and as part of its HLP Programming, staff will ensure strict adherence to these policies and other universal principles enshrined in the international human rights instruments and the Bill of Rights in the Transitional Constitution of the Republic of South Sudan with reference to other related laws of the land, will incorporate protection principles to ensure and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity while implementing this project. In consolidating that, CINA HLP staff will be thoroughly trained on PSEA and sign the Code of Conduct before implementing the activities. The team will then strive to mainstream PSEA throughout the implementation process by giving awareness to the Community Based Protection Networks and Information Assistance Desks established at the community level. PSEA messages will be printed on banners and disseminated along with HLP awareness messages. The HLP beneficiaries will be empowered to report any information regarding sexual exploitation and abuse using these structures. Finally, CINA will analyze and provide appropriate feedback through the PSEA Focal Person or the Project Manager. This will also include attending the inter-agency working groups in Duk and Pibor to understand the trending protection concerns such as GBV Related issues alongside HLP issues from other humanitarian actors and consequently also alerting them on safe/unsafe environment. The HLP beneficiaries will be empowered to report any information regarding sexual exploitation and abuse. Finally, CINA will analyze and provide appropriate feedback

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CINA will recruit local volunteers and mobilizers who will help to translate HLP messages into local languages to ensure meaningful information reach vulnerable people. The views of people will be gathered through focus group discussions and awareness raising and use of Toll Free Hot Line to inform project implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CINA will establish HLP Information Assistance/Help Desks in Community Centres. These will be used to identity, respond and report HLP issues. Most importantly, they will receive complainants and give feedback to the beneficiaries through Complaints Box and Hotline Number that will be provided by CINA.
