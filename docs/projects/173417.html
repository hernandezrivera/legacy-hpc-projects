Project Summary

UNICEF aims to transform the lives of vulnerable people, especially children and women, adolescent girls, by improving their nutritional and health status, given that these groups often have the greatest unmet needs. UNICEF Nutrition programming seeks to enable a child's full capacity through addressing preventative and curative needs at the age of 0-59 months. A high incidence/prevalence of malnutrition is often reported in children with disability, however, nutrition and disability are rarely linked. Children with disabilities scarcely have the same level of access to health and nutrition services; are often unaware of, do not plan for, and are unable to communicate effectively. UNICEF pursues to provide children and their families through lifecycle-based programming that addresses the holistic nutritional needs of every child at each stage of life including the special needs of children with disabilities. UNICEF’s aims to provide life-saving treatment through supply provision of ready-to-use therapeutic foods and works with partners to manage the urgent treatment of children suffering from severe acute malnutrition in the camp.
UNICEF will ensure that women and children have access to supplementation to address micronutrient deficiencies. To improve access to the quality nutrition services UNICEF will promote micro-gardening at household-level, with an emphasis on vegetables and fruits that is rich in nutrients. Additionally, knowledge and practices on appropriate food and nutrition practices will be improved through nutrition education sessions which will include cooking demonstrations for increased consumption of nutrient-rich foods produced at household-level in the host community. Therefore, UNICEF will support counselling and establish safe spaces within integrated nutrition facilities for promoting infant and child feeding in camps. UNICEF will support timely nutritional assessment and surveillance systems in order to track malnutrition rates and improve programme performance. The interventions will also promote speciﬁc and sensitive nutrition needs from an integrated multi-sectoral lens (including Water and Sanitation Hygiene, Health, and Gender) to ensure fulﬁllment of rights for each stage of the lifecycle for the most vulnerable and marginalized women and children. The project will ensure integration of Accountability of Affected People (AAP) through a multi-channel Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism where feedback and complaints will be processed, analyzed and shared on a regular basis.
Mitigation measures are critical during the pandemic to ensure malnourished children continue to have access to nutritious foods and essential nutrition services in the context of COVID 19. UNICEF will ensure to prepare and respond to the COVID-19 pandemic by intensifying the public awareness, protection, promotion and support of appropriate and safe feeding for all breastfed and non-breastfed children and use all opportunities to include hygiene messages, key messages on COVID-19 symptoms, and Infection, Prevention and Control (IPC) measures. To mitigate any adverse weather incidents a contingency plan is developed and accordingly a buffer stock of supplies and medicines will be prepositioned in the INFs for the rapid deployment in situations of flood and cyclones. In addition, intensifying pre-positioning (with a minimum buffer stock of 2 months) of essential commodities for nutrition programming nutrition facility and community level in anticipation of supply chain disruptions. Furthermore, strengthen and reinforce the capacity of mothers and caregivers to detect and monitor their children’s nutritional status. As sector lead, strengthening coordination and partnerships with other institutions involved in the Rohingya response will also be an important aspect of UNICEF's interventions.

1 - Identified Needs

Refugee Camp: Acute and chronic malnutrition among children 06-59 months in the Rohingya refugee camp have improved since the influx in 2017, but still remains above the global emergency threshold. SMART Survey Round-4 in the Rohingya camps from October 2019 showed a Global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of 10.9 per cent which was observed 11.0 per cent in 2018, is considered as 'high' based on the new WHO/UNICEF prevalence thresholds. A slight reduction was recorded in Severe acute malnutrition (SAM), with a 0.9 per cent prevalence in 2019 compared to 1.1 per cent in 2018. It should be noted that as per global Nutrition Cluster guidance for COVID-19, no SMART survey was conducted in 2020. The 2019 SMART survey indicated that 32.6 per cent children under 5 were chronically malnourished (stunted), which is considered as very high based on the new WHO/UNICEF prevalence thresholds. The survey also reflected poor Infant and young child feeding practices as indicated by 26.4 per cent children 6-23 months having a minimum acceptable diet supplemented by 44.8 per cent had minimum dietary diversity and 56.2 per cent reached minimum meal frequency, which highlighted the need to implement a comprehensive IYCF interventions. COVID-19 can both directly and indirectly impact the health and nutrition status of children and women. The infection itself can lead to undernutrition - direct effect; or through its effect on Health and WASH systems, worsening food security, poverty, job loss etc. can indirectly cause undernutrition. To try to understand the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the nutritional status of children 06-59 months in the camps, UNICEF, WFP, UNHCR and Nutrition Sector partners undertook two rounds of house-to-house mass screening campaign in June/July and October. First round Vitamin A and mass screening campaign in June/July showed a SAM prevalence of 1.7 per cent (MUAC < 120 mm) and MAM prevalence of 6.8 per cent (MUAC >= 120 to < 130 mm) and 5.8 per cent of the total children were found with MUAC 130 to <135mm are at risk of acute malnutrition. Second round of mass screening campaign conducted in October, 2020, showed a SAM prevalence of 1.7 per cent and MAM prevalence of 8.8 per cent and 9.5 per cent of the total children were found are at risk of acute malnutrition; significant changes have been observed among the children with MUAC between 120mm to <135mm from 12.2 per cent in June/July to 18.3 per cent in October. Although, the SAM and MAM prevalence from the Mass screening and SMART survey cannot be compared because of completely different methodologies however the high levels of SAM and MAM as shown by the Mass screening data may reflect a worsening situation. Between the months of June-September monitoring of nutrition services through mobile phones found an average of 53 per cent mothers feed less diverse food to their children while 46 per cent feed less food and 45 per cent reported food shortage in their house. Considering the situation, it will be extremely important to intensify the nutrition response through the curative and preventive activities i.e. strengthening community outreach activities, active screening (and mass screening), mother led MUAC (MLM), management of children with SAM and MAM and IYCF counselling etc. to address program gaps which occurred due to COVID-19 related restrictions and constraints. Host Community: The recent Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) conducted in March 2019 showed that the prevalence of SAM was 1.8 per cent and that of GAM is 9.7 per cent among children in Cox’s Bazar district, which is comparatively higher than other districts. The same survey also pointed underlying causes of malnutrition which were related to poor IYCF practices, food diversity and quality. According to MICS only 34.5 per cent of the children had been introduced to breast milk within an hour of birth which is essential to save lives.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity-1: Treatment and management of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) among Boys and Girls aged 6-59 Months through Out-Patient Therapeutic Care Programmes (OTP). Activity-2: Promotion of appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) Practices for caregivers of newborns, infants, and young children age 0-23 months through community-based IYCF promotion, messaging and quality one-to-one counseling. Activity-3: Community based Management of Acute Malnutrition for caregivers of newborns and infants 0-6 months (CMAM-I). Activity-4: Emphasis on programming holistically designed to promote and improve optimal Complementary Feeding, which includes service delivery that is specific (community gardens and community kitchens) and specific (dietary diversity and cooking demonstrations), promote hand washing and protected measure considering COVID 19. Activity-5: Two rounds of deworming and Vitamin A supplementation for all children age 6-59 months. Activity-6: As Cluster Lead Agency (CLA), capacity building, technical assistance, and supportive supervision for all Nutrition Sector implementing partners in the ongoing and overall improvement of nutrition programming, and thereby, overall nutrition outcomes (Sector). Activity-7: Community systems-strengthening via outreach, awareness, and sensitization for all community members to promote ownership and utilization of services, which includes active case-finding, referrals, beneficiary household follow up and defaulter/absentee tracing. Activity-8: Direct procurement of essential nutrition therapeutic commodities and supplies, and support for capacity strengthening of Government of Bangladesh, such as Ready to Use Therapeutic Food, Therapeutic Milks and Essential Medicines, facility equipment for facility-based prevention and treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (Procurement). Activity-9: Support to Government of Bangladesh for establishing and strengthening data and information management systems to enable timely, relevant, and quality nutrition programming that is responsive to both immediate and long term needs for both Rohingya and Bangladeshi communities.
