Project Summary

It is estimated that over 2.6 million people will face Gender-Based Violence (GBV) moving into 2023. Widespread flooding, intensified conflicts and financial hardships are the main drivers for increased GBV risks and vulnerabilities. Duk, Ayod, Yirol East, Yirol West, Tambura and Magwi are among the counties facing extreme and severe GBV needs (HNO 2022) exposing 302,133 vulnerable people to unprecedented multiple risks.
A snapshot of the scale of GBV risks in South Sudan indicates that 50% of girls are married below 18 years, 25% of them reach the secondary level of education, 97% of GBV incident survivors are female, only about 34% of females own a phone and 19% of births are attended by a skilled health worker. Sexual violence is a principal safety and protection concern for women and girls, while revenge killings, abductions, and forced recruitment are GBV risks facing young men, men, and boys.
The protection mechanisms available to women and girls facing GBV issues include protection from family members, community chiefs and women groups. However, these mechanisms and structures are wrecked by protracted emergencies, exposing women, girls, and persons with disabilities to unbearable suffering.
This project aims at response services to strengthen primary GBV prevention, GBV risk mitigation and integration into other sectors, and scaling up GBV response services for 13,500 vulnerable people (6,000 women, 4,700 girls, 1,950 boys, 850 men) including 1,950 people with disability from the IDPs, returnees and host community. The project will deliver comprehensive GBV services through Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery; Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support; Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support; Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well being and empowerment; Production of Fuel Efficient Stoves to mitigate risks of GBV; Provision of legal services that protect the rights of GBV survivors and promote their access to justice; Provision of livelihood and economic support including Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multisectoral GBV response; Implement community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle; Provide capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to frontline workers and community mobilizers.
Given its technical capacity in GBV, including the recent Multi-Sectoral Safety Audit work in the country and static presence in Tambura, Magwi, Duk, and Yirol East among other locations in Jonglei, Lakes, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria, CINA will continue to work with communities to accelerate GBV response, prevention and mitigation and ensure community participation and involvement as key stakeholders in the implementation of the project. CINA has offices in Tambura, Magwi, Duk and Yirol and can access these locations both through water, air and land means. CINA has two motor speedboats in Twic East which are used to reach populations in Duk, Twic East and surrounding islands.
CINA will ensure quality of project progress through quality assurance and monitoring and evaluation frameworks through capacity building, technical support, and on-job mentorship for all the staff involved in the project implementation and will coordinate efforts with GBV AoR at national and state levels, other GBV partners and non-GBV actors including those working for peace and development to integrate and mainstream GBV in their programming.

1 - Needs

South Sudan reported over 300 cases of gender-based violence in 2021 according to Anadolu Agency and it is estimated that over 2.6 million people risk facing gender-based violence in 2023, which represents a 25 per cent increase compared to 2021 and 2022 estimates. As a result of structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships, women and girls and especially those who are internally displaced or living near cantonment sites, or with a disability continue to suffer from GBV. It is estimated that Magwi, Duk, and Yirol East have 115,407, 92,326 and 94,400 people in need of GBV services respectively (HNO 2022) & 15 GBV cases from Tambura. GBVIMS for September 2021 reported that of the 6,238 GBV incidents, 28% are cases of child survivors below 18 years. Since the opening of the GBV and Juvenile court to April 2022, 668 cases have been reported with 180 concluded. The effects of COVID-19, food insecurity, and floods and frequent subnational violence and community-based organized conflicts in Lakes, Jonglei, Western Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria continue to weaken the protective environment for women and girls and significantly impair the capacity of communities to appropriately care for vulnerable people at risk of violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation. The gaps in GBV services include and not limited to lack of health services, lack of shelter and non-food items, lack of sanitary materials, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) materials, insecurity with reported cases of conflict related sexual violence, killings, child abduction, and child recruitment by armed forces. Protection Assessments in hard-to-reach areas by REACH in August 2021 found out that Yirol East and Duk are among top five counties in South Sudan where women and girls mentioned that their main protection concern is conflict which does not allow them access to their preferred market. IRNA reports from Duk in October 2021 indicated immense protection needs in the islands of Koyom, Amech and Kawer, members of the 27,006 IDPs and host communities there reported that they sought safety in the islands due to violence and cattle raids. Widespread flooding and conflicts in these locations have resulted to destruction of shelters, crops, drinking water sources; preventing access to child friendly spaces, schools, health facilities and market centers and impeding humanitarian’s ability to move across the affected areas. Women, men, boys and girls affected by crises suffer loss, fear, panic and anxiety and lack psychosocial support, recreational and learning facilities. Women and girls are exposed to widespread family separations, recruitment by armed forces and groups, sexual abuse and exploitation. The 2022 FSNMS+ qualitative assessment identified rape, physical assault, sexual abuse, forced and early marriage as GBV concerns facing about 33% of at-risk populations.Family and community support systems have been devastated and harmful cultural practices including girl marriage and other forms of violence against women and girls. In fact, these needs are critical and require immediate response to save the lives of the affected people in these areas. The increased risk of GBV and gaps in response services, as revealed by the assessments, highlight an urgent need or GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and scaling up of specialized GBV response services. Specifically, there is a need to strengthen GBV case management, medical care, psychosocial support, safety and security, legal service, safe houses, livelihood, economic support, and GBV referral pathways. Primary GBV prevention interventions such as EMAP, SASA, and the Community Cares program, integration of GBV into other sectoral responses including safety audits, strengthening of GBV coordination, and provision of dignity kits are urgently needed to save and protect lives of the most at-risk women, girls, and boys in locations facing catastrophic, extreme and severe GBV needs.

2 - Project activities

In accordance with Protection cluster strategy for HRP 2022, This project is going to focus on the following interventions: Provision of dignity kits to women and adolescent girls as a means of GBV risks mitigation . Provision of appropriate GBV case management and referral services including coordinated care and support. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral pathways and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, wellbeing and empowerment. Provision of livelihood and economic support including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectoral GBV response. Training and Provision of Fuel Efficient Stoves to women and adolescents girls as a measure to mitigate GBV. Conduct community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle. Conduct training and provide strengthening to community mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning. Provide capacity building on GBV guiding principles and response services(Case management, PSS etc.) To staff and frontline workers Conduct GBV awareness raising, including prevention messages using approaches such as SASA, EMAP, etc. GBV risks mitigation and recreational activities to foster harmony; Using Protection Monitoring System (PMS), timely GBV monitoring , coordination and assessments will be conducted within the deep locations of the prioritized counties using PMS tools to inform GBV response and advocacy as well as providing guidance to appropriate multi-sectoral interventions. CINA will make use of existing operational GBV tools including revised Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, GBVIMS, case management SOPs, MHPSS manual and GBV and CBI tip sheet and support to GBV survivors initiative during project planning, implementation and coordination.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Cash based programming for this project will be informed by a well coordinatedimproved market analysis, which will examine market functionality predictors such as an increased focus on income and impact measurement, absence of evidence of harming the market, functional and healthy market, increased availability of products in the market, increased quality products in the market, resilience of households, gender, protection and inclusion of vulnerable people, shifts in income and gender perception, decreased disease rate, quality occupancy of housing and increased labour capacity in construction, for it to feasibly implement cash based assistance.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

From CINA's past experience in cash programming in protection, cash-based intervention has the potential to respond quickly and efficiently to a variety of urgent protection needs offering dignity, flexibility and choice to beneficiaries and supporting local markets growth for long term development. Assistance covers both cash and vouchers that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief and which contribute to meeting their basicneeds or to assisting them in protecting or reestablishing their livelihoods. Cash-based assistance, particularly when delivered in the form of unconditional cash is a people-centered approach and greatly contributes to centrality of protection. For this project, GBV survivors would need cash to meet the medical, legal and psychosocial which are often accessible with cash out of pocket.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the most vulnerable groups like women, girls, persons with disabilities, including those who can not read/write will be involved during the activities in WGFS, and sensitized about their privileges and rights. Feedback from these groups will be collected during FGDs project M&Evisits and on regular basis by M&E Assistants. A complaint box and hotline will also be displayed andthese groups will specifically be empowered to provide their views using theseplatforms.About 15% of people targeted by this project will be persons with disabilities. They will be involved in project designs, implementation and monitoring and their feedback will be used to provide assistance. For example, the vulnerable PWDs, GBV survivors, etc. will decide which type of assistance eg. assistive devices, legal aid and counselling, medical referral, etc. should be provided for them?, protective cash or unconditional cash?, how to receive information and assistance?

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CINA and project team will ensure transparency and information sharing with different groups throughout the project cycle. During the project inception at the community, beneficiaries will be informed about the project objectives, targets, activities, and their participation in the project. Community-based protection networks will be formed/strengthened to convey any project information to the beneficiaries during the implementation. To ensure adequate beneficiary feedback, response and complaint mechanisms, Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGPS) will be established in the community and will be managed by community mobilizers who will use these forum to provide information to beneficiaries. Community Complaint boxes will be displayed in the WGFS, beneficiaries sensitized about its use, and will be usedto collect complaints. In addition, a hotline number will be displayed on GBV awareness banners, which the beneficiaries will use to report any complaint. During M&E activities, a beneficiary complaint and feedback form will be completed by project beneficiaries about their satisfaction on services provided.These complaints/feedback will be analyzed by CINA management and feedback promptly provided through Project Manager to the beneficiaries.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA has an existing PSEA policy and a PSEA focal person. CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. All CINA staffs are subjected to enhanced reference checks up on taking job offer. Acommitment statement on PSEA is found on CINA’s job adverts. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible. CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve PSEA objective. CINA will ensure project beneficiaries are sensitized about the PSEA, and their role to identify andreport it appropriately. CINA will display PSEA messages on the banner during awareness raising activities. A PSEA reporting form will be kept at Community centers and beneficiaries well be sensitized about it. They will use this to report cases directly to PSEA focal person at CINA's head Office. A hotline number and email address will also be displayed on the PSEA banner which beneficiaries can use to call and report PSEA cases in the community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CINA will ensure that women, men, children, adolescents, youth, persons with disabilities are involved and meaningfully participate in and influence decisions from the needs assessment, project designs, assistance delivery, through to project evaluation. To this end, their feedback during any stage of the project which will be used to guide planning and response. In particular, women and girls will provide feedback through Focus Group Discussions in the WGFS. They will also be specifically targeted during radio campaigns, Safety Audits and Needs Assessments and 16 Days of Activism to share their views, especially on how to receive GBV services in a dignified, gender-sensitive and respectful manner. These will be incorporated during targeting of beneficiaries, procurement of dignity kits and delivery of services including CMR, counselling, risks mitigation and prevention programming.
