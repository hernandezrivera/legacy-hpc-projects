Project Summary

According to the HNO-Protection Needs Overview of 2020, boys and girls continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights including family separation, sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), physical abuse, psychosocial distress, child labor, wide spread recruitment and use of children, forced displacement, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices and exploitation. This situation is exacerbated by the limited availability of basic services, weaken governance, negative social norms, and persistent marginalization. Persistent violations of children’s rights continue to be a critical concern in Somalia as children and adolescents are often targets of killing and maiming, abduction, recruitment into armed forces or armed groups, detention, and sexual and physical violence. From January to June 2019, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified and documented. 1,473 cases (1,272 boys and 201 girls) of violence against children, with abduction and recruitment/use of children accounting for 41% and 55 % of the overall number of children affected. Child labour was the most cited child protection concern. More than half of the respondents (55.8%) identified children in employment, 55.1% identified children engaged in shoe shining, and 40.6% children selling in the market. As well most of the children separated from their usual caregivers were most frequently mentioned in IDP camps (48.3%) and urban areas (44.1%) than rural locations (7.1%). Children associated with armed groups were reported by 19.8%. Child trafficking was reportedly 18.2% and in IDP camps, 43.4% in urban and 3.6% in rural areas. Physical abuse was also identified a child protection concern which mainly involved children being beaten as report shown by 16.4% of the respondents. The project is aiming to strengthen the existing and establishing new community based child protection mechanism in the target areas. The proposed interventions are within the 2020 Protection Cluster Objectives and Responses to address the recent conflict, displacement, and violence in the target areas are current priorities within the cluster. New Ways will respond to the needs of vulnerable populations including boys and girls from affected communities, IDPs and host communities in rural, remote and hard-to-reach areas. NEW WAYS will ensure to provide immediate comprehensive child projection care packages and complementarity services for all children with protection concerns and vulnerable disabled people in Elbur, Dhusamareeb Jowhar, Balcad, Barawe, Kuntuwarey, Walaweyn, Qoryoley and districts of Lower / Midde Shabelle, and Galgadud regions. Care services including awareness-raising and outreach on recruitment/CAAFAG, family separation and children's right, strengthening capacity of duty bearers, service providers and community leaders will be trained on protection, child protection case management, child friendly spaces and psychosocial support, support of family tracing & reunification for UASC and support for reintegration of released children and other vulnerable children in the community, provide structured psychosocial support to children of protection risk and their caregivers as well as strengthening of referral pathways as well as the project will ensure provision of protection-oriented support to people with specific needs or heightened vulnerability. The project will give special attention to reduction of child labour rate among the destitute and poor economic vulnerable people in each of the targeted areas by increasing number by 2022. New ways in close collaboration with its international partner ONE WORLD STRONG is aiming to largely contribute to the empowerment of disabled populations by establishing a new networking app (Similar to Facebook) that brings together survivors in Somalia. The overall project direct targeted beneficiaries are 42,994.00 (IDPs 14,798, and HC 28196).
