Project Summary

The proposed 12 months project is intended to enhance the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialised services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard to reach and priority geographical areas linked to intersectoral SO2. The intervention is designed to:
1) Increase access to life saving GBV case management, psycho-social support (PSS) and legal aid services for women, men, boys and girls.
2) Enhance the protective environment through protection programming by ensuring inclusive access to critical cross-sectoral services.
The project will cost $500,482 ($302,093 for GP+HLP and $198,389 for GBV) and will be implemented in Juba (Central Equatoria State), Rubkona County (Unity State) and Rumbek Center (Lakes State). It will provide key multi sectoral services that compliment case management and PSS, legal support and livelihood assistance for survivors according to their wishes. The multi sectoral services for survivors will be provided in line with GBV sub cluster standard operating procedures with a particular focus on guiding principles, survivor centered approaches and referral pathways.
According to the 2021 HRP targets for Rubkona, Juba and Rumbek, a total of 327,407 people will be targeted under GP, HLP and GBV. Of these, the project will benefit 17,570 internally displaced persons, returnees and the host communities comprising of 7475 women, 4020 men, 3575 girls and 2500 boys and 2624 persons with disabilities. STW has presence in Juba, Rumbek and Rubkona with available WGFs i.e 1 in Juba, 2 in Rumbek and 1 in Rubkona with available case workers i.e 2 per WGFs. Through these spaces, STW will provide case management through legal information, medical, PSS, livelihood and referral support to women and girls attending the WGFs in order to provide holistic GBV response. The utilization of the WGFs will be in line with South Sudan WGFs guidelines which ensures that women and girls are protected, empowered and feel physically and emotionally safe, comfortable and able to express themselves.
STW will also provide legal advise/counselling and awareness raising sessions to women, men, girls and boys who fear to come forward to access the available GBV services and also reach out to persons with disabilities and the sick in the neighborhoods and those who seek legal services at their convenience. These sessions are meant to encourage survivors to timely seek out for help provided at the health facilities and at the WGFs where specialized individual and group psycho-social support sessions are provided to survivors’ to facilitate healing process from traumatic events. 75 (51 GBV survivors & 24 HLP cases) who may wish to pursue legal services, will be supported through initiating legal proceedings according to their wishes.
STW will also provide trainings to male and female staff, traditional chiefs, women leaders, local authorities and partners on GBV legal frameworks in South Sudan, GBV guiding principles, referral pathway, the GBV IASC guidelines and legal aid. STW will train 210 men and women on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building aimed at addressing risks identified, and strengthen community based support mechanisms as a means of creating safe and meaningful access to relevant services. We shall also hold consultations with women, men, boys and girls throughout the project to ensure that their concerns are included in the project implementation. We will continue to ensure that all our activities take a “Do No Harm and conflict sensitive approach”. IDPs, returnees and local host communities will be targeted, as well as all those at risk of protection.

1 - Needs

The effects of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services continue to be felt throughout South Sudan with some 8.3 million—over two-thirds of the total population—are estimated to need humanitarian assistance in 2021 according to the findings reflected in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview-HNO. This is an increase of 800,000 people from 7.5 million people in need in 2020. The HNO notes insecurity, lack of basic services, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevented people from returning home in large numbers. Further, access to essential services, including health, protection and legal services, was already limited and much of the service infrastructure was damaged, destroyed or closed in 2020. According to the January 2021 South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot, Sub-national violence and insecurity continued to displace people in Central and Western Equatoria among others states. In Juba, daily reports of horrendous act of two types of rape on women and girls i.e. rape as sexual intercourse without the consent of a woman or against her will, and rape as early/child marriage, as provided in the Penal Code Act 2008, CAP 247. Women and girls in Juba like any other parts of South Sudan continue to grapple with rape and early/child marriage. The conflict has increasingly weakened survivors' protection pathways, and created gaps in the societal fabric. Displaced women and girls continue to be raped, and girls forced into marriage even in the IDP camps, including PoCs. Juba has remained catchment area for IDPs, including gangs. In Rumbek, most civilians face multiple forms of insecurity, adolescent girls are affected by the crisis in ways that are different both from adolescent boys and adult women, and in ways that is often overlooked. Physical violence- early and forced marriages continues to be the major source of insecurity to adolescent girls. About 77% of adolescent girls lack access to sufficient food, which limits their capacity to lead healthy lives. Adolescent girls are engaged in extra unpaid household care labour, which intensifies during emergencies such as COVID19 with closure of schools with negative impact on their education and life journeys. Adolescent girls only have sporadic access to medical and psychosocial support services [https://plan-uk.org/file/plan-uk-voices-from-south-sudan-reportpdf/download?token=D7xsjaUV, accessed 19th January, 2021. Adolescent girls rely on traditional coping strategies to tackle risks and vulnerabilities, which is inadequate. STW December 2020 field monitoring report in Rubkona found out that , the threat of GBV faced by women and girls namely forced child marriage and domestic violence remain on the increase. There are also gaps in the legal services that include absence of static formal courts which allows informal courts to handle GBV cases including rape. Traditional chiefs usually rule against the women who try to report their cases due to lack of knowledge about their jurisdiction. There are no permanent judges in Bentiu. The only permanent state prosecutor lives in Juba. For the judge and his team to be brought from Juba for the mobile court, cases have to be readily investigated. The UNMISS supported mobile court is meant for the Bentiu POC henceforth not benefiting returnees and host communities in the surrounding areas of the PoC. This proposed intervention will provide multi-sectoral support services with a focus on PSS, livelihood, basic medical support and legal services for women and girls to ensure holistic support for survivors but also provide the necessary legal information and counselling to women and girls including on HLP. STW consulted the National GBV sub-cluster, UNFPA, DRC and UNMISS Human Rights Division, State Directorates of Gender, Child &Social Welfare to inform the intervention of this project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

As provided in the STW PSEA Policy, STW recognizes that PSEA must be integral to its humanitarian and resilience programming work and will work with program partners to ensure the following interventions are implemented. STW will ensure the active participation of the beneficiaries we work with in assessing, planning, implementing, monitoring and evaluating programs through the systematic use of participatory methods. We shall ensure communicating complaints reporting mechanisms to the beneficiaries we work with and actively engaging them in the design of these mechanisms. STW recognizes that sexual exploitation and abuse is often grounded in gender and other inequalities, STEWARDWOMEN will ensure that development and humanitarian activities draw on women’s rights and intersectional feminist analysis. STW will ensure a clear risk assessment in place for all projects outlining sexual exploitation and abuse and safeguarding risks within the project and putting in place measures to address these in project plans and risk monitoring and that all responses are developed in a manner that balances respect for due process with prioritizing the safety, dignity and rights of survivors. We shall also integrate and mainstream PSEA into all development and humanitarian work, as well as promote standalone protection programming in humanitarian settings with clear complaints and reporting mechanisms. STW will ensure that assistance for those affected by violations is provided in a non-discrimination manner and in ways that prevent additional harm. STW will ensure that all staff, volunteers, contractors and partners are aware of the responsibility to maintain an environment that is free from sexual exploitation and abuse and to report any abuse they suspect or witness, STW, community or neighbourhoods.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be set up to monitor programme implementation and ensure the voices of the beneficiaries are heard. Accountability groups will be formed based on community-defined vulnerability criteria and consulted each month to provide feedback and influence our programming on an ongoing basis. STEWARDWOMEN will continue to support established survivor support groups through its communicate with communities strategy to reach out to women survivors with key messages on human rights, access to justice and referral pathway. Also, documentation of activities is a critical component of our communication strategy in the project. Stories of change will be documented in form of videos and newsletters. We will also ensure that a gender-sensitive community feedback mechanism (for consultation, feedback and complaints) is established to inform and change programming, including the establishment of accountability groups based on community-defined vulnerability criteria. The team plans to use Excel and SPSS software which can link beneficiary feedback and track responses over time. This will provide valuable lessons and accurate credible data for multiple actors on trends, the quality of current programming, and the potential for scaling up impact.
