Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to reduction of excess morbidity and mortality caused by common communicable epidemic prone diseases (malaria, AWD, pneumonia, measles and meningitis), STIs and injuries sustained from violent conflicts, through ensuring access to quality essential lifesaving integrated primary health care services to vulnerable women, men, girls and boys. This project is designed to benefit a total of 2,500 direct beneficiaries well segregated by age (1,175 children & 1,325 Adults), gender ( 1,275 females & 1,225 males) and Persons with disabilities being 15% (370 people) of the target population in Pibor (1,220 people) and Jur River (1,280) counties of Jonglei and WBeG states respectively. The estimated annual budget for the proposed activities is 150,000 USD.
The remaining PIN population from the target counties may indirectly contribute an estimated 1,500 indirect beneficiaries; men, women and children with special attention to the persons with specific needs among the affected and marginalized vulnerable people in IDPs, returnees and host communities often susceptible to highly endemic communicable disease burden and malnutrition scourge in the remote rural project designated locations of Pibor and Jur River counties.
Through this project, RHS will establish 2 static primary healthcare units (PHCUs) in Pibor (Wunkok PHCU) and Jur River (Maranya PHCU, Kuarjena) counties, and continue supporting its current 3 mobile clinics at Teneth, Manydakel (Pibor) and Maranya (Jur River) to scale-up healthcare services to vulnerable populations; IDPs, returnees and host communities. This will ensure continuity of essential health care services aimed at saving lives through provision of timely integrated nutrition, WASH, GBV sectoral-related services to reduce morbidity, mortality, suffering and enhance resilience to acute shocks & chronic stress and vulnerable; women, men, girls, boys, elderly and people living with disabilities, chronic illnesses recovery from the crisis. This is linked to CO1 which aims at the reduction of excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases. The static (PHCUs) and mobile clinics will provide OPD consultations, ANC & PNC; conducting clean, safe and hygienic deliveries by skilled birth attendants, EPI and mass vaccination of children under 5 years, screening, treatment and referral of MAM/SAM, ill-health conditions to the next level of healthcare services (PHCC), integrated disease surveillance & response (IDSR)/EWARN, hygiene promotion and health education, training of key project staff in preparedness, response and control of epidemic prone disease outbreaks with the focus on cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis and pneumonia and other viral diseases; Ebola, Yellow fever and hepatitis A,B,C &, E as per CO2 which aims to improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations. The project will paying special attention to management & referral of SGBV, clinical management of rape cases (CMR) and basic mental health psycho-social support (counselling) in line with CO3, increasing access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.
Other activities will include; management of war wounded injuries, provision of basic & minimal initial HIV/AIDS/TB services, Screening services (RDT) to pregnant women, distribution of LLITNs and micro nutrient supplementation, deworming, growth monitoring and nutrition assessment to under 5 children.
The above project activities are equally in keeping with the Cluster objectives 1, 2 & 3. Lastly rigorous monitoring, evaluation and reporting with appropriate response mechanisms shall be important components of the project.

1 - Needs

An estimated 4.8 million people including vulnerable women (especially pregnant and lactating women) children and people with disabilities have no access to sufficient healthcare, WASH, nutrition and protection-related services in the country. Former Jonglei and WBeG states, with 1.25 million (64.8%) and 440,000 (68%) vulnerable populations respectively, are expected to be in IPC 3 & 4. As acute food insecurity persists-driven by localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions-increased levels of acute malnutrition are expected as well, and high morbidity, poor access to health, nutrition and WASH services will continue in the coming year in Pibor and Jur River Counties. (IPC Aug. 2019)The prevailing health system challenges are exacerbated by lack of access to clean safe water, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management culture, extensive malnutrition and inadequate health workforce amid threats of disease outbreaks in the counties. Displaced populations face the most complex challenges in accessing healthcare services exacerbated by the current flooding in Pibor County. Children under 5 years are among the most vulnerable to the 6 child-killer diseases, owing to poor nutrition and low levels of immunity; this is complicated by immunization coverage which remains under 30% in the county (below the country percentage coverage estimated at 48%) (Health Cluster, 2019). Consequently, 42% of children less than 1 year are at risk of measles since 2017-2019 (OCHA, Jan 2019). The two counties are expected to experience the highest risk (73.0% and 79% of crisis, emergency or catastrophe) (IPC, 2109). According to IOM DTM March-June 2019 and UNHCR Aug 2019 reports, conflicts/unrest in Kuarjena & Rocrocdong Payams of Jur River County resulted in the displacement of 9,672 individuals from an estimated 1,512 HHs from Akau, Werbet and Madhony with spontaneous settlement at Akwoy, Kuarjena and Mapel villages within host community settings and POC. About 24,009 IDPs in Jebel Boma Payam (Upper Boma, Nawayapuru and Nyat), Pibor County (Medair, 2019) are in dire need of urgent humanitarian assistance and response. While there are few returnees due to continued uncertainty about the current peace process, relative calm & stability in WBeG has led to arrival of about 3,793 returnees; Jur River with 769 individuals/128 HHs in Kuarjena (in Akwoy, Mapel & Kuarjena villages) and Jonglei state with 44,024 returnees; 1,250 individuals/208 HHs in Pibor Payam) and 128 voluntarily returned individuals/21 HHs in Teneth cantonment site (RHS, Jul- Aug 2019). Inter-communal tensions and dry season-based cattle raiding persist in both Counties in the past year with diseases and related population dynamics driving the humanitarian needs compounded by the remoteness and difficulty of physical access to many parts of the regions, restricting humanitarian presence. Thus, immediate and unhindered scale-up of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring in the locations is required. Pibor and Jur River population estimates 24,879 and 273,118 respectively (NBS 2019 population projections). According to the RHS Pibor and Jur River field reports, measles outbreak is currently very high and endemic conditions like malaria (43%), AWDs (29%) and RTI (19.3%) remain the leading causes of morbidity and mortality. High GAM prevalence rates between 10-15%, affecting mainly children, elderly and the PLWs across the various communities cut off from life-saving health care services is also a matter of concern. It is also reported that high GBV caseloads in Pibor (59%) and Jur River (29%) reveal the need to double efforts in the area. This project therefore aims to scale up health response in the two counties by upgrading the two mobile clinics at Maranya and Manydakel while taking on two more mobile clinics in addition to the current one at Teneth.

2 - Activities

The project activities are need-based; special consideration of the needs of various vulnerable gender, Disabled persons and age groups; men, women, children and persons with specific needs (persons living with disabilities, chronically ill and elderly persons) has been accorded. RHS project activities will include the following:1. OPD curative consultations including; integrated clinical case management (iCCM) of highly endemic communicable diseases; Malaria, AWDs, RTI (Pneumonia), STIs and epidemic-prone diseases 2. Screening services for; malnutrition with MUAC screening of U5 children and PLWs for MAM/SAM cases, Rapid testing of malaria for under 5 children and pregnant women, Tetanus vaccination of pregnancy, vaccination needs to all under 5, Uncomplicated diseases & TB.3. Provision of essential Health care services for; Management of uncomplicated MAM cases in areas with severe food insecurity through OPT, SFP, Outpatient consultation for common diseases, Vaccination for pregnant women on Tetanus (if not received), Routine EPI & Mass vaccination (case management for epidemic-prone diseases; Meningitis, Measles and OCV), Provision of psycho-social support therapy, Basic management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care ), MCH (ANC & PNC services /check-ups and normal delivery, First aid and basic management of war-wounded and mass casualties management, Establishment of ORS Corners & Outpatient for TB , Application of the minimal Initial response for HIV, Treatment of in-health facilities Water, Vector control and medical waste management4. Treatment of uncomplicated; MAM with RUFT: Plumpy nut, BP5, AWDs cases with Zinc and ORS, RTI (Pneumonia) Amoxicillin and Malaria (RDT) with ACT, Rectal Artesunate and Paracetamol.5. Referral of complicated medical cases, SAM & SGBV survivors with medical complications to PHCC, Upgraded PHCC or County Hospital6. Promotive and preventive health services; Folic Acid distribution to PLWs, Vitamin A supplementation to under 5 children every 6 months, Malaria RDT and prophylaxis for pregnant women, Deworming treatment for all under 5 children every 6 months, Condom distribution to reproductive age groups, Training of CHW on minimal initial response for HIV, Routine immunization against all national target diseases and ensuring adequate cold chain in place, ANC & PNC check-up with Family planning services and Introduce minimum WASH standards7. Health promotion and risk communication; Education/ Promotion of exclusive breastfeeding, Health seeking behavior, Malaria; avoiding stagnant water pool, Use of bed nets, Use of clean water and sanitary facilities, Awareness raising of the availability of services for SGBV survivors, TB and people living with HIV, Mobilization for vaccination campaigns8. Surveillance/EWARN & Reporting; Epidemiological surveillance IDSR/EWARN data at health facility and community levels, Active case finding for Epidemic-prone diseases, HIV & TB, Basic lab diagnostic (Malaria, TB, HIV/AIDS, Kala-Azar), activity-based reporting, Sentinel site of EWARS of epidemic prone diseases, outbreak response, Emergency Cholera treatment unit (CTU)

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project is based on a protection risk analysis because it pays specific attention to the needs of the different groups in a gender sensitive manner. other cultural sensitivities and variations will be put in much consideration during implementation of the project. Specific respect will be given to each beneficiary to take part in the decision making process. We also take special precautions to protect children especially unaccompanied minors in the IDP Communities. This project will involve communities and households in particular, because it has designed to protect the vulnerable household members especially women, girls and children from sexual exploitation and abuse. Complaints that will not reach through the normal channels i.e. from the supervisors and project officers shall be received directly by the M & E Field visitors. After proper investigation of the alleged issues, the head office shall provide feedback to both beneficiaries and partners including the corrective measures taken, equal involvement of the beneficiaries in decision making, giving them opportunity to employment and empowering them to avoid and report violence against other more vulnerable groups ( women, girls, boys and disable groups) within the community.
