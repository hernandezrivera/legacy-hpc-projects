Project Summary

The BAY States have remained a priority states for WASH intervention since the start of the ongoing BH conflict and continues to have a significant IDP population and affected host population that remain vulnerable under the current circumstances. Following numerous interventions in the states with the additional burden of COVID-19 Pandemic and high possibility of Cholera and acute watery diarrhea (AWD) diseases outbreak, Caritas Nigeria will undertake an emergency WASH intervention focused on preventing the spread of these diseases among vulnerable populations of IDPs and Host Communities. Proposed target LGAs include: Yola North and South, Gerei and Mubi North and Mubi South (in Adamawa State); and Magumeri, Konduga, Kaga, Chbok and Gwoza (in Borno State). All these locations have benefitted from emergency WASH and NFI response by the WASH Sector partners, including Caritas Nigeria, however, there is now an increased need to continue with an improved WASH interventions in these areas to ensure that minimum standards are at least met for the population.
This response will be focused on increasing access to safe water supply and improved hygiene and sanitary practices among vulnerable populations of concern through context specific approaches that acknowledge the limited access to sustained material support and the potential for future displacement of the population. More specifically this emergency WASH intervention will aim to increase basic access to safe drinking and portable water, improved sanitation and hygiene practices through gender, age and vulnerability specific messaging and community hygiene promotion campaigns. Climate-smart approach will be adopted in the programming wherein renewable energy is prioritized; in addition, a focus will be on rehabilitation and repairs of dysfunctional water points rather than new drillings with reticulation to other points for increased access to potable water.
Provision of safe water supply to affected populations remains one of the main priorities for the WASH Sector strategy and this project, will work through a gender sensitive approach to improve access to safe sanitation facilities by repairing latrines, bathing shelter, desludging and decommissioning of filled up latrines in IDP camps; and improving access to safe water sources for the communities through chlorination of water sources, repairs and rehabilitation of water facilities, installation of Handwash stations in healthcare facilities; installation of waste disposal bins in IDP camps and healthcare facilities and training gender­ balanced water user committees and technicians for future repairs and sustainability. For hygiene and sanitation, actions will be taken to improve knowledge and behaviour through targeted messaging, with targeted distribution of hygiene Kits, NFI and personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitary supplies to the most vulnerable groups. Community hygiene committees will be formed and trained to continue the hygiene promotion activities within target communities for sustained good hygiene practices after the end of project. The intervention will contribute to increased community preparedness for WASH associated disease outbreaks through prepositioning of items and training of staff and community workers on acute watery diarrhea, cholera and COVID-19 response activities.
The result is to provide people with both the capacity and knowledge to maintain an adequate level of protection against disease outbreak while improving their overall hygiene practices that will pave the way for a more long term WASH programming in the area. These activities will lead to reduced incidents of water related diseases, thus reducing morbidity, mortality and malnutrition.

1 - Needs

A rapid needs assessments was conducted by Caritas Nigeria using our field officers in some of the target locations to assess the WASH needs in some communities and IDP camps, hence, this proposal is premised on those findings. We will also leverage on the WASH sector needs overview for the North East Nigeria.The collapse of the health system due to the insurgency has exacerbated prevalence of communicable diseases. In the NE Nigeria, intestinal diseases are second only to malaria and acute respiratory illnesses (ARIs). Children are particularly vulnerable with 90 percent of mortality attributed to diarrheal diseases being children under 5 years. Diarrheal diseases are the second cause of mortality after malnutrition for children under 5 years. Water borne diseases including cholera are endemic to the region. In the latest outbreak, over 6,367 cases with 73 associated deaths (CFR – 1.15%) were reported in Borno State in December 2018. These cases were concentrated in the following LGAs (Jere – 2,482); MMC (1,556), Magumeri (340), Konduga (166), Chibok (136), Askira-Uba (91) and Kwaya-Kusar (161). Out of the 196 samples collected and tested in the State using Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs), 161 (82%) were positive while 40 (44%) of 91 samples were culture positive. The World Bank attributes 88 percent of intestinal illnesses to lack of potable water, proper sanitation and hygiene knowledge. Unhygienic practices, open defecation, poor access to safe water and toilet / latrines particularly during the current conflict, high levels of poverty and the poor state of healthcare are among the factors that exacerbate vulnerabilities to cholera and other water borne diseases. Violence and the associated destruction of property and displacement has offset the gains made in reducing Open Defecation that stood at 11.4 percent in 2014 in the NE. OCHA estimated that the ongoing conflict had destroyed 75 percent of WASH infrastructure in NE Nigeria. Access to potable water in region is highly restricted as insecurity has rendered some water sources inaccessible, insurgents have deliberately destroyed part of the water infrastructure and the sudden influx of IDPs has overwhelmed water sources that are accessible. From the rapid Assessment conducted in Mubi North and South, Magumeri, Konduga, Kaga, Yola North and South LGAs by Caritas Nigeria and JDPC Maiduguri Diocese, the following were reported: • The available latrines in these communities are pit latrines. Most of them are Public Pit latrines, they are not separated by gender or age or disability status. • There are currently no facilities for hand washing, no soaps, and the women do not wash their hands before cooking, after cooking and after going to toilet. • There are no available facilities or mechanisms for solid waste disposal. When the latrines are saturated, they are abandoned and another latrine is constructed.• The current sources of water include 80% wells and 20% boreholes and hand-pumps. It is important to note that in these communities, the aquifer is very low. The depth runs from depths of 120 metres. As such, the construction of boreholes and hand-pumps may be difficult. Wells that are in use usually dry up between February – May annually. In the communities there are only 12 boreholes, out of which, only one is functional. • The extant sources of drinking water are Hand-pumps and wells. These water sources are not treated. Water is not available at all times. Walk to the water sources are safe for everyone and are at maximum 2-3 kilometres. • There are not enough containers for everyone for water collection.The Covid-19 pandemic has further exacerbated the WaSH humanitarian situation in these locations; those already predisposed to Cholera, Diarrhea, Typhoid (due to lack or water or contamination) have increased chances of being affected by the Covid-19 Virus. Young school girls of reproductive age are predisposed to infections due to lack of menstrual hygiene

2 - Activities

• Increase access to water for vulnerable people through the rehabilitation of damaged WASH infrastructure (especially boreholes) and the conversion of some from Generator powered borehole to Solar motorized in communities in Magumeri, Kaga, Gwoza and Mubi South LGAs. This is drawn from the fact that these locations have demonstrated Water Supply needs. As mentioned previously, Caritas Nigeria will increase its focus on climate smart technologies and strategies through the repairs and rehabilitation of dysfunctional water points. Also, the motorised water points will be converted to renewable energy source such as solar-powered water points. This also ensures sustainability while reducing carbon footprint through the use of fossil fuel. WaSHComms will be trained on the maintenance of these water points and will be equipped with Toolkits to ease their operations• Increase access to latrines for vulnerable persons, alongside hygiene promotion activities using the school and community-led total sanitation approach (SLTS and CLTS): In all aforementioned LGAs, there were still prevalence of poor hygiene stemming from open defecation. Open defecation breeds diseases and bacteria that could be injurious to the health of the already vulnerable persons. • Distribution of Water, hygiene and dignity kits: considering the departure of the WaSH sector from in-kind hygiene kits to multipurpose cash, Caritas Nigeria will, following in-depth needs assessments in the locations, adopt multi-purpose cash transfer to selected vulnerable Households to enable them address their hygiene needs. • Water quality control of existing water sources (test and disinfection of water source): Most existing water sources are untreated and after testing samples, there were salmonela typhi, e.coli, and other harmful bacteria, mostly from open defecation. Ensuring treatment of existing and new water sources in the selected locations will reduce/mitigate health implications coming from contaminated water sources. • Establishment of WASHCOMS to ensure sustainability with regards to the operation and maintenance of WASH infrastructure: A basic standard is the training of WaSHCOMs in the communities to better equip them for the maintenance and repairs of the water points in the communities. - Young school girls of reproductive age will be trained on the fabrication of reusable sanitary pads, and will also benefit from the distribution of reusable sanitary wares. - In schools in primary healthcare institutions, proper hand washing culture will be promoted through the sensitization and distribution of foot operated handwashing stations. Caritas Nigeria has capacity and expertise in WASH and has been an active partner in the WASH sector since 2014. With the past and current humanitarian interventions across the BAY states and other states in Nigeria over the years, Caritas Nigeria Caritas Nigeria has developed institutional capacity in WASH interventions and can deliver on all WASH components. Since 2014, Caritas Nigeria has continued to implement lifesaving WASH interventions across the BAY States and other locations across Nigeria where there is need for life saving emergency WASH interventions. Caritas Nigeria derives its funding from a variety of donors/partner agencies including, Australian Direct Aid Program (DAP), UNICEF, Caritas Confederation member organizations, Czech Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Union Bank Nigeria Plc, Malteser International and Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs among others. Caritas has demonstrated capacity in its WSH interventions covering hygiene promotion campaigns; distribution of WASH/hygiene kits and Dignity kits; geophysical surveys; construction and rehabilitation of boreholes and wells, construction/conversion of boreholes to solar powered water facilities and reticulation to neigbouring communities and construction of WASH stations in schools and hospitals; provision of mobile foot operated hand-wash stations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Caritas Nigeria will use a multi-sectoral approach to address the needs of refugees, displaced persons and returnees, in order to provide holistic services which will ensure the wellbeing of all beneficiaries. This will be achieved through various strategies, some key strategies for health & nutrition intervention will include: Community mobilization, sensitization, awareness raising and education using various platforms including already established extensive church structures and systems; Capacity building and system strengthening for healthcare workers in the diagnosis and management of GBV; Direct service provision such as HIV testing and counseling services and community diagnosis and referral of identified non communicable disease to nearest health facilities; Referral and support services; Food demonstration to improve nutritional status of beneficiaries. Given the peculiarities of the SGBV project, several platforms have been identified to provide SGBV interventions as best practices. These will form Prevention and Response components; Stakeholder engagement, awareness creation on SGBV using community platforms to implement Gender Norms Interventions (GNI). Post GBV care services will be provided to identified SGBV survivors as part of the clinical and non-clinical response services to SGBV. Caritas Nigeria will establish Community-based Protection Committees that will support restructuring when necessary, to ensure active involvement of women, young people and marginalized groups. The strategies highlighted to carry out gender activities within already existing platform where GNI cohort coordinators/champions will be selected and trained to provide community Cohort Sessions in selected communities with high prevalence of SGBV cases includes;• Cohort (homogeneous groups) sessions that include gender based issues will be discussed.• A total of 10 meetings of one hour over the 1-year period will be counted for reached individuals. To respond to SGBV cases identified and reported, SGBV Champions will be identified and trained to provide psychosocial first aid and refer for Post GBV care First aid services provided in health camps located in the communities and link survivors for other post GBV care services mapped out in the communities that provide clinical and non-clinical services not provided in health posts.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A feedback mechanism shall be instituted through complaints/suggestion boxes, toll free lines as well as stakeholders email address at designated locations within the communities which will be accessible to all, with dedicated contacts details for complaints/feedback will also be made clearly visible at these points for those who may wish to call or email their complaints and or feedback. The Community Implementation Committees and the various support groups shall designate one or two members with a clear mandate to animate the complaints/feedback of affected individuals to Caritas Nigeria.We will also institute a quarterly beneficiary satisfactory survey to get feedback from the beneficiaries with regards to the program, as well as take their inputs to ensure a better programming subsequentlyA monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning framework will be established to ensure that the beneficiaries participate fully in the monitoring the implementation of the project. This plan will keep track of activities through regular communication with field staff, weekly management meetings and monthly project review meetings. Appropriate tools for gathering information at community level will be designed with input from the CPICs and beneficiaries. Gathering of monitoring data for the project will be done by diocesan Caritas staff and partners. Information on project progress will be shared, adjustments made as required and lessons learnt quickly put into use.
