Project Summary

QC will provide a comprehensive Education response to girls and boys considering CwDs in NWS especially, in areas with high severity as per to OCHA, by:
1- Increase physical access to safe learning environment for all children by:
In 2022:
a- Provision of 20 Classroom caravan (42m2) and 4 WASH caravans with one WC for CwDs in each for (1,400) displaced children in Aleppo camps, equipped with full school furniture.
b- Suppling of 285 Tent-classrooms to camps and crowded schools in Idleb to provide more opportunities for 19,950 children to access education.
c- Conduct rehabilitation works for 720 classrooms in Azaz, Albab, Afrin, Tal Abyad and Ras Alayn in Aleppo. which will benefit 43,200 students.
d- Construction of 16 classrooms school in Albab and 4 medium capacity schools in Qabasin camps in Aleppo where no schools are there. Schools will receive 2,240 children.
d- Ensure that all provided services support the equal access of children (girls and boys) with special focus on CwDs.
e- Ensure that all the provided, rehabilitated/constructed learning spaces have good ventilation and provide air circulation to mitigate COVID-19 spread risk.
f- Provision of BTL campaigns targeting 8,000 children in Idleb camps with open day activities.
g- Support access to formal Education for 97,390 students in Aleppo and Idleb (1,400 in school caravans + 19,950 will benefit tent classrooms + 43,200 will benefit 720 rehabilitated classrooms + 2,240 will benefit constructed schools in Albab + 22,600 student in 27 supported schools by QC in Idleb + 8,000 in BTL campaigns).
In 2023:
a- Construction of 2 schools in Azaz and Afrin to provide more learning spaces.
b- Replace tents in camps with school caravans in Zoghra camp in Jarablus.
2- Enhance the Quality of Education services by:
In 2022:
a- Provision of learning materials (Schoolbags, stationery kit, teacher's kit) for 24,350 students girls and boys (22,600 in QC schools in Idleb + 1,750 in Aleppo camps in Qabasin) and 800 Teachers and education personnel in QC supported schools in Idleb and western rural of Aleppo.
b- Enhance the capacity of Teachers and school cadre (females and males) by conducting physical training to 2,500 teachers about EiE and CPMS (considering WHO recommendation about COVID-19). Physical training will be conducted in cooperation with EDs in the targeted schools.
c- Provision of Teachers' incentives for 882 teachers and other Education personnel (females and males) for one academic year.
d- Printing and distributing educational curriculum in place to the benefit of 240,000 (SIG curriculum) students (girls and boys), considering the sensitivities of the Syrian crisis.
e- Provision of fuel and other running cost for 27 schools (22,600 students; 882 teachers and Edu personnel) supported by QC in Idleb area.
In 2023:
a- provision of teachers incentives for 500 teachers and other Education personnel in Idleb schools.
b- Printing of textbooks for 500,000 students (girls and boys).
c- Provision of Stationery kits for 12,000 students in Idleb schools.
d- Provision of running costs for 11 schools in Idleb.
All activities will be monitored by QC MEAL staff to ensure the quality of provided services. QC has developed tools and surveys to collect data through all stages of the project. Accountability mechanism that is child friendly is also in place in all schools. PDM reports will be issued to measure the satisfaction of beneficiaries about the received kits. Teachers learning cycle and PTA ToRs and meeting documents are developed to be shared on monthly bases.
Coordination with stakeholders is ongoing by signing MoUs with partners and education authorities in Aleppo and Idleb, where QC has a valid access and big community acceptance. QC signed protocols with Turkish EDs and has a valid access to the areas in northern Aleppo.
IPs, under QC technical supervision will implement project activities and report to QC which will later report to EDU cluster.

4 - Needs

The needs in Education sector remains huge more than before: 1- 25 Verified attacks on schools causing causalities among students and educational cadre, according to HNO 2022. 2- Over 20,000 teachers are working in Opposition-held areas. Around %35 of them don't receive salaries and working voluntarily as per to ED annual report in Idleb. 3- 58 secondary schools are not supported out of 112 schools in Idleb as per to ED statistics. 4- Many students continue to learn in unconducive and/or unsafe learning environments. At the national level there is an average of one operational classroom for every 54 school aged child. The highest ratios are found in Idleb (1:178), as per to HRP 2022. 5- more than 25 tent classrooms in Zoghra camp in Jarablus are in a very miserable condition and need to be replaced with caravans that provide minimum protection and safety to children and preserve their dignity - QC need assessment 2021. 6- COVID-19 still a big threat in NWS. Less than %50 of teachers are vaccinated. Rumors about the side effects of vaccine. in start of 2021/2022 academic year, the EDs in Aleppo and Idleb delayed the start of the school year to October instead of Sep 2021 due to the wide spread of COVID-19 in NWS. 7- Education fund is shrinking, and donors are less interested to support Education activities - Education cluster. 8- Out of school children remain one of the most affected groups. Over three quarters of 12-17year-olds who were not in school had dropped out.

- Increase access to formal Education to 97,390 students’ girls and boys by: -Suppling of 285 Tent-classrooms and 20 school caravans (42m2) in addition to 4 WASH caravans (4 WCs in each, one for CwDs) to the camps and crowded schools in Idlib and Aleppo. All tents and caravans are equipped with school furniture (desks, table and chair for teacher, FAK and fire extinguishers). - Conducting rehab works for 720 classrooms in Azaz, Albab, Afrin in Aleppo, Tal Abyad in Raqqa and Ras Alayin in Hasakah. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Children are deprived their right to Education and forced to leave school and work to help their families. Mitigation measures: - QC through BTL campaigns will advocate to child right to education through tent to tent visits, explaining NFE mechanisms like SLP which is flexible to meet best interest of child. - QC will target the caregivers of children with cash for work activities, where children should achieve %80 attendance every months. Risk 2: CwDs cannot access Education because of physical barriers. Mitigation measures: - Ease access for CwDs in all QC supported schools by adding ramps and side tubes and WASH facilities that meet the needs of CwDs.- Provision of school transportation when requested, to bring CwDs to schools. - Ensure the integration for CwDs in all Education and PSS/recreational activities.- Raise awareness for CwDs caregivers about the importance of education. QC will commit to Do no harm principle by committing to Education cluster salary scale for Education personnel. This Project adopts protection mainstreaming approach in project design and implementation including: 1- Preserve safety and dignity of students and school cadre (females, males, girl and boys) by provision of safe learning spaces (classroom caravans) and separated sanitation caravans to girls and boys ensuring each genders' privacy, 2- Ensuring meaningful access to all beneficiaries including those with special needs by adding ramps which allows children access.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R1: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by adults such as teachers, school personnel, organization staff, other adults or older students. Mitigation measures: - QC will ensure a survivor centered approach that imposes safety and confidentiality of the affected person. - QC have an updated service mapping to refer such cases to experienced organizations. - QC has a PSEA Code of conduct, that all children and Edu personnel are aware of and commit to apply.R2: Female teachers are being harassed by male teachers, school principals or indirectly through WhatsApp groups. Mitigation measures: - QC will make sure that a very transparent channel with immediate response in place, and all staff are aware how to report a SEA violation. - Include indirect question about SEA in the individual surveys when meeting teachers in-person. R3: Girls are getting harassed in their way to school which is far from where they live. Mitigation measures: - To provide school transportation for adolescent girls. - To raise awareness through community leaders on harassment through all the available channel (Juma' prayer, schools, etc.)


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1:- Suspension of cross-border assistance from Turkey to NWS.Mitigation Measures:- To work in coordination with AFAD and Turkish Red Crescent to deliver aids to Syria.- To agree with the suppliers to provide all the goods to inside of Syria through border gates as a commercial materials. - Participate in the different coordination bodies to organize assistance and avoid duplication to NWS.Risk 2:- School closure in northwest Syria due to the increasing number of COVID-19 cases.Mitigation Measure: - To shift to Distance learning modality whenever possible. - To encourage teachers to get vaccinated by addressing their concerns and solve them. - To disinfect schools and educational spaces. Risk 3: Teachers or other education personnel participate in a strike to stop working voluntarily. Strike may close schools and more children would ask to be registered in QC supported schools. Mitigation measures: - QC will discuss with caregivers the limitation of spaces in the supported schools. – QC will be part of advocacy campaign to highlight the issue of supporting teachers’ incentives on regular basis. Risk 4: The inflation in Turkey against the USD which impact directly on NWS in procurement and staff salaries. Mitigation measures: - plan budgets with USD to avoid any inflation on TL and pay all education personnel with USD. – support schools with running costs and support children with stationery to encourage them to attend schools.
