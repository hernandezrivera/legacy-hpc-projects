Project Summary

The project aims to achieve the main goals:
Vulnerabilities of IDPs and recently war-affected populations in the Al-Bab and Jarablus districts of Aleppo are reduced through improved access to safe water and equitable access to wash facilities with the provision of personal hygiene items in emergency settings to the targeted population/IDPs, conforming to the sphere standards and local customs, taking into consideration the special needs of men, women, boys, and girls, and paying special attention to the needs of the disabled persons in the camps and informal settlements. QRC will mainstream protection for all aspects of this intervention during the project cycle.
The proposed intervention will provide life-saving integrated WASH services (safe and adequate water supply, provision and maintenance of gender-appropriate water distribution points, water quality monitoring, solid waste collection, and management, as well as the periodic distribution of hygiene items accompanied by hygiene promotion based on training for community hygiene promoters) in camps and informal settlements for IDPs displaced from Idleb, in addition to long-term IDPs living in settlements. This intervention supports the main strategic priority and matches with WASH sector objective 2 "Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to the most vulnerable people." This intervention is directly supporting WASH cluster strategic objective 1 "Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people, with emphasis on areas with high severity of needs, including those in UN-declared besieged and hard-to-reach areas."
QRC’s WASH field team intervention will provide basic WASH support to internally displaced populations who have settled in 55 camps and settlements in the Al-Bab and Jarablus districts of Aleppo. QRC’s framework for response builds on 12 months of intervention and seeks to ensure that the households residing in these settlements continue to survive in a way that respects their right to water and safety. Humanitarian actors in Syria have struggled to secure funding for informal camps and self-settlements, where conditions have continued to deteriorate as influxes continue to arrive in what remains of a haven in Syria. QRC will provide chlorinated water to 40,600 individuals in 55 camps and settlements in Al-Bab and Jarablus districts. The trucks used to transfer this water will be cleaned and chlorinated by QRC staff to prevent waterborne diseases. The water will be delivered to steel tanks (QRC will provide 1587 tanks during this intervention), which will provide a ‘water point’ in the settlement where beneficiaries will be able to access water at their convenience, decided on through discussions with the community. Each water tank can provide water to four people simultaneously, and the placement of the water tanks will reduce the waiting time and distance that people have to travel to access water. QRC will test this water (at the source, tankers, and in tanks) on an ad hoc basis to ensure it is potable.
Based on QRC's observations in camps, much-needed de-sludging will be undertaken in 50 of the camps; the total quantities will be 711 trucks (with 4500 liters as capacity), to ensure their ongoing operationality and to prevent overflow and the dangerous risk of spreading disease.
QRC will remove solid waste from 55 camps and remove the waste to landfills; this is a crucial step since much of the waste is currently dumped within proximity to the camps. QRC will distribute rubbish bins to prevent littering. QRC will distribute 9,022 Hygiene Kits in 55 camps. Important hygiene promotion will take place in gender-segregated groups, accompanied by the distribution of hygiene kits. This component contributes to the mobilization of the community, as the hygiene promoters will come from the communities themselves.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

QRC conducted comprehensive assessments across 55 camps situated in north Aleppo (Euphrates Shield), revealing persistent and substantial WASH-related deficiencies adversely impacting internally displaced persons (IDPs). The challenges encompass access to safe water, sanitation facilities, waste management, and essential hygiene provisions, posing formidable hurdles for both displaced communities and vulnerable households. Regrettably, existing camp infrastructure and resource allocations fall short of meeting the exigencies of the swiftly growing population. Nonetheless, a positive note emerges as 55 camps in the Al-Bab and Jarablus districts benefit from a consistent supply of potable, chlorinated water on a daily basis.Within the context of Protection Risk Analysis, identified vulnerabilities such as gender-insensitive hygiene kits and precarious water distribution channels present discernible threats, both in probability and consequence. Proposed interventions underscore the imperative to standardize gender-sensitive hygiene provisions, ensuring equitable distribution and adherence to recognized SPHERE standards. Mitigation strategies pivot on securing requisite funding, optimizing logistical support, and mobilizing specialized personnel adept in social engagement and hygiene promotion. Crucially, the paramountcy of accessible water distribution, particularly for marginalized groups, mandates sustained financial backing and dedicated workforce allocation.As an engaged member of the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Network in Gaziantep, QRC actively advocates for the establishment of robust inter-agency reporting mechanisms aligned with community preferences and sensitivities. Staff members pledge adherence to a stringent code of conduct, safeguarding the confidentiality of beneficiary information. Moreover, QRC has instituted a comprehensive feedback mechanism, inclusive of a mobile hotline, facilitating beneficiary engagement and soliciting valuable input.
