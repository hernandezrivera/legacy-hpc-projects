Project Summary

Under the proposed program, LABDI will provide vital food assistance to 15,000 vulnerable households in Geidam and Yunusari LGAs, Yobe State. The decision to target households and individuals based outside of camp-settings is based on our gap assessments and analysis. For instance, in Geidam, WFP is currently handling food assistance from camps, while LABDI will be providing assistance in the host communities. This will help in the coordination and collaboration under the proposed intervention. In Geidam, WFP is providing food assistance in the camps in Yusufari, but a major gap in food assistance remains in the host community. This proposed intervention will fill this gap.

1 - Needs

According to the latest OCHA Situation Report (26 November 2020), 4.3 million people in Northeast Nigeria are in need of food security assistance – with that number predicted to rise to 5.1 million people by June 2021 – numbers not seen in Northeast Nigeria since 2016-2017 when the region was facing a famine. Currently, both the most recent Cadre Harmonise (October 2020) and FEWSNET (https://fews.net/west-africa/nigeria) are predicting Geidam and Yusufari LGAs to be in IPC Phase 3 (Critical) from early 2021 onwards. In particular, the period from the start of land-cultivation and planting(March/April) to the harvest (September/October) marks a period of time where food insecurity is predicted to increase in the locations targeted under this proposed intervention. This may be exacerbated further if vulnerable populations are cut off from their current humanitarian assistance – with FEWSNET predicting the possibility of this leading to instances IPC Phase 4 or even IPC Phase 5, i.e. famine. The latest FEWSNET update notes that “households worst-affected by the conflict who are unable to engage in normal livelihood activities, facing constraints in accessing markets, and remaining inaccessible to humanitarian actors will most likely have significant difficulty accessing food. As a result, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes are expected in some inaccessible areas of the northeast. Areas where populations are affected by significant losses of livelihood activities and who remain inaccessible to humanitarian actors are likely facing similar or worse food security outcomes as adjacent, accessible areas. A risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) persists in the northeast in the event there is a dramatic shift or uptick in conflict that would significantly reduce household movement to access food and income sources and humanitarian access for a prolonged period of time”.

2 - Activities

Under the proposed program, LABDI will provide vital food assistance to 15, 000 vulnerable households in Geidam and Yusufari. The decision to target households and individuals based outside of camp-settings is based on our gap assessments and analysis. To ensure the targeting captures the households most in need, LABDI uses a Food Consumption Score (FCS) survey that is adjusted to the Yobe context. Selection criteria for the unconditional food-assistance package through E-Voucher will focus on food insecurity, using the FCS as the predominant measure to determine levels of food insecurity for vulnerable households. Furthermore, the selection criteria are aligned with the required BHA indicators for food security – i.e. FCS, HHS, and rCSI. In so doing, LABDI uses an approach that negates the need for a stand-alone baseline assessment, as the relevant indicator data is captured for each beneficiary household prior to the commencement of food assistance. This helps to ensure the timeliness of the food security intervention, while also increasing the reliability of the data used for determining the baseline conditions prior to intervention – thereby allowing for a more accurate comparison at the final evaluation stage of the program. The targeting approach also aligns with both Food Security Sector recommendations as produced by the Food Security Working Group in Nigeria, and with the Food Security Sector Sphere standards. Key indicators include: • Households with poor or borderline FCS• Households with moderate or severe Household Hunger Scores (HHS)• Households with a Reducing Coping Strategies Index (rCSI) showing high levels of coping strategy usage• In addition to the above criteria, the following criteria will be used to further narrow down the selection to the most vulnerable households:• Households caring for the sick, elderly, and/or persons living with a disability.• Households with pregnant and lactating women and/or children under five.• Vulnerable female and/or child-headed households.• Households with case of MAM/SAM (such households may be accepted through initial referral from CMAM/OTP centers and will, if not currently receiving treatment, be referred to nearest CMAM/OTP center for further treatment). All food-assistance will be provided through unconditional, restricted value vouchers, using an e-voucher modality.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

LABDI consistently adds a gender lens to its “Do No Harm” analysis, especially in conflict areas like Northeast Nigeria, because applying such a perspective can help teams to identify special opportunities to avoid worsening the conflict and exposing people to further hazards and violence, including sexual or gender-based violence(SGBV) and/or undermining local protection coping mechanisms. Furthermore, LABDI specifically targets vulnerable female-headed households for assistance, as these find themselves in a uniquely vulnerable situation, because of a shortage of alternative income generation opportunities – with the few available typically being occupied by men. This leaves women with fewer, safe potential livelihoods and, therefore, in greater need of food assistance. The Fuel Efficient Stoves distribution will also be targeted at women. In addition, LABDI trains all project staff, vendors and volunteers on Do No Harm principles and PSEA. All staff volunteers and vendors are required to read and sign the Code of Conduct, including LABDI PSEA policy. LABDI will also rely on protection master trainers to increase participant’s awareness of PSEA.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

LABDI has set up a community accountability and response mechanism in place to ensure community members and beneficiaries have access to information and be able to give their feedbacks. At the community level, LABDI uses focus group discussions and key informant interviews to get feedback from the community. Also during giving assistance, complaint desk will be setup at each distribution sites for easy complains by beneficiaries.
