Project Summary

Strengthening routine vaccination activities is still an essential concern to increase the national coverage rate at national and governorate level, regular routine immunization activities is provided in all governorates, the vaccination activities are continued in coordination with all related partners (including UNICEFf and NGOs) to cover the needs of children under 5 and to increase the community immunity especially among all vulnerable groups , WHO has facilitated the delivery of vaccines( by road& Air ) to all governorates in addition to support the operational cost of the filed work including :transportation cost, incentives for mobile teams , operation cost , which will facilitate the field work of the EPI program at national, governorates and district level. Capacity building will be provided to the healthcare workers and managers involved in the reporting, planning, implementation, and monitoring of the immunization activities. MOH is providing vaccination services to all children of Syria (target 2021 is 572,825 children under the age of 1 year) in all 14 governorates. Several tactics are used for service delivery through fixed vaccination sites and mobile teams as well in coordination with the local communities to facilitate the access for the families and service providers; WHO and partners support in the coordination, technical and financial support of the activities including the IPC material procurement and cascade training for service providers and supervisors capacity building for quality services and gain community trust as well.
In northwest Syria, WHO supported re-establishment of EPI services, and has increased the number of active vaccinations centers from five in March 2017 to 93 in 2021. WHO supports the operational costs of 40 of these centers, covering 62 of a total of 134 vaccination teams. This work has been impeded by security issues leading to huge population movements at the beginning of 2020 and loss of accessibility to some areas. The latter issue necessitated the reallocation of 23 EPI centers to new areas to serve displaced populations inside NWS.
Within implementation of this HRP project WHO in cooperation with Syria Immunization group (SIG) and in close collaboration with UNICEF and other NGOs/INGOs actors in the north of Syria is planning to sustain services of the Expanded Program on Immunization EPI in the NWS, including routine re-mapping of communities and new settlements to be able to follow population movements inside the country.
All EPI activities held in Syria is according to the set plans preplanned prior to the beginning of the year. Those plans are valid for all territories under the government of Syria including the Self administrated areas of NES. WHO and UNICEF do have also representation in the 5 hubs to support the MOH further in the implementation and the monitoring of the EPI program. For the implementation of the vaccination plan in northwestern Syria focus will be on: mapping high risk areas with low routine immunization performance and developing special strategies to close gaps; training new vaccinators and providing on-the-job refresher courses for existing vaccination teams; and outreach efforts, especially to nomadic populations, IDPs and hard-to-reach communities.
Monitoring and evaluation on the other-hand, is also supported by WHO and UNICEF, the MOH does conduct regular monthly visits to from the central level and governorate level to the district and health centers based on a preset plan the findings are being shared with the field level workers for immediate adjustments and follow up.
One of the reasons for the increased cost for this project, including the monitoring cost, is the inflation rate and the devaluation of the Syrian pound which had an impact on the program cost in terms of transport for personnel and vaccine across the country. All incentives have been increased based on the official request sent to the UN agencies (since September 2020).

4 - Needs

The conflict in Syria has resulted in disruption of the Immunization, resulting in the occurrence of polio and measles outbreaks. There are several tactics adopted to boost the immunity of the children through Fixed sites and Mobile vaccination teams hence the continuous need for technical support of WHO and partners for training and strengthening supervision for quality activity and reach to HRA. Coordination is an integral component to improve the population engagement for children protection. Sustaining the activity especially under current pandemic need regular and continuous funding.Disruption of routine immunization services was significant in the NES that was not accessible for cross-line operations for vaccine delivery at least for 5 years from 2012 till 2016 and was relaunched as a result of the tedious efforts by the NGOs, SIG, WHO, and UNICEF in March 2017. Currently immunization activities inside NES are implemented through vaccination teams, which administratively are belonging to NGOs implementers, and technically following to Syrian Immunization Group technical committee. Menbej and Kobane are taken care of by the Department of Health in Aleppo. EPI activities are conducted through mobile team approach to villages based on a set plan and on a monthly basis. In Aleppo governorate, and in absence of government, a supervisory team is formed to manage the technical issues of the program in addition to governorate level there are 5 supervisory teams at district level, these supervisory teams manage the technical aspects of EPI in the field under the supervision and support of SIG committee in Gaziantep in Turkey. There is critical need for technical support.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks have increased recently following the eruption of the COVID-19 outbreak and the extra challenges faced by the families to access EPI services in addition to high transport cost and remoteness of some of the health centers. Lack of health personnel is particularly acute in areas affected by recent hostilities and displacement, with 64% of the population living below minimum standards for healthcare workers. Overcrowding and long waiting times are top access barriers reported by patients. Also issues access constraints areas controlled by different parties; WHO coordination/facilitation is clue in these situations beside provide financial support for transport and incentives to enable the EPI workers perform their duties on outreach basis as well as investment in the solar system for the cold chain in Syria. Attacks on health care workers including the vaccination team at risk both inside the health facilities or the during the outreach campaigns. Careful security assessments and coordination with the community leaders could mitigate this risk to certain level. Access to immunization centers for disabled children or their parents and for female-headed households leaving children un-vaccinated. Outreach vaccination teams including female staff to reach families facing access constrains. Discrimination against vulnerable groups or certain geographical areas. This can be addressed by close coordination and information sharing between different hubs to ensure coverage from any side as situation allows.The availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Sudden change of the political control of the area which might lead to access constraints and delay in approvals. WHO hubs will coordinate the geographical response and shift the responsibility as situation evolves to ensure continuity of immunization programs. Lack or miss-leading information from the field with the subsequent low community acceptance and hesitancy in the wake of the ongoing COVOD-19 pandemic as well as the difficulty in population movement. WHO network of local staff in the field will provide accurate and timely information and coordination with local authorities.The continuous devaluation of the Syrian pound is a factor that could reduce the population capacity to access to vaccination sites hence the need to coordinate mobile vaccination activities to remote localities. WHO teams will provide both technical and financial support to facilitate access to life saving vaccines to children. While the cold chain maintenance is crucial and is a risk in many countries due to the lack of electricity and the high cost of fuel to run generators. The solarization, when complete, will suffice the maintenance of quality vaccine till the village level (currently the vaccines are stocked at district level)Sudden massive population movement due to any conflict and substandard living conditions increase the risk of outbreaks. A contingency plan already prepared with HR,Failure of the renewal of UNSC resolution in NWS. WHO functions in the areas of health coordination, HIS, technical support and capacity building are very important and don’t involve cross-border movement and not impacted by UNSC Resolution. WHO has been instrumental in providing support for cross-border operations supplied through Turkey.
