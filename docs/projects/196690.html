Project Summary

In 2023 WSA will respond to the increased GBV Risks facing women and girls in NW Syria by providing prevention, response, and mitigation GBV activities at three static facilities as follows: WGSS in Kafr Takharim, WGSS in Hazano, and WGSS in Kafr Lucin in addition to two widows’ camps in NWS focusing on the most vulnerable people including adolescent girls, elderly women, and PWDs
as well, 3 outreach teams (one team in each location) will conduct GBV activities in the three locations for all groups
The main activities will be provided to 18900 beneficiaries from IDPs and hosts as follows:
-specialized GBV services(case management, specialist PSS, case fund and referrals),
-Preventive activities, such as GBV Awareness sessions and campaign targeting women, men, boys, and girls (GBV-SC toolkit), and information sessions about Covid and Colera
-Empowerment activities: vocational training, Individual Protection Assistance (IPA)in the form of direct cash, Life skills programs for women and girls, providing small grants for 50 women with the goal of achieving economic independence, Young Mothers Club program, Robot activities for adolescent girls
in addition to implement safety audits in the project locations to assess and address risk factors related to protecting women and girls from GBV in camps
as well as capacity-building training for humanitarian actors (workers in other sectors like WASH, Shelter/NFI, and Education) on GBV topics will be conducted to reduce the risks of GBV and refer to specialized services.
Moreover, WSA will also coordinate with women-led organizations in the targeted locations that are implementing GBV projects and will respond closely to their needs, specialty technical GBV capacity building training
CP:
-Provide prevention activities,Parenting skills program for caregivers,save the children or IRC curriculum,Group PSS sessions, and recreational activities
-Register and refer child cases exposed to protection risks after coordination with specialist organizations working in the same locations
The three WGSSs are equipped to receive women and girls with disabilities and all WSA staff received any training on how to deal with the PWDs
After assessing the type of disability and the need and desires of women and girls with disabilities, they are directed to activities appropriate to their situation
and regarding the elderly women, we will target them during the implementation of the activities in proportion to their specific needs, like their age, and health status.
MEAL Staff in HQ are in day-by-day contact and follow-up with the MEAL Assistants in Syria (each project has its own MEAL Assistants). Of course, the MEAL Team will be depending on the project log frame, as the MEAL plan is focusing mainly on indicators measurement, beneficiaries' feedback, and reporting
WSA MEAL Staff will design and adapt monitoring tools according to the second GBV M&E Toolkit.
Regarding the working on GBVIMS
WSA technical teams in three WGSS and HQ had accounts logged into the GBVIMS, and WSA will start raising new GBV cases, starting from the second half of the first month
WSA has two case workers and a GBV field technical supervisor in each WGSS , who are familiar with browsing and using the Internet
Also, There is one computer for each case worker who will enter data into GBVIMS, with a capacity of 64 GB, and the Internet is available in the centers permanently
all technical teams received training on how to use GBVIMS
The income generation service will provide to 4 women according to criteria in both locations to improve the economic situation of vulnerable women and help them enter the labor market and reduce the GBV risk they face them. the women beneficiaries will identify during the activities we implement. WSA will work to build women capacities by providing project management training with the aim of clarifying the concept of project management, and at the end of the training, women got the small grant 500-700$

3 - Needs

According to VOICES FROM SYRIA 2022, the most common form of violence mentioned by women and girls in NWS was IPV and family violence, digital/online violence, restrictions of rights and movements, denial of education - linked to early marriage and child labor, deprivation of inheritance especially widows, sexual exploitation and harassment - especially at work and for rent and early marriage continues to be mentioned. women and girls with disability are most commonly subjected to physical, psychological, and social violence: including IPV, psychological violence by the family, public bullying in school, social stigma, and neglect. in addition, widowed women are at increased risk for Forced re-marriage and forced abandonment of children in some cases.elderly women are subjected to neglect by family members, perceived as an economic burden, and they are exposed to some physical and emotional violence by adult children, in addition to unmet medical needs, distance to services and general vulnerability are mentioned as key deterrents from seeking protection services or reaching distribution pointsAccording to HNO 2023, These forms of violence are systematically normalized and justified through patriarchal gender norms and institutions that maintain gender inequality. Women and girls’ capacities to cope through positive coping mechanisms are constantly reducing. Silence is the most common coping mechanism to deal with GBV.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: 1-Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services, Impact: High, Mitigating: 1. Service mapping includes only those that can provide survivor centred services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral.Protection risk:2- Persons with disabilities, the elderly, and widows have difficulty accessing to WGSSs.Impact: High. mitigation:Provide transportation, and mobile teams will be allocated to reach those who cannot access the WGSSs:Protection risk: 3-Lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access services Impact: High Mitigating measures: Thorough gender analysis prior to the start of programming. Equip all WGSS with child-care services. Provide transportation support for persons with disability. Include persons with disability in all conducted assessments at the level of WGSS and/or community to understand their needs and gaps in their access to services.Protection risk: 4-Some beneficiaries were exposed to PSEA by WSA staffmitigation: raise awareness among the beneficiaries about complaints mechanisms regularly.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on WSA needs assessment conducted within the targeted locations, WSA will priorities the most vulnerable persons to receive services during this project. However, they may face risks of exploitation by service providers or by another actor in the community and WSA will work on mitigation measures through the following: 1. Ensure participants know services were to disclose if violence occurs 2. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and how to report 3. Establish good relations with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles, and stick to them. 4. ensure the compliant and feedback mechanisms are accessible for all. 5. train all staff and contractors on PSEA CoD. Also, the project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups, not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality. Starting with the project's first needs assessments and during the whole project period, WSA will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach to integrate conflict sensitivity wherever the feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them. It will be confirmed that the service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against beneficiaries. Apply the principle of “different needs equal opportunities.”


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: 1) the large spread of Cholera in targeted locations.Risks: 1-Movement restriction to basic services.mitigation: Offering more online sessions to raise more awareness about cholera prevention.Assumption: 2) a High number of GBV cases in said widows' camps.Risks:1-Preventing women, girls, and PWDs from accessing services within the widow's camps.mitigation: Ensuring the provision of services for family members and camp management during the implementation of the project activities and in particular, awareness activities and advocacy campaigns to mitigate risks and reduce the risks of preventing the access of beneficiaries to services, Provide transportation support for them.Assumption: 3) all WGSSs managed by WSA are located in safe areas.Risks :1-Changing the security situation that interferes with the implementation of the project. Mitigating Measure: We will establish a response plan that can be activated according to the situation. Designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the wanted results.
