Project Summary

Orange aims to establish a child-friendly space to provide the affected children with a safe playing space and to use the location as a launching point for structured PSS and early childhood education services and will focus on IDPs out-reach activities through ECDE mobile teachers who will focus on the children in surrounding camps. This project will particularly target 3-6 age group of children who were born into conflict are directly exposed to the effects of the war, which harms children’s’ developmental stage and psychosocial wellbeing.

4 - Needs

By the end of the 10th year of the Syrian crisis, it is estimated that 1 in three schools are totally or partially damaged in Northwest Syria, 2.1 million children are in need of education and protection services and 1 in 8 school children is needs of psychosocial support services. According to Orange Needs Assessment on Early Childhood Development Needs in NW Syria, there is a vital need for safe space for the children to play, develop psychosocial wellbeing, and a concrete early childhood education program. Due to the lack of early childhood education opportunities, children’s psychosocial wellbeing has not been supported, and children’s psychosocial support needs have not been addressed. According to UNICEF’s data in 2019 , Syria has 5 percent pre-primary gross enrollment ratios across countries. With this statistic, Syria is the one of lowest rate among countries about young children who are accessing early childhood education opportunities. Furthermore, the increased internal displacement, covid-19 pandemic, economic downturn, and violence negatively affect young children in their early childhood stage. In these circumstances, especially young children could not find any opportunity to stabilize and increase their wellbeing, improve their social and emotional skills, safe space to play around, and care providers could not prioritize early childhood development and education for their young children.

5 - Implementation of activities

During the ten implementation months, this project will support two ECDE modalities that aim to support and stabilize children’s wellbeing between 3-6 years old. This will be done through:- Establishing an ECDE center offering recreational and non-structured educational activities following the child-channeled intervention methodologies such as early childhood education, social-emotional learning, and non-structured psychosocial support activities in the child-friendly space.- Hiring 3 ECDE Mobile teachers who will be responsible for visiting the random camps and conducting specific activities can improve children’s wellbeing and increase their resilience and develop their different skills. This will ensure access to the most vulnerable IDPs in the areas of implementation. The project will include providing daily healthy food rations, equipping the center with needed materials and items, and implementing light rehabilitation works to ensure children’s safety and accessibility.The project will also focus on the development of educational staff capacities in child safeguarding, protection, and early childhood education, in addition to parenting skills for children’s care providers focusing on child development issues (child’s natural stages, normal problems according to each age stage, child protection policies, child rights).Considering Covid-19 risks and situations in the area, project activities will be arranged for remote implementation (if needed) through online platforms such as Zoom and WhatsApp in close cooperation with partners.

1) Lack of awareness about early childhood education and development, and the risk of low participation among the community including young children and their parents.Likelihood: High, Impact: HighMitigation Measures:• Parents will receive online sessions to raise their awareness and develop parenting skills. • Project staff will conduct the programme in close cooperation with parents. • Early childhood development and education sessions will be 1 hour per day. • Parents and young children will be encouraged to actively participate.Monitoring: Participation will be followed by project staff and MEAL Department through attendance sheets. If absenteeism will be identified, staff will contact parents. 2) Children are exposed to abuse or harassment.Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation Measures: • Complaint mechanisms will in place. • Train the staff on Child Safeguarding Policy. • Train the staff on code of conduct.• The code of conduct will be in the contracts of all the staff, and it is against sexual exploitation and abuse.Required Resources: Staff Monitoring Policy and/or Approach.3) Increased needs of specialized and qualified mental health and psychosocial support services. Likelihood: Low, Impact: LowMitigation Measures:• Following related cluster and sub-cluster meetings.• Conducting regular service mapping activities in the areas and follow service map through system. • Working in close cooperation with other humanitarian actors. • Safeguarding Officer will receive training related to referral services and forms.
