Project Summary

Provide targeted food assistance for Rohingya & enhance food security in host communities through FFA Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) by building or rehabilitation of assets that will create healthier natural environments, reduce the risks & impact of climate shocks, & strengthen resilience to natural disasters over time. Such FFA activities will ultimately address immediate food needs through cash, voucher or food transfers.

Needs

The spontaneous arrival almost 1 million refugees within a very short period of time has placed considerable pressure on an already impoverished local population, exacerbating poor living conditions of the refugees, especially those in in the mega-camp. Due to large-scale clearing of trees and plants for settlement purposes, the entire area is highly susceptible to land degradation and exposed to eminent disasters such as landslides, mudslides and flash floods during the monsoon and cyclone seasons. Livelihood opportunities in the camp is extremely limited therefore a large number of dis-engaged youths are in greater risks of being radicalised or being engaged in illegal activities. Ensuring community-led, sustainable and well-coordinated disaster risk reduction and alternative livelihood interventions is therefore crucial to ensure the resilience of both the refugee communities and the host population.

Activities or outputs

(1) Disaster Risk Reduction - Cash for Work WFP's key activity is to implement disaster mitigation activities and alternative livelihood through an approach known as Food Assistance for Asset (FFA) approach whereby refugees and host communities are engaged in community-based, labour intensive activities in exchange for cash/food. The activities will: a) improve the safety and living conditions of the refugees through community-based public works, community services as well as disaster preparedness; b) engage youth-at-risk by employing them in productive activities hence preventing the potential for engagement in radicalisation, drug abuse and trafficking; and c) reduce and reverse the ongoing environmental degradation through reforestation. The cash/food transfer amount will be in line with Transfers Working Group guidelines. The following are key activities under the FFA-DRR interventions:a) Rehabilitation of ACCESS includes a renovation of bridges, footbridges, roads, footpaths, steps & stairs, handrails, flags and signs etc. b) Risks mitigation through SLOPE STABILISATION, terracing, perimeter fencing, c) Constructing/Rehabilitating DRAINAGE include the construction of dams, cleaning drainage canals etc.d) Community-based environmental protection including promoting reforestation and restoration of the damaged environment, camp clean-up activities etc.e) Capacity Development Programme for government officials and national NGOs on disaster preparedness, e.g. early warning systems. All project activities will integrate gender equality, women’s empowerment and protection principles. These elements will be mainstreamed throughout the FFA/DRR project design, implementation as well as through its monitoring and evaluation. Concretely, WFP will ensure the maximum participation of women in the CFW activities by ensuring an enabling environment for them (e.g arranging babysitting arrangement for women who are engaged in CFW). In addition, WFP and its Cooperating Partners will strive to ensure that the FFA/DRR projects are inclusive and cognisant of the needs of people with disability (for example, by ensuring that the output of DRR projects such as evacuation shelters take into account their particular needs). Innovative Pilot Projects d. Building the capacity of the refugee community linking with self-reliance and DRR, in the construction activities inside the camp.e. Building the capacity of the host community linking with Livelihoods in activities in the activities for casting yard for the pilot projects f. Currently SMEP employs more than 25 percent women in different types of activities. For improving participation of women, we work on a Pilot Project "A Take care children" jointly Gender and School feeding.Objectives:- Increased number of women - Ensuring nutrition security for young children of SMEP Rohingya women- Promoting women’s participation in income generating activities- Preparedness to Emergency Response
