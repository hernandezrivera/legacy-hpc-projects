Project Summary

Syria continues to be one of the most complex protracted conflict humanitarian crises globally, exposed to high levels of ongoing conflict, and prone to natural disasters and epidemic outbreaks. These factors have forced an increase of 1.4M of people in need (PIN) since 2023, totaling 16.7M (HNO, 2024) people in need in Syria. Given the myriad needs and vulnerabilities, Concern proposed this action across Northwest Syria (NWS). Concern has proposed intervention areas in line with the severity of the needs identified by Humanitarian Needs Overview where there continues to be lack of, or challenges for NGOs to provide assistance in Afrin and Azaz Districts. Concern will work with local partners to comprehensively address gaps in the response, ensuring the inclusion of diverse beneficiaries, including girls, children with disabilities, and marginalized communities.
The intervention will involve implementing cash for winterization for the crises affected population in NW Syria. the cash will be distributed during the last two months of the year prior to the coldest winter months and locations be selected in coordination with the SNFI Cluster.
Cash Value: Concern will comply with the SNFI Cluster recommendations currently fixed at $150 and the transfer currency will be primarily USD to preserve the value of the assistance and protect the purchasing power of recipient HHs, as long as 100 USD / 50 USD bills remain available. An alternative would be TRY if local/current context justifies; and provided decision is consulted with the SNFI Cluster, Donor, and other NGOs in the same area.
Distribution point selection: All distribution sites will be agreed upon with the local authorities for security and coordination purposes. The selected distribution point should be meeting the minimum requirement of safety and security including:
• Safe for both staff and Beneficiaries, with a minimum of one entrance and one exit
• Located in a central location from all beneficiaries’ residence
• Accessible for all beneficiaries including PWDs, and should have enough space for two separate lines
• Shaded from weather conditions, equipped with a water resource for drink, and separate latrines for males and females
Beneficiary Selection Criteria: the below criteria serve as a guideline to identify and prioritize households in need of assistance during the winter period:
1. Disability.
2. Chronic Diseases.
3. Pregnant/Lactating Women.
4. Children Attending School.
5. No access to Non-Food Items (NFI) in the last 6 months.
6. Living in a temporary settlement.
7. Household with no source of income.
8. Household headed by a person with a disability.
9. Household hosting orphaned, unaccompanied children or homeless relatives due to the conflict.
10. No access to fuel or other heating resources in the last 1 to 3 months.
11. Households affected by recent floods/last winter floods.
Beneficiary Invitation: each of the selected beneficiaries will receive a coupon from the local council agent specifying the date, time and location of the distribution.
Voucher Distribution: The beneficiaries will collect their vouchers from the distribution table in the distribution point after verifying the beneficiary ID against the approved beneficiary list. The program staff responsible for voucher distribution will stamp the voucher “verified” once the verification is completed and collect beneficiary’s signature on the Voucher Distribution Form, then guide the beneficiary to the redemption table.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Voucher Redemption: when arrived to the redemption table, the programme staff responsible for verification will compare the beneficiary ID with the list and stamped voucher, and ask the beneficiary to sign the Cash Redemption Form. Once the signature is completed, the designated staff will take a photo of the voucher and beneficiary ID to be archived as a Mean of Verification. Then stamp the voucher “Paid” before handing it over to the Financial Services Provider who will exchange the voucher for the determined value ($100 per voucher) and keep the voucher for Financial Reconciliation.Distribution/Redemption Monitoring: The MEAL Field Team will be present in the distribution point to fill the cash distribution/redemption monitoring form. They should focus on the process of the distribution. They provide an opportunity to gather feedback on how distribution proceeded; whether the information provided before a distribution matched what beneficiaries experienced during the process; whether beneficiaries received what was expected; and whether there were any protection concerns.All cash transfers will be conducted through the Turkish Post Office (PTT). A separate FSP will be selected by Concern and Implementing Partners to distribute the cash. The process for selection will follow a procurement process based on the value of the funds being transferred and the transfer fees related to this process. The procurement process will follow standard Concern Worldwide tools and other criteria will also be considered in the selection. The FSP will be subject to legal vetting to ensure they are not a prohibited party.Post Distribution Monitoring: PDMs are crucial to ensuring that beneficiaries receive the full value of cash transfer and are treated fairly. A ten percent sample of beneficiaries will be selected after each distribution round to gather information on the winterization intervention, and the PDM visit will take place within two weeks of the last distribution day. Key topics for the questionnaire include whether there were any problems in the receipt of cash, including in the transfer value, at the distribution site, or in spending the money. Staff will also ask about security or safety issues happening as a result of the program at the community or household level; and any general feedback on program implementation.During all project phases, Concern and Implementing Partners will consider the AAP measures, mainstreaming of protection, and PSEA. The project has a risk analysis and mitigation matrix. Along with all activities, monitoring, and evaluation processes will be carried out. At the end of the project, a meeting will be made for the project staff and key stakeholders to evaluate the project results and register the lessons learned and the best practices that have been followed to achieve project results.
