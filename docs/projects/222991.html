Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan continues to deteriorate since the country gained its independence from Sudan over a decade ago in 2011. The multidimensional crisis in the country is defined by insecurity from unresolved sub-national conflict and communal violence, economic downfall triggered by exchange rate fluctuations and currency depreciation, climatic shocks, volatile political dynamics, and lack of basic services.
This already challenging humanitarian situation is further exacerbated by an economic downfall trigged by exchange rate fluctuations (US dollar/South Sudanese Pound) and a significant rise in food, commodity and fuel prices due to the Sudan conflict. Trade and supply chains have been disrupted, driving up food prices and constraining food access for the returnees and the host communities. A comparison of the cost of the standard food basket before and after the outbreak of the conflict in Sudan found a 28 percent increase in the cost of standard food basket across monitored markets. Additionally, fuel prices continue to be elevated contributing to increased transport costs and food prices.
The IPC results from November 2023 indicate that between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.17 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). Approximately 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. An estimated 72 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. Almost 58% of the counties are classified as IPC Phase 4 (critical) and 19% as IPC 3 (serious).
This is a significant deterioration from last year, with contributing factors of high disease prevalence (55% of children reported to have been ill two weeks prior). Poor access to improved drinking water and sanitation compromises hygiene practices. Only 58.4 % of the population reported having access to improved sources of water leaving close to half the population depending on unsafe sources. Inadequate feeding practices of infants and young children is another high-risk contributing factor to acute malnutrition. Only 5 children out of 100 are getting the recommended quality and frequency of food required for optimal growth. 34.6% achieved the Minimum Meal frequency while only 21% achieved the Minimum dietary diversity.
In response to the dire nutrition needs in South Sudan, WFP's project will facilitate the provision of nutrition prevention and treatment services to populations affected by conflict, displacement, climate change and insecurity. The project will target residents, IDPs, refugees and returnees to treat and prevent malnutrition related morbidity and mortality.
WFP will continue to work in close collaboration with the Ministry of Health, the Nutrition Cluster, sister agencies and with implementing partners to support an emergency nutrition response in two key ways: First, through a preventive supplementary feeding programme targeting children aged 6-24 months (and up to 59 months in IPC 5 locations), pregnant & breastfeeding women, and people living with HIV/TB in highly food insecure locations of South Sudan. Second, WFP will implement a treatment of acute malnutrition programme in 61 out of 80 counties in the country, targeting children under 5 years of age, PBWs, elderly and PLHIV/TB/Kalazar.
In hard-to-reach areas, WFP will conduct direct delivery of life-saving nutrition services through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The project implementation will be coordinated closely with the Nutrition cluster, UNICEF, MOH and use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (health, education, FSL, WASH).

1. Needs

The project aims to address the nutritional needs of vulnerable women and children in populations affected by food and nutrition insecurity resulting from conflict, insecurity, climate change and displacement. The nutrition situation is projected to worsen in 2025, with an estimated, 2.1 million children and over a million pregnant and breastfeeding women being at risk of acute malnutrition. Data from Round 30 of the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) survey reported a deterioration in GAM prevalence, with 50% of counties having a GAM prevalence of over 15% (above the WHO emergency threshold) and as many as 22 counties reported a GAM prevalence above 20%. The dietary diversity has deteriorated compared to 2024, with only 2% of children receiving the Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) compared to 5% last year. Child morbidity remained high, with 50% of children reporting an illness in the 2 weeks preceding the FSNMS data collection, 74% people still practicing open defecation and less than 50% have access to clean drinking water. To address these needs, WFP will provide a comprehensive package of curative and preventative nutrition interventions in collaboration with the government, sister UN agencies and implementing partners and in close consultation with impacted communities. WFP's focus will be on 60 of the highest impacted counties, consistent with 2024 targeting.

2. Project activities

In collaboration with UNICEF and the jointly contracted WFP-UNICEF cooperating partners, WFP will: 1. Provide children aged 6-59 months, PLHIV/TB and PBW screened in communities and identified as moderately malnourished with an appropriate treatment protocol, inclusive of specialized nutritious food (SNF), through the Targeted Supplementary Feeding programme (TSFP) under the CMAM approach. The expanded criteria will be used as strategy in some areas when WFP/UNICEF /Nutrition Cluster jointly agree. 2. Provide preventive ration of specialized nutritious food to children U2 and PBW in counties with GAM rates above 10% through a preventive Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme in the aim of preventing acute malnutrition. The Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme will be implemented in combination with the General Food Distribution in areas of high food insecurity. In areas classified as IPC 5, the BSFP target group will be expanded to children under 5 years of age. 3. Support an outreach network of over 7000 community nutrition volunteers to provide nutrition and health message, outreach services, actively screen and identify those at risk or with acute malnutrition and ensure proper referral to health and nutrition services. 4. Ensure the project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (Health, FSL, Education, WASH, Protection, NFIs). 5. Ensure revised delivery modalities will allow for prepositioning in locations with irregular access year-round, in strategic hubs with the aim of reducing pipeline shortfalls during the rainy season. River deliveries and airlifts are conducted to Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei States to the final distribution point where access is impossible by road to ensure the programmes are not disrupted. 6. Monitor and provide joint supervision through a network of field monitors, programme and nutrition staff to ensure implementing partners activities and stocks are meeting the high quality and reaching the most nutritionally vulnerable individuals. 7. Contribute to enhancing the nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information through supporting the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System. 8. Undertake actions to mitigate the diversion/sale of specialised nutritious foods through working closely with the government (on legislations and criminalization of sale & purchase), partners and communities. WFP-UNICEF will continue work on the transition to the new WHO guidelines on a pilot basis in all of Lakes and Northern Bahr El Ghazal states, building on the groundwork done in 2024. WFP undertakes the FSNMS twice a year, once in the lean season and once post-harvest and collects a range of food security and nutrition indicators to inform the situation in country and thereby programmatic adjustments as needed.
