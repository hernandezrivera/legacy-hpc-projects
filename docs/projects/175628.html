Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to increase availability and accessibility of quality life-saving GBV prevention/response in conflict-affected locations where women and girls have very limited access to survivor-centered GBV multisectoral services and to basic needs such as Shelter, Food, Wash and dignity kits. Additionally, lack of privacy, crowded living conditions, inadequate lighting compound with the COVID 19 restrictions contributed to increased GBV risks for women and girls.
The project aims to establish new and sustain existing WGSSs, offering comprehensive PSS support services including GBV case management and in-kind/CBIs as well as GBV awareness sessions, increase free access to integrated GBV/SRH services (with a focus on CMR services) through mobile brigades/static health facilities are the key priority interventions. The use of existing hotlines and GBV focal points/women networking will be strengthened too.
UNFPA will continue supporting and co-leading the GBV SC coordination, strengthening the GBV Capacity of GBVSC members, services providers (including security personnel), volunteers, social activists, GBV focal points and women led organization. IASC GBV guidelines sensitization/workshops will be also provided to non-GBV actors.
Furthermore, UNFPA commits to procure dignity kits and post-rape kits to cover the needs of the affected areas. Target groups are IDPs/host most vulnerable women/girls including GBV survivors and those left behind unable to leave conflict areas with a focus on female headed households (widows, divorced), adolescent girls, women/girls with disabilities and older women. Men and boys are also included.
It is anticipated that UNFPA may take part in ad-hoc Rapid Responses or declared Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) for which substantial Dignity Kits are planned for blanket distribution to fleeing families (especially single mother head of households, older women, single young mothers, etc.) aiming at mitigating risks to GBV.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNFPA will prioritize the dignity and safety of the target beneficiaries in line with the do-no-harm approach. UNFPA has a long presence with field offices in Pemba, Nampula and Niassa hence is very familiar with the situations of communities and the dynamics of IDPs/host communities. The UNFPA relationship with local authorities and specifically DPGCAS and the Ministry of Health is longstanding. GBV service mapping will be updated on a regular basis to ensure referral pathways are well-understood by target groups. GBV data will be protected and treated with confidentiality as GBV caseworkers will be trained on the GBVIMS and data protection protocols. UNFPA ensures during the project’s life refresher sensitization sessions on PSEA to partners staff and GBV SC members. Women and girls as well as civil society organizations and mainly women-led organizations will be consulted, engaged and their voices listened during all phases of the project cycle. Mitigation measures will be taken to avoid discrimination among beneficiaires. UNFPA with the GBV SC member will conduct a PRA of GBV programming to identify protection risks (including risk of gender-based violence) and sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as strategies to mitigate these risks. UNFPA will make sure to collaborate with protection cluster colleagues to identify and reach the most vulnerable women and girls.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

UNFPA is committed to providing beneficiaries with quality services in line with their needs. To this end activities proposed within this project are based on the interagency needs assessments, regular coordination meeting with actors providing GBV services through the GBV Sub cluster coordination meetings, regular communication with women and girls through the UNFPA IPs feedback mechanisms. UNFPA has a long standing presence in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa through its development and humanitarian programs, having developed strong networks with government-line structures, as well as civil society organizations. UNFPA, through its IPs, continuously seeks beneficiaries inputs through feedback boxes located strategically, for example, in the WGSSs, to validate the project idea and monitor impact and need for adjustments as well as through hotlines number and client exit interviews. Sex and age disaggregated data through data collection, monthly report, analysis, will be used to be able to report on gender equality. Female-friendly feedback mechanisms will be put in place to collect challenges from female beneficiaries, hence re-adapt the assistance accordingly.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Women and girls living with disabilities due to physical, mental, intellectual, vision and hearing impairments are perceived as different, and/or have less power and status therefore they may be more exposed to GBV because of this. Women and girls with disabilities survivors of GBV face barriers to access information and services, therefore UNFPA with its partners will be committed to make WGSSs accessible to them (e.g. physical accessibility in the space), and make adjustments to mobile brigades outreach. Training/sensitizations on disability inclusion in GBV programming will be offered to GBV SC members, GBV service providers, social workers, social activists, GBV focal points. GBV safety audit exercise will include questions related to GBV risks/barriers faced by IDPs women and girls with disabilities in the IDP sites. GBV SC key messages will be integrated with disability and inclusion key messages for the communities. Women and girls living with disabilities will be consulted at all times on how to reach excluded and marginalized groups of women and girls and Dignity kis distribution modalities will be adapted taking into consideration COVID-19 restrictions and women and girls with disabilities to ensure distribution is accessible by them.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Environment will be mainstreamed within every stage of the HRP cycle. UNFPA will strengthen knowledge of IPs staff on environmental related issues. WGSSs and mobile brigades will adhere to waste treatment standards. Environmental awareness components will be integrated into community sensitisation initiatives.
