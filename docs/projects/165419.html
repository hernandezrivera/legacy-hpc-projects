Project Summary

Many interventions were made for multiple types of vulnerabilities but none so far targeted disabled people with an intervention to improve their access to job market taking in consideration the lack of job opportunities inside Syria in general and for disabled people specifically.
Through the proposed intervention SR will give disabled people the opportunity to work in the international market through providing them with professional interactive courses in fields that give them the ability to work online such as design (Photoshop, illustrator), video montage (adobe premiere, adobe after effect) or marketing (SEO, Social Media Marketing) depending on a market analysis which will be conducted on freelancing platforms.
By enabling them to gain skills that they are able to use from the home, they will not be competing for the few remaining job opportunities within the area, and they will be able to overcome barriers to employment
The training design (potential, to be revised upon the online market assessment and beneficiary preference):
Duration: up to 3 Months
Training program: the training will include two daily sessions, morning session will be theoretical and afternoon session will give the chance to the trainees to apply what they learned practically, success stories for people who already working online will be displayed.

4 - Needs

More than eight years of civilian war caused destruction of many facilities, more than 1,2M people in Idleb are in need for early recovery and livelihoods and more than 800 Thousand People are in need for education according to HNO 2019 and the majority of Syrians lost their jobs or the chance to continue their study.In recent years, Idleb is hosting more than 2,5 million displaced people from Raqqa, Deir ez-zor, Aleppo, Homs and Damascus, as well as IDPs from Idlib communities who were displaced due to the continues targeting of their areas. According to the last HNAP “DISABILITY: PREVALENCE AND IMPACT” report, 3.7 million or 27 percent of the total population (aged 12+) are disabled, in idleb thirty percent of households have disabilities while forty four percent of them reported one or more family members with disability, confirming that prevalence and negative impacts of living with a disability are more pervasive in developing and crisis-affected countries. The impact of disabilities are compounded inside Syria where disabled individuals experience increased threats of violence and limited access to essential income generating activities and medical assistance There remain significant employment gaps across the country, however unemployment rates disproportionately affect persons with disabilities. Regardless of difficulty type, the majority (62 percent) are out of the formal labour market.16 percent of children between the ages of 12 and 17 have a disability. Of those with a disability, 56 percent are not attending formal education.

5 - Implementation of activities

Through the proposed intervention SR will give disabled people the opportunity to work in the international market through providing them with professional interactive courses in fields that give them the ability to work online such as design (Photoshop, illustrator), video montage (adobe premiere, adobe after effect) or marketing (SEO, Social Media Marketing) depending on a market analysis which will be conducted on freelancing platforms. By enabling them to gain skills that they are able to use from the home, they will not be competing for the few remaining job opportunities within the area, and they will be able to overcome barriers to employment. SR will rent a ground floor house and conduct preparation and rehabilitation to make it disabled friendly space and equip it with the needed IT equipment for the training purposes.The training design (potential, to be revised upon the online market assessment and beneficiary preference): Duration: up to 3 Months Training program: the training will include two daily sessions, morning session will be theoretical and afternoon session will give the chance to the trainees to apply what they learned practically, success stories for people who already working online will be displayed.Content:1. Online working: how it works and different types of online working (17 Hours)2. Fundamentals of Graphic Design (17 Hours)3. Learn to see: (70 Hours)• Learn how to draw • Learn about color, typography, and designing with a grid 4. Learn how to use Illustrator (70 Hours)5. Guiding the trainees through most popular online working websites and Building their portfolio (35 Hours):• Design simple ideas for t-shirts.• Design simple Logos.• Design personal cards ideas• Design Advertising Posters and flyers • Design simple ideas for Visual Identities.6. success stories for people who already working online, this success stories will be displayed two times a month to motivate the trainees

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Syria Relief staff will participate in protection working groups, and child protection and GBV sub-clusters to take lessons learned for mainstreaming of protection in all Food Security projects. In order to reduce risks of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence, considerations will be made with respect to the distribution locations in order to make sure that there is no sexual exploitation occurs through the implementation process and this issue is going to be managed and monitored through the complaint mechanism and roles of the SR MEAL staff that will always be available in the targeted locations. Moreover, SR team will be coordinating closely with partners in order to develop a special tool for monitoring the issue related to this topic. SR will focus on protection of beneficiaries especially women, and children from sexual abuse and harassment in schools, camps, and communities. In this regard monitoring and reporting tools will be developed and implemented and during the project cycle. SR will focus on enhanced participation of women in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities. All proposed activities are in full alignment (UNSCR) 1325. They have also been designed to be culturally appropriate, inclusive, and confidential. In this project, Syria Relief will ensure the safe access to services for all genders and age groups at all times. This measure will protect beneficiaries. Likewise, by installing complaint mechanisms in the targeted locations, which will be checked by Syria Relief staff during the time of the project, we will allow any protection issues related to this project to be known and seek immediate advice from the senior project staff in Turkey for a response.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Closure of Bab Al Hawa crossing border resulting zero or limited access for staff or goods inside Idleb - Lack of access to targeted areas for the team inside Idleb due to Security situation - Limited capacity for staff to transfer funds to Idleb due to Turkish regulations - Turnover of staff - Delays in the procurement process as a result of prices fluctuation - landowners refusal for signing the MOUs for rehabilitation of WaSH infrastructure due to frictions with local councils. - Delays in project implementation due to the Turkish restriction on transporting goods inside Idleb, Hama, and Aleppo - Delays in project implementation due to the interference of the armed groups in procurement/ imposing taxes /selection of the project locations. - Overlapping of project activities and double agreement by local councils as a result of a lack of coordination. - Syrian regime offensive onto Idleb governorate (airstrikes, a ground offensive, chemical attacks…) - Violent armed conflict between opposition armed groups in Idleb. - Turkey takes control of Idlib governorate. - loss of the neutrality of the organization. - Being associated with any particular conflicting group(s) (much in line with the aforementioned, loss of neutrality) - Fuel sources directly contributing towards that of the war economy - violation of humanitarian principles or code of conduct by staff/ organization loss of confidential data (Data security) - noncompliance with donor requirements in line with a signed agreement - corruption, mismanagement of funds and fraud Assumptions and Mitigating measures: -Constant Security monitoring and analysis of Security Risk Environment and Political Developments which impact on Access - Cross border SOP’s in place. - Registration in Syria with the local authorities. - Compliance with Turkish law, - Full compliance with the Turkish authorities’ regulations for NGOs. - Constant monitoring of Turkeys political and regulative developments which may affect NGOs.
