Project Summary

The 2019 AVSI's interventions in the area of Ikwoto, Torit and Magwi, for Easter Equatoria and Cueibet and Rumbek, for Lakes will utilize an integrated approach to address the food security needs of the population. The overall objective of the interventions will be to reduce the food insecurity of the selected areas in a sustainable way, focusing on the implementation of activities that will provide benefits to the population both in the short and long term and that will bear results also after the end of the projects. To achieve this goal the activities will target both the local institutions (with a strong component of capacity building and awareness) and the local communities (with direct support, capacity building and access to improved agricultural inputs and services).
At the institutional level (County and local authorities) the interventions will focus on strengthening/creating the monitoring system in the fields of livestock (disease early detection and outbreak prevention) and agriculture (establishment of a crop monitoring system), in order for them to became the focal point for farmers and animal keeper, and on natural resources management, trough the formation and empowerment of NRM committee.
At community level there will be two main groups of beneficiaries, IDPs/returnees and local communities. For the first one the activities will focus on their integration/interaction with the local communities through agriculture (cash for work in local communities farms) and in their enhanced access to quality and improved agricultural inputs (seeds, tool, drip irrigation kits) and general services (cash for NFI, housing and school fees).
For the second one the intervention will cover a wide range of activities, starting from the capacity building in agriculture using the Farmers Field School (FFS) approach, the formation and professionalization of community animals health workers (CAHW), the differentiation of the livelihood activities (fish farming, alternative livelihood, vegetable production, agro-forestry and fruit trees), distribution of improved agricultural inputs. Crosscutting interventions will be implemented in the field of hygiene, sanitation and health in order to create a healthy environment from which all the other interventions will benefit.
Finally, in Cueibet support will be provided to already established farmer groups who will be supported with advanced services (agronomy experts who will involve also county extension services) and trial for seed improvement will be established too. This intervention is part of a long-term EU-funded project which aims at shoring up the production of market-oriented farmers and improving nutrition in Cueibet and Rumbek Centre.

Needs

The food security and nutrition conditions of Ikwoto, Torit and Magwi, in Easter Equatoria, Cueibet and Rumbek, in lakes, continue to be worrisome and, after a slight improvement in conjunction with the peak of the harvesting season, they are expected to continue to be so in 2019. Based on the most recent data (FSNMS round 22) the UN-led IPC analysis estimated for October-December 2018 the food security condition of these counties to be in Crises (IPC Phase 3), with the exception of Torit (IPC Phase 2). While Magwi and Ikwoto are expected to benefit from the harvest, and reach an IPC Phase 2 in the period March-April, with the new lean season, starting from May 2018 the food security conditions will presumably return to a Crises scenario throughout these three counties of EES. The IPC has not foreseen a similar temporary improvement in Lakes State due to the limited rains of 2018, which have severely affected this central part of the country. Preliminary data presented at the 2018 September IPC reveal that, in fact, a severe state of food depravation is affecting Lakes, where the average number of meals was 1.18 in Cueibet and 1.55 in Rumbek Centre. By comparison, in the three counties of EES the same indicator show that the average number of meals is 1.99 in Ikwoto, Torit and Magwi. Similarly, while in the 3 counties of EES farmers expect the 2018 harvest to provide food for 4.8 months on average, 2.4 months is what the farmers of Cueibet and Rumbek Central expect to last their harvest. However, when it comes to overall food security, which consider the diet variety along with quantities of food consumed, a somehow different picture emerges. On one hand the families of Ikwoto and Cueibet county consume very little nutritious food, whereby less than 4.6% of HHs report consuming pulses, and less than 5.6% consumes Meat proteins (among the most protein constrained diet in the country); on the other hand a significant share of families in Magwi and Torit consume pulses (above 35.8% does so) and proteins (above 11.0%); Rumbek is somehow in a mid-situation for there is an above-average share of families who consume meat (15.9%), but a below than average share who consume pulses (3.7%). The same preliminary presented at the 2018 September IPC show that the size of land cultivated is rather small in Torit and Ikwoto, where at least two-third of farmers cultivate less than one feddan of land (well below the national avarage), and relatively large in Rumbek Centre and Magwi, where around two-third of farmers cultivate more than one feddan of land (above average); Cueibet lays in an intermediate position. It is important to consider, however, that Cueibet is the only county where the husbandry of livestock represents a noticeable activity, whereby 13.9% of HHs rely on it for their living; livestock is marginal and geographically circumscribed in Ikwoto, county where 5.6% of families rely on livestock for their living, and negligible in the other counties, where less than 2% of HHs depend on it. Other structural aspects that distinguish these five counties is the different degree of access to market, quite constrained in Ikwoto and Cueibet; and relatively good in Magwi, Torit and Rumbek. It is worth noticing that, as indicated by the lasts FAO/WFP Crop Assessment, Rumbek and Torit register a deficit in cereal production, an indication of the viability/remunerativity of a market-oriented production in these two counties and neighbouring communities. With regard to the most recent trends and shocks shown by the FSNMS round 22, Magwi was severely affected by the fall army worm in its latest harvest (82% of farmers reported it), and so was Torit, albeit less strongly (55% reported it). In Cueibet 70% of livestock owners lamented a reduction in the number of animals, In Ikwoto only 26% lamented a decrease in their livestock activity, mostly as a result of animal diseases.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yearly AVSI conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, the county and subcounty (payam) officials and traditional leaders (boma and sub-boma chiefs). During these events AVSI explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. Last September, for instance, in Ikwoto, along with the results of the 2018 Greater Ikwoto Multi Sector Household Survey, the need to move toward more development-oriented interventions and thus to apply more demanding criteria for the selection of beneficiaries was discussed with the communities. In that occasion AVSI received very positive feedbacks from the community as well as suggestions regarding operative mechanisms to employ for the implementation of projects.AVSIs extension officers, before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiary, establish a case specific dialog where AVSIs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in order to evaluate possible challenges and /or intervention failure chances.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As we work with vulnerable farmers, the messages, training and guidance provided by AVSI personnel are tailored to the needs and capacities of people with almost no schooling experience and provided in the local language. Most of the field work realized by AVSI is facilitated by the employment of local personnel who speak the local language and have the notions and the experience needed to contextualize guidance and messages.On the other hand, as AVSI approach educated people, the engagement process is brought to another level and methodologies and thematic are adjusted to the education level.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The numerous permanent bases that AVSI has throughout the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes for unanimous reporting of abuses or complains. Moreover, prevention of GBV (sexual exploitation) is persued by displaying SEA code of conducts at services access points (schools, health facilities and community).

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Besides the continuous dialogue with state, county and subcounty officials and traditional leaders, the interventions here proposed were crafted on the base of solid multi-sector household survey conducted in Ikwoto and Cueibet (Torit is ongoing), following a two-stage sampling of approximately 400 caretakers whom answered around 400 questions. The main findings of the surveys, and their implications in term of activities recommended, were discussed and validated through community meetings attended by RRC representatives, subcounty authorities (payam administrators) traditional leaders (boma and sub-boma chiefs) and open to the population at large.AVSIs extension officers also collect feedbacks and complaints from the beneficiaries on a regular base during field monitoring visits in order to adjust the approach methodologies.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Besides the annual surveys, AVSI has a tested system of Detailed Post-Distribution Monitoring which is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing, transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at the community at large. During each field visit AVSI personnel is required to fill apposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks and complaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes for unanimous reporting of abuses or complains.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Every other month AVSI conducts community campaigns that allow for open discussion over the quality and progress of the activity conducted. Several actions, for instance Field Farming Schools, have built-in systems of community monitoring. Monthly AVSI reports to the FSL State cluster the result of its activities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on GBV, hazards, protection, discapacities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 5 of such surveys which, along with other researches aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Besides the above-mentioned survey, for its largest projects AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analysed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention. For the subject who are found out to lag behind a follow up interview of Key informants, field personnel is conducted to review precisely the barrier that impede a more equitable targeting of the intervention. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers. This has led, to cite some examples, at timing the interventions of FSL more in line with the seasonality followed by farmers, and focusing educational support to nurseries and lower primary classes.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

AVSI’s Foundation shall ensure that the assistance during this project does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the women, men, girls and boys receiving it, and is provided in ways that respect their rights. Equal participation will be ensured through the involvement of women and men equally in decision-making right up from induction meetings, and routine focus group discussions. Likewise, it will be made sure that trainings will target at least an equal number of female and male, that the monitoring will includes sex- and age-disaggregated data, that the project personnel is trained on gender/ protection topics including GBV identification, handing and referral. Moreover, AVSI’s projects include activities of livelihood and capacity building especially tailored to women, individually and as women groups, and is matched by educational projects that always include GBV and peace building sensitization for pupils and training on GBV and referral path for teachers and PTA members. Finally, AVSI operates through numerous permanent bases distributed across the targeted communities and serves an adequate number of service sites equally distributed throughout the counties as a means to avoid crowding of beneficiaries per sites, reduced distance to access services, and thus diminish the gap for remote, inaccessible communities.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews for its projects that include questions about the safety of beneficiaries. For instance, post-distribution monitoring asks beneficiaries about any hazard met on the way to reach the distribution sites. Moreover, annually AVSI conducts multi-sector surveys which include numerous questions aimed at assessing the safety, perceived safety, incidence of hazards, and conflicts and disputes over natural resources.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

AVSI has among its core objectives to defend and enhance the dignity of the people that it serves. AVSI surveys include questions such as “Do you think you can bring a special and unique help to your family and community”, which are aimed at monitoring the level of self-esteem and capacity to control one owns life of the beneficiaries of AVSI support, for that is the ultimate objective pursued by AVSI.
