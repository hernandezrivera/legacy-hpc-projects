Project Summary

In 2022, IRC will ensure bringing back the already enrolled children in learning facilities after re-opening, identify drop out children, create access opportunities for new children who became eligible for schooling and other drop outs, identify learning loss and mitigation plan, include technology based innovations tested during COVID – 19 closure in the learning process and ensure quality learning for the children.
The project designed to reach 5775 Children (Female – 2912, Male - 2863) and Parents/caregivers - 10175 from both Rohingya (Children – 4275, Parents - 6675) and host (Children – 1500, Parents - 3500) community through existing interventions in camps (2E, 11, 15 and 22) and host community (Ukhiya and Teknaf). The activities will align with education sector’s 3 Objectives: Access; Quality; Community Engagement and System Strengthening and will reach its goal through quality teacher professional development, children assessment, innovative technology based intervention and improving access opportunity through existing and newly established Home based Learning (HBL) facility to address the scarcity of available space in camps.
Apart from the regular LCFA/GIEP focused teaching learning activities, IRC will create technology based (Learning apps, IVR messaging, WhatsApp) learning opportunities for children and teachers to support enrollment and retention in the MCP program and ICT-enabled learning through leadership of the Cluster Task Team. ECD program will specifically focus on parents/caregivers, ensuring support to serve 0-3 year’s old (primary) and 3 – 8 years old (secondary) children as target to be benefited by the intervention.
IRC will conduct participatory needs assessment (baseline and end line) for identifying relevant age group, specific target group (Girls, person with disability) and educational activities as well as implementation research and Randomize Control Trail for evidence generation, cost effectiveness and scalability of models. Specific focus will be given to identifying children with disability, their access support with alternative ways, assistive device and ramps where possible to ensure continuation of education. Autonomous Learning program will support the children with limited mobility (and girls) as it address travel issues in the camp.
The project will increase capacity of LCMC member/caregivers through community outreach, awareness session on conflict sensitivity, local conflict drivers and social cohesions, equity and gender mainstreaming and inclusion of children with special needs with overall impact on children education. Considering the experience of COVID – 19 restrictions, IRC will include distance learning support for facilitators, children and caregivers, developed around specific vulnerabilities faced by children, such as inclusion of children with special needs and inclusion of females in learning activities, to ensure quality education at beneficiary level.
The project will establish client feedback and feedback mechanism (CFRM) (i.e client satisfaction survey, feedback box) in different locations, do quarterly accountability monitoring and beneficiary level data collection through dashboard to ensure IRC’s global approach of Accountability. Integration with IRC’s Protection Department for specialized referrals for GVB and case management will be done.
Following the instructions of RRRC and CiC office, IRC will implement all activities in the camps aligning sector’s standard and guidelines and share all required data agreed with sector. IRC will follow risk mapping and sector approved design while establishing learning facility, develop emergency preparedness plan including communication tree, will train facilitators to be deployed on emergency and will orient relevant stakeholders on DRR. The specific contingency budget will be used to support emergency renovation of facility, procuring safety materials for facilitators and if needed for the children.

1 - Identified Needs

Since August 2017, an estimated 745,000 Rohingya refugees fled to Cox’s Bazar, increasing the total number of Rohingya refugees to more than 860,000 (ISCG situation report, June 2020). As different organization started to move their response strategy from initial response phase to sustained response, necessity of information on needs and vulnerabilities of the population increased to design and implement programs. According to the different need assessment in 2019, only 76% of assessed facilities offered services for the 3-5 age group, compared to the 99% of assessed facilities providing services for children aged 6-14. (REACH/ENA 2019). The Multi-sector Need Assessment 2019 stated that, 69 % of the households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not attending any learning opportunities. Pre-dominated socio-cultural barriers (23%), marriage (36%) and domestic support (20%) are the main factors preventing children and youth to fully access education opportunities (J-MSNA, 2019).Due to COVID 19, the needs of affected population has increased to a whole new level. According to the MSNA 2020 fact sheet for Rohingya community, “86% of children previously in education reportedly continued studying remotely as learning centers were closed. However, 14% of households with children previously in education reported planning not to send all children back to learning centers, in particular households with high dependency ratios.” While addressing the challenges, various issues came up like – insufficient learning materials support, having low level of guidance from teachers, household engagement and having no one in household to support in education. Among the issues, lack of guidance from teachers and no one having to support in household have been reported as nearly 44% (MSNA 2020 fact sheet, Rohingya response).In host community, according to the MSNA 2020 fact sheet, “One quarter of households reported that the COVID-19 outbreak disrupted education. At the same time, 31% of households reported that school-aged children were not attending any formal learning before the COVID-19 outbreak.”To ensure the educational support to both Rohingya and Host community, Education Sector regularly conduct meetings with DPEO and ADPEOs. This is also an opportunity where education sector get some idea of the need identified by DPEO and ADPEOs. Based on the regular official meeting on November 2020 with Education Sector, DPEO and ADPEOs have also focused some areas where educational need still exist. The major focus was to provide technology based educational support to the children as well as extend support to the parents to support their children with education at home. From the findings of another report (The Gendered Impact of COVID 19, October 2020), school closer is the most significant impact on children for their education. This has been reported by both women and men in Rohingya and host community and the % is over 80% in all cases. In addition, from the MSNA 2020, half of HH’s reported rarely or never being consulted about their needs and preferences with humanitarian aid. HH’s not speaking English or Bangla report being even less likely to be consulted. This highlights the need for enhanced community voice and participation in our programs. This is also a priority area for the JRP review-Feedback and Participation.The impact of COVID – 19 for last one and half year caused millions of children unable to access educational institutions with limited opportunity of alternative supports through online or household level. The situation was same for the Rohingya and surrounding host community which has changed with the re-opening of educational institutions in September 2021. The Education sector’s Learning center re-open report in October 2021 shows that only half (51%) of the Learning facilities re-opened so far in Rohingya camps where only 64,237 children are attending the educational activities so far.
