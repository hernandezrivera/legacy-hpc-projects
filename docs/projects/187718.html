Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address the basic needs of most vulnerable communities through the rehabilitation of key infrastructure, provision of shelter rehabilitation grants and distribution of tents. The cash modality (shelter grants) will be used in order to ensure a quick and flexible response to immediate needs of IDPs and vulnerable host community members. DCA will prioritise beneficiary/ households who are recently displaced, caregivers for households with disability family members, single parent headed households and children/ households without livelihoods. DCA will coordinate with local leaders, community leaders, disable people organisations, local authorities , respective WGs and HAO offices for shelter/ classroom rehabilitation and tent distribution. For tent distribution DCA aims to reach 6000 beneficiaries in Menbij and Ar-Raqqa incoordination with mentioned authorities. For shelter rehabilitation DCA aims to target, 19,185 beneficiaries in Deir-ez Zor, Ar-Raqqa and Menbij respectively. Moreover, DCA will consider with persons with disability households for their shelter rehabilitation work. Lastly, DCA will rehabilitate key infrastructure in Menbij, Ar- Raqqa and Deir-ez Zor. For all these activities to be implemented, DCA will be targeting IDPs, returnees and host communities in mentioned governorates.

4 - Needs

Humanitarian situation is incredibly complex and constantly evolving due to 10 years of civil war. These has caused unprecedented devastation of shelters and critical infrastructures, 40% in Raqqa; 41% in Deir ez-Zor and 14% in Aleppo as per HNO 2021. In NES, majority (over 400,000 IDPs) live in informal settlements, leaving them without consistent access to essential services as mentioned in HNO 2021. The above mentioned locations are prioritised by DCA to conduct Shelter rehabilitation operation. This situation exacerbates access and safety concerns for those with mobility challenges or disability (25% of people in Syria report having a disability of some kind). Ar-Raqqa remains in dire need of reconstruction and rehabilitation, according to the NES Shelter working group(WG). Shelter conditions also have serious protection implications with risks of increases in mental stress, domestic violence and general feeling of living an undignified life. According to REACH, Ar-Raqqa has a total of 4,362 (32%) damaged or destroyed structures and 1,948 (15%) demolished structures. Substantial damage has also been reported to key infrastructures, such as water stations, sewage networks, healthcare facilities and clinics, and schools.

5 - Implementation of activities

To address the significant lack of safe and sustainable shelter options in NES, DCA will establish safe and accessible shelters for households impacted by conflict. Shelter rehabilitation activity will take place in Deir-ez Zor (6315 beneficiaries), Ar-Raqqa (8520 beneficiaries) and Menbij (4350 beneficiaries) respectively. To execute DCA will do following activities: Community Liaison, identifying most impacted communities; Survey and Clearance of Community Homes (coupled with EORE);HHs Assessments and Repair Plan development; Cash / Contractor support to execution of repair plans; Technical Follow up. HHs to be supported will be selected based on the severity of the damage, vulnerability (single parent or child/ female headed HHs, person with disability, recently displaced), with no livelihood source, lack of liveable /sealed space (in line with Sphere Standards), access and functional issues with WASH and kitchen facilities, lack of safety features (doors, locks, windows). For tent distribution, HHs who are recently displaced will be prioritised. DCA Shelter teams will do technical follow up to the HHs who received tents.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Increase risk of intercommunity tension due to diverse assistance packages across membersLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation Measure:• Agree to minimum assistance package across sector• Assess and validate any changes before they occur• Identify or mitigate or exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions• DCA will establish and facilitate complaint response mechanism to mitigate grievances in the community Protection Risk: Housing land and property issues (Eviction of tenants after completion of works, confusion between owner and occupant)Likelihood: MediumImpact: MediumMitigation Measures: DCA will coordinate with local authorities and local leaders for shelter rehabilitation interventions who will oversee verifying the ownership of the shelters where rehabilitation works is carried out. Through the signature of an MoU (between the project, -local authority, occupant and shelter owner- if applicable, the responsibilities of all parties will be agreed upon before any rehabilitation work commences. The MoU notes that should the occupant of the shelter be a tenant, the owner commits to allowing the tenant to remain rent free for at least 3 months and in the shelter for at least 12 months with no increase in the rent.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members.Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of Do No Harm across its project activities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threated the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place.Risk: Local authority- imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent DCA project staff from moving and/ or working, and/ or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/ KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries. Assumptions: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff) and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money.Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority- imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent is not able to transfer funds.
