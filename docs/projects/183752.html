Project Summary

The proposed project is designed to respond to the most essential need for child
protection responses children with and without disabilities. Based on Atfaluna needs
assessment it becomes clear that children with and without disabilities are of need child
protection activities. They need to enjoy a safe environment within their homes, schools,
and the whole surroundings. The project tackles the problems faced by the children that
threatens their protection and mental-wellbeing. Children protection and psychosocial
support is of significance to mitigate the effects of child violations in family surrounding
and schools. The project will provide equal access to children with and without disabilities
through distributed service points across the Gaza Strip. Medical care will be provided to
children whose families cannot afford the cost of treatment. Children who were exposed
to severe harmful act will receive legal counselling and representation. Children will learn
to have a voice and express and report child protection issue that they encounter. Parents
will be targeted to help them cope with life burdens and turn improve their parenting skills
which will create a safe environment for children at their homes. Service providers,
teachers, and community members will be sensitized on child protection issues such as
child labor, schools’ dropout, and abuse to that they integrate the child protection
mechanisms to mitigate the risks that children face in the community.
The project will be implemented in partnership with five childhood centres across the
Gaza Strip.
Due to current COVID19 situation, the protection and psychosocial support will be
implemented in remote and blended modalities that involves including technological
solutions to make sure that most children have access to protection interventions.
Families who do not have internet access will be reached through the field team taking all
needed precautionary measures. The 2021 May aggression has caused children with and without disabilities to suffer from severe post-traumatic disorders which makes it is important to have structured psycho-socials support program for children with and without disabilities.

1 - Needs

Exposure to violence in the short and long term negatively impacts on the mental, emotional, behavioral, social and physical well-being of children and unless they receive counselling these effects can become worse over time. During 2021, child protection needs in the oPt have been compounded by the continuing COVID-19 pandemic, as well as an increase in conflict-related violence in the West Bank including East Jerusalem and the May 2021 escalation in Gaza. The Child Protection HNOs states that there are considerable needs for MHPSS interventions including specialized case management, and approaches targeting adolescents and caregivers, particularly males. The Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR) estimates that some 678,000 children (345,800 boys ad 332,200 girls) across the oPt are in need of child protection and MHPSS services6, including 122,0007 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. More than half (53%) of all children in Gaza are in need, and 12% of all children in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Additionally, 185,000 caregivers overall, including 137,000 caregivers in Gaza, are also in need of various MHPSS services. The CP indicators in the MSNA analysis identifies child protection issues, as it reports that 7% of households report at least one person under the age of 18 working, of which 5% are in the Gaza Strip. The MSNA indicate the following alarming issues in the Gaza Strip: 11% of girls and 6% of boys suffer from verbal harassment, sexual harassment among girls is 5% and among boys is 3%, 5% of girls and 4% of boys reported to be robbed, and 30% of girls and 3 % of boys were reported to be kidnapped, and 3% of girls and 4% reported to be under threat of violence. In May 2021 following the escalation, ASDC conducted: 97.5% of the children with and without disabilities boys and girls reported psychosocial trauma among children as their mothers expressed that their children experience nightmares, sleeping disorders, behavioral changes, lack of concentration, all in severe posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) symptoms among mostly children and women. 81.6% of the children with and without disabilities boys and girls reported being anxious even after the end the aggression, and 77.4 % reported changes in the children behavior due to the escalation out of them 50.7% become feeling terrified when hearing loud sounds such as doors closures, 48% become aggressive, 19.5% bed wetting, 10.4% crying without any reason, 29.9% lack of concentration. 53.8 % of the families reported that they are not aware of how to calm down their children during the aggression and the heavy airstrikes, 45.6% of families report that they didn’t use the efficient techniques to calm down their children like drawing, play with the children, talking to them, etc. and 78.9% of the parents reported that they need more awareness on how to deal with the children, and techniques to reduce children fears during conflict times. 58.3% of the respondents reported that the family violence increased against women and girls during the aggression. 53.2% of the assessment respondent reported that the family violence increases against the children with disabilities during the aggression. In conducted by Atfaluna in March, 2020 targeting 63 of parents, and 312 children (144 males, 168 female) with the ages 6-17 years old, out of them 87 children with disability from all areas in the Gaza Strip the following was reported from the interviewed key informants: 52% of the parents reported that they use violence with their children on their daily interactions with them, while 55% of the children (61% m, 39% f) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that they are subjected to the violence. The violence is increased against with the children with the disability to reach 64%. Neglect was one of the major things reported by children and their parents as in 32% of the parents reported that they have poor communication with their children, while 22% of the children (50% m, 50% f) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that their parents do not consider their complaints, the neglect increased to 28% among the children with disabilities. About 19 % of the children (64% m, 36% f) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that they didn’t receive the medical care when they are in need for the medical services, and about 36% of interviewed CWDs reported that they do not have access to medical intervention. While 5% of the interviewed children are forced to work to earn money for their families. About 27% of the children (60% m, 40% f) subjected to bullying at schools, among them 48% CWDs. 29% of the children (55% m, 45% f) reported that the school are not safe for them where their classmate brings sharp tools at the schools. 37% of the parents reported that the teachers are aggressive, while 40% of the children with and without disabilities (56% m, 44% f) with the ages 6-17 years old reported that the subjected to the violence from the teachers at the schools. 65% of the parents reported that there are not adapted safe spaces for their children, among them 52% of the children with and without disabilities (56% m, 44% f) with the ages 6-17 years. 61% of the parents reported that they have the fears to subject to death or injury due to military escalation, and 69% of the children with and without disabilities (45% m, 54% f) with the ages 6-17 years, while 60% of the children with disability reported their fear and anxiety of any escalations. And about 41% of the parents reported that their children didn’t have access to psychosocial interventions. And 26% of the children (42% m, 58% f) reported lack of school counseling support, this percentage increased to 40% when interviewing children with disability of the CWDs. Based on findings of CP AoR and ASDC’s assessment the psychosocial support interventions are of high priority to be provided to children.

2 - Activities

Result1.) Children with and without disabilities at risk of violations and psychosocial distress resilience is enhanced Activities: • Establishment of an inclusive online psychosocial support activities and materials for children with and without disabilities the materials shall be developed in inclusive and accessible formats that meet the different needs of disabilities such as visual, hearing, and learning difficulties. The materials will introduce to the children and their families PSEA guidelines and complaints mechanisms in an inclusive manners. • Structured psychosocial support group and individual activities to children with and without disabilities 3,000 (1500 girls, 1,500 boys) who are at-risk and affected by violations. (A total of 1,000 children will be targeted per each center, pre and post evaluations will be conducted on children to determine their needs and impact of the intervention, the group PSS will evolve 12 session about, 8-12 children per each group). • Provision of child protection case management services to 500 children (250 girls, 250 boys) who were exposed to different types of abuse • A total 30 interactive sensitization sessions for 3,000 (1,500 boys, 1,500 girls) children on how to be self-protective • A total of 30 life skills sessions for about 3000 children (1,500 boys, 1,500 girls).All interventions will be provided in inclusive and accessible formats, as ASDC has adapted the MHPSS guidelines to respond to the different needs of children with and without disabilities. • Provision of emergency medical services to the most needed children • Use child protection referral pathways and Legal Task Force to ensure children with and without disabilities, boys and girls, have timely and confidential access to protection services • COVID19 precautionary measures awareness raising messages directed to children and their families in inclusive and accessible formats • A total of 30 parenting skills awareness workshops for about 3,000 mothers and fathers • Provision of PSS kits and hygienic kits to children and their families Result2.) Targeted community will acquire increased understanding and sensitization of child protection and issues of abuse • Documentation and monitoring of child violations through quarterly fact sheets • A total of 20 awareness workshops to 400 service providers (100 females, 100 males) on inclusive approach in serving children with disabilities • A total of 10 auditory sessions with 50 social actors, Mokhtars, parents, media, CSOs, and DPOs with participation of Children’s Councils • A total of 30 community outreach workshops on child protection issues for 1,500 community members • Printing of awareness materials on child protection issues • Training of 100 females and males pss workers, case managers, social workers on integarting PSEA in their pss work • Sensitization sessions for 3,000 parents, females and males on PSEA and safeguarding of children with and without disabilities. • Introduce PSEA complaints pathway and the hotline to children and parents through delivery of the interventions and producing of materials that are accessible to persons with disabilities. ASDC facilities and partners facilities are adapted to receive persons with disabilities. The adaptation works for the partners were carried through ASDC funded programs.
