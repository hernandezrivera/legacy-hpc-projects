Project Summary

The ongoing crisis in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon has displaced over 74,607 households, with children making up a significant portion of the affected population. The crisis has severely impacted children’s mental health and well-being, with over 19,000 children separated from their caregivers due to displacement (MSNA, 2024). Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) rates remain alarmingly high, reaching 30.3% in the North-West and 27% in the South-West. These children, along with adolescent girls, have been exposed to traumatic experiences, separation from caregivers, and gender-based violence (GBV).
This project aims to provide critical mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), case management services, and legal identity documentation through a holistic approach that empowers families, strengthens community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPMs), and builds the capacity of frontline child protection staff. The project will engage adolescents, families, and communities through safe spaces, recreational activities, positive parenting interventions, family business support, and dignity kit distribution to address the deep psychological, social, and protection needs of vulnerable children, particularly girls.
The project therefore aim to:
1. Provide MHPSS to adolescent girls through safe spaces and recreational activities.
2. Empower families through positive parenting interventions and support to family businesses.
3. Strengthen case management services for vulnerable children, particularly UASC and GBV survivors.
4. Strengthen and expand Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms (CBCPMs).
5. Provide dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls to enhance their safety and well-being.
6. Build the capacity of frontline child protection staff to better support vulnerable children and families.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

1. Safe Spaces for Adolescent Girls and MHPSS Activities: Create and maintain safe spaces for adolescent girls where they can receive MHPSS through recreational activities, counseling, and peer support groups. These spaces will serve as places for emotional healing, and empowerment, safe environment where girls can interact, share their experiences, and participate in skill-building activities that foster resilience. Recreational activities will provide a foundation for psychological healing while creating a sense of belonging and community among the girls. Conduct awareness-raising campaigns and peer-led activities focused on GBV, child protection, and mental health awareness, aimed at reducing stigma and promoting resilience among adolescent girls. 2. Empowerment of Families Through Positive Parenting and Family Businesses:Conduct workshops on positive parenting, targeting parents and caregivers in affected communities. These workshops will focus on fostering healthy family dynamics, improving communication, and teaching coping mechanisms for parents to better support their children’s emotional and psychological well-being. Facilitate the creation and strengthening of family businesses, providing training on entrepreneurship, financial literacy, and small business management to improve the economic independence of families. This will also serve as a form of psychosocial support, as financial stability reduces stress and improves overall family resilience. 3. Case Management and Referral Services for Vulnerable Children:Strengthen case management services to identify and support unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), GBV survivors, and other vulnerable children. The case management system will offer targeted support, including family reunification, safe housing, and access to essential services such as education, healthcare, and legal support for birth registration. Ensure that case workers are trained in trauma-informed care and child protection protocols, and that they work closely with local authorities, community leaders, and other stakeholders to facilitate appropriate referrals and support for vulnerable children. Coordinate to ensure that birth certificates are established to children, allowing them access to education, healthcare, and social services. 4. Strengthening Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms (CBCPMs):Conduct training workshops for community-based child protection committees (CBCPMs) on child protection standards, case management, as well as monitoring and reporting mechanisms. Strengthening CBCPMs will ensure that communities are equipped to identify, report, and respond to cases of child abuse, exploitation, and neglect. Provide technical support to CBCPMs to develop and implement community child protection action plans, fostering a culture of prevention and early intervention within communities. 5. Provision of Dignity Kits to Adolescent Girls and Women:Distribute dignity kits containing essential hygiene items, sanitary products, and other necessary items for adolescent girls and women, with a particular focus on survivors of GBV and those living in vulnerable conditions. These kits will help reduce the stigma and shame surrounding menstruation and improve the dignity and well-being of affected women and girls. Include in the dignity kits materials promoting mental health awareness and referrals to MHPSS services, empowering women and girls to seek support when needed. 6. Capacity Building of Frontline Child Protection Staff:Provide training sessions for frontline child protection staff, including caseworkers, social workers, and community-based volunteers, on best practices in child protection, MHPSS, trauma-informed care, and GBV response. This will ensure that staff have the necessary skills to provide comprehensive, compassionate, and effective support to vulnerable children and families.
