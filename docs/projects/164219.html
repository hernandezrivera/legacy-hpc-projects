Project Summary

The programme provides life-sustaining shelter and NFI support and contributes towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure. Targeting both IDPs (70%) and host communities (30%) involves activities; 3,000 standard NFI kits distribution (3,000 HHs) and winterization components such as distributing (500,000 L) heating fuel to 1,667 HHs, and (2,000) stoves to 2,000 HHs and (25,000) winter clothes kits for 25,000 children both gender . As well as providing restricted cash for house rent services (1,000 units) for those IDP vulnerable groups and prioritizing female or child headed house holds and elderly people and considering people with disabilities (1,000 HHs; 5,000 individuals).
This programme includes 2,500 house rehabilitation activities which will target both IDPs and host communities (2,500 HHs ; 12,500 individuals), this activity includes rehabilitating partially damaged units through ceiling repair, walls buildings and holes ﬁxing, doors and windows, infrastructure, electricity network and solar panels, plastering, plumping and providing the water tank. This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensuring that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel with ﬁeld coordination with the relevant local councils
The programme will seek feedback from both male and female beneﬁciaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access and equal opportunities.

4 - Needs

Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in significant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people.1 Of this, 3.8 million (69 per cent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city and camps in the north-east. Up to one million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements. The NFI PiN has declined by 22 per cent from 2019 to 3.42 million people in 2020.3 This reflects both a continuing but slow improvement in market functionality and the successful efforts of the sector to distribute assistance across the country.4 However, this progress is fragile as many communities suffer from high prices, unpredictable inflation, and low wages, while populations routinely report need for items outside the core NFI packages.5 There is still ongoing displacement, with roughly one million people expected to be displaced in 20206 while return movements have increased to some areas and an estimated 64 per cent of communities are underserved7. This is exacerbated by the deterioration of purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their needs during harsh weather with fuel and power sources remaining in high demand.IDPs are particularly affected by shelter damage and deterioration - there has been a 30 per cent reduction in the number of IDPs with access to undamaged shelter15. However, the overall IDP population has not decreased, while return movements to some areas have increased over the last three years16.

5 - Implementation of activities

The programme considers addressing the following activities outputs: 1. Providing accommodation and resilience to the 2,500 HHs through damaged houses rehabilitation, coordination with the local councils, and contracting with the contractors, considering HLP issues and develop a plan for implementation. Finally, those rehabilitated units will be delivered to the targeted assessed beneficiaries (12,500 individuals). 2. Supporting 17,167 HHs (33,333 persons) and easing their sufferings through the distribution of life saving NFIs assistance, and through seasonal and supplementary NFIs. (500,000 Liter of heating fuel for 1,667 HHs 300 L per family, 25,000 Winter clothes kits for children both gender 3,000 NFI standard kits and 2,000 stoves). This requires selecting 17,167 benefiting HHs according to set criteria with focus on women-headed families and the most vulnerable families. A final post- distribution assessment will be conducted to make sure that the project achieved its set objectives. Monitoring and reporting all over the project life cycle. 3Providing emergency cash for rent for the most vulnerable HHs (1,000 HHs for three/four months), this involves 1,000 benefiting families (5,000 individuals) according to set criteria with focus on women-headed families and the most vulnerable families and the IDPs HHs lost their shelter considering the forced evacuees HHs. rehabilitating the buildings and assessing those IDPs. with targeted number of units of 1,000 in total. A post-distribution impact phase will also be undertaken to obtain beneficiary feedback on quality, quantity for the services/goods received

Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, veriﬁed ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP, 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed, 4. Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form and verify the documents available. Monitoring: 1.
