Project Summary

The project aims to ensure for all refugees living in camps 24 block G and 27 block A and for the affected host communities:
- regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access to safe water for drinking and domestic needs.
- adequate, appropriate and acceptable sanitation facilities (including household waste).
The proposed project will be implemented in an impartial and non-discriminatory manner promoting the safety, dignity, and integrity of the beneficiaries. Actions are taken to ensure that the beneficiaries understand that they are entitled to free, fair, and certain assistance.
Gender and age often determine the actual access that people have to WASH services. They can also determine how limited or inadequate WASH services affect different groups. It is important to understand these differences and to offer services that address all demographics. The WASH-infrastructure will be set up inclusively, with consideration given to special needs. The hygiene workshops will be broken down by gender and age and will address the special needs of the individual groups.
Safety, privacy and dignity in accessing and using of WASH facilities and services will be at the heart of WASH strategy and programming. Protection strategies in WASH will ensure that all groups of people facing additional vulnerabilities including girls, women, older people, people with disabilities, have adequate and safe access to water collection and use; functional latrines and bathing units with safe and accessible pathways, solid doors, locks and other inclusive features such as screening and lighting; and safe spaces for practicing Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM).

1 - Identified Needs

Already before the influx of refugee Cox’s Bazar District was identified as one of the 20 (out of 64) most ‘lagging districts’ in Bangladesh, while Teknaf and Ukhia are among the 50 most socially deprived Upazilas of the country. The difficult terrain, lack of cultivable land, underdeveloped infrastructure, and scarcity of water contribute to the poor living conditions. Teknaf and Ukhia are high-risk areas for storm surges and cyclones, with Cox’s Bazar District as a whole experiencing a severe storm almost every year since 2015. Infrastructure to mitigate hazard risk is limited; embankments along the Naf river in Teknaf are in a poor state, and the capacity of existing cyclone shelters covers less than 50% of the pre-displacement population in both Upazilas.In areas where refugees and host communities live side by side, many are now accessing the same services and depend on the same labour markets (market prices have risen while the wage of daily labour has been halved from 400-500 TK/day to 200-250 TK/day), creating potential sources of tension as well as common vulnerabilities to hazards and shocks. While many newly built facilities provide services to both the host community and refugees, there are disparities in the quality between new facilities and pre-influx facilities.The Joint Response Plan 2020 estimates that 1.14 million people (855,000 RRs and 284,587 HCs) affected by the crisis are in urgent need of WASH support. The needs range from access to safe drinking water and water for domestic needs, over adequate sanitation facilities, to access to hygiene items and a need for improved solid waste management. The proposed project focuses on Block A in Camp 27 and Block G in Camp 24, where many HHs have high needs in all of these four areas simultaneously.The JRP 2020 found that a large number of women and girls use makeshift bathing spaces inside their shelters to avoid overcrowded and unsafe sanitation facilities. Altogether only 78% of latrines in the camps are functioning (JRP 2020) and many households suffer from low coverage of sanitation and hygiene infrastructure: too many people are using the available latrines and many people face challenges due to the distance from their shelters to reach them (e.g. elderly and people with disabilities; girls and women being exposed to gender-based violence, especially at night). Poorly lit facilities further exacerbate the issues. While this situation holds true also for Block G in Camp 24 and parts of Block A in Camp 27, thanks to the previous project, parts of Block A in Camp 27 are relatively well covered and will only require maintenance and small-scale construction during the proposed project.Needs related to environmental sanitation are vast: trash and solid waste, as well as stagnant water and human faeces endanger the health of the camp residents and the surrounding communities. Awareness for adequate waste management, as well as sufficient facilities are urgently needed. In addition to this, hygiene promotion related to safe water handling practices and hygiene practices are essential to enable beneficiaries to lead healthy lives. Many needs of women and girls are related to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). The material (pads) are too expensive and/or other needs are prioritized within the household.

2 - Activities or outputs

Output 1: Critically needed WASH infrastructure and services are established and maintained in camps and partly in host communities.- Activity 1.1: Waste collection (provision of bins, establishment of collection points, organized collection – in camps only)- Activity 1.2: Operating of solid waste management plant (segregation, recycling and composting – in camps and host community)- Activity 1.3: Faecal sludge management FSM (Desludging and rehabilitation of latrines – in camps only)- Activity 1.4: Construction/rehabilitation and operating of faecal sludge plant (in camps and host community)- Activity 1.5: Maintenance & Operating of WASH facilities (hand washing stations & bathing cubicles, drainage channels, etc. - in camps only)- Activity 1.6: Construction/Replacement of WASH infrastructure (mainly Camp 24)- Activity 1.7: Upgrade of WASH infrastructure to meet protection standards (lights, locks – in camps only)- Activity 1.8: Establishment of WASH maintenance groups (in camps only)During the construction, operation and maintenance, the following aspects will be taken into consideration: Operation and maintenance are in accordance to SPHERE sector standards Protection sensitivities are taken into consideration by ensuring that the latrines are simple and safe to access for vulnerable groups, segregated by gender, well-lit, and lockableIn total 18 volunteers/community mobilizers will be involved in the implementation of operation and maintenance. Community engagement will be ensured that will create an essential understanding of perceptions, needs, coping mechanisms, capacities, existing norms, leadership structures and priorities, as well as the appropriate actions to be taken.Full or non-functioning latrines will be identified, and the decision to empty the same will be made on the basis of the guidelines of the WASH sector. As long as no FSM facility has been built, the latrines will be emptied into a second ditch with a manual pump, stabilised with limestone, closed and secured with a fence.A team for emptying latrines will be identified in consultation with the communities and in collaboration with other relevant actors and trained. It will be provided with protective equipment and have access to shower facilities with atomising sprayers to guarantee decontamination after work. Each team member will be immunised (against types of watery diarrhoea, especially typhus).In the event of an epidemic, e.g. Covid-19, a team of chlorine sprayers will be selected and trained to disinfect the latrines on a daily basis. This will be coordinated with the crisis team at camp level. People need places in which they can bathe in privacy and with dignity. In over-crowded camps, this is often not possible on the household level. To meet this need, one bathing cubicle per 50 people will be established in accordance with sector strategy and Sphere standard.Proper locations for the construction of latrines and bathing cubicles will be selected in collaboration with the communities, the controllers, the camp authorities, and the WASH sector to ensure the protection needs are met. For the design of the bathing cubicles and latrines the beneficiaries, including young women and people with disabilities, will be involved and protection needs will be taken into consideration (simple and secure access for vulnerable groups, segregation by gender, good lighting, and locks).In most of the camps, faecal sludge management facilities (FSM) are missing or inadequate. Based on the technical standards, health and environmental aspects, cultural acceptance, appropriateness, operation, and maintenance, the FSM disposal and treatment system will be established after the monsoon season.
