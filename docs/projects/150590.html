Project Summary

The overall objective of the project over 2 years is to respond to the immediate shelter needs of vulnerable populations in Area C communities surrounding area A and B of the West Bank. This response will be through self-help approach for the most vulnerable families to provide shelter repairs and transitional shelter solutions close to existing homes, in order to: 1) Promote self-reliance, maintain, and support most vulnerable families who live with the effect of violence, coercion, and deprivation with shelter related solutions. 2) Enhancing the protection and reducing the likelihood of displacement or forced eviction. 3) Contributing in solving the problem of overcrowding houses by creating new spaces through rearrangement to ensure privacy and dignity 4) Reduce the cost of living by adopting green approach. 5) Extend the life span of existing shelters; in sight of the nullity of the issuance of building permits in Area C. Thus, complying with the second HRP strategic objectives “SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services”. And complying with the shelter cluster strategic objectives “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity” and “Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased”.
This project comply with PHC’s Rehabilitation Program objectives, aiming at contributing of elimination some poverty effects on vulnerable women HH’s, improving the quality of the proper housing stock, reducing land designation for housing and infrastructure by restoring the existing housing stock and improving the social, economic, and health conditions for the beneficiaries.
The project will provide urgent housing rehabilitation to 196 FHH or vulnerable families in Area C of the West Bank whose shelter is unfit for weather conditions, overcrowded, and/or unsafe. In the first year, PHC will commence to respond to 50 vulnerable families located in Area C of the West Bank as described hereafter.

Needs

The Israeli occupation and the separation wall in the West Bank have negatively affected Palestinian communities especially women’ and children’ lives and rights. These policies significantly increase the humanitarian vulnerability of Palestinians, and affect their residency status, economic situation, access to education, health services, and their ability to plan and develop their communities. The lack of planning and the nullity issuance of building permits increases the likelihood of displacement and forced eviction of Palestinians. Displaced women and girls lose the space where they enjoy privacy, autonomy, social networks and the center of their lives and are negatively affected by the occupation-related political, social and economic oppression. Safety, security risks and threats, including the violence perpetrated against women, the restrictions imposed on their freedom of movement, and the family forced displacement, are direct consequences of the Israeli occupation policies and measures, as perpetrated by the Israeli military, settlers and systematic discrimination. These threats are reflected as domestic and social violence for females, and military violence for males. In addition to the declining economic conditions across Palestine led to a situation of a chronic shortfall of housing, and an inability of vulnerable families to maintain their current housing units.According to 2018 HNO: At least 1.9 million Palestinians experience, or are at risk of, conflict and violence, displacement and denial of access to livelihoods, among other threats. The most vulnerable are currently denied or restricted in their access to essential services. Elderly women are more vulnerable to poverty, disease, and have limited access to services. There is no social security system in place to ensure a decent life for the elderly and to provide the different life requirements, such as housing, healthcare, income, disability needs. Palestinians are expected to continue to depend on their children in their old age instead of social security, and the burden of the career role is often placed on the shoulders of women. Disability remains a critical source of vulnerability, that can lead to gender based discrimination. Women and girls with disabilities suffer from amplified forms of GBV, and have limited access to services.In mid of 2017, as a joint work between PHC and Ministry of Social Development MoSD approved a list of 327 vulnerable families in urgent need for assistance to rehabilitate their houses in different geographic locations in Area C surrounding area A and B in the West Bank. In mid of 2018 PHC assessed and updated that list and approved a revised list that consist of 196 (50% FHH) vulnerable families in immediate help. However, 50 (25 FHH) vulnerable families were assigned to be targeted in 2019 in: Ramallah, Salfit, Jericho, and EJ (outside the wall). Through the selection criteria, PHC Grading System identifies the neediest beneficiaries, this system assigns that divorced and widowed women or FHH have high weight that leads to increase their benefit chances in 2019. Furthermore, this system designed to respond to different segments of the beneficiaries (women, men, boys, girls & elderly) and their different needs. Findings of the assessment in Area C mainly were:1.Inadequate housing conditions increase likelihood of displacement from area C to area A and B, in particular for FHH families.2.Weak capacities for self protection and limitation of the Palestinian Authorities.3.Nullity of issuance of building permits in Area C made keeping and maintaining the existing shelters in that area very significant especially for the vulnerable families.4.FHH often have less capacity to do the basic repair works themselves.5.Some HH's have family members with disabilities reducing the chances for the active members to seek employment and limiting their income mostly due to social welfare related to the disability.

Activities

PHC’s methodology ensures the participation of different gender/age groups in the project cycle; starting with the needs assessments, to implementation, monitoring & evaluation. PHC will develop a proactive approach to emergency preparedness, in order to ensure early action is taken when required. This approach includes risk monitoring, establishment of coordination and management arrangements and preparing for joint needs assessments along with the Palestinian official bodies especially MoSD, CBO’s. The official bodies will provide and support services inside the community using available resources, voluntary and skills, and will manage adverse conditions that potentially could lead to emergency, this will lead to increase beneficiaries coping capacity and contributing to building resilience.PHC aims to avoid, minimize and mitigate the risks associated the delivery of this project, by identifying risks of the targeted beneficiaries in terms of safety, dignity, do no harm by mitigating physical and sociological threats through prioritizing intervention to provide the most appropriate assistance to beneficiaries without discrimination or exposing them to further risk. PHC approach is also guided by Meaningful access related to the provision of its assistance (i.e. handicapped, elderly). –Accountability: maximizing transparency and setup inclusive and accessible feedback & complaint mechanism. Beneficiaries’ participation and empowerment: ensuring that the beneficiaries are actively involved and participated in all the process; decision-making, implementation, monitoring, evaluation & management. They are the key element to analysis their needs and the means of solving their problems. Since the building is prohibited in area C The project will contribute to reduce the likelihood of displacement of vulnerable families to area A or B through upgrading existing unsuitable shelters to avoid replacement of place of residence from area C to area A or B. The project team will revisit 50 assessed vulnerable families in area C to define the rehabilitation budget for each beneficiary, & to prepare an accurate BoQ’s and specifications for each shelter.3. Indicators

PHC aim to achieve the optimum results regarding beneficiaries by defining the aptness support for emergency vulnerable families with a reasonable cost. Previous phases of the program showed that small grants make a significant difference in the lives of vulnerable families at several levels:At the community level, PHC formed the Advisory Committee, which consists of community female and male representatives who acquaint with the areas of the target group and familiar with their needs and challenges; to support the program implementation and participate in the selection of the beneficiaries according to housing conditions and socio economic situation of families.PHC involves beneficiaries in the different stages of the project cycle. One of the assessment criteria is to involve the families in determining their needs according to their gender and age (e.g. vulnerable FHH’s, the elderly,…etc) or individuals with special needs. At the beginning, kick off workshops with beneficiaries will be conducted to discuss the project components and to take into consideration any recommendations from beneficiaries’ side in project design and implementation. During the project, another workshops will be conducted to gather their feedback, complaints and lessons learned in order to take the suitable action accordingly. At monitoring and evaluation level, PHC will monitor the progress of works on daily basis. Monitoring process for this project will be based on regular field visits (PHC, Shelter cluster, and OCHA), and periodic reporting. As a result of the field visits reports the Project Committee will share feedback and information concerning challenges and issues raised on site to partners. PHC always aims to provide assistance in ways that are supportive of recovery and long term development, and do its best to “do no harm” by concealing them from additional riskEvaluation process will be based on a questionnaire, financial audit, meetings and quality assurance process. It is worth to note that the questionnaire is a tool designed and used in the recent phases of the program to indicate the impact on the living conditions of the most vulnerable families, the degree of safety and adequacy, quality of work, and the progress achieved. And in order to assess the changes that were attributed to the project intervention, as well as to examine whether targets have been achieved. Knowing that one of the Project committee mandate is to implement and monitor the project to be gender balanced. Through the evaluation process, the changes occurred in terms of safety, dignity and integrity will be measured separately for women, men, girls and boys where according to this project 225 are direct beneficiaries; 121 Females, 104 males, 81 Children, 34 elderly.PHC pays special attention on the environmental issues; neutral impact on environment with mitigation or enhancement, to achieve this PHC improved technical practices for basic repairs, resulting in more energy efficient and environmentally friendly living environments. PHC will take into consideration during the implementation of the project measures to promote more environmental friendly housing, cost and energy efficient building practices.To achieve this, the indicators of the project will be:Shelter upgrading through providing cash or materials assistance and technical support:* 50 HH's assisted to upgrade substandard housing unit in Area C in 2019, 146 in 2020.Provision of winterization assistance or household NFIs to vulnerable households at risk of exposure: * 28 HH's provided with climate protection assistanceCommunity capacity building and awareness raising:* 50 HH's supported with training and tools for self recovery or protection measuresFamilies under protection concerns or at risk of displacement provided with protective shelter materials: * 3 HH assisted with shelter protective materials
