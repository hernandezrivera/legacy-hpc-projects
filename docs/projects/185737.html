Project Summary

INTRODUCTION:
Since the arrival of the Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar District in 2017, waste generation has increased from 32 tons/day to 146 tons/day in the most affected Union Parishad in Teknaf and Ukhia Upazilas (WASH sector data). Without SWM systems in place, waste ends up in drains and open spaces and pollutes the environment and creates unsanitary conditions for the community. While in the refugee camps, SWM operations were immediately set up by WASH partners of the international humanitarian response, host communities adjacent to the camps faced unprecedent unsanitary conditions. In 2018 UNDP initiated 3-year project to establish basic solid waste management systems in the most affected Unions adjacent to the camps of Teknaf and Ukhia Upazilas (Raja Palong and Palong Khali, Whykong, Nhilla, Baharchara and Teknaf Municipality) and supported the camps with waste cleaning operations and the construction and management of a Sanitary landfill in Ukhia camps. The overall SWM systems and infrastructure will be hand it over in April 2022. However there are still major gaps and needs that the new project proposes to address/reinforce (see section needs). Based on the lessons learned of that ongoing initiative, the current project aims at supporting the local government to lead quality and community-driven service, with particular attention to gender, age and people with disabilities barriers to participate in the SWM systems in place, and continuing the waste management operations for final waste disposal and collection of recyclables in the refugee camps.
PROJECT GOALS:
1) Keep the host community adjacent to the camps free from uncollected waste and treat waste as a valuable resource, which will reduce the risks to vector-borne diseases, the needs for landfill and the impact to the ecological value of Teknaf and Ukhia Upazilas.
2) Build awareness and capacity at all tiers of the government for SWM and general behaviour change towards waste (no littering, reduce, reuse, recycle) addressing particular gender, age and people with disabilities needs in the host community.
PROJECT LOCATIONS (see Ward number in Part 3):
Raja Palong (Ukhia Dorga Bazar, Kutupalong Bazar), Palong Khali (Thaingkhali Bazar, Balukhali Bazar, Palongkhali (jointly managed with BDRC); Whykong (Kharangkhali, Noya Bazar, Mina Bazar, Whykong, Unchiprang Bazaar); Nhilla (Jahdimura, Leda bazar, Hnila Bazar, Naikongkhali bazar, Dhamdamia Ghat, Muchoni Bazaar); Baharchara (Bazar); Teknaf Municipality (all). New locations: Nhilla (Dhamdamia Ghat, Muchoni Bazaar); Whykong (Unchiprang Bazaar), Teknaf Municipality (uncovered area).
GENDER, AGE, PEOPLE WITH DISABILITY NEEDS MAINSTREAMING:
Solid waste management systems in host community are based on daily waste collection from house to house, which reduces any kind of cultural, physical, gender barrier to access the service. However, the project will integrate specific gender, age, people with disability needs, and reach out all members of the HH with awareness, young and adults, as they may play a different role in the SWM system that can impact negatively the goal of the project. Zero tolerance will be applied to child labour within the recycling waste collection.
ENVIRONMENT AND CLIMATE CHNGE MAINSTREAMING:
Proper collection and management of solid waste significantly contributes to reduce the impact that solid waste causes to the environment. SWM also contributes to mitigate climate change effects. As for instances, the production of compost reduces the emission of Methane that would be released if dumped mixed in landfill (1Tn compost reduces 1 Tn of CO2e); segregation and recycle waste also helps to preserve natural resources such as oil for virgin plastic, and forests for virgin fiber paper, both linked to important greenhouse gases cycle.

1 - Identified Needs

1. STRENGTHEN WASTE MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS IN HOST COMMUNITIES. Local markets in host communities play an important role as service providers for the camp response. Thousands of people move in and out the camp to purchase commodities every day. Uncollected solid waste in marketplaces creates unsanitary conditions, which lead to vector-borne diseases; and open burning of waste which causes direct health hazards. SWM systems are needed as strong preventive measure to spread disease. Currently, 13 of these marketplaces are covered by SWM systems, serving 5,236 HH & 5,390 shops. The systems have proved to be effective to reduce waste littering, as 71% of waste generated is collected and proper disposed, including 12% of organic waste for compost. The collection of recyclables has increased 49% over the last year (UNDP SWM KPI, Aug’21). In terms of beneficiary’s perceptions, 98.9% of the direct beneficiaries are satisfied with the waste collection service; 83% of them would pay for such a waste management system and allow the daily collection of waste from their place (UNDP SWM KAP survey, June’21). However, not all marketplaces adjacent to the camps in these locations are covered or fully covered by SWM systems, and waste dumping points are still scattered along the road. 2. SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT INFRASTRUCTURES, PARTICULARLY SANITARY LANDFILL IN HOST COMMUNITY. In comparison to the refugee camps, host communities do not have any site where waste can be safely disposed. The facility has been demanded by different NGOs and local government. In the camps, the Sanitary landfill constructed for the 27 camps in Ukhia has proved to be very effective to support the camps in reporting proper management of solid waste. Gradually, camps are using the landfill and consolidating best practices (69% of the total). The disposal of more than 10,000m3 have improved the sanitary camp conditions. Continue efforts of maintenance and operation works of the Sanitary landfills is required from April 2022. Transportation system to the landfill is also necessary, as only 27% of the WASH partners have trucks (UNDP, May'21 Report). In the host, construction and maintenance of small facilities are necessary at this stage as Union/Municipality budget is still limited. 3. AWARENESS AND BEHAVIOUR CHANGE - NO LITTERING AND 3R’S. Awareness raising and behaviour change on no-littering and waste segregation at source needs to continue, as HH and shops still mix and dump waste at source; 67.3% of the HH and shops reported segregating the waste in two bins. More efforts are needed to expand the concept and practice of 3Rs (Reduce, reuse, and recycle) principle to also reduce the waste entering into the camps, as only 36.3% of beneficiaries reported having a little knowledge on it (UNDP SWM KAP survey, Jun’21). In camps, the collection of recyclables requires a more systematic collection approach and access of the recycling dealers to maximize recycling and reduce child labour (UNDP SWM Gender Strategy, 2019). 4. LOCAL GOVERNMENT CAPACITY BUILDING. For the sustainability of the SWM systems, the Unions and Municipality government have been fully engaged to lead the change from the very beginning. The project is strengthening the role of the WATSAN committees, creating SWM Steering Committees, providing office equipment, monitoring KPI to optimize resources, initiating fee collection mechanism and establishing community feedback mechanism. The SWM systems will be handed over to the local government by April 2022. However, Union Parishad chairman and WATSAN members, as well from the Municipality, are requesting continue support to manage the systems (UNDP SWM Study of Teknaf and Ukhia, Nov’21). 5. URGE RESPONSE CONTINGENCY PLAN. Lessons learns from COVID19 response, preparedness cleaning activities, fire incidents, etc. prove the benefits of providing the project with capacity to prevent / respond crisis while large funds are awaiting in camps and host communities.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1. Scale up the existing SWM system in 4 new locations in Nhilla, Whykong and Teknaf Municipality, including Distribution of bins, PPEs, Safety trainings, Working tools and Vans, Deployment of CfW for 6 months and establishing KPI monitoring systems. This activity also includes the field and monitoring support to the local government to continue managing 14 locations with existing SWM in place in 5 Unions and 1 Municipality.Activity 2. Construction of a Sanitary Landfill for host communities and construction and basic maintenance of Union/Municipality SWM facilities - including Secondary collection points, Compost plants, MRFs.Activity 3. Operation and maintenance of Sanitary landfill in refugee camps Ukhia and Teknaf and facilitation the collection of recyclables from camp MRFs by the Local Recycling sector. Cfw and supervisors and compactor will be on daily work at the facilities. Association of recycling dealers will develop SOP and collect recyclable waste from camp MRFs in Teknaf and Ukhia.Activity 4. Strengthen the SWM governance system, capacity building, feedback mechanism, monitoring system and fee collection mechanism - ward committees, bazar committees, WATSAN committee, ISWM Committees in 5 Union Parishads ant Teknaf Municipality. Upazila and District level authorities will be also engaged for decision-making and policy development. Activity 5. Awareness raising and behaviour change on no-littering, waste segregation and 3Rs (reduce, reuse, recycle) in HC. Volunteers, IEC materials, radio program, orientation sessions, etc. Initial assessment to identify special needs/barriers to SWM to male-females, children, group with disabilities, elder people will tailor the communication campaign and design of the community engagement mechanisms and waste collection services.Activity 6. Contingency plan for SW emergencies and COVID mainstreaming. CfW and supervisors for preparedness/urge response to waste disasters.
