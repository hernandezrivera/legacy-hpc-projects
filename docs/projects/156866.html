Project Summary

This project aims to raise awareness of people in the field of risk education, psychosocial support for children in this field, in addition to training humanitarian workers to be effective people in raising the awareness of society in the context of risk education.
HRO will deliver the planned activities through a remote management model, and through teams working inside Syria. Over the course of the project, we aim to build our teams capacity to address the needs caused by the explosive remnants of war.
HRO will recruit Syrian staff for the program who will be trained by RE Specialized technical advisers to conduct:
Risk Education
Community Liaison
ERW marked for subsequent demolition, leading to reduced accidents, increased community resilience and an enhanced sense of security, and safe and unhindered humanitarian access allowing for implementation of projects designed to alleviate the crisis in Syria
Two locations will be targeted, in Aleppo and Idlib. It is expected that 10,000 beneficiaries will be targeted within one year.
HRO implemented one project in 2017 for 6 months, in two locations :Aurm AlJouz and Azmarin in Idleb, we reached the total of 6549 beneficiaries.

Needs

1 in 2 people in Syria are potentially at risk from the threat of explosive hazards. Based on current population estimations, 10.2 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 1,980 communities reporting explosive contamination in the last two years. These communities are spread throughout the country and correlate to areas that have seen most explosive incidents over the past 12 months. The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating, with the loss of community members, an increased number of people with permanent injuries and loss of access to properties, basic services and livelihoods. It also prevents safe delivery of humanitarian aid, affects population movement and limits the scope for recovery and reconstruction efforts. A wide range of infrastructure, such as private property, schools, hospitals, markets, agricultural land, wells and roads may be blocked by explosive hazards, impeding access to education, health services, marketplaces and safe livelihood opportunities. Respondents in the assessed communities reported that agricultural land, roads, and private property were the most contaminated, followed by other public infrastructure, schools, and hospitals. Provision of risk awareness on these hazards is crucial to provide civilians with basic safety messages to reduce the daily risks posed by contamination. The survey of suspected hazardous areas, followed by the marking and clearance of located items is the only way to remove perceived and confirmed threats. Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families. Based on available data from where collection has been possible, on average 1.5 people are killed and 2 are injured in explosive hazards accidents, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation. The number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased the burden on the health sector, in particular, on the provision of specialized services including trauma care, physical rehabilitation, and psychosocial support on the short and longer term. In addition to physical and psychological impact, explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities, depriving them of livelihoods and sources of income, increasing vulnerabilities. While all population groups are vulnerable to the threat of explosive hazards, certain groups are at a higher risk in correlation with gender and age, as well as social roles and responsibilities. Certain identified activities expose particularly children. In correlation with previous findings, farming, herding, moving or travelling, tampering or playing with items, and rubble removal were reported to be the most at-risk behaviors’ or activities by communities reporting deaths or injuries from explosive hazards . The scale of the explosive hazard threat in Syria is not yet fully known and the prevention and mitigation measures to reduce the impact of the contamination on civilians need to be comprehensive and large scale, through the conduct of surveys, marking of dangerous areas, removal of hazards and risk awareness. Greater support is also required to support survivors and their families.

Protection Risk Analysis

Community-based protection services and targeted assistance might be perceived as delivered in a discriminatory manner - a way that favors one group of beneficiaries over another. This is likely to lead to disagreements amongst potential beneficiaries. The limited availability or absence of redress mechanisms was identified as a risk in connection with protection monitoring and assessment activities. Grievances might arise between beneficiaries and humanitarian actors if there is no protection response provided once needs have been identified. To prevent this and mitigate expectations, clear communications with communities is recommended, along with appropriate training of staff carrying out these activities and operational coordination to avoid assessment fatigue. Strengthening of referral pathways by organizations is a key measure to be taken. Engagement with Syrian state institutions shall be in accordance with the Charter of the United Nations and as per A/RES/182 and governed by the framework of agreements between the Syrian state and the UN agencies, funds and programmes, as well as S/RES/2393

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Circumstances of the conflict in Syria &fluidity of the situation require operations to be conducted on the ground by the field teams inside Syrian, who have been carefully selected to ensure that they are able to work in the areas identified for this project, &that they have the correct networks in place to conduct activities safely in each location. HRO has proven experience working in Idleb &Aleppo; in both areas we have an established field office, used as the base for our ongoing projects. We will provide regular information about the security situation, to work safely in the chosen locations, our capacity to expand into new areas which have become more stable, or our need to change, suspend or cease operations in a worsening security environment. The same contextual approach &continuous conflict assessment will be applied for all phases of this project.The main risk to the project is the unstable security situation in Syria &the challenge of working in a potentially volatile &sensitive environment. We are continuously monitoring the security situation and conducting the security assessments so that substantive information &warnings can be passed to staff on the ground about threat patterns. This helps them to avoid areas of bombardment &changing areas of armed conflict, including shifting front lines.
