Project Summary

MOH indicated that 16,665 surgeries are on the waiting list whereas the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2022 mentioned that the number of patients that need specialized surgical intervention is around 5,400 cases.
Unfortunately, It is known by experience that in any emergency that hits Gaza such as escalations, the Great March of Return (GMR), internal political/civic fights or even an epidemiological crisis such as COVID-19, elective surgeries are the first thing to be deprioritized. Hence, regardless of the efforts of health care providers to cope with the needs of the waiting lists, the issue comes up frequently and remains a need area.
To respond to this need, Al Ahli Hospital (AAH) in Gaza has proven capacities as a surgical hospital with a high reputation in safe surgical management. It has operated as a surgical hospital for 140 years and has been an implementing partner for three previous Country Based Pooled Fund (CBPF) projects either in elective surgeries, trauma management and limb reconstruction. Therefore, AAH with the support of Augusta Victoria Hospital (AVH) operated by the Lutheran World Federation (LWF), found it crucial to support vulnerable women, men, boys and girls who are now on the waiting list of the Ministry of Health (MoH). Coordination was done with MoH to conduct 1,000 surgeries to 1000 patients. 500 will be ENT. The rest are advanced Minimally Invasive Surgeries (MIS), general surgery, orthopedic, gynecology, and urology. AVH will further develop AAH’s capacity in Infection Prevention Control (IPC) and rational uses of antibiotics. AVH will train 30 staff from AAH either through coaching, mentoring and onsite demonstrations. AVH is aiming to visit AAH 3 times, the 1st for follow up visit/training and another 2 visits for follow up. LWF has strong financial, monitoring and accountability systems in place and adheres to policies ensuring sustainable quality programming, such as the Code of Conduct, Child Protection, Core Humanitarian Standards, Gender Justice and Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) all to be shared with AAH. Furthermore, AAH will work with Handicap International to develop a checklist to determine the People With Disabilities (PWD), to track and report on them. GBV cases will be referred to specialized centers to address this issue. Scenario risk analysis was done based on previous experiences and similar projects as mitigations were planned such as remote management with AVH. Coordination with MoH, the Health Cluster (HC), Women’s Centers, the Union of Health Work Committees (UHWC) for complementarity and referrals are well defined in the project. Regarding finance and procurement, AAH has implemented successfully USAID projects, CBPF projects and followed either USAID or OCHA guidelines and has a strong PMER (planning, monitoring, evaluating, reporting) system in place. This project links to Health cluster Obj. 1 Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt. and to strategic obj.2: ,meeting health needs and providing basic health services.

1 - Needs

As the Gaza Strip is having a continuous protracted crisis and a 15-year blockade for goods and people, socio-economic and health situations are deteriorating. In coordination meetings with NGO units at the MOH as well as the international cooperation department, they mentioned the service needs to be backed up by a well-equipped surgical hospital to minimize the waiting list for elective surgeries at the MOH, especially the Shifa hospital. It is known by experience that in any emergency that hits Gaza such as escalations, the Great March of Return (GMR), internal political/civic fights or even an epidemiological crisis such as COVID-19, elective surgeries are the first thing to be deprioritized. Hence, regardless of the efforts of health care providers to cope with the needs of the waiting lists, the issue comes up frequently and remains a need area.MOH indicated that 16,665 surgeries are on the waiting list where major surgeries are 3,075, moderate 4,530, minor 8319 and skilled needed surgeries are 741. Most of these cases are in Shifa and Naser hospitals where Shifa has 5,542 and Naser has 2,442. Among the 5,542 in Shifa, there are 2,238 E.N.T cases, 303 urology cases, 585 General surgery, and 307 orthopedics. However, the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2022 mentioned that the number of patients that need specialized surgical intervention is around 5,400 cases.AAH has requested a sample patients list to conduct FGDs for the project assessment, gender analysis and power dynamics. The Focus group discussions targeted men, women, and children’s parents. 7 FGS were conducted, and 3 were with women with a total of 25 while 35 men participated in 4FGDs. Results showed that parents were so worried about their children who have E.N.T problems as they are unable to afford surgeries. It was so clear that children who need endoscopic sinus surgeries, Septoplasty, and Rhinoplasty have recurrent infections and need hospitalization and medicine. The average cost of the mentioned surgeries which are considered major is around 6,000 ILS. Some need more than one procedure and some families have more than one child. Interestingly, a woman said, “I was able to get a loan and make an operation for the girl because I am afraid that she loses hearing and not get married while the boy can wait till we get some extra money”. Furthermore, women declared that some government doctors prioritize cases who visit them in their private clinics which they can’t afford to.Caregivers as always in Gaza are the care holders in homes so recurrent hospitalization overburdens them. They were convinced that why should we keep visiting clinics and stay overnight in hospitals while one surgical procedure can solve the issues. Another woman mentioned that the repeated absence from school is affecting the educational level of their minors. Concerning men, they explained that they have chronic E.N.T issues and they prioritize family food over certain surgeries. However, on said” winter is coming” and we suffer a lot in winter. He hoped to end his suffering by doing the surgery.General surgery and gynecology looked like a priority area also where both men and women were angry because of waiting and poverty, especially those who needed MIS.In addition, many men showed that they have done operations under “general surgery” and discovered later that a urology intervention is still needed. On said “ I would be happy to do my operation at the less crowded hospital as each time I go to Shifa for any procedure, I take antibiotics for a long time to get recovered infections” A woman said that “I need MIS as I was diagnosed with a gynecologic problem and don’t want to open my abdomen, my sister had stayed a long time in the hospital after the open abdominal surgery while others left the hospital quickly after the MIS. I can’t afford MIS on my own''. She further said when I have abdominal pain, I suffer a lot and become unable to do my home duties regardless of using the painkillers.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Based on International Humanitarian Law (IHL) that includes of Right to Health andRight to Life and equal and impartial access to aid, the MoH which indicated that 16,665 surgeries are on the waiting list for surgeries .Patients who are in need for elective surgeries should be prioritized by health providers with focus onE.N.T.. AVH and AAH want to support Gaza’s vulnerable population by conducting1,000 electives surgeries to needy patients. These patients have the right of access tohealth care which is not fully met as the MOH is not able to do these surgeries on timerelated due to many factors; the15 years blockade on Gaza, 2014 war, the internalPalestinian division, other recent humanitarian crises, COVID-19 pandemic which aredepleting the MOH resources. These factors have made a domino effect and shifted theelective scheduled cases which are in need for surgeries to months and even yearsespecially E.N.T cases. Ahli Management is committed to protect all children from harm, Ahli has a very detailed policy that revised every 3 years the latest revision was during 2020. The Ahli safeguarding and child protection policy has clear set and detailed procedures and guidelines that all Ahli staff, volunteers, Contractors and CBO’s partners have to read, comprehend and sign the policies before starting any work with the hospital. The policy ensures that every child, regardless of their age, gender, disability, color, religion has the right to equal protection from harm, such as physical, sexual, emotional abuse, maltreatment, medical care negligence, exploitation, child labor etc The policy describes what to do when there are concerns about a child’s safety or wellbeing. Also, it explains the procedure of the selection of the right person to work with children and young people. Regular refresher courses are also conducted to equip and update the staff knowledge on safeguarding for the staff every two years.
