Start Date

01/01/2020

End Date

31/12/2020

Project Summary

1. Establishment of 5 standard emergency medical care posts at the 5 exit-entry checkpoints (EECPs) to provide primary health care and emergency medical care to people crossing the contact line both ways.
2. Establishment of 6 mobile emergency medical care teams (6 emergency medical vehicles) attached to each medical post at EECPs to provide primary health care and emergency medical care to people living in the GCA within the 5km zone from the contact line. Each mobile team would function within an operational zone of 200 km2 (20 km lengthwise on either side from the EECP and 5 km in depth from the contact line).
3. Establishment of the Call Center, a unit for collecting information about the people’s need for medicines and humanitarian aid in the so-called ‘gray zones’, in order to promptly respond to risks or events hazardous to the health of the target groups.
4. Establishment of approximately 100 workplaces for the population of the Luhansk and Donetsk regions through full or partial employment of health care and trade jobs professionals, regardless of their age or gender, in the project.
The project focuses primarily on target groups living in isolated settlements in the GCA within the 5km zone from the contact line. Medical assistance will be provided to everybody on the basis of medical ethics and medical secrecy, regardless of gender, age, race, nationality, linguistic choice or presence of mental illnesses, including to the LGBT community representatives and people with HIV/AIDS.

1 - Needs

As of October 2019, there are 5 functioning EECPs at the 427 km long contact line. They were crossed 9.2 million times in the 8 months of 2019. The situation with regards to opening of the sixth EECP in Zolote remains unresolved after nearly one year of unsuccessful negotiations. More than half of all crossings are made by elderly people, which make up 30% of all those in need of help. 14 civilian deaths were recorded at the EECPs and near them in the first quarter of 2019. There are no first-aid posts at 4 EECPs, and the NGO PREMIÈRE URGENCE first-aid post is located 1 km away from the Mar’inka EECP. The only first-aid post deployed directly at a checkpoint is the emergency medical care post at the Stanytsya Luhanska EECP operated by the International Charitable Organisation ‘International Medical Care’ (InterMediCare). About 400 thousand people live within the 5km zone from the contact line. About 60% of families lack safe and sufficient access to hospitals and medical services. The situation is further aggravated by an isolation of a large number of settlements, vast distances between medical facilities in rural areas and major health centers, lack of a sufficient number of qualified medical personnel, inadequate road conditions, medicines price increase, high travel costs for their purchase and much more. Also, cases were recorded when the state health care providers refused to provide services to people living in the 5km zone due to restrictions on movement through roadblocks and significant land mine contamination of the territory. Frequent hostilities and the paralysis of economic activity caused a significant outflow of the employable population, especially males, as well as youth, from the conflict zone. Monitoring of medical assistance requests from the population that crosses the contact line and lives in a 5-km zone indicates that its main recipients are women (69%) and people over 60 (63%). Moreover, women made twice as much requests for emergency medical care as men. Project needs. 1. Acquisition and installation of 5 emergency medical care posts at the 5 EECPs in the GCA, supplying them with medical equipment, furniture and necessary medicines, providing residential lease for medical personnel, purchase of special-purpose clothing and employee labour remuneration. The posts would operate following the model of the InterMediCare first-aid post at the Stanytsya Luhanska EECP, fully functional since 2018 with the support of the Slovak Government. The post has all necessary equipment, furniture and medicines; the working environment for its personnel is fully ensured. 2. Acquisition of 3 equipped emergency medical vehicles, operational support to all mobile emergency medical teams, providing them with essential medicines and fuels, residential lease for medical personnel, purchase of special-purpose clothing, employee labour remuneration. The Government of Slovakia has provided InterMediCare with 3 equipped emergency medical vehicles, which will be deployed at the Stanytsya Luhanska, Maiorske and Mar’inka EECPs. 3. Deployment of the Call Centre, equipping it with appropriate technical means, employee labour remuneration.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Activities1. Provision of primary health care and emergency medical care, as well as of OPD, PHC, MHPSS and SRH services within the 5km zone from the contact line and directly at the EECPs.2. Support of health referral procedures within the 5km zone from the contact line, as well as directly at the EECPs. 3. Collection of information about the need for medication and humanitarian aid of people living within the 5km zone from the contact line and those crossing the EECPs.4. Dissemination of information and advocacy regarding sexual and reproductive health, mental health and gender-based violence in the GCA.5. Regular joint monitoring missions with representatives of local authorities and the UN agencies to different areas of project implementation in order to provide technical support and services quality control.Outcomes1. Six medical posts with appropriate medical equipment, furniture, essential medicines and personnel which provide primary health care and emergency medical care services, as well as OPD, PHC, MHPSS and SRH services at the EECPs.2. Six mobile emergency medical care teams / 6 emergency medical vehicles which provide mobile primary health care, emergency medical care, OPD, PHC, MHPSS, SRH services to the population living within the 5km zone from the contact line, especially in isolated and hard-to-reach settlements.3. Health referral procedures in the isolated and hard-to-reach settlements within the 5km zone from the contact line. 4. Identification of the needs for medicines and humanitarian aid of the population living within the 5km zone from the contact line and crossing the EECPs.5. Raising local people’s awareness of the sexual, reproductive and mental health issues, as well as of gender-based violence in the GCA.6. Generating of approximately 100 workplaces; increasing, as a consequence, the employment level of local medical and working personnel, reducing the outflow of qualified medical personnel from the conflict zone.
