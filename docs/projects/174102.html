Project Summary

The project contributes to the prevention of Severe Acute Malnutrition and provision of nutrition SBCC, in Yobe and Borno States, among children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) by ensuring adequate food intake, improved diet diversification, early detection of SAM and referral, improved Infant and Young Children Feeding Practices (IYCF).
In addition the project intends to contribute to the nutrition sector of HRP for Nigeria by:
- Strengthen the quality and scale of preventative nutrition services for most vulnerable groups through supplementary feeding activities, appropriate infant and young child feeding practices, micronutrient supplementation and optimal maternal nutrition.
- Improve access to quality curative nutrition services through the most appropriate modalities, systematic identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished cases in collaboration with the health sector to enhance sustainability.
- Reinforce appropriate coordination with other sectors and strengthen situation monitoring by undertaking joint assessments and analysis, while strengthening integrated response that mainstreams protection.
- Capacity building and training for MoH health workers in Yobe and Borno and community mobilizers in all the target areas.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition remained a public health concern in North-East Nigeria and a key priority for emergency response. Acute malnutrition is among the leading causes of death whether directly or indirectly for children under the age of five. According to the recent Nigeria situation report as of 09 December 2020, the nutrition situation in the BAY states significantly deteriorated in September with increased rates of acute malnutrition attributed to the reduced access to food during the on-going lean season (increased food prices, low harvest) and disease outbreak. Continued insecurity also generated disruption of health and nutrition services and new population displacements. According to HNO 2020, 1.5 million people, including over 1 million children under five out of which about 200,000 are living with a disability, and half a million women of reproductive age within the BAY states, need life-saving nutrition services. Of these, 15 percent are IDPs; 15% returnees and 70% are in host communities. Children who are severely malnourished are 12 times more likely to die (all-cause of mortality) compared to a well-nourished child. A non-breastfed infant under 6 months is 14 times more likely to die from pneumonia/diarrhea than an exclusively breastfed infant <6months. The nutrition situation in northeast Nigeria according to the HNO 2020 is projected to progressively deteriorate in 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic negative impact of the overall socio-economic situation and increasing insecurity. The situation may further deteriorate than projected if communities continue to avoid utilization of health and nutrition services in fear of contracting COVID-19 and a reduced number of health workers, especially in rural health facilities. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with GAM levels of 13.6% in Yobe, 10.7% in Borno and 7.5% in Adamawa. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. Based on the NFSS IX, approximately 809,814 children under five will suffer from acute malnutrition including 295,288 with SAM and 514,526 with MAM. Furthermore, based on the analysis conducted jointly with the Food Security Sector, factoring projected food security degradation, COVID-19 impact, and seasonality, it is expected that the number of children with acute malnutrition will progressively increase and peak in the 3rd quarter of 2021 (20% in quarter 1, 24% in quarter 2, 30% in quarter 3 and 26% in quarter 4. The number of children with severe acute malnutrition admitted into the treatment programs increased by 30% in September compared to August 2020, clearly indicating a seasonal period of a high prevalence of malnutrition lasting longer than in previous years. The nutrition sector partners admitted 28,723 severely malnourished children into the treatment programs across BAY states in September 2020 (approximately 70% of the annual target). Over 90% of all children in the outpatient therapeutic programmes and stabilisation centers (SCs) were successfully treated and discharged as cured. However, approximately 4% of the children admitted in the SCs died. The current nutrition situation in Yobe (Yobe State Humanitarian Situation Analysis As of 30 June 2020) is of great concern, with a sharp rise in acute malnutrition rates being reported across all LGAs which is consistent with the HRP 2021 as it shows that the prevalence of underweight children was highest in Yobe (28.6%) by state and Central Yobe (33.4%) by domain. Geidam, and Jakusko (Yobe) are among the identified LGAs that have the highest acute malnutrition rates.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The complexity of the current protracted humanitarian crisis has led the international community to realize that a silos approach is inefficient to respond to the needs of conflict-affected populations. As emphasized at the 2016 World Humanitarian Summit (WHS), it is of utmost urgency to mainstream humanitarian, development and peace realms into an integrated action capitalizing on their respective comparative advantages to successfully achieve the 2030 Agenda - Sustainable Development Goals. Indeed, there is worldwide growing recognition that there can be no peace without sustainable development and no sustainable development without peace. COOPI fully embraces this "New Way of Working" and, in alignment with the HDP Nexus, it is proposing a project with a cross-cutting and inter-sectoral perspective which synergically combines life-saving humanitarian assistance with long-term development activities and peace-building dialogue sessions at community level. This is based on the premise that to achieve long-lasting peaceful coexistence between IDPs, returnees and host communities, it is paramount to address immediate basic needs through a development lens which does not only ensure early recovery but aims to strengthen beneficiaries' resilience to conflict-driven and climate stressors in the long-run. As a proof, besides promoting the recovery of the main livelihoods and the rehabilitation of basic public infrastructures, COOPI simultaneously aims to provide skills-training for youth and capacity-building sessions for LGAs departments as well as to carry out peace-mitigation and conflict-management dialogue sessions at community level. By doing so, COOPI's goal complimentarily encompasses a strong contribution to social cohesion, economic development and self-reliant livelihoods through a localization approach and state system's strengthening: both of them ensuring the sustainability of the action and thus an enduring peace consolidation. To this purpose, COOPI relies on a broad variety of partners, widely ranging from LGAs departments and International Organizations to local associations and community leaders, all across the three interlinked sectors of humanitarian assistance, development and peace building.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Mainstream protection into humanitarian assistance programs Protection mainstreaming ensures that human rights and their promotion are central to each intervention, laying the foundations for reconstruction and a more equitable and sustainable development. In line with the international developments within the theoretical framework, COOPI also ensures the full participation and involvement of women, men, boys and girls. Protection is mainstreaming in the key component of the design and the implementation of each step of the project. COOPI will closely monitor the respect of its application. A strong sensitization will be carried out among the beneficiaries. They will be informed of their right, and how to report any breach. Redemptions will be conducted in locations that are within easy reach for the target beneficiaries and during day time to ensure that beneficiaries get to their homes before dark. The planning of project days and times will be done in a manner that will not interfere much with other community daily activities and programs targeted at children, pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls. Special considerations for pregnant and breast-feeding women and girls, children and people with specific needs will be made to ensure that priority is given to them during distributions. The staff on site will be recognizable by wearing visibility and reflective jackets. COOPI staff involved in the project will be trained and sensitized on the COOPI Code of Ethics and COOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to sign COOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after the completion of the training sessions. Beneficiaries and community leaders will be sensitized on PSEA and the COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition to reporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistleblower policy via email or helpline phone number. In each LGA Protection Officers and one CRM assistants will be available to gather and answer to the questions of the members of the communities. They will fill a complaint book that will record all the complaints. At every step of program, beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries will be reminded that they are free to use this platform to share information or complaints. A help and feedback desk and complaint boxes will also be set up at each distribution site for the people that would like to remain anonymous. In order to reduce inter-communal tensions and foster impartiality, COOPI will encourage WFP select beneficiaries according to vulnerability criteria only, not taking into consideration their status (Host communities and IDPs). Gender will be taken into account in the selection of the beneficiaries. However, the increased knowledge of the women regarding food security and nutrition will empower them and might foster a more important role in the decision making among the households. The Project Management Committees formed by COOPI in the previous phase of the project have a strong woman representation and they will continue to be involved in the monitoring of the project. Gender and protection will be taken into account in the entire project implementation. The strong presence of women in the project and their continuous protection will indulge COOPI to make sure the project respects the safety, the dignity and the integrity of the women, men, girls and boys receiving the assistance. COOPI monitoring team will ensure that the rights of all beneficiaries are fully respected and prevent gender oppressive stereotypes.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI is committed to ensuring that its program implementation process is accountable and transparent. One of the ways of achieving this is through the establishment of a feedback and complaint mechanism to record and act on expression of satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the quality of its services. The key objectives for COOPIs complaints mechanism will include: Recognition and protection of the beneficiaries’ rights; preventing and mitigating problems before they escalate; building and maintaining good relations and dialogue with the community; and to identify weaknesses and areas for improvement in the service delivery, general accountability toward donors and partners. The complaint mechanism will contribute towards creating a safer environment for the most vulnerable members of a community as well as increasing their voice during the implementations of the projects. COOPI shall identify moth appropriate channels (entry points) to receive feedback and complaints from the various stakeholders it works with: the beneficiary level; the village level; the wider administrative unit level (i.e. the District) level; the headquarter level; The key principles of the complaint mechanism include: Staff receiving complaints are trained on Complaint Handling System. Staff shall be prepared to listen, accept the complaint, be courteous, not interfering with the complaint description and committed to solving the complaint raised. Where possible, minor non sensitive complaints willbe solved immediately and reported in any case to the PM;. If a complaint is not immediately solvable it needs to be recorded on a standard Complaints Registration Form. Maintenance of database.
