1 - Needs

The basics WASH services remain high in North Darfur localities ( Ambaro, Kornoi, Al Tina and Kabkabyia) where the people in need are surfing from accessing the WASH facilities as significant number of people observed returning to their areas of origin putting extra pressure on the limited existing facilities. HDPO assessed the water situation in these localities and found that about most of the people depend on haffirs as drinking water while few available water sources are not functioning well, HDPO has connection with many village development committees from these localities with sort of community mobilization to contribute in any rehabilitation work starting in almost all the areas however these initiatives need real humanitarian support. Between 13 and 16 August 2020, OCHA led an interagency assessment team to Kebkabiya town in Kebkabiya where A total of 9,145 houses were reported to have completely been destroyed and 8,513 others were partially damaged with an estimated of 7,013 households’ latrines were stated to have collapsed. and from the field monitoring also found almost the open dug wells been affected by the follow and remain un accessible. Since last year, COVID19 surging with high rate of infections in Khartoum, Senar , Red Sea and ALGazera states. in July 2020, HDPO conducted a awareness messages quality assessment in Khartoum and AL Hesahesa localities where it is found that 78% people of in Alhasahisa reported that they did get a direct quality messages for any of the authorities, organization about the seriousness of COVID19 at the time the CIOVID was started where among other locations 47% at Umbada16, 69% at Umbada 14 and 58% at Umbada 13. The involvement of women's in WASH committees remains low while the CLTS approach has to be introduced in rural areas.The issue of hygiene kits for people with special needs and MHM kits for the adolescents and women in reproductive ages also curial and there is serious gap in these areas spec ally the rural ones.improvement of WASH in schools and other institutions remains needed and that was clear from the MoUs that HDPO has signed with ministry of health, WES, equation and protection in North Darfur.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Water supply: • Provision of spare parts, technical knowledge to conduct the quality operation and maintenance 23 water sources in Tina, Kapkapiya, Amboru and Kornoi localities with focus on community contribution and participation where women will be involved in WASH committee and decision-making process following the water safety process . Improve the drainage system to ensure the wells are protected enough from the any external contamination; this will be done thorough repair and maintenance of the platforms and soak way pits where possible.• Drilling of new bore holes to cover the needs in areas with no access to water following the WASH sector guidelines • Water quality monitoring and testing as per Sundanese water quality standards Sanitation and Waste Management: • Provision of materials and mobilize the communities in repair & maintenance including new 450 units of latrines and bathing facilities where communities will be trained to maintain the gender use of the facilities associated with hand washing practices and soap . • Waste segregation will be ensured from the source, i.e. from household, communal place and the marketplace through the hygiene promotion team and the required community engagement. Based on the experience of UNDP solid waste management project to collect the waste, segregate and provide household bins where communities will be engaged in operation and maintenance of these bins through assigning users’ groups connected to WASH committee, volunteers and WASH field team. Hygiene promotion with focus on AWD and COVID19 • Form WASH management committee develop a community engagement mechanism including participatory monitoring and feedback system ensuring sufficient representation of different population sub-groups including at least 50% of participants as females. Organize regular meeting and consultation with the committee members ensuring the users are empowered to report to HDPO staff members and volunteers about any issue of concern related to WASH facilities and services which will be timely and appropriately addressed by the BRAC field team.• Conduct focus group discussion session at camp, village or block level , in markets and open spaces, this will target the whole population over the project period• Bi- weekly clean up campaigns session in each block followed by the cleaning campaigns where total number of the participant will be clearly recorded.• Weekly Jerry can and water containers cleaning campaigns and conduct water dialogue during the campaigns.• Conduct MHM session through trained hygiene promotion officers/volunteers, the activity will include mapping the adult’s girls and women in reproductive age, they will be trained in MHM and specific kits will be distributed while the MHM session group will be facilitated at villages/camp and block levels.• Household visit using the BRAC household visit checklist and modules to measure the indicators with clear documentation of the records and messages delivered to the household members. • Post distribution survey will be conducted and reported while the finding will be shared with the communities and for both IOM and BRAC for lesson learning and adapting the distribution.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will give the high attention to the environmental impact while designing the intervention hence ensure the sustainability of ground and surface water supplies by conducting assessments, implementing water conservation measures and planning for the appropriate disposal of liquid waste, prevent over-pumping of groundwater aquifers, Conduct hydrological surveys and assessments of sustainable water yields before well drilling and carry out groundwater monitoring of wells to ensure natural recharge is not exceeded,Promote innovative approaches to water management such as rooftop rainwater harvesting.Undertake community consultation to understand cultural and traditional practices regarding local water use, including the needs of women and marginalized people,Set up appropriate waste management systems to deal with solid waste, wastewater, hazardous waste, human waste and animal feces in order to prevent severe damage to the environment and public health and Improve public awareness on effective water saving and household level water reuse techniques

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

TWhile constructing the facilities, Locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement with Installation of lights near communal facilities of to coordinate with NFI sector for the provision of solar lamps /torches for each household. The distribution and placing of facilities will not take place near military installations or threats such as attacks stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Introduce accompaniment arrangements to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls e.g. fetching water in groups. The distribution points will be set up in places Identify central locations for distribution points - beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.hygine kits distributions will be done tain daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. • Build separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females. Make sure they are clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and work with community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. For privacy, provide secondary enclosures around facilities or put locks on the doors to latrines and bathing houses. Discuss this with beneficiaries to consider their preferences. Raise platforms and ramps at water points to ensure safe manual lifting to all, especially children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities. Cater for the disposal of personal hygiene materials, in particular sanitary pads, nappies and adult diapers. • Consult both displaced and host communities about WASH needs and assess whether WASH interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. • Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in hygiene kits, feedback box at WASH facilities, phone lines.
