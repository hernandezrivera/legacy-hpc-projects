Project Summary

Providing Shelter and NFI emergency assistance for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community families in conﬂict-affected areas of North East Syria, reaching a total of 72,600 conflict-affected individuals. Through the provision of winterization and NFI assistance to IDPs in informal camps and settlements, delivering shelter support to recent returnees, supporting economic recovery and market systems initiatives in urban and rural settings.
NFI, Shelter and Winterization support will comprise three related but distinct components. First, supporting IDPs living in camps, informal settlements and collective centers to survive the harsh winter months with the provision of 4,000 kerosene heater kits with three months cash for fuel, supporting 24,000 individuals. Second, essential NFI in-kind kits and vouchers will also be distributed to the same population and to other new IDPs or returnees, targeting 48,000 individuals. Third, we will also undertake shelter activities to support recent returnees, particularly in Ar Raqqa, Tabqa and Manbij, living in unsafe and inadequate housing. This will be done through undertaking essential repairs and winterization of 700 partially-damaged homes as well as ensuring a minimum level of privacy, dignity and access to WASH facilities for each household. This will benefit an estimated 4,200 individuals.

4 - Needs

The 2020 HNO reports that around 5.65 million people are in need of shelter assistance and 3.42 million in need of NFI support in Syria, approximately 1.5 million located in NES in Hasekah, Ar Raqqa, Aleppo and Deir ez Zor governorates. With widespread displacement to Al-Hol camp, the number of IDPs living in camps has risen above 100,000, levels not seen in NES since 2017. At the same time, many more IDPs are scattered in urban and rural areas – in informal settlements, collective centers and in urban centers – across NES. There also remains a growing housing crisis in some of the largest urban centers in NES, particularly in Ar Raqqa, Tabqa and Manbij. A significant surge in returns to Ar Raqqa city over the past few years has led to a sharp increase in rental prices and a shortage of adequate shelter. Many new returnees are forced to occupy partially and severely damaged buildings, raising serious health and protection concerns. Given the high levels of vulnerability of many of these returnees, most are unable to afford the cost of even the most basic shelter rehabilitation works. These shelters are often unsealed and exposed to the elements making them difficult to insulate, and raising serious safety, protection and health concerns. According to the last REACH report (December 2019), shelter was reported as a priority need in 58% of communities hosting IDPs and 77% of communities with new IDP arrivals since October. In the largest urban centers in Ar Raqqa governorate (Raqqa and Tabqa cities) most returnees returned through 2018-19 to their home and previous community, however a large number cannot do so and instead seek shelter elsewhere, with many resorting to unfinished/damaged shelter. On the other side, long term IDPs coming from GoS areas or the areas of Turkish military operation between Tal Abiad and Ras al Ayn are in need of shelter assistance, unable to afford rent or small shelter rehabilitation and not being likely to return soon to their homes.

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with the Whole of Syria sector objectives, this project comprises three core activities. First, in line with Sector Objective 1 (Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support), this project will provide (Sector Activity 1.1) core NFI kits to 8,000 IDP, returnee and vulnerable host community households in need (48,000 individuals). This kit will be provided either in-kind or through a voucher modality. The second activity also under Objective 1 (Sector Activity 1.2), comprises seasonal support in the form of winterization kits (heaters, jerry can, fire extinguisher) and cash for heating fuel for three months. This support will be provided to 4,000 vulnerable households (24,000 individuals). Kits will be distributed in November and cash for fuel starting December.The third activity, which falls under Sector Objective 2 (Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure) entails rehabilitation of partially damaged and unﬁnished buildings (Sector activity 2.1) in urban areas. 700 vulnerable households will be supported through shelter rehabilitation (4,200 individuals). In collaboration bilaterally, and through the NFI/Shelter working group, with other humanitarian actors working in shelter rehabilitation, will develop a shared beneﬁciary selection process, HLP due diligence, MoUs and other best practices. In terms of beneﬁciary selection within these districts, house-by-house assessments will be conducted and referrals accepted. Each shelter targeted will be rehabilitated to meet basic SPHERE shelter standards and will be in accordance with the Shelter sector guidance on rehabilitation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The SNFI cluster Protection Risk Analysis identifies over 50 key protection risks specific to the sector. Based on this, and the NGO's own internal protection analysis, identifies the key risks as follows: inter-community tension as a result of inclusion/exclusion, unclear selection criteria or varied assistance packages by different actors; increased tension between host and IDP communities; and risks particularly associated with distributions.To mitigate the first risk, the NGO adopts SNFI cluster guidance in its vulnerability assessments and its assistance packages to ensure harmonization with other actors. Similarly, the NGO hires local staff to carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks and good community acceptance. This ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness of the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants.In terms of tension between host and IDP communities, the NGO conducts extensive consultations with both groups before commencing any activities. The NGO does not target on the basis of status, and thus supports IDP and host communities. In order to mitigate for protection risks for all beneﬁciaries during SNFI distributions, they will be carried out early in the day to ensure beneﬁciaries can come and go with daylight. Women and men have separate lines at distribution points that are staffed by male and female ﬁeld workers. In camp settings, multiple distribution points are established to help keep the number of people at one point to a minimum and to reduce waiting times.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RisksFurther escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation, with wide impact on access routes, borders and leading to increased conflict. -IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conﬂict areas-Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations Assumptions - This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. - The security situation will be stable enough to operate in the proposed target area, especially in Dier Ez Zour so that new offices can be established granting better access to this hard-to-reach area.- There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and shelter/NFI goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures • Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. Establish new offices as required.• Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. • Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to supported communities.
