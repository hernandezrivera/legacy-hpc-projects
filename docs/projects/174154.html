Project Summary

The project intends to provide condiments for project participants in Dikwa, Kala Balge, Karasuwa, Nguru and Yusufari LGAs of Borno and Yobe state. Based on experience gained with the continuous engagement with the community in participatory meetings and project implementation.
The project will also target project participants who will participate in some agricultural interventions to enable vulnerable households (IDPs, returnees, host community) build resilience and re-establishments of their livelihoods through:
• Poultry, fish farming and crop productions: CARE will target the elderly, child headed and female headed households with these interventions.
• Briquette and energy efficient stove production: CARE will target both girls, boys, men and women who have the responsibility of sourcing firewood for food preparation. Due to the ongoing insecurity, the communities have experienced challenges sourcing fire wood for cooking.
• Skills acquisitions training: CARE will identify project participants and empower them in capacity building in agriculture and resilience.

1 - Needs

The impact of several contributing factors such as hazards and vulnerabilities, food availability, food access, food utilization including water and stability was assessed on these outcomes variables. The results indicate that about 10 million (9.8 %) people of the analyzed population require urgent assistance in the current period (October to December 2020). During the projected period (June to August 2021), these figures are expected to increase to 13.8 (12.9%) million people unless resilience driven interventions and humanitarian assistance in conflict affected LGAs is sustained. (CH Analysis, October to December 2020) For the current period (October to December 2020), about 10 million people (9.8%) of the analysed population are in the critical phases (phase 3-5) of food and nutrition insecurity. Of these population, 689,128 (6.9%) are resident in completely inaccessible or inaccessible communities of partially accessible LGAs in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. During the projected period (June to August 2021), in the absence of intensified resilience-driven livelihood interventions and food aid support from the humanitarian actors; these figures are expected to increase to 13.8 million (12.9%) people across the 15 states and the FCT. Of the vulnerable population during the projected period, about 867,470 (6.25%) persons are located in inaccessible or partially accessible communities of Borno, Adamawa (Madagali) and Yobe (Geidam) States. (CH Analysis, October to December 2020)

2 - Activities

Food Assistance (Condiments) – CARE will identify 40,000 project participants in Dikwa, Kala Balge, Karasuwa, Nguru and Yusufari LGAs of Borno and Yobe state. (Dikwa – 5000, Kala Balge – 5,604, Karasuwa – 7,000, Nguru – 12,800, Yusufari – 9,596) SAFE energy – CARE will identify 4,700 project participants in Dikwa, Nguru and Yusufari LGAs of Borno and Yobe state. (Dikwa – 1500, Nguru – 2000, Yusufari – 1200). Participants will be provided with raw materials for energy stove production. Trainings on briquette and stove production will be conducted by CARE staff to the Participants across all locations. Participants will be grouped into 25 each (females – 20 & males – 5 per group). CARE staff will train the groups on general business management principles and provide them with the knowledge and skills on how to conduct and manage a business effectively putting into consideration the financial management skills and other soft skills. Agriculture resilience - CARE will identify 7,000 project participants in Dikwa, Karasuwa, Nguru and Yusufari LGAs of Borno and Yobe state. (Dikwa – 1000, Karasuwa – 1000, Nguru – 3500, Yusufari – 1500) Each participant will be supported with chickens, veterinary standard drugs and 10 bags of 25kg feeds to support start-up. Participants will be trained on best practices in poultry production and management. Participants will also be supported with poultry shelter.Participants will also be supported with agriculture inputs for crop production. Trainings will be conducted to participants on general crop production Livestock Management. Capacity building – CARE will identify 11,700 project participants in Dikwa, Kala Balge, Karasuwa, Nguru and Yusufari LGAs of Borno and Yobe state. (Dikwa – 2500, Karasuwa – 1,500, Nguru – 5,200, Yusufari – 2500). Participants will be trained in agriculture and resilience. They will also be provided with the knowledge and skills on how to conduct and manage a business effectively putting into consideration the financial management skills and other soft skills.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CARE will ensure that it is accountable to the affected populations through implementation and adoption of a range of strategies, approaches and processes in the designing, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project. These include the following: - Affected Persons will be involved throughout the project cycle where they will actively participate in all aspects from design phases.- Explaining the project activities, participants’/beneficiaries identification and selection criteria, ration entitlements and the right to provide complaints and receive feedback. - Facilitate the establishment of Project Management Committees (PMCs) who are representatives of the wide range of beneficiaries from the various communities and the PMCs will constitute at least 50% women while representing all categories of the beneficiaries (women, men, youth, people with special needs (eg disabled). - The inclusion of all categories of the affected populations (women, men, youth and people representing people with special needs, will ensure that the concerns of all people are addressed and information dissemination is done in a way that will ensure equal access to information.- Posters will be displayed at all accessible places for beneficiaries such as entrances and waiting areas of registration and distribution sites so that they are aware of their entitlements, criteria used among others and put in a manner that will enable their understanding such as use of symbols and pictures. - Protection cases and people with specific needs will be prioritized. Protection is mainstreamed in all CARE projects and interventions it will be mainstreamed in the proposed interventions at all the stages from design phase. CARE will also use the guidance on mainstreaming protection into all programming shared with all partners and a protection risk analysis will be promoted. CARE will ensure that services to be done in the communities will reduce waiting time, identified protection risks and propose remedies to mitigate such. CARE staff, community volunteers and PMCs will be trained on how to identify, report and refer protection-related concerns to appropriate actors for follow-up and feedback. CARE will strengthen its feedback and complaint mechanisms to receive continuous feedback from affected populations on the interventions. Protection cases and people with specific needs will be prioritized. CARE is well aware of the main protection risks prevailing in the targeted LGAs. CARE has a dedicated full time GBV and Protection Sector Manager responsible for ensuring Gender and GBV dimensions are mainstreamed into all of CARE’s operations. CARE’s Gender and Protection Sector Manager ensures that all projects include a thorough gender analysis and that analysis results are integrated into project M&E and Learning systems. All forms of protection will be monitored (especially sexual abuse) and suspect cases reported promptly. CARE will make sure that project sites and activities provide safe area for all participants. All staff involved in this project will be trained (for some refresher) on gender, protection, humanitarian principles and accountability including CARE’s Policy on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Child Protection. CARE is an active member of the Protection Working Group and the GBV sub-working group led by UNFPA.
