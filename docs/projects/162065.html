Project Summary

The project aims to improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary health services for refugees. CAID will continue to strengthen our provision of quality essential primary health care service delivery at health posts (in line with defined minimum package of primary health services) through capacity building, continuous quality monitoring, functional accountability mechanisms to the refugees, and supportive supervision. Privacy at the health posts will be maintained to ensure respectful care. CAID will prioritize utilization of essential health services, particularly for vulnerable groups and hard to reach areas, our community health workers will continue to engage at household level on health awareness and education. This will also help address health seeking behaviors related to home-based deliveries and seeking care from private drug shops, which is resulting in debt accumulation. Community health activities on non-communicable diseases will also be expanded and refugees’ capacity to build community resilience for health-related issues will be strengthened. Availability, accessibility, quality and continuity of care will be improved through capacity building initiatives, developing and distributing protocols, and procuring medical supplies, with a focus on non-communicable diseases, laboratory diagnostics, rational use of medicines, and mental health and psychosocial support services. Proper medical waste management and expired medicine disposal will be implemented. We will ensure continuous availability of sexual and reproductive health commodities and equipment with support from health sector, including family planning methods to meet contraceptive needs of refugees. Continue capacity building for healthcare providers on sexual and reproductive health, gender-based violence, adolescent health, newborn care. Will follow the standard referral protocol and pathway to minimise loss of life for emergency and specialized health care referrals. A robust referral mechanism will be implemented for both the camps and host community through trainings and knowledge exchange among and implementation of a functional hospital rotation plan. Maternal and neonatal death surveillance by the CHWs will be expanded to help identify and address avoidable mortality factors. Routine immunization activities and vaccination campaigns will be expanded to reach targeted coverage for children and pregnant women. WASH in health facilities will also be strengthened. Will continue DHIS2 and EWARS reporting regularly. For host community, the same minimum standard health service package will be provided including medical referrals, behavior change activities in the host community to address health seeking behaviours. In addition, we will participate in any emergency response(such as MMT) through our expertise and medical supply both in the camps ans host community.

Objectives

1. Improve equitable access to and utilisation of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psycho-social support; and non-communicable diseases. 2. Ensure the prevention and timely response to communicable disease risks including diseases with outbreak potential, and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone. 3. Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behaviour and utilisation of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender and age considerations and vulnerable groups.

Needs

Ensuring adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities is a continuous challenge, in part due to large case load in health facilities and high staff turnover.There are shortages in Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity, mental health and psychosocial support services (including inpatient/psychiatric care) and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs). An overall health care waste management solution is needed including safe disposal of expired medicines. Rational use of medicines to reduce risk for antimicrobial resistance is an emerging priority which requires engaging with private drug shops as well as health facilities in the camps. There are critical gaps in comprehensive emergency obstetric care, newborn care,family planning provision (particularly long acting methods), availability and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape/intimate partner violence services and first line support for gender-based violence survivors. In 2019, 70% of Primary Health Care facilities had no systems in place to inform community and stakeholders of the GBV health services available in the facilities and were not adequately trained to identify signs and symptoms of sexual/intimate partner violence. (GBV Service Quality assessment 2019). There is a need to better understand the reasons/social beliefs around decision making and engage men in health education Adolescent health has been identified as a priority need for 2020. Risks for communicable disease persist due to living conditions in the camps. Immunization coverage for Pentavalent 3 among children under 1 year old has improved from 67% at the start of 2019 to 78% by end of September 2019 but requires substantive improvements to achieve >95%. The refugee population remains vulnerable to weather-related emergencies which also impact disease trends. Referrals for emergency medical and obstetric cases, surgery, trauma and complications for NCD/chronic conditions remains challenging and costly. Many of the same needs are observed in the host community, particularly regarding healthcare waste management, rational use of medicine, facility-based deliveries, risk factors for communicable diseases and vulnerability to weather-related emergencies.

Activities or outputs

1. Outpatient consultation and treatment through 5 existing static medical centres.2. Patient treatment/consultations through 6 exixting satellite health posts for the most vulnerable and PWSN (Persons with Specific Needs) with restricted mobility.3. 15000 door-to-door awareness visits on SRH and family planning by trained health workers to couples.4. 7500 door-to-door surveillance and referral consultations by trained health workers.5. Health awareness and community mobilisation workshops with Rohingya community leaders for wider community health surveillance and referral support.6. Group counselling sessions on adolescent reproductive health rights.The existing health posts also require renovation and repair to recover from damage caused during monsoon and regular wear and tear.
