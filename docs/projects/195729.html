Project Summary

This project is designed to improve Syrian families living in poor livelihood conditions, focusing on those living in their damaged apartments facing harsh weather circumstances without any protection and privacy.
The rate of people in need in this sector increased an 8% from 2022- to 2023 and reached 5.5 million people who need shelter assistance. The areas in need are spread all over the country but mostly concentrated in Aleppo, and rural Damascus governorates, where approximately 2.5 million of PiN are living in conditions ranked between severity levels 3-4 (Shelter HNO 2023). In Rural Damascus areas such as Moaadamieh, Modira, Misraba, Zabadin and Arbin, there is a high need to rehabilitate damaged houses. The high apartment rental prices forced people to return to their original damaged apartments or to stay in them illegally.
In Deir Ezzor governorate, more than 135,000 people are in shelter need (Shelter HNO 2023). In addition, ADRA identifies a high need for the rehabilitation of damaged houses in the neighbourhoods of Al Omal (west and east) and Al Arfi, where there is an increased number of returning families because the directorates, other government bodies and INGOs are working to provide essential services (electricity, water, sanitation…). Most of the buildings are structurally safe.
In Aleppo, as per ADRA’s assessments, the needs in Southern Rural Aleppo areas (such as Al Hajeb and Khanaser) are amongst the highest, and also in Aleppo ( Al Shaara Karm Al Jabal and Al Sakhour) due to the increased number of returnees and lack of presence of humanitarian partners.
ADRA scoop of work will be under the following activities:
Activity 1.1.1.2: Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs. (550,000 USD)

Activity 2.1.1.1: Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished housing. (660,000 USD)
Activity 2.1.1.4: Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished building common areas. (30,000 USD)
In 2023 ADRA is planning to respond to 12350 individuals including (male, female, boys and girls) from the most vulnerable families (IDPs, Returnees and host communities) through several shelter activities such as the rehabilitation of damaged houses, Shelter kits distribution and Winterization vouchers distribution and supply and installation of HH level solar energy systems in the governorates of Deir Ezzor, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, and Homs. A total of 200 apartments will be targeted under the long-term shelter solutions.

3 - Needs

The war in Syria has lasted for over ten years. Despite estimates of the destruction and the damage are still far from precise, 5.7 million people will require shelter support. The 34% of the total population live in substandard (damaged and unfinished shelters, prefabricated units and tents) with nearly 85% stating an inability to meet their basic needs, leading to secondary displacement (HNO 2023). Almost 27% of the host community households are living in damaged and/ or inadequate shelters where repair/rehabilitation is needed alongside a multisectoral response to ensure access and availability to essential services. These tend to be heavily overcrowded with insufficient services.Families and friends host the majority of IDPs. However, this situation inevitably creates a severe strain as the hosting period becomes prolonged, leading to overcrowding, additional intra/inter-family tensions, and additional costs, pushing even more IDPs towards collective shelters or other emergency alternatives. IDPs and returnees cannot meet their basic needs, including shelter, due to already strained resources spent on food and clothes. The economic condition in Syria, including ﬂuctuation in the exchange rate, and the sanctions' impact in compound with COVID-19 and cholera recently also contributed to the put more negative impact on Syrian families who are not able to pay the high rental cost or provide essential shelter needs and forced to spend more money on their basic needs.

4 - Implementation of activities

ADRA Syria will identify the most vulnerable and in-need persons aiming to contribute to the SO1 target (9.1 million) and reach 1.9 million people at risk. The potential household list will be obtained from SARC. ADRA will do a comprehensive market assessment to identify vendors Vouchers will be distributed through multiple points, with easy access for all targeted groups. ADRA staff and volunteers will be available on the site to give the project information and receive feedback and. ADRA will be available at distribution points and vendor stores to ensure equal access for beneficiaries. The voucher value will be 115$ for one round. ADRA will coordinate with the relevant governorates, MoLAE and the local municipalities to obtain a technical assessment and implementation approvals for the shelter rehab project. The most vulnerable families will be targeted by conducting social assessments and following the selection criteria. The implementation process starts with selecting the potential areas and then running the social and technical evaluation. The BoQs will be prepared and shared with the relevant stakeholders. ADRA will conduct the tender process and select the most successful bidder based on the submitted samples, experience in similar projects, and technical and ﬁnancial offers. The MEAL team will assess the process of projects and the quality and satisfaction of beneficiaries. All the activities will be carried out according to the shelter sector guidelines.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA ensures mainstream protection and adherence to humanitarian standards of 'Do No Harm'. ADRA will work closely with local authorities and municipalities to explain the selection criteria for NFI and shelter projects and the criteria. Also, coordinate with other partners in order to avoid conflict on the community level between beneficiaries and other individuals. There is the risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members. Leaders of the community where ADRA will intervene will be involved, and the phone number where beneficiaries and other partners call will be shared to allow the raising of any complaints. ADRA will ensure each rehabilitated apartment has lockable doors and windows and can provide protection. The common areas in buildings will also be targeted to provide lighting. ADRA will ensure that male and female staff are available during the project's life. The MEAL team will conduct field visits and distribute the complaint cards with a hotline number allowing beneficiaries to raise complaints. All complaints will be followed up on sensitive issues and will be referred to the ADRA Protection policy. For the NFI project, There might be difficulty in accessing distributions for women, elderlies and people with disabilities. ADRA can provide seats for PWD and the elderly. Choosing the location to be close to the beneficiaries. And consulting the community on the location and time of distribution.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ADRA includes the PSEA activities like quality control, reporting, analysis, policy setting and overseeing and supporting the collective PSEA activities. To prevent sexual exploitation and abuse in all parts of the program operations, the ADRA code of conduct will be strictly followed. Support and training will be provided to our staff on PSEA. In addition, we strengthen safe PSEA procedures through complaint and feedback mechanisms and raise awareness on key PSEA messages. During the implementation of the project, any field visit will be conducted by females and male workers. Moreover, in the voucher’s distribution points ADRA staff will be present to monitor the process and ensuring the safety of the beneficiaries. In this way, ADRA guarantee that the implementation of the activity does not pose any risk of sexual abuse and exploitation for the beneficiaries of the project. In case of unclear selection criteria for beneficiaries among the partners and the possibility of duplication. ADRA will work with local GBV child protection to help ensure equitable access to services and ensure coordination with the NFI and relevant sectors to avoid any duplication.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. Inflation in the local market causes the withdrawal of the contractors from the projects during implementation. 2. COVID-19 pandemic and the preventative measures that can result in a delay in the implementation of the project. 3. Lack of fuel due to the imposed sanctions, causing the inability of the ADRA team to conduct field visits 4. Delay in obtaining necessary approvals and beneficiaries lists from the MoLAE, which may delay the implementation. 5. Delayed fund transfer resulting in delayed implementation.6. suppliers and vendors in rolling out voucher assistance Mitigation measures: 1. ADRA will try to shorten the procurement process duration to allow them to commit to their prices. 2. ADRA has a COVID-19 response policy that ensures the staff's protection during site visits. In case preventing measurements were taken, ADRA will consult with donors to explain the situation and find possible solutions. 3. ADRA will ensure to provide several cars to the project to use during the project cycle and may use taxis for field trips. 4. ADRA has a good relationship with MoLAE and the relevant governorates. ADRA will conduct regular visits to follow up on the approval process. 5. ADRA will provide more than one transfer pathway, will closely monitor the situation inside and outside Syria, and keep close coordination with donors.6. ADRA will keep monitoring of vendors and suppliers during the distribution through daily monitoring visit to the distribution locations.
