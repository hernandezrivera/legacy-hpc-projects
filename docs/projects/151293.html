Project Summary

In south west state generally and in Baidoa district in particular, the situation has been dire with fresh IDP influx continuing unabated .People have been displaced to Baidoa, not only due to drought triggered factors but also due to security issues as some areas with repeated fighting between Al shabab and Somali government forces have witnessed the recent population movement as people are fleeing from the recruitment campaigns of Al shabab targeting to children and adolescents of ages above nine year old. The newly displaced have joined existing settlements for internally displaced or set up new settlements. South West State is the most affected state with more than half (301,202) of the total number of IDPs attributable to drought and insecurity (548,573) moving internally or out of the state due to drought or insecurity. According to education needs assessment conducted by MoE at State level in April 2018, there are 84,753 out of school children living in Baidoa IDPs. Protection concerns over gender-based violence, recruitment and use of children by armed forced or armed groups, unaccompanied/separated children are all on the rise, in particular in areas such as Baidoa with high concentration of internally displaced people. Many children from vulnerable families cannot afford to pay school fees in schools in Baidoa town because they are economically affected by the severe drought. To address these issues BREC partnership with UNICEF reached out 13 IDP schools with 2357(1117 girls) vulnerable children with education in emergency response in 2017 and since this project has phased out in December 2017, BREC working with Community education committee continued to retain children in the schools but due to the long gap and the limited resources the drop out of students have reached 40% which is quite disappointing To address this problem, BREC is highly proposing the continuation of supporting this vulnerable children as this will encourage enrollment of 952 out of school children 40% girls and retention of 1265 drought affected children whom (637) are girls will decrease the problems of dropouts. Moreover, supporting 39 whom 13 are female teachers in the targeted 13 IDP schools with monthly incentive and training them on psychosocial support will encourage teachers and also will enable them to identify and support children in need of such support in school, children have access to psychosocial support. Therefore, BREC believes that teacher training mitigates the psychosocial impact of shocks and disasters to children by giving a sense of normalcy, stability, structure and hope for the future during a time of crisis and will carry out resilience building education interventions to ensure that all boys and girls in target locations have access to basic education while their families develop strategic coping mechanisms of dealing with disasters and shocks such as the current situations. This education program will provide safe clean drinking water, teacher incentive, and school feeding program, refresher training for the teachers on psycho social support and pedagogy, CEC refresher training, construction of 10 twin gender–segregated latrines as almost all of the latrines has been destroyed and children remain with no latrines, rehabilitation of 10 TLS and WASH facilities and to equip the TLS with school furniture to attract the disadvantaged children in order to maximize the impact of the intervention. If the drought continues and these children will continue missing support it will have a negative impact their future learning and they won’t comeback. Ensuring the continuity of education to children from these districts is an important protection measure to keep children way from risks. This education project aims to provide physical and psychosocial protection for the drought affected children. It is expected that children will be protected against all forms of harmful violations through provision of quality and free primary education. Providing life-saving assistance through the schools is a unique chance to both save the lives of these children and also ensure that they get the opportunity to continue their education in a safe environment.

Needs

According to education needs assessment conducted by MoE at State level in April 2018, there are 84,753 out of school children living in Baidoa IDPs. Protection concerns over gender-based violence, recruitment and use of children by armed forced or armed groups, unaccompanied/separated children are all on the rise, in particular in areas such as Baidoa with high concentration of internally displaced people.Many children from vulnerable families in Baidoa cannot afford to pay school fees in schools in Baidoa. To address these issues BREC partnership with UNICEF reached out 13 IDP schools in Baidoa with 2357(1117 girls) vulnerable children with education in emergency response in 2017 and since this project has phased out in December 2017, BREC working with Community education committee continued to retain children in the schools but due to the long gap and the limited resources the drop out of students have reached 40% which is quite disappointing. Some of the main education challenges in Baidoa IDPs are: lack of quality learning spaces with wash facilities, congestion at the existing TLS Classrooms, inadequate teaching and learning materials, lack of incentives for teachers, lack of qualified teachers, low community support for education. Teachers and CECs are not trained on identification, prevention and response of protection concerns such as sexual abuses, exploitation and violence. To address this problem, BREC is highly proposing the continuation of supporting this vulnerable children as this will encourage enrollment of 952 out of school children 40% girls and retention of 1405 drought affected children whom (637) are girls will decrease the problems of dropouts when meaningfully provided with food and water at school level. Moreover, supporting 39 whom 13 are female teachers in the targeted 13 IDP schools with monthly incentive and training. CEC training on advocacy and resource mobilization will also strengthen the sustainability of the community,

Activities

Outcome 1: At least 2357(1117 girls) IDPs out of school-aged children have access to quality and life-sustained education opportunities in protective safe environment. 1405 children( 637 girls) students from the most vulnerable IDP families currently enrolled in the 13 targeted schools will be retained. An additional 952 new students from the IDPs 40% girls will be enrolled to start learning.Output 1.1: Access to safe and protective learning environment is provided to 2357 children( 1117 girls) Crisis Affected Children, 39 teachers(13 female) and 91 CEC(39 female) members through provision of safe and clean drinking water, school feeding program, Construction/Rehabilitation of TLS with wash facilities. Activity 1.1.1: Refurbishment of existing 10 temporary learning spaces. Activity 1.1.2: Construction of 10 gender sensitive twine latrine in 5 of the 13 targeted IDP schools. Activity 1.1.3: Refurbishment of 16 existing gender sensitive twin latrines in 8 of the 13 targeted IDP schools. Activity 1.1.4: provision of school furniture to equip 20 existing TLS classrooms in order to attract the disadvantaged children. Activity 1.1.5: safe clean drinking water will be provided with the targeted 2357 children (1117 girls) for daily consumption for 6 months.Activity 1.1.6: provision of in kind grants to 2357 (1117 girls) in the targeted IDP schools.Output 1.2: At least 2357 displacement affected school-age children benefit from enhanced quality of education in safe and protective environments in Baidoa IDPs.Activity 1.2.1: 39 Teachers (13 female) will be supported with monthly incentive. Activity 1.2.2: 39 teachers (13 female) will be given 5 days capacity building on pedagogy principles and psychosocial support. Activity 1.2.3: 91 CEC members (39 female) will be trained for 5 days on school management and school development plans. Activity 1.2.4: Provision of basic teaching & learning materials for 13 targeted schools.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Ensuring the continuing of education to these children contributes to peace building and state rebuilding education system that has been distraught by conflicts and droughts. Education, as an immediate peace dividend, will support peace building and resilience of children by providing conflict sensitive education services that will facilitate the effective reintegration of IDPs. This will be done by addressing specific psychosocial and life-skill needs of IDP children to support their transition into schools and addressing social pressures unique to IDPs and host communities in Baidoa, which is experiencing high levels of IDPS. Additionally, critical support for children will be provided through school based approaches to support resilience against drought and famine by introducing school based in kind grants, water vouchers and other critical life-saving support for children and communities surrounding schools.To ensure sustainability program, BREC will train the CECs on advocacy and resource mobilization and also Community participation will be given a special attention to enable all members of the community and the larger society to actively contribute to and influence in the provision of free primary education.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Some of the displaced people from marginalized communities’ risks being excluded in the designing of the project by the IDP Camps leaders.Mitigation measure: To ensure accountability to all, Women men, boys and girls receiving this humanitarian assistance are the primary stakeholders of this emergency education response and will be given equal right to participate in the decisions that affect their lives, receive information they need and informed decision and provide suggestions and complain if they feel the help they receive is not adequate or has unwelcome consequences.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: Poverty, marginalization and deep rooted socio-culture values may put girls at disadvantage.Mitigation measure: To address this GBV and equity in education for girls is prioritized in this project by establishing gender sensitive wash facilities for girls which will also improve their hygiene and sanitation and protect against the spread of disease. This project will also ensures almost 50% of gender equality beneficiaries. additional to that teachers and CEC members will be trained on prevention and response of protection concerns such as sexual abuses, exploitation and violence

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Risk: Provision of this free primary education in the IDP Camps may raise conflict tensions between the host communities and the IDPs.Mitigation measure: To avoid community tension, the host communities will be provided with access to the project services in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic education services and facilities.
