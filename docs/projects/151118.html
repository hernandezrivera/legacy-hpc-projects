Project Summary

The project aims at addressing the most urgent winterization needs of the conflict affected populations residing in the 5 km zone near the Line of Contact in Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts (GCA) by providing winterization cash grants to 1000 households for the winter season of 2019-2020.
Currently ASB in consortium with Agency for Technical Cooperation and Development (ACTED) and Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU) implements a multisectoral humanitarian programme funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany. The current project (February 14, 2018 - June 13, 2019) is registered in the HRP-2018 within the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster (ASB as appealing agency) as well as in Shelter/NFI Cluster (where ACTED is appealing agency). Ongoing project provides cash and in-kind assistance to meet basic winterization, food and hygiene needs of the beneficiaries residing in the 5 km zone along the line of contact.
The follow-up project (starting tentatively in June 2019) will continue with provision of winterization cash grants in the target areas until December 2020.

Needs

As a result of the conflict in Eastern Ukraine, 3,4 million people in Ukraine need assistance (OCHA). According to the FSLC Humanitarian Needs Overview summary, as of 2018, there are 267,000 food insecure people in Luhansk and Donetsk GCA. The proportion of moderately or severely food insecure population is much higher in the localities close to the line of contact (15 %), with the majority of population in the GCA 5km zone (57%) being marginally food secure (FSLC HNO summary). These households become even more vulnerable during the winter months, due to increased share of expenditure on heating costs. According to OCHA, the number of households requiring winter assistance in winter 2018-19 is expected to be higher than during the previous winter season (Ukraine: Humanitarian Dashboard, January-June 2018). According to the latest assessments, total monthly income per household member does not reach the actual subsistence level, with the rural populations having the lowest income (REACH Trends Analysis, 2018). Therefore, increased heating costs during the winter months pose a serious challenge to vulnerable households, especially food insecure ones. As identified by REACH in the winter assessment, 11% urban and 37 % rural households within the 5-km zone along the line of contact in GCA experienced insufficient heating (Shelter/NFI Humanitarian Needs Overview summary). With the reduction of heating subsidies, heating costs have increased significantly (doubling in 2016 alone) in cities and towns connected to the central heating grid. Within the framework of the current project “Emergency Assistance to Conflict Affected Populations in Eastern Ukraine” (14 February 2018-13 June 2019), 3900 beneficiaries receive cash and in-kind assistance to cover their basic food and hygiene needs, whereas 1000 households (2000 beneficiaries) receive winterization cash grants. Considering vulnerable population’s limited ability to cope with the winter due to increased heating costs, the follow-up project will support target households with winterization assistance in late 2019. In particular, households who are connected to the central grid but cannot cover the heating expenses will be provided with winterization cash grants. Based on the primary data from the ongoing emergency assistance project implemented by ASB in consortium with ACTED and SSU in the 5-km zone (GCA), the population profile shows significant vulnerability: • Older persons represent 38 % of the caseload;• Women represent 63 % of the caseload; • Children represent 18% of the caseload; • People with disabilities represent 16% of the caseload.• The average household size is 1,9.By providing winterization cash grants, the proposed project will contribute to the Shelter/NFI Objective 1: “Address essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through monetised/in-kind assistance and contingency.”

Activities & outcomes

The consortium is present in the target locations of 5-kilometer zone along the contact line implementing its current emergency assistance project. The ACTED teams operate in Sloviansk, Sievierodonetsk and Mariupol, whereas ASB is present with its office in Sievierodonetsk, with frequent monitoring visits of staff to the 5-kilometer zone along the contact line (Donetsk and Luhansk GCA).Purpose: to meet winterization needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA).Output: 1000 households (2000 beneficiaries) receive winterization cash grants to cover heating costs during the winter season 2019-2020.Outcome: vulnerable 1000 households (2000 beneficiaries) have access to heating. Activity 1. Beneficiary identification The beneficiary assessment will be conducted in September-October 2019 to identify 1000 particularly vulnerable households (2,000 individuals) in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA) who will receive winterization cash grants in November 2019. Beneficiary selection will be undertaken in coordination with Shelter/NFI Cluster, local authorities and humanitarian agencies operating in the area. Local populations will be consulted in the various assessment phases. The consortium will ensure that beneficiaries meet vulnerability criteria identified by HRP 2019-2020 (i.e. older persons, people with disabilities, female-headed households, IDPs). Considering the significant strain on households’ incomes that heating costs represent, vulnerable categories of population tend to have less funds available for their food and hygiene needs during the winter months. Therefore, depending on the results of the beneficiary assessment, food insecure households from the beneficiaries’ list of 2019 may be partially included into the winterization cash programme. Currently, the emergency assistance project is being implemented in 25 localities along the line of contact (Donetsk and Luhansk GCA), with the localities subject to adjustment if needed after the next round of assessment. Activity 2. Distribution of winterization cash grantsFor the winter season 2019-2020, the Consortium will follow the revised recommendation of the Shelter/NFI Cluster (as per 2018-2019 Winterization Guidance). 1000 vulnerable households in the localities connected to the central grid will be provided with winterization cash grants to cover the cost of utilities for the period of 6 months (680 USD per household). If the Cluster revises its recommendation in 2019 again, Consortium partners may reduce total number of beneficiaries or duration of assistance (e.g. covering 3 instead of 6 months) to meet the Cluster requirements.Cash is the most appropriate aid modality to address the winterization needs of the households residing in the houses connected to the central grid. Financial service providers (FSPs) include Oshchadbank and UkrPoshta’s cash home delivery system. According to the Humanitarian Trend Analysis conducted by REACH in summer 2018, for the population in the 5km zone near the line of contact (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts), the most preferred modes of delivery for cash assistance are bank account (48 %) and post office (32 %). Thus, the modes of cash disbursement used by the consortium in the current project are designed in line with the needs and preferences of the affected populations. Post Distribution Monitoring will be undertaken to measure the utilization of the aid provided. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age and gender disaggregation.
