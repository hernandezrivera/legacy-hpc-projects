Project Summary

This project is proposed in the context of the Food Security Cluster Response Plan for South Sudan for 2021. The project will contribute to FSL objectives (CO1) “provide emergency food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption” and CO 2 “enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihoods and fishing and livestock supports”, directly linked to Strategic Response Plan (SRP) objective “reducing mortality and morbidity, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents of the most vulnerable populations.
The project aims to address the immediate food and livelihood needs of people facing severe food insecurity in 9 Counties across four States of South Sudan. In 2021, the project will target 466,524 people expected to face severe food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) in the four States specifically 208,878 people in Joinglei State, 106,452 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, 83,850 in Unity, and 67,344 people in Warrap State. Project participants will be selected through a rigorous community-based targeting process with due emphasis to vulnerable groups such as female and child headed households, disabled, the elderly and IDPs.
To achieve its objective, the project will advance two major interventions which reinforce one another. These include the direct delivery of food assistance to affected people complemented by interventions targeted at restoring local food production through the provision of key emergency livelihood inputs and resilience building measures. By transferring food assistance to vulnerable people, the project fills immediate consumption gaps and address ongoing hunger. This action will stabilize households in the short run by safeguarding livelihoods and remaining assets and set more resilient foundation on which households can build on and engage in more productive livelihood activities. Building on the consumption stability achieved through food assistance, the project extends appropriate emergency livelihood inputs and skills as well as support community participation in asset creation actions which will lead to recovery of local food production and the development of durable and productive assets which in turn enhance food security, restore livelihoods, and build resilience to future shocks and stressors.
Specific interventions include: a) General food distribution and cash-for Assets which are meant to ensure access to adequate and nutritious food to address ongoing hunger. As much as possible and feasible, these assistances will be used to encourage targeted community members to engage in complementary asset creation activities such as restoration of crop lands which will in turn improve farmers’ productivity and resilience to shocks; b) providing emergency livelihood assistance including the distribution crop and vegetable seeds, agricultural tools and fishing kits coupled with skill enhancement measures to stimulate local food production. Vegetable demo plots and kitchen gardens will be promoted to showcase best practices for improving production and strengthen linkage with nutrition measures. Especially, Kitchen gardens will be promoted linked with nutrition centers and serve as demonstration for simple garden practices at household level targeting families with malnourished children. The adoption of better farming and fishing methods will go a long way in improving food self-sufficiency and reduced dependency on food assistance in the long run.
The project will set up mechanisms through which target community members can voice their needs and preferences and demand inclusive processes. The project will also integrate COVID-19 prevention activities throughout the project implementation and COVID-19 risk communication will be an integral part of the project’s engagement with the target community.

1 - Needs

According to the latest IPC findings released on 18 December 2020, severe hunger is projected to persist in South Sudan in 2021. The analysis projected that between April and July 2021, an estimated 7.24 Million people or 60 percent of the country’s population will experience severe acute food insecurity or worse (IPC Phase 3 or worse), with 31,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). During this period, an estimated 2.47 million are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The IPC finding further revealed that acute malnutrition will remain a major public health emergency in several parts of the country with an estimated 1.4 million children under age 5 expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021, which is the highest number in three years. The ongoing food crisis is the result of a convergence of multiple shocks, which have exhausted the emergency coping capacity of most of rural households and urban poor families, leading to crisis (IPC Phase 3) and worse outcomes. These include unprecedented levels of inter-communal conflict, the macroeconomic crisis, recurrent flooding, and the impact of COVID-19 pandemic are leading (FEWS NET-August 2020). An estimated 856,000 individuals have been affected by flooding since July 2020 across the country with some 400,000 displaced (South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot (OCHA) Oct 2020). The impact of consecutive flooding and associated crop loss is feared to result in lean season earlier than expected and without external assistance, thousands of these families may spiral into more severe acute food insecurity with little or no means to cope. Four of the States targeted by this project are projected to have the highest number of people facing crisis or worse food acute food insecurity in the country. Specifically, Jonglei State with 84.7% of its population, Northern Bahr el Ghazal 69.7%, Unity 68.7% and Warrap 61.4% its population. Further, two of the targeted counties for this project- Pibor and Aweil South are projected to face catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. The project will target 9 counties all facing severe acute food insecurity namely Pibor, Bor South and Twic East counties from Jonglei State; Aweil Center and Aweil South Counties from NBEG; Guit and Rubkona from Unity state; Gogrial West and Twic from Warrap state. Due to the multiple impact of shocks, a significant number of the farming families in the proposed counties missed the previous year’s agricultural season, remaining without access to food and livelihoods opportunities including planting, harvesting and income to meet immediate household needs. This is critical as most of communities in the targeted counties rely on crop cultivation and livestock as their principal livelihood activities. Crop productivity remains low due to limited size of cultivated land associated with labour shortage, unreliable rainfall and limited availability and access to agricultural inputs including seeds and tools (FEWS NET LZ 2018). While fishing is a common coping strategy among the targeted communities, its potential is underutilized due to limited access to appropriate fishing tools and production and preservation methods. The poor road conditions and complete inaccessibility during the rainy season in many of the targeted counties means there is a paralysis in market routes, leaving many of the people vulnerable to market shocks. Therefore, efforts targeted at stimulating local food production by capitalizing on the existing livelihood opportunities through improving access to inputs and skills will go a long way in improving household food security and enhance resilience of affected communities in the long run.

3 - Project indicators

The project will use a variety of tailored qualitative and quantitative tools and processes within its M&E system to both assess progress and to support learning. Routine project monitoring generates learning on an ongoing basis and identifies issues to be addressed in depth during assessment. Various monitoring tools will be utilized including; activity delivery monitoring, spot checks, beneficiary satisfaction, compliance monitoring and financial monitoring. The project will also conduct assessments using standard M&E tools including FGDs, key informant Interviews, support supervision assessments, site visitations, post distribution monitoring (PDM) and accountability to affected population modalities. The Project Log- frame and Work plan will form the base for monitoring and evaluation actions and will be used to guide the project implementation. The selected indicators and targets will be subjected to regular monitoring and evaluation to track progress in implementation and to measure achievement of outputs and outcomes. The following indicators will be monitored to measure progress and achievement in the implementation of this project: • Number of beneficiaries receiving food and cash transfers • Quantity of food assistance distributed (tonnage/ total cash value)• Quantity of livelihood kits delivered• No less than 50% women committee members, in roles mirroring that of men;• Number of Final Distribution Points with functioning Complaint feedback mechanisms (CFM). • Level of beneficiary satisfaction with information, participation and CFM; • Number of Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) undertaken; • Number of people participated in asset development• Size of feddan of land opened/cultivated• Types and number of community assets created• Number of people/HHs attending sensitization session (WASH/Nutrition/Protection/COVID-19)• Number of people trained on good agricultural and fishing practices

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will use various channels to inform the affected population. The project will organize wider community sensitization events before the start of the implementation to inform the target communities about the objective of the project, the specific activities involved and other support services available. The project will assign sufficient field monitors and closely work with local institutions such as Project management Committees (PMCs) to reach the community on regular intervals with key messages and information including on targeting criteria, entitlements, distribution place and times, the importance of complaint mechanisms including protection against sexual implementation and the channels available for reporting any abuses related to this matter. The project will take advantage of all community mobilization platforms to sensitize the community including during targeting, training session, participation for asset creation and during distribution periods. Printed materials such as posters and banners will be used to disseminate information on entitlements and other key messages on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse in a manner that are appropriate and understandable to specific community. Where opportunities exist, the project will use local media channels in particular radio to broadcast about the project and its strategy regarding PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will strictly ensure all vulnerable groups including people with disabilities women and girls are fairly represented in all community mobilization events and local targeting committees related to the project. This is done to help most vulnerable people have influence over the local decision-making processes and ensure their perspective and needs are properly considered in the project’s implementation. The project will ensure all information communicated to the target community is appropriate and tailored to local specific circumstances. The information will be delivered in local languages and will be culturally and gender sensitive. Printed information will take into account the high level of illiteracy rate and will be presented in pictorial forms.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Because of information gaps, fear and lack of understanding of the procedures for complaints, a significant number of the beneficiaries may not use complaint feedback mechanisms. To mitigate such a highly likely risk, the project will take the following actions: a) conduct proper sensitization to all beneficiaries to ensure they are aware of the availability of complaint and feedback mechanisms, where it will be located and what kinds of complaints can be reported; b) establish safe and secure feedback mechanism that encourages beneficiaries to feel free to express their views; c) put in place clear and simple complaint and feedback mechanisms so as to make it easier to beneficiaries to give feedback; d) ensure beneficiaries get response to their complaints on timely basis so that they have trust in the system and continue to use the platform in the long run.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will careful review feedbacks collected from beneficiaries for course correction. Depending on their nature, the feedbacks will be categorized into non-sensitive feedback or complaint and sensitive complaints. The Non-sensitive feedbacks commonly concern with implementation of activities/ dissatisfaction with activities. Such Programmatic complaints/ feedbacks are commonly collected through the feedback desks and are analyzed by a team comprising of M and E, technical officers, and project manager and immediate actions are taken in the consecutive activities for course correction and future programmatic consideration. Beneficiary feedbacks or complaints on fraud and corruption, abusive behavior and sexual exploitation and abuse are considered sensitive cases and handled at country office level and are reviewed through the higher management organ.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services To mitigate risks from violence, the project will: a) conduct specific risk analysis regarding security situation of the targeted areas before deciding on the specific location and period of key project’s activities; b) Set up registration/ distribution sites closer to settlement areas so as to reduce distance travel and exposure to the risk; c) schedule registration/distribution early enough in the day so as to avoid traveling at nightfall and; d) coordinate with local protection partners and UN system to obtain regular updated information on security situation and forage protection supports when necessary. 8.2. Risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. To mitigate the risk of Sexual exploitation and abuse, the project will: a) provide regular training to JAM field staff on PSEA to enhance their awareness on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse so as to protect risk of being a perpetrator; b) conduct specific risk analysis to identify factors that expose women and girls to sexual violence and take the necessary protection measures. For instance, distribution sites will be set up closer to settlement areas to reduce distance travel and exposure to the risk. Distribution will be scheduled early enough in the day and priority will be given to vulnerable groups such as women and girls so as to avoid traveling at nightfall which may expose them to SEA; c) put in place complaint and feedback mechanisms- help desk, at distribution sites to provide safe and secure avenue for women and girls to encourage them to report on any act of abuse and seek for assistances. 8.5. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities To mitigate risk of not participating marginalized groups in consultation and decision making process, the project will strictly ensure all vulnerable groups including people with disability and women are properly represented in key local community processes and decision making structures. Particular attention will be given to CBPP local planning exercise, Project Management Committees and Boma targeting committees. Ensuring gender balance and equal opportunities for marginalized groups such key local community institutions will empower them to influence local decision making processes related to the project’s resource allocation. 12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project will mainstream protection concerns including GBV through all FSL planned activities. The project considers protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse at all level to address both risk of being perpetrator or victim. At organizational level, sexual exploitation and sexual abuse constitute acts of serious misconduct and therefore are ground for major disciplinary measures. The project provides regular training to its staff to ensure they acquire the required knowledge on protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) both to discipline their action and provide better support to the community.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The proposed project seeks to provide immediate food and livelihood assistance to affected population to save lives and restore livelihoods while helping households to develop resilience to future shocks. This action directly aligns with FSL cluster objectives 1 (CO1) “provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3). The proposed intervention for livelihood assistance through the distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits will further contribute to the FSL cluster objective 2 (CO2) “enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary crop, vegetable and fishing inputs provision which aims to increase production and productivity of the agriculture sector. Hence, the project will have significant contribution to decreasing the number of people facing IPC 3,4 and 5

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Partnership will be strengthened with other development actors to ensure targeted communities accessed additional cross-sectoral services and improve impacts from the proposed intervention. The project will conduct stakeholders mapping to identify and link with other actors in the area of Nutrition, WASH, Health, Shelter and Protection activities. The project will directly link with JAM’s own nutrition program (OTP) to ensure its beneficiary access equitable nutrition services. The project will capitalize on the recent JAM’s experience in “WASH Voucher” pilot project initiated with UNICEF in Aweil South to improve access to WASH services. Further, the project will introduce complementary programming approach, using the opportunity to interact with target groups beyond the activity of food and livelihood transfers. The project will take advantage of all community mobilization platforms to disseminate key messages on cross-sectoral basic services including on nutrition, WASH, health, Shelter and protection (e.g during targeting, CBPP, participation in asset creation and during food distribution).

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project action through distribution of food assistance will directly contribute to availing nutritious food to affected people and hence contribute to decrease prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under 5. The distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits through the project further ensures the production of diversified foods by targeted households which in turn increases households’ access to diversified diets. Kitchen gardens will be promoted linked with nutrition centers and serve as demonstration for simple garden practices at household level targeting families with malnourished children for complementing households with vegetable products. The adoption of better farming and fishing methods will go a long way in improving food self-sufficiency, access to nutritious diet and reduce the prevalence of acute malnutrition under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4.
