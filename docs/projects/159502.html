Project Summary

This project is designed to respond to the existing food security needs in Somalia, and prevent further deterioration of the food security situation of the effected population of south central through timely and appropriate assistance to affected households that lack access to food and other household needs. This will be done through the provision of lifesaving food assistance to restore acceptable household food consumption and complementing the household income of the affected vulnerable people (pastoral and agro-pastoral) through cash for work intervention further improving their capabilities in accessing food.
The selected responses are designed to provide lifesaving assistance to effected people enabling the most urgent scale up before the next rain season. Both the food transfer and the cash for work intervention will allow pastoralists, farmers and agro-pastoralist to free up some of their meager income and establish a quick economical boost which acts as a supplement to their household income helping them to afford culturally appropriate food and establish an income for further investment in their livelihoods while reducing the level of vulnerability. The planned humanitarian interventions are designed to meet the immediate needs and build resilience: provide food vouchers, conditional and unconditional cash transfers to host communities and vulnerable IDPs respectively in the Agro pastoral, pastoral and riverine livelihoods. Households will be provided with agricultural inputs before Deyr and GU rains and trained on improved farming techniques for improved production. Household silos provision and training on proper food storage will be done before harvesting to reduce post-harvest losses. The area lacks proper infrastructures due to poor maintenance of canals and river embankments. Reflection workshop with irrigation committees will thus be conducted to work out modalities of restoration and maintenance of these infrastructures. Wocca will engage the communities in cash for work activities, there will be Creation of temporary jobs to men and women just before the rain seasons through cash for work activities, providing 5USD daily rate to the laborers which will increase their income. The infrastructures will contribute to provision of water and also facilitate transaction of businesses through feeder roads. There will be Provision of vocational training especially to the unemployed youth and provision of materials to start up a business of their own (market surveys will be conducted to identify the most profitable business ventures and training provided based on these findings). During mobilization, other protection services of Wocca will be mentioned as well in order to create outreach to women, girls but also others and also an accountability to affected population framework will be put in place. Authorities, village elders and committees will be engaged in the monitoring of the food transfer activity while a fingerprint and verification exercises (vouchers) are taken with pictures from gps camera and videos. The implementation, Monitoring & Evaluation of cash for work will based on the following criteria: initial data collection and survey oriented to assess the number of people at work on daily basis with signatures collected at the end of the work day. Vouchers will be developed redeemable through the Hawala company for transparency. Identification, verification of the vouchers versus the list of beneficiaries will take place during disbursement of funds to beneficiaries. At least 50% of the target beneficiaries physically verified against agreed target criteria

Project Activities

- Facilitate food access for communities in south central region through Provision of food vouchers (Maize, Sugar, Oil and pulses) to 1800hh ( 10800 beneficiaries) (806 men headed households and 994 women headed households), for 6 months for communities in south central region. - Increase minimum household basket for communities in crisis targeting 800hh(352 men headed households and 448 women households) who are extremely vulnerable through cash relief worth 150 USD for 4 Months per year. - Enhanced food production and assets through Provision of seeds (maize seeds, sorghum seeds, cow peas, mung beans, onion, tomatoes, lettuce) farm tools and land preparation to 3150 hh (1390 men headed households and 1760 women headed households); - Provision of household metal silos to 53 men headed households and 68 women headed households and training on food storage and the importance of using household metal silos other than the traditional food storage and thus reducing post harvest losses - Provision of 4 hours Tillage support for land preparation to 100 destitute poor farmers/IDPs and marginalized groups - Increased livelihoods of the community by development of community infrastructures through cash for work - Provision of vocational training to 45 (20 young men and 25 young women) individuals (unemployed youth in urban area) and provide them with materials to start up a business of their own. - Improve protection against floods and lesser risk by building stronger river embankments through cash for work.

Mitigation Measures

To ensure that members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are included in consultations and/or are not excluded from humanitarian services/assistance and that Women, girls and boys are included in consultations and decision-making processes, Wocca will employ staff representative of all groups in the community by gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group and disability. Gender balance of distribution teams and implementation committees will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. During distributions, we will ensure that the mode and frequency of distribution minimizes safety risk putting in mind that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking rations home. Distributions will take place giving sufficient time to travel home before nightfall while also ensuring that the location, distance and routes to the distribution points is accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable. Separate male and female queues will be designated with female staff present at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls. special needs of pregnant and lactating women, under 5 year olds and older persons will be responded to and the quality of their assistance made equitable to that of other groups. ration cards to female-headed households, child-headed households and unaccompanied children will be issued in their own name. Livelihoods programmes will be developed in a way that protects and supports household caring responsibilities and promotes family unity. They will be developed in a manner that ensures that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities (including cash/food-for-work schemes), micro-credit, cash transfers, agricultural inputs and skills training and that they receive the same benefits for their input and work. Clear information will be provided to the affected population explaining that they do not have to pay or provide favors to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance or being selected for livelihood projects. Deliberate efforts will be made to ensure that this information is communicated to women and girls. To ensure that delivery of humanitarian assistance does not affect peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources and Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance does not occur Wocca will promote equitable and impartial access to all; we will Consult both displaced and host communities about food security and livelihoods needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities and make decisions. We will apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries to ensure the displaced as well as the mobile populations are not excluded, lack of documentation will not necessarily exclude individuals from accessing services Wocca will employ staff who are representative of all clans and groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and ensure all staff and members receive protection mainstreaming training, they will be sensitized sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. This will ensure that our staff play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voices of marginalized groups are represented. To further avoid community tensions between displaced and host communities we will provide the local/host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of food, assets and inputs. We will implement livelihoods components in a way that protects and supports household caring responsibilities.

The planned humanitarian interventions are designed to meet not only the immediate needs but also to build resilience: Households will be provided with agricultural inputs before Deyr and GU rains and trained on improved farming techniques for improved production. Household metal silos provision and training on proper food storage will be done before harvesting to reduce post-harvest losses; it is anticipated that the households will not only preserve food for the future consumption but will also preserver seed for planting during successive rain seasons. The area lacks proper infrastructures due to poor maintenance of canals and river embankments. Reflection workshop with the government and irrigation committees will be conducted to work out modalities of restoration and maintenance of these infrastructures. The infrastructures will contribute to provision of water domestic use and irrigation purposes and also facilitate transaction of businesses through feeder roads. There will be Provision of vocational training especially to the unemployed youth and provision of materials to start up a business of their own (market surveys will be conducted to identify the most profitable business ventures and training provided based on these findings). This activity will make the youth self-dependent and less vulnerable for recruitment into militia groups.
