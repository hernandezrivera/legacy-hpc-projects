Project Activities

- Ensuring the HCT Centrality of Protection strategy drafted under the leadership of UNHCR is kept at the heart of all interventions and also advocated through the UNHCR lead/co-lead clusters, to mainstream the priorities of the strategy into all activities. - Supporting implementation of the National Action Plan and the revised National Development Plan, UNHCR will support the implementation of durable solutions initiatives and advocate for and support the mainstreaming of solutions considerations throughout all humanitarian programmes. UNHCR will support the (re)integration of IDPs and returnees, through an inter-agency approach including the implementation of joint programmes. UNHCR’s focus will remain on protection, including community cohesion and HLP. - UNHCR will continue to lead the Protection and Returns Monitoring Network (PRMN), reporting monthly on total displacements and in near real-time to clusters on significant new displacements (including evictions) and rights violations among IDPs, and increase the use of PRMN data, including through improved collaboration with other reporting mechanisms. For protection violations identified, PRMN partners also provide response or referrals as appropriate. UNHCR will also continue supporting the Protection Cluster’s Somalia Protection Monitoring System and conduct and contribute to other forms of protection monitoring and assessments for other clusters. UNHCR will carry out comprehensive return monitoring of refugee returnees and share findings with relevant stakeholders to inform programming and advocacy. - Strengthening Community based activities, including through the construction and running of community centres that will benefit IDPs, returnees as well as the host community, and through carrying out participatory assessments and furthering other means of participation among displaced communities. - As co-lead of the HCT PSEA Task Force, lead inter-agency efforts to ensure partners and community leadership structures are aware of their obligations and means to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse. Communities will be sensitized on complaint mechanisms to ensure high standards of accountability. UNHCR will also provide psychosocial, legal, and medical assistance and provision of dignity kits to SGBV survivors. Capacity building and awareness raising sessions will take place for government agencies, partners and the community on SGBV prevention and response. -Support will also be provided to IDPs and to local government on land tenure and documentation.

Mitigation Measures

The risk of exclusion is significant in IDP settlements. There is a low level of understanding and acceptance of the rights of refugee returnees and IDPs both within the host community and with authorities, as well as prevailing misconceptions of respective roles and responsibilities. Likewise, discrimination against minority clan members is another protection challenge, with IDPs and returnees considered to be foreigners and a burden on local resources. There is lack of mutual trust, respect and recognition for the need of social cohesion, inclusion and integration between IDP, returnee and host communities. Most IDP settlements are manned by gatekeepers that often act as informal managers. In the absence of the government authorities providing IDPs and returnees with assistance and protection, informal managers have often become the primary go to person and provider of support and, to a certain extent, protection. This dependency can lead to exclusion from services that are provided. Returnees are faced with similar risks of exclusion after absence from the country to prolonged periods. UNHCR’s protection activities aim to counter exclusion risks as part of the Centrality of Protection IDPs and returnees continue to face a myriad of protection risks, including lack of access to basic services, risk of exploitation and abuse including at the hands of so-called ‘gatekeepers’ and social exclusions. A significant proportion of IDPs are members of minority groups lacking the protection of major clans. GBV remains a pervasive issue, with domestic violence the main form of GBV in IDP sites, though sexual violence and harassment occur when girls and women leave settlements to collect firewood, feed livestock or on the way to and from school, and girls working as house maids report sexual exploitation and abuse. Fears of reprisal lack of confidentiality and a weak police and court system results in a majority of GBV cases going unreported. Young girls and single women are at the greatest risk of GBV, especially in temporary sites of newly displaced population that lack adequate WASH services or community lighting. UNHCR is engaging with communities to better understand protection challenges and to strengthen the community’s “protection networks” so that they can respond to protection problems and facilitate access to institutions and available services. Building trust with the local authorities, service providers and the international community will require continuous investment, both in areas of displacement and in return areas. The participation of the displaced population needs to be ensured through the establishment of committees coupled with the presence of community mobilisers, including for the provision of information on essential services, available assistance and returning process. Participation of women in community-based committees is rare and needs to be reinforced to guarantee a truly participatory approach and give voice to the whole community. UNHCR will further strengthen community structures and engage with all persons of concern through age and gender sensitive approaches.Displaced women, men, girls and boys remain victims of various protection incidents and other human rights violations. Violence is occurring in places of refuge and in areas which are controlled by non-state actors and which are not accessible by humanitarian actors. Continuous displacement and lack of livelihoods has also reduced the capacities of these uprooted communities to access humanitarian and medical aid. UNHCR will continue to monitor closely protection incidents occurring within the context of forced displacement. By advocating and putting in place response systems, protection incidents should get referral or direct assistance. Additionally, the information gathered through a thorough monitoring system feeds into the wider system to better analyze main protection incident in the country and better tailor programming to needs.

Please provide further explanation

UNHCR will work with government and other partners to bring displacement on to the development agenda and early solutions planning on to the humanitarian agenda, and support durable solutions through local integration or, where feasible, return or settlement elsewhere. Overall, engagement will be guided by a focus on inclusion, national capacity building, and sustainable, long-term and multi-partner planning; and principles and objectives of the CRRF, and other applicable frameworks such as the NDP, the National Action Plan and National IDP Policy, and the National Durable Solutions Strategy under development. UNHCR will pursue an integrated approach that aims at mainstreaming protection and solutions considerations into all engagement, underpinned by a strengthened evidence base through improvements in Information Management initiatives. The most vulnerable IDPs will be identified through collaboration with IDP committees and provided with access to shelters and support for sustainable livelihoods and basic services. Market surveys will be conducted to determine the sustainability and viability of livelihood opportunities. UNHCR will advocate for the availability of permanent land in locations where integration with host communities and access to basic services will be improved. To ensure that IDPs are aware of their housing, land and property (HLP) rights, UNHCR will undertake awareness raising on relevant HLP laws and advocacy with government for non-discriminatory application of HLP laws in coordination with partners/clusters. Inclusion of IDPs in the planning process will be crucial for planning peaceful coexistence activities and achieving implementation that enhances integration. Awareness raising amongst host communities and IDPs and returnees will take place to promote peaceful coexistence, in coordination with peaceful coexistence activities.The potential for local integration will be realized through the inclusion of IDPs and returnees in local level development plans, prioritization of civil documentation, by way of advocacy to the government, and the implementation of the area specific strategic plans.
