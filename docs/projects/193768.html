Project Summary

UNMAS’ Programme Strategy consists of two outcomes: Outcome 1. The United Nations, and its humanitarian partners in Gaza, maintain critical functions, uninhibited by explosive hazards. UNMAS is the only provider of specialized explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) capacity that is permitted to operate in Gaza by the Gazan and Israeli authorities. UNMAS’ continued presence has been requested by the Palestinian and Israeli authorities, the UN RC/HC, OCHA, UNRWA and UNDP. UNMAS continues to provide explosive remnants of war (ERW) mitigation measures to UN and humanitarian partner sites to ensure that vulnerable communities are protected and prepared against the threat of explosive hazards from past, present and future escalations in conflict. UNMAS maintains an emergency EOD presence in Gaza to remove and destroy ERW posing a threat to Gaza communities. This allows UNMAS to be ready for a rapid response during potential conflict and to maintain an Emergency Response Preparedness Plan (ERPP) to ensure access for the humanitarian response in any future Gaza conflict. Throughout this project, UNMAS will contribute to this outcome primarily through the provision of risk assessment to UN agencies and humanitarian partners sites suspected with ERW contamination, to ensure they run their humanitarian operations safely and securely without exposure to any risks posed by ERW contamination.
Outcome 2: People know how to mitigate the risk of explosive ordnance. An individual’s risk of exposure varies on factors that include where they live and work and the information they have at hand. Explosive ordnance risk awareness is critical to mitigating the threat posed to civilians. UNMAS has identified two types of educational activities that are required in the specific context of Gaza. After several years of community engagement with beneficiary stakeholders and local partners, UNMAS has developed targeted curricula which consider the needs of different gender and age groups as well as persons with disabilities: (i) explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) provides information for civilians to protect themselves from existing ERW threats; and, (ii) Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) is a form of risk education providing resilience training to prepare for future escalations, and is critical to avoid civilian casualties. To address COVID-19 restrictions, remote delivery methodologies have been developed as well as dual messaging addressing ERW and pandemic mitigation, which will be used when the epidemiological situation deteriorates, and following WHO guidelines. Informal street sessions with social distancing measures have also been utilized.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in Gaza has left the land contaminated with ERW. From January 2014 to September 2022, 81 ERW accidents have been reported in Gaza causing 20 fatalities and 249 injuries. In particular, during the escalation of hostilities in Gaza in May 2021, the Israeli Defense Forces (IDF) launched over 4,000 strikes via air, sea and land according to the UN Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS). At least 19 deep buried bombs (DBB) were identified across Gaza, including in UN premises, the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) schools, and in densely populated communities posing immediate threats to civilians of all ages as well as UN and humanitarian personnel. It should be noted that, as a consequence of the escalation during 5-7 August 2022, two UNRWA schools were confirmed as impacted. ERW in residential areas and amongst the rubble poses an immediate risk to life, precludes the delivery of humanitarian aid, inhibits safe movement, and prevents access to basic services. Existing ERW contamination poses a threat to communities across Gaza. The current level of ERW contamination is impossible to determine; however, ERW continues to be found and accidents involving ERW continue to occur. During the escalation of hostilities in Gaza in May 2021, the damage to basic infrastructure in Gaza severely affected the provision of electricity, piped water and essential services. A sizable proportion of the items dropped did not explode on impact, with an estimated failure rate of 10 percent, meaning the conflict has left a dangerous explosive legacy with a large number of ERW remaining on the ground across Gaza. The recurrence of the conflict in Gaza creates a trend of increased ERW incidents which indicates an ongoing ERW threat to the local population. Men are more vulnerable to loss of life and injuries by ERW due to their larger engagement in the public sphere, including participation in the provision of first response services, construction work, scrap metal collection, farming and fishing. More than 16 known and accessible DBB have been located in Gaza since 2021, with the most recent clearance activity conducted in September 2022 by UNMAS, which posed a threat to the local population in a populated neighborhood in Rafah. There remains at least 4 known DBBs that need to be cleared. While the exact number of cases will only be known when bombs are discovered, it is estimated that there are many items of uncleared unexploded ordnance, clearance of which would support vulnerable communities to mitigate the threats of ERW. An individual’s risk of exposure varies on factors like where they live, work and the existing knowledge on how to protect and prepare themselves against ERW threat. UN/humanitarian staff, construction workers, and other at-risk populations including children, persons with disabilities, boys and men are particularly vulnerable. All populations at risk from ERW require targeted knowledge to safely respond to ERW contamination. There is concern amongst mine action partners of an increase in desensitization to the risks of ERW amongst the Gaza population leading to accidents. There exists a gap in the capacity of the Gazan authorities to respond appropriately to ERW contamination, highlighting the need for intervention. According to protection analysis update (PAU) published by the Protection Cluster in 2022, ERW is a high-priority risk that entails maintaining a mine action presence to respond to ERW in and around humanitarian premises , and continuous delivery of EORE and CPP messages to the affected communities in Gaza to increase capacity for personal safety before, during, and after conflict. The multi sector needs assessment (MSNA) conducted in 2022, found only 25.6 per cent of households in Gaza with at least one family member received information or training on the risks of ERW which indicates an urgent need to ensure that humanitarian actors and the general population receive risk education to reduce risk from ERW. Community engagement with female groups and local community service organizations (CSOs) have enabled women to be particularly targeted in CPP sessions as they have a key role in the household to prepare and protect themselves and their families against ERW incidents and conflicts. UNMAS provides different types of EORE to mitigate the risk of explosive ordnance, including coordinated community sessions, training for professionals including UN and humanitarian staff, street sessions for beneficiaries otherwise not reached, especially children, and reconstruction sessions for construction workers on sites in addition to the recently introduced module targeting the fishermen, which are provided on request. UNMAS also delivers CPP resilience training to empower the local community, especially females, to understand ERW risks, how to prepare homes for bombardment, or evacuate persons with disabilities safely. According to the MSNA only 44.4% of the households reported that they had taken measures to protect themselves in case of armed conflict. Since 2021, UNMAS has reached 29,678 people (8,738 women, 9,405 men, 5,846 girls, 5,689 boys) via 1,527 EORE class/street sessions and a further 400 CPP sessions have been delivered to 8,183 beneficiaries (6,035 women, 2,148 men). In response to the three days of escalation in August 2022, UNMAS immediately initiated an emergency EORE/CPP campaign across social media, radio, and SMS reaching more than 850,000 Gazans. Since 2020, new approaches have been developed to ensure continuity of EORE/ CPP delivery during any future movement restrictions, such as those required due to COVID-19.

2 - Activities

Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) response: UNMAS will conduct EOD response at UN premises, including UN reconstruction sites and other UN and humanitarian locations, primarily through risk assessments. UNMAS will liaise closely with the EOD Police in Gaza and the Coordinator of Government Activities in the Territories (COGAT) as well as the Coordination and Liaison Administration (CLA) in Israel during and after EOD responses, enabling UN agencies and humanitarian partners to deliver humanitarian activities safely and efficiently. UNMAS will focus on schools, medical facilities, and reconstruction sites and will be guided by the priorities of UN agencies including UNRWA, UNDP, and OCHA. The below activities will be delivered through UNMAS local implementing partner, the Save Youth Future Society (SYFS).Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE): UNMAS will predominantly target groups who are most at risk of encountering and being injured by ERW to receive the life-saving messages of EORE. ERW accident data shows that Gaza’s high-risk groups are: (i) children–nearly 80 percent of victims of ERW incidents after the May 2021 escalation were children; (ii) displaced people and those living or passing through areas affected by the escalation; and, (iii) men–specifically those involved in rubble removal/construction, agriculture, fishing, and scrap metal collection. EORE sessions will be delivered via two modalities: 1. ‘community sessions’ in community-based centers across Gaza; and, 2. ‘street visits’ to high-risk populations, such as scrap metal collectors and rubble removal workers. EORE sessions will also be provided to staff of UN agencies and international and national non-governmental organizations (I/NNGOs) based in Gaza to mitigate the risk of ERW incidents and ensure safe delivery of humanitarian response.Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP): CPP activities are designed to empower civilians to assess risk and make decisions to be better prepared in the face of the use of explosive weapons in populated areas (EWIPA) by providing ERW safety messaging and resilience training. The primary target is women as they are not usually engaged in training or provided with updated safety information and resources despite being the ones who are most often responsible for protecting their children and homes from the impact of hostilities. Along with women, staff of humanitarian partners including UNRWA, I/NNGO, CBOs, and family centers will be prioritized. The CPP sessions will be delivered in a classroom setting or at community centers and will include, inter alia, practical lessons on how to reduce bleeding, how to use a fire extinguisher, and how to prepare an emergency bag. In addition to the EORE and CPP sessions delivered to affected communities, UNMAS will conduct ToT sessions on EORE and CPP for volunteers in Gaza, to develop their capacity to to enhance local ownership and sustainability for mine action in Palestine. UNICEF is a member of the Mine Action Working Group for Gaza which enables UNMAS to consult with other mine action partners to incorporate child protection principles. UNMAS has specific risk education messaging in a simpler and child friendly manner that have been developed by risk education experts globally. In order to ensure participation of the community, this project will conduct focus group discussions (FGD) with men, women, children, people living in rural areas, and persons with disabilities, during the planning, implementation, and evaluation phases of the project, to identify the needs and the best methodologies and communication channels to deliver effective EORE and CPP messages. Based on the results of the surveys, UNMAS will utilize context-appropriate communication channels to enhance community participation, particularly among women, persons with disabilities and other marginalized populations, and develop user-friendly communication materials and platforms grounded in the context of communities, including leaflets, social media, and radio programs that are simplified with diagrams and translated into local languages. For the selection of the participants with disabilities for FGD, UNMAS will consult with the local organizations that support persons with disabilities. Gender analysis will be conducted in this project, separately or as part of existing needs analyses of target populations. Systematic use of gender analysis, sex-disaggregation of data, and commissioning of surveys is planned to be conducted in this project.. Throughout the implementation of this project, the project will ensure all the UNMAS and implementing partner staff to complete a disability awareness course. The materials developed through this project will use images/drawings that show persons with disabilities in empowering positions, in order to mainstream their participation and equality in all aspects of life. Collected data will be disaggregated by gender, age and type of disability. This includes training of internal and external staff in data collection using the chosen tool. During the implementation of the project, UNMAS will consult with the organizations of persons with disabilities that operate at the target locations to ensure that the developed materials are disability inclusive in the local context. Prevention of sextual exploitation and abuse of authority (PSEA); UNMAS will establish a generic email address to receive misconduct reports including SEA incidents. Beneficiaries will be provided with a misconduct briefing in addition to awareness printed materials so they can inclusively fathom how to complain when needed. Complaints will be handled according to UNMAS guidelines on complaint mechanisms. Staff and contractors will be provided with PSEA training as well.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

One of the aims of the project is to deliver the service of ERW clearance in Gaza which is essential in restoring normality to Gaza, a place that is under constant threat of war. UNMAS ensures through pre- and post-survey on clearance and risk education is carried out to effectively take the principle of DNH into consideration in relation to beneficiaries by improving accountability towards beneficiaries, building relations with beneficiaries, and understanding the contexts and community dynamics in which the projects take place. UNMAS consults and involves communities from the identification phase of a project through surveys, community consultations and workshops with staff, partners and other relevant actors. The project design has considered the safety and the dignity of beneficiaries as a first priority, given that the target group is very broad and covers men, women, boys and girls directly affected by ERW contamination. All staff involved in the project, including partner organizations’ staff, are or will be trained on protection and gender mainstreaming as well as on PSEAH to promote best practices and to ensure that no harm results from UNMAS intervention. UNMAS interventions have also all been planned in a gender sensitive manner, and collection of disaggregated data collection according to the protection principles is maintained. Child protection principles have been integrated in project planning, and UNMAS ensures that the different needs of children are being met in targeted risk education sessions. In case of COVID-related restrictions: UNMAS ensures that its offices are safe for the staff members to return to, and adhere to all minimum precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and good hygiene measures. All UNMAS activities have been adapted and implemented during the COVID-19 restrictions. Remote messaging to beneficiaries will be activated as soon as face to face is no longer advisable, shorter and more informal street sessions have been devised and regular need assessments will be conducted to identify any new emerging need.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through its activities UNMAS works to ensure that equal access is available to all those in need. In its focus on the technical side of ERW clearance, and the provision of EORE and CPP, UNMAS creates an atmosphere of safety that enables people to move around and rebuild the lives of people in need. UNMAS risk education is tailored to different groups, taking into consideration the needs of the groups. For example, CPP sessions are provided at times that stay at home mothers are available, and delivered in locations where childcare can be made available. Sessions tailored to children ensure that children who have other child care duties can attend with their siblings, and are directly after school or a time that is convenient. UNMAS also ensures that access for persons with disabilities is made available through its CBO at women and family centres. Throughout the project cycle, persons with disabilities will be mainstreamed in line with the IASC guidelines. UNMAS and its partners will ensure that persons with disabilities are able to access the UNMAS interventions on the same terms as other members of the population. During the preparation phase of the activities, the accessibility of persons with disabilities to the activities will be assessed and adjusted to promote the participation of persons with disabilities. UNMAS intervention indirectly touches on human rights in Gaza by enabling people to go about their normal lives, go to work and schools without the threat of explosions. In addition, age and gender disaggregation is systematically included in all reporting and data capture. UNMAS will continue monitoring data related to victims of ERW and other explosive hazards, disaggregated by gender/age/location, to ensure early identification of potential/actual new trends in terms of at-risk groups and victim profiles, and timely gender sensitive recommendations for preventive action. UNMAS will also continue to work with partners to engage men and boys particularly, who in Gaza are most at risk of being injured/killed by ERW. At the same time, UNMAS will ensure that women continue to be identified and encouraged to actively attend CPP sessions and ERW briefings in order to further reduce the low number of female casualties caused by ERW in Gaza and to better relay awareness messages amongst families. As mentioned above, PSEAH complaints received from beneficiaries will be handled based on the complaints handling workflow prescribed in the UNMAS guideline on complaint mechanism. UNMAS will ensure the complaint mechanism to be accessible for all beneficiaries through provision of a misconduct briefing for beneficiaries, distribution of awareness cards in Arabic, and providing PSEA training for staff and contractors. Beneficiaries are able to report misconduct anonymously without giving her/his name through a confidential contact line.
