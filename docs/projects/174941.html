Project Summary

The current situation of Food security in communities affected by the crisis in the South West region is deplorable with the number of people in need in the South West and North West regions significantly increasing from 1.3 million to 2.3 million (HRP 2020). According to the various reports published in 2019 and 2020, there is an urgent need to scale up food security and nutrition supplementation to about 270,000 persons in the South West region with over 119,463 are in the crisis phase. Ndian division in the South West region of Cameroon is highly affected by the ongoing conflicts, yet it is largely underserved by humanitarian action based on several factors including physical access challenges as well as access in terms of acceptance of humanitarian actions by the parties to the conflict. The division is home to over 122,579 inhabitants (2005 PNDP statistics) with over 731 internally displaced persons. WFP’s remote household food security monitoring (mVAM) found that in July 2019, millions of people in the SW have insufficient food consumption, thereof 300,000 with poor food intake. While WFP’s in-kind food assistance has been effective in reducing the share of households with a poor food consumption score (by 5% in NW/SW), the proportion of households with an acceptable food consumption score also dropped (by 2%). However, the only recent assistance related to food security was from IRC (where 250 HHs in Ekondo Titi were assisted via three rounds of food distributions) and LUKMEF (where 220 households in Bekora, Masore Lobe Estate and Lobe town were assisted with 4 rounds of voucher destribution). IDPs and host communities Households living in Ekondo Nene, Bekora, Lobe Estate, Lobe town, and Masore in Ndian Division are food insecure and in dire need of food assistance which is further aggravated by shelter, health, and WASH needs caused by the armed conflict in the South West and North West Regions.
There is therefore an urgent need for humanitarian intervention in this division and region in view of enhancing food security within this region. This project is aimed at supporting the most vulnerable households (IDPs, returnees and Host community members) in 4 communities as direct beneficiaries (800 (200 households per community)) to the provision of basic food support through food vouchers distribution, and 300 women through the creation of small businesses and other income generating activities to support household finances.
In the realization of this project, the following activities will direct realization of the project’s purpose;
Act #1: Engage and promote agricultural activities through established farmers’ associations within Ekondo Nene, Masore, Bekora, Lobe Estate and Lobe Town in view of increasing the impact of commercial agriculture on the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and Host Communities members by supporting large scale crop production, mitigation of post-harvest losses and effective commercialization of agricultural produce.
Act #2: Provide and enhance practical and employable skills through workshops and vocational training for 6 months to 200 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community members in view of improving their contribution to their communities and reduce local unemployment and dependency.
Act #3: Strengthen the capacity of 200 local leaders (cultural, religious, minority groups and associations) through a series of trainings to prevent, mitigate, mediate and manage violent uprisings, community instability as well as water and food crisis within their various communities.
Act #4: Meet the food needs of the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and hosts community households (800) through monthly (6) food voucher distributions.
