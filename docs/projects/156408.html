Project Summary

The hereby action aims at providing life-saving and life-protecting assistance to vulnerable Syrian in Eastern Ghouta, rural Hama and Dara’a, responding to acute needs in the Education sector, more precisely Education in Emergencies (EiE). All targeted governorates have been identified as relevant for intervention, with regards to the needs identified, to the TGH operational capacities, to which add potentialities for new emergency needs, such as population displacement and returns. First, the presence of TGH in the areas targeted will ease the implementation of the different activities. Second, the needs assessment conducted by TGH team attest of acute needs in terms of Education in both governorates, especially regarding the number of Out-Of-School Children, the learning environment (school destructions and damages) and the education staff capacities. In the frame of a resilient and long-term approach, it is necessary to empower the future generation that will be needed in the perspective of the country’s reconstruction.
To respond to the Education needs identified, TGH estimated relevant to focus on Non-Formal Education activities, reintegration of Out-of-School children into the education system, as well as light rehabilitations of schools.
Most of the teachers, especially in Eastern Ghouta, did not attend any training before being enrolled in the targeted schools and are working there either as “non-formal” teachers or as “volunteers”. Furthermore, the field assessment showed that most of the volunteers are female workers, this project aims at reinforcing their integration into the job market and at the same time to promote gender equality. At this extent, the education staff will be supported through adapted and required training promoting conflict-sensitive approaches. TGH will reinforce schools’ staff capacities regarding their knowledge and capacities in NFE and PSS and accompany them in the implementation of these activities in the targeted schools.
In order to favour the well-being of children attending school and foster the reintegration of Out-of-School Children, education facilities will be improved thanks to light rehabilitations. The provision of school kits and recreational materials will also contribute to improve learning environments, contributing to attract OSS children to go back to school but also convince the parents that their children are safe and that the time they spend in public schools is a well-spent time.
Besides the proposed improvement of learning spaces and the implementation of NFE and PSS activities, remedial classes will be organized in order to complete the curriculum B developed by the MoE. The different components designed within the hereby project will take part to the reintegration of OOS children into the education system but also prevent the registered children to drop-out.
The identification of Out-of-School children will involve key stakeholders (DoE, Schools’ principals), but also Community-Based Education Child Protection Committees (CBECPC) that will be created within thin project.
This project will benefit to all the children attending school and NFE activities, with no specific eligibility criteria in accordance with the do no harm principle.

Needs

The three governorates targeted by the action have experienced long and violent sieges that exhausted populations deprived of basic goods and services and causing destructions and casualties. Eastern Ghouta (EG) faced a peak of violence when the Syrian Army Forces (SAF) launched an offensive in February 2018 to regain control over the region, provoking extensive damages as well as new internal displacements. The demographic upheaval and damages to infrastructure have been less severe in the targeted area of the Hama governorate. Needs are however dire in this rural underserved area which witnessed population returns and is prone to new ones. Both targeted areas have so far received little humanitarian assistance in comparison with the scale of needs, due to limited humanitarian response capacities and funding. Humanitarian actors remain few in government-held Syria, and continue to face strong operational constraints. The targeted locations however had been initially selected due to their high vulnerability with regards to their recent history in relation to the conflict, and are characterized by acute humanitarian needs fully falling within the frame of the priorities defined in the HIP. Most targeted locations have a 6 score on HNO’s vulnerability scale, and those with a lesser score (3) are in the vicinity of areas prone to conflict and potentially source of new displacement thus humanitarian needs. According to secondary data analysis, a third of the schools are no longer operational in Syria due to destruction or for being used as temporary shelters or contaminated with explosives. 2.1 million Children aged 5 to 17 reportedly do not have access to any education facility (HRP-HNO 2019). Among the reasons behind this high number of out-of-school (OOS) children, the lack of functioning schools has been commonly quoted and observed during field assessments, in particular in EG where schools have been massively damaged either due to shelling or for being used by the parties at fight. Light rehabilitations have been requested by the population and the education staff in the schools assessed. Children work enrolment is another factor of dropping-out. Indeed, nearly 70% of the interviewed households in both governorates stated that only one person is contributing to the household income, confirming that children early work is a tangible risk. In Harbanifse (Hama), TGH team was reported that many children enroll in the local shoe factory as soon as they get their ID (when they get 15 years old in Syria). Besides children labor, the lack of education services brings in other protection issues such as early marriages, especially for girls, therefore raising gender concerns. Measures to prevent children to drop-out and to integrate OOS children into the education system seem necessary to mitigate the potential Protection risks. A better school environment and adapted education system (NFE, catch-up classes, PSS) are adjusted components to respond to those needs. Eventually, teachers’ capacity building appears as a need during the field assessments. As mentioned above, only a few regular teachers, that are not even trained to work in sensitive conflict-situation, are still working in the schools targeted, assisted by volunteers. The Syrian context requires specific pedagogic methodology in order to avoid the phenomena of ‘lost generation’ and support the resilience of the future generation, in a long-term perspective.

Implementation of activities

1: Light rehabilitation of 11 Schools The proposed activity aims at providing pupils with education facilities promoting the safety and well-being of learners, teachers and other education personnel, in adhesion to INEE principles. TGH targeted schools where clear rehabilitation needs have been identified. Each school targeted by TGH has been significantly damaged by fragments and bullets impacts. Windows have been blown apart exposing children to humidity and cold, which is a cause for drop out and may deter currently OOS children from reintegrating school. The community will be involved in the proposed rehabilitation of schools, through contributing to the cleaning of school sites prior to the start of works. Limited incentives will be provided. Communities will be informed about the rehabilitation process and its different stages upstream the beginning of works, which will allow individuals interested in being involved in the clearing works to apply through TGH focal points. In total, four schools will be rehabilitated. 2: Provision of recreational and learning materials, small equipment and school kits This activity is a natural complementing feature to activity 1, both activities aiming at granting the targeted children with a dignified learning environment, conducive to their well-being and personal development. The proposed provision of pedagogical materials and school kits will respond to the identified very poor situation in that regard, which is reportedly a cause for dropping out. Three types of kits will be distributed. School kits to provide the minimum material dedicated to non-formal education and catch up classes. 1 kit per classroom will be distributed. Student kits providing children with the minimum stationaries to learn properly. Student kits will be distributed including to OOS children in a enrollment path to foster their motivation and well-being at school. Recreational kits provided to each rehabilitated school, in support to the building of quality learning environments and of PSS activities. 3: Identification of OOS children Gathering accurate information and figures regarding OOS children and approaching them has been a challenge during the assessment. Although MoE along with UNICEF started 3 years ago the “back to learning” campaign, there is still a large number of children of primary school age that are not enrolled in the educational system according to the people and teachers interviewed, who however cannot provide figures about OOS children in most cases. OOS children often dropped out a long time ago thus disappeared from records. Aiming at favoring the identification of OOS, TGH decided of a community-based approach rather than limiting to resort to the sole usual key informants. In that aim, TGH will set up Community Based Education and Child Protection Committees, one per targeted location. Being from the community will put the members of CBECPC in a good position to engage with vulnerable families and identify OOS children through outreach activities. 4: Training of teachers in Education in Emergencies, Active Learning Program and PSS Children are exposed to violence, conflict, loss and displacement. In these exceptionally difficult circumstances, EiE and PSS activities are of great importance as they provide children with a feeling of normalcy, hope for the future and an opportunity to develop mentally appropriate activities. It is crucial that educators understand this and that schools provide a child-friendly environment that fosters psychosocial well-being, resilience and coping. 5: NFE and PSS activities directed to both Oout-of-School Children and regular pupils The Education system takes into account part of the needs created by the conflict. The curricula B indeed aimed to respond to educational backwardness, and will be used in remedial classes. Catch-up classes will be organized around the self-learning program whose organization is proposed for OOS children.

Protection Risk Analysis

Children need to learn in conducive and protective environment. The crisis led to the systematic destruction of schools and all public services. TGH is engaged in the rehabilitation of the damaged school under the activity 1 and identified the risk of selecting an inappropriate contractor to perform the rehabilitation. The risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members, service providers is real (likelihood medium but high impact). To reduce the risk, TGH will request at least 2 years of experience and recommendation letter from previous client to make sure of the quality of the services and preserve child safe environment. In addition, TGH will organize information and awareness session for staffs, volunteers and contractors before any activity at school to embed culture of child protection and safety to all actors. A child safety code of conduct will be signed by all actors involved in the implementation of the project. TGH field based team will also organize a close monitoring of the work done by contractors. Activities based on distribution of kits can generate frustration and risk of discrimination or perception of discrimination between schools, communities and children. The likelihood is between medium and low since TGH plan to distribute to all children either out of school children or attending schools, but the impact is really high. To mitigate this risk, TGH team will ensure consultation and involvement of the community and will clearly communicate with people of each cities and neighbouring community. The long distance to go to school for some of the students, even after the rehabilitation of the school is a risk already identified (likelihood high and high impact) during the assessment phase. Indeed, in some of the cities, the route taken by children is not safe and exposes them to GBV risk, UXO/ERW hazard, especially for girls. To mitigate this risk, TGH will consult separately girls, boys, women and men in the assessment, design and implementation phases to understand what are the most important challenges and the way they would like to deal with it. TGH will also add explosive hazard awareness material and GBV awareness to the program. Furthermore, the light rehabilitation of the schools targeted will enable to organize only one shift which will reduce the school day duration and prevent the children to go back home too late. No gender sensitive toilets available in the school represent GBV, health and safety risk. The likelihood is high and already identified and the impact is severe. TGH proposed under the WASH sector, the rehabilitation of the toilets in schools and the provision of clean water to mitigate the risk. The Education activities are closely related to Child Protection components. As mentioned in the needs analysis, numerous children are working instead of going to schools and girls are exposed to early marriages, increasing the gender-based violence risks and deterioration of the child protection situation. Encouraging the children to go back/stay at school thanks to the stationaries distribution, the improvement of the learning environment and the adaptation of the classes methodology (PSS, NFE) will enable to mitigate these risks. Finally, as many volunteer teachers are female, TGH will assure an equal access to the trainings for all volunteers, both male and female. This teachers’ capacity building component of this project will support the gender equality within the school staff.
