Project Summary

The purpose of this project is two-fold: to provide critical information on lifesaving and life-enhancing issues to Somalis across the country, using daily radio broadcasts, to enable them to make better informed decisions; and to amplify the voices of local communities, including the most marginalized and excluded, through a trusted feedback platform, to enable communities to participate in the overall humanitarian planning and response cycle.
The daily radio broadcasts are produced by Radio Ergo, the only dedicated humanitarian radio in Somalia, with an established reputation among listeners and partners for quality and professionalism. Radio Ergo’s mission is to provide relevant humanitarian and public interest information to listeners (men, women, boys and girls) in rural as well as urban areas, and to facilitate a participatory communication cycle between the listeners and the humanitarian community and local/national governmental authorities. The broadcasts contain programming based on a number of complementary sources. First, Radio Ergo’s unique network of around 20 locally based Somali reporters gather interviews, reports, features and other information from the villages, towns, IDP camps in the areas where they live. The local reporters’ job is to reflect humanitarian issues on the ground from as many perspectives as possible, including different genders, age groups, social backgrounds and livelihoods. Second, practical humanitarian information and advice, aligned with cluster key messaging and advocacy, is incorporated into the radio programming in formats including interviews with sectoral experts, short humanitarian advisories and messages, and question-answer segments with listeners and experts e.g. on health, farming, livestock and other. Third, a wide variety of speakers from governmental, non-governmental and community spheres contribute to radio discussions around key issues across the humanitarian-resilience-development/peacebuilding nexuses.
The Radio Ergo community feedback platform is the only voice-based channel open continually to callers from every location across all regions of Somalia on any humanitarian-related topic or issue. Free of charge to the caller, people can call in and leave a voice message from any phone connected to the three main network providers: Hormud for southern and central areas, Golis in Puntland, and Telesom in Somaliland. The majority of callers seem to be in rural areas, where they rely on Radio Ergo for information, and would fall generally overwhelmingly within vulnerable and/or excluded groups. Many are pastoralists, farmers, displaced, returnee refugees, among others. Radio Ergo collates the feedback weekly and shares an analysis report on main themes and trends with the humanitarian clusters, as well as a broader list of partners, NGOs/CSOs, media, donors and others.
In terms of methodology, the feedback is intended as information for action. It provides a unique source of unstructured feedback emanating from communities many of whom are in rural and hard-to-reach areas. Radio Ergo follows up on some comments for investigation by its reporters. Some of the feedback is incorporated into programme segments e.g. the radio doctor and farming programmes respond to listeners’ questions using sectoral experts. The feedback also allows the clusters to compare information against other sources and to spot early warning trends. Agencies use the feedback to inform planning and response activities. It serves as an integral part of collective efforts in Communications and Community Engagement (CCE) and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP).
The project harnesses the power of radio, being the most popular means of mass communication in Somalia, to save lives, raise awareness and enable informed decision-making; and to ensure the inclusion of voices that would not be captured in forums for service planning and provision.

Project Activities

The project has five main activity components: 1. Programme production; 2. Broadcasting; 3. Managing Feedback platform; 4. Building local partner capacity; 5. Monitoring & Evaluation.Indicative activities include the following:- Humanitarian Reporting from all regions of Somalia using Radio Ergo's local Somali journalist network and staff production team;- Production of daily programming on humanitarian issues across Somalia, with news, features, interviews, discussions, advisories and messaging, drama, and feedback;- Development and production of informative life-saving and life-enhancing programme segments in collaboration with clusters and other experts, on disaster risk reduction, health, nutrition, food security & livelihoods, protection, resilience, and more;- Broadcasting of daily one hour programme on shortwave (national coverage) and distribution of daily programming to local Somali FM radio partners for rebroadcast;- Production of web stories, Newsletters, social media products for wider reach of humanitarian information;- Managing 'Freedom Fone' listeners' feedback platform, involving collecting & collating voice messages from all regions, and preparing and distributing weekly analysis reports;- Regular and ad hoc information sharing discussions and activities with NGOs/agencies/other local partners resulting from the feedback analyses;- Capacity support and training with local Somali FM radio partners and local reporters in the field;- Coproductions with local partners of programming targeting specific groups, notably women, children, youth, disabled, farmers, pastoralists, IDPs, others;- Research with different audience groups disaggregated by gender, age (children, youth), region, education/social background, livelihoods, and including disability groups, IDPs, returnees.

Project Indicators

Output indicators: the following will be measured on an ongoing basis throughout the project cycle:Number of programmes broadcast on shortwave and FM;Number of female sources included in programming (gender indicator);Number of early warning/advisory messages aired on each humanitarian themes;Number of listeners reached (disaggregated to include gender, geographical location, age groups, social background, etc);Number of local Somali journalists/radios trained/capacitated;Number of community feedback calls received by gender/region/theme;Number of feedback reports distributed. Outcome indicators: the following will be evaluated at mid-points and at the end of the project using largely qualitative methods.- What impact/s has the Radio Ergo programming had on the lives, livelihoods and decision-making processes of different audience groups (gender; age groups, geographical locations, disabled, IDPs, farmers, pastoralists, possibly others). - How has the feedback platform been used by/helped community users in different categories?- What actions and responses (by communities and clusters/other partners) can be attributed to the feedback platform?- To what extent has the project's capacity building impacted local radio partners and journalists' coverage of humanitarian and public interest issues?

Mitigation Measures

The project aims to be as inclusive as possible of gender, age, location/region, social background, disability, etc. mitigation measures include:1. National broadcasting via shortwave to reach rural communities especially.2. Rebroadcasting via local FM partners to maximise dissemination of information.3. Gender and disability inclusion in capacity building activities. 3. Broad coverage of issues within programming as well as specifically targeted segments to capture the voices and serve the needs of women, men, girls and boys, youth, disabled, farmers, pastoralists, IDPs, and other groups.4. Managing an open, free of charge feedback platform to capture views and comments and complaints from anyone anywhere at any time when the service is running.5. Regular analysis of the feedback for appropriate response where possible.
