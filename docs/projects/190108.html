Project Summary

Ethiopia has experienced an unprecedented high number of conflict-induced displacements across Oromia, SNNP, Tigray, Amhara, Afar and Benishangul-Gumuz regions. The persistent high number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) coupled with multilayered complexities of the displacement and inadequate humanitarian response have resulted in high levels of unmet humanitarian needs. In Oromia, the security situation, remains volatile and unpredictable as a result of expanding military operations. The total number of IDPs in the two severely affected zones of East and West Wollega has reached 184,000 from this 22,630 HHs (134,338 individuals) are living in fraught living conditions in East Wollega zone alone. (IDP Response Plan Report for Wollegas and Kamashi Zones). According to 2022 HRP plan for Kiramu and Sasiga woredas WASH cluster caseloads 78039 and 10430 IDPs and 41618 non- displaced host communities have planned to be reached with WASH responses. The major WASH gaps identified from the multiple assessments, in the selected Woredas of Kiramu and Sasiga, access to water continues to be a critical challenge for IDPs and host communities’ due to the insufficient availability of water, distance and the cost of accessing water, and the significant number of non-functional water points. The limited water resource has led to tensions between IDPs and host communities, resulted in protection concerns as girls are traveling 2-4 km to fetch water, increased the prices of water and there is an anticipated Cholera outbreak. Overall, the existing water sources are not adequate to meet IDP needs in collective centers and for host communities. IDPs also lack access to sanitation facilities and they are likely to practice open defecation, leading to the prevalence of poor hygiene conditions and increased risk of disease. Health and school institutions in these areas also lack proper water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities and there is also huge gap regarding WASH NFI, WTC and dignity kits.
This proposed WASH program will cover the WASH needs to address the gaps in conflict affected IDPs, and host communities in both selected woredas by increasing access to WASH services through the rehabilitation and maintenance of 11 nonfunctional water systems, three months of water trucking in the absence of other alternatives for IDPs, NFIs for 12000 individuals, distribution of hygiene supplies, water treatment chemicals and dignity kits, construction of 28 stances of sex segregated and disability inclusive sanitation facilities (latrines capacity building for 77 WASHCo members, hygiene promotion in two targeted woredas to reach 47,960 individuals. AIDRO will assure close collaboration with the WASH cluster, concerned government bodies, and beneficiaries in implementing and involving them in the decision-making process of the proposed project.

1. - Projects activities

Rehabilitate the existing water systems through replacement of nonfunctional generators at 2 boreholes and rehabilitation of 9 Shallow wells, construct 28 emergency semi-permanent latrines (4.35x2.15x1.5) in Sasiga woreda IDP sites in line with the SPHERE standard, with one stance serving 50 people, construction of hand-washing facilities at each emergency latrine, excavate waste pits for the disposal of waste materials in the IDP site, disinfect the rehabilitated water system, three months of water trucking in the absence of other alternatives for IDPs, establish complaints and feedback response mechanism (FCRM) at appropriate location and orient beneficiaries on use (cross-cutting AAP activity) and conduct post distribution monitoring (PDP), procurement, transportation, and distribution of WASH NFI items like laundry soaps, body soaps, washing basin, buckets, and jerry cans for 12,000 individuals, procurement, transportation, and distribution of households' water treatment chemicals for 3 months, procurement of hand tools for latrine and west disposal pit excavation and earth work per communal latrine, organize hygiene promotion training to 72 hygiene promoters (43 for Sasiga woreda and 29 for kiramu woreda, conduct hygiene and sanitation promotion activities using megaphones to avoid mass gathering, printing and distribution of 500 posters for hygiene promotion messages displaying hygiene practices, production and duplication of hygiene promotion guideline for hygiene promoters, provide Personal Protective Equipment and hygiene materials (Face masks, hand sanitizers) for 72 hygiene promoters, conduct monthly consultation meetings with beneficiaries regarding the services and activities that affect them, establish and train WASH-Cos on water management and maintenance of water infrastructure and sustainability in IDP sites, conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM).

Alliance for Integrated Development and Relief Organization will maintain a strong working relationship with other implementing partners operating in the target regions to avoid duplication of effort and active participant of national WASH coordination groups with relevant Government and non-government stakeholders to discuss priorities, share best practices, and make decisions on issues of common purpose. AIDRO will coordinate and closely work with protection partners / GBV response partners/ in the project location and regions to ensure gender mainstreaming and protection under WASH programs and achieve the targeted indicators and activities. Moreover, this project will closely work with government sectors and user community specifically with WASHCO to support the ongoing effort by Government and AIDRO, and other stakeholders to bring meaningful result that avert the impact of displacement, food insecurity, poor/no access to health services, and low access to safe water and sanitation, as well as WASH items provided for the most vulnerable displaced community. Existing government and stakeholders’ efforts are filling gaps for the most needs for both IDPs, provision of supplies to mitigate and prevent the outcome of ongoing and potential outbreaks in targeted woredas. AIDRO has a very good experience of working with different partners in different roles for a common goal.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

AIDRO ensures the participation of community members throughout the life cycle of the project. It will establish clear mechanism that allows the community members to provide feedback on the project implementation. The mechanism will ensure comprehensive and confidential system set up. This project is designed by engaging community members through their representatives such as community leaders and religious leaders, concerned government bodies and other partners. During startup of the program, information about the program, beneficiary selection criteria, and information on their entitlement, the beneficiary’s right to raise any complaints and complaining mechanisms will be provided for beneficiaries and will continue to be communicated to promote transparency. Site and beneficiary selection will be take place after consultation meetings held with venerable groups, representatives of the affected population government stakeholders and cluster representatives. Different review meetings will be held with community members to provide information and discuss on planned interventions from beneficiary targeting, verification, prioritization, distribution process, to informing the rights and entitlements of recipients. AIDRO works to integrate inclusion of people with disability in all cycles of the project. It will ensure the participation of this groups in beneficiary selection committees, complaint handling committees and monitoring framework. It will also organize consultation on planned projects with people with disabilities and understand the barrier to access to assistance. Feedback mechanisms will be designed to adequately record issues faced by persons with disabilities and staff is empowered to act on these suggestions. The reporting mechanism will involve assigning focal person to record and follow up on any complaints received. Locked complaint boxes will be prominently displayed and regularly checked to collect feedback and to take appropriate measures. Information sharing and awareness creation about accountability to affected population will be held during distribution by using different printed IEC/BCC materials. Complaint handling and Beneficiary selection committee along with responsible government stakeholders and AIDRO project staffs will involve handling any concerns or complaints that arises from the beneficiary and act accordingly. The committee will be comprised of women, people with disabilities, elderlies to ensure maximum community participation. Aside from the committee, AIDRO will also set up alternate ways for feedback and complaint mechanism to promptly respond to concerns of beneficiary and adjust implementation. Mechanisms to receive this complaint will consider the preferred mechanism of the community but will mainly use Help desk, community feedback suggestion boxes, provision of local stakeholders of contacts of AIDRO staffs and selected focal representative of the community. AIDRO will use the standard checklist on incorporating accountability to affected population in the humanitarian programme cycle developed by the Global protection cluster to ensure that AAP is integrated into all cycles of the project. AIDRO will also ensure the collection and utilization of sex and age disaggregated data that adheres to recommended age groupings and AIDRO project staffs and community volunteers will be trained on humanitarian accountability and its importance to mainstream them on ground in every level of the project.
