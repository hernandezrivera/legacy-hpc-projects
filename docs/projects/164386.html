Project Summary

This project aims to provide rapid response to the urgent food needs of food insecure population in the Northwest Syria, providing in-kind, ready-to-eat (RTE) emergency rations for 14,185 newly displaced individuals and/or other vulnerable populations.

4 - Needs

As the situation remains extremely volatile with continuing hostilities across southern and eastern Idlib as well as western Aleppo, the Northwest Syria has been one of the highest food needs areas, more than 3.2 million people in need of food assistance, of which over 2.2 million are in acute people in need. Families across Syria, particularly in Northwest, are facing a deteriorating food security situation, with rapidly growing food prices. The latest WFP research shows that the average food price increased by 120% in Idleb in 2019, while it’s 60% increase nationwide. Amid on-going conflict causing economic crisis and unemployment, vulnerable population faces increasing constraints in accessing livelihood opportunities and many are exclusively reliant on food assistance to cover their needs. Food remains one of the highest priorities needs for IDPs, and there’re continuously growing needs of first-line response to newly displaced families in Northwest. 948,000 people fled towards Northwest Idleb and North Aleppo from 1 December to 23 February, the largest wave of displacement in nearly 9 years of the crisis. More than 80 % of the recently displaced are women and children. Dana sub-district remains the primary destination of the recently displaced in Idleb Governorate, followed by Maaret Tamsrin sub-district. Afrin is the primary displacement destination in Aleppo Governorate. The recent artillery attacks targeting Dana and Sarmada, hosting large numbers of IDPs, is spurring secondary displacement towards Afrin. The prioritized areas for emergency food assistance are rapidly expanding to Olive Branch and Euphrates Shield Areas. High gaps are found in locations such as Salqin, Dana, Afrin and Azaz. Sources: HNO 2020, OCHA: Recent Developments in Northwest Syria(Feb 2020), HNAP: MOBILITY AND NEEDS MONITORING(Dec 2019), WFP: Emergency Situation Report 13(Feb 2020), HNAP: IDPs in Northwest Syria(Feb 2020), FSL Cluster: Ad hoc MTG of Emergency Situation(Feb 2020)

5 - Implementation of activities

For emergency life saving assistance and to meet the immediate food needs, this project will provide emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable populations with short-term food assistance through distribution of Ready to Eat Rations. Ready to Eat Ration will be a full 2100 kcal/person/day package, following the cluster suggested sample for a harmonized response. Area selection and targeting will be conducted through a combination of assessments using appropriate modalities, aiming to ensure that food assistance is received on the basis of need and endeavours to prevent ‘harm’ by limiting any negative impact of assistance. 4,000 RTE kits will be distributed based on the following breakdown according to household size:1-5 people in HH 1 kit6-10 people in HH 2 kit10 + people in HH 3 kit Estimate number of beneficiaries by sub-districts:• Afrin: 3,500• Jandairis: 1,185• Sharan: 1,000• Armanaz: 1,000• Dana: 2,000• Qourqeena: 1,000• Salqin: 1,500• Maaret Tamsrin: 3,000

Staff training on code of conduct, community mobilization 2. Meetings with community leaders/gate keepers/local councils on targeting criteria etc. 3. Setting community feedback mechanism 4. Social media monitoring

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There’s a potential risk of deterioration of the security situation preclude access to our areas of implementation. For the mitigation measure, our implementing partner has experience implementing actions in such context. We'll maintain a regular monitoring of conflict dynamics in areas of interventions and undertake periodic assessments of the operational contexts in order to respond to changes. There’s also a risk of more constrained cross-border access or that the regulatory environment in Turkey will not facilitate the operations of INGOs and Syrian NGOs from the north inside Syria. For the mitigation measure, contingency plans are in place by our implementing partner to relocate our operations in case operational access is reduced. Assumptions are: - Security and political considerations allow for the safe movement of our field staff. - Local institutions, community, and armed groups remain open to external project interventions that alleviate the conditions in which the targeted population lives.
