Project Summary

In line with UNICEF’s Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC) and Core Commitments for Children, UNICEF will continue to provide for South Sudan’s humanitarian affected populations, especially children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating women and girls, with equal access to quality preventive and curative health services, and respond to diseases outbreaks. To achieve this, UNICEF adopts a two-pronged programme approach by which static and mobile mechanisms are put in place to support humanitarian delivery in a complementary manner. Integrated Rapid Response Mechanisms (IRRM) activities enable UNICEF, through direct implementation, to provide high impact and immediate lifesaving emergency responses.
This project aims to increase access to quality equitable essential primary health care interventions through IRRM and provide a core pipeline to increase access to quality equitable immunization services by vulnerable populations living in host communities and internally displaced persons (IDPs) living in POCs, formal IDP camps or host communities in conflict affected areas in South Sudan.
The activities to be implemented are:
• Procurement and distribution of emergency vaccines.
• Procurement, distribution and maintenance of cold chain equipment
• Technical support for improved vaccine and cold chain management
• Training of front line partners of vaccine and cold chain management
• Conduct 36 RRM missions (3 per month) in the project duration will include:
The expected beneficiaries of the immunization core pipeline activities are 150,000 IDP children aged 6 months-years (78000 females and 72000 males). These children will benefit from emergency measles vaccines and cold chain equipment to be procured.
The expected beneficiaries of the IRRM mission are estimated 30,000 individuals (16,600 female and 13,400 male). These will include children aged 6 months-15years who will be reached with immunization services and curative consultations and pregnant women who are expected to benefit from ANC services, tetanus toxoid vaccination, curative consultations and the provision of long lasting insecticide treated nets (LLITN).

Needs

About four million people in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian healthcare services with 2 million targeted for health assistance in 2019 following over three years of conflict which has made the population highly susceptible to infectious diseases. At least four in ten health facilities have been affected by the conflict, from being looted, destroyed, damaged and/or occupied. It is estimated that a maximum of 43 per cent of the country’s 1,384 health facilities have remained operational, with less than 20% of health facilities being functional in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity are functional. This makes access to health services difficult for the large proportion of the vulnerable internally displayed person and host community members. Even those that are open are providing only an extremely minimal range of services due to a chronic lack of essential medicines, limited funding and high operational costs. Overall, health service operation in South Sudan is very expensive due to poor road networks, necessitating the use of costly charter flights to transport supplies to some locations. During the dry season, whilst more road transport routes are open, the likelihood of ambushes and looting is very high which makes transportation of supplies by road very difficult. Meanwhile, rains during the wet season render approximately 60 per cent of roads in the country unusable. The coverage for all vaccine preventable diseases remains below 50 per cent and is less than 20 per cent in Unity, Jonglei and Upper Nile. This gap in immunity had put children at a high risk of epidemics. This is partly evident in that a total of 427 suspected cases of measles with 5 deaths (CFR 1.19%) reported in South Sudan from Jan to October 2018. The consistently low measles vaccination coverage predisposes returnees, refugees, IDPs, and the surrounding host community to the risk of infectious disease outbreaks given the increasing pool of unimmunized susceptible children under-fifteen as well as the poor living conditions. This situation is further complicated by looting of cold chain equipment. For instance, in 2017, out of 128 cold chain equipment installed, about 12 per cent were vandalized and looted, mostly in Upper Nile and Jonglei. Moreover, while 93% of the Counties have functional vaccine stores, at the point of service delivery, only about 50% of functional health facilities have functional cold chain equipment. Against this backdrop, in hard to reach, conflict-affected areas with intermittent periods of stability and access, a key strategy for delivery of lifesaving health services is the use of Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism. This because the IRRM platform enables the delivery of essential healthcare package that can be accessed by IDPs in inaccessible areas, hard to reach areas in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states. Meanwhile, improving the immunization core pipeline through procurement of vaccines, especially measles vaccines, installation of cold chain equipment and use of portable cold chain equipment will assist in increasing access to quality equitable immunization services among the IDPs. Combined, these efforts will contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality among affected vulnerable populations.

The activities to be implemented during the project duration are:• Procurement and distribution of emergency vaccines.• Procurement, distribution and maintenance of cold chain equipment• Technical support for improved vaccine and cold chain management• Training of front line partners in effective vaccine and cold chain management• Conduct 36 RRM missions (3 per month) that will provide:  Curative consultations including malaria case management  Emergency measles vaccination ANC services including immunization with Tetanus Toxoid for pregnant women  Health education to promote healthy life style and health seeking behaviours (bolstering community resilience)
