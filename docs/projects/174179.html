Project Summary

In Rann, the proposed project perpetuates the current humanitarian intervention funded by the NHF ending on the 30th of April 2021 in close collaboration with the State Ministry of Health. Tdh has supported the reproductive health services in Rann for over a year, through a community-based approach involving Traditional birth attendants together with a community health extension worker, ensuring clean and safe home-based & Clinic based delivery, thereby contributing to the reduction of maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality. This proposed project seeks to sustain the sexual and reproductive health services provided to these vulnerable groups, as well as sustaining the provision of GBV services; Clinical management of Rape cases and referrals to other sectors as required.
On the other hand, In mafa, This project will ensure the provision of integrated PHC, including sexual and reproductive health, management of acute malnutrition and the provision of drugs and medical supplies among children, vulnerable girls and women, identified through a community-based mechanism. Tdh will target women and girls, as specifically vulnerable individuals, often left without qualitative healthcare.
The logic of intervention is built on four core components:
1. Support the community with the provision of SRH services and MNCH at different levels, to reduce maternal, neonatal and young children’s morbidity and mortality.
Midwives engaged by Tdh will conduct practical training to Community Health Extension Worker (CHEW) and Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs), who will be provided with clean delivery kits (CDK) whilst conducting basic antenatal and postnatal care, identifying danger signs of pregnancy and delivery. Tdh engaged medical doctor will visit Rann weekly to provide attentive supervision and treatment for complicated cases, if appropriate medications are available or certify medical referrals, depending on the security situation to the secondary healthcare.
However, in mafa, the proposed project will Support one health facility based in the IDP camp with medical supplies and skilled health workers providing services to both IDPs and host communities and ensure that it is equipped with the essential medications and equipments according to the required recommendations. Health workers; Midwives, Nurses, Community health extension workers (CHEWS) who are Government staff) will be trained and given support to provide MNCH services, including ANC, PNC, clean/ safe delivery, newborn care, basic essential emergency obstetric services and treatment of under 5 children through mobile clinics (using IMCI protocol).
2. Cash for Health Programming (E vouchers for specialized healthcare); Harness technology to significantly improve access to specialized healthcare services for vulnerable populations within Mafa LGA. Beneficiaries with complications who cannot be managed at the primary healthcare level will be provided with a referral voucher to access free medical services and drugs in any of the referral Secondary/Tertiary facilities. In addition, a potent referral pathway will be strengthened, and a means of Transportation/communication provided.
3 Tdh will focus on the capacity building of the Midwives and CHEW on gender-based violence (GBV) and scale up the primary support for clinical management of sexual violence, including the provision of post-rape care, counseling, and referral.
4. Support and engage targeted communities through community-based mechanisms, contributing to creating behavioral change and positive attitude regarding reproductive health, family planning as well as prevention of GBV. Although the primary target of the project is women and girls, Tdh will ensure awareness-raising among the entire community.
Tdh will also ensure the prevention of major communicable diseases, including Cholera and COVID-19.

1 - Needs

Tdh conducted a rapid multisectoral needs assessment, from the 16th to the 19th of June 2020.Data collection comprised of a total of 147 households (HH) interviews. All population groups residing in the area were assessed, including host communities, IDP, and returnees. The finding evidence that 28% of HHs are women-headed households and therefore are more likely to be vulnerable (for the reasons explained in the following section). These vulnerabilities hinder their access to public services, among which health services. This is a concerning issue, as 47% of the HH in Rann host an average of 1 pregnant woman and 13% of these pregnant women are under 18 years old. Besides, 58% of the women deliver at home and don’t necessarily have access to the appropriate equipment for delivery, which is a factor that contributes to mother and newborn mortality. Even though 91% of women deliver with the assistance of health workers or TBA, this doesn’t unequivocally ensure that the deliveries are performed safely and cleanly.In addition, at the onset of the Tdh presence in the community, child spacing/ Family planning was an idea that was not welcomed since many believed it was against their religious belief. With joint efforts through community awareness and sensitization, enlighten community stakeholders, and religious leaders in the AQIC meeting, more women are now requesting child spacing programs. However, Tdh’s recent assessment highlighted that 70% of women in Rann still don’t prevent or delay pregnancy, which might indicate that they don’t know how or don’t have the means to do so (among other possibilities such as not wanting to do so). Efforts remain to be done in that matter and this project seeks to scale up reproductive health programs to further include family planning activities.According to the service mapping that was conducted by the Rann Coordination group, no health partner is currently providing activities relating to SGBV, especially the clinical management of Rape and according to the GBV sub-sector an estimated 1.3M people need GBV services, of these, 45 percent are girls and 34 percent are women. In Mafa, IDPs (46% of households, Tdh data) outnumber host communities (45% of households). IDPs have often fled local Boko Haram violence from their different communities and are residing in camps and Host communities within Mafa. An influx of IDPs has increased the demand for health care services thus making the Health systems overwhelmed, and health staff overstretch to the reduced human Recourses. Only 17% of women in Mafa deliver children with a skilled birth attendant, while 83% prefer to deliver at home or with a traditional birth attendant. Referral pathways are lacking as both the secondary and tertiary healthcare facilities are located in Maiduguri, the state’s Capital with a minimum travel time of 1h30 to access secondary/ tertiary level care, only when there a means of transportation and funds to cater for the cost of specialized care.The FGD conducted in September 2020, with representatives of the affected population across the various social groups highlighted that Within GSSS IDP Camp, there is no access to maternal and child healthcare (MCHc) service at night and during the weekends because 24/7 services are not provided and due to the security constraints and imposed curfew, IDP residing within the Camp are unable to go outside the camp to seek healthcare in cases of medical emergencies.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will ensure women's participation while selecting the community health volunteers to ensure it is gender-sensitive. In addition, the timing of health promotion sessions and other committee meetings will be considered to ensure suitability.Close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by Tdh to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material, and as far as possible key actors working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation, and interests. Before and after each session group play activities will test participants’ understanding of specific issues, if needed, topics will be re-discussed. After the training cycle is completed, Tdh staffers initially will facilitate regular meetings with the community members for the establishment of CPFP and the subsequent design of an active work plan and afterward to follow up on the action plan and its results. The CPFPs will be closely collaborating with Tdh caseworkers at each step of the activity’s implementation. Tdh facilitator will ensure continued assistance and support in the development of community designed and led actions to avoid imposing external model but to build on sustainable solutions Including the prevention and response to GBV in the proposed project, Tdh ensures that the guiding principles are respected in the planification and implementation. Indeed, Tdh will honor safety and security, by ensuring the safety of the survivor, child, and family and will maintain the awareness of safety and security of people who are helping the survivor. Tdh will also respect the confidentiality of the survivor, child, and family at all times. Relevant information on the case will be shared with other humanitarian actors for helping purposes if and only if the survivor agrees to it. Tdh will indeed seek patient consent before engaging in any response or sharing of information. If the survivor is not able to read and write, he/she will provide his/her consent verbally. Tdh will also comply with the respect of the patient: the patient will be provided with information on options available to them and Tdh will maintain a non-judgment behavior. Tdh will provide its support to GBV cases without discrimination based on age, sex, religion, ethnicity, language, etc. Through awareness-raising, Tdh engages the community fully in understanding and promoting gender equality and gender power relations that protect and respect the rights of women and girls. Tdh will also ensure equal and active participation by women and men, girls, and boys in assessing, planning, implementing, monitoring, and evaluating programs through the systematic use of participatory methods (this will for example be done through the AQIC committee).Besides, Tdh will ensure that the promotion among the staff of Tdh’s safeguarding principles. Lastly, the staff will be trained on security, PSEA policy as well as bribery and corruption policy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The accountability and quality improvement committee (AQIC) composed of community members, plans community actions, implements, and acts as a feedback mechanism between Tdh and the community. This committee has had monthly meetings since its inception and had discussed several issues affecting the community. For example, it had been reported that men were not satisfied that the project would only address MNCH and that men were not provided with health kits, Tdh had addressed this issue, emphasizing on the project’s objectives, Tdh technical expertise and had explained the referral pathways.During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to health were also aired, and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners. The AQIC committee is currently acting as community sensitization agents for behavioral change. This feedback mechanism will continue to be in place and functional as Information about the project will be shared using pictorial materials. The Tdh team is gender-balanced and speaks the local languages to ensure the information about the project is disseminated through community leaders. By doing so the target population will receive clear, relevant, and timely information regarding the project. The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback mechanism that can be received using various means, such as FGD, interviews, and feedback boxes. Information on relevant services including referral pathway if available will be shared in coordination with the Protection team and WASH teams. Tdh as a coordinator of the humanitarian actors In Rann will ensure the same is done by other actors working in Rann.
