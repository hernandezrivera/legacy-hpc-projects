Project Summary

The proposed interventions aim to ensure that children and youth (boys and girls) have access to quality education and those already in schools are retained including children and youth with disabilities in line with INEE Minimum Standards for Education based on HRP priorities for 2019 and SCP CSP 2019. SCI targets to enroll 46,790 (25,818 boys; 13,902 girls) primary school age leaners in 75 primary schools Jonglei (Nyirol 15; 10 Uror 10 Akobo west; 10 Bor South); Upper Nile (10 Maban) and Lakes (10 Rumbek Centre; 10 Rumbek East). The programme will further target 2,500 (1,625 boys; 875 females) youth will life skills and business skills in 18 drop-in centres and 2,500 (1,625 boys; 875 females) young children in 60 ECD centres in all five locations including CES (Juba) and EE. SCI will focus to deliver life-saving education interventions and learning opportunities through the provision of safe learning spaces, education supplies, psychosocial supports (PSS), capacity building and back to learning school campaign including provision of school meals and take home ration for adolescent girls. SCI will link these interventions with other existing grants (NORAD, UNICEF and DANIDA) grants. SCI is commit to reaching vulnerable children by adopting flexible or alternative modality of delivering education. SCI will undertake multi sectoral and crosscutting components such us gender, protection, disability, child participation and accountability in their activities. Strong advocacy will be carried out to ensure prioritization and protection of education throughout the crisis. Monitoring will be done joint with the Child Protection cluster as a way of mapping and updating education facilities used as shelter by IPDs and/or attacked.

Needs

2,808, 299 children aged 3-18 years, 51% boys, and 49 % girls; and 42,902 adults i.e. teaching personnel (80% M, 20 % F). The total number of people estimated to be in need of emergency education assistance in 2019 is over two million, an increase of 66.5 % as compared to 2018 (HNO, 2018). The total population in all the target 7 counties is 735,510 and out of this, 315,845 (42.9%) are in need of education (HNO 2018). Over the years, conflict, widespread insecurity, economic pressures and food insecurity has severely worsened education across country. The results of the Education Cluster needs assessment survey (Sep-Oct 2018) indicated 21 % of schools to be non-functional and insecurity was the major cause behind school closure (HNO, 2018). Many children have lost almost an entire school year, and reintegration will become harder the longer children stay out of school. The two biggest reasons for girls’ dropout are child early forced marriage and early pregnancy. The marriage dowry is a lucrative incentive for parents to marry daughters off at early age, especially in rural areas. Families favour sending boys to school. Girls’ domestic responsibilities within the household increase with age, which leaves less time for girls to attend school and study. Fear of attacks on girls by men and boys between home and school keeps girls home. Lack of facilities, latrines and sanitary napkins inhibit girls’ attendance. All these children out of school particularly susceptible to dangerous labour practices, recruitment into armed groups and other negative coping mechanisms such as crime, substance abuse and perpetuating gender based violence. The low capacity of teachers limits provision of safe, protective and accessible learning environment for children with special needs including the fact that most teachers have been displaced and lack payment. In many ways, the goals to provide basic education for children of primary school age in all locations remains a daunting challenge, given the current very low enrolment, attendance rates, completion rates and attainment of functional literacy and numeracy. Many children show signs of ill health and are frequently absent from school while others drop out to search for food or income. The displacements in most of the target locations has had a direct bearing on school attendance, especially in rural areas. Lack of food as the number one reason for dropping out from school (Education Cluster, 2017). In this circumstance, adolescents and girls are pulled out of school first so that they can work or find ways to increase the food security in the family. Likewise, as the food and nutrition in a household suffer, children are less likely to attend school regularly for lack of energy and ability to concentrate and thrive in the classroom. Girls are affected most acutely by this phenomenon, as they are the first ones to be pulled out for work, early marriage or if a family cannot afford to send more than one child to school. Adolescents with income-generating ability are also impacted significantly and dropouts at the P4 and above level are increasing in the current food crisis climate.

Activities

Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies 1. As a strategy to increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education services, community engagement and mobilization will be done and have all target children enrol in school. 2. The youth will be trained in life skills and business skills at the drop-in centres. 3. TLS and gender segregated latrines will be established or/and rehabilitated in selected schools. 4. All targeted schoolchildren and youth will be provided a fortified meal and adolescents will be provided take home rations. 5. ECD children at the ECD centres will be provided with ECD kits. Establish and rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms. 6. The intervention will improve the quality of learning for all learners. Children and youth will with quality education and recreational materials. 7. Teachers will be trained on EiE skills, PSS and child centred pedagogies including referral pathways on protection/GBV, and health and nutrition. 8. The Response capacity of target communities and education actors will be strengthened to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth through training of all PTAs and education authorities on EiE skills, PSS, leadership, management, roles and responsibilities, referral pathways on protection/GBV, and health and nutrition.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Save the Children recognizes the right of beneficiaries and community members to give feedback and seek response from projects affecting them. In this response, the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will deliver a mechanism that enables the beneficiaries and community members to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of this response and other development actors in their communities, in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible. The initiative will promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to other development actors. Consultative meetings will held with the learners, communities and local leaders. The beneficiaries and community members will be encouraged to give feedback and seek responses from the projects affecting them. They will be sensitized right from the start of the project through kick off meetings and community consultation workshops on key deliverable’s including community roles and responsibilities.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Working with the protection cluster and other development partners, SCI will activate in consultation with protection in line with the IASC Commitments on Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. It will put in place a collective mechanism to ensure participation of affected people in planning and programming of humanitarian interventions, and to regularly monitoring of their satisfaction and priorities. It will adopt a collective Code of Conduct and the establishment of a network of focal points for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. SCI will put in place reporting mechanisms and guidelines to ensure that cases of suspected child abuse and exploitation of adults and children are immediately reported and followed up on, through our clear guidelines for reporting. Any allegation of abuse or exploitation of an adult or child associated with a Save the Children program will lead to the immediate suspension and removal from the location of the accused individuals.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Views and feedback will be collected from the community beneficiaries, school authorities, teachers and children from the start (design) of the intervention. Key informant interviews will done among the local leaders, learners and community leaders over the period. All stakeholders will be involved in all assessments including assessment among IDPs, host community and other interest groups. In essence, SCI will approach the principle of participation from equity perspective and advocate for equal participations of different groups. Based on the cluster framework, the project will put in place and implement and monitoring and reporting system, progress, best practices and lessons learned will be presented in cluster meetings based on the 5 Ws formats modified by the cluster.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The PTA and school authorities will take lead in school governance and school feeding activities. SCI will be transparent in all operations while involving the local authorities running all the project activities (planning, implementation, monitoring and reporting). The PTAs/SMCs together with Head teachers will be involved in monthly review actions to assess the progress in terms of challenges and prospects that will inform the action to be taken. This will be done through review sessions. Roles and responsibilities will be outlined clearly as a strategy and clear working framework to avoid conflicts and provide a framework where accountability can be discussed between SCI and the affected population.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project intervention’s will incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in provision of school meals and youth services. Safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm for the target beneficiaries will be the priority. The interventions will prevent and minimize unintended negative effects under this project, which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. The project will provide meaningful access to all the beneficiaries. Children, youth and target communities will access services without discrimination. This will target Individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing meals and youth services especially children with disabilities, girls and teenage mothers. Community and beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be set up and made functional at school and community levels. Children, youth, parents and communities will measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints. The project will support and enhance participation and empowerment of children, youth, school authorities and communities. This will support and develop their self-protection capacities and assist them to claim their rights, including not exclusively the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. SCI Child protection interventions training PTAs, teachers, children and youth on identification of children at risk will remain key intervention. Help desks will be established at school and drop-in centre levels and all cases identified will be referred to OTP sites (Nutrition) and PHCC centres (Health) established and managed by SCI under SSHF 2 and UNICEF funding. While holding school and community level consultative meetings, awareness campaigns and risk assessments, the project will identify protection needs and priorities. It will document and report protection violations taking place.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Parents will be support to reinforce positive methods for bringing up children including positive discipline practices. Working with PTA and respective communities, the intervention will address safety of learners to and from school especially girls taking the home ration. This will include travel of learners to and from school especially the girls benefiting from the take home ration. Schools will print and post rules against sexual harassment, exploitation, abuse and other forms of gender based violence. The project will link interventions of training teachers, school authorities and other personnel through the ongoing integrated initiatives. The training will focus on psychosocial support and wellbeing. This will be cascaded to the learners through structured learning, use of child friendly methods, play/recreation, teaching life skills and referrals. The teachers will participate in enhancing positive classroom management while the communities will take a lead role in creating, sustaining and protecting the learning environment. Coordination for protection mainstreaming will be undertaken through inter cluster coordination, joint assessment’s, information sharing, joint advocacy and ensuring protection is mainstreamed into school meals and youth interventions.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

SCI has its own accountability systems, founded on Humanitarian Accountability Partnership Standards on accountability and quality management, and IASC recommended principles and values. SCI conduct community sensitization and information sharing sessions on project activities with girls, boys, women and men, together with community and government leaders at the onset of implementation, to ensure communities are well informed about the project and to receive feedback from beneficiaries and communities. SCI seek and consider the views of beneficiaries and community members throughout the project to ensure that their feedback and complaints are addressed in an effective and timely manner. SCI has an established complaint response mechanism (CRM) with two components a) beneficiary complaint/feedback collection mechanism and b) complaint handling and response mechanism. In addition, accountability materials are displayed at different service delivery sites and translated into local language. Learning is captured through program team meetings, program reviews with project stakeholders, findings from monitoring visits, surveys and capturing of best practice and beneficiary case studies.
