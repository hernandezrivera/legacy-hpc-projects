1 - Needs

Blue Nile state is classified as a war-affected state since the last conflict erupted in September 2011. The conflict has resulted in massive new displacements and badly deteriorated the basic services and impacted the social wellbeing situation in the state. According to OCHA 35,000 are estimated to be in need of protection assistance. These vulnerable groups lost their homes and shelters and they have received very limited support from humanitarian actors. There were many cases reported of massive violations of girls, boys, women and men rights. The state is undeserved specifically in relation to the social welfare and emergency shelter gaps. The insecurity has also contributed to restricting access to the vulnerable groups who need life-saving emergency assistance. Up until 2018, approximately 47,392 people are estimated to have been displaced mainly because of conflict and sought shelter primarily in urban settlements in Blue Nile state. BN localities, most of the villages in rural areas have been abandoned due to insecurity and fighting, with displaced people currently living in outskirts of towns or gathering near major urban settlements. The fact that most IDPs are living in urban or semi-urban settings makes it difficult to identify their numbers and exact scope of protection and other needs. South Kordofan is one of the three states of the Kordofan region. Between 2011 and 2016, the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the SPLM-N fought for control over parts of South Kordofan State resulting in the displacement of thousands of people. This resulted in massive displacement within and outside Rashad Locality. Currently, there are 22,324 IDPs composed of 3,576 households living in displacement in Rashad. They comprise of 12,520 females, 9,794 males and 4,702 children under five years of age. IDPs originated from Tabdoun, Terre, Tagelbo, Um Darawa, Tendimin, El Beyeera, El Mangala, Serein, Alsaraf, Woroula, Douma, El Mansour, El Moglum and Keleiro. (IRNA Report: Rashad, South Kordofan, Sudan, Oct. 2018). As per the Inter-Agency Rapid Need Assessment conducted in October 2018; There are 38 schools in Rashad locality, only 7 of them in Rashad town and 31 are in locality villages and towns. Most of the IDPs in Rashad life near schools but are unable to access them because of lack of school fees, textbooks, and other learning materials as well as school uniform besides the cost of school meals. The affected population was displaced locally from 14 villages in four localities mainly Abu Kershola, Al Abassiya, Rashad and Heiban and were re-settled by the GoS in thirteen locations within Abu Kershola locality. IOM verified 7,007 individuals of IDPs (1,530 men, 1,701 women, 1,964 girls, and 1,812 boys) who remain in displacement and are unable to return to their places of initial displacement. IDPs get part of their food from limited farming in areas the food production is inadequate due to the poor state of land and lack of farming implements. There is a significant humanitarian needs in West Kordofan, WK is one of the three states of the Kordofan region. Due to the conflict among different tribes and disputes towards the resources and areas exposed by flash floods, the Sudanese nationals migrated from Darfur to West Kordofan states and. Right now Elmeiram District and Gebaysh locality reported a considerable number of peoples shifted from different states of Darfur region and South Sudan country and Internal displacement (IDPs) from nearby localities such Elodaya, Elnhuod, Elmuglad and Abeyai, resulting in raising the prices of the entire commodities and pressure on the basic services.

Child Protection: 1. Conduct campaign awareness sessions for protection issues (GBV, FGM, Child rights)2. Training of child protection Humanitarian workers on PSS.3. Direct and focused PSS provision to children through the CBPSS methodologies 4. Direct PSS to children, using traditional games and local material 5. Provision of PSS to excluded children (children in Khalwa's, children with a disability)6. Build the capacity and skills of PTA members and Teachers on child protection, psychosocial support, disaster risk reduction. WASH intervention: • Rehabilitation of 20 nonfunctional hand pumps (5 in each state) • Distribution of 2 sets of maintenance tool kits for hand pump repaired. • AORD will work with Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) groups to mobilize and sensitize the target communities on the need for safe sanitation and the availability of latrines in their communities through awareness-raising sessions. The community CLTS groups will work with their respective communities to identify target locations. • Training 500 P (250 M, 250 W) in Community hygiene promoters on WASH-related diseases and practice good hygiene. • Conduct 20 awareness sessions on environ issue and correct disposal techniques for Solid waste. • Conduct hygiene promotion awareness in 30 Schools with deliver to schools Hand wash facilities with soap. Education activities: 1. Constructing (10) Semi-permanent Classrooms in the existing schools 2. Rehabilitation of (10) existing classrooms with (10) gender-sensitive latrines and constructing (10) handwashing facilities. 3. Provide the constructed (10) Semi-classrooms in targeted 10 schools with gender & environment-sensitive (10) drinking water facilities and the needed learning material. 4. PTAs will be established in each targeted school, members 50% to be women. Will receive training on institutionalization, EIE, School management, child safety, and protection. 5. Advocacy camping on girls’ education. FSL activities: 1-Provision of Veterinary and livestock. 2-Agricultural training and distribution of seeds. 3- Arrangement of training and provision of treatment kits for community health workers 4-provision and distribution of tools 5-Provision of food assistance through in-kind, voucher or cash during or in the aftermath of a shock 6-Building, restoring and maintaining productive assets, through provision of inputs and capacity building for climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary services to vulnerable small-scale farmers

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project will be implemented in three states BN, SK, and WK. This project will work on components of (WASH, Education,FSL and CP). The project considering the environment throughout the project cycle phases, with good environmental planning and management purpose to reduce short-term risks to those affected by the disaster, as well as supporting disaster risk reduction and reducing exposure to natural hazards in the future, and hence decreasing household and community vulnerability. Within our project activities; 1.Awareness Raising: Include environmental awareness sessions in projects, and support solid waste management schemes. 2. Ensure WASH Facilities in schools are adapted to the child’s age to guarantee the proper use of facilities and reduce environmental risks due to standing water in connection to hand-washing stations, or open defecation 3. For construction and rehabilitation of classrooms consider using environmentally friendly materials and encourage community members to plant the shadow trees around the school for the fencing to keep school more attractive as well as fruit trees. 4. Mainstream environmental issues in project activities.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

AORD organization will follow non-discriminatory and impartial approach that promotes the protection, safety and dignity of the people receiving assistance. AORD will deliver the intended services in consistency with humanitarian principles and encompasses human rights-based programming that recognizes the beneficiaries’ rights. AORD will carefully avoid, minimize, reduce any unintended negative consequences or impact of its intervention and is committed. ‘Do no Harm’ Equality, accountability to affected population approach within its programming. Complaints boxes at facility and community level, community volunteers, PTAs, and Community-based Child Protection Network are the mechanism put in place by AORD for suggestions and complaints and reporting on SGBV or any other type of complaints, ensuring their proper periodic review, analysis and feedback. This intervention will strengthen the protective environment of vulnerable population particularly women and children from, IDPs, SSRs and host community as well. AORD will also mainstream protection concerns in activities like men and women need privacy during consultation, gender designated latrine sites inside the Schools to ensure that women and girls are protected and considering privacy. AORD will ensure that beneficiaries are consulted in the identification of needs and design of activities. AORD will also consider equal access to all sex, age, clan groups with meeting their specific needs. When managing data, AORD team will ensure confidentiality for the best interest of beneficiaries. The designed education and protection interventions will require the participation of all community members with their full rights and access to the delivered services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

AORD will engage the beneficiaries in all stages of the project to ensure accountability and transparency. The existing complaint and feedback mechanisms in AORD the system at the CO and field offices will be used to measure the satisfaction level of beneficiaries against services provided by AORD. The accountability phone is standing by to receive feedback and complaints from the project beneficiaries and stakeholders. The call-in number will be printed on posters and displayed at service outlet centers to easily access to callers when interested to deliver their feedback. The M&E and Quality Assurance team alongside the program, staff will conduct focus group discussions and community meetings to share project information with direct and indirect project beneficiaries and collect their views to respond accordingly for corrective action. All feedback and complaints received will be logged into the Complaints and Response system, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning Therefore will hold the quarterly meeting with stakeholders (beneficiaries, Counterparts) to review the project status of implementation to highlights the strengths of the lesson learn and weakness for the corrective action.
