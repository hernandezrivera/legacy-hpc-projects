Project Summary

Nearly two million Somalis live in conflict-affected and hard to reach rural areas of southern and central parts of the country, as well as in the contested Sool and Sanag regions, in conditions of vulnerability to displacement and protection concerns. Gender based violence (GBV) incidences remain high, especially among Internally Displaced (IDP) population. While Somalia has currently the lowest school enrolment rates in the world, especially among the girls, forced recruitment of boys as young as 12 years old to the armed/terrorist groups is among the main child protection concerns. The main objective of this proposed project is to reduce the vulnerability to the above protection concerns in Lower Juba, Benadir, Sool and Sanaag by promoting an institutional framework to reduce and respond to sexual and gender based violence (SGBV), decrease the incidence of violent conflict by training the communities on methods of peaceful co-existence and conflict resolution, and addressing child protection concerns. In order to address SGBV, the project will empower communities, equip them with tools to identify and report incidences of SGBV, and also provide material support to SGBV survivors in order to ensure that they are given an opportunity to earn income and achieve self-sufficiency. This is based on the assumption that poverty is often associated with SGBV and many times intersects with and reinforces gender inequality. To address insecurity and conflict, the project will train communities in mediation and peaceful co-existence. The mediation skills are expected to help the communities to negotiate even threats like forced recruitment of children. The project will address the abuses of child rights by sensitising the communities on child rights, including right to education, and on harms of child labor. It will also provide psychosocial support and counselling to children victims of abuse and those rescued from armed groups. The project will target both men and women, with a special focus on including female-headed households (HH), widows, youth, and marginalized groups, mentally physically disabled as well as gatekeepers, local leaders and key decision makers.

Needs

Armed clashes between Puntland and Somaliland have exacerbated displacement in the regions of Sool and Sanaag. Benadir and Lower Juba are also suffering from extending state of conflict, including the active conflict between government forces and Al Shabaab (AS). In the same time, after a prolonged state of drought, recent rains have both provided relief as well destruction of infrastructure thus negatively impacting the security situation for the already marginalized community. The Multi-cluster needs assessment preliminary findings indicate a slight improvement in the overall protection situation in the country however there are growing concerns in the increasing gaps of humanitarian service provisions. SGBV and child protection concerns are predominately common in many of the hotspot location hosting IDPs in northern, southern and Central Somalia. Forced recruitment or use of children has increased by 83% in 2018 compared to previous year. SGBV is exacerbated by conflict and humanitarian crisis, as people are more vulnerable and the capacity of the state to maintain law in order is compromised. The HRP 2018 confirms that many HH have been forced to engage into harmful practices like early forced marriages to provide income for the parents and “reduce their burden of having to care for the girl “. Other forms of SGBV can be said to Domestic violence, including wife beating and verbal abuse, Denial of access to resources and decision-making, Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) has also been promoted by some actors that see this as a way to make a living when other livelihoods have been undermined. There is also the perception that girls that have undergone FGM are harder to rape, creating an incentive to perform FGM in insecure environments.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Given the lack of objective legal services for SGBV survivors, ARC will work with relevant stakeholders including local authorities to strengthen formal justice systems such as the police service to better enforce existing legislation around sexual violence as well as build their capacity so that they project can continue after ARC close out. The project will also provide economic interventions such as cash support and vocational skills training and start up grants to SGBV so that they become productive and self-sufficient. The project strives to create and strengthen community based mechanisms to prevent and respond to protection concerns. This will be achieved by training community members (women, men, girls and boys), each group with tailored message aiming for behavioural change and providing information about mechanisms to address GBV. Increased knowledge on solutions enables achievement of durable solutions by enhancing community engagement in provision of service. The Action will not only target girls and women but also train different categories of men (police, elders, religious leaders etc.) who are not only perpetrators but also decision makers on importance of preventing and responding to SGBV. The project will be implemented with focus on survivor-centered approach that prioritizes survivor wishes, needs and rights hence adhering to the principles of confidentiality, non-discrimination, respect, safety and security based on the international IASC guidelines. This Action focuses on strengthening coordination mechanism and/or referral pathways, and strengthening the capacity to provide quality and comprehensive case management services including psychosocial support (PSS) and primary healthcare attention, and in the same time empowering the survivors of SGBV and other forms of violence and human right abuses to seek these services.In order to address forced child recruitment by armed groups, ARC will initiate community sensitisation campaigns and provide the communities with peaceful mediation and negotiation techniques to overcome conflict situations and threats.ARC is fully conscious about beneficiary accountability and right to access quality services by the beneficiaries. A beneficiary satisfaction survey mechanism called Kuja Kuja (www.kujakuja.com) will be put in place to collect beneficiary feedback and to improve service delivery. Furthermore, ARC protection sector will provide technical support to other program areas to mainstream SGBV prevention measures and to take measures to prevent SGBV incidences and enhance existing protective structures throughout all activities and projects (health, nutrition, wash and livelihoods) with the aim of reducing risk factors in accordance with Zero tolerance policy.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

ARC will prevent risks of exclusion based on displacement by ensuring that internally displaced persons benefit from the interventions just like the host communities. The project will target women, men, girls and boys in both IDP camps and host communities so that equity and equality is maintained at all levels. In actualizing this, there are risks that ARC will need to mitigate so that service provision to the most deserving communities is not hindered. Major humanitarian crisis such as drought due to climate change, disease outbreaks and floods may divert attention from project implementation- Crisis modifiers will be built in consultation with the donor to allow the project to adjust and appropriately respond to such a crisis.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

More often than not, the risk of exclusion based on gender and age is as a result of lack of not only consultation but also participation of women, which remains a major problem in most regions of Somalia. ARC strongly believes in consultations with beneficiaries both at the informal level in the context of its activities, and through establishing mechanisms (IDP committees, etc.). Additionally, ARC follows an annual process aimed at discussing needs and possible actions together with the beneficiaries in the context of focus groups representing men, women, boys, girls, elderly refugees, etc. This process aims at ensuring that all groups are provided with an opportunity to express their views and participate in project design. ARC field staff undertakes regular Participatory Assessments in cooperation with partners, district authorities and cluster members. ARC appreciates the Age Gender Disability Mainstreaming methodology, which is a community-based approach that allows women, girls, boys and men in the community to participate in a process and to express their needs and to decide their own future. The AGDM depart from a rights based approach.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The widespread use of violence has caused massive physical and social catastrophe for the people in Somalia. Years of violent conflict and warfare have not only shattered lives and livelihoods of the Somali population, but have also severely destroyed the social fabrics of the society. Social cohesion and relationships have been weakened and the capacity of the communities to resolve local disputes and conflicts has immensely reduced. Through the adoption of "Do No Harm" approaches, efforts are made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs are implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of Somalia conflict situation. ARC believes that understanding the situation and the context in which we operate is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and to avoid negative consequences in the operation area. As a part of the Protection project, ARC will carry out community capacity building to promote peaceful co-existence and conflict resolution. Insecurity arising from extremist groups and inter-clan clashes through contextual understanding and working with the community to acquire information about threats; ARC will maintain a high level of acceptance at community level and with key stakeholders and decision makers. ARC will continue monitoring the security situation and trends in the region, analyse risks (their likelihood and impact), review mitigation measures, and integrate the findings in to ARC’s Safety and Security Management Plan, and ensure that all staff are trained on and adhere strictly to our security guidelines.
