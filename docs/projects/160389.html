Project Summary

The project intends to provide a holistic, comprehensive and intersectoral response to the problems of the Education System in EES and Lakes State, starting from the assumption that schools represent platforms through which also Protection, Nutrition, Hygiene, Health and Psychosocial needs of vulnerable children can be addressed. Moreover, AVSI response wants to support the entire education system, starting from nursery and primary school, up to secondary and university program, and including the alternative programs offered by the Accelerating Learning Program (ALP). As the main sponsor of St. Mary College, the only institution in the country offering a 3-year diploma program devoted exclusively to the training of primary school teachers, AVSI has contributed to mitigate the downfall in the public and private investment for the training of new teachers and dealt with the challenges of providing in-service teacher training to primary schools. Finally, after 14 years of experience in running a comprehensive and personalized scholarship program for primary and secondary students in South Sudan, AVSI has refined an approach that has proved to reduced dropout, improve cognitive results, and promote female education.
Therefore, learners and teachers from all the different steps of education system will be supported in an integrated way, by providing scholastic materials, training and sensitization sessions on subjects such as education, child protection, SGBV, health, psychological well-being. Structural interventions will be done in schools to improve the quality and safety of the learning environments, i.e. Temporarily Learning Spaces, renovation of classrooms, construction of latrines and school kitchens, erection or rehabilitation of fences. Schools will be used as an entry point to promote good hygiene and health practices through hygiene sessions, HIV/AIDS prevention and reproductive health sessions. The students and teachers will also be supported in improving their food security status, by receiving training and agricultural inputs for school gardens for their daily consumption.
On the basis of a holistic concept of education, the project aims to involve also the other relevant actors in this process, first of all the parents and the communities at large. For these reason, parents, community members, PTAs & SMCs will be involved in Campaigns, Open Days and meetings to promote the importance of education and discuss about sensitive topics such as education for girls, violence and SGBV, child protection, among other. The objectives of the project are:
• To improve the quality of education and to ensure access to safe learning spaces for vulnerable children, adolescents and youth
• To provide access for children, adolescents and youth to integrated social protection services
• To promote resilience and capacity building of teachers, parents and community at large

1 - Needs

The state of primary education in SSD reflects the quasi-collapse of supply of public education, which peaked in 2017, when payment to teachers halted and the coverage of a temporary teacher payment program, IMPACT, was still limited. As one of the main EiE partner with UNICEF, AVSI has witnessed the state of emergency of primary education – the lack of facilities and the high dropout and turnover of teachers and pupils being cases in point. Nationally, as per the education cluster’s 2018 national assessment, some improvements have been made where 80.4% of schools were functional. However, insecurity, lack of teachers and insufficient teaching (30% of teachers were absent on day of assessment) and learning materials are still found to be the major reasons for school closures across the country. In SSD, 40% of enrolled students are girls. Nationally, reasons for drop out and non-attendance are dominated by lack of food and for girls more specifically due to marriage, pregnancy and domestic duties.AVSI’s May 2019 research conducted in 50+ primary schools supported in EES found that in some schools up to 1/3 of students were previously enrolled in another institute; similar high turnover is found among teachers. These figures, along with the severe teacher absenteeism underlined by the SSEC’s latest National Assessment (up to 40%), provides a measure of the challenges ahead. The lack of adequate teaching, learning materials and functioning school infrastructures, both in term of quantity and quality continues to be a major challenge. Based on AVSI’s experience in more than 60 schools supported in 2019 across SSD, the average enrolment for P1 and P2 classes is 25% less compared to P3 and P5 classes. Likewise, dropout rates are greater for lower classes, especially for P1 pupils.Girls are another vulnerable group of students, both in terms of enrolment (high masculinity rate) and dropout rates. By analysing 33 schools across EES, AVSI found that there is a gender gap in terms of enrolment (not much noticeable in Torit, Magwi and Ikwoto, where the masculinity rate ranges from 53% in P1 to 59% in P8, remarkable in Kapoeta, and very severe in Cueibet - around 70%) and in terms of dropout (very widespread across EES and Lakes, especially among students enrolled in higher classes). The mentioned weaknesses are strongly exacerbated by the lack and turnover of teachers and pupils. As revealed by AVSI’s 2018 Greater Ikwoto Multisector survey, this problem is very much felt by families, who report the lack of teachers as being the second most important reason for not enrolling children in schools. Additionally, AVSI gathers information on the conditions of the schools. By so doing, AVSI found confirmation of the alarming WASH conditions, whereby the baseline conducted in 10 primary schools in Cueibet supported with UNICEF-funding showed that the average number of pupils par latrine to be 150, well above the SHPERE standard.Finally, according to IPC analysis of August 2019, in EES an estimated 560,000 people (52.6% of the state population) were facing a food security phase of Crisis or Emergency (Phases 3 and 4). Very similar prospects were also shown for LS where an estimated 660,000 people (57.9% of the state population) were facing a food security phase of 3-5. The latest round of FSNMS data (June 2019) found that on average 69% and 72% of boys and girls respectively, between the ages of 5 and 17 ate 1 cooked meal per day in Cueibet. At the school level, the nutritional status of pupils is closely monitored by AVSI. By gathering anthropometric measures in June 2019 AVSI found that in 4 out of 10 schools of Cueibet more than 50% of children demonstrated severe thinness; non-supported schools are very likely to be worse. All reported surveys and assessments conducted by AVSI can be found at: https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/south-sudan/assessments/organizations/avsi-foundation.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

AVSI’s effort in the field are oriented to ensure the respect of human rights to life and education. The combination of such response aims at fostering the conditions for people to know their rights and to have the means to make them.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

AVSI conducts annually survey to review the conditions and views of alumnus and teachers. In the context of such survey it was found that in counties like Magwi, where more pronounced had been the displacement, nearly 40% of students reported to have lived in (not too far way) refugee camps over the course of the last two years. More recently, in the context of a projects that aim to support the socio-economic reintegration of returnees, implemented along with its sister organization AVSI Uganda, which works in the refugee camps just across the border, AVSI conducted a long survey based on open ended questions to review how displaced people move across the borders, and what kind of support they would need to find in South Sudan to have a chance to return safe and resettle successfully. The project under which was conducted such survey offers vouchers to returnees families to enrol children to primary schools.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

AVSI, given its steady presence in EES since 2005, has had the opportunity to establish over the years a deep relationship with local authorities, both at the county and at the state level. AVSI foresees the presence of staff that support local authorities in defining needs, implementing intervention strategies and putting into action planned activities. Additionally, AVSI improves the capacity of community stakeholders, through proved community mechanisms (PTA, Hygiene Clubs, local government) and a minimum and constant supervision and support thanks to ever-present support staff.AVSI also closely cooperates with UNICEF and local authorities in project implementation, not only in order to follow one of its pillars, which puts attention on the concept of “working with” the partners and beneficiaries, but also in order to follow the South Sudan Minimum Standards for Education, as urged by the National Education Cluster.AVSI’s interventions have the purpose of strengthening the local governance structure, making the project more sustainable and durable over time, both by nurturing and strengthening the capacity for management, organization and monitoring of the educational situation in SSD.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project supports all objectives of the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan for 2020 and more specifically, the education cluster’s objectives to improve access to safe and protective education services; to improve the quality of education services at all levels to all children; and to strengthen of capacity of the community and education actors. In addition to the structural enhancement of schools, provision of material, capacity building of teachers, among many other activities to improve overall quality of learning and increased, stable school attendance, AVSI also aims to improve the food security, health, protection and education conditions in South Sudan through prioritized school feeding provisions and WASH interventions in areas where needs are most severe and no school feeding is provided. The provision of food in schools, in fact, as confirmed by the SSEC National Assessment in 2018, is one of the most effective tools to attract and retain a significant number of young people to school, where it is provided a first response to their basic needs in terms of food security, safety and health, as well as education, and ultimately in line with national objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The conditions of protection are far from stable, let alone safe. Especially in Lake state, abuses are common and need of mental support are high. In Ikwoto county, AVSI survey found that around 15% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physically abuses and harsh punishments, 8% was discriminated against, and 7% sexually abused. In Torit county, around 11% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physical abuse (the double of what was found in Ikwoto County, EES), 2% were sexually abused, and 7% do drugs or engage in other deviant practices. Bur seems to be the place where children are more subject to abuse. In Cueibet county, around 37% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physically abuses (the double of what was found in Ikwoto County, EES), 7% was sexually abused, and 12% does drugs or engages in other deviant practices. Moreover, mental distress is also quite much more common than in EES, with almost half of the population seeking the repeated intervention of some healer (whose qualifications are difficult to assess but presumably inadequate). 40% of Cueibet’s families, moreover, report that women are face specif risks, physical assault, rape and forced marriage being among the most common. In the face of such threats, leveraging on the expertise gained in relation to its education projects, AVSI promotes the development of capacity in the field of protection for all its project officers, actively seeks the inclusion of women in all its interventions, including with local civil associations of women, and implements projects that integrate education components, as a means to provide protection to young children, and FSL projects, to ensure the sustainability of such support.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The involvement of the communities (in particular PTAs) and the strong collaboration with local authorities will ensure the institutional sustainability of the project. Additionally, authorities and partners operating in education will benefit from information sharing based on data gathered on educational outcome, and nutrition level of pupils. A strong capacity building of the involved local authorities will make them able to replicate the same methodology of implementation in future. Moreover, the engagement of PTAs members will create a sense of ownership and belonging toward the project, thereby assuring the project's continuity and sustainability in the best interest of children and, at the same time, it will guarantee a continuation of the activities since the members of the communities will be involved right from the beginning as key actors in the implementation of the project and not just as mere beneficiaries of the outputs delivered.The project will also enhance the role that AVSI is playing at State level, as lead of the education cluster in Eastern Equatoria State. In EES the good relationship with the State Ministry of Education and the County Education Departments of Torit and Magwi County will help the project implementation to best achieve the goals planned.Moreover, AVSI uses such multi sector surveys to orient our holistic response in selected locations, as we simultaneously intervene in education, nutrition, FSL, WASH and some protection, in most of the counties where we are operative. The findings of such surveys are shared with key stakeholders including the various clusters, where we have an active participation, for individual or collective effective planning and response in the state.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Firstly, as is often reported from communities, humanitarian assistance is at times channeled to more organized communities, leaving other communities (even more vulnerable populations) less assisted. To minimize this, AVSI always attempts to cover large as well as small communities in both central and more peripheral locations. For the second risk, rather than lack of awareness of referral services, there is more a problem of lack of access to psychological support to traumatized children and victims of GBV. AVSI focuses on prevention and mitigation by providing safe spaces to children, promoting female education and gender inclusion in general. Lastly, beneficiaries may not access or use complaint and feedback mechanisms. To mitigate this risk, besides the annual surveys, AVSI has a tested system of Detailed Post- Distribution Monitoring which is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing, transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at the community at large. During each field visit AVSI personnel is required to fill apposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks and complaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes for unanimous reporting of abuses or complains.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 5 of such surveys which, along with other researches aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl. The most recent example of one of AVSI’s feedback mechanism happened in the Lake States in August where AVSI staff validated the finding of two researches on challenges faced by Persons with Disabilities (PwD) and on Gender Based Violence. The 3-day validation meeting was attended by key stakeholders including PwD. AVSI ensured that at least half of the 50 attendees were females in positions of authority at the institutional or community level.
