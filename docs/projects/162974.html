Project Summary

The vulnerability of women and girls to GBV is heightened in the Zimbabwean multi-hazard humanitarian crisis, where violence and discrimination directly related to the emergency crisis exacerbates pre-existing, persistent gender and social inequalities, as well as traditional harmful socio-cultural practices. Women and girls are disproportionately affected by the protection consequences of the climate change and economic hardship related challenges. As a result of these multiple factors, more than 1,255,000 women and girls will face increased risks of exposure to GBV in 2020.
GBV vulnerability assessments conducted between August and November 2019 reported that drought is contributing to modify daily routine patterns (e.g. water fetching and firewood collecting) which forces women and girls to walk long distance and spend long hours away from home, generating tension on the homestead, and leading to increased IPV. In the eastern provinces, where Apostolic faith sectarianism is predominant, girls are increasingly exposed to early marriage, in an attempt to appeal spirits responsible for the drought and economic hardship. Bordering towns also represent hotspots for GBV, as women and girls are exposed to trafficking and illegal migration, while female headed households become the most frequent target for SEA. IDPs in the eastern provinces continue to face increased GBV risks as they continue to be hosted at temporary camps with limited GBV risk mitigation measures. Women and girls with disabilities are also among the most vulnerable to GBV risks, as three times more prone to sexual gender-based violence, compared with others.
The protracted drought, compounded by the economic crisis, contributes to constraining the capacity of survivors to sustain transport fees to reach services. The gradually decreasing availability of basic health services further contributes to the pre-existing critically low access to Clinical management of sexual violence. From January to October 2019, 7.971 cases of Sexual violence were reported to health facilities (Data source: HMIS), with only 27.8% of total cases reporting within 72h. When available, GBV multi-sectoral services remain fragmented, and mostly available at provincial and district level, often resulting out of reach for those living in most remote areas. The proliferation of traditional healing systems deriving from the health system constraints, also results in an increased GBV risk (sex for treatment fee).
In order to respond to the heightened GBV concerns, UNFPA with support from international and local NGOs will scale up GBV mitigation and response interventions targeting a total of 323,040 individuals in 26 districts among the most vulnerable to GBV and SEA. The proposed interventions aim at:
1) Enhancing effectiveness of community-based GBV risk mitigation mechanisms;
2) Strengthening the availability and accessibility of life saving multi-sectoral survivor-centered GBV services for GBV survivors in the most hard to reach areas
3) Improving the capacity of GBV specialized and non-specialized humanitarian actors to integrate GBV and PSEA interventions across programs, to avoid unintended GBV consequences and ensure DO-NO Harm.

1 - Activities

1. Establish community-based mechanisms for GBV risk mitigation and protection from SEA, including:- Set up and operationalize 52 safe spaces (2 in each of the 26 targeted districts), targeting a total of 20,000 most vulnerable women and girls (385 reached in each safe space with PSS and livelihood activities) - Conduct Community-based GBV risk mitigation outreach, targeting 323,040 women and girls, boys and men in hard to reach areas, including 250 women and girls with disability- Distribute NFIs (15,000 Dignity kits, MHM kits, mama kits) to most vulnerable women and girls2. Scale up availability and accessibility of GBV life-saving multi-sectoral services (Health, PSS, Safety and legal), through:- Establish 26 roving mobile one stop centres in most hard to reach areas of the 26 targeted districts- Provide multi-sectoral services at mobile OSCs for 10,500 GBV survivors, including for 250 with disabilities3. Strengthen referral mechanisms for multi-sectorial GBV services, including:- Train 520 (20 x district) Community volunteers on GBV risk mitigation, GBV surveillance, and the referral pathway, including sign language, PFA and PSEA- Disseminate life-saving information on the referral pathway, through IEC materials, community radios, roadshows and other community mobilization tools.4. Scale up GBV risk mitigation and PSEA across all humanitarian response sectors, including:- Train 1,220 humanitarian aid workers, including 600 specialized and 620 non-specialized service providers on integration of GBV mitigation and PSEA into humanitarian action. The project interventions will be tailored to the specific needs of the targeted districts, and refined on the basis of the information received through FGDs and community members’ engagement. Safe spaces will be set up through a co-creation model, and in close consultation with women and girls, in order to understand the most critical needs and define specific PSS interventions within the safe spaces. Traditional Chiefs and village headmen will also be engaged throughout the process, to ensure that timelines and activities within safe spaces and other community-based programs are in line with locally acceptable socialization patterns and routines, and as a way to ensure community buy in towards transformative results. Women and girls with disabilities, as well as disability organizations, will be critical actors for the definition of specific project components and location sites, in an attempt to leave no one behind. Mobile GBV services will be provided through coordinated efforts of the multi-sectoral teams on the ground, in close consultation with community volunteers and cadres (Behavior change facilitators, Ward coordinators, Village health workers) in order to improve mobilization of community members and enhance service uptake in the areas that are hardest to reach. The existing community GBV surveillance system will be utilized as a project monitoring mechanism in order to ensure constant feedback from the recipient community on appropriateness of interventions and needs for adjustments. A survivor-centered approach will be applied throughout the project implementation in order to ensure vulnerabilities of most in need are not heightened as a result of the project.
