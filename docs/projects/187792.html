Project Summary

The intervention aims to enhance the resilience of crisis-affected populations in northern Syria for the upcoming two years, 2022 and 2023.
This response mainly falls under specific sector objectives 1 and 2, focusing on improving equitable access to basic services and enhancing access to livelihoods and job opportunities.
The project targets 220,443 beneficiaries ( 90,843 in 2022 and 129,600 in 2023) who are in need of ERL assistance, which includes people who are directly benefiting from the assistance and those who benefit from accessing rehabilitated infrastructures and enhanced services.
The type of assistance proposed within this intervention is:
- Rehabilitation of infrastructure including roads and markets and enhance basic services like waste removal and management.
- Provision of vocational and business skills training,
- Support MSMEs to scale up and create jobs,
- Create short-term jobs opportunities linked to infrastructure rehabilitation and to internships opportunities. In this activity, we will ensure to consult the local communities and other stakeholders for more informed design, mapping the services, and coordinating with local and international services providers in the area before implementation. Consideration to enhancing social cohesion will be ensured while designing and implementing this activity.
- Build the capacity of youth and enhance their opportunities to access employment through soft skills training and apprenticeships.
All activities are to take place in different locations in northwest Syria in northern and western rural of Aleppo and Idleb governorate.

4 - Needs

The final draft of Syria HNO 2022 estimates that 13.8M in 2022 and 15M in 2023 are in need some form of assistance in the ERL sector. For 2022, about 50% of the people in needs are children (under 17 years old) and elderly (above 60 years old). The many relevant reports have highlighted the following:- Economic activity declined by more than half compared to 2011 - the result of fragmentation, trade autarky and the depreciation of the Syrian Pound.- Deterioration of the HH’s ability to meet basic needs is observed across all population groups.- 58.9% reported they had a job in the last 3 months in NWS .- households across Syria are increasingly dependent on humanitarian aid and external networks.- The top need for 71% of HHs remain food baskets/bread, immediately followed by livelihoods (56%).- The pressure on the availability and affordability of key essential services has been additionally under strain.- 50% of respondents in NWS noted that, despite the availability in the markets, they cannot afford essential items, pointing to the instability and constant fluctuation of prices.- Between 2011 and 2019, the rate of individuals living in extreme poverty increased by 39 percent and there was a 65 percent decrease in purchasing power.- Rising debt has been observed across Syria and across all population groups since 2021, with price increases and loss of income as the two top reasons reported. 5 - Implementation of activities

- Roads rehabilitation for 70KM (30 KM in 2022 and 40 KM in 2023). This activity will be carried out through contractors and with cash for work modalities.- Removal of 60 tons of solid waste removal (30 in 2022 and 30 in 2023) and strengthening of sustainable waste management system for two communities. This activity will be carried out through contractors and with cash for work modality. - Support 3334 MSMEs (2134 in 2022 and 1200 in 2023) to be established/expanded. This activity includes mapping and assessment of MSMEs, identifying the needs, providing training and coaching, and providing financial support. 2984 are start-up kits.- Provide 3809 beneficiaries with vocational and/or business training (2309 in 2022 and 1500 in 2023). This includes labour market assessments, career orientation, and vocational training and /or business skills training.- Create 2300 short-term job opportunities (1000 in 2022 and 1300 in 2023). 900 are planned to be less than 3 months job opportunities (including paid internships opportunities) and the rest are linked to enhancing basic services delivery and rehabilitation of basic infrastructures.- Rehabilitation of 8 markets (3 in 2022 and 5 in 2023). This activity to be conducted through contractors and with cash for work modality.- Capacity building for 1000 unemployed youth (400 in 2022 and 600 in 2023) through provision of soft skills training and market-based apprenticeships opportunities. - Market functionality will be ensured.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Children are left at home with an unqualified caregivers during the daily training hours for the mothers.Mitigation:- In each training center there will be a child friendly spaces with a trained and qualified caregiver.Risk: women participating in the vocational training are exposed to GBV. Mitigation: - Ensure safe transportation of women to/ from training centers.- Organize separate session for women, and make sure of contracting with female trainers. - Ensure that multiple feedback and complaint mechanisms are in place and easy to access by all beneficiaries.Risk: Cash for Work beneficiaries working on rehabilitation of roads are exposed to risks of land mines explosive remnants. Mitigation: - Provision of Risk Education sessions to CFW beneficiaries, mainly Mine Risk Education when undertaking activities deemed to be risky.- Coordinate with partners concerned with Mine Action about the safety of the specific area in which the rehabilitation activities take place. Risk: Escalation of the security situation. Mitigation:- Prioritize vocational training in relatively stable areas. - Project implementation through partners, whose staff are embedded in the community and maybe able to retain access for a longer period, particularly for CFW activities. - Ensure there is a process established that allows consulting the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project has the following strategy to prevent/mitigate any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse:- Ensure that all newly recruited local staff and members of the affiliate workforce are provided with the knowledge and skills to handle SEA issues.- Make sure that all staff sign the Code of Conduct declaration. - Ensure that PSEA focal points are appointed in each targeted area.- Strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising and make sure that people of concern are aware of their rights and entitlements.- Coordinate with other actors on the ground and design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities and partners.- Include PSEA indicators in the MEAL tools to be monitored on a regular basis.- Ensure that all staff are enrolled with PSEA training.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Issues of HLP in terms of roads. Assumption: The rehabilitated roads are not new ones. Mitigation: All the roads will be repaired and rehabilitated, not constructed. Risk: Cash and/or assets assistance may be misused, stolen, or diverted. Assumption: All cash and asset assistance provided to the beneficiaries are used to cover their basic needs and for income-generating activities. Mitigation: - Conduct reference checks for BNFs in coordination with local authorities. - Limiting knowledge of cash movements and smaller/more frequent transfers.- Varying distribution days and locations.- Screening all vendors.-Agreements with BNFs.- Post-distribution monitoring and follow-up.- Ensure proper complaint and feedback mechanisms. Risk: Unintentional tension with community members as a result of beneficiaries’ selection.Mitigation:- Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent.- Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the local authorities and communities concerned.- Publicize the selection criteria.- Make sure that feedback mechanisms are in place. Risk: CFW may cause disruption to local labour markets.Mitigation:- The wage rates will not be above the average level at the targeted community.- We will avoid implementing CFW at times when they could compete with normal labour opportunities, e.g., harvest time.- We will observe the market before.
