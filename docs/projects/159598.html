Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to HRP’s Country Strategic Objective 3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The specific objectives of this project are as follows: • Improve food security and nutrition of 600 vulnerable households • To provide poor men and women with space and support to realize Income Generating Activities (IGAs) for self-reliance. The project aims to reduce the immediate food gap of 600 vulnerable households (HHs) (3420 individuals) by providing a bundle of food items for a period of 6 months to those HH’s with no/or very limited access and raise awareness on nutrition and health in order to improve eating habits and consumption, particularly for children and women. Voucher value and type is updated in this project and tailored to beneficiaries specific needs. Based on the feedback reported through PDM, and endline surveys from beneficiaries of previous projects from similar profile, almost all identified the amount is insufficient and majority expressed the need to cover grocery items in addtion to fresh food. The project also aims at enhancing the personal and household economic capacity of selected (100 HHs) vulnerable breadwinners, particularly women, who demonstrate entrepreneurial behaviour by establishing income generating activities to sustain households’ food access and improve self reliance and reduce the use of negative coping strategies for 40 HHs (228 ind.) Finally, the project will address key gender injustices by reducing specific food insecurity, poor nutrition and health practices and discrimination against women and children. The project is aligned with the Food Security Sector (FSS) specific objectives 2 and 3 by providing direct food assistance and addressing the inaccessibility of income opportunities by enhancing skills and supporting IGAs.

1 - Needs

Shaped by almost 52 years of Israeli occupation and 13 years of ongoing blockade, and torn by the political divide between the de-facto government run by Hamas and the Ramallah-based Palestinian Authority (PA), the Gaza Strip bears the brunt of a protracted humanitarian crisis for which 1.6 million of Palestinians in Gaza are in need of humanitarian assistance (OCHA) .With collapsing systems and ongoing de-development, different service sectors are crippled by the lack of resources. The economy in Gaza Strip is described to be in a free fall and there is a dramatic increase of food insecurity,poverty and unemployment. ”Unemployment in Gaza reached 54 per cent in the second quarter of 2018, with over 70 per cent of young people and 78 per cent of women unemployed. Poverty has soared to 53 per cent and food insecurity to 68 per cent”.(OCHA) .The deepening of the crisis in the Gaza Strip is combined with a significant shortfall in donor assistance to oPT. At the same time, the PA implemented a policy of cutting the salaries of its public employees in Gaza Strip. Such measures meant a loss of cash injection to the local markets and a negative impact on the purchasing power of households. Adding to the already deteriorating situation, the cuts in funding imposed on the PA and the UNRWA by the US Government is affecting the delivery of emergency assistance to vulnerable families in the Gaza strip. Since the beginning of 2017, the failure to achieve reconciliation between the PA and Hamas has led the PA to continue its imposition of measures on Gaza by reducing salaries of more than 50,000 PA employees by 30-50%. This is exacerbated by the delays in transferring cash assistance for over 74,000 vulnerable HH’s. Whilst these measures have a direct impact on the affected households, it also affects the wider economic situation in the Strip due to reduced cash injection into the economy. The MoSD social safety net cash programme is also crippled by the political environment and limitation for resources to cover new needs of vulnerable population. There are around 11,000 HH’s which are eligible for the MoSD social safety net allowances according to assessments carried out in between 2016 and 2019 by the MoSD, but remain on a waiting list. Even the HHs who are benefiting from the social safety net cash assistance that has also been challenged with delayed payments, have high rates of food insecurity. As per Oxfam's previous projects targeting this population in the area of Gaza city and North Gaza, it was found that even with support and with cash assistance they remain to have food gaps. This deteriorating economic situation with the already existing unjust gender norms, add an additional layer to the suffering of people in Gaza that have a negative and devastating impact on vulnerable groups, especially women and girls. There are numerous factors that contribute to high gender discrimination, such as limited access to productive resources, which negatively affects food availability for female headed HH’s, and gender inequality in allocation of HH resources, including those related to nutrition. For example, it was reported that women in the HH tend to eat less in order to prioritize children and elderly. There is also extremely high burden on women in securing their food and basic needs within the HH due to lack of support systems. Vulnerabilities, and food insecurity and poverty in particular are therefore deeply gendered in Gaza, generating a devastating impact for women and girls. Oxfam has been implementing with MA'AN several projects targeting this vulnerable population in Gaza City and North Gaza aiming at reducing food security and the use of negative coping strategies. Previous projects including only fresh foods of vegetables, fruits, chicken and eggs. Voucher was updated to be tailored to bnfs needs and to include grocery items. Bnfs also identified that the value of the voucher was too little especially for larger families.

2 - Activities

Activities are tailored to respond to the specific needs of different age groups and sexes by targeting HH’s who have more children (under the age of 5), women and elderly to address their specific food and nutrition needs. A training will be conducted that will target a pool of vulnerable beneficiaries who have the potential to alleviate themselves from poverty, before providing capacity building on technical skills and guidance based on gender and age perspectives, marketability, and sustainability. Additionally, awareness sessions will be held on women empowerment, female participation and decision making to address the vulnerabilities of women. Activity 1: Provide Food Vouchers to 600 vulnerable food insecure HHs (3420 ind) (including fresh vegetables, chicken, eggs, and grocery items) , and conduct complementary nutrition and gender awareness raising. 1.1: Monthly voucher with an average value of 83-480 ILS depending on the HH size of the selected beneficiaries (based on four categories of Hh sizes) for a duration of 6 months to be provided for 600 vulnerable HHs (3420 individuals) 1.2: Hold 30 nutrition and health awareness sessions for at least 500 women and men to increase their awareness on good feeding practices for children, pregnant and lactating women and nutritional information on specific diseases impacted by nutrition. The activity has been coordinated with Health & Nutrition Cluster. 1.3 Hold 5 public awareness sessions on promoting gender justice and female empowerment. 1.4 Conduct a weekly market survey to update ceiling prices of commodities in shops. Activity 2: Support the creation of small IGA’s 2.1: Improve technical and personal skills (includes visioning and developing a business idea etc). of 100 HH breadwinners who will be nominated/referred from original pool of potential bnfs initially nominated by MoSD. A livelihood capacity assessment will be carried out to assess the potential among nominated bnfs and to understand gender dynamics within the HHs by collecting information on women and girls role in the HH, capacities of women and girls and how women can have or can not have control over resources at the HH level. Women and women main breadwinners will be prioritized. 100 persons one from each HH will be participating in general training that will enable them formulate their project idea on how to support exiting livelihood or start a new one. 2.2 Advanced technical capacities of 40 breadwinners this includes training on managing a small business, bookkeeping, marketing and sustainability) this group will be selected from the original 10 based on a competitive criteria where (those with most passion and persistence demonstrated in the training, the most feasible ideas, financially sustainable, socially and environmentally friendly will be selected . The agriculture related trainings will be coordinated with MoA. 2.3: Provide grants to 40 IGA’s BNFs of Activity 1 will be selected from MoSD nomination of waiting list HHs who are under deep poverty line. Selection criteria will be based on lack of access to food and fresh food items, socio-economic conditions, the negative coping strategies, specific nutrition related problems, and protection related issues. The full selection process will be done in consultation with the FSS and MoSD, the final list will depend on their vulnerability scoring as well as MoSD and FSS cross checking. Female headed and PWDs HHs will be prioritized as they may have less opportunities to secure income. Women of all target groups will be provided with nutrition and health sessions as they have a bigger role in taking care of the nutrition of the family and the handling of food & health issues at the HH level. Bnf of IGAs are expected to decrease use of negative coping strategies as it was demonstrated.

Oxfam is committed to gender-sensitive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) across all its programs and projects which ensure equal access to Oxfam project services for males and females from all age groups. Oxfam also developed a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability which forms the basis of M&E system for all Oxfam projects. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies will be applied. The CAMSA approach comprises how gender-relevant needs and issues are monitored during the implementation and how project reviews and evaluations take gender sensitive issues into serious consideration. All tools, data gathering, data analysis, reporting, recommendations and action points are gender and age disaggregated where required. Throughout the project, MEAL will be undertaken in accordance with a comprehensive M&E plan which will track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe, and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. Data collected will be gender sensitive and disaggregated by age and using different tools such as questionnaires, Key Informative Interviews (KII), stories of change, and focus group discussions (FGDs). In terms of accountability, Oxfam launched its Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) in September 2018 which provides wider access for diverse and vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts and concerns, provide feedback and submit complaints in a safe and private manner. The system automatically refers the received feedback to the relevant program staff and will be monitored by Oxfam MEAL Officer. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The FCRM system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team in-line with Oxfam’s confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Media and communication tools will be used to raise the awareness of targeted groups towards safe and responsive feedback tools.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection of at risk and affected persons is one of the governing factors of Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions, informing humanitarian decision-making and response. It is central to preparedness efforts, as well as part of immediate and life-saving activities. Protection mainstreaming is evident throughout projects cycles premised on required risk identification, analysis and mappings to ensure that risk mitigation measures are in place. Selection of beneficiaries takes into account the socio-economic indicators, including food security and protection indicators, by which the potential HHs have ranked scores and those with highest scores are then selected. This also would encompass prioritization of HHs having persons with disabilities. Oxfam is also committed to data protection principles, confidentiality and privacy of beneficiaries and facilitating safe access to services it provides to different categories of people. Oxfam introduced a new guidance on safe programming which is implementing in all projects and programmes. the safe programming guidance include implementing measures that identifies, analyzes and mitigates risks along the project implementation. Oxfam carries out safe programming training with project partners to ensure joint and consistent safe programming along the project cycle. Measures taken by previous project involving voucher distribution were such as having accessible shops, shops in areas that are safe to men, women and elderly, shop owners are provided with clear induction on how to treat beneficiaries with dignity, respect and humility. Beneficiaries also have right to be served in the best manner and have the right to provide feedback and complain to the organization providing the service.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Selection of beneficiaries will be carried out through requesting nomination form the MoSD HH list of those eligible and are not reached under the social safety net. The list will be further assessed through HH visits to carry out vulnerability and food security assessment. The criteria for selection will be based on a set of parameters including food insecurity, negative coping strategies, protection, and debts. Female headed households and persons with disabilities, elderly special needs, and children special needs will be prioritized by adding weight to the scores given in the vulnerability assessment. A feedback mechanism for selection of beneficiaries will be activated and made accessible and operationalized to ensure proper responses to beneficiaries on their inquiries and appeals in case of non-selection.
