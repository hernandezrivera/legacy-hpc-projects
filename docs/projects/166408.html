1 - Needs

As the Novel Coronavirus (COVID-19) continues to spread in an unpredictable manner it presents a growing risk to COOPI frontline personnel and beneficiaries, particularly during food distributions. This provissions aims to cater for the revision of Food Distribution Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) in the COVID-19 context at the country level, to minimize the risk of exposure of personnel, partners and beneficiaries. Adjustments to proposed food security intervention shall be in alignment with country-specific guidance shared by the relevant health authorities and partners (e.g. Ministry of Health, WHO), where available.

2 - Activities

Taking a “Do No Harm” approach, COOPI operations need to be aware of COVID-19 and implement general preventive measures to reduce the risks of spreading the virus:1- In coordination with local health authorities and inter-agency coordination fora, ensure dissemination of preventive guidance and tools. This includes making awareness and prevention guidance available at each of our sites in the field, including Registration, Distribution, CBT cash and/or voucher outlets (Banks, cash out points, Retailers etc.) in local languages.2- budgetary provisions to make available hand-washing stations and/or hand sanitizer to staff and beneficiaries on those sites and ensure service providers follow this advice. Ensure Staff have access to masks where needed.3. Allocate cost to explore the possibility of mixing/diversifying modalities. Where there is high reliance on a single modality, causing overcrowding in distribution and/or redemption sites, consider ways to shift parts of the beneficiaries to another modality where possible and reasonable. 4. Allocation of budgetary costs where contextually feasible, quickly assess and contract additional service providers to allow beneficiaries shorter transit time and more options for locations to redeem their assistance. This includes adding cash out agents, banks, mobile network operator cash points, retailers etc. Cost proposed for scale back to remote data collection wherever possible to limit the frequency, proximity, and quantity of person-to-person contact. Please do reach out to discuss how you can maintain data quality while conducting surveys remotely; we can help you think through the process, as well as more guidance for teams shifting to a remote data collection model. Also note that for those already using digital data collection systems, it is possible to extend services to embed direct messaging to clients (e.g. SMS surveys, Interactive Voice Recordings, etc.). Stagger delivery of CVA to reduce individual mobility, and congestion in market places and stores. If doing physical distributions, this means smaller distribution cohorts staggered over a longer period of time. If doing card-based payments, then stagger distributions to cohorts over several days. Research has shown that transfers are put to use on the day of the transfer. Increase the frequency of price monitoring surveys focusing on basic goods included in your (S)MEB.

7.3 - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

In the project locations of Potiskum, Damatutu and Geidem where COOPI is partnering EU supported resilience program, and those in LGAs COOPI shall ensure to work with the FSS and OCHA local coordination to identify and select gap locations to avoid duplication and to enhance the complimentary actions of other agencies conducting resilience building interventions. In liaison with the SEMA to work support the government's strategic plan and to avoid friction between the government agencies and COOPI programs as proposed. COOPI has established presence in Yunusari and Potiskum LGAs Yobe state, and recently closed out projects in Jakusko, and Kaga LGA Borno state, hence a referral and linkage system shall be established to enhance the resilience of this FSL intervention by linking beneficiaries to complimentary projects from COOPI where co-location is envisaged. The beneficiaries’ selection process will be as following: 1. In order to ensure a relevant targeting of the most vulnerable people, a geographic targeting shall be used to identify the LGAs with poor food security indicators as published by the 2019 WFP EFSA and Cadre Harmonise reports for Yobe state: following two criteria: • 20% or more of the district population severely food insecure; or • Districts with a total number of 1,000 or more IDPs/returnees. • District level estimates of proportion of population with severe food insecurity are extrapolated from the WFP EFSA and FEWSNET Cadre Harmonise level IPC results based on geo-statistical analysis. IDPs/Returnees data is from the most recent IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix published monthly. 2. a census of all the population in the targeted area will be done, including household composition and household socio-economic situation. 3. Once this data collected, the selection process will be based on food security criteria, by using indicators such as FCS and CSI. 4. In parallel, in order to ensure a well acceptance of the program and an adaptation to the context of the targeting process, the participants (including IDPs, host communities and community leaders) will be involved through focus groups and committees, in the definition of the socio-economic criteria. The vulnerability criteria will be established in collaboration with the community, through these focus groups, with an emphasis on persons with specific needs. These vulnerability criteria can be: widow, elderly, disabled people, household with no income. 5. Finally, the selection of the most vulnerable households according to the thresholds previously defined will be done. The weighting of the socio economic criteria is done with the community. Past experiences have shown that household headed by widow, disabled and elderly are usually considered as the most vulnerable households by the community. This community-based approach enables selection of beneficiaries while preventing or limiting frustration from people not included in the final selection.
