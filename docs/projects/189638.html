Project Summary

Concern is the State focal point for Shelter NFI (SNFI) cluster in Unity State, manages the SNFI re-enforcement needs in Bentiu IDP camp, Rubkona and Guit Counties. This project will focus on improving the living conditions and protecting dignity of the people through SNFI assistance to protracted IDPs, newly displaced flood affected HHs and returnees. The SNFI materials will be sourced from cluster stock. Concern will collaborate with protection cluster to receive referral for PSNs including PWDs, women headed HH, elderly etc. In addition to routine repair and maintenance of shelter through cluster stock in Bentiu IDP camp, Concern will work on upgradation of the shelter through locally available materials and promoting community ownership in their shelter repairs and improvements. In close consultation with the Camp High Committee (CHC), Camp management, protection partners and local authorities, the target beneficiaries will be provided with a voucher to redeem for grass mats from locally identified women groups who are engaged in production of grass mats within IDP site. Concern will train and provide technical oversight to the beneficiaries on installation of grass mats to improve existing shelters. The vulnerable groups such PWDs, child headed HH and elderly will be supported the labour for repair of their shelter. The flood affected displaced HH will be supported with emergency SNFI assistance (materials sourced from cluster stock) based on assessed needs using SNFI cluster standard operational procedures. The returnees will be supported with shelter and NFI through voucher depending on market assessment. Given, Housing, Land and Property (HLP) issues and related conflict, Concern will closely work with the local government, protection cluster to ensure do no harm and land right issues will be considered during shelter assistance. Concern in coordination with relevant stakeholders including protection partners will identify legal and/or community-based solutions to resolve HLP disputes.

1 - Needs

The 2022 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimate that about 2.4 million people will need emergency shelter and non-food items from the impact of ongoing flooding and conflict. By end of December 2021, over 220,000 people have been affected and displaced by the heavy flooding in Unity. As per the flood assessment conducted by Concern, the recent flooding resulted in large scale displacement, damages to shelter and NFI. As of December 2021, a total of 112,829 IDPs (16,880 households) are living in the Bentiu IDP camp. There has been a significant rise in S/NFI repair and replacement requests since the reconstruction of the PoC (now IDP camp) in 2015. Concern is the State focal point for Shelter/NFI cluster in Unity State and manages the S/NFI re-enforcement needs in the entire Bentiu IDP Camp while also coordinating the shelter/NFI activities across Unity state. The current rise in insecurity and conflict has increased the displacement that will further compound the vulnerabilities, with increase need of SNFI assistance. Due to regional humanitarian crisis in bordering host countries, Unity is also witnessing an increased level of returnees, mostly from Sudan. Given the high level of food insecurity, economic crisis and flooding, the household have minimal means to access basic SNFI needs. This has increased the population who are in need of basic life-saving assistance.

2 - Project activities

CO1: Percentage of the targeted population able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition -- New displacement: SNFI mobile team will respond to alerts of new displacement as a result of any disaster such as flood, conflict in Rubkona and Guit counties to meet the needs of the newly displaced IDPs. The IDPs will receive emergency shelter/NFIs sourced from the core pipeline. Following an alert, Concern will conduct multi-sectorial assessment through the cluster where it is State focal point in Unity and coordinate with IOM DTM. Concern will collaborate with protection partner to receive referral for PSN including PWDs and other vulnerable groups. PDMs will be conducted after distribution. CO2: Percentage of the targeted population able to access shelter material and non-food items in situations of emergency and transition -- Protracted displacements: Concern will support the protracted displacement in Bentiu IDP camp. As Shelter Cluster Coordinator, Concern will continue to meet shelter repair needs (materials sourced from cluster stock) in Bentiu IDP camp (hosting 16,880 HHs) based on requests for shelter assistance received by camp management. In addition, in collaboration with protection partner and camp management, Concern will provide shelter re-enforcement in Bentiu IDP camp, where Concern will work with women mat fabricators across all the five sectors to provide grass mats through voucher for upgradation of shelter. This will increase the life of shelter and promote local solution for shelter upgrade and reduce burden on cluster stock. Concern will collaborate with protection partner to receive referral for PSN including PWDs and other vulnerable groups. The vulnerable HHs such as PWDs will be supported with labor in upgrading their shelter. CO3: Enhance community dignity and engagement in the provision of S/NFI servicesAs part of APP tool and cluster guidelines, Concern will ensure the dignity of target beneficiaries and focus on owners driven approach to contribute toward cluster objectives of increased ability to respond to shock and build resilience. The local materials and skills from the community will be used to leverage existing skills to ensure sustainability and increased coping capacities. Beneficiaries will be sensitised on the project scope, entitlements, and CRM. The increase in return and displacement may further inflict HLP related conflict, Concern will coordinate with relevant authorities and protection partners prior to providing shelter materials and lobby for allotting land to women headed HHs who often lack ownership of land. As per cluster guidelines, Concern undertake Due diligence and will work with the local government, protection cluster to ensure proper tenure system in accordance with the customary and the lands acts. Concern will conduct trainings and sensitizations on importance of providing equal opportunities for land ownership across all genders. To avoid conflict, Concern will also target HHs from host community mainly the PSNs. PDMs will be conducted after each distribution to measure beneficiaries satisfaction on process and SNFI received. CO4: Community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures: Concern will consult with the target community to ensure that the shelter construction consider and take necessary disaster risk reduction (DRR) measures for sustainability of shelter infrastructures from erratic weather patterns. The construction supervisor will provide technical assistance to ensure DDR measures. Shelter committees will be trained on DRR. Conduct community sensitization on DRR to mainstream disaster prevention. Concern will collaborate with the State Ministry and the local leader in verifying land ownership prior shelter assistance to avoid forceful evictions and negotiate through the protection cluster to lobby for returnees with no land ownership.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Concern intends to provide both in-kind and voucher assistance based on the context, target population profile, consultations with community on preferred mode and market functionality. The Voucher assistance will provide broader choice to beneficiaries to purchase items based on their need and preferences. In addition it will contribute to more dignified assistance, community ownership and benefit to local traders by injecting cash into local economy. The purchasing of the local materials from local traders in the market will encourage the stocking of shelter materials in local market that will contribute to sustainability and availability of materials for repairs & maintenance in the long run. The proposed approach of procurement of grass mat locally from affected women in IDP camp will contribute to injecting cash locally, promoting livelihood and encouraging use of local materials for improving the shelter that will benefit the community in the long run to meet their future repair needs.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The current flooding impacted market in Unity, prior to project interventions, Concern will conduct market assessment using cluster guidelines and modality will be selected accordingly.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engages with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The broader consultations with the community during the assessment will inform about the project design, priority activities and modality of assistance. The project/villages committees including most vulnerable such as women, elderly, disable will be formed at inception stage to engage in project delivery and monitoring. The project/villages/boma committees from target beneficiaries share feedback to adapt the assistance accordingly. The regular monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through lead mothers and CNVs hired from local community.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. The safeguarding focal points each office ensure implementation and compliance with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders, Concern informs them about right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern’s policies and operational guidelines are in line with IASC’s five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations. Prior to initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern will conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, target criteria and their entitlements. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the distribution points. The monitoring and CRM (Dedicate staff) will conduct regular visit to refresh beneficiaries about the right to complaint, suggestion and how to make complaint.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with CCCM and Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Distribution sites will be located in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe locations. Prior to distributions, Concern will conduct sensitisations on how beneficiaries can reduce protection risks such as travelling in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Information on voucher and distribution days will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulation. For land right and location for shelter, Concern ensure that the beneficiaries have documents for possession of land to construct their shelter and Concern will coordinate with relevant stakeholder/state authorities to regarding land issues. The CRM will be used to identify protection cases and make subsequent referrals to Protection partners. Concern will provide assistance to PSNs such as disabled to transport shelter materials and NFIs. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
