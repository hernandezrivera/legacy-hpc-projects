1 - Needs

According to 2022 HNO, 2.4 million IDPs, returnees and vulnerable residents as having shelter and NFI needs, driven by the multitude of conflict-related, ecological, and economic factors, further exacerbated by the still looming threat of COVID 19. These localities include protracted and newly displaced people, returnees, and vulnerable host communities. 1.3 million people with most severe needs (56% of the PiN) will be targeted by the sector in 77 localities across Darfurs, Kordofans, Blue Nile and flood-affected states, by delivering timely emergency assistance to newly displaced as well as addressing mid to longer-term needs of returnees and communities living in protracted displacementWorld Vision will target some of the South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile localities that are affected by natural hazard, such as annual cycles of flooding to support newly IDPs, protracted IDPs, returnees, vulnerable host communities and other hazard affected populations to restore their dignity and reduce exposure to protection and health risks. Among all vulnerable populations, pregnant and lactating women, chronically ill female and male people, Female and Male elderly, unaccompanied minors, the elderly and people with disabilities are highly vulnerable without shelter to protect them from the elements and avoid exposing them to further health risks especially given the outbreak of COVID19 pandemic Shelter and NFI assistance is required for Internally Displaced People (IDPs) – new, protracted, and integrated and newly accesses, refugees and returnees. Pregnant and lactating women, chronically ill people, elderly, unaccompanied minors, and physically disabled are particularly vulnerable amongst all affected populations who, in addition to their NFI needs, are highly vulnerable without shelter to protect them from the elements and remain highly exposed to further health risks (SNFI Sector HNO 2021) Shelter solutions and NFIs - plastic sheeting, plastic mats, jerry cans, blankets and cooking sets. These items help restore a minimal sense of dignity and protection against exposure to the elements, mitigate health risks and provide some privacy and security to those in need. More sustainable shelter solutions for returnees and protracted IDPs affected by not only recent conflict but also natural hazards need to be addressed. This project will target 1000 people with the most severe needs 22 localities across South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan, and the Blue Nile States, by delivering timely emergency assistance to newly displaced as well as addressing mid to longer-term needs of returnees and communities living in protracted displacement, through the following two objectives: • Provide lifesaving NFI assistance to crisis-affected populations• Enable crisis-affected populations to access adequate shelter solutions that provide protection, safety, security, and space to live in a dignified manner

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WV mainstreams protection activities throughout its programming in several ways. 1) WV applies the “Do No Harm” program analysis framework in each of our programs, ensuring that project activities do not exacerbate tensions and conflicts in the proposed program areas. Staff from all different sectors have been or will shortly be trained in this approach. 2) When conducting routine community assessments and monitoring, WV also includes an analysis of protection issues to better understand the community’s special protection needs and concerns. For example, factors that might increase the exposure of women and young girls to violence when collecting water and firewood or using latrines and bathing facilities are identified to minimize risks in designing program activities. Special focus group discussions with women and other vulnerable groups are also routinely undertaken. 3) The results of these assessments are then used by the different program sectors to identify the most appropriate way of implementing activities in ways that enhance protection and minimize risk. For example, in the S/NFIs sector, bathing shelters are only constructed in central locations where the safety and privacy of women can be assured. 4) A proactive monitoring and reporting system exists to monitor, document and report on any protection violations such as threats, denial of access or violence upon users. These monitoring systems are put in place through community committees, which have been formed and trained by WV. Any violations of human rights are documented and reported to the appropriate authorities or monitoring institutions. 5)Protection will be mainstreamed at both levels of the project cycle. The selection of the targeted villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNHCR. The project will ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. WV will utilize its current standard operating procedures for the safety and security of personnel and beneficiaries to ensure staff and beneficiary security. All activities will be designed to be accessible to people with disabilities, elderly women and children. Each staff, vendor, contractor, volunteer and other third parties in business with WV will have to sign the Child Protection protocol to ensure adherence to WV Safeguarding (Child Protection) policy. Each staff is oriented on the WV Code of Conduct as well as the Red Cross Code of Conduct. WV will that COVID-19 mitigation measures adhere to all activities as per the Sector guidance note 2020

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

World Vision (WV) recognizes that the essence of accountability is to respect the needs, concerns, capacities and disposition of those with whom we work and to answer for our actions and decisions. We intend to contribute to changes that result in the improved well-being of children and communities. WV works with the most vulnerable to identify critical needs and seeks to ensure their right to receive assistance and protection based on their informed consent. Globally, World Vision follows 2010, Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines for accountability to affected populations and the organizations LEAP for rapid-onset emergencies. In practice, this focuses on 5 key commitments for how World Vision works with affected populations. Transparency: Provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected populations over information provision. Feedback and complaints: Actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about the breach in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. Participation: Enable affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence. Design, monitoring and evaluation: Design, monitor and evaluate the goals and objectives of programmes with the involvement of affected populations, feeding learning back into the organization on an ongoing basis and reporting on the results of the process Leadership/Governance: Demonstrate commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into country strategies, programme proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, training and performance management, partnership agreements, and highlighted in reporting. We also understand that working to implement these commitments can be particularly challenging when humanitarian access is limited or absent and with the current COVID 19 pandemic where social distancing and other protocols to minimize infection will limit accountability activities, for example, communication and information flow can be intermittent and difficulties can emerge in ensuring participation or representation; Basic elements of an accountable response. Therefore we will work closely with other partners in the affected areas to ensure that a harmonized approach to the key aspects of HAP guidelines can be adapted.
