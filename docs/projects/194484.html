Project Summary

Samaritan’s Purse SP, South Sudan, propose to provide essential life-saving primary health services to using a mobile health approach to affected peoples and communities in crisis and who are under-served and or unable to access existing health care services. SP propose to continue Mobile health services in Mayendit and Mayom Counties, Unity State to eight (8) crisis affected communities who are unable to reach existing health care facilities and who are impacted by conflict and flooding, with many needing to temporarily relocate as well as host IDP’s from surrounding areas.
As an extension to the above activities Samaritan’s Purse propose a Rapid Mobile Health Response to newly crisis affected communities by prepositioning resources and mobilizing a team to respond rapidly to emerging crisis as way to seek to prevent an escalating humanitarian situation for affected communities. This response may be up to three months while other partners ready themselves or the crisis is settled and the community return to their area and regular services. This rapid mobile health team may also be mobilized to respond quickly when there is an outbreak and need for a reactive vaccination campaign.

1 - Needs

Samaritan’s Purse propose to continue providing essential primary health care using a mobile modality to the people of Mayendit and Mankien/Mayom County. Mayendit is a very resource poor area and isolated County with a vulnerable population due to its lack of development and geographical remoteness as a result of the surrounding rivers and wetlands that impede free movement. There are few roads connecting Mayendit easily to other counties or internally among its different payams. The situation worsens during the rainy season – when flooding and swamp areas remain in place for between 7-9 months a year. Flooding has been a record levels and affecting more than 60,000 people, causing displacement (Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report, 2021). The only referral locations include the PHCC in Rubkai (limited capacity) and for surgery is MSF in Leer, which is inaccessible to many locals for extended periods of time (Relief Web Health Service Functionality Monthly Bulletin, March 2020). Increased access to primary health services by means of a MMU in Mayendit North will improve health outcomes for treatable illness and decrease late presentation at the Leer health facility, which is 20 km (or approximately 40 minutes’ drive) away by 4WD. Conflict and resource scarcity have impacted the population. Mobile Medical Health services are at times necessary to provide essential primary health care to challenged populations in humanitarian crises. The mobile medical teams are positioned in difficult to reach areas with people in need identified by the CHD/MoH and/or the Health Cluster and SP. Mayom County experiences significant gaps in health care provision (WHO Health Service Functionality Monthly Bulletin, March 2020). The Implementing partner for the HPF also has significant gaps in human resource and supplies, Conflict and resource scarcity have affected the population. Mobile Medical Health services are at times necessary to provide essential primary health care to challenged populations in humanitarian crises. The people of Mayom also make up a proportion of the more than 200,000 people who have been severely affected by the protracted flooding since 2019 and into 2020 and 2021 (IOM DTM Mayom, September 2020). The mobile medical teams are positioned in difficult to reach areas with people in need identified by the CHD/MoH and/or the Health Cluster and SP. SP are targeting approximately 45,000 (22,500 male; 22,500 female) people (an estimated 8,700 children U5; 16,000 children ages 5 to 18 years; 18,000 adults ages 18 to 59 years; and 3,200 adults over 60 years) with life-saving primary health services. - Primary health consultations, Reproductive Health, Nutrition, SGBV/IPV and MHPSS screening & referrals (particularly with increased trauma and stress due to flooding), routine vaccinations and community health education and awareness; - Health care to remote communities (without functional facilities) - To provide Primary Health Care to the most vulnerable in a community – women and children, the elderly, people with a disability and those with severe illness or trauma – for whom reaching a static facility is impossible – particularly when cut off by floods. - To flood affected communities, - To IDP’s (from flooding and or conflict).

2 - Project activities

The project proposes to continue to engage with community members by means of open community meetings within the community itself, ensuring diversity in age and gender as well as people with a disability represented in meetings and ensuring there is plenty of time given to hear from participants as well as well as meeting with the CHD, RRC Chiefs and other partners - meeting to listen and discuss needs and ensure the mobile services and health education are meeting the needs of the communities and reporting activities. The team will travel to target remote communities (by vehicle, quad bike but often by canoe) to provide essential life-saving primary health services, including; Outpatient (OPD) services; management of communicable and a few non-communicable diseases, early child health, MUAC assessments, maternal (ANC) & newborn health & reproductive health (RH), wound care and community health education. - Screen, Identify, provide initial ‘first aid’ and referral for survivors of GBV/IPV, as well as people identified with PHPSS needs (- with referral to specialist partners) - Provision of Snake bite venom to the local PHCC when a snake-bite victim is referred by the team as a response to an increase in snake bites in flooded areas – with monitoring as needed. - Distribution of long lasting insecticide mosquito nets, birth kits and soap. - Active and ongoing triaging ensures the medical teams prioritize care for vulnerable groups, including women and children, people with a disability, the elderly and those seriously unwell or with trauma. - Care, referral and evacuation for people with gunshot wounds (GSW) in conflict prone areas as in the last 12 months in Mayendit and Mayom. - The team engage CHD trained vaccinators to conduct routine vaccinations/EPI (both Mayendit and Mayom bases have cold chain capacity and will coordinate with the CHD EPI officers. – The team will coordination with the SP WASH team to work with the community to install and or maintain latrines near each of the sites and conduct WASH hygiene promotion activities in the target communities. - Synchronize with the WASH and FSL teams to engage with and roll out activities in the eight target communities on the same days where possible. - Build the capacity of the team through ongoing trainings and CME. - Ensure responsible and appropriate dispensing practices through training and through the implementation of a solid pharmaceutical tracking system. - Collaborating with the Health IP and the CHD in providing/ donating essential medicines when there is an urgent need to the IP, as well as logistical support for example to transport vaccines. The Rapid Health Response strategy proposes to essential life-saving health services as outlined above to newly crisis affected communities by prepositioning resources (medical supplies/logistical/HR budget capacity) and mobilizing a team to respond rapidly to an emerging crisis as way to seek to prevent an escalating humanitarian situation for affected communities. This response may be up to three months while other partners ready themselves or the crisis is settled and the community return to their area and regular services. This rapid mobile health team may also be mobilized to respond quickly when there is an outbreak working with the CHD to conduct a reactive vaccination campaign.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In line with AAP priorities Samaritan’s Purse is dedicated to providing programming with safety, dignity, and a ‘Do No Harm’ policy. Health services are promoted so that all people have meaningful access.– including women and girls and those with a disability. Samaritan’s Purse teams will actively seek the views of communities to improve the program, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are visible, streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to handle (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. The team will have a feedback box available where messages can be left anonymously and the team plan regular stakeholder meetings every month which provide an opportunity for meaningful feedback to be exchanged. Furthermore the Mobile Medical Teams (whether responding regularly or as a short term rapid response to a new crisis) will begin each day with Community health sessions, in the present programs this is well known to the beneficiaries. It is the experience of the team that community members attend the sessions even when they are not unwell or waiting for services. These informal regular open meetings enable engagement between the team and the waiting patients, their care takers and other attendees. –This includes the vulnerable such as persons with special needs, the elderly, pregnant mothers, and GBV survivors. In order to ensure all are considered from the assessment to completion of the project, information will be timely and accessible. The team are well known to the community and communication is free flowing. Feedback mechanisms are communicated to the community as part of health education and CC. Feedback is free flowing – aside form CC - at times a community member may wish to remain anonymous and will feedback through the locally engaged CHW’s.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Prior to the launching of the MM services, there was significant community consultation between SP, the CHD and local community members, including; regular community members, community leaders, religious & spiritual leaders, youth and women’s associations. Ongoing community consultation provides a platform for community members to express their priorities and particular health concerns. The team conducted sensitization and awareness about the scope of service provision by the MM teams - to manage expectations and ensure the target communities were informed regarding what the MM teams will be providing and when and where they will recommend referral. This is a dynamic process, while the MM modality may not meet all the team preferences and needs - consultations has enabled a free flow of communication.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Each SP field office has a designated Safeguarding Officer, who actively promotes protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and supports creating an environment that protects and prevents harm as a result of all country activities. Beneficiaries are made aware of the Code of Conduct that SP staff and representatives have to abide by, the known consequences and how to report any concerns through the established in-country complaints and feedback mechanisms. In addition, SP employs an experienced Gender and Protection Program Development Officer (G&P PDO). The G&P PDO works closely with project staff to develop and promote cross-cutting social safeguards to be embedded in project activities. SP provides training on PSEA to all their staff. The team provide PSEA awareness as part of their community education sessions on clinic days. The MM team have also engaged recognized Protection partners on the ground to travel out with the team to the sites to provide awareness and community education as well as regular training for the team The SP MM team have weekly meetings where community and staff feedback is discussed and have endeavored to adjust priorities to meet community expectations and feedback. over the last 9 months of services - consultation and feedback has led to changes and improvements such as site location based on changing community needs and movement due to flooding, suggestions for health education topics by the community, improved triaging to ensure the most vulnerable are prioritized, cross sectoral collaboration such as the provision of latrines by the SP WASH team as part of Hygiene promotion, better community participation in protecting & maintaining of the mobile sites, an improved ANC set up (more privacy in some locations) and to fast track the roll out of EPI services at the request of the community and the CHD. The code of conduct is based on the IASC six core principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse, 2019.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

As a key part of the AAP mechanism the Samaritan’s Purse team will ensure robust community consultation on a regular basis as well as on ad hoc basis according to the needs of the community. The MMU teams may adjust their target population and destination based on identified needs, as has been the experience of the mobile health work in the past – communities have discussed appropriate days for service, proposed preferred sites for activities, have mobilized resources to improve the mobile clinic sites to provide improved shelter for waiting patients and privacy, in particular for women. Communities are involved in the selection of Community Health Volunteers in each of their own communities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1 The regular MMU team will ensure a well communicated routine – the team store left mats with the community for people to sit and wait. There is a strong established understanding by the community that the team will conduct their triage process and will not be intimidated or bullied into giving any service. There has never been an incident of violence in any of the sites for the duration of this project so far. By strong relationships that have been built the community know what to expect and the team and the community respect the processes. Despite reducing the distance for people to access health care – some vulnerable community members may travel a long distance – the team hope by prioritizing women and children that this risk will be reduced. The mobile health team aim to complete their activities by 3:30 to ensure and enable people to return home safely during daylight hours. 8.7 - All patient information is recorded on consultation forms designed to capture essential patient/beneficiary related information. These forms are kept in a secure cupboard in a secure cupboard and office. Essential Patient information is entered into the Patient Database Workbook – The workbook collates and summarizes the overall patient/beneficiary clinical activities and is used to track clinical trends, common disease burden and generate reports such as the EWARS and DHIS 2 and Health facility functionality (HFF). Database workbooks are password protected with access limited to a few key staff. With significantly improved internet patient information could be entered into the workbook in real time reducing the need for paper forms. 8.8 - The information and recommendations from external stakeholders is measured up against – population data, assessment information/publications, distance from existing services, roads and access and impact form environmental disasters – In order to make balanced decisions. 8.10 - The risk may be there however the sites where the team set up are well traversed and so may not have a risk of explosion – there may be risks in bush land for people walking to the sites however the community may know the history and safe areas to walk. Include awareness of unexploded ordinances into the health education.
