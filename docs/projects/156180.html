Project Summary


The project aims to support the Education opportunities in north of Syria for the most vulnerable Syrian children (girls and boys, considering; physically disabled children, adolescents and upper-age girls ). QC will provide complementary Education services to improve the educational infrastructure that increase physical access to schools for all children. Also, the project is designed to enhance the quality of Education through building capacity of teachers and schools’ admin staff and support their monthly stipends as well as schools running costs in addition to distribute teaching and learning materials, stationery and school bags to both children and teachers (girls, boys, females, males).
Finally, and in regard to system strengthening, the project will respond to the gap of school textbooks by printing and distributing educational curriculum in place, considering the sensitivities of the Syrian crisis.
QC intervention is planned to cover all the Education objectives after 8 years of the Syrian crisis that imposes education as urgent priority to prevent a lost generation.

Needs

It has been almost eight years since armed conflict erupted in Syria. The ongoing conflict has negatively impacted all aspects of the social and economic life of the population. Humanitarian conditions have dramatically deteriorated, particularly in the areas controlled by the opposition groups such as North-west of Syria and areas in the East controlled by Kurdish forces (SDF).According to the 2019 Syrian Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) report, 13.2 million people inside Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance (including 1.8 million in acute need), and 5.8 million of school-aged children (girls and boys) as well as 245,000 education personnel are in need for Education assistance inside Syria (%61 are in acute and immediate need). UNHCR statistics state that 6.6 million of Syrian population are internally displaced, 3 million of them are children. Many of these people have been displaced multiple times. Also, the fluxes of IDPs puts additional pressure on the access of public services particularly in affected host communities in North Syria who themselves do not have sufficient services. Critical public services, such as education, are hindered due to the limited funding and capacity of the public institutions or NGOs to provide services at scale. Schools in host communities which were crowded before the war, have to accommodate more than their original number of students. At the same time the systematic bombardments hindered the access to education, as 762 attacks on education facilities have been reported, 426 of which are verified, since the crisis started in 2011. According to the Access Coordination Unit, 252 schools were totally destroyed, and 1,252 schools were partially damaged. Also, the misuse of the schools as detainees’ centres or collective shelters, reduced the number of the functioning schools. It’s also remarked that most schools and WASH facilities in north of Syria, lacks the logistic installations that increase access to Education for the physically disabled children and adolescents; girls and boys. The thing that add more to their sufferings. Statistics of HNO 2019 state that 2.1 million children are currently out of school in addition to 1.35 million children and youth at risk of dropping out. It’s also known in Syria that the education enrolment of adolescents (girls and boys from 12 to 17 years old) is very low comparing with younger children enrolment as they are mainly affected by child-labour and early marriage.The remaining teachers in Syria face tremendous pressure in the context of teaching. Where they are fully exhausted to meet the educational needs in overcrowded areas. More than 140,000 teachers and education staff are no longer in their teaching jobs. Resulting in poor quality of education in general. Quality of teaching is an essential component of student performance, and teacher experience is essential to give children quality education. Syrians who graduated from different faculties and those who dropped out of university because of the crisis, are volunteering to teach with no previous experience and limited knowledge in the minimum standard of child protection and classroom management. Female teachers number has remarkably decreased in light of marked decline of women role in the society and the lack of security in generalChildren in Syria are deprived from most of their basic Human Rights including “Right to Education”. Over the past eight years, the formal education system has deteriorated, due to the civil crisis. Like any war context, local resources dedicated to other purposes than civil services or education. Moreover, the population is overwhelmed with poverty, an unstable security situation and displacement. This pushes families not to send children to schools. Finally, eight years of bombardment, dead and injured bodies and live-executions have all left trauma, aggression and bullying in children's behavior that needs a proper psycho-social support.

Implementation of activities

QC will provide evidence-based activities in line with HRP and HNO needs and demands that consider Education cluster priorities for cross-border response. Moreover, QC will run in-depth Education need assessment in the targeted areas to identify the urgent gaps and response accordingly. QC will conduct BTL Campaigns in the communities to advocate for the wright of Education to Adolescents and girls in all grades. In schools’ and WASH facilities rehabilitation and based on the need assessments; QC will consider the population in the targeted communities wherein schools need rehab works, hence, the needs of more education opportunities for school-age children (particularly; children with physical disabilities) to facilitate their access to education. Additionally, QC will provide school furniture (Desks, White-boards, Teachers’ tables and chairs and safety and security kits). Also, in the topic of improving Quality of Education, QC will enhance the capacity of educational actors by apply training for teachers and school principles on Pedagogy and teaching methods, Teachers learning-cycles, child protection, self-development of the trainees, management skills for school principals and problem-solving skills. Meanwhile, QC will support the monthly stipends for teachers and schools’ admin staff to ensure the continuity of education in addition to supporting the monthly running costs of the schools (i.e. water, hygiene, fuel, stationery, etc.) as well as distributing teaching and learning materials, school bags and stationery to beneficiaries and teachers in the targeted communities. Girls Education will be advocated through the communities targeted with BTL campaigns. Girls will be encouraged to join schools again by considering school transportation for girls. After the rehab and furniture works of the schools and supporting schools’ cadre with training and stipends and distributing of learning materials and the support of girls’ education; QC will print and distribute the textbooks for children in all grades. Printing of textbooks will create an attractive education environment for children and bridge a big gap of education needs in north of Syria. The project will encourage a community-based education intervention by establishing PTAs that meet regularly to discuss challenges, identify solutions, and support children’s enrollment and learning in project locations. Also, QC will ensure building the resilience of the affected populations through providing an important context for restoring familiar roles and routines following the crisis. Project will be implemented by well identified hierarchy and structured team inside and outside Syria, monitored through QC M&E team. Satisfaction Surveys that are related to services provided, will be held to target beneficiaries and other stakeholders to measure the quality. Project will be divided to milestones and will be monitored and evaluated accordingly. A post evaluation for QC project will be done after project’s closure, to analyze the impact of projects’ done activities. Meanwhile, a feedback mechanism with clear steps and response deadline will be installed in all project targeted locations. Complaint boxes, hot-line and WhatsApp number and other means per to the context of the targeted locations, will be in place to address the complaints and increase the accountability . Project will consider all children to facilitate their access equitably to Education. For this sake, children with disabilities’, girls’ and adolescent’s wright to Education will be advocated through BTL campaigns activities and Brochures. School Transportation will be provided, and rehab works will consider the access barriers for the disabled children in both school and WASH facilities. These elements will support all together, the concept of inclusive Education.

Protection Risk Analysis

QC project is in line with HRP objectives in regard to access, quality and system strengthening in reference to Education. In term of Access to formal and Non-formal Education, we assume the following risks: • Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. The likelihood of this risk is High to take place during the year 2019 with a high impact on project activities. To mitigate this risk QC will: - If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform internal emergency focal point on the situation; b) Coordinate with partners, cluster and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessary; c) Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. Decisions are taken in advance whether hard copies will be destroyed or not. - Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed. • Attacks on schools. This risk has a High possibility and High impact comparing with 2018 reported incidents. QC immediately will:- Alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc.) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks. - Duty of Care policies are in place, and the staff are informed on the responsibilities as well as limitations of the organization. - Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff - Using OCHA’s Deconfliction Mechanism as a method to hold attackers accountable for their actions QC will also Construct and rehabilitate classrooms and provide schools with school furniture and running costs. Threats for this activity might be: • Risk of children being out of school due to unavailability of school furniture upon completion of school rehabilitation and construction of prefabricated classrooms The likelihood for this risk is Medium with a Medium impact on project activities in general, but in this project it’s very low as QC planned to: - Include school furniture in school rehabilitation plan or sign an MoU with authorities to ensure provision of school furniture by them upon completion of school rehabilitation QC will Provide students and teachers with teaching and learning materials, school bags and stationery to both teachers and children (girls, boys, females, males). The threats for this activity are: • Interference of armed groups, including confiscation of sensitive materials (laptops, phones); with a Medium likelihood and a Medium impact on project activities. To mitigate this, we will: - engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities. In term of System strengthening, QC intends to Provide textbooks for children in all grades. The related risks to this activity could be: • Delay of the printing and timely delivery, with a Medium likelihood and a Medium impact. QC will mitigate the risk by: - Print books with and without logos depending on the location served. QC will pay attention to ‘Do no harm' principle by identifying unintended negative or positive impacts of Education interventions in settings where there is conflict or risk of conflict. QC will consider ‘Do no harm' principle during planning, monitoring, and evaluation to ensure that the intervention does not worsen the conflict but rather contributes to improving it. QC is totally aware that ‘Do No Harm’ is an essential basis for the work of organizations operating in situations of conflict.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In this intervention, QC assume that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. In regard to this, some risks might be beyond QC control. Through all project locations, Environmental risks (floods, earthquakes, etc.) may have an effect on implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster happen in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff, will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with all staff and schools’ principals. Moreover, and after Sushi agreement between Russia and Turkey, QC assumes that areas out of Syrian government control in north-west of Syria will be stable for the whole next year. Yet, the risk of increasing pressure on project due to influx of IDPs is very high. Project activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs some areas suddenly increases. The mitigation of this risk will be achieved by: 1) Out-reach sessions to community leaders, stakeholders, families and beneficiaries to explain the limited absorption capacity of the project. 2) On-going coordination and advocacy with Education cluster and Education authorities to bridge the gap. Also, after 8 year of Syrian crisis. We assume that Girls Education is not a concern anymore. Nevertheless, in some locations in north of Syria, the risk of hindering access of girls to school is still taking place in some communities. To mitigate this risk, QC will: 1) Advocate to girls Education through BTL campaigns.2) Provide school transportation for girls to access schools.
