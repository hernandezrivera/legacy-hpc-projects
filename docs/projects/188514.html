Project Summary

The general nutrition situation in Northwest Syria deteriorated severely in 2021, as stated in the needs section. Poverty, low socioeconomic situation, unsafe or unhealthy living conditions, inadequate maternity, infant, and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices, and high rates of maternal malnutrition have all contributed to this deterioration. A significant continuous contributing factor is a mass internal displacement that occurred at the end of 2019 and early 2020. A further deterioration of the nutritional situation is expected as a result of the economic slump and depreciation of the Syrian Pounds, as well as the impact of COVID-19 in NWS.
BAHAR will execute a bundle of activities in response to the identified needs, that will assist 35,000 individuals.
1. Screening for malnutrition, identifying and referral if any through rapid response teams
2. Provide one-on-one counseling on WHO-recommended newborn and young child feeding and maternal nutrition practices in facilities and communities.
3. Providing children between the ages of 6 to 59 months with specialized nutritious food (LNS).
4. Early detection and referral of cases of Acute Malnutrition
5. Providing medical/nutritional treatment to all cases of severe acute malnutrition with complications (identified and/or referred) in BAHAR's stabilization center in Afrin city.
the proposed project will target the flowing subdistricts Afrin, bulbul,Jandairis, Raju,Sharan, and with special focus on M'abatli and Sheikh Alhadid
this is going to be achieved through the deployment of RRT teams that will conduct a general screening to detect malnutrition cases of children U-5 and PLW/Gs within targeted communities, referring the identified cares either to OTPs or BAHAR SC for further investigation and management
the case management will include medical supervision and treatment for SAM cases with medical complications
In addition to that RRTs will conduct regular follow up visits to the previously identified, referred ,and treated cases up to six months
with special and focused intervention

4 - Needs

According to the SMART Survey (NWS, July 2021), malnutrition has been rising in recent years. In Aleppo, sub-districts such as Bulbul, Raju, Sharan, Jandairis, Ma’btali are flagrant examples of communities with high severity ranking in stunting (children 6-59 months), where the rate was 25% (severity rank 3). This is coupled with high iron-deficiency anemia rates reaching 46% among children between 6-59 months, 54% in PLW (severity rank 5) (SMART Survey NWS, 2021). Moreover, IYCF practices were found to be sub-optimal. Only 21% of children between 6-59 months consumed the minimally acceptable diet (MAD), while only 53.4% of infants 0-5 months were exclusively breastfed. This highlights the requirement for improved IYCF capacity building in health workers, enabling key messaging for mothers to improve maternal diets as well as breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices. IYCF services are underfunded and underserved and there is an urgent need to scale up services across the NWS. The drivers of malnutrition in NWS are multifactorial and apart from displacement include high levels of food insecurity. With regard to children living with disability, many have disability-related complex feeding disorders however they lack access to health and nutrition services supporting appropriate management of their special feeding needs. In NWS 10% of acute malnutrition cases were children with special needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

BAHAR will provide the LNS product, nutributter, to children 6-59 months, in highly vulnerable and IDP families living in informal settlements and IDP camps in North Aleppo. Alongside these supplements women of reproductive age and caregivers of children, 6-59 months will be trained on the family MUAC approach and provided with a MUAC tape to take home as well as with information on the nutrition treatment and prevention services available in their local area, if they need to access them. This aims to increase early self-identification of acute malnutrition within the households and increase health-seeking behaviors. Nutrition counseling will be provided to the same populations on the prevention and treatment of malnutrition and maternal-infant and young child nutrition. Coordination and referrals will be carried out between all health & nutrition actors in the area through the Nutrition Cluster and bilaterally. Coordination and referrals will also take place between the food security actors in the area to ensure that the people most vulnerable to malnutrition receive both food assistance and nutrition services.Moreover, BAHAR will provide a full treatment package including admission, 24/7 monitoring, treatment of all identified cases of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications within Bahar's stabilization center in Afrin city.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Beneficiaries' exploitation of violence or abuse during distribution rounds.Mitigation: PSEA orientation will be conducted to team leaders and they will train their teams. Also, the accountability field team will monitor the services provided. Risk: GBV and CP’ discovered cases during the screening.Mitigation: Detection and referral orientation from the field coordinator to the team leader to observe the visual signs and refer the victims to the case management teams according to service mapping in the region. Risk: Gender imbalance among project staff and higher risk of GBVMitigation: the nutritionists, rapid response members, and other project team members will be both male and female, however, Bahar will hire a higher proportion of female staff given that the target population is PLW and children under 5.Risk: Access restrictions to the area due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines that prevent the humanitarian actors from providing timely emergency relief to the affected population. Mitigation: Constantly monitoring the security situation on the ground and developing contingency planning, as well as exploring different implementation modalities.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying the related procedures. Bahar ensures that the field team attends PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal point's responsibility is to ensure training is delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of the online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complains pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor and report if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, security conditions remain sufficiently stable to allow BAHAR to implement. Risk: Continued military offensives present the risk of death or injury to Bahar staff, damage to or destruction of Bahar premises or assets; reduced access to program areas, and border closure. Mitigation: Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and SOPs are in place and an experienced security team.Assumption: Bahar has the capacity to safely store contingency stock.Risk: Border closure disrupts the supply chain.Mitigation: Bahar keeps stores stocks sufficient to meet requirements for two months and continues to explore the possibility of local procurement.Assumption: COVID19 will continue to be a risk for 2022. Risk: Urge or resurge of COVID-19 in northwest SyriaMitigation: Bahar will aim to continue, in as much as possible, project services delivery. However, certain measures such as MUAC screening, outreach may need to be curtailed in light of high transmission rates. Where there are localized lockdowns, Bahar will aim to deliver assistance, perhaps at the HH levels with increased COVID-19 mitigation measures being utilized to protect staff and beneficiaries.
