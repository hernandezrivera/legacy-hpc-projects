Project Summary

The proposed project aims to support most vulnerable families in the Governorate of Anbar, particularly in Heet District. CCR and its partner AL MAHABA WES-SALAM (Al Mahaba) will identify and prioritize the most vulnerable families in Heet district whose houses were damaged in different levels due to the military operations. Beneficiaries will be selected based on a social and economic vulnerability assessment (recommended by the Cash Working Groups in Iraq) and the damage categories of their shelters (Based on Iraq Shelter Cluster’s Guidelines).
Partners will rehabilitate 100 damaged shelters in Heet District. Targeted houses will fall under category 2 (and 3 based on the technical assessment) of war damaged shelters. Shelter rehabilitation activities will be implemented through framework agreements with local suppliers and locally hired skilled labours. Partners will verify the local market capacity through market assessment prior to start rehabilitation.
Partners will select 330 vulnerable families whose damaged shelter fall under damage category 1 who will be eligible to receive Sealing Off Kits (SOKs). SOKs will be designed based on the findings a community-level assessment at the beginning of the project where technical engineers will evaluate the most critical shelter repair needs and advice on selecting the components of the kits. Distribution of SOKs will follow the method of house to house/ family distribution given that selected families lack resources and capacity to manage transporting kits from central locations. Partners will ensure that targeted families will receive training on the best usage of the SOKs and distribute manuals along with the kits.
Activities aim to improve safety and privacy elements in families’ shelters which are critical to adequate shelter conditions for women, men, girls and boys. Furthermore, the proposed project will contribute to improve the substandard shelters by protecting from climate and foster healthier conditions.
CCR will coordinate with the Community Resource Centers in Anbar Governorate and HLP actors to address the needs of HLP intervention of targeted families. CCR will ensure that no harm is associated with its intervention on vulnerable families especially related to governmental compensation and lands properties and rights issues.

Needs

The continuous conflict in Iraq between 2014 and 2017 forced more than 4 million of Iraqis to flee from their homes and seek shelter in safer areas. Anbar is the Iraq’s largest governorate with a significant number of remote hard-to-reach communities and high insecurity. ISIL seized control over Anbar Governorate in June 2014, which caused the massive displacement of more than 50% of the local population seeking shelter in other governorates. Following the retaking of Anbar Governorate, displaced families slowly started to return to their homes. According to the October 2018 update of the Displacement Tracking Matrix, around 182,850 individuals (30,475 families) already returned to the district of Heet from their areas of displacement.Taking into consideration the launch of Voluntary Returns Committees in 2018 and the strategic decision of closure of some of the camps in Anbar, Salah Al Din and Baghdad, that host displaced families from Anbar among others, more returns will be observed in the next year.According to the assessment information that AL MAHABA WES-SALAM (AL MAHABA) NGO conducted in Heet District, a total of 1,250 shelters are damaged in Heet town and its vicinity due to the military operations that these areas witnessed during retaking. Returnee families found their houses damaged partially or totally and their furniture and belongings were lost. Reported damage is varies between category 1, 2, 3 & 4. Findings of Rapid Overview of Area of Return conducted by REACH Initiative asserted that 20% of shelters were damaged because of the conflict and that the areas of Anbar witnessed intensive damage. Presently, people living in damaged shelters do not possess tools and materials to repair their accommodation. The existing sub-standard living conditions and expected further damages are likely to impact more significantly those already most vulnerable, such as women, children and persons with disabilities, heightening existing vulnerabilities due to the lack of privacy and decreased safety in the shelters and community where they reside.Heet District is located on the banks of the Euphrates River, with fertile lands suitable for agriculture. Around 37% of families relied on agriculture as the main source of income in the past. In addition, industrial infrastructure in Anbar Governorate used to provide employment opportunities for families of the three targeted districts prior to the occupation by ISIL. However, it has not yet been reopened as of today, causing a huge employment gap at the district level. Furthermore, public sector employees did not receive their salaries over 3 years of the ISIL control over Anbar. As a consequence, families were obliged to consume their personal savings, sell assets and rely on relatives and other social networks to cover their various needs. Based on this fact, many vulnerable families are not able to perform house repairs without humanitarian assistance especially women headed households, households with at least one person with special needs physically and mentally and extremely poor households with negative coping strategy.Both groups of population, returnees and stayees, in Heet District remain vulnerable, mainly due to the extensive damage of houses and infrastructure, high-level of contamination of the areas with Unexploded Ordinance (UXO) and the lack of basic services. Moreover, young boys have experienced several years of ISIL propaganda, while having limited or no access to education. These boys are highly prone to enduring long-term psychological trauma, based on interviewed families by the International Rescue Committee (IRC) in January 2018. In the same aforementioned assessment, interviewed women narrated that women and girls had a very restricted movement during the ISIL occupation, restricting their access to schools and livelihood opportunities, combined with pre-existing gender norms about female behaviour. Thus, vulnerable families have scares income generating opportunities and are not able to cover the critical needs of improving their shelter conditions and cope with weather conditions.

Activities

Specific Objective1: Improve living conditions and enhance resilience of vulnerable returnee families in Heet District. Outcome 1.1: Damaged houses under category 2 & 3 of the most vulnerable returnee families in Heet District are rehabilitated. Outcome 1.2: Damaged houses under category 1 of the most vulnerable host communities, including returnees and stayees, families in Heet Districts receive SOK kits for house repair. CCR will commence the proposed project with a preliminary community level assessment in Heet District to evaluate essential needs and prioritize neighbourhoods/ communities with highest percentages of war damage. The community level assessment will inform on the essential repairing materials and tools for shelters which will play a vital role in selecting the components of the Sealing Off Kits. The components of the SOK will be tailored with the specific needs of shelter rehabilitation and will be consulted with the Shelter Cluster. CCR will avoid the procurement of items that would not usefully meet identified needs.CCR and Al Mhaba will conduct a social and economic vulnerability assessment along with technical household level assessment in the prioritized communities. This house to house assessment will enable technical teams to evaluate damage category and classify houses into damage categories according to the Shelter Cluster guidelines. CCR will make sure of communicating the selection criteria with not only selected households but also other community members through community leaders and public announcements. The social and economic vulnerability assessment tool will be consulted with the Cash Technical Working Group in Iraq.Shelter engineers will visit selected vulnerable houses and prepare BOQs of required items for the rehabilitation. BOQs will be combined and supported with photos that show damage before intervention. In the meantime, monitoring and evaluation staff will conduct market assessment to confirm the availability of shelter repair items in the local market. The findings of the market assessment will advise the best practice and methodology of implementing the shelter repairs whether through direct purchasing of materials locally by signing framework agreements with local suppliers or through subcontracting to specialized contractors who might be able to transport required materials into the area of implementation. In both cases, CCR will strive to strengthen and avoid harming local market. Local suppliers are expected to mobilize local skilled workers to perform the rehabilitation tasks which will provide livelihood and income generating opportunities in the local community.CCR and Al Mhaba will rehabilitate 100 damaged shelters. Findings of the technical assessment will determine the exact number of shelters per damage category.CCR and Al Mahaba will also target damaged shelters under category 1 with minor and small-scale damage. Partners will distribute a total of 330 Sealing Off Kits (SOK) to the most vulnerable families whose houses fall under damage category 1. The SOKs are intended to upgrade shelters to protect families from weather conditions, improve privacy and personal safety measures. Families will use SOKs in repairing windows, doors and locks as well as installing required plastic sheets for insolation and privacy of latrines and showers. CCR will ensure that beneficiaries are briefed and trained on the use of SOKs by the distribution team. Distribution team will also use leaflets that provide guidance on how to use these kits. SOK kits will be procured through national call for tender, local suppliers will be prioritized to enhance the capacity of local market. As mentioned above, components of the kits will be determined and prioritized based on the identify needs of vulnerable families. In-kind distribution methodology is selected based on the context dimensions. Vulnerable families are less likely to have familiarity with rehabilitation materials to negotiate in the market and to have means of transportation specifically women-headed or child-headed families. In-kind distribution will mitigate the risk of exploitation of vulnerable families and will eliminate tailored cost of transportation of individuals and materials. Families will be trained on the best use of the SOK kits by the distribution team. Illustrating leaflets will be distributed along with the SOK kits as well.CCR will ensure that the proposed intervention do no harm in terms of HLP management. CCR will verify the HLP practices that vulnerable families proceeded already and refer any issue to the community resource centre in Anbar to coordinate these cases with HLP actors in Anbar. CCR will ensure high level of coordination and collaboration with relevant actors and Shelter Cluster to identify best practise and lessons learnt.

Protection Risk Analysis

Specific objective 1:Risk 1.1: Deterioration of the security context in Anbar that hinders access to the targeted areas in Heet District. Risk 1.2: Tension among local communities during the selection of beneficiaries based on the social and economic vulnerability criteria. Risk 1.3: Tension among local community because of including families who are accused to be affiliated to certain armed groups. Risk 1.4: Shelter rehabilitation repair tools and materials are not available in the local market of Heet. Risk 1.5: Selected vulnerable families have limited knowledge and skills to improve their shelter conditions using the received items of the Sealing Off Kits. Risk 1.6: Targeted communities might be contaminated with Unexploded Ordinance and explosive remnants of war.

Mitigation Measures

Specific objective 1:Risk 1.1: CCR and Al Mahaba will negotiate proper access with local authorities to the areas of proposed activities. Partners will recruit local staff which might alleviate the implications of limited access. Partners have operational offices in Ramadi and Qa’im. Risk 1.2: CCR and Al Mahaba will properly communicate the criteria of selection with local community through community leaders and public announcements to mitigate community tension. Partners will ensure that selected families are aware of the selection criteria and they understand why they were selected over others. Risk 1.3: CCR and Al Mahaba will explicitly communicate the selection criteria and steps with local community through local leaders and public announcement. Partners will mobilize local leaders to communicate that selection is made on needs-based of families based of neutrality and impartiality principles. Risk 1.4: CCR will subcontract the rehabilitating activities to a specialized company that might be able to supply materials from stable areas in centre and south of Iraq. Risk 1.5: Partners will ensure that targeted families receive proper training on the best usage of the SOKs components and receive illustrating printed illustrating manuals along with the SOKs. Partners will identify specific cases of families with no able-bodied member by mobilizing local community members to support. Trainings will include: 1) personal safety and fire prevention and response, 2) good practices of constructing using the SOK components and installation guidance and 3) potential risks and hazards within the shelter and means of mitigation. Risk 1.6: Partners will consult mine action agencies who are active in the area as well as local authorities to ensure that targeted houses and areas are cleared from explosive hazard.

How is your project accountable to affected population

This intervention was proposed based on the partners’ initial needs assessments carried out prior to the project preparatory phase and developed further in coordination with local partner. The data was collected through local partner’s intervention in Heet District as Al Mahaba is implementing similar shelter interventions in West Anbar and have a strong network with local authorities and relevant institutions.During the implementation phase, both organizations will ensure a high level of community engagement throughout all steps of the project cycle. This has started already through the communication with local leaders (Mokhtars) and municipality in Heet Districts and with potential target households.CCR and Al Mahaba will ensure that beneficiaries, both male and female, are briefed on details of the project structure and benefits that it can bring to the local households.Beneficiaries will be consulted during community level assessment on their preference of the Sealing Off Kits components through highlighting essential materials and tools they lack to improve their shelter conditions. Partners will communicate the criteria of selection with the community through local authorities and leaders like Mokhtars and religious leaders and through public announcements and leaflets in the public places to ensure that the community understand the criteria of selection and the logic behind selection. During activities implementation, CCR will apply a complaint mechanism using a hotline that link directly to CCR office in Erbil. CCR will provide a complaint mobile box with the distribution team too. CCR will ensure that complainants receive timely feedback. Partners will conduct a post distribution monitoring assessment by the end of SOKs distribution and shelter rehabilitation activities to measure the overall satisfaction level of targeted families with the received kits and services and report success stories and lessons learnt. The intervention team is aware of the complexity and deeply-rooted patriarchal norms governing social and economic life of local communities and that their disruption will take a very long-time. Nonetheless, it is envisaged that this intervention can contribute to some positive achievements.CCR will specifically coordinate with the Community Resource Centres in Anbar Governorate to address any identified HLP issues that vulnerable families might encounter. CCR will ensure doing no harm in the intervention and that families have proper documents and documented the damage already with relevant authorities.
