Project Summary

2.4 M children in Somalia are in need of urgent protection assistance account for 66% of the population in need in Somalia. Of this, 1.3m are Non-IDP children, 108.3k are children living in IDP Camps, 20.3k are refugees/ asylum seeking children and 87.2m are returnee children. Children with disability account for 10%.
Compared to the 2021 analysis, the estimated number of people in need increased by 25 per cent in 2022. According to the JMCNA 2021 it is estimated that 607,546 children are in extreme need (26 per cent per cent), 1,449,144 in severe need (61 per cent) and 302,440 in stress (13 per cent), and require humanitarian child protection assistance.
According to HNO 2022, children (boys and girls) face existential risks of violence at home, school and in the community. The key drivers of child protection risks in Somalia are; conflicts/displacements, recurrent climatic shocks and devastating socio-economic impacts of COVID-19 Pandemic. These triple shocks have perilously exposed children to risks of violence, exploitation, increase vulnerabilities and shrunk their protective environment.
Somalia’s youthful population is estimated to be 81% (below 35 years of age) of this 27% are age 19 years and below. This age bracket is compelled to adopt maladaptive coping mechanisms due to structural deprivation in the society. Children are faced with risks of rape, violence, murder, recruitment to armed groups and early marriages and FGM. These risks are exacerbated by protracted forced displacement, cyclic conflicts, and limited access to child protection services in the country.
Somalia is one of the countries that records the highest grave violations against children. In the first half of 2021 alone 1,797 grave violations affecting 1,392 children (330 girls; 1,062 boys) have been documented, with 631 children (97 per cent boys aged 11-17) recruited and used by parties to the conflict, accounting for 15% rise compared to the same period in 2020. Particularly at risk are adolescent girls who are faced with risks of child marriage, sexual violence, and other forms of gender based violence. CTFMR reports arise in the cases sexual assault against girls by parties to the conflict, rising from an average of 23 reported cases per month in 2020 to 27 cases monthly in 2021 during the same period. Middle Shabelle, one of the target regions of this project, is one of the regions that reported the highest incidents rape and sexual violence. Though Somalia constitution out-laws child marriage, the country still records high rates of early marriages, one in three girls is married off before age18.
According to June 2021 SPMS report, child marriage is commonest protection risks faced by girls out of school, particularly those in areas under armed groups of CSZ and Puntland. Those attending schools have also reported sexual harassment by teachers. A quarter of children population in Somalia are employed, a phenomenon that is common even in relatively stable regions. The prevalent forms of Child labor are: recruitment to armed groups, begging under difficult conditions, and engaged as house helps.
The number of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) continues to rise, with more than 11,235 (5,802 girls; 5,230 boys) UASC documented from January to August 2021, a 24 per cent increase when compared to last year during the same period. This is attributed to: conflict, migration, COVID-19 pandemic, and deaths of a child’s caregiver. Separation from one’s primary caregivers exposes children to greater risks of exploitation, abuse, neglect, and psychosocial distress.
The proposed project will: provide structured MHPSS services to children and care givers alike, identify and reunify separated children with primary care givers or alternative care givers, train CP actors to increase their knowledge and skills, enhance CMCPMs’ capacities and carry out community led messaging on CP and conduct safety walks in targeted IDPs.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups who are not often included inconsultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be deliberately targeted to partake in decisions. Clear stakeholder analysis will be undertaken to ensure inclusivity at decision making levels of the project. Women, girls and boys are often the ones left out in consultations and decision-making processes because of their age due cultural barriers. The project will ensure community decision makers will be representatives of all, young and old, men and women, boys and girls toensure the specific needs of each are adequately listened to and addressed. The project will address the risk of exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. This will be done through: staff and vendors signing code of conduct that expressly prohibits the above violations and makes consequences of the same clear.Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services will be addressed through scheduling of distributions, meetings and consultative forums in such a way that they do not pose risks to beneficiaries, particularly girls and women. The distances covered by girls and women to seekservices will also be compliant to Sphere Minimum Standards and other best protection best practices. Synergies and collaborations will be developed with other partners in the field shelter and camp coordination to ensure there is complementarity in addressing needs of IDPs in holistic manner. Aid Vision runs health and nutrition projects in Jowhar District. This will complementthe proposed protection project.
