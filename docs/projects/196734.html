Project Summary

GC implements integrated protection services to prevent, mitigate, and respond to harm exploitation, and abuse for the most vulnerable boys, girls, men, and women in northern Syria in Idleb, Hassakeh, Raqqa, and Deir Ez-Zor governorates, through static, mobile, and outreach approaches. Under this program, GC will manage 16 safe spaces and 11 mobile teams and will also conduct online activities to respond to and mitigate GBV, CP, and PSSissues within the target communities. Safe spaces, in the form of Community Centers(CCs), Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS) and Women/Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS), YouthEmpowerment Centers will provide vital protection response services including case management, skill development, and livelihood courses, basic PSS, referrals to the external service providers, while the outreach teams will primarily be focused on the provision of prevention services including awareness-raising, community mobilization, information dissemination, engaging men and boys through accountable practices to ensure meaningful access and expand support to isolated communities. The objective of the program is to improve access to and awareness of safe and dignified, survivor-centered life-saving responses to address the needs of extremely vulnerable individuals mainly survivors of gender-based violence with a focus on at-risk groups in thecommunities affected by the crisis to build resilience through learning positive coping mechanisms. Hence, GC will continue the above-mentioned activities, however with a focus on preparedness and resilience to respond to heightened protection risks as a result of future crisis/displacement. Response and mitigation interventions will continue, but time, frequency, location, and running will be adapted to income-generating, community empowerment activities in recovery rather than post-disaster context. GC will ensure that communities have an increased ability to withstand protection-related consequences of displacements, through awareness of risks, prevention, and mitigation of protection issues. Community-embedded systems for child protection, GBV, and Community BasedProtection will be in place/strengthened, enabling particularly female-headed household heads to engage in safe livelihood opportunities and reduce the risk of exploitation and negative coping mechanisms. Mainly there will be no change in the centers as these centers are currently supported and are well established, known, and trusted in the community and have sufficient human resources to introduce the two new activities proposed: the mentorship program and GBV community dialogue workshops. However, GC is discontinuing protection activities in Hasakeh City in the Al-Hasakeh governorate and will not be able to fund a much-needed expansion of protection activities to Derik City, which has significant unmet needs for protection services. 3 - Needs

This violent conflict has led to a severe protection crisis in NES with children and women in particular exposed to serious protection risks. Inadequate living conditions in make-shift settlements or overcrowded sites, particularly in NES, as well as the loss of livelihoods, property, and asset compound protection risks, including risks of GBV and CP concerns. Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbated these risks and represented an additional barrier to access to services for survivors with an increase in GBV within the home, including IPV and domestic violence. The impact of GBV on women and girls is tremendous and long-lasting, aggravated by victim-blaming, social stigma, and social isolation. Regarding GC need assessment, In both NES and NWS, GC has found in our ATLAS baseline that women and girls are more susceptible to GBV, including early/forced marriage, domestic violence, sexual harassment and violence, denial of resources, and psychological and physical abuse. Similarly, we have found that 97% of past program participants have reported an improvement in feelings of well-being and ability to cope due to participation in GC programming, indicating persistent needs and gaps that would go unaddressed should the supports be removed.

4 - Implementation of activities

GC has been implementing specialized and non-specialized protection services in northern Syria since early 2016. We are currently operational in Idleb, Raqqa, Hassakeh, and Deir Ez-Zor governorate and will continue working in these locations throughout 2022 to target17,500 beneficiaries in NES and 89,000 beneficiaries in NWS by providing direct implementation comprehensive protection services, covering CP, GBV, and Community-Based Protection through static facilities and mobile teams. We support vulnerable IDPs and host community members through services in and out of camps and informal settlements where there is no access to community-based PSS support and children and women score low on protection indicators such as sense of safety and well-being and Resilience. With a child survivor, the case is referred internally between the CFS and WGSS team or CP and GBV caseworkers. If the child needs services that GC does not provide, then we proceed with an interagency referral. Also, GC will continue using GBVIMS+Primero (a GBV information management system) for the GBV case management data in NWS. Individual PSS and counseling will continue to play an important role in case management where both the psychological aspects are related to the survivor and the social circumstances. Also, caseworkers will focus on facilitating access to services with survivors’ consent such as health, education, and livelihood opportunities, legal services, and access to durable solutions.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risks: raising expectations of response/services in locations where services may be limited/non-existent Measures: Develop clear communication to manage expectations and receive feedback on the quality and usefulness of the information being provided. Member agencies to use referral pathways where possible. Risks: GBV service providers' safety and security because of their role and PTS dealing with violence. Privacy concerns on confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging. Persons with disability have difficulties accessing GBV services. Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girls’ mobility. Measures: Self-care strategies as part of programs and organization HR policy. GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, and ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry points. CP Risks: Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All staff, partner staff, and contractors will be trained on PSEA with refresher briefing to be conducted on a regular basis. When recruited, all staff will have signed the GC’scode of conduct and PSEA policy. PSEA will be the responsibility of all staff, partner staff, and contractors and will be a core part of all activities and outputs in this project. To ensure that communities are actively engaged in recognizing and mitigating risks associated with SEA and to prevent abuse, we will scale up efforts to provide messaging to communities with their rights to humanitarian assistance, vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse, reporting mechanisms, and correct access to services with the support of protection, feedback, and M&E teams.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The border crossings to Syria will remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff, without extended closures, and/or alternate border points are open and accessible. Markets continue to function and goods are available locally. If the border closes for humanitarian goods or commercial traffic and our ability to receive essential items or funds, we will consider other, less accessible means of delivery. GC/CHF staff continue to have consistent access to target locations while maintaining acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality. We will ensure this by continuing to utilize our community engagement team to liaise with the relevant stakeholders and continue to reinforce that we are a humanitarian agency that abides by the humanitarian principles of independence, neutrality, and impartiality. Should our level of access change significantly or impact our ability to work within our humanitarian principles, we will look to identify alternative locations or solutions. Active violence does not directly impact our areas of operation for an extended period of time; Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level that allows GC/CHF to implement the program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable for provision of services.
