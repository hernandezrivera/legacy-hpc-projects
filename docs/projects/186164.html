Project Summary

The Anglophone crisis that started in 2016 has led to the frustration of some people in the North West and the South West region of Cameroon. For five years since this crises started, many people have become stranded and due to the destruction of properties, businesses, burning of homes, family members killed, this has rendered families homeless which has caused them to be internally displaced to areas that are better secured. This has really displaced men, women and most especially the youths and the disabled persons from some villages in Donga-Mantung to come and settle in Nkambe town which is secured. These youths and women from these affected villages of Donga Mantung: Wat, Luh, Ngarum, Taku, Chup, Tabenken, Lower And Upper Mbot, Njap, Ako Nwa, Ndu etc displaced to Nkambe central town have involved themselves in unhealthy practices for example some female youths have engaged themselves into prostitution and have acquired sexually transmitted infections, some youths are involved in stealing, some have died, some get early or unwanted pregnancies, some have been raped both by non-state armed men and military, others have become beggers due to lack of income generating activities and some are sick and do not have adequate treatment. Unhealthy practices like prostitution and promiscuity has increased the overall infection rate of HIV/AIDS and other venereal disease in this community as compared to the period before the crisis.
This affected population who have been displaced to the central town have received some support such as food items from organizations but this has not helped them because what they need is not only food, they also need other things like sanitary kits, livelihood activities and startup capitals. So, they need to be taught how to fish for themselves rather than given fish all the time.
Because of all these, we wish to train 1800 internally displaced persons (IDPs) on livelihood activities then provide them with start-up capital/materials after training to be independent and also distribute sanitary kits to female youths to improve their menstrual hygiene and sanitation.
Response plan
- Activity1: Train displaced women, young girls including the disable aged 13 and above on the production of beaded shoes and locally made “omo” (detergent).
 Indicator1: Three training sessions will be done after every three months to train 900 women, youths (females and males) including the disabled then start-up materials will be provided to them at the end of the training.
- Activity 2: Train women and youths on poultry and pig farming.
 Indicator 2: 500 displaced women and youths (males and females) including the disabled persons will be trained on poultry and pig farming then chicks (broilers), piglets, feed and other materials to sustain the livestock will be provided to them at the end of the training.
- Activity 3: Train displaced boys (youths) in barbing skills.
 Indicator 3: 150 young men will be trained on barbing skills and start-up materials such as rechargeable shaving machines, mirrors, shaving chairs and disinfecting materials will be provided at the end of the training.
- Activity 4: Train displaced youths and women on gardening skills.
 Indicator 4: 200 displaced youths and women who indicate to have plots and can do gardening will be trained on gardening skills and then provided with seeds and farm tools at the end of the training.
- Activity 5: Provision of health relief materials (sanitary kits) to 600 female youths from within the project population.
Overall indicator: Mid-term and end-term evaluations shall be carried out to ensure the sustainability of the project and also to measure the life-changing effects of the intervention.
We have targeted Nkambe because it is the central town that has accommodated all the displaced persons from various villages in Donga/Mantung and because it is also safe for the population and to carryout trainings.
