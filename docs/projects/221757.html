Project Summary

This project is an intersectional life-saving and resilience-building project that is going to be implemented in the Twic East, Duk, and Uror counties of Jonglei State. The resilience of communities in the three counties has been tested repeatedly by natural disasters (floods and, in some cases, dry spells), conflicts, tribal attacks, cattle rustling, and abduction of children and women. Twic East has been challenged with flood since 2020 to date; while much effort has been made on primary dyke repair (western side of the county across Sudd wetland to mitigate flood from the Nile) by WFP and its partners in 2024, the flood has already affected the community. The county has faced floods, which are less than what happened in 2021 compared to its impact and coverage. Similarly, the Western part of Duk County has experienced floods since 2020, and in 2024, from rain and mainly from Nile River water flowing through the Jonglei canal to Duk from the side of Bor South County and Twic East County. Uror County was affected by torrential rain from July to August 2024, and the floods impacted the community negatively. Coupled with natural disasters, man-made disasters, physical access challenges to the counties, high inflation rate and poor economic performance of the community, and the impact of flood ( damage to planted crops, collapses of shelter, submerging of schools, water points, sanitary infrastructure, impact of overall livelihood), the majority of community has been facing food insecurity, effect of weather on health due to poor shelter of displaced sites, children hardly getting education as state in need component of this proposal. In IPC, Twic East and Uror are projected to be in IPC 4 (emergency) from December 2024 to July 2025, while Duk will be in IPC (crises) from December 2024- to March 2025 and in IPC4 from April to July 2025.
In response to these multifaceted challenges, the project is planned in an integrated and intersectoral manner to address needs for food, livelihoods, shelter, NFI, and education. The project addresses food needs for households and individuals through unconditional cash for food while supporting the same families for cereal and crop production and fishing where applicable; in addition, the project works with the farming community on climate-smart farm field schools and community-managed disaster risk reduction. The supported people can also get shelter and NFI assistance depending on their status (IDPS, returnees& host with a special focus on Most Vulnerable households and Persons with special needs). learners from these communities can be supported and empowered to attend schools in a designated manner. The project will mainstream protection/ child protection and GBV in all its interventions.
Currently, LWF is identified 2000 HHs for shelter/NFI support through cash modality , for whole 2024 it has been supporting 21 schools in the areas and supporting agriculture and fishery at different scales
The project will be intended to reach 89,019 most vulnerable people in Twic East, Duk, and Uror with the integrated intersectoral response,
The project will be implemented with coordination and collaboration with partners and stakeholders on the ground in lieu of the sustainability of its outcome. That means it will ensure its outcome’s sustainability by working with the customary institutions, line departments, women groups, youth groups, and local administration from the beginning of the project and coordinating with humanitarian actors at all levels.
LWF will try to achieve these HNRP objectives by soliciting funding via SSHF and LWF strategic partners such as CLWR/CFGB, ALWS, ACT CoS, GNC, and ACT Forum members.

1. Needs

Jonglei State, South Sudan’s largest and most populous state, is home to approximately 1.1 million people across 11 counties. It is one of the most affected states by flood, conflict, and related crises, and communities’ resilience has been challenged repeatedly. Twic East and Duk have been particularly hard hit by recurrent floods from 2020 to 2024. In response to floods, humanitarian actors like the World Food Programme (WFP) have partnered with others to rehabilitate primary dykes along the Nile River and the Sudd wetlands to mitigate flooding. Nevertheless, the counties have yet to recover fully from past floods, and continued heavy rains in the region since June 2024 have worsened conditions. Initial Rapid Assessments (IRNA) conducted in October 2024 in Duk County, 22,750 (14236F, 8514M) people were displaced, while IRNA September revealed 55,300 (32923F, 22426m) people in Twic East faced similar fates. The floods have displaced these populations and destroyed nearly 70% of cereal and vegetable crops, further threatening food security in the region; most schools are affected by the flood, and shelter is taken by water, especially in Twic East and part of Ageer, Payuel and Padeit params of Duk and some are submerged. Displacement and crop destruction have deepened the food gap in affected areas, leaving many families struggling to obtain even one meal a day. In Twic East, the community depends on food assistance complemented by fishing to feed their families, and women also collect wild fruits and water lilies to feed their families. In Duk communities in three of the six payams viz. Payuel, Dongchak, Padeit, and Ageer params focus on fish catch and hunting of wild animals to get meals for the families, while hunting is also a means of substance in Panyang and Pagaak. In Uror, the flood in July and August 2024 due to heavy rain displaced over 22,290 population. It affected most farms and settlements, leading people to lack food and live in poor, risky shelters. According to the IPC report on acute food security in November 2024, for the period covering December 2024-March 2025, Twic will be under IPC 4 with a population of 26000, Duk County will be under IPC 3 with 31000 people under IPC 4, and Uror will be under IPC 4 with 51000 people. For April 2025, Twic East, Duk, and Uror counties will fall under IPC4 with 38,000, 51000, and 61000 populations. In Affected areas of Duk, people who settled across the Jonglei canal and in the Primary dyke areas in Twic East lack proper shelter, hence exposed to bad weather, mosquitos, reptiles, and protection threats of GBV. The flooding, human-made crises, and limited investment in infrastructure have severely impacted the availability and access to essential services, particularly children’s education. In 2024, the education sector faced numerous obstacles, including natural disasters, insecurity, a lack of resources such as teachers, and poor education facilities. Flooding partially/or fully affected East County schools and Payuel, Padeit, and Ageer Payams of Duk County. Lack of access to basic amenities, including clean water and soap, and lack of toilets in schools affected by flood/ toilets damaged by flood/ hampers education. Barriers to education include distance to schools, limited financial resources, and cultural perceptions discouraging girls from attending school; delayed or unpaid teacher salaries result in high turnover rates and poor-quality teaching. Children with disabilities face challenges accessing quality education due to limited resources, infrastructure, and inadequate teacher training. A gender-based needs assessment conducted in Twic East, Duk, and Bor South in July 2023 and updated in 2024 revealed that patriarchal structures continue to dominate decision-making.The project uses a cash model based on market analysis to respond to emergency food assistance and life-saving/shelter NFI; however, depending on the context, look for options.
