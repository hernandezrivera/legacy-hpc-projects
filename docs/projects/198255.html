Project Summary

Ensure access to and continuity of essential primary health and MHPSS services, including capacity building, procurement of equipment and medicines, service delivery, emergency referrals and outbreak response and surveillance operations for vulnerable displaced people and host communities. This will be implemented through an integrated community-based approach in Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces and through the deployment of mobile clinics, mobile brigades and fixed health posts.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Through this project IOM proposes to deliver community-based support, supplement primary health care service delivery to hard-to-reach populations and improve access to mental health care in Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces, which continue to be affected by pre-existing social issues and ongoing violence and displacement. IOM will provide primary health care services through the staffing and operation of mobile clinics and fixed health posts in hard-to-reach areas, support integrated mobile brigades, and provide trainings and mentorship for healthcare workers, to meet the needs of displaced persons and host communities. Further, IOM integrated teams, in coordination with local authorities, will ensure community-based support for mental health and psychosocial support services, TB/HIV care and referral to specialized services. Community health workers will conduct community engagement, health promotion and community based surveillance activities in resettlements sites and among host communities to identify and respond to cases of communicable diseases and chronic conditions, including HIV, TB, COVID-19, diarrheal diseases, and more broadly public health events with epidemic potential. This latter support will include capacity building of health authorities on public health surveillance activities. IOM will ensure coordination with DPS, SPS, SDSMAS, MISAU, INS and the health cluster partners at national level and Cabo Delgado. Key target groups will be IDPs and host community members including women, girls, men, boys, PWDs and the elderly.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM has a mandatory instruction, which binds staff members to standards of behavior at all times and especially when working with beneficiaries of assistance, including during emergency response. Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and is grounds for disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate. In 2019, IOM launched the We Are All In platform, which includes a publicly available reporting form and an internal space with awareness-raising and training materials. IOM has also made PSEA commitments that include inter-agency coordination to prevent and address SEA incidents, including active participation at cluster levels in Mozambique and engagement with the PSEA focal point. IOM provides humanitarian health services free of charge and all IOM staff attend an obligatory training on PSEA and health projects are required to outline necessary actions for the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. There will be regular reminders advocating for all health responders to undergo training on PSEA, and to facilitate training for their target populations. As much as possible, health teams will consider gender balance in staffing, so that women and girls feel safe when they access services.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

IOM has an integrated protection-MHPSS approach which ensures intersectional considerations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with health programming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM Health Teams who support mobile brigades and community based activities and referrals are trained on protection principles. IOM identifies individual and structural-level protection risks by conducting the protection/safety audits in the districts. Also, the individual cases are identified and referred to specialised protection services in order not to cause further harm. IOM follows strict data protection rules and assistance standards in order to ensure meaningful access, not to cause further harm and empower individuals and communities assisted.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams identify people with special needs including individuals with physical and mental disabilities under the integrated health-protection-MHPSS activities. Also, IOM uses a diverse range of health information dissemination materials and channels (including door-to-door, community theatre groups, radio, and other community-based channels) to reach individuals with impairments.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project will include construction work on fixed health outposts with minimal environmental impact. IOM’s community-based health and MHPSS activities strive to use environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for the environment is included in some awareness-raising sessions with communities.
