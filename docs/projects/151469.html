Project Summary

This project is part of scale up of emergency WASH activities in Banadir, Lower and Middle Shabelle regions of Somalia. Over 126,400 beneficiaries’ disaster displaced IDP population are targeted in Afgoye corridor, Arbiska, Lafole, Balcad and Jowhar district. At the moment, IDPs and urban poor and rural community are among the most vulnerable groups in the society. Since the capacity of the local community to address their needs is very weak it is clear that the increasing number of IDPs and urban poor in crisis requires an urgent comprehensive action to support the target populations.
The project responds to urgent humanitarian needs including: (1) Deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable settlements and communities (2) 2 - Reduce risk of violence against women, children and vulnerable groups when accessing WASH services, and (3) Provide or restore sustainable access to safe water and adequate sanitation services in targeted communities and institutions.
In Banaadir, population influx has put pressure on existing WASH services. There is a risk of disease outbreak in the new IDP settlements due to poor sanitary conditions, rampant open defecation, poor hygiene practices, and lack of safe water supply. The project will provide emergency water supply to IDPs through in kind water provision and or pipeline extensions. Project will provide hygiene kits, hygiene promotion and construct community latrines. In Middle and Lower Shabelle the project will target riverine farming communities where AWD/cholera outbreak is common, and the community have high incidence rates of malnutrition in Somalia. Emergency water trucking will provide temporary safe water supply while sustainable exit strategies are put in place. Open defecation is widespread, and CLTS will be implemented in the early recovery phase of the project. Project will support WASH services and infection control measures at cholera treatment centres, case tracing and follow-up WASH actions in affected communities. Development of water supply infrastructure will be undertaken as part of exit strategy. Community institutions such as schools and health units should be used as entry point for behavioural change in hygiene practice. All activities will be delivered using a stronger protection lens and where feasible and adequate. Many components of the project will involve close collaboration with government and local communities including sensitivities surrounding latrine construction, land tenure and latrine de-sludging work.

Needs

The WASH HRP projects that About 2.9M people need urgent WASH assistance in Somalia. In October 2018, Polish Humanitarian Action’s (PAH) carried out a needs assessment in Banadir, Middle and Lower Shabele and Galmudug regions in supplement to secondary sources such as FSNAU analysis, cluster specific assessments and OCHA bulletins and IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix. The assessment found that water consumption to be low in IDP settlements due to high cost of water 1000 som/shilling for every 20L. Moreover, the capacity of water points was reported to be overstretch in many locations due to increasing IDP population. According to PAH’s assessment, in new informal settlements 32% of population interviewed stay at queues between 30-60 min every day before collecting one jerry-can, whilst 34% in Dharkenley and Lower Shabele remained in queues for 1-2 hours and 19% reported waiting over 2 hours to access water at overcrowded water points in Lower shabele. This has a specific impact on women and girls who reported limited time to engage in income earning activities, and school attendance due to water collection tasks. In terms of Hygiene, PAH’s assessment found that 27% of interviewed people do not have access to sanitary facilities. For those households that did, the majority (59%) use public or communal latrine, 38% use shared latrines and 3% use household latrines. HH surveys also found that 63% of IDP population don’t have access to soap for hand washing – contributing to high AWD prevalent rates. In PAH’s assessment, 30% of the households reported at least one of their family member experienced water related diseases in the past two weeks with the most common WASH related diseases reported were: diarrhoea (58%), cholera (25%), malaria (27%), skin infection (12%) and flu (7%). In drought and AWD/Cholera historical hotspots, there is a need to deliver more long-term WASH solutions when feasible to progressively improve the water and sanitation ladder.

Activities

Objective 1: Deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable settlements and communities. This objective will be achieved through temporary access to safe water, provision of emergency latrines and equipping targeted communities with good hygiene practice. Under this activity PAH will provide water to 1800HH IDPs in Banadir. Each household will receive 60 liters of water per day. Water treatment will be done at the water source and house hold level to ensure the water are safe for drinking. Residual chlorine levels will be measured to ensure it’s in the recommended range. 270 emergency latrine will be constructed in the IDPs settlement and a total of 16,200 beneficiaries will benefit from this activity. Hygiene promotion session will be conducted at IDPs and community level to ensure the community are educated on good hygiene practices. Objective 2 - Reduce risk of violence against women, children and vulnerable groups when accessing WASH services; PAH will ensure water source are allocated in a safe distance that is safe and accessible to female and a girl to attend water collection during the day and night. The water point will be constructed at a minimum distance of 50m and maximum distance of 500m as require in WASH sphere standard.2100 individual (35 latrines) from Targeted IDP settlements will have access to a gender separated sanitation facility and located at less than 50 m from the household and equipped with a lockable door. Objective 3 - Provide or restore sustainable access to safe water and adequate sanitation services in targeted communities and institutions; As an exit strategy for the program PAH will provide sustainability water to 7,500 Beneficiaries through construction of 3 water kiosks and provision of spare parts to 4 boreholes. 90 Adequate sanitation facilities will form a pillar for this objective and a total of 5,400 Beneficiaries will benefit from this activity in Middle Shabelle.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Durable solutions have been factored in planning, design of the interventions under the programme. PAH will ensure the project is line with Somalia humanitarian responses plan for 2019. Moreover, Environmental sustainability will be taking in to consideration by ensuring the water sources are protected through the limitation of extracting of water to a capacity below what is available as well as sustainability in terms of design and managements. To ensure water output quality, the water source distance will be based on the maximum distance of 500m and source of contamination from surface water source to be expected within 30m radius to avoid contamination of groundwater and surface water source. The water kiosks and boreholes rehabilitation will be answerable to demand and will provide the required amount of 15 liters per person day. The proposed construction activities are accountable to the needs of beneficiaries and are in line with WASH cluster/MoEWR technical guidelines. Before the kick off of any construction activity PAH will liaise with Shelter, WASH cluster and Banadir local authority to ensure the IDP camp targeted have valid title deed and are not plan for any eviction. The establishment and capacity strengthening for WASH Committees and sanitation team, selection of water sources based on sanitary survey and conducting of water testing, will ensure the quality of water provision outputs. Distances will also be considered as per WASH sphere standards. PAH will also collect GPS coordinates of wash structures and share with WASH cluster/MoEWR. The Exit strategy for the emergency activities include rehabilitation of critical water infrastructure and capacity building of target groups in operation, maintenance and hygiene education. DRR Capacity building for flood mapping and infrastructure repair will also be integrated in the project. As a result of consultative meetings held with government counterparts in September 2018, project design considers environmental impacts, promotes locally appropriate technological options for WASH services, and affordability of services, management capacities and social cultural factors. Key stakeholders and target groups have been consulted and will be involved in implementation. Many components of the project will involve close collaboration with government and local communities including sensitivities surrounding latrine construction and land tenure. Senior FGS government teams (DG MoW&NR and senior engineers) have been consulted who provided inputs on water quality monitoring and environmental impact assessments to be included in the interventions. This engagement with FGS will enhance sustainability as their involvement will be on-going until project completion. PPP for water supply will strengthen sustainability of the water supply system. Training approaches/designs will promote adoption and behaviour change rather than just retention of knowledge.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

In Mogadishu IDPs, especially those fairly recently displaced constitute a group with very high protection – related vulnerabilities. Majority of them come from minority clans hence does not benefit from community-based safety nets or protection. This combined with lack of any legal protection framework or law enforcement makes IDPs prone to wide range of abuses. Forced-taxation and related abuse of IDPs human rights is a frequently encountered based on PAH findings. Camp managers, gatekeepers and landowners, expect return from providing services to IDPs. The process is rarely transparent and beneficiaries learn about the expected contribution usually only after they receive assistance. So far it seems the gatekeepers and related power structure cannot be eradicated, still their actions affect the do no harm principle and support aid diversion. PAH will address this issue by engaging with gatekeepers and camp managers and create a more accountable and transparent structure. In camps targeted with WASH assistance PAH wants to put focus on the promotion of IDPs rights both among gatekeepers and affected communities resulting in the formation of an acceptable framework for IDPs rights implementation in the camp. This will be validated by the camp community. These management structures will be included in resolving conflicts and supporting WASH Committees in the management of provided facilities. Gatekeepers capacity building will also cover issues of HLP and support for establishment of written land tenure agreements endorsed by local authorities will take place. At the same time PAH will clearly define and communicate the red lines for engagement with gatekeepers to both the power holders and beneficiaries. At the same time advocacy for durable solutions for IDPs will be undertaken including accountable management structures, local integration, HLP rights and support for written agreements between camp management and landowners.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

This project promotes gender equality. Challenges related to insufficient access to WASH have a significant gender dimension. Fetching water has been traditionally perceived as a women’s chore, but at the same time poses SGBV risks for women and girls. Women and men have been involved in planning and design of this project to factor unique needs of either gender. Siting of WASH facilities is particularly important to reduce SGBV risks. project will ensure effective participation of women in implementation and monitoring, and equitable gender representation in decision making processes. PAH will gather gender segregated information for planning and reporting and will ensure that service delivery considers and addresses the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men in a systematic manner.To mitigate the risk of violence against women, children and vulnerable groups when accessing WASH services, the infrastructure location (distance from settlements) and design in all project sites, will ensure safe and equal access. The queuing time will be reduced to less than 30min per water point which will also decrease potential protection risks. Latrines, with handwashing facilities will be constructed, based on community consultation on their location and design, ensuring needs of people with disabilities and elderly are addressed as well as gender-specific protection risks mitigated. Design will ensure disease transmission is prevented and will be in line with the protection guidelines to ensure safety and comfort for female users. Some of the latrines will also be adjusted to the needs of elderly and PLD.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Conflict-related displacement has been recently increasing constituting the main displacement trigger in Banadir. Forced evictions and abuse of HLP rights damage the fragile livelihoods assets owned by displaced families and have a negative impact on service delivery and its sustainability by humanitarian actors. Accountable delivery of aid is challenged by the existence of gatekeepers and land owners’ expectation to receive payments for the land rented to IDPs, usually as a percentage of overall received assistance. Cash and in-kind remains the main source of revenue extracted in form of taxation, often forced one. PAH will ensure all its beneficiaries are protected and are centred in the program while ensuring Do No Harm approach. PAH will carry out sensitization training for Gatekeepers as well as ensure active and effective feedback mechanisms.
