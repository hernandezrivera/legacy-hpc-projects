Project Summary

As a result of the lack of effective maintenance and the aging WASH infrastructure due to the prolonged closure and repeated escalation of hostilities, the Gaza Strip has seen a significant deterioration in WASH services. Although the coverage of water supply and sanitation services exceeds 90% overall, significant disparities exist, and the service itself is both irregular and below standards. This often leads to vulnerable households seeking alternatives, particularly for drinking water that is often expensive and of quality below the WHO standards. Access to sanitation services, especially outside the urban areas, remains a major challenge in terms of coverage and quality of service, especially for women and girls and people with disabilities.
UNICEF and its partners will undertake actions to address the urgent needs of the population of the Gaza Strip as outlined in the humanitarian needs overview for 2022. UNICEF and partners will focus on increasing access to WASH services among the most vulnerable communities and institutions by restoring, sustaining, and expanding coverage to reach the most vulnerable populations with a clear multisectoral approach and build on the humanitarian-development nexus. This project will ensure that the most vulnerable women, children, and men have sufficient domestic and drinking water storage capacity whenever it becomes available and have better access to safe water and sanitation facilities and services. UNICEF and the partners will also address the systemic gaps in humanitarian needs, especially targeting female-headed households, including widows. UNICEF also discussed with its partners the importance of having the voice of the participating communities heard and receiving their feedback. UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in programmes by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners, including complaint mechanisms. The proposed activities will take into consideration the special needs of women and girls – for example: design of sanitation facilities with specific attention paid to location, safety and protection, and those families and children targeted through e-vouchers and the Child Sensitive Cash Support in Gaza implemented by UNICEF, focusing on the most vulnerable families. Furthermore, this proposal aims to strengthen the WASH partners' capacity to better prepare and respond to potential seasonal flooding, which is expected to be the worse in 2022 due to the impact of the May 2021 escalation of hostilities. As part of the UNICEF framework on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), the office had designated two complaints handlers.
Therefore, this project aims to improve access to basic life-saving WASH services for 238,000 people, especially among vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip. The project will address the negative coping strategies employed by vulnerable, such as reducing the frequency and duration of handwashing, sharing sanitation facilities, and overcrowding at public water points, due to limited or lack of access to water supply and sanitation facilities, engaged in resulting from the lack of access to WASH services. These coping strategies undermine infection prevention and lead to increased COVID-19 community transmission and other public health and social, economic consequences, coordinated with other key areas such as child protection, health, and education. Additionally, the project will cover advocacy tasks for the limited technical and financial capacities of the WASH service providers.

1 - Needs

The WASH Cluster partners’ assessments and consultations identified four main key issues generating people’s need for WASH assistance: insufficient water supply to households, poor sanitation and solid waste services, limited WASH services to public facilities, and risk of flooding.The May 2021 escalation of hostilities in the Gaza Strip led to the damage of at least 290 WASH infrastructures, worsened the already fragile, inadequate, and poorly maintained system due to the prolonged closure and restriction of access to essential operation and maintenance components. In coordination with relevant authorities and cooperation with the Palestinian Water Authority, WASH Cluster conducted several rapid needs assessments; the first assessment covered 25 municipalities across all Gaza Strip to assess the urgent needs related to the impact of COVID 19, including the maintenance requirements associated with the operational WASH facilities. The second assessment was conducted to identify winterization needs at the community level to implement flood preparedness and response interventions. Another technical assessment coordinated with Khanyounis Municipality showed the need to construct a stormwater system and protect AbuAlReesh area closed to AlAmal neighborhood in Khanyounis from winter flooding. Furthermore, the WASH cluster, through different implementing partners, conducted WASH in institutions assessments covering both schools and health care facilities. The assessments reflected the current situation in schools and health care centres and presented the WASH needs. As a result of the assessments, UNICEF with its implementing partners in coordination with the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Health will address the WASH needs in the targeted schools and strengthen the emergency preparedness for the WASH facilities in the health centres through contingency planning and prepositioning supplies. In addition to that, and based on the WASH Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021 related to households, different partners updated their need assessments at the household level after the spread of COVID-19 and following the May escalation in 2021. Cluster partners conducted focus separate male and female group discussions and technical assessments across Gaza Strip. The assessments and field visits showed that the demands at the household level are still very high. Urgent interventions at WASH facilities, including rehabilitation of latrines and provision of water tanks and hygiene kits, are required. Many families lack the financial ability to afford hygiene materials to maintain personal hygiene and house cleanness and to have sufficient storage capacity for water during the interruption of water supply and shortage of power. All the assessments and focus groups were conducted, taking into account the specific gender needs of men, women, boys, and girls with disaggregated data collection. The discussions with various vulnerable groups, predominantly girls and women, in different localities estimated that families spend more than 20% of their income to access water. Women and girls identified access to hygiene products, including menstrual hygiene management, as a major financial burden on the head of the family. In most cases, they have resorted to negative and unhygienic coping strategies to cut costs. The use of shad and unhygienic sanitation facilities in schools was also noted as one of the obstacles to keep girls in schools. Furthermore, since 2018, in collaboration with the Child Protection Networks (CPNs) and through home visits, UNICEF and its partner have recognized households’ poor access to adequate and gender-sensitive WASH facilities. A detailed assessment was conducted to identify and meet the WASH needs of people with disabilities. Accordingly, UNICEF will focus on specific households as per the mentioned needs.

2 - Activities

In collaboration with the Palestinian Water Authority, Coastal Municipalities Water Utility, Ministry of Education, and Ministry of Health; UNICEF will work to implement the following activities in the Gaza Strip. UNICEF implementing partners will support data collection and preparation of detailed need assessments. They will contribute to the implementation of the community engagement activities and provide technical support during the design of the activities, including the Bill of Quantities and drawings, coordination with other partners and the cluster, project completion and handover and documenting any lessons learned and best practices. UNICEF partners will monitor and supervise the progress of the works daily and share issues and concerns with UNICEF. 1. Improve access to water through:• Supply and install 165 household water storage tanks to increase the storage capacity of households in Gaza, North Gaza, and Dier Al-Balah Governorates, targeting 1,208 vulnerable populations. Priority will be given to large families living in critical conditions, female-headed families, and families who have children with disabilities in the same targeted areas or assessed by the Child Protection networks.• Undertake repairs, operation, and maintenance of 7 water production and distribution installations. 2. Improving access to sanitation and hygiene through: • The installation/ rehabilitation of 15 wastewater pumping stations • Provision of 12 wastewater facilities with critical operation and maintenance materials and tools • Rehabilitation of sanitation facilities for 520 households to ensure accessibility, privacy, and ease of use for the 96 households that include a family member with physical disabilities (including elders).• Undertaking the delivery of 670 family hygiene kits to the most vulnerable identified households. This is to alleviate the significant financial burden experienced by women and girls. The kits include dignity and menstrual hygiene management products. During the delivery of the kits, PSEA awareness, feedback, and complaints channels will be delivered.• Undertaking social mobilization and hygiene promotion activities to improve hygiene practices among communities. The focus will be on infection prevention related to COVID-19 and other WASH-related infections. 3. Flood mitigation, prevention, and response through the following:• Rehabilitation of 15 stormwater systems, including installing stormwater, wastewater pipes, manholes, repairing drainage systems, removing solid waste and sludge from the valley streams to ensure people are protected from seasonal flooding.• Provision of 13 stormwater facilities with critical operation and maintenance materials and tools.• Construction of stormwater network in ALshiekh Nasser area in Khanyounis.• UNICEF will undertake capacity building of partners on flood prevention and response planning and will undertake flood prevention and impact mitigation awareness sessions with communities. This will include awareness on PSEA that includes the various complaints channels and feedback mechanisms and management of sensitive information. 4. Improve access to WASH services in Schools and Health Care Facilities through:• Rehabilitation of WASH latrines and rehabilitation of handwashing facilities in 28 targeted schools. The rehabilitation shall consider enhancing privacy and dignity especially for safe and hygienic menstrual management and improve accessibility and user-friendly for children with disabilities, this will also reduce the congestion around otherwise scarce facilities, thus increasing social distancing and improving COVID-19 infection prevention. The rehabilitation of the sanitation facilities will consider girls' unique needs, including enhancing privacy and dignity, especially for safe and hygienic menstrual management, improving accessibility, and making it user-friendly for children with disabilities.• Establish preparedness and response plans, including preposition of WASH supplies and materials for cleaning, disinfection, and maintenance.• Strengthen sectorial coordination, managerial and technical capacity for local health authorities in managing WASH in health facilities and appropriate hygiene practices. 5. Advocacy interventions: • UNICEF will develop advocacy articles with the partners about the limited technical and financial capacities of the WASH service providers in the Gaza Strip.

Women and men’s participation in workshops will strengthen social relations between family members and unify them in their efforts to assure their long-term water and sanitation needs are met. Where required, separate focus group discussions will be conducted to create an open environment where women are free to discuss key issues. A detailed survey, field visits, and water analysis will be conducted to supervise, monitor, and evaluate the activities. A Knowledge, Attitudes, and Practices (KAP) survey will be conducted to assess and evaluate the impacts of the activities on women, men and boys, girls, persons with disabilities, and the elderly. UNICEF and its implementing partners will contribute to the exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets, and stories among the cluster community, which are to be used for advocacy purposes. The monitoring procedures include regular program visits to the field where implementation progress, achievements of results, and means of verification are systematically checked against the agreed framework including the project's indicators. Recommendations in response to the field visit findings, including bottlenecks will be documented and systematically followed upon. Overall, the Project will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation, and the results will be communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through the establishment of a beneficiary feedback mechanism

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in programmes by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. All UNICEF partners have been assessed on their risk to SEA and given access to a capacity-building program, which is continuous this year. UNICEF is implementing a project to strengthen the capacity building of 12 national NGOs on AAP & PSEA. The work includes the development of AAP & PSEA Context Analysis, capacity building, and AAP/ PSEA system strengthening. UNICEF SoP has also initiated a new project to digitalize AAP-PSEA mechanisms in the country office. UNICEF has expanded support to the SAWA hotline to be able to handle SEA claims at the interagency level, which includes referral of claims among agencies. Awareness and communication actions have started to progressively publicize the PSEA system and the right of affected populations to the complaint.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards and WHO standards for medical equipment and medical supplies procured. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and mitigate any risks identified by end-users. 11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNICEF verify needs through WASH cluster members and through cluster assessments, vulnerability mapping conducted by the cluster, child protection networks and also MOSD data. This is to ensure targeting the most vulnerable. UNICEF applies multi-vulnerability targeting criteria to ensure equity and impartial interventions are designed.
