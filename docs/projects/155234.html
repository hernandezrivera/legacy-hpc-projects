Objectives

UNHCR will work towards enabling refugees to access shelter to minimum standards and live in dignity in secure settlements that improve their social, economic and environmental quality of life as a community. UNHCR will address the needs of the Rohingya and host population through a focus on mid-term shelter support and sustainable settlements. Objective 1. Provide lifesaving emergency Shelter/NFI support to households affected by natural disasters or other shocks.Objective 2. Improve living conditions; contributing to reduced suffering, disaster risk reduction, and enhanced protection, dignity, and safety.Objective 4. Promote use of sustainable solutions to reduce impact on the environment.

Needs

While all refugees have access to makeshift emergency shelters, at present, most do not live in dignity with access to minimum covered living space. Many, in particular women and girls, do not feel safe where they live and do not have access to minimum services or sustainable settlement and shelter possibilities. Their access to basic needs such as essential household items, domestic fuel and shelter remains limited and the need for humanitarian support remains widespread despite the efforts of humanitarian actors over the past year. ShelterThe extreme density in refugee settlements (45,000 people per square meter in Kutupalong settlement), the lack of space indoors, and the limited durability of refugees’ shelters (less than a year) have a negative impact on the physical and psychological well-being of refugees, in particular the well-being of women and children, and vulnerable refugees such as the elderly or persons with disabilities.Emergency shelters were constructed as temporary housing, for a short life-span, and most now require improvements or regular maintenance to ensure refugee’s living conditions remain acceptable until temporary shelters are replaced with mid-term solutions. Adequate, upgraded shelter for all Rohingya refugees is critical. Refugees need materials, tools, labour and cash support to meet upgrade and site improvement needs. Many are going into debt to secure access to land or shelter materials, leaving them vulnerable to exploitation, including boys while girls are especially vulnerable to child marriage: over 78% of the households assessed by the sector had purchased additional material for their shelter, such as bamboo, tarpaulin, and rope. With the above needs in mind, the Shelter/NFI Sector Partners carried out a shelter survey in July and August of 2018 to further guide the Sector’s longer-term interventions. Refugees’ main concerns regarding their current shelters were related to strength of shelters, quality of materials, lack of private cooking and bathing spaces, and lack of ventilation and privacy. In 2019, UNHCR’s shelter interventions will aim at improving the durability and safety of refugees’ shelters taking into account space limitations and adverse weather conditions.Non-Food Items (NFI)New arrivals will continue to need basic household items while a number of families who received them more than a year ago may need these items replaced. Moreover lack of adequate lighting in shelters and public spaces was reported as a main safety concern along with theft as shelters, in particular shelters where weakened by the monsoon, can easily be broken into. Firewood is the main source of cooking fuel across all refugee settlements, and for Bangladeshi communities, and the collection, purchasing and selling of firewood for cooking fuel in the settlements is increasingly generating environmental, protection, and health risks for refugees. Smoke in the main accommodation is reportedly a contributor to high rates of upper respiratory tract infections, especially for women and girls who are often confined indoors. Safety concerns related to open fires inside the shelters are also high is such congested spaces, compounded with the limited emergency vehicles available and the lack of vehicular access in many areas where refugees have settled.In addition to the above needs, UNHCR estimates that overall, 50 % of the total number of households might require emergency shelter assistance during disasters, including monsoon and high wind.Based on the above needs, in 2019, UNHCR’s NFI interventions will aim at improving refugee’s safety and living conditions, while trying to mitigate the refugee population’s inevitable impact on the environment and the host community.

Activities or outputs

UNHCR's Age Gender and Diversity (AGD) policy seeks to ensure that all refugees fully participate in and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others. During implementation, the AGD policy is fundamentally linked to UNHCR's rights-based and community-based approach, which seeks to facilitate the meaningful participation of refugees in assessment, planning and monitoring processes and, therefore, decisions that affect their lives and communities. Following the joint rapid assessment and comprehensive participatory assessment it led in October and November 2017, UNHCR and its partners will continue to adopt this approach, based on identified needs, especially in case of cyclone, landslides, flooding, disease outbreak, fire or major change in the protection environment. Home visits for those unable to access registration and protection will ensure outreach to all individuals in need.AGD requires UNHCR and partner staff to adopt specific and appropriate methodologies for meaningful engagement with individuals and different groups (by developing child-friendly information materials and consultation exercises for men and women of different ages, for example), which aim at empowering both individuals and communities in decisions that affect their lives.In addition to the above, and following its long-standing experience in ensuring accountability to affected populations, UNHCR has put in place a two-way reporting and feedback and complaint mechanism to allow refugees express their opinions on the assistance provided. The complaint mechanism includes through in person feed-back, a hotline, letter and suggestion boxes at the Information Points it has established across some of the camps, and individual case follow-up and regular focus group discussions. It also allows the reporting of cases of sexual exploitation and abuse and of fraud, therefore is an integral part of PSEA activities integrated into UNHCR programming.ShelterIn 2019, UNHCR’s shelter interventions will aim at improving the durability and safety of refugees’ shelters taking into account space limitations and adverse weather conditions.More specifically, UNHCR proposes to transition to more durable shelter solutions through planning of camp upgrades, in order to shift the response to a resilient approach in areas that would allow it. To counter the lack of space for shelter footprint enlargement and the lack of prospects for obtaining additional land for camps expansion, UNHCR with partners, and in close coordination with the sector, will develop innovative solutions to address congestion and lack of space while improving living standards for refugees. Mid-term shelter development will include a combined plot development (several shelters per plot), a where possible, two-story shelters, recognising that shelter and the larger concept of settlement (vs. spontaneous camps) are inextricably linked and should be addressed as a whole rather than separately.UNHCR will provide transitional shelter assistance consisting of a combination of technical assistance, and in-kind replacement of core shelter material and tools to refugee families until mid-term shelters replace all temporary shelters in camps and settlements.Monsoon and cyclone preparedness remain a priority. Based on UNHCR’s post-distribution monitoring surveys, the last one conducted in September 2018, post-monsoon, refugees who received UNHCR shelter kits and tie-down kits (TDK), 99% used both kits, with the TDK’s iron pegs scoring the highest for quality. 97% reporting that the TDK proved useful during the monsoon season. UNHCR will assess shelters and distribute shelter reinforcement material such as tie-down kits based on needs. UNHCR is also planning to preposition emergency shelter/NFI assistance ready to be distributed in case of disaster.Non-Fond ItemsIn 2019, UNHCR’s NFI interventions will aim at improving refugee’s safety and living conditions, while trying to mitigate the refugee population
