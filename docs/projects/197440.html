Project Summary

AHF through this project aims to increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health services for the most vulnerable and in need; strengthen the health sector's capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks, including Cholera & COVID-19, and strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care.
Through the proposed intervention AHF will support the operations of its facilities on different levels of care to continue the provision of PHC, SHC, and THC services as follows:
- PHC services through: Atareb PHC with an attached mobile clinic, Salamah PHC with an attached mobile clinic, and Kherbet Eljoz PHC, with EPI supervision project, the estimated number of beneficiaries from PHC services is 267,300.
- SHC through Sham Children's Hospital in Sarmada, Sham pediatrics hospital in A'zaz, Sham surgical hospital in Tal Elkarameh, and Sham ambulance system in many locations across Idleb and Aleppo a part of the central ambulance system, the estimated number of beneficiaries from SHC services is 207,548.
- In addition, providing specialized health services includes rehabilitation services to persons with disabilities within 2 centers in Tal Elkarameh and Sarmada, and 1 Dental mobile clinic in A'zaz, the estimated number of beneficiaries from the specialized health services is 15,888.
- Outbreaks response will be maintained through the COVID-19 department at Alsham Internal medicine hospital in Dana, two Cholera treatment centers in Dana and Atareb, and our emergency COVID-19 ambulances, the estimated number of beneficiaries from AHF's outbreak response is 9,266.
The mentioned facilities are also putting into consideration easy access to people with disabilities through the prepared infrastructure of those facilities to make sure of full access.
As always, AHF will have the previous interventions fully coordinated with all health actors through the Health Cluster whether through the data in 4Ws, HeRAMS and DHIS2. or through its constant participation and active membership within the TWGs like SRH, PHC, and Non-Communicable Diseases, SIG, Rehab, referral, Supply Line, Cholera TF and COVID-19 TF and their related groups, and on the ground, AHF constantly coordinates with the HDs.
Before the project implementation, the M&E team will make regularly collect the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels:
1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible.
2) The technical, for which the technical team is responsible.
3) The objectives, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
• Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued on a monthly basis.
• Satisfaction surveys among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
• Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project term.
The project activities will be coordinated with other actors including partners through the TWGs meetings and health cluster meetings, in addition to the regular meetings with HDs, so we have full coordination with all parties working in the same field, and for the community within the project areas we are coordinating with the local councils through regular visits will be conducted by the ambulance system administration manager.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, the fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population.Moreover, according to OCHA NWS Sitrip published on 24 October 2022, 3.1 million out of the population in NWS are in need of health assistance, over 70% are considered people in acute need of life-saving assistance, women and children are estimated to make up for the 76% of this population, with around 2.8 IDPs, with only 372 out of 541 facilities remaining fully or partially operational as mentioned in Q2 HeRAMS report 2022.Based on CCCM data, 4.2M people in NWS, 2.7M are IDPs, 2.8M are PiNs, in Idleb: 2.96M, 1.86M of them are IDPs. In northern Aleppo: 1.4M, 836K of them are IDPs.Additionally, the fast-spreading Cholera outbreak with more than 19,000 suspected cases and 12 deaths in NWS till 05/12/2022, increases the burden on the already-fragile health services and systems.Relatedly, with the UN Security Council resolution extending the authorization for Bab al-Hawa until 10 January 2023, health actors in Northwest Syria are heavily focused on increasing operational supply chain capacity. At the same time, increases in poverty across the country which have already resulted in economically-driven displacement may also increase and worsen determinants of health thereby negatively impacting health outcomes.

4 - Implementation of activities

Through this project, AHF will work on the recruitment and provide the salaries to the HFs' staff, carry out general maintenance for the HFs and support the operational costs, and provide capacity building program to staff members (615 medical and nonmedical) of the facilities covering many topics like IPC, PFA, Cholera & COVID19 case mang., NRP, NICU, BLS, ACLS, ATLS etc., Provide PHC services according to revisited EHSP including CD, NCD, child health, and SRH, provide trauma, surgeries, and in-patients services and specialized centers including ICU, PICU, NICU, maternal and child health, and COVID-19 isolation departments and Cholera CTCs. In addition to procuring medical/non-medical supplies for the HFs, Raising awareness sessions, health-promotion, to the community through CHWs, Provision of emergency referral services (COVID and NON-COVID), Providing physical therapy sessions and prostheses through 2 rehab centers, Implementation of IPC program in all HFs, Sustain supervision of EPI services, and Provision of PSEA and safeguarding training.Coordination of all the activities with IHD for Idleb, and with AHD, Turkish health Authorities in Afrin and Azaz, technical coordination with Health cluster, WHO, and with partners working in the same areas. During the project implementation, AHF's Meal unit will contribute to improving decision-making and management, by keeping the project on track to achieve the outcomes and objectives and by benefiting from prev. lessons learned.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Acts of sexual exploitation and abuse either carried out by staff or not prevented by them.Mitigation: The availability of a clear complaint mechanism within the service centers and explain the mechanism, in addition to the availability of periodic training for service providers.- Changing the front lines between the parties may compromise the safety of the staff and the beneficiaries.Mitigation: Share project locations with deconfliction mechanism Continues and coordinate with AWG.- Cholera or COVID-19 transmission to the staffMitigation: Our staff is trained on IPC, and we will conduct an online refreshment IPC training for the staff at the start of the project, moreover, the staff members will be always equipped with PPE & IPC materials, & if any staff member developed symptoms we will communicate EWARN for sampling and ask him to be isolated at the CTC immediately until the result comes out- Projects may be adversely be affected by external incidents.Mitigation: Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System.- Women and girls are prevented from attending certain training or sessions.Mitigation: Coordinate and communicate with the community leaders to identify our work, in addition, to conducting outreach visits.- Lack of service for persons with disabilities, older persons, and areas away from centers.Mitigation: Coordination with other NGO's Outreach teams and referral services will be allocated


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AHF is an active member of the PSEA network, AHF has updated PSEA and child safeguarding policies and procedures in early 2021, including all levels from prevention, reporting, investigation, and survivor support. AHF has male & female PSEA focal points in Syria and in Turkey offices, they support the implementation of AHF PSEA strategy, and they carry out the following tasks:- Ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.- Equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to Both.- Create mechanisms for reporting protection from sexual exploitation and abuse at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.- Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.- Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.- Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and society.- Ensure all project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the Health cluster, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustment is needed. AHF has access and qualified program team, the Equipment is available, the place is available, the field staff is available, the required supplies and medication available, the electricity is available by functioning electrical generators.Risk 1: Conflict-related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors, and shifting alliances (L)Response: Postponing the services till safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations.Risk 2: Change of control in the services area, access constraints will continue to be the most significant threat to humanitarian operations. (L)Response: A comprehensive security and safety plan will be prepared so that the staff and the patients will be secured and we will coordinate to relocate the services depending on the new needs.Risk 3: The number of patients exceeds the capacity of the HFs (M)Response: In this case, we will coordinate with the cluster, Donors, local authorities and the partners on the ground to fill these gaps.Risk 4: Finance: cash transfer to Syria and within Syria with the absence of a banking system. (L)Using Banks for all money transfers to Syria either for operational costs, or vendors and contractors.
