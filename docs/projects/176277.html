Project Summary

Integrated Project for the improvement of Food & Nutrition Security and the provision of safe drinking water in underserved communities in Western Bahr el Ghazal and Jubek State. In the sector of FNSL, school feeding activities and unconditional cash transfers are conducted in addition to agricultural support in the form of trainings and supply with inputs.
The Yei Civil Hospital is supported via medical and laboratory supplies.

1 - Needs

FNS – Agriculture Support: A group of small-scale farmers owns fertile land. The other group has land without direct access to water and with less fertile soils, where seasonal, rainfed cultivation is practised. In identifying the small farmers to be supported, a special focus is placed on households headed by (widowed) women or hosting IDPs. Women and girls suffer particularly from the ongoing conflict, the economic crisis and the resulting food insecurity. Men control the majority of agricultural goods and prevailing cultural norms reinforce the existing gender inequality. All small farmers have many years of experience in traditional farming techniques. However, there is often a lack of seeds and tools. Post-Harvest Loss also poses a problem. For these reasons, the yields achieved are often hardly sufficient to feed one's own household, let alone to sell surpluses. The nutritional situation of the pupils at the schools is also alarming. Country-wide, the prevalence of moderate and severe stunting among children is 31 percent. Malnutrition among children is widespread. Girls are often not sent to school and 80% of South Sudanese women cannot read or write. Girls play a key role in the fight against malnutrition as the future responsible persons for the nutrition in their households. Although national policy requires primary and secondary schools to run a school garden to teach children agricultural knowledge and the importance of a balanced diet, this goal cannot always be achieved. Schools often lack seeds, tools, teaching materials or qualified teachers. For small farmers, the project therefore aims to complement existing practice with modern cultivation techniques and post-harvest handling knowledge, and to increase yields by providing seeds/seedlings and tools. Schools will introduce school gardens and provide seed/seedlings and tools, as well as food security and hygiene training. WASH: prevalence of diarrhea is high especially in rural communities who lack access to safe drinking water and good hygiene practices CVA: due to the inexistence of a social support system there are very vulnerable households in Wau who lack regular support and are severely food insecureHealth: fill gaps of drugs and consumables which exist at Yei hospital

2 - Project activities

FNS – agricultural support to small-scale farmers:- Promoting urban gardening and the use of fallow land, diversification of their product range, trainings in modern cultivation techniques- Target group received agricultural tools and seed quantities- Target group receives training- Target group is involved in seed production- Collectively cultivated large areas is made agricultural usable - The functionality of existing institutions to solve seasonal land conflicts due to cattle migration is guaranteed for the duration of the project.FNS – agricultural support to primary and secondary schools- Young people (upper secondary and secondary students, as well as students) have deepened their knowledge of modern agricultural production techniques theoretically and practically. Students of the University of Wau have the possibility of doing practical theses at the university's farm. - Around 15 school in Wau and Jubek State actively run school gardens and teach agricultural subjects. - The Catholic University of Wau runs a training farm, enabling its students to practice and carry out research. A breeding project of local millet species is successfully completed and documented in a research report. WASH- 40 rehabilitated deep wells are maintained- Drilling of 6 new boreholes- Construction of 15 shallow wells - Biweekly water testing - Maintenance and management of deep wells in cooperation with the users and the Rural Water Department - During the project period, all rehabilitated deep wells will be maintained and managed in cooperation with the users and the Urban Water Department, and downtime per well will be limited to a maximum of 10 days per year.- provide access to safe drinking water for rural communities through drilling, supporting local hand pump mechanics with tools, spare parts and trainings and training of water user committee; - support vulnerable households to construct a latrine and emphasizing on the importance to have a latrine; - hygiene promotion through house to house visits and mother support groups which are trained not only on good hygiene practices but also on infant and young child feeding CVA: monthly distribution of unconditional cash grant to the most vulnerable households in Wau municipalityHealth: regular delivery of essential generic drugs and consumables to Yei hospital in coordination with the administration of the hospital

3 - Project indicators

Outcome 1: Improving the Food and Nutrition Security of refugees, IDPs and host communities by increasing agricultural production, diversification of crops and training in modern agricultural techniques, with particular emphasis on adolescents (lower secondary and upper secondary and university students). Indicator 1.1: Improve the Food Consumption Score (FCS) of all participating households by 10% at the end of the project. This includes the households covered by the local partner organisation Mary Help Association. Indicator 1.2a: 60% of primary school (5x) and secondary school (1x) students have significantly increased their practical knowledge on agricultural subjects. Indicator 1.2b: All graduates of the Catholic University of Wau have the opportunity to conduct application-oriented and directly problem-related research. Outcome 2: Sustainably improved drinking water supply and use in the southern areas of the city of Wau to support returning households. Indicator 2.1: 70% of the existing deep wells and water access points in the project area in the south of the city of Wau provide safe, i. e. regularly tested, drinking water, which is being used. Indicator 2.2: Waiting time at the water access points is below 30 minutes. Indicator 2.3: Down time of deep wells and water access points is limited to a maximum of 30 days per well and year through effective operation and maintenance. To this end, local water management committees are formed and trained together with the Urban Water Department.Health: The most urgently needed essential generic drugs will be delivered in regular tranches to the hospital in Yei

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project activity was implemented in Wau, Western Bahr el Ghazal. Malteser International is present in Wau since 2014 and knows the market very well. In addition to that MI is active member in the Cash Working Group and participates in the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative of REACH regularly. From those forums we determined the cash to be given to beneficiaries.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Unconditional cash assistance to vulnerable households in Wau town was chosen because it gives the household the dignity to choose what to spend the money on. This is possible because there is an existing and functional market in Wau.Another activity is promoting the business activities of beneficiaries. The beneficiaries receive training in business skills and one-time cash assistance with which they can put their business plan into reality. This approach must be very flexible and support need to be adjusted to the business idea and the conditions of the beneficiary. Therefore, cash is the only option.We decided against the Mobile Cash modality for the distributions, as the additional transfer costs for mobile cash are quite high in South Sudan.

Within the framework of the baseline study, a gender analysis will be carried out in which the roles and needs of women, men, children and adolescents will be differentiated. The activities will then be adapted to the needs of the different groups. We pay special attention to the promotion and consideration of women and girls in all project measures. The target group selection is based on criteria that reduce inequalities and promote social integration. When selecting the target group, care is taken to ensure that internally displaced persons and households managed by women, especially widows, are included. In addition, the People First Impact Method (PFIM), which has already been tested by MI in South Sudan, is used to conduct separate group interviews with disadvantaged population groups, including women, people with disabilities and others. Their specific life situations will be discussed in depth. The results and findings of these discussions will be integrated into the project implementation. Furthermore, feedback and complaint mechanisms will be anchored in the project.For the cash assistance a very thorough screening process took place to target the absolutely vulnerable households only that are not receiving any support
