Project Summary

ZOA and CNSF will partner to implement a Cash for Shelter project to rehabilitate 230
homes in Al Wahadena neighbourhood of Tel Afar as decided through mutual agreement and coordination with the communities and other actors in the region. In addition, ZOA and CNSF will upgrade and repair 4 collective centres. The objective is to support highly vulnerable households in Tal Afar to achieve transitional shelter solutions through activities which supports the dignity and empowerment of the
beneficiaries.
Tal Afar was selected as the target location for this project following a needs assessment
and based on our previous projects and good relationships with the communities in Tal
Afar. This project will target highly vulnerable families inhabiting homes with “major”
damage (category 2) as outlined by the Shelter Cluster and requiring interventions of
$1500 USD in line HRP 2019 Shelter Strategic Objective 2, “Assist highly vulnerable people
out of camp transitioning to durable solutions through the provision of adequate shelter
and non-food items in priority areas of displacement and return.”
The outcomes of this project include:
1) Highly vulnerable families have access to durable transitional shelter solutions.
2) Highly vulnerable families are empowered
Methodology:
Households will be assessed using the Shelter Cluster War Damage Assessment
guidelines to categorize the damage level of each household and the CWG Vulnerability
Assessment tool which uses socio-economic factors to identify the most vulnerable. Once
the target households have been selected a Scope of Works and BOQ will be established
and approved by each household and Project Engineers.
Previously, ZOA repaired collective centres in Mosul. Based on this experience, ZOAs M&E team will assess collective centres in Tel Afar and ensure the best use of funds by selecting the collective centres where the living conditions are poor and where the most vulnerable people reside, in order to improve the living of those forced to remain displaced there.
Each household selected for the shelter rehabilitation part of this project will be eligible for a conditional Cash Transfer to support the repair works to be done on their houses. This will be available in two forms for the household to choose the most suitable:
1) Direct cash – using the Hawala system (or other method based on community
consultation). Conditional cash will be provided to the households who choose to
undertake the repair work themselves if they have the required skills. A work plan will be
created with targets for the household to meet to receive the installments of the transfer.
The transfer value will correspond with the Minimum Expenditure Basket amount as set
for the region.
2) Vouchers – If families do not have the skills to make the repairs themselves then they
can access restricted cash vouchers to be used at pre-approved and vetted local
contractors who have agreed to a Scope of Works Agreement to complete the work at set
rates.
ZOA will be responsible for all monitoring during this project, also to do a quality control of the local partner CNSF. All work undertaken by the households or the contractors will be monitored and signed off by the Project Engineers. The Engineers will also provide mentoring, guidance and support to the families. The cash component will empower families to rebuild their homes or to support the local economy by linking local contractors with local needs. Through our experience in the region and understanding that the average home consists of 7 individuals we anticipate that 1610 individuals will receive durable shelter solutions in line with the Shelter Cluster guidelines while supporting the local economy. This response was originally designed for Tal Afar but can be rolled out in multiple locations following a market assessment and needs assessment.

Needs

In February 2018 ZOA and CNSF conducted a scoping mission and needs assessment in Tal Afar using IOM rapid needs assessments as a base from which to build on. We found that Al Wahedena neighbourhood showed obvious signs of severe war damage. A CNSF structural Engineer was present on the visit to conduct a war damage assessment and concluded that of an approximate 900 houses 50% had category 1 damage, 30% category 2, 10% category 3 and 10% category 4 damages. Tal Afar has been identified by the Shelter Cluster as a priority area in the 2019 HRP. Early reports of the humanitarian situation in Telafar showed that approximately 60,000 - 80,000 residents had returned as of June 2018.

Activities

Assessments: A needs assessment was conducted by CNSF and ZOA in this area inFebruary 2018 and a desk review is ongoing to monitor the other actors in the area andidentify the gaps. In order to implement this project we will undertake a market analysisand community consultations to ensure the project meets the required pre-conditions forcash transfer. Collective centres selection: In response to collective shelter needs, ZOA will provide repairs and maintenance in collective centres in Tel Afar, through a Cash for Work scheme involving vulnerable IDPs under engineers’ supervision (IDPs either living in the shelters or IDPs, retrurnees and Host Communities identified through the assessments conducted for the shelter repair component of this project). In total, 4 collective centres will be repaired and 10 Cash for Workers per collective centre will be employed, therefore 40 CfW overall. Centres will be selected based on the condition of the centre and on the vulnerability of the people living there, thereby aiming to have the biggest impact. On average, 30 households live in a collective centre, meaning the total target population is 120 households (840 individuals). Beneficiary Selection shelter rehabilitation: Household assessments will be conducted using theCWG Vulnerability assessment tool based on socio-economic vulnerability criteria asrecommended by the Shelter Cluster and the CWG. The scoring system will enable us toidentify the most highly vulnerable households. Prior to beginning this assessment we willcoordinate with the CWG to identify whether this assessment has already been conductedby another organization, if so then we will contact them to set up a data sharingagreement. A War damage assessment of each household will be completed by projectEngineers to define the categorization of damage and will support us in selecting thebeneficiary households. The initial beneficiary assessment will include a gender analysisto discover the threats which women may face in accessing this type of assistance andappropriate mechanisms will be developed to mitigate these risks.Following selection each household and Engineer will sign the BoQ which will comprise details of the workwhich is to be completed on the house. Engineers: A team of five engineers will beemployed to oversee the repairs to ensure they meet minimum standards, is safe and ofgood quality, they will support households who choose to undertake the work themselvesor through family members. We will stagger the start dates for each batch of houses tobetter monitor progress and to identify areas where issues could arise and makeadaptions as the project progresses. The Engineers will provide training for thebeneficiaries regarding construction and repair methods or other topics based on theresults of community consultations and requested directly from the beneficiaries. Cash forWork/Vouchers: Each household will be eligible for a cash transfer payment to supportthe work on their house. This will be available in two forms which each household mustchoose at the start of the project. 1) Conditional cash direct transfer – Beneficiaries canchoose to undertake the work independently can receive a cash payment based on aschedule of work completed on the house. It will be split into 4 installments which will bereleased when the specific work has been completed and a retainer held for the final signoff. The transfer method will be decided based on community consultations, feasibilityanalysis and available Hawala companies. This option will allow beneficiaries to maintaincontrol of their finances and have decision making power as well as supporting thosehouseholds who have the skills within the family to undertake the work but perhaps notthe cash to purchase the materials. The transfer value to ensure this intervention ismarket aware we will have conducted the initial market analysis based on the availabilityof the most common materials needed to repair category 2 houses. The supply anddemand will be analyzed through vendors capacity to scale up stocks and the marketsystem infrastructure. Project staff will reassess the market dynamics by reviewing thecost of the items on the BoQ every four months. Transfer Value: Based on shelter clusteradvise the transfer value per household will be $1500. It is anticipated that the project willhave a 4-week initial set up period, followed by 10.5 months of beneficiary selection,assessments, construction, cash transfer, monitoring and evaluation. An additional 2weeks will be utilized at the end of the project for Post-Project Monitoring and reportwriting.

Protection Risk Analysis

Security: The presence of cash in an unstable environment can make the beneficiaries atarget for looting, theft and extortion. Diversion: If cash goes to unintended beneficiaries.Market risks: The increase of disposable cash into the local market has the potential todistort market norms, prices and items. This can cause the market to shift to meet theneeds of the beneficiaries and may not be healthy for the market as a whole. Cash as apull factor risk: The targeting of this area may pull other returnees to return to Tal Afar andpotentially to return to homes which are not safe or where there is presence of UXO’s. Thispull factor can cause people to risk harm by moving back into unsafe environments.Beneficiary selection risk: As this project will identify 230 households it is likely that theywill be chosen from part of a larger neighborhood which might cause tensions within thecommunity because not all households will receive the cash transfer. GBV: Giving cash toFHH can lead to conflict within the household or impact the gender dynamics which couldlead to GBV as male family members could feel useless or uncomfortable with womentaking part in this kind of project which is traditionally male activities.

Mitigation Measures

Security Mitigation: A common form of transfer is through the Hawala system whichreduces the amount of physical cash being moved around in one go. The distributions willbe capped at 100 households per day of distribution. We will not publicize the dates of thecash transfer or identify the beneficiary’s location to protect them from being targeted forextortion or coming to harm. Diversion Mitigation: The beneficiary selection process isdesigned to target the most vulnerable and each household will need to register to receivethe transfer using their ID cards or other forms of official ID. This will help to ensure thatthe cash is going to the intended beneficiaries. Market risks Mitigation: The cash will betransferred in installments based on progress towards pre-agreed construction targets.This will distribute the impact on the market. In the assessment stage a market analysiswill be conducted which will assess the availability of the required materials in themarkets and the vendors capacity to scale up to meet higher demand for the time periodof the project. The prices in the market will be monitored throughout the project to identifyif there has been a change in prices and to analyse if that is a result of this project. Cashas a pull factor risk Mitigation: We will avoid a pull factor by controlling the visibility of theproject and by working with the community to explain the vulnerability criteria andpurpose of the project and the timeframe. With continued consultation andcommunication CNSF and ZOA will ensure that the community understands the wholeproject and that returning to the area is not a guarantee of humanitarian assistance.Beneficiary selection risk Mitigation: CNSF and ZOA will aim to target peri-urban, underservedneighborhoods and will conduct 450 assessments to select 230 beneficiaryhouseholds. GBV Mitigation: We will minimise the risks to women and children bymonitoring the project throughout and by conducting a gender analysis at the beginningto identify the kinds of barriers and risks women may face. We will select a low visibilitytransfer mechanism to keep the cash transfer discreet. We will endeavor to hire female project staff where possible. We will consider ways to ensure this project is accessible to people with disabilities and other minority groups through assessments and consideration for specific needs. We will also take HLP risks into consideration and will work with the beneficiaries to ensure they have proper documentation for the house before beginning. We will liaise with the HLP sub-cluster to ensure we are following correct procedure and will participate in the referral system for households who require support.

How is your project accountable to affected population

This project aims to be accountable by involving the community and beneficiaries in allphases of the project. We will consult them regarding the most appropriate method ofcash transfer and what kind of training they require from the Engineers. A Complaints andFeedback Mechanism will be established based on community preferences and anEngineer’s work phone number will be active should the homeowners wish to contact theEngineer with questions. The initial assessment will include a gender analysis and willdisaggregate data by gender and age. We will consider the needs of women andof elderly households to identify the barriers they may face in taking part in this project.These barriers will be discussed and mitigation measures put in place to ensure that allages and genders can participate in the project aims.We will take cross cutting themes including gender, environment, age and protection into consideration in the project design and through our methodology and by linking with other relevant sectors such as legal assistance through the Protection cluster if required.
