Project Summary

This project aims to restore and enhance access to quality education for school-aged children living in refugee camps across Cameroon, targeting displaced populations from neighboring countries affected by conflict, political instability, and climate-induced crises. The intervention addresses critical gaps in educational infrastructure, teacher capacity, learning resources, and psychosocial support to ensure no child is excluded from learning opportunities.
The primary goal is to provide inclusive, safe, and sustainable education by constructing and equipping classrooms, training teachers in refugee-specific and trauma-informed approaches, and integrating life skills and peacebuilding into the curriculum. The project also prioritizes gender equality by promoting girls' education, creating safe spaces, and addressing cultural and logistical barriers to their attendance.
Additionally, mental health support services will be embedded within schools to help children overcome trauma and thrive academically. The intervention engages community stakeholders to foster local ownership and ensure culturally sensitive implementation. By enhancing educational access and quality, this initiative will empower refugee children to rebuild their lives and contribute to peace and development in their host communities.
Targeting approximately 10,000 school-aged children across refugee camps in Cameroon, the project aligns with the global commitment to ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The "No Child Left Behind" project targets critical education gaps in refugee camps across Cameroon, identified through assessments of displaced communities. Refugee children face challenges including: Lack of Access to Education: Insufficient schools and classrooms lead to overcrowded or non-existent learning spaces.Shortage of Qualified Teachers: Camps rely on untrained volunteer teachers, resulting in poor education quality.Language and Curriculum Barriers: Refugee children often struggle to integrate into local education systems due to linguistic and curricular misalignment.Psychosocial Needs: Children exhibit trauma-related learning difficulties and require mental health support.Gender Disparities: Cultural norms and safety concerns disproportionately limit girls’ education access.Insufficient Learning Materials: Many schools lack essential textbooks, teaching aids, and student supplies.
