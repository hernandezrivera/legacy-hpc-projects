Project Summary

According to the HNO, it is estimated that approximately 5.7 million people in Syria will require shelter support. 34 per cent of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelters. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity and lack of privacy are the top shelter adequacy issues identified. The vast majority of the IDPs (93%) reside in the tents that were provided to them more than a year ago. Unfortunately, the condition of these tents does not offer sufficient protection against rain and wind and the harsh effects of floods and winter are severely impacting their living condition. Humanitarian shelter response resources continue to decrease each year and it is expected that the Shelter sector’s ability to respond to urgent needs will diminish further. To date, the Shelter sector has been required to prioritize emergency shelter support for lifesaving purposes, focusing mainly on last resort sites. However, with existing needs and a potential increase in the rate of return (IDPs and refugees), a comprehensive ‘longer term’ shelter response is required across response areas to maintain the dignity of the vulnerable and improve privacy.
Furthermore, the non-food items (NFIs) (clothing, blankets, sanitation and hygiene items, fuel, and school supplies) are another top unmet need reported by 45 per cent of households compared with 21 per cent last year (HNO, 2023). While some NFIs are not available, access to NFIs is mainly impeded by lack of affordability of items, largely due to socio-economic circumstances. To a higher extent than other population groups, IDPs in camps reported being unable to afford NFIs such as heating fuel (65%), cooking fuel (57%), children’s clothing (43%) and winter clothing (34%).
• Intervention, Implementation of activities.
For emergency shelter, community and technical assessments will be conducted for shelter repair and rehabilitation, and rent-free lease agreements will be signed for one year. For HHs with PwD, designs will be adapted based on the needs. HLP due diligence will be conducted for all relevant activities. Camp infrastructure upgrades will be done in sites identified as flood-affected by the Cluster and designs will be created depending on the needs. For dignified shelters, IOM will replace tents with dignified shelter units in vulnerable informal camps in need of shelter upgrade. For all activities, IOM will conduct beneficiary selection based on vulnerability criteria. IOM and its IPs will identify suitable land and develop technical designs in line with SPHERE and the S/NFI’s guidelines.
In addition, IOM will implement NFI and tents activities through the SNFI Cluster Rapid Response Mechanism, which uses a pool of preselected IPs with proven SNFI capacity to respond to urgent SNFI needs with NFIs and tents. Provision of NFIs will be implemented in part through the RRM, with other stocks being shipped to partners through the Single Source Procurement Pipeline. IOM will coordinate with the S/NFI Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure needs are addressed and there is no duplication. IPs will select locations and beneficiaries in coordination with clusters on the basis of emerging needs and vulnerability assessments and will be verified by TPMs. PDMs will be conducted for both activities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

• Protection Risk Analysis In NFI and tents distributions, as well as for the construction of the transitional shelter units, tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees is a risk. IOM will mitigate this by involving all stakeholders, communicating the selection criteria, distributing according to verified needs, and establishing a complaint mechanism. Given the unstable security situation in NWS, there is the risk that conflict may impede humanitarian access to certain locations. IOM continuously monitors the security situation and has contingency plans in place, and IOM and IPs maintain communication with local authorities. Additionally, some of the access risks associated with transshipments could be mitigated through the use of cash modalities. For camp infrastructure upgrades, provision of transitional shelters and rehabilitation and repair of buildings, there is a risk of eviction of IDPs from sites due to HLP issues. This risk can be mitigated by only starting works once all HLP due diligence has been conducted and written ownership has been received and verified. Additionally, for rehab works, a tenancy agreement clause should be included in any agreements or MoUs signed with the owner of the building. • PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategyAll IP staff are required to have undertaken training on PSEA and signed a PSEA Code of Conduct. Details of IOM’s AAP mechanism are communicated to participants, as well as the Interagency PSEA hotline where appropriate. Gender segregated lines and gender-mixed distribution teams are always used by IPs to further mitigate SEA risks. As IOM is the leading agency for PSEA, all programmes are required to follow the network’s recommendations and incorporate them into each project. For shelter and infrastructure, IOM makes sure all IP field teams consist of male and female members, and each beneficiary will receive a poster with the PSEA hotline number to report any misconduct by the project staff.
