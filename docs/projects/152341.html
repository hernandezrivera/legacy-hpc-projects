Project Summary

The project will contribute to improved aid coordination and delivery (both humanitarian and longer term recovery planning). To continue informing the humanitarian programming cycle with field evidence, REACH will provide information management and assessment support to the humanitarian coordination architecture. Capitalizing on the work implemented in 2016, 2017 and 2018, REACH will provide the inter cluster coordination group and humanitarian partners support to implement multi-sector needs assessment analysing gender, ability and age disaggregated data in both government and non-government controlled areas in addition to ad hoc information management technical expertise.

Needs

Humanitarian funding keep being steadily reduced, with only 30% of the 2018 Humanitarian Response Plan funded as of October 2018. In order to target the needs of the most vulnerable including the elderly, (single) female headed households and families with children, interventions in 2019-2020 will have to be guided by solid evidence.The humanitarian needs overview of 2019 has shown that after 4 years of active conflict along the contact line, people in eastern Ukraine continue to face conflict related challenges. Continued conflict poses major protection risks to populations living closest to the line of contact. Restrictions of movements between non-government controlled and government controlled areas are disrupting access to services and markets and limited perspectives for a peaceful resolution to the crisis have created a protracted humanitarian situation. Based on preliminary figures, as of October 2018, there are more than 3 million people in need. Access to reliable and timely information is critical for empowering aid agencies to meet the multifaceted and evolving needs of women, men, girls and boys affected by conflict in both GCA and NGCA. Since 2015, REACH Ukraine has supported clusters (Shelter Cluster, 2015 Shelter & NFI Needs Assessment), UN agencies (UNICEF, 2016 WASH Assessment), the humanitarian country team (REACH, 2016 Interagency Vulnerability Assessment; Multi Sector Needs Assessment 2016, Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2017, Multisector Needs Assessment 2017 and Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018) in designing and implementing multi sector needs assessments to inform strategic level documents including UN, donor and NGO strategies. This was achieved due to REACH’s comparative advantage in collecting primary data and capacities in the analysis of secondary data in both GCA and NGCA. The need for detailed, gender and age disaggregated data will persist in 2019 especially given the specific vulnerability of single women headed households identified in previous REACH assessments. Only through the implementation of large-scale household surveys using similar indicators as in 2016, 2017 and 2018 can the humanitarian situation be monitored accurately and reliably for the benefit of aid programming. These assessments will also provide significant field evidence with regards to accountability to affected populations. Based on agreed indicators from the inter agency standing committee (IASC) on AAP, REACH assessments will continue monitoring the implementation of accountability mechanism in the field as per good practice from 2017 and 2018. Capitalizing on these achievements, the project will support assessment and information management in eastern Ukraine.Data from the 2018 humanitarian trend analysis and preliminary results from the 2018 multi-sector needs assessment has shown that humanitarian needs persist especially for population living closest to the line of contact. Residents living within 5km of the contact line are more likely to be exposed to conflict risks, be food insecure and experience challenges in accessing healthcare services.In line with commitments from the Grand Bargain, the Ukraine humanitarian community with support from REACH has been able to rely on ‘Improved joint and impartial needs assessments’ to develop the humanitarian needs overview and humanitarian response.To further increase the localization of assistance REACH will continue strengthening local level capacities in information management through its three IM_HUBs in Slovyansk, Severodonetsk and Mariupol.In line with AAP commitments REACH will also maintain its efforts to ensure survey participants can raise complaints to an anonymous hotline with the phone number distributed after all surveys.

Activities & outcomes

Link to Coordination Objective 2: Guide humanitarian action by joint strategic planning, improved information management and needs assessment, and respond based on prioritized needs, including preparedness and resilience aspects Outcome: Humanitarian aid actors have improved access to reliable evidence to inform their programming Activity 1: Implementation of two comprehensive multi-sector needs assessment in government controlled and non-government-controlled areas. Capitalizing on the assessment work completed in 2016, 2017 and 2018, REACH will conduct follow up assessments using comparable indicators and sampling methods to inform strategic level programming. Using household surveys representative at a 95% confidence interval and 5% margin of error, REACH findings will be generalizable to the surveyed population. Key informant interviews will complement quantitative data collection methods by continuing approaches piloted in 2018 of reaching people who cross the line of contact or who call humanitarian assistance hotlines. Data collected by REACH will have a high level of granularity given the data collection scope.This approach will complement programmatic local level assessments funded outside of the HRP. The project will continue engaging with the inter cluster coordination group and NGO partners to ensure that the data collection tools are adjusted where necessary.All data collected will be disaggregated by gender, age and other household indicators to identify specific vulnerabilities for women, elderly, households caring for persons with disabilities, and IDPs. Activity 2: Building field level capacities of local and international partners in GCA through the ACTED/REACH IM_HUBs and offices in Slovyansk, Mariupol and Severodonetsk. REACH will continue to implement regular capacity building workshops for NGOs and local authorities on three important information management topics: i) mobile data collection through Kobo Collect ii) data analysis using Microsoft Excel and iii) geographic information systems using Quantum GIS. Following the three core principles of the IM_HUB of using open source or widely available solutions, small training groups and applied learning methods, REACH will strengthen IM skills of organisations and authorities supporting the response in eastern Ukraine. REACH will organize regular workshops in its three field bases as well as provide standing capacities for ad-hoc partner requests. All assessments will be designed, implemented and reviewed through multi-stakeholder engagement to ensure the highest quality possible and stay in line with AAP commitments. The protection of all respondents is REACH’s first priority. For all assessments, qualitative methods (key informants, focus group discussions), will be employed in a gender sensitive manner to ensure that women of all ages and other vulnerable groups are comfortable sharing information on their perceptions, needs and priorities. REACH will ensure that enumerators and participants in IM_HUB trainings are trained on gender/age disaggregation and gender sensitive information collection.REACH will also ensure fair targeting of women under its IM_HUB activities by collecting gender disaggregated data of its participants.

Indicators & targets

i) # of multi sector assessments available to the humanitarian and development community (target: 2)ii) % of humanitarian partners that are satisfied with REACH multi sector assessments products (target: 90%)iii) % of REACH Assessments with gender/age disaggregated data (target:100%)iv) % of respondents that are satisfied with the quality of the survey they have participated in (target: 90%)v) # of trainings delivered to NGOs and local authorities through the IM_HUBs (target: 4)vii) % of training participants who show improved scores between the pre-training and post-training tests (disaggregated by gender) (target: 70%)
