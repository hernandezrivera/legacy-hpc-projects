Project Summary

Ensure equitable access to WASH essential services, in safety and dignity, to the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys located in unconnected communities, thus contributing to the achievement of WASH cluster objective no.1. The project addresses immediate needs as well as the underlying drivers of those needs, in line with the water master plan for Area C developed by WW-GVC and AAH, in full cooperation with PWA, as well as the PWA National Water Sector Strategic Plan and Action Plan (2017-22). The intervention encompasses: - A responsive measure, comprehensive of water access massive interventions (pipeline, filling point, HHs connection) and storage water intervention (rain water harvesting cisterns, domestic water tanks, reservoir); - A remedial measure, through the provision of sanitation facilities. The initiative allows to reduce and more effectively address the core vulnerabilities of people in need, increasing water access for unconnected communities in area C and guarantee a safe level of sanitation for the most vulnerable groups in the West Bank. The initiative aims at; connecting 4 communities (3,300 people) in Area C with safe water domestic connections. Increase the water storage capacity for 5 communities in Area C and H2 (3,985 people) through the construction of a concrete reservoir, rainwater harvesting cisterns and the provision of domestic tanks; 4 communities in Area C (391 people) will see improved their level of sanitation and hygiene. The action aims at supporting gender and age-sensitive- relief provision and responsive protection to meet the material and safety needs of specific vulnerable group, to ensure gender and age responsive assistance and equitable access to humanitarian goods and services, to prevent/mitigate potential negative effects of the action on different gender/age groups and ensure adequate participation.
The entire project cycle including; need assessments, designed strategies, proposed interventions, set indicators, outcome measuring procedures take special consideration to the need the most affected groups such as women, children, people with disabilities and elderly. and the elderly throughout the cycle of the project. Needs analyses, activities, outcomes, and indicators consider the diverse needs of each community member and vulnerable groups. All the interventions will trigger a positive effect on the reduction of vulnerability and the increase of resilience of the community members, especially the most vulnerable groups as boys, girls, women and men. The action has been endorsed by PWA and in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) –, and is in compliance with the water Master Plan for Area C. These activities are in line and part of the 2017-22 PWA Strategy Plan, and the priorities set for 2020 by the Joint Service Councils, West Bank Water Department and PWA. The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing to “Goal 6: Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”.

1 - Needs

The most vulnerable people living under occupation in Area C and H2 Area are in urgent need for an increased sustainable access to essential WASH services. Ensuring sufficient, safe, physically accessible and affordable water for personal and domestic use. This project lays its basis on three recent thorough assessments conducted by WW-GVC which allowed identifying the most unconnected communities and groups in terms of WASH services and communities lacking proper coping mechanisms to withstand the protracted threats and shocks, with a high risk of displacement. Those assessment are (i) Water Master Plan for Area C for 247 vulnerable communities, published in 2017 (iii) Community based Protection Approach conducted by WW-GVC (and the other agencies of the West Bank Protection Consortium) for the development of the Protection Community Profiles and Responses Plans – including a specific analysis of the WASH sector of 184 communities located in Area C and H2, initiated in 2015 and updated on yearly basis- (iii) Water access indicators survey conducted on 2018 in 56 communities, in the framework of the UNICEF WW-GVC water trucking programme and updated in September 2019; initiated in 2014 in 90 communities, this assessment has been updated in a yearly basis. The identified communities are dependent on the direct purchase of water for around 7 m/y, failing to match WASH Humanitarian Benchmarks (HB) of 60 l/c/d, and even the critical minimum HB of 30 l/c/d/ for 25% of the population. Water expenses can account for up to 50% of a household monthly expenses with a negative impact especially on women who prioritize the water use for domestic uses rather than personal hygiene, and men who prioritize the use of water for their livestock rather than themselves. According to the WASH Cluster parameters 100% of water trucked sources are unsafe, 86% of the communities are located in Area C and over 54% in sensitive Area C. These trends generate a coercive environment with specific protection impacts increasing the risk of displacement of the populations, and the construction of water facilities stands as the humanitarian response to improve their access to basic services improving their living conditions. Particularly, communities that are characterized by a chronic erosion of income, depleting their livelihood and affecting the overall communities’ resilience (division of the income within the family critically affect the needs of youth and women, since the main amount of income is addressed to purchase water for drinking, domestic and agricultural/herding purpose). Increase exposure to settlers’ attacks and IF interventions, while fetching water from distant water sources. Men and young boys are specifically affected when using mobile tankers, women and girls while fetching water from cisterns located in unsafe zones. Constrained access to domestic/drinking water has direct consequences on the general wellbeing of the population. Children and women are particularly affected. Lack of water and sanitation facilities affects specifically hygiene and dignity of women and girls during their menstruation, and leads to extra workload and stress, being in charge of all domestic labour. The sense of privacy and personal safety of women and girls is critically jeopardized. Most of the communities targeted do not dispose functioning latrines for all the inhabitants, which means open defecation practice is still widely used. This produces direct negative consequences on protection and privacy of most vulnerable groups, such as women and adolescent girls and on health leading to the propagation of water-borne disease, extremely dangerous, especially for infants. Women and girls are the most affected group based on three dimensions: 1) Quality of life 2) Feeling of Protection 3) division of duties.

2 - Activities

The initiative will encompass, when feasible, the adoption of WW-GVC Community Implementation methodology. This methodology entails the creation of a Project Committee composed of representatives of the communities/ CBOs (gender-balanced), which are the local implementing partners. CPCs are responsible for the design, implementation, and maintenance of the infrastructures (construction of rain water harvesting cisterns, reservoir, pipelines, provision of mobile latrines and domestic water tanks). CPCs will receive 15-20% of the direct cost, through conditional cash transfer modality. The entire amount of direct costs (which includes material and cost of labor) is therefore paid to them upon receipt of a payment request with a progress report (prepared by the sector responsible and verified by Technical Coordinator and Project Manager) that verifies the work accomplishment along with the payments amount eligibility. Capacity building and monitoring will be done by WW-GVC along the intervention. Prior to any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community and the Project committee will be conducted. Specific SoP are followed during implementation to minimize/mitigate risks. The Community Implementation will allow to reduce the risk of confiscation and other potential obstacles related to the coercive environment as Area C, and to enhance the commitment and ownership of the beneficiaries. The project will benefit 12 communities located in 6 Governorates in Area C of West Bank (Bethlehem, Hebron, Jericho, Ramallah, Nablus, Tubas) and 4 neighborhoods in Hebron H2 area (Haret al Salaymeh, Wadi al Hussein, Tel Rumeida, Old Suq). Activity 1. Needs assessments: at the beginning of the action, a need assessment will be carried out to verify any changes of needs and/or objectives, as well as to assess the security situation to enable the interventions to be conducted according to plan. The need assessment will analyze the needs, the role and the dynamics of the beneficiaries through the following Implementation of the Community Protection Approach: a contextualized sectorial protection analysis (including WASH) will comprise Focus groups/ Individual Interviews/ Observatory walk/ Daily and Seasonal calendars/ Risks and Resources Maps/ Legal Mapping involving women, men, boys and girls separated for all the steps. Activity 2: Community Implementation: creation of a Project Committee. Prior to any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community and the Project committee will be conducted Activity 3. Improvement of water access through domestic water connections: • Construction of transmission pipeline from a filling point to the community : Jourat Al Jamal 2.5km (steel); • Expansion and rehabilitation of the existing transmission pipeline; installation of house connections: Al Masara 2km (steel), Wadi Fukin 4km (steel) , North Al ‘Auja 2.2 km (HDPE). Activity 3. Improvement of water storage capacity through construction of cisterns and reservoir and provision of domestic water tank: • Construction of rainwater harvesting cisterns (70m3) to increase water availability and storage capacity (locations and n. of cisterns): Arab Ka’abneh 7; Al Rahnia 20; Kheroshewesh Wal Hadedeyah 20 • Construction of a concrete reservoir (300 m3): Bardala 1 • Provision of domestic water tanks (1m3) in Hebron H2 Area : Haret al Salaymeh 25, Old market (old suq) 25, Tel Rumeida 25, Wadi al Hussein 25. Activity 4. Improvement of sanitation services through the installation of latrines: • Provision of mobile latrines (locations and n. of latrines): Al Hadidiya 12, Al Qaffaf 11, Hammammat al Maleh al Meiteh 6, Al Al Mu’arrajat Center 7. Proper coordination will be done between PWA, WBWD, the Governorates, local village councils, Joint Service Councils, Municipalities, major stakeholders active with WASH in the targeted areas in order to avoid overlapping (e.g. ACF, PHG, CISP, etc..)

3 - Indicators

The overall intervention aims at preventing the forcible transfer of targeted communities by improving access to basic WASH services, and the fulfillment of the fundamental human right of access to water in sufficient quality and quantity, safely physically, accessible and affordable. The action is targeting the most vulnerable communities in Area C, close to settlements or military zones, facing restrictions for development and affected by or under the threat of forced displacement. All the beneficiary communities fall within the priority geographic locations and within the vulnerability benchmarks identified by the WASH Cluster and the Palestinian Authorities. All the interventions aim at reducing the distance to access to water for people, increasing their resilience and reducing their probability to be forcibly displaced. The beneficiaries will benefit of an increase of water access through two main initiatives: the construction of the pipeline, HHs connections and filling points; and the increase of storage water capacity through implementation of rainwater harvesting cisterns. The targeted people will benefit from the increase of availability and an improvement of quality of drinking water. This will be reflected by a decrease of the water cost and purchase of truck water influencing directly the management of the household income and their livelihoods. Especially men and boys will benefit from a sensitive improvement of security from the reduction of the distance drivento collect water with tankers. In communities where women and girls were tasked to walk to collect, women and girls will be the most positively affected beneficiaries. In addition, the beneficiaries will benefit from an increase of access to sanitation through the provision of mobile latrines aiming to reduce the risks of infections and water-born typical of a coercive context when most of the HHs practice open defecation. In particular, children and women will benefit from a the anticipated reduce in the workload and risks of water contamination with direct impact on health, especially of the children and adolescents. Through the provision of safe drinking and domestic water services, the action will achieve the following HRP Indicator: 7,676 people with increased access to safe drinking, domestic water and sanitation/solid waste services. The action will specifically achieve the below targets: - 7,285 people (1,656 women, 1,743 men, 1,851 girls, 1,949 boys, 42 elderly women, 44 elderly men) have improved their access to water through HHs connection, rainwater harvesting cisterns domestic water tank and reservoir. - 391 people (89 women, 94 men, 99 girls, 105 boys, 2 elderly women, 2 elderly men) have increased access to safe sanitation services. – 1,745 adult women have reduced their daily workload due to increasing of water access and improvement of sanitation services. - 147 families have increased storage capacity of beneficiary households. Ongoing monitoring against the initial disaggregated risk analysis will be carried out as part of the continuous process of analysis, combining both quantitative and qualitative data systematized through a series of outputs. The action encompasses a participatory approach that includes focus group and individual interviews (with men, women, girls and boys, with and without disability separated) to assess the needs and identify activities that take into account the needs and dynamics of the beneficiaries, analyzing the different effect of the initiative on different age and gender groups. After the completion of the intervention, the qualitative impact of the initiative will be recorded through FGs to collect the feedback of different vulnerable groups (women, men, boys, girls, elderlies). Through the Community Implementation approach, the feedback of the beneficiaries will be collected along the WASH infrastructures.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Complaint response mechanism is part of WW-GVC accountability and commitment strategy to guarantee transparency, fairness, and equity towards all our beneficiaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints or suggestions in a secured way and receive a response in a timely manner. All complaints are reported to the admin officer and project managers. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the Admin officer analyzes complaints and classifies each of them according to the internal classification system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by WW-GVC team. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call by WW-GVC’s admin officer.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The Do No Harm principle is assured by the whole implementation of the project, guaranteeing that every project’s phases and steps are endorsed by the beneficiaries themselves, to prevent -in so doing- any potential negative consequence. Particular attention is given to input/feedback provided by women (whose participation is strongly encouraged to any decision-making process – including gender-balance project committees following the WW-GVC Community Implementation methodology) and specific attention will be also given to the lesson learned coming from previous similar projects. Further, the action takes also into account the indications provided by the “Gender and WASH toolkit for Palestine”, which provides applicable and feasible suggestions on how to mainstream gender in WASH projects in the Palestinian context. WW-GVC adopts a well-designed and established complaint mechanism system allowing beneficiaries and affected population to provide feedback and complaints, which will then -if needed- influence management decisions. Safety and dignity have been taken into consideration by designing tailored activities to address the differentiated needs and risks girls, women, boys and men are currently facing when we talk about access to WASH services.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project follows a sustainable humanitarian approach aiming to provide immediate relief, ensuring equity, sustainability and impartial access of the action to ensure WASH services for conflict-affected un- or under-served communities of Area C and H2 area.The project aims to increase the access to water, as well as the efficiency, predictability and equity of the service and has been designed considering the direct and indirect impact on women, girls, boys, with or without disabilities. The strategy, activities and materials used for implementing the project have been developed and selected to tackle the needs assessed, in the best interests of beneficiaries taking into account feedback and lesson learns resulting from previous similar implemented projects. This project is implemented following the Do No Harm principle, throughout all phases: design, planning, implementation and monitoring. There will be a noticeable decrease of time and effort spent in fetching water at the household-level, especially for girls and women who are usually in charge of this task; therefore, fewer tasks will be imposed on girls, and women who will not have to face long walks to collect the water, reducing exposure to the protection risks related to their trips to the water source (i.e. attacks and harassment from Israeli Forces and settlers). The project positively affects the members of the family that are more exposed to the risks mentioned, especially women, girls, and boys with or without disabilities. The following positive impacts pertain to: quality of life – women will improve their quality of life due to the ease of access to water and a decrease in their workload and stress due to improved access to drinking water.In case of families with PWDs, access to water will help to alleviate the work of the caregivers and help to overcome difficulties; feeling of protection – women, girls, boys and men with or without disabilities will increase their sense of protection due to water availability; division of duties. A specific design of WASH facilities (i.e provision of latrines, domestic water tank, RWHS) will be guarantee making them physically accessible for use and maintenance to all members of the community taking into consideration the different needs of girls, women, boys and men with or without disabilities and of all ages might have.
