Project Summary

This project aim at promoting Nutrition for vulnerable groups (children 0 – 59 Months, PLWs and caregivers of children less than 2 years) among IDPs and affected communities in the NW and SW regions of Cameroon through. The project is going to: Improve access to quality lifesaving services for management of acute malnutrition for children through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acute malnutrition; Improve access to services preventing under-nutrition for the most vulnerable groups (0 – 59 Months, PLWs and caregivers of children less than 2 years); Enhance nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information for decision making; Enhance effective coordination of partner’s response to the needs of affected population. The project activities include:
Building the capacity of CHW to set-up CMAM (early identification, referral & treatment), optimal IYCF-E and nutrition assessment.
• Screen 9000 children 6-23months and 3000 PLW for MAM & SAM in Menchum, Momo, and Manyu division of the NW and SW respectively
• Community mobilization and sensitization to inform communities on the availability of CMAM services.
• Prevention and protection of vulnerable groups, against the deterioration of nutrition status through Infant Young Children Feeding in Emergencies,
• Conduct Vitamin A Supplementation, and Deworming,
• Participate in multi-sectorial and cluster coordination meetings
The project targeted population includes Momo and Menchum divisions in the NW AND Manyu division in the SW

Besoins / Needs

The socio political crisis that started since 2016 in the two English speaking regions of Cameroon escalated into an armed conflict and insecurity in 2017 has rendered many people displaced from their localities. The increasing tension and hostilities has promoted insecurity and significant internal displacements. While humanitarian needs, joblessness, crime and scarcity of food are on the increase, most farmers have fled from the villages into bushes, forest and a large number made it to nearby cities. Consequently many have become vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. The causes of malnutrition basically in the SW and Northwest region especially during this season have been increased by the scarcity of food due to the continuous displacement of people due to the sociopolitical crisis. Reports of assessment by World Food Program (WFP) indicate that 900,000 people in the Northwest region are food insecure. In this population 13% of IDPs and 10 % of non IDPs are severely food insecure. 36.6% of IDPs and 29.9% of NIDPs are moderately food insecure while 38.2% of IDPs and 29.5% NIDPs are marginally food insecure. The continuous aggravating situation and sustained prevalence of critical Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) levels (2.8%) in the Northwest region suggests that general food assistance is needed to treat children with moderate acute malnutrition and prevent malnutrition. According to the Emergency Food Security Assessment (EFSA) reports, the prevalence of GAM stands at 4.4% in the Northwest revealing 5.1% amongst boys and 3.6% amongst girls. The prevalence of MAM is 1.7% in the Northwest revealing 1.5% amongst boys and 1.8% in girls. OCHA estimates about 7000 children between the ages of 6-59months are presently suffering from SAM in the two affected regions with the North West prevalence levels of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) at 2.8% exceeding the critical threshold of 2%. Reports of hospitals with SAM, MAM on the number of cases are seriously under estimated since most integrated health centers in the communities are non-functional due to attacks on health care. This therefore places a key need for a referral mechanism (pathway) for cases of SAM. There is also a need for periodic screening for MAM and SAM cases. Poor nutrition often results in infections, malnutrition which is major cause of stunting (a common growth failure in children). Stunting and poverty are well known risk factors for poor child development including cognitive functions which is now common in the regions. It is therefore imperative that interventions aimed at providing the much needed supplements, parenting education, promotion of exclusive breastfeeding practices, vaccination, proper material nutrition etc be developed to avert a potential catastrophic public health emergency situation in future while addressing the already recorded cases
