Project Summary

WASH needs are directly linked to high levels of malnutrition, disease, food insecurity and protection risks, with communities in Greater Upper Nile and Eastern Equatorial displaying high levels of malnutrition, food insecurity and disease. Provision of adequate WASH services is key to achieving sustainable solutions to communities in emergency while equipping communities with abilities to mitigate and respond to shocks.
Christian Mission for Development (CMD) works in Greater Upper Nile and Equatoria regions and plans a response aimed at reaching internally displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable host communities with water, sanitation and hygiene services.
Spanning a period of 12 months, the project targets the locations of Ayod, Duk, Nyirol, Uror, Fangak and Twic East (Jonglei), Ulang, Maiwut and Longechuk (Upper Nile) and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Budi (Eastern Equatoria) with WASH services, to address identified needs, public health and protection issues.
The response aims at mitigating WASH related GBV by providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities and tackling issues surrounding gender, safety and WASH. Persistence of food insecurity, and malnutrition signaled by high GAM rates, with <5s and PLW mainly affected will be mitigated by the provision of WASH in nutrition centers, through targeted WASH minimum packages at OTP/SC mainly for mother/caretaker – malnourished children.
Water related disease outbreaks have been traced to health facilities and community infrastructures/settings without adequate WASH facilities.
The project targets a total of 120,000 people; 61,800 of these in Jonglei (51.5%); (Ayod – 14,400; Duk – 10,200; Nyirol – 12,000; Uror – 8,400; Twic East – 6,000.); 23,160 in Upper Nile (19.3%); (Ulang – 6,960; Maiwut – 4,800; Longechuk – 11,400) and 35,040 in Eastern Equatoria (29.2%); (Kapoeta North – 7,440; Kapoeta East – 9,600; Kapoeta South – 13,200 and Budi – 4,800). 58% of those targeted are females, 28% of them girls. IDPs consist of 52% of those targeted.

1 - Needs

Poor WASH standards have continually undermined gains achieved in nutrition, health, protection and food security in Greater Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria States. The national prevalence of GAM and SAM for children 0-59 months are 13.3% and 2.7% respectively. GAM prevalence exceeded the WHO emergency threshold in Jonglei (19.4) and Upper Nile (16.3). During the most recent FSNMS, a total of 7,151 women (15 to 49 years) old were measured using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC). The prevalence of wasting based on MUAC (<230cm) was 23.8% up from 20.2 % reported last year. Overall, access to WASH non-food items is poor, reflective of the general WASH situation across the counties targeted. While some locations reportedly having better access to WASH services and practices, the general trend across the counties is concerning, exposing households to numerous negative compounding effects that poor WASH conditions can contribute too, including food utilisation and increased morbidity. 32 per cent of HHs reported a river or stream as their main source of drinking water. The insufficiency of water points was further exacerbated by increased concentration of IDPs in safe havens, increase in returnees due to stability, breakdown due to over usage and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitations and repairs. 18.12 per cent of HHs use latrines, due to unavailability, safety concerns, knowledge and attitudes amongst other challenges with 81.39 % reported practising open defecation. There’s been a destruction of WASH infrastructure, due to floods and a tendency of communities to settle in far to reach areas that usually lack adequate WASH services thereby increasing likelihood of resorting to unsafe excreta disposal and surface water consumption leading to water related diseases. Increased caseloads have further strained available sanitation and hygiene facilities, with majority of these requiring rehabilitation and/or upgrade. Unsafe sanitation and hygiene habits are a common practice with open defecation widely visible in areas where large groups of IDPs are concentrated.Soap ownership is also reportedly minimal; No soap ownership was reportedly the highest in Greater Upper Nile state 85.09% Overall, the enormously high levels of open defecation combined with minimal access to soap is an extremely concerning, potentially leading to increased exposure to water borne diseases and poor food utilization. During the wet season, an average 77% of households reported a self-diagnosed water and vector borne disease. Malaria was reported as one of the most common diseases, 56.86% followed by acute watery diarrheal (41% ) and typhoid 15%Protection risks have been increased to girls and women who have to stray into nearby bushes due to lack of gender appropriate facilities. A significant number of persons interviewed indicated safety concerns prevented them from accessing the preferred water source and SGBV as the main protection concern for women and girls. Institutional WASH gaps have been identified in schools, health facilities, markets and nutrition centres, requiring response for outbreak control and mitigating public health risks. Significant disruptions of markets and high inflation rates in Jonglei, Eastern Equatorial and Upper Nile have further lessened opportunities of already vulnerable communities to access basic WASH supplies such as soap, PuR tablets, Hygiene Kits amongst others. Lack of Hygiene Kits and other WASH NFIs has reduced chances of enrolling girls into institutions of learning in an emergency setting making them more vulnerable to early marriages. Provision of emergency WASH services will reduce likelihood of deaths due to water related diseases, provide a platform for community settlement and provision of other lifesaving services.

2 - Activities

CMD plans an integrated WASH in Nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package, WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level, provision of timely and sustainable equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees and to mitigate WASH related gender based violence through the following activities. • Setup/rehabilitation of collective water points with secure location and access agreed after a GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staff, in the community or schools. • Provide safe drinking water at nutritional centers (OTP/SC). • Setup/ Rehabilitation of Emergency safe, gender appropriate latrines, bathing shelters and hand washing facilities (agreed upon by GBV analysis through focus groups conducted by female staff) at designated schools, communal and household settings with high GAM, GBV/SEA and cholera prone areas. • Provision of water purification tablets (PuR/Aquatabs) on a case-by-case basis to vulnerable communities. • Distribution of MHM kits to vulnerable women/girls of menstrual age to ensure dignity and mitigate risks of GBV. • Provide WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC. • Carry out post distribution monitoring of MHM kits to female adolescents and women WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention interventions. • Carry out hygiene promotion services, that are inclusive, multi sectoral and community driven. • Provide a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an adhoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community.

Yes, as an accountability to affected populations commitment, the project will uphold rights of beneficiaries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, Internally displaced persons are one of the primary target groups to be reached, apart from the returnees and host communities. The project will eventually evolve based on arising needs of IDPs while focusing on the key cluster objectives.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, CMD works with national, state and county officials and inline ministries as key stakeholders in the implementation of the project. Feedback mechanisms are constituted to enable smooth implementation of key deliverables, working with local community structures. Existing WASH committees will be utilised including water management committees, pump mechanics, hygiene promoters etc.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, inline with national strategic frameworks, and the humanitarian response plan, the project seeks to leverage on gains made, while providing a robust and multi - sectoral lifesaving response in the thematic areas of WASH, Nutrition, Health and Protection.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is relatively stable however with a few risks identified especially towards girls, women, boys and the elderly. Majority of these are related to safety while accessing social services and possibility of recruitment into armed groups. A risk matrix has been drawn up to minimise and counter possibility of these occurrences, while providing humanitarian assistance without compromising safety of the beneficiaries.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, sustainability is at the core of the project, with CMD working with local authorities and community structures as key stakeholders in the project to enhance a sense of project ownership. Community participation in project activities will be vital for the success of the handover process, with CMD maintaining presence in the locations inorder to oversee key deliverables after the closure of the project.
