Project Summary

In line with our organisational Core Commitment to Children in Emergency, UNICEF will continue to provide the population of South Sudan especially children, pregnant and lactating women, with quality preventive, and curative services as well as outbreak response activities. The IRRM enables UNICEF, through direct implementation, to provide high impact lifesaving emergency response in inaccessible and conflict affected areas. UNICEF will ensure availability of core pipe line vaccines and cold chain supplies to respond to vaccine preventable disease outbreaks.
This project aims to increase access to quality equitable essential primary health care interventions through IRRM and provide a core pipeline to increase access to quality equitable immunization services by vulnerable populations living in host communities and IDPs living in Protection of Civilian Sites (POCs), formal IDP camps or host communities in conflict affected areas as well as refugee returnees.
The activities to be implemented are:
• Procurement and distribution of emergency vaccines.
• Procurement, distribution and maintenance of cold chain
equipment.
• Technical support for improved vaccine and cold chain
management
• Training of frontline partners of vaccine and cold chain
management
• Conduct 30 IRRM missions
The expected beneficiaries of core pipeline supplies are 203,750 vulnerable children living in camps, PoCs, formal IDP camps or host communities in conflict affected areas and refugee returnees. The children will benefit from emergency vaccination to sure up their immunity to vaccine preventable diseases. Vaccines against measles, meningitis, and cholera as well as cold chain equipment and devices will be procured.
60,000 children are expected to be reached during the Rapid Response Missions. These will include children aged 6 months-15years who will be reached with immunization services and curative consultations and pregnant women who are expected to benefit from ANC services, tetanus toxoid vaccination, curative consultations and the provision of long-lasting insecticide treated nets (LLITN).

1 - Needs

About 3,296,823 people in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian health care services with 2,049,990 people targeted for health assistance in 2020. The findings of SARA survey (2018) show that, only one third of (1,384) health facilities in South Sudan have the overall capacity to provide general health services (in terms of having basic amenities, equipment, IPC standard precautions, diagnostic capacity, essential medicines). At least four in ten health facilities have been affected by the conflict, from being looted, destroyed, damaged and/or occupied in 2016 and 2017; especially in the former conflict affected States of Unity, Jonglei, and Upper Nile. This makes access to health services difficult for the large proportion of the vulnerable internally displayed person and host community members. Overall, health service operation in South Sudan is very expensive due to poor road networks, necessitating the use of costly charter flights to transport supplies to some locations. During the dry season, whilst more road transport routes are open, the likelihood of ambushes and looting is very high which makes transportation of supplies by road very difficult. Meanwhile, rains during the wet season render approximately 60 per cent of roads in the country unusable. Routine immunization coverage remains very low (50 per cent) and much lower in former conflict affected states below 30 per cent. Even though 71 per cent of the facilities provide immunization services, only 40 per cent offer the services daily and 28 per cent offer the services weekly (SARA 2018). The consistently low immunization coverage predisposes children, living in PoCs, IDPs and refugee returnees, to vaccine preventable diseases such as measles, pertussis, and diphtheria disease. Measles outbreaks continues to be reported throughout the country. As of October, a total of 3525 cases, including 22 deaths have been reported, resulting in a case fatality rate of 0.6 per cent in 18 counties and four PoCs. Suspected cases of diphtheria and pertussis outbreak has also been reported. South Sudan is in the African Meningitis belt, hence is prone to seasonal outbreaks, in 2018 about 107 suspected meningitis cases and 28 deaths have been reported. Cholera outbreak ended after one-year long transmission in 2017, four counties and three POCs are identified as hotspots for cholera transmission hence vaccination needs to be given to the population to prevent cholera outbreak. Against this backdrop, in hard to reach, conflict-affected areas with intermittent periods of stability and access, a key strategy for delivery of lifesaving health services is the use of IRRM. This is because the IRRM platform enables the delivery of essential healthcare package that can be accessed by IDPs in inaccessible areas, hard to reach areas in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states. Meanwhile, improving the immunization core pipeline through procurement of vaccines, especially measles and meningitis vaccine, and distribution of oral cholera vaccine, installation of cold chain equipment and use of portable cold chain equipment will assist in increasing access to quality equitable immunization services among the IDPs. Combined, these efforts will contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality among affected vulnerable populations.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will ensure activities are implemented in safe locations by engaging relevant security resources to provide safety of the beneficiaries prior and during each RRM mission. Overall, the project will ensure that services are provided in decent, neat, friendly, inclusive and conducive environment by trained health workers. UNICEF will work with other partners to conduct robust awareness and sensitization campaigns to raise awareness about their protection concerns and human rights. The project will make use of community-based local protection mechanisms such as community complaints and management structures. Persons with specific needs like unaccompanied boys and girls, older people (men and women) and disabled women and men in the project implementation area will be given priority in emergency health services.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project activities will target children, women both IDPS and vulnerable in the host communities in the hard to reach areas and other areas with limited access to services. In order to create community ownership. RRM teams engage with the communities and local authorities in each mission and upon arrival. Once deployed, UNICEF/WFP/FAO teams’ leaders organize a preliminary meeting with the local authorities of the location to discuss population movement and estimated figures of the displaced population to be covered by the rapid response services. They also explain the content of the mission and the services that will be delivered. The teams then recruit community mobilisers to inform and mobilize the communities to benefit from the services made available during the duration of the RRM mission. At the end of each mission, the RRM teams organize a meeting with the leaders of the community and encourage them to give their feedback on the services provided, to raise any concerns or discontent they might have and to flag any protection or other issue related to the mission. In locations where there is a presence of implementing partners and where there is a health centre, community feedback can also be channeled after the departure of the RRM teams through the implementing partners and health facility staff who remain in the location. This feedback then forms part of the project performance reporting to the Health Cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experiences.
