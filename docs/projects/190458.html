Project Summary

In Tigrai there have been over 494 thousand children and adolescents who have stayed out of school because of the fact the living standard of their families is below poverty line. This has been confounded by the concurrence of the one-year-old armed conflict and that of the corona virus pandemic that happened in Tigray which has sadly left all of the children and adolescents out of school for two consecutive academic years. Out of the total school-age population in Tigrai, around 494,148 children and youths that account for 28.8 percent have not enrolled to attend school. Of these, 48 percent are girls, and 136,453 children are under the age of 10.
According to the statistics of the regional bureau of education, in 2020 there were more than 16,705 out-of-school children living in southern zone of Tigrai. However, the war that has happened in Tigrai exacerbated the situation of the school age children in that many more children are encountering humanitarian and socio-economic crisis. They have become displaced and now living in an adverse condition. Their familial and social structures have also been disrupted in a way that precipitated children, adolescents and youths to have very limited opportunities to develop social and emotional life-skills (SELS) that involve self-control, empathy, sympathy, negotiation, reconciliation and problem solving, which are normally cultivated at the family level and further developed at schools.
In southern zone of Tirai are 12 urban and rural woredas where there are two types of out-of-school children: those who have never gone to or dropped out of school and those whose schooling have been interrupted due to poverty, the ongoing armed-conflict and COVID 19, respectively. As a result of such circumstances, the children automatically turned above school age. So, children who passed the normal age to start schooling are very much unlikely to attend grade 1 since the educational system of the Tigrai region doesn’t allow them to do so and that they feel so ashamed that they hardly want to learn in such a grade level class where they don’t find their peers/equals. In other words, traditionally it is difficult for 12 years old children to sit learn with that of the 7 years of age in grade 1. Above all, it might be very challenging in terms of finding content and methodological choice appropriate to their age. Furthermore, whenever both older and younger children are supposed to learn in the same grade, there might exist multiple challenges that would put a strain on the already overcrowded classrooms, limited teachers, and insufficient learning materials. To put in a nut shell, combining of learners of wider gaps in terms of age in the same classrooms could make the teaching learning process so tough and raise possible protection concerns.
Here, it is worth pointing the tremendous experiences TDA has developed in the past six years in light of addressing such problematic issues by employing various strategies. One of the most realistic and pragmatic approach is the Accelerated Learning Program (ALP). TDA was successful in applying ALP project in three selected woredas of Tigrai where thousands of children of 9 to 14 of ages have been brought in to schools relevant to their grade level.
Education is a key to tackle the multiple challenges the OOSC are facing following the current war and the existing poverty. For this reason, TDA now intends to support 15,000 OOSC in 250 sites/schools. In order to successfully undertake a two-year ALP project that begins this academic year, TDA has prepared this concept note to work with partner organizations to provide financial donation. TDA will work with Child Protection, WASH and Nutrition clusters for the cross sectoral linkages, and will coordinate with other education cluster members to implement and build on their best practices.

Project Management To accomplish the planned activities and achieve the envisaged project results, a need for involvement of various stakeholders and clear structure that reflects each specific responsibility and their relationship shall be put in place. The key stakeholders of this project are the target school and their communities, Tigray Regional Education Bureau (TREB) and its subsequent structure at woredas, Tigray Development Association (TDA), the funding partner and woreda administration office. TDA will be responsible for the implementation of this project. The whole process of tendering and associated activities i.e procurement or distribution of equipment and educational materials will be the responsibility of TDA. The TDA financial manual will be involved in providing various technical assistance to the organization whenever need arises. The day-to-day activities will be managed by the concerned project coordinator, woreda education offices (WEO) and school administration. Hence, it would be the responsibility of the various organs including the experts of the TDA to see the project is implemented efficiently and effectively. The same bodies shall also be responsible to supervise and prepare the most possible transparent, timely and up-to-standard reports. As to the financial management aspect of the project, it will follow the established financial rules of the TDA and donor guidelines. The required equipment or educational materials will be procured by the TDA in consultation with the representatives of the donor. A bid document will be prepared and invitation for bid will be noticed to local suppliers either internal or external depending upon their availability, and a contract will be awarded after selection. As soon as the equipment or materials are purchased, the TDA will distribute them to the target schools.
