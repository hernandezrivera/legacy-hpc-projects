Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon continues to wreak havoc on vulnerable populations, particularly children, adolescent girls, and teenage mothers. This crisis, fueled by violent clashes between non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and State Security Forces (SSFs), has resulted in widespread displacement, destruction of property, and loss of life. Vulnerable groups, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities, face extreme challenges due to the collapse of social systems, lack of livelihood opportunities, increased rates of teenage pregnancies, and preventable maternal deaths.
The project focuses on addressing the critical gaps in gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response, tackling trauma, and empowering affected populations with life skills and access to essential services. Additionally, the project emphasizes raising awareness of GBV and child protection issues to strengthen community-based prevention mechanisms and provide sustainable support for survivors. Targeting the divisions of Boyo, Bui, Donga-Mantung, and Ngo-Ketunjia in the Northwest, as well as Fako, Kupe-Manenguba, Lebialem, Manyu, and Meme in the Southwest, the project will create safe spaces, provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), vocational training, and raise awareness of reproductive health to reduce preventable maternal deaths.
By fostering community resilience, this project aims to restore dignity and improve the quality of life for adolescent girls, teenage mothers, and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) in these conflict-affected regions, while addressing the root causes and consequences of GBV and child protection issues.
Project Objectives
The project integrates GBV prevention and response strategies with child protection and general protection activities to reduce risks, prevent maternal deaths among teenagers, empower adolescent girls, and support vulnerable children.
The project aims to:
1. Reduce GBV risks, by providing prevention strategies such as community awareness campaigns to promote understanding and protective behaviours, establishing safe channels for reporting SEA, and conducting safety audits to identify and mitigate risks. Response strategies focus on providing targeted case management, MHPSS, and medical care, including strengthening referral pathways for specialised services and training service providers in GBV case management to ensure survivor-centred care.
2. To prevent maternal deaths, the project raises awareness of the links between GBV and reproductive health risks, including early pregnancies, provides access to maternal health services, and trains health workers to identify and respond to GBV cases. Livelihood opportunities are fostered through vocational training for adolescent girls and teenage mothers and the establishment of Teenage Mothers’ Clubs to promote economic independence and peer-led GBV awareness.
3. Protect conflict-affected children by supporting vulnerable children and Unaccompanied and Separated Children through case management, including delivering MHPSS, family reunification services, and creating child-friendly spaces while strengthening community-based child protection mechanisms and integrating GBV awareness.
4. Provide civil documentation, protection monitoring, peacebuilding, and training community members on protection standards to build resilience.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The crisis in NWSW has led to alarming rates of GBV, trauma, and maternal health challenges: 1. GBV Risks: Adolescent girls face heightened risks of exploitation and abuse. With 26.2% of IDPs living in precarious conditions, the lack of GBV services further exacerbates their vulnerability. Teenage pregnancies have surged, leading to preventable maternal deaths due to inadequate healthcare access. 2. Trauma and Psychosocial Distress: Over 60% of children in the NWSW regions show signs of psychological distress (Save the Children, 2023). UASC face compounded challenges, including exposure to violence and separation from caregivers, with 30.3% of UASC in the Northwest and 27% in the Southwest. 3. Lack of Livelihoods and Education: Teenage mothers and adolescent girls lack access to vocational training and education, making it difficult for them to provide for their children or escape cycles of poverty. 4. Preventable Maternal Deaths: The increase in teenage pregnancies has resulted in preventable maternal deaths due to a lack of reproductive health awareness and services. Teenage mothers often lack access to antenatal care, safe delivery options, and postpartum support.
