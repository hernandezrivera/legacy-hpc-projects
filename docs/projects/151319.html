Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains critical due to ongoing impacts of drought, displacement and conflict. As per the FSNAU-FEWSNET 2018 Post Gu findings, 4.6 million people are still in need of urgent assistance, including an estimated 2.5 million children.
The Expanded Program of Immunization’s (EPI) performance is suboptimal according to WHO data, routine child immunization coverage among one-year-old children for measles is 24% and for diptheria, tetanus and pertussis (DTP3) is 31%. It is estimated that in Somalia only 30%–40% of children are immunized against the six major childhood illnesses, relatively low compared to the global coverage of above 80% . The leading causes of infant and child mortality are illnesses such as pneumonia (24 %), diarrhoea (19 %), and measles (12 %), as well as neonatal disorders (17 %).
The project will target women of child bearing age (WCBA) and children below 5 years of age in Bondhere, Shibis in Banadir region, Dhusamareb and Elder in Galgaduud region and Harardhere in Mudug region. This will encompass the host community and Internally displaced persons that are vulnerable. 31,734 pregnant and lactating women will have access to quality RMNCH Services, while 84624 children under-5 have access to essential primary health care, immunization against vaccine preventable diseases to reduce morbidity and mortality from main childhood illnesses, including malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea and measles as well as support implementation of community hygiene Promotion for AWD/Cholera Prevention.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The community resilience approach will be overlaid with basic service provision of key health and nutrition, basic community-based public health services for prevention, promotion and referral and will be done by trained community based workers. By building the capacity of the local community continuity of care is expected to exist and guarantee sustainability since the trained staff will are the local community and can deliver services with little support from the community’s internal resourcesThe community based strategy will be channeled through service committees such as community development committees (CDCs), Community health committees through Community consultation and action planning which will support the delivery of community based public health and nutrition. Linked with local governance structure, we will train community based workers on delivering of prevention and promotion services through training and supportive supervision for stewarding community public health interventions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The Internally displaced persons have a higher risk of exclusion than the host community due to their vulnerability and manipulation by community gate keepers.CISP will prevent exclusion and inequality by ensuring equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion and ensure they reach their full potential. This will be done by ensuring:• Equal representation in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees (CDC) so that the displaced persons have a say in these community leadership staructures• Training the Health staff on do no harm;

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Exclusion through gender and age is rife in Somalia due to deep customs and culture which denies women and girls to compete with the their opposite gender, having this in mind the target beneficiaries are also at risk of exclusion due to the above mentioned factors but this will be mitigated from planning through to implementaion of the project to ensure that none of the target population is excluded. The following will be put in place to mitigate any form of exclusion• When hiring incentive staff equal opportunities will be given to all irrespective of their social background, age and gender. • Equal representation of both genders in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees (CDC). so that women remain part of the decision makers of the community.• The proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health without giving preferences to anyone based on gender and age.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The target population faces continuous displacements due to armed conflict between SNA/AMISOM on one side and the Militant group on the other.The target population also faces forceful recruitment of young boys to the militia of the militant groups, although some local communities fought back and resisted they could not sustain and were ultimately defeated like what happened in some areas in Mudug region like Harardhere.As per the current situation the only way to mitigate is to families to send the young to government controlled districts and for long term solutions is to liberate those areas from the iron grip of the militants
