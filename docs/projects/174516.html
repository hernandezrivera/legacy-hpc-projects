Project Summary

According to UNICEF, children worldwide suffer insidious forms of violence, exploitation and abuse. It indicates that violence against children knows no boundaries and it happens in every country, and even in the places where children should be most protected – their homes, schools, online and can be physical, emotional or sexual. This is worst for children in humanitarian settings who are more vulnerable. During armed conflict, natural disasters and other emergencies, children may be forced to flee their homes, some torn from their families and exposed to exploitation and abuse along the way. They may be injured or killed by explosive weapons in conflict, or recruited by armed forces. Especially for girls and women, the threat of gender-based violence soars.
African countries with Cameroon inclusive, have signed and ratified the African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child. In Cameroon, children are considered to be every human being below the age of 18 years. Several legislative acts and decrees protecting children’s rights and welfare have been put in place. These include the right to education, health and a standard of living. This has however not been the case of Cameroon as reports from Child Protection, Global Protection Cluster indicates that the country is affected by three, concurrent, complex humanitarian situations: Boko Haram violence in the Far North region; the influx of refugees from the Central African Republic into the eastern regions (Adamawa, North and East); violence in the North West and South West regions with spillover effects in the West and Littoral regions. As a result, violation of children’s rights such as physical abuse, rape, forced marriage, child trafficking, child labor has been noticed.
This situation has been worsened specifically in the North West and South West Regions of the country which has been witnessing a socio-political crisis since 2016.
With the increased deployment of defense forces and proliferation of non-state armed groups, the crisis has become an armed conflict. Increased insecurity, the burning down of homes, violence and consequent widespread injury and civilian loss of life have forced thousands of families to flee their homes. Displacement continues to have serious consequences on the livelihoods and living conditions of the affected populations. According to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) Situation Report No. 3 As of 31 January 2019, the insecurity has forced more than 430,000 people to flee their homes, more than 380,000 people need shelter, and some 418,000 people need Non-Food Item (NFI) assistance. This situation has affected children and young adults diversely; many are now heading households, many unidentified and are separated from their primary families. Youths have had to stop schooling and many have pulled out of vocational activities and are now jobless.
There is urgent need to provide immediate humanitarian support as all children have the right to be protected from violence, exploitation and abuse. Child protection systems help children access vital social services and fair justice systems including those with disabilities. 1 - Besoins / Needs

For the past four years the Anglophone regions of Cameroon have been experiencing a sociopolitical crisis that has affected every other sector in the two regions - especially the economic, social, education and health sectors. More than 437.000 people are internally displaced (United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs, Cameroon Situation Report). The crisis has forced many children and adolescents from the villages to take refuge in major towns and cities. This has led to high rates of Juvenal delinquency, overcrowded cities, high crime wave, dependency and mistrust of authorities. As indicated by reliefweb.int report on Cameroon, most schools have been closed for the last two years and more precisely, schools have become an important focus of the crisis due to the implementation of school boycotts, severely affecting a whole generation of children. Not only due to the intrinsic dangers and physical violence involved, but because forced absenteeism has exposed these children to significant risks of diverse forms of sexual violence, association to armed forces and armed groups, and family separation; all leading to extended psycho social distress and mental disorders. The Geneva Palais briefing note on the situation of children in the North-West and South-West regions of Cameroon suggest that some 1.3 million people, including around 650,000 children, are in need of some form of humanitarian assistance. Strengthening immediate integrative response to children and adolescents in need stands as a priority for these conflict-driven regions. To meet those needs, this project, in collaboration with major stakeholder will provide needed comprehensive response to the target population using the following: Activities • Establish and support
