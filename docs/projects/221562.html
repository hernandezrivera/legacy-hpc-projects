Project Summary

Concern will continue providing life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services in Aweil North County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) state; and in Guit County in Unity State. In Aweil North, Concern will provide nutrition services across 22 sites and 9 in Guit. Concern will use the Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach in Guit County, while in Aweil North, Concern in collaboration with the Ministry of Health (MoH), Nutrition Cluster, UNICEF and WFP piloting the new WHO guideline. Concern nutrition services include: Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP), Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) and Stabilization Center (SC) among children under 5 years, pregnant and breastfeeding women who are acutely malnourished. Concern will continue to contribute on the prevention of malnutrition through a combination of interventions including Social Behavior Change (SBC) through individual counselling based on the mother and child pair assessment findings and group sessions at the nutrition sites and community level, promotion of home gardening, cooking demonstrations and promotion of good hygiene practices, multipurpose cash programing for at risk groups and integrating the nutrition programme with other sectors such as Health, WASH, FSL and protection for improved health, water hygiene and sanitation, household food security and to prevent risks of gender based violence (GBV). For better outcome and ownership, Concern ensures community participation and improve access and coverage through the community nutrition volunteers (CNV) strategy for community mobilization, screening, follow up cases and dissemination of recommended nutrition behaviours. Concern will continue its success with reducing risks of malnutrition through supporting mother and father groups in improving Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN). The SBC curriculum of mother/fathers groups promotes gender equality and male involvement on maternal infant and young child nutrition and care and feeding practices.
In Raja County in WBeG state and Aweil West county in NBeG state, Concern will support 4 mobile clinics to provide health and nutrition services to refugees, returnees and host communities. The intervention aims to reduce morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition, so the mobile clinics will increase access to and utilization of preventive nutrition services and early referral to SAM/MAM treatment services. Active screening at entry and transit points is vital for early detection and prevention of severe cases as refugees/ returnees face increased risk of malnutrition. Concern has integrated nutrition screening and referral to the nutrition sites managed by AFOD in Raja and Alight in Aweil West. The program will coordinate with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH), County Health Department (CHD) and the Health Sector Transformation project (HSTP) partner on the bi annual Vitamin A supplementation, and deworming campaign.
To ensure good cooperation and coordination, Concern will work closely with the local authorities, County Health Department (CHD), MoH, Nutrition Cluster, UNICEF, WFP and other stakeholder.
Concern will reach a total of 83,201 individuals (6,679 SAM cases, 27,921 MAM children and 16,422 MAM PBW) during the project period.

1. Needs

The humanitarian crises in South Sudan continues to deteriorate in 2025, driven by multiple, converging factors. The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation is deteriorating due to the economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding and low agricultural production. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. The latest IPC analysis indicated that 7.7 million people (about 57% of the analysed population) are projected to face acute food insecurity (IPC phase 3 or above) during the lean season (April-July 2025). Similarly, the latest IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) analysis indicated that between July 2024 and June 2025 an estimated 2.1 million children under 5 years are suffering or expected to suffer from acute malnutrition. Of which, 646,362 are at risk of severe acute malnutrition and need outpatient therapeutic support while, over 1.4 million children are at risk of moderate acute malnutrition and need supplementary feeding support. In addition, 1.1 million pregnant and breastfeeding women are at risk of moderate acute malnutrition and need supplementary feeding support. Based on the latest IPC analysis projection, the proposed counties, Aweil North and Guit are in an emergency level (IPC 4) of food insecurity and IPC AMN. Moreover, the latest SMART survey conducted by Concern and REACH in Aweil North in April 2024, indicated GAM of 26% and SAM of 3.9%. Similarly, high level of GAM (24.1%) and SAM (6.9%) recorded in Guit SMART survey conducted by Concern in May 2024. The key drivers of high level of acute malnutrition include, inadequate child feeding practices, high diseases burden, low programme coverage, limited access to sanitation facilities and food insecurity. Given the depleted local coping capacities, the need for the continued nutrition services has increased. High levels of acute malnutrition is often found among women and girls due to gender-inequitable access to nutritious foods, healthcare and WASH services. It is therefore, imperative that Concern continues its life-saving interventions for the provision of gender-responsive nutrition services, integrated with Concern’s other donor funded multi-sector interventions for wider and better outcomes. The programme will also contribute to Concern Country Strategic Plan 2025-2027 with a specific focus on quality nutrition services and addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition.

2. Project activities

CO-1: Concern will strengthen the prevention of acute malnutrition intervention through Social Behavior Change activities tailored to address specific risks and challenges for mothers, fathers, and caregivers with children under 5 years and adolescents in practicing key recommended behaviour. Concern will set up 62 mother and 62 father groups to address specific risks and needs following the mother and father group curriculum. Demonstration gardens will be established in 31 nutrition sites for mothers/caregivers to learn on how to produce vegetables. In Aweil North, Concern will pilot new WHO guidelines, focusing more on prevention and for children registered in the nutrition programme, post exit interventions will be included. When children exit nutritional treatment, mothers and caregivers of infants and children treated in OTP and TSFP will be provided with interventions, such as counselling (on IYCF practices, recognition of common childhood illnesses, and health-seeking behaviours), support to provide responsive care, and safe water, sanitation, and hygiene to improve overall child health and prevent relapse to wasting. Concern will support CHD and SMoH in bi annual VASD campaigns.CO2: Concern will follow CMAM approach for the treatment of both severe and moderate acute malnutrition. Concern uses CNVs for early detection of malnourished individuals, follow up and home visits and will promote family MUAC to monitor and screen and refer their own children for further assessments and enrolment. Concern will provide nutrition services in 9 sites in Guit and 22 in Aweil North. Severely malnourished children who are identified with MUAC<115mm or presence of bilateral pitting oedema grade 1 and 2 or WHZ score <-3 without medical complications will be managed through the weekly OTP while those with medical complications and presence of oedema grade 3 will be referred to the stabilization centres managed by Concern until the medical complications are resolved and return to OTP to complete treatment. Children discharged from OTP will be referred to TSFP to prevent relapse. Children 6-59 months with moderate acute malnutrition (MUAC <125mm or WHZ score >=-3 and <-2) and Pregnant women with visible pregnancy (4 months and above) and breastfeeding women with infants below 6 months with MUAC<230mm will be treated in TSFP. In Raja, Aweil West and Leer, Concern will conduct screening through mobile and static clinics and SAM/MAM will be referred to nearest nutrition sites managed other nutrition partner. CO3: Concern will contribute to national database by providing monthly NIS data and monthly 5W on activity info. Concern has a CMAM database to monitor the admission trend and exit performance indicators monthly. The analysis will be used to adapt implementation strategy for further improvement. Concern will conduct SMART surveys in Guit and Aweil North and findings will be used for IPC classification. CO4: Concern will provide integrated health and nutrition services in 8 PHCUs in Aweil North, and 2 in Guit. On the remaining nutrition sites, Concern will integrate the nutrition interventions with HSTP partners. Concern will integrate WASH in health and nutrition facilities to ensure infection prevention and controls. OTP beneficiaries will receive mosquito nets and to promote hygiene practices OTP and TSFP beneficiaries will be provided with soap. Concern promotes home gardening for OTP, TSFP beneficiaries and mother groups through demonstration gardens, training and vegetable seeds. Concern also promotes VSLA by providing training and kits. Moreover, cases with protection issues will be referred to the protection partners working in the area and Concern will conduct safety audits using tools developed by the Nutrition cluster.Nutrition staff will be trained on Integrated Management of New born and Childhood Illness (IMNCI) protocol focussing on the identification of danger signs in children for referral to the health facilities.
