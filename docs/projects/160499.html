Project Summary

This project will seek to provide immediate lifesaving response to vulnerable crisis affected children from returnees, IDPs and host communities of Nasir, Ayod and Fangak counties through strengthened comprehensive case management including family tracing & reunification; referral for services; provision of critical age and gender appropriate psychosocial support services in a timely, holistic and child friendly manner as well as to the traumatized adult caregivers whose children are also facing trauma, coordination monitoring and assessments of child protection related risk at entry points and in the community including prevention from and response to GBV related risk to girls and boys under 18 years old e.g. abuse, exploitation, rape, violent and earlier or force marriage to children under 18 years old. The projects will also conducts awareness raising activities on prevention of family separation.
ADA under these projects will provide critical age and genders appropriate psycho social support services in individual and group sessions including Psycho social support to caregivers of children who are traumatized and undergoing PSS activities, identification, registration, interim care, family tracing & reunification to unaccompanied and separated children, advocate for the GBV related risk prevention including early marriage, Sexual abuse, exploitation and violent, referral to other services such as health, education, nutrition, shelter and NFIs among others to those at risk and other vulnerable children including those facing other forms of violence, orphans and children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) in a timely, holistic and child friendly manner and also promote resilience to the traumatized ones.
The project will also seek to enhance the protection environment of vulnerable crisis affected girls, boys and youths to enable durable solutions through establishment of community based child protection networks, foster family groups, youth/adolescent clubs, Child Friendly Spaces, child rights clubs, and recreational centers. This will create enabling conditions for realization of solutions and prevention of further displacement which leads to family separations; identification of risks, dangers and impediments; advocacy and mitigation measures, psycho social support, peaceful co-existence initiatives and resilience.
A total of 15,000 people (7,500 males and 7,500 females) will benefit from this project. Out of this, 10,000 (75%) will be children (5,000 girls; 5,000 boys) and 5,000 (25%) will be adults - 2,500 males and 2,500 females. However, 3,500 of the total beneficiaries (25%) will be from host community while 7,000 (45%) will be from Returnees and 4,500 (30%) from IDPs.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2020, 4.8 million people are mapped to be in need of protection assistance country wide. The needs are vast and the 2020 hotspots include Nasir County in Upper Mile State, as well as Fangak and Ayod Counties in Joglei State – areas being targeted by ADA. Nasir County, at the border of Ethiopia and South Sudan has been experiencing multiple armed conflicts and natural disasters like floods – all leading to displacements, death and associated child protection concerns e.g. abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation. These problems are compounded further as many people experienced multiple displacements. The majority and most vulnerable are women, children, and elderly people. Based on the March 2019 IRNA assessment conducted by different clusters like (WASH, FSL, NFIs, Nutrition, Protection, Health) in Nasir County, child protection gaps were clearly identified, especially with the over 40,000 returnees in early 2019, with more arriving almost every day into Nasir. The report observes the very limited protection partners in Nasir and the need to reinforce child protection in the area. It also recommended: ‘1. Urgent need of Child Protection Partner to implement CPiE (Family Tracing & reunification, psychosocial support via CFS and Mine Risk Education) and GBViE in Nasir County. 2. Community sensitization and awareness raising on safety of children and child right. 3. Establish, capacitate and strengthen community base child protection’ Thousands of children are out of school. Hundreds are missing from unit families, others were separated and facing abuse or neglect as well as traumatized due to loss of some of their beloved ones due to the conflict. Al these are people in need of psycho social support, family tracing and reunification plus provision of comprehensive case management. Due to the signed peace deal between the warring parties in the South Sudan conflict, over 75,000 returnees were reported to be returning back to Fangak and Ayod counties (in Jonglei State) from Sudan, Malakal POC as well as from Bor and Juba POCs according to REACH assessment of March 2019 in Jonglei State. Returnees are said to be facing a number of protection concerns which includes trauma, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation, others are reported to be missing their children. There are a number of unaccompanied and separated children, orphans . Some were reported to be facing GBV-related risks like domestic violence and early or forced marriages which need to be mitigated through targeted psychosocial support. Besides, there are a number of protection concerns reported at the entry points. Therefore, there is high need for the implementation of CPiE activities including (Family Tracing & reunification, Psycho social support via CFS and Comprehensive case management to the UASC children who are in critical need of basic services, mainstreaming of GBV in child protection as well as community sensitization and awareness raising on safety of children and child right in all targeted locations of Ayod and Fangak counties. ADA will identify more beneficiaries especially those who have suffered multiple displacements within Nasir and those who are returning from the neighboring countries and in need of family tracing and reunification, comprehensive case management and psychosocial support services as well as those in need of basic services.ADA is already aware of the situation in all the three locations. ADA is having field bases in Ayod, Fangak and Nasir Counties. ADA intends to target 15,000 people (M=7,500 and F = 7,500) in all the three Counties through the 2020 HRP.

2 - Activities

ADA will provide comprehensive training in case management and psychosocial support. Besides, it will provide the actual case management and psychosocial support services to the community through the people trained. The activities will be conducted in Ayod and Fangak alongside INTERSOS and CAO, but only in the following areas not covered by the two partners: Menime, Pagil, Haat, Mogok and Jiech in Ayod County; Mareang, Keew, Paguir, Toch and Nyadin payams in Fangak County. ADA is the only partner doing child protection in Nasir County currently, but not covering the entire county. In 2020, it will extend services to Roam, Maker, Kiechkuon, Dingkar, Dhording and Wanding areas. The specific ADA interventions will include the following: Activity 1: • Conduct induction training on case management and psychosocial support to 24 staff (12 case workers & 12 PSS facilitators) for 3 days. 24 new staff from Nasir, Fangak and Ayod will be recruited and trained. These will be comprise 12 case workers and 12 PSS facilitators (12M, 12F). Case workers will identify; document; assess the protection needs of children; plan and implement the plan for intervention on the identified needs including referral to other actors for services; and follow up of cases; using global child protection Case Management minimum standards, PSS facilitators will be trained on basic skills like how to communicate with children, signs of children facing distress or other forms of abuse, Psychological First Aid, and planning and management of mobile Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) activities, which are age, gender and culturally appropriate and provide Psychosocial Support activities including caregivers support sessions etc.Activity 2:• Provide Comprehensive case management including for UASC and use of Cash Based Intervention (CBI) for vulnerable children. This will also include service mapping and establishing strong referral pathways. ADA will provide comprehensive case management service to 900 children (450 boys and 450 girls). Case workers will identify 900 children (450 boys and 450 girls). Identification will be done using priority list from standard operating procedures for case management in South Sudan. Once identified, children with critical protection concerns or those at high risk will be supported through comprehensive case management procedures in line with minimum standards of case management. • Children with health needs and those malnourished will be referred to other actors in charge of health and nutrition service provision; and those out of school will be referred to actors providing education for school enrollment. • All child survivors of Gender Based Violence (GBV) will be referred to GBV actors in the target locations. Cases of child recruitment and use in armed groups will be reported to UNICEF through Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) focal person. ADA will ensure that children released from armed groups will benefit from release and reintegration services. The well-being of all children with critical protection concerns will be monitored through regular follow up visits to ensure that new protection needs and risks are prevented or responded to in good time. 300 most vulnerable children will be supported through case based intervention (CBI) as part of comprehensive case management.300 UASC will be identified/registered, documented and referred for family tracing and reunification and 4500 children and some of their caregivers will be receiving PSS activities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, beneficiaries rights will be legally recognized, respected and promoted through this project design in accordance to the United Nations Deceleration on Human Rights, international human rights treaties and commission. The human rights deceleration requires that projects that intends to address crisis, emergency or catastrophes disasters be designed to allow maximum participation for all affected persons regardless of their color, race, religion, affiliation, sexual orientation.This project is designed to allow equal participation and engagement done through consultation of the affected persons to ensure interventions respond to solve the immediate and priority needs of the affected people. The beneficiaries have the legal right to report tribalism, sexual abuse and exploitation, mismanagement of project funds including diversion of funds for person gains by Agency staffs to the donors, cluster and the government for action and accountability. This can be done through established complaint reporting and feedback mechanism.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project target to reach to 15,000 people from IDPs, Returnees and host community in Ayod, Fangak and Nasir counties. Caseload allocation; 5000 individuals will be in Nasir and 5000 individuals in Ayod and 5000 individuals in Fangak, which is in line with the needs analysis of the affected populations. Caseload allocation per beneficiaries category; 4500 individuals will be IDPs, 7000 individuals will be Returnees and 3500 individuals will be the most vulnerable Host Community, in regards to the project beneficiaries.The project intended benefits will be awareness raising campaigns, comprehensive case management including PSS activities, Family tracing and reunification and will preach child protection risk prevention messages including early/ forced marriages, GBV etc. to both children and adults.The project is designed to allow for maximum meaningful participation and engagement of targeted people without discrimination and therefore, it will provide a durable solution through resilience, transparency and accountability to the affected persons. Most vulnerable people will be identified and will be included in decision-making on project matters that impact on them. A complaints and complements mechanism and desk will be established to ensure fair play within the project implementation system.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, ADA jointly work with the government mainly RRC/ROSS through project coordination and joint cluster missions which are planned collectively and aligned to the government priority plans. Prior to project inception, project meetings for implementation will be conducted with the local authorities in the project areas, at the State, County, Payam or Boma levels as safety audit measures for projects links with government plans. The communities in question were consulted prior to the project design phase through needs assessment and existing community feedback mechanism and the interests, needs, aspirations and future goals were included into the programming and implementation plan. This project complements the many contributions of the national government, Jonglei and Upper Nile States and Protection cluster (Child Protection Sub cluster) plans and objectives on the communities needs in Nasir, Fangak and Ayod Counties. It recognizes and will endeavor to work with existing network of local community structures; utilizing the support of local leadership and other partners such as INTERSOS, CMD, ADRA, NH, CRS, ALSI, RMF UNKEA, RI etc. in Ayod, Fangak and Nasir areas as well as the beneficiaries to ensure that only the most people in need receive the project inputs and skills. During the selection of the community structures, ADA will consult with the local authorities and provide the existing selection criteria for the community structures which includes community-based child protection networks and foster parents.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this project is aligned to the national plan or/framework for returnees, IDPs reintegration and it is consistent across clusters in the country. The humanitarian relief programming and coordination is regulated by the Relief and Rehabilitation commission at the national, state and county levels in conjunction with OCHA support. The Child Protection sub cluster and UNHCR does the alignment and coordination with support of sub cluster state focal points to ensure that project goals, objectives and activities are in line with national objectives and that the implementation criteria does not deviate from planned cluster methodology. The project developed its goals and objectives from the overall cluster general objectives which were developed in consultation and participation of national ministry of Gender, Child and social welfare resources priorities for 2020. The project aligned these to its organizational strategic plans and priorities for Ayod, Fangak and Nasir counties.The Child Protection Sub cluster has been and is working hand in hand together with UNHCR the general protection cluster lead to ensure the cluster priorities are well aligned to the national returns/refugees framework

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the security situation of Nasir, Fangak and Ayod counties is currently stable as a result of reduced armed conflicts and the revitalized signed peace agreement between the warring parties. This allows opportunities and an enabling environment for affected populations’ participation and engagement in productive activities. These include community-based psycho social support that can be linked to existing he child friendly spaces; awareness raising activities in the community; monitoring of the child protection related risks in the community; comprehensive case management in the community and petty trade. The projected availability of stable markets will help the children to be supported through cash-based intervention (CBISome slight inter communal incidents do happen, but the authorities in the ground usually handle the situation without further escalation especially in Nasir County hence, still the situation is expected to be normal as the project supports the needy people in a safe environment.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

As peace returns to the country, ADA proposes to start linking the various PSS and Child Protection services to relevant Government line ministries as national duty bearers. This will be discussed with the local authorities in Ayod, Fangak and Nasir. Actionable points will be derived for post-2020 scenario with prospective partners of Government in handling the situation, including close relatives of the affected. ADA’s experience in Ayod, Fangak and Nasir Counties and involvement of other partners, will help the team to identify and map other service providers, ensuring that all the gaps identified are reported to specific partners for further intervention as needed. Furthermore, ADA will apply its funding strategy to solicit for other donor resources for these locations to ensure that vulnerable children that may be left out in this rapid response will be covered with none HRP funding.ADA will provide trainings to the selected community structures and its staff in order to carry out what it takes to sustain the activities initiated by the project. This will help promote community self –reliance and resilience. It will be stressed severally that the intervention has a life span and that the community should create a sustainability option in the absence of external donor funds. As stated above, ADA will maintain its presence in the three counties and will be able to monitor the child protection situation once there is funding.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ADA will carry out activities that aim at reducing and mitigating protection risks including the cases related to GBV, family separations and recruitment of children into armed groups. ADA will continue promoting an environment where protection and assistant are delivered in safety and with dignity. Risk, Vulnerability and Capacity Analysis: ADA will create a context-specific protection risk and benefit analysis. ADA will analyze relative importance (likelihood and impact) and manageability (prevention or mitigation) for different groups and individuals. Desk Review and Needs Assessment: Gather situational protection information on the major risks for the affected population (what and for whom), sources of risks, and any community-based or self-protection mitigation mechanisms. This information could come from protection needs assessments, case management, and feedback mechanisms. ADA will build a monitoring system and an accountability framework on the basis of identified protection risks and benefits. ADA will continue to ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all of its activities as noted in the previous sections. ADA being protection actor will plan to further strengthen its links with protection cluster in 2020. ADA is a protection implementer and will remain as protection actor to continually monitor the protection needs in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as youths, adolescents, child headed households especially female headed ones, separated and unaccompanied children, children engaged in worst forms of child labor, exploitation and abuse; and other most vulnerable children. In line with protection referral systems. ADA will continue to conduct comprehensive case management and PSS activities for especially vulnerable children. ADA will also continue to coordinate with Mobile response to take the available service nearer to the people in need and ADA will promote strong measures for data management.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ADA will use Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) tool to ensure the community have multiple ways to share complaints and information in respect to this project. ADA will improve an inclusive system of interacting with communities and promote minimum standard for all mechanisms for regular tracking, reviewing and action feedback for the project. A complaints desk during project implementation will be installed in ADA field offices and Child Friendly Spaces to allow complains and feedback from the community. All cases will be recorded and addressed accordingly. Confidentiality will be assured across all the processes. Before the starting of the projects, ADA always does an initial needs assessment and engages the beneficiaries on how they can receive an assistance. ADA always provide information to beneficiaries before the implementation of projects. ADA will conduct induction meetings with the local authorities and the community members informing them on the project activities. Complaint mechanism will be provided and complains will be managed with care, dignity, confidentiality and respect while reporting to complaints desk. The beneficiaries will be encouraged to raise any complaint they like to raise. During the project implementation ADA will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, or address concerns and complaints. ADA has a robust M&E team to timely support the project team on all M&E related tasks, including the complaints raised by beneficiaries ADA will use its existing Pre and post project implementation assessment tools/cluster tools to assess the challenges and further needs before, during and after project implementation . ADA has staff trained on PSEA. A strong referral pathway will be build where the survivors of SEA will be referred to the concerned actors in line with cluster data management policies.
