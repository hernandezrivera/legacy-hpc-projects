Project Summary

The project aims at alleviating the negative effects of the country multi-level crisis on the accessibility to and quality of learning opportunities for Lebanese and migrants children. The action encompass community based activities for the most vulnerable HHs to ensure children continue their education. OOEC will be identified through an HHs level needs assessment in El Metn (Bourj Hammoud), Chouf (Sibline, Barja) and Zahle (Bar Elias) districts building on Cesvi and URDA presence in the areas with education, protection and CfW since 2011. The assessment also aims at identifying HHs needs for continuity/resumption of learning activities in terms of stationary, books, other education related costs and parents/caregivers lack of basic and digital literacy, which has been identified as one of the main reasons for children dropout. Assessment will include a protection risk analysis. 238 individuals outreached will be engaged in BTS awareness activities, while 182 are expected to be referred to basic services through existing referral pathways (RIMS, IA). 1650 most vulnerable families will receive unconditional, unrestricted CA to cover education related costs. The amount of 20 USD*child is aligned with UNICEF “Cash for Education Program” 2021-2022 for II shift students. The activity led by Cesvi intends to foster children retention by reducing economic barriers. The activity aims at targeting: 1) I shift students in schools identified in coordination with MEHE and the sector; 2) II shift students where not covered by UNICEF program; 3) OOEC and children enrolled in NFE at risk of drop outs. Target 3) will be selected based on the initial assessment conducted aimed at identifying vulnerabilities such us families adopting LH and FSS negative coping strategies and protection risks (not limited to). The activity will be coordinated with the CfE Task Force, which Cesvi is member of, in order to harmonize transfer modalities and PDM approach (ideally, assistance will be disbursed in USD through OMT, as one of the most reachable providers). In order to increase the effectiveness of the financial support and reduce the HHs economic burden, Cesvi recommends to distribute scholastic kits (stationary, booklets, light equipment) to the children benefitting of CfE (3300 based on an average of 2 children*HH). Learning material will be selected in coordination with MEHE/PMU and based on the needs identified during the assessment. Homeschooling support will be provided by URDA to 165 parents/caregivers selected among the HHs receiving economic support. Certified educators will deliver online modules aiming at enhancing parents/caregivers’ basic and digital literacy and strengthening their engagement in teaching and learning process. Modules will be tailored to each target group needs and beneficiaries will receive mentoring and constant follow-up. PSS-SEL programs to 743 individuals will integrate the homeschooling to tackle children and parents/caregivers protection needs, enhance wellbeing, especially in light of the consequences produced by Covid-19 outbreak and distance learning. Violence, bullying, cyber bullying, online safety, PSEA awareness activities and risk reduction strategies will complement the intervention. Activities build on URDA experience in conducting similar programs in the target areas.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Cesvi and URDA commit to the humanitarian principles and strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to the mere presence and provision of assistance. The project will make every effort to ensure a safe and secure environment for the beneficiaries. Particular attention will be paid to CfE to identify protection risks and mitigation measures. Staff will adopt the Do No Harm principle as outlined in Cesvi Code of Conduct and policies including, but not limited to, Child Safeguarding and PSEA. The Safeguarding Senior Specialist will train the staff. The program will adopt a gender-sensitive approach in all phases (stakeholder analysis, needs assessment, sampling, selection, implementation, MEAL). Gender and protection cross-cutting issues (differentiated impact, opportunities, threats, capacities, coping mechanisms and other protection issues), are mainstreamed through all the phases of PCM.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

As part of Cesvi commitment to higher accountability and its adherence to the Core Humanitarian Standard, a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be developed. Cesvi will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats and languages. The management of the CFM system will be shared with relevant stakeholders to ensure that it is built based on a participatory approach and that multiple communication channels are in place. This will minimize the risk that conflict of interests might prevent the complaints positive handling. Develop appropriate skills in frontline workers so as to guarantee full information on the mechanism and processes associated with receiving assistance (duration, value, frequency, eligibility…) will play a crucial role, as well as a mid-review and update of the protection risk analysis. If specific risks are detected, the project will activate referral procedures in order to refer identified cases to appropriate agencies and/or institutions.
