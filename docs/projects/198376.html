Project Summary

INTERSOS is a non-profit humanitarian organization that is committed to working at the front-line to bring humanitarian assistance to people in danger, victims of natural disasters and armed conflicts. INTERSOS has been implementing integrated multi-sector lifesaving and protection interventions in the proposed project sites since July 2017.
INTERSOS will continue to strengthen its communication with communities to support the drive for Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), with a strong focus on making service delivery equitable and transparent, through regular community meetings with community representatives and service providers on service provision, site management and site planning in close coordination with local authorities. In addition, INTERSOS will ensure that space is given for AAP mechanisms such as information desks and the operation of a complaints and feedback mechanism.
The protection project is designed to alleviate suffering, save lives and maintain human dignity with the aim to build the resilience of the community and promote local ownership so the benefits of the project are relevant beyond the emergency phase into development phase. Thus, engaging the community in planning, implementing and monitoring is a fundamental strategy to ensure that the project is guided by the identified needs and capacities. Gender-based violence is considered a public health issue and a human rights violation, which means that mitigation and response to GBV are among immediate life-saving priorities in emergencies and need to be integrated in all sectors of humanitarian response. Since the experience of GBV can be a distressing event, MHPSS will be a component intertwined to GBV response, prevention and risk mitigation and a part of care and support in this project.
The project will focus on provision of protection assistance to beneficiaries through capacity building, awareness and sensitization, sexual and gender-based violence related services, psychosocial support and material assistance. The protection interventions aim at providing the targeted population with quality services to respond to current protection needs but also contributing to strengthen their capacity for self-protection reinforcing positive coping mechanisms to stressful events.

1 - Needs

Protection monitoring reports highlighted violations of the right to life, liberty, and personal security as well as the right of freedom from torture, cruel and degrading treatment with various forms of physical and psychological violence committed against the population in Borno. This included abductions, extrajudicial killings, arbitrary arrests and detention, enforced disappearances, inhumane treatment of the elderly and the disabled (especially persons with mental illness), rape, sexual and physical assault, exploitation and abuse, Female Genital Mutilation (FGM), child recruitment. The COVID 19 pandemic and ever deteriorating global food security have exacerbated already precarious conditions, leaving a projected 8.4 million people across the BAY states in need of humanitarian assistance and protection in 2022. Among them, 4.2 million people live in Borno state alone with around 1.6 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) out of the 2.2 million persons displaced across the three North-Eastern BAY states 5 . In the displacement trends noted by the protection sector, out of the 1,613,019 persons are displaced in Borno state out of whom 49% live in host communities and 51% in camps and camp-like settings, 4.3 million of which are people in need of protection and 1,362,447 are experiencing gender-based violence. Women and girls are under the constant threat of violence, abduction, and rape. Gender-based violence (GBV) including domestic, sexual violence as well as forced and child marriages continue to be reported and are attributed to among others the conflict, insecurity, and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. Violence against women and girls continues to manifest in various forms like GBV, sexual violence, trafficking, and abductions that are believed to be widespread but underreported. Insufficient response services available for survivors of GBV further exacerbates the risks for women and girls. Some of the needs the project seeks to address include:1. Limited specialized, confidential and safe case management services for GBV survivors 2. Sexual and reproductive health needs: this project will seek to address this need through the provision of dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age.3. Mental health and psychosocial support: Women and girls who are survivors, at risk of GBV and women and adolescent girls will be provided with a safe and confidential space for the peer to peer support, group counseling, experience to improve bonding and build on positive coping mechanisms4. Limited information on GBV, protection and rights will be addressed through community awareness sessions, trainings and local capacity strengthening.5. Limited livelihood options: this will be addressed through the introduction of sustainable livelihood activities by managing income generating activities to provide resilience building support.6. Limited community-based protection networks will be addressed through identifying, establishment and strengthening of community protection networks.7. Limited protection advocacyThese needs were identified through analysis of INTERSOS GBV safety audits, risk mapping and protection needs assessments conducted with members of affected population. Further assessments, safety audits and review of assessment reports of other partners will be conducted to identify protection risks and trends in the areas of implementation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will access the protection situation and respond to immediate needs among vulnerable groups, especially persons living with disabilities, in the areas of intervention. INTERSOS has strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, supported by its international protection coordinator and seasoned national protection programme staff. Furthermore, all INTERSOS staff members are bound by the INTERSOS Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all INTERSOS operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature to its nature as a humanitarian organization, humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination and transparency. Also, INTERSOS, through its protection team, will document all feedback and updates from safety audits, analyze, and review alongside the GBVIMS. In addition, the Protection team will hold periodic dialogue meetings at the safe space to note and act on community opinion in relation to protection and empowering the community on protection and GBV prevention and response mechanism. This project will strengthen communication with community (CwC) to enhance Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) while ensuring AGD and protection mainstreaming (such as inclusion of PWD, PSEA, GBV prevention and response) approaches to increase the project impact. Protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures:1. Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects or activities2. Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries easy access to assistance and services and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, and3. Ensuring participation and empowerment by supporting the development of self protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

INTERSOS will establish functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. Help-desks will be set up in the target project sites to enable the beneficiaries report their complaints or give feedback on the project. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including face to face with field staff, suggestion boxes, help desk, help line and email. Complaints and feedback received from the population are registered in the help-desk registry including the MEAl database. In addition to the set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls is at the base of the project design. Since the onset of INTERSOS action in the region, it has become well-known and respected within the targeted communities, positively engaging with both the hosting and IDP communities. INTERSOS conducts focus group discussions with target beneficiaries to improve its programming, taking into consideration the suggestions coming directly from the affected communities. Feedback from the complaint and feedback mechanisms including the help-desk will be incorporated into the learning and information utilized in improving the quality of services provided and for future programming. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted after distributions to help INTERSOS gauge relevance, timeliness, and quality of items distributed to beneficiaries. Client feedback surveys will be administered at the end of service provision especially case management to measure the level of satisfaction with the services provided. INTERSOS has also trained staff on PSEA and put in place PSEA and safeguarding policies with stringent measures for defaulting staff. In every location of implementation, there is a PSEA focal point responsible for receiving complaints and reports on alleged acts of SEA. INTERSOS makes use of community based approach to gain insight into particular needs of vulnerable groups within the communities
