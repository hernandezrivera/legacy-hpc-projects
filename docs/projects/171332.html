Project Summary

At the national level in Somalia, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (11.8% in 2020 Gu, 13.1% in 2019 Deyr and 13.8% in 2019 Gu). Despite some improvements over the past seasons, malnutrition remains a challenge for children under-five and women of Somalia.
In 2021 the emergency nutrition needs will remain high in Somalia and without any nutrition assistance the situation may worsen. This is particularly true in the light of the influence of desert locust, potential drought conditions likely developing as a result of below average 2020 Deyr (October to December), a harsh 2021 dry Jilaal (January to March) season and a possible delay and/or poor performance of the 2021 Gu (April to June). The Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) assessments show that 5.1 million people across Somalia will face acute food insecurity through December 2020.
The recent needs analysis of 2020 conducted by the nutrition cluster estimates that around 962,000 children under the age of five (girls; 490,620, boys; 471,380) will require lifesaving nutrition services in 2021, of which 162,000 may be affected by life threatening severe acute malnutrition and 800,000 by moderate malnutrition. In addition, 181,954 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) will require malnutrition curative and preventive services. It is also estimated that around 771,802 boys and girls between the age of 6 to 23 and 389,205 PLWs will need preventive services.
The desert locust, the flash floods, ongoing conflict and Covid-19 pandemic continued to be the driving force and has contributed to the vulnerability and has remained predisposing factors for malnutrition. Furthermore, lack of access to primary health care by many Somalis, food, vitamin A, poor hygiene practice, poor compliance of feeding practice, lack of access of immunization service and lack of access to safe and clean drinking water are some of the key factors that has cause malnutrition. To address the predisposing factors, IOM will therefore propose to implement the following activities,
1. Integrating nutrition activities in 21 static and 13 mobile teams across Somalia (Puntland - Sanaag, South west state - Baidoa, Jubbaland - Kismayu, Gedo and Afmadow, and Somaliland - Sanaag region- Ceel-Aweyn.)
2. Ensuring mothers and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) key influencers are given necessary information of IYCF in Emergencies (IYCF-E).
3. Supplementing Vitamin A to children under-five.
4. Screening PLW and refer to the nearest Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) providers.
5. Conducting SMART and IYCF survey, to support and strengthen the surveillance and monitoring system in the country.
6. Ensuring prepositioning of Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) in co-ordination and partnership with UNICEF.
7. Training and capacity building of local staff on the management of acute malnutrition.
8. Implementing Mother Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) project so that mothers are equipped with necessary information of identifying malnutrition and referring to the nearest health center.
IOM will continue to co-ordinate with cluster stakeholders and local authority to avoid disruption and duplication of activities. IOM will implement these activities with the Ministry of health.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1: Integrate 23 OTP centers into static facilities currently supported by IOM, and into 13 mobile clinics in Jubaland, Southwest State, Somaliland, and Puntland. Activity 1.2: Identify and treat 3,200 children aged 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition through access to equitable, high-quality lifesaving OTP services using RUTF and micronutrient supplementation. Activity 1.3: Screen under-five both at the community and at the health centers with target number of 90,000 (45,000 boys and 45,000 girls). Activity 1.4: The project will provide Vitamin A supplementation to 9,000 (4,500 boys and 4,500 girls) under-five children who are admitted at the health centre. Activity 1.5: Provide 40,000 pregnant and lactating women with infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling and training, and linkage to immunization services especially measles vaccination. Activity 2.1: Preposition and distribute ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) and medicines to 21 static OTPs integrated into primary health facilities and 13 mobile OTP sites. Activity 2.2: Establish referral mechanisms from community level to OTP sites, and from OTP sites to/from other nutrition programs, to/from therapeutic supplementary feeding centers (TFSC) for moderate acute malnutrition, and to/from stabilization centers (SC) for SAM with complications requiring inpatient care. Activity 3.1: Provide training and on-the-job mentorship for 200 clinical health and nutrition staff (100 women, 100 men) across all integrated OTP and primary healthcare centers on IMAM and IYCF guidelines. Activity 3.2: Train 300 community outreach nutrition volunteers (150 women, 150 Men) via on-job training who will provide MUAC screening, referral to OTPs, and counseling in catchment communities of the OTPs. Activity 4.1 Conduct SMART surveys and IYCF surveys to strengthen monitoring and surveillance, analysis and utilization of early warning information. Activity 5.1: Implement Mother lead MUAC project so that mother can identify and refer children with malnutrition to the nearest health centers.

Mitigation Measures

• Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: Nutrition service delivery locations (static health facilities and mobile units that will have integrated OTPs) will be put in central, visible places and not more 500 meters away from settlements, and ideally within settlement areas. This will help protect women and girls from risks of sexual violence/exploitation when accessing the services. Separate toilet facilities with internal locks and secondary enclosures will be in place for women and men in OTPs (in the health facilities they are integrated into) with clear labels such as pictorial/signs to ensure that they are used by intended gender. In setups where threats are anticipated or previously experienced, location, timing and modality of service delivery will be considered. Coordination will be done with Shelter/NFI partners to put solar light installations at OTPs (nutrition service delivery points) to deter any risk of violence against women and girls. Nutrition services at community-level (screening, nutrition promotive services, OTP patient follow-up) will be delivered during daylight hours to allow beneﬁciaries who are often women and girls to reach home early. • Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.):The project will hire female staff members at project locations to ensure that women and girls feel more comfortable accessing services and communicating with health professionals. This will also provide an immediate feedback mechanism. Girls and boys will actively be engaged in children nutrition, health and hygiene promotion groups and campaigns. In the event there is a GBV survivor at the project site, the individual, IOM will ensure referral to the closest relevant body that handles GBV and protection issues. • Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities:IOM will gather data (age, sex, diversities) of communities and beneﬁciaries for effective and informative curative and preventive nutrition programing. This will help identify vulnerable and marginalized persons for planning, inclusion purposes and engagement. A systematic outreach process will be done to reach marginalized/minority vulnerable groups with nutrition community screening, mobilization, and outreach to avoid exclusion. Efforts will be made to ensure that information regarding provision of nutrition services will be disseminated to all members of communities (including women, girls, boys, men) through appropriate channel of communication such as radio. An inclusive feedback mechanism that will include all beneﬁciaries will be put in place which will help evaluate selection criteria and overall service gaps. In all nutrition service delivery points, a mechanism will be established to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women. • Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms:During the implementation of the project, IOM will ensure that the beneficiaries are given the necessary information related to the project, including that services are free. There will be free call number where they can lodge their complaint and proper response will be provided. Doing this will build the confidence of the beneficiaries and encourage more people to come forward to access the services and raise their issues without fear of intimidation.

Please provide further explanation

This project is based on national humanitarian response frameworks and will leverage on existing government and community structures. It will be implemented in partnership with the Ministry of Health and will include community engagement and participation throughout the project process to enhance accountability to affected populations. By implementing through the Ministry of Health, this project has sustainability inbuilt in its implementation modality in that the capacity of the Ministry of Health staff will continuously be strengthened eventually providing an enabling environment for full government ownership/operation of the nutrition services and reducing dependency. The entitlement of beneficiaries to lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups in this project is generally legally recognized in government-controlled areas, but not necessarily for all groups of population (e.g. migrants, refugees).
