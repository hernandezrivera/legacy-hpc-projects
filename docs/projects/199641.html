Project Summary

Since 2014, the crisis unfolding in Lac Province has been characterized by numerous challenging factors, such as insecurity, flooding, large-scale and continuous population movements (Internally Displaced Persons, returnees) as well as community clashes and socioeconomic-related tensions. Currently, there are more than 400,000 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) within Lac Province, mainly due to the deterioration of security conditions. In addition, as the majority of these displaced persons are mostly women and children, serious concerns have been raised about the risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV) and of violence directed specifically against children such as forced marriage, exploitation, and child labor in the crisis areas. However, while humanitarian conditions in Lac Province are deteriorating, resources to provide assistance are diminishing. Funding is being reallocated to other ongoing global crises such as the war in Ukraine, additionally there is general decreasing trend: 58 per cent of the Humanitarian Response Plan was funded in 2019, 44 per cent was funded in 2020 and 30 per cent received funding in 2021. Thus, optimal use of available humanitarian resources is crucial to reach the most vulnerable groups. To tackle this need for data-informed and targeted responses, IOM will conduct ad-hoc biometric registration, regular site and village assessments and emergency tracking data collection to ensure that assistance to vulnerable, displaced persons is grounded in data.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

The proposed project has for objective to improve humanitarian support for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and migrants in Lac province by strengthening availability of displacement data and trends in Lac province. In order to respond to the need for more reliable information on the displacement situation, IOM, as a key data management stakeholder in the tracking of movement of populations area is planning to continue the implementation of its Mobility Tracking activities in Lac Province. Indeed, DTM data is used as a basis for the elaboration of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) in the country, as demonstrated through the numerous references to DTM figures in the two strategic documents. Chad’s 2021 and 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), as well as the HCT-designed biometric registration strategy, identified urgent needs and gaps for Chad. Consequently, as shown by the results of the last feedback survey on the use of DTM information products, various humanitarian partners often use DTM information for the design of programs and projects as well as operational programming. To provide partners with timely and accurate information for decision-making, IOM will continue the implementation of its mobility tracking in Lac province, including regular site and village assessments to update the overall displacement situation in the province. In addition, IOM will continue the implementation of its Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) and will support the implementation of rapid biometric registration to inform life-saving assistance.
