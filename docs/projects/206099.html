Project Summary

This project is linked to Aidlink’s South Sudan Joint Response (SSJR) 2024-2026 Education project in Akobo in collaboration with Save the Children. The project is also aligned with Education Cluster objectives for 2024 HRP.
In order to achieve the intended goal, the program will implement interventions that will lead to increased access to dignified education for 5000 children (2600 boys and 2400 girls) and youth (aged 3-18) in the target counties. The interventions will be implemented in accordance with Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) and other quality standards as set in INEE minimum standards of Education in Emergencies. Community participatory mechanisms will be utilized to promote school enrolment, retention and acceptable transition of learners to next class/school at the end of the school year.
Aidlink will establish and rehabilitate a total of 13 TLS in Bilkey, Buong, Nyandit, Walgak, Adongo and Akiela primary schools in Akobo and Pochalla. The establishment of the 13 semi-permanent learning spaces shall be based on the approved MoGEI’s establishment designs, specifications and all engineering requirements stipulated in the design. ALO will also construct 16 gender sensitive WASH facilities (8 Akobo and 8 Pochalla) in the selected schools in the target counties. Protective fences will be erected around semi-permanent structures and schools for added safety.
The established infrastructure shall also be disability sensitive, gender friendly and in such a way to withstand adverse weather conditions. PTAs, SMCs, Chiefs, Teachers and MoGEIs officials will be engaged from the onset to ensure accountability and quality assurance.

1. Needs

South Sudan has the highest rate of primary school-aged children out of school in the world, with an estimated 3.3 million children under the age of 18 facing education barriers in 2023, 53% of whom are girls (HNO 2023). Many barriers to accessing quality education in South Sudan exist and include a lack of schools within a reasonable distance from learners, school facilities such as classrooms in poor condition, inadequate WASH facilities and supplies, lack of female teachers (affects girls’ enrolment), lack of assistive devises for Children with Disabilities (CwD) and issues with teacher absenteeism, inadequate teaching and learning materials (HNO 2023). Education quality is further impacted by the insufficient retention of qualified teachers, who often face irregular and low wages. An assessment conducted by Aidlink on August 2023 for selected schools in Walgak, Buong and Meer Primary Schools indicated dire needs for scholastic materials, games and sports materials, psychosocial support and additional learning space (6 classrooms and 5 stance of pit latrines to cater for learners at the ratio of 1:50 learners per classroom and 1:60 for pit latrine).About 64% and 70% of girls across South Sudan were victims of early marriages and teenage pregnancies respectively, in addition to care work, lack of menstrual hygiene materials and fear of SGBV during travel contributing to low education enrolment rates for girls (UASC-FTR Analysis, 2022).
