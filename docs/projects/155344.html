3- To implement and strengthen rapid response activities, with contact tracing, chemoprophylaxis, active case research and reactive vaccination in small geographical areas with outbreaks or persistence of cases (‘hotspots’) in order to reduce transmission and to prevent the expansion of the outbreak

Needs

From December 2014 to November 2018 a total of 764 probable diphtheria cases (including 107 deaths) have been reported across the country. The increase in the number of probable cases of diphtheria has evolved from sporadic cases to a significant outbreak in recent years: 2014 (n=4), 2015 (n=76), 2016 (n=113), and 2017 (n=172) and 2018 (360). Of the total reported cases from 2014 to 2018, 256 were positive (249 confirmed by the lab and 7 by epidemiological links). This suggests that the outbreak will continue to grow in 2019 posing a great risk for becoming a significant public health emergency. Controlling the diphtheria outbreak is a priority for Haiti.The case fatality rate of diphtheria was exceedingly high in Haiti, especially in the beginning of the outbreak. Among the confirmed cases, the case fatality rate was 23% in 2015, 39% in 2016, 8% in 2017 and 9% in 2018; this still remains relatively high for the disease. Diphtheria primarily affects children under the age of 15 (77% of the suspected cases). Of the total cases reported, 85% were from four departments: West (35%), Artibonite (32%), Centre (9%) and the North (9%). The unvaccinated population reported in the last years is high. Haiti has not reached the recommended level of 95% of infants aged less than 1 year, vaccinated with the three Pentavalent vaccine doses: 2014 (60%), 2015 (72%) 2016 (70%), 2017 (72%) and 2018 (75%). During 2018, only 40 communes have carried out reactive vaccination activities, (2,251,581 children were vaccinated) during April and May; and one commune (Gros Morne) vaccinated 57.572 children in November 2018. A non detected diphtheria case poses a high risk for transmission of the disease to family members, in schools and ultimately the community (known as ‘contacts’). Currently, a significant number of cases remain undetected, or are reported late by the epidemiological surveillance system due to personnel in health centers not being able to accurately diagnose a case based on the case definition, and/or do to delay in reporting. Case investigation forms are not always completed and the case is not always sampled. Currently, the field investigation and the contact tracing, are not carried out systematically. There is a lack of human resources and logistics (transport, per diem) to complete the active case search/tracing of contacts. There are also challenges in the samples transportation from institutions to the laboratory, which delays the immediate confirmation. Appropriate case management is important to reduce diphtheria case fatality rate. Standard operating procedures for probable cases, such as referral pathways to reference hospitals equipped with isolation rooms, other necessary supplies and trained staff to treat diphtheria cases have not been fully elaborated. In order to reduce the case fatality rate, health personnel need to be trained on how to detect, diagnose, report and treat diphtheria cases with diphtheria antitoxin (DAT) serum and appropriate antibiotics in the first 48 hrs. At the beginning of 2018, PAHO/WHO procured 2,000 DAT serum doses (approximately for 334 cases); however, the monitoring system is weak to ensure an adequate supply of DAT and antibiotics, which poses the risk of a stock shortage at the institutional level, which could lead to delayed treatment and increase the likelihood of death and severe disease. The estimate DAT need for 2019 is 3000 vials for 400 expected cases.As diphtheria is spread through respiratory droplets, such as from coughing or sneezing, strict infection prevention and control (IPC) measures need to be implemented in reference hospitals in order to prevent nosocomial infections of the disease among health care workers and other patients. This requires personal protective equipment (PPE), vaccination of health personnel working with diphtheria patients, and the maintenance or adaptation of isolation rooms. Only 56% of the 54 institutions evaluated in 2018 have an isolation room.

Activities or outputs

In order to control the epidemic and to reduce the mortality associated with diphtheria, it is important to strengthen all activities associated with the response: epidemiological and laboratory surveillance, infection prevention and control, clinical management, community response (contact tracing, prophylaxis and vaccination of contacts). 1- To strengthen epidemiological surveillance and laboratory capacity to detect and test every probable case of diphtheria Epidemiological surveillance and laboratory testing capacity need to be improved in order to detect and test every probable case of diphtheria in an accurate and timely manner. This requires the training of health personnel to clinically diagnose cases according to the case definition determined by the MSPP, an adequate supply of medical and laboratory supplies to sample and test every case, and a reliable network for the timely transport of samples from health centers to laboratories. The sampling of every case and the improved laboratory capacity to test every case in order to orient public health activities is imperative. Additionally, an information management system needs to be implemented in order for laboratory test results to be integrated with epidemiological data to better orient case management and the response in the field.2- To ensure proper case management of diphtheria cases in order to reduce case fatality rate and to prevent nosocomial infectionsAppropriate case management, through the early administration (within 48 hours) of diphtheria antitoxin (DAT) serum and antibiotics decreases the likelihood of death and severe disease among diphtheria patients. Health professionals in health centers need refresher training in order to clinically diagnose cases and to take appropriate actions. Proper infection prevention and control (IPC) measures in health centers decreases the risk of transmission to health care workers and other patients. This requires the sufficient availability of medical supplies, case management kits, personal protective equipment (PPE) (diphtheria), vaccination of health personnel working with diphtheria patients, and the improvement or construction of isolation rooms (diphtheria).3- To implement and strengthen rapid response activities, with contact tracing, chemoprophylaxis, active case research and reactive vaccination in small geographical areas with outbreaks or persistence of cases (‘hotspots’) in order to reduce transmission and to prevent the expansion of the outbreakContacts (family members, schools, community) of diphtheria cases that are identified during field investigations must be rapidly vaccinated and administered appropriate prophylaxis within 48 hrs. The alert and response capacity should be supported in the national and departmental level. Reactive vaccination activities targeting children aged 1 to 15 years in small areas determined to be at high risk, where deaths or confirmed cases were reported. Also, health care workers in contact with diphtheria patients should be immediately vaccinated. The teams responsible for all aspects of the response (vaccination, contact tracing, investigation, chemoprophylaxis) will be composed of MSPP staff, selected PAHO/WHO and partner personnel.
