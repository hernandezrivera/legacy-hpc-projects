Project Summary

Through this project, the Danish Refugee Council (DRC) aims to contribute to operationalize the 'Government of Bangladesh-United Nations Framework on Skills Development for Rohingya refugees/FDMNs and Host Communities', endorsed by the Government in August 2022. DRC will build the skills and capacities of Rohingya refugees/FDMNs commensurate with livelihood opportunities available in Rakhine State to support voluntary and sustainable repatriation in Myanmar when conditions are conducive. DRC aims to uphold LSD sector objective 1, 2 and 3. Through these initiatives, DRC aims to increase socio-economic access of both Rohingya and host community young adults and middle- aged adult by placing a strong emphasis on creating livelihood opportunities through skill training as a key driving force.

Given DRC's organizational focus on protection, the LLH programming will strictly adhere to all protection policies and compliances under the guidance and coordination of the protection sector. The practices of general protection, protection monitoring, case management support will be sought from the DRC’s internal protection sector and the capacity of the LLH staff will be developed by DRC's protection sector. DRC has further collaborated with the PSEA Network to provide training for camp volunteers during inductions, and a designated PSEA focal point within DRC will address reports related to DRC staff throughout the project lifecycle, including needs assessment, risk analysis, project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluations.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In the arena of livelihood and skill development, the incorporation of measures to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) holds paramount significance, particularly within the confines of refugee camps and host communities, where displaced populations are especially susceptible. Prioritizing the gaps and address the gaps with implementation of comprehensive PSEA strategies is pivotal for organizations to safeguard the dignity and well-being of beneficiaries during crises. This entails the establishment of explicit policies, the cultivation of a culture of accountability, and a resolute stance of zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse. Foundational to this approach are training and capacity-building initiatives, equipping both staff and community members with the knowledge and skills needed to identify and respond to instances of sexual exploitation and abuse. Outreach programs within refugee camps and host communities play a crucial role in disseminating awareness about PSEA, fostering community engagement, and promoting the reporting of incidents without fear of reprisal. Efforts to mainstream PSEA will not only safeguard the immediate well-being of vulnerable populations but also will contribute to the overall resilience and stability of communities affected by displacement. The holistic approach of the initiative reflects a commitment to the protection principle, ensuring that humanitarian action not only provides immediate relief but also contributes to the long-term well-being and resilience of the communities involved.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the repairing and maintenance of solar panel is green energy related activity that contribute in mitigating of climate change. Furthermore, skills development training on recycling garment Jhoot waste for producing households’ usable items is under waste management activity that substantially mitigates environmental pollution. However, the proposed garment jhoot recycling interventions will also address and enhance technical skills of business entrepreneurs to recycle and reuse garment industry leftovers turning to households useable bedding and domestic usable materials and minimized environmental hazardous impact of garment manufacturers in Bangladesh.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

DRC has a distinct Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan. In this plan livelihoods and skill development plan are also well defined with business continuity plan as well as contingency plan. As practice, DRC regularly updates it on a quarterly basis.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

DRC’s target population for livelihoods programming are only adult age between 18-59 years ( mostly 18-40) but some priority would be given to the 18-24 years of age and . The targeted youth (women) will be capacitated on advanced garments jhoot recycling training and entrepreneurship/business both in host and camps and the youth men will be on gas stove repairing and management, plastic shredding and business. Necessary market linkage developed skill, and knowledge will also be delivered to them along with business plan development on Micro and small enterprises. The youth will also be engaged in plastic waste and garments trading with other private sectors and agencies as per the gain skill from DRC. A good percentage of youth (18-24 years) 30 percent in Rohingya camps and 25 percent in host community will be also trained on the above interventions support with other awareness.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project delivery and the consequences of the activities will support to build the targets self-reliance by skill transfer and income generation of the gained skill. However, by applying the gained skill and knowledge the Rohingya targeted group (youth, adult men and women) will also improve their resilience on climate adaptive entrepreneurship and business skill development approaches thus will contribute to enhance to meet their sustainable livelihoods.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, DRC has already been engaging and following the Upazila Development Plan, Ukhiya 2023, in all its programming in the host community thus also contributing to achieve the District Development Plan by targeting the youth and women skill development for reduce unemplyment, enhance financial inclusion programming-access to credit, savings and insurances along with private sector enhagement and increase social cohesion. Under this project, DRC will closely work in the identified gaps areas like women’s entrepreneurship development and business skill development. This skill will be developed on garments, jhoot recycling and its products, plastic waste recycling/management and its product promotion along with CBO’s business skill and marketing linkages development.
