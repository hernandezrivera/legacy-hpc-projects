Project Summary

Terre des hommes (Tdh) is the leading Swiss child relief organization, and has been operating in Juba PoCs since 2014 and in Yei River State since 2016, expanding its services to rural Yei (Mugwo and Otogo counties) in 2019.
As a protection driven agency, It works to improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable children and their communities in an effective and sustainable manner, by providing direct child protection services; as well as by strengthening the resilience of communities through a holistic and child-centered protection approach to intersectoral needs in education and WASH.
The WASH program implemented by Tdh will stand within comprehensive and integrated approaches aimed at covering the basic WASH needs of the most vulnerable populations in Tdh’s areas of operation. This will be achieved by increasing sustainable use of protection and gender sensitive WASH services such as water supply rehabilitation and construction of latrines, alongside comprehensive hygiene promotion activities inclusive of menstrual hygiene management. The project will provide WASH assistance at community level as well as connect to its Education programming in schools, reaching out to communities around them particularly where communities access water through boreholes located within unfenced school grounds to ensure support and proper use. Focus on rehabilitation will be coupled with set up of water management committees, training and provision of spare parts. Tdh aims to enable the beneficiaries to have improved access to safe drinking water particularly where unprotected water sources prevail in areas who have had limited access to services such as Mugwo, Otogo and other rural points in Yei River State. Tdh staff will focus on enhancement of community knowledge on WASH practices, to reduce prevalence of water borne diseases and where feasible integrate basic Infection Prevention Control that can support the ongoing EVD preparedness and response by enhanced community capacity.
In view of responding to ongoing clashes and displacements that continue to take place in locations outside Yei town, Tdh is on standby to provide distribution of emergency hygiene kits, dignity kits and support to displaced communities. Tdh will continue to support provision of hygiene promotion and replenishment of supplies as necessary to existing populations displaced to Yei town, primarily in the areas of Atende, Wuluturu, and Marakule. Yei storage facilities of Tdh are sufficient to support necessary stockpiling of supplies as per contextual needs.
Given its longstanding presence in Yei River State, Tdh benefits from favorable acceptance from the communities and has established strong relationships with local authorities, officials and state ministries, including the Ministry of Infrastructure, the Ministry of Cooperatives, Rural Water and Sanitation, the Ministry of General Education and Instruction and Ministry of Child, Gender and Social Welfare. Tdh also collaborates closely with other international and local actors in the targeted areas, state level coordination mechanisms and working groups and is a key actor particularly in joint assessment and response in view of ensuring complementarity in intervention. In Mugwo and Otogo, Tdh is one of the only operational actors delivering CPiE, education and plans to extend WASH services to hard-to-reach highly vulnerable beneficiaries throughout 2020 and access permitting to broaden its scope of outreach work.

1 - Needs

The breakout of violence in South Sudan since 2013 between the government forces and opposition non-state armed groups spread to the Equatorial states, including Yei River State, in 2016. Human rights violations have been widely reported and high numbers of civilians have been forced to flee their houses and villages as a result of armed conflict. The crisis led to massive displacements, disrupted livelihoods and increased food insecurity. Consolidated IDP registration has been a challenge, and was further emphasized in the response to a small-scale displacement of 1,500 individuals at the end of September 2019, moving into town following clashes in the area of Logo. The number of IDP settled in IDP sites around Yei (Atende, Wuluturu and Marakule) is currently estimated at 9,000 individuals, to which Tdh has been providing ongoing support. Coordinated response in Yei requires concrete support as the partner presence should be sufficient to deal with the existing needs and response to sudden shocks and influxes which can be expected until the mobilization of non-signatory groups armed groups and tensions persist. While the rural areas of outside Yei were inaccessible for a long stretch of time, a window of opportunity between April and September saw increased movements, particularly of partners with strong operational capacity to extend their outreach. While the assessed locations through individual and joined assessments were accomplished, follow up response has been extremely limited, populations not accessing services for protracted periods of time around Wudabi, Tore, Rasolo, and Morobo clearly displayed the need to restored access to basic WASH services and clear cut coordination of static partners in Yei is essential to follow up on the latter. Tdh would seek to step up its WASH capacity to ensure that response capacities as an INGO are extended and possible to additional locations throughout 2020. A joined needs assessment was conducted in Mugwo and Otogo counties in May 2019. The assessment underlined very high needs, in access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene. In both locations, only half of the boreholes visited were functional, and most of the time providing bushy/coloured water. IDPs in Wadupale (Mugwo) were solely drinking from streams as no functional hand pump was provided. Furthermore, the average ratio of latrine per person was very low in both counties, leading to open defecation and contamination of water sources. Only one latrine per 150 individuals was available in Otogo county. PAH, deployed an emergency team to Yei town carrying out regular outreach missions. As Tdh moved primarily for multisectoral responses, it confirmed findings particularly in relation to SGBV concerns arising when latrines were made available to the population, including in and around schools and other community centers. This was primarily in connection to numbers being inappropriate for daily use. Most latrines did not close and were not segregated for men and women. The needs assessment also noted limited knowledge on hygiene best practices. In Otogo county, only a quarter of the population surveyed demonstrated basic knowledge on handwashing before and after eating and/or going to the latrines. No awareness raising activities were ongoing in both counties on hygiene promotion. Focusing on schools and temporary learning structures as the main entry point to provide safe and equitable access to WASH services to children and the communities, Tdh will coordinate with PAH to respond to needs in Mugwo and Otogo counties; and will continue to assess and respond to the emergency needs of recently arrived IDPs inside Yei town. Over 2019/20, Tdh will also improve equitable and inclusive access to safe sanitation facilities for boys and girls. Implementing a pilot Gender Marker and connected Gender Analysis tool, the WASH team will be one of the spearheading teams to implement the tool.

2 - Activities

Tdh currently operates in Yei town and two rural Counties of Otogo and Mugwo. Its 2020 strategy focuses on extending its WASH intervention to build upon extension of its sister prorammes. Therefore, it seeks to operated within a fully integrated approach to ensure equitable access to clean and safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDP and host communities including children. The current focus will continue to service communities in the proximity of 10 schools inside Yei town and 6 payams outside Yei town where Tdh already operates with a potential eye to extending support and ensuring, through the use of its storage facilities the capacity of stand by response. Activities fall within a broader WASH program supported by the Swiss Development Consortium which Tdh seeks to integrate additional funding to extend the remit further. The activities under the proposed program aim to address the humanitarian needs of those affected by the crisis in a flexible manner, with a strong focus on assistance to address gaps in the provision of humanitarian assistance to newly accessible areas and benefciaries not currently receiving any assistance. Through an integrated approach, Tdh aims to provide multi-sectoral lifesaving assistance to the crisis-affected populations to ensure safe, digni􀂡ed, and sustainable livelihoods. Tdh will implement activities responding to the specific needs identified in its current areas of operation, as well as new locations identified for expansion. Activities will address the critical points of increasing access to basic WASH services, linking with child protection and food security outcomes where possible. Activities will focus on: - Maintaining/Rehabilitating/Constructing Water supply infrastructures (boreholes & rainwater harvesting systems) – 16 . Approximately 8,000 individuals can have access to safe and clean water. - Conducting Water quality tests throughout the project period by Tdh team. - Forming/Reactivating/Supporting Water Management Committee is for intervention locations consisting of a targeted 144 WMC members (101 M; 43 F) will receive O&M training. - 60 Handpump mechanics (45 M; 15 F) are provided with basic maintenance training and/or refreshers training. - 10 boreholes are repaired with provision of spare parts and handpump tool kits for Boma administration for the quality O&M by the handpump mechanics. - Sanitation facilities are rehabilitated and/or newly constructed with consideration of a gender-sensitive approach and inclusion of individuals with special needs.- 15 group handwashing facilities are installed/rehabilitated to encourage group handwashing and cleaning materials are provided.- Emergency latrines constructed at IDP-influx areas with soap and cleaning materials provided. - Participatory dissemination on key hygiene/sanitation messages e.g. hand/personal hygiene, solid water management and menstrual hygiene management (MHM) is through the Blue School methodology and material to communities and the children within up to 15,000 individuals reached. - Provision of awareness raising on MHM to 1,040 adolescent girls aged 13-18, including training on how to create reusable sanitary pads. - Provision of dignity kits for 400 adolescent girls aged 13-18; and hygiene kits to 400 adolescent boys aged 13-18. Appointment or reactivation of community hygiene promoter network and provision of training to voluntary networks established. - Capacity for stand by Distribution of emergency hygiene kits for up to 1,200 children (660 M; 540 F) outside Yei town- Distribution of emergency HH hygiene kits, soap, cleaning materials/protective gears for HH in the communities outside Yei town. Tdh will ensure that WASH-related gender-based violence (GBV) is mitigated by conducting a GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, conducted by a female staff, before defining the location of the safe water, sanitation and hygiene facilities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Tdh does not provide legal recognition as an actor and operating upon a basis of need and vulnerability criteria will work for service provision to all identified within its capacity.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Focus group discussions are vital for the implementation of protection sensitive activities, these are therefore carried out across various age, gender and vulnerability groups, to ensure that displacement affected communities, whether host or IDP can access services which they have contributed to develop. Development and improvement occurs through regular and ongoing consultation. Tdh has extended its consultation group by extending to unserviced rural locations and thus integrates broader perspectives into its implementing strategy.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Tdh liaises with numerous authorities, community leaders, religious leaders, community elders, CBOs and more. These are engaged to collaborate with Tdh primarily through the establishment of Community Based Child Protection Networks and brought into other programming according to the integrated sector operated within. Awareness raising hygiene promotion activities and where necessary training the latter allows for high impact thanks to their access to the community as they are key multipliers. Water Management Committees and pump mechanics will be trained within the communities, these may be men or women alike in line with traditional water-seeking practice. Links with the 1) Ministry of Physical Infrastructures and Bridges (2) Ministry of Cooperatives, (3) Ministry of General education and Instruction (4) Ministry Gender, Child and Social welfare in Yei are long standing and will be maintained along with the strong network with the Governor’s office and the various county commissioners and chiefs supports to ensure that upon handover of the repaired, rehabilitated or constructed structures is done, efficient use is conducted and monitored with broad community ownership thus catering for a long term vision that falls within the durable solutions framework.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project focuses on WASH and thus looks to Water management and use-related frameworks. Furthermore, Blue Schools efforts and all activities carried out by TdH inside schools will aim to support the roll out of the National Education Curriculum and capacity building will always be done further to bilateral consultation with state authorities, in particular the MoGCSW, the MoEI and the MoWI, to ensure alignment with their specific objectives. Finally, the project is also aligned with the National Refugee Return Framework (aware of its redrafting) in view of its recognition of the possibility of supporting returnees as a beneficiary group due to non-discrimination on the community of origin of children. Vulnerability criteria are applied across host and hosted alike, refugees, returnees would be catered to accordingly including in distribution plans.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

While there is a constant growth in cautious optimism in the country, Tdh is well aware of the sheer volatility and proximity of conflict operating in and around Yei. The state is a peculiar and yet literal representation of the humanitarian development nexus. While there is increased stability and access in certain locations, the population remains vigilant due to ongoing clashes outside the town between non-signatories and government forces. Tdh is of the opinion that the programming it rolls out needs to straddle the reality, pave the way for future implementation and yet maintain its capacity to respond to shocks be they brief or small, or protracted and of a larger scale. The outlook changed drastically throughout 2019, particularly around Yei whereas of April, increased movement was a possibility - this has allowed for extended remit of planning and a notably palpable sense of stability in certain locations. The patchwork of the area outside Yei ton nevertheless proves to be tedious and open to stark and sudden changes, or slow onset of renewed conflict should all collapse. Tdh has sufficient reason to believe that the Protection environment has the potential to be safer and more stable. Tdh has launched operations in Mugwo and Otogo further to extensive mapping and assessment actions and seeks to continue to extend its reach to the rural communities detached from services in the locations not prone to clashes between government and non-signatory armed groups.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, Tdh’s sustainability strategy around WASH activities will be centered around: - Advocacy initiatives at national and local levels to ensure the proper O&M of the facilities and the sustainability of the programs, through a structured handover and training around facilities to the relevant institutions. Tdh will organise information sessions / 1-day presentation and participatory workshop in Yei and Juba to present its intervention to the Ministry of General Education and Instruction, the Ministry for Water and Irrigation, the Ministry for Physical Infrastructure and the Ministry for Gender and Child Welfare. - Institutionalisation: institutional actors will be closely associated to the implementation of the program (all Ministries cited in Stakeholders section) and will be the official recipients of the handover of the water and sanitation facilities. - Capacity building of key stakeholders: strengthening the capacities of key stakeholders such as local administrations, key CPCN individuals (identified through integrated programs of Tdh), elders, community members is at the core of the proposed program. In-depth and regular trainings, made of theoretical and practical exercises, will enable the targeted beneficiaries’ autonomy and resilience in the management of water supplies and infrastructures together with the promotion of health and hygiene practices.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Few specific risks have been selected since Tdh attempts to plan its intervention aiming to minimis e the multitude of risks above through varied consultation with multiple actors. Nevertheless, particularly in the context of Yei matters related to high incidence of GBV, child safeguarding and its impact on the communities in and outside town is extensive (as can be corroborated by a recent PSEA task force risk assessment exercise). Due to a limited presence of actors in town, limited access to services and possibility of redress, prevailing cultural norms and social and anthropological factors the impact of GBV and child safeguarding risks is high for both the population of concern, beneficiaries and national staff of Tdh (and other organisations). This has led the organisation to prioritise Gender and Diversity mainstreamining components across the whole country program thanks to direct support from Headquarters. By putting explicit focus on the matter, Tdh has shifted to dedicating clearly defined time for programme staff to undergo and roll out training around PSEA and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms internally and externally. This has supported in the hand-in-hand roll out of the complaints and feedback mechanisms available to beneficiaries and staff alike and will continue to be an area of regular refresh to counter the turnover of staff and beneficiaries.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members through: - Surveys and FGD with specific age groups, women, men, students, youth out of school and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis. Quarterly focus group discussions will be conducted with children and communities to assess child protection knowledge, attitudes and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with participatory assessment. This incorporates a broad spectrum of perspectives and allows for ongoing dialogue with community members.- Tdh will maintain and raise awareness on its Complaints, Feedback, and Response Mechanism. This mechanism will follow Tdh guidelines, ensuring multiple methods for submitting complaints and receiving responses, including FGDs, ability and possibility to request individual sessions, CFRM boxes in schools and safe spaces, as well as through static helpdesks. Integrated programming also ensures that the communities serviced have access to different members of Tdh teams, including protection staff. Where applicable, this CFRM will be adapted to fit with existing Community-Based Feedback Mechanisms already used in the location.

9.15 - If you have not selected any of the items 9.1 - 9.13, please explain why here.

Comprehensive case management services provided by Tdh is what is referred to for individual needs definition.In regard to 9.4, assistance preference is currently built into the evaluation of the existing assistance methods rather than embedded into our current new programming modalities. This will be considered for future use.
