Project Summary

In 2025, BRAC’s Education sector aims to support 25,700 children aged 3 to 17, extending various educational services to Rohingya and host communities across 12 camps and 8 unions, respectively. This program targets 51% female learners and ensures the inclusion of 3% children with disabilities. Of the total beneficiaries, 17,120 Rohingya children and 8,580 host community children will participate in the initiative.
In alignment with the first objective of the Education Sector, BRAC plans to construct 35 two-story Learning Centres (LCs) and to reconstruct and renovate 143 existing facilities, which will significantly improve Rohingya children’s access to education. Through these efforts, a total of 11,600 children will benefit from enhanced educational opportunities. Recognizing the needs of adolescent girls, the program will prioritize the construction of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) blocks and gender-segregated toilets to promote a comfortable, inclusive environment in 15 LCs supporting 600 adolescent girls. Meanwhile, BRAC will operate 160 schools in the host community, supporting 4,800 learners by providing pre-primary education to prepare them to be enrolled in primary schools for formal primary education. Moreover, the 3,180 pre-primary completed learners will be supported through home-based remedial support to cope in primary school. Besides, 600 host community adolescents aged 12-17 will be supported through mentorship programs for their leadership quality, and awareness of different social issues will be increased. To ensure a safe and supportive learning environment, BRAC plans to improve sanitation across these facilities by repairing 140 gender-segregated toilets and adding 82 MHM blocks, which will help encourage higher attendance among adolescent girls. In collaboration with seven partner organizations, BRAC will further facilitate learning opportunities for an additional 4,920 students in 187 facilities within the Rohingya camps. Besides these, BRAC will distribute essential educational materials to 25,700 Rohingya and host communities students. These resources will include pens, exercise books, and specialized science materials, such as geometry kits, globes, and skeleton models, to promote a comprehensive and interactive learning experience.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the ISNA 2024, several facts and phenomena are observed related to the Education of the Rohingya people. 20% of the children are found to be completely disconnected from educational activities. Of these, 28% think, their age is inappropriate for getting an education (either too low or too high). The enrollment rate of boys (62%) is higher than that of girls (57%). For girls, specifically, some challenges include the absence of girls-only shifts, fear of eve-teasing, fear of abduction, absence of WASH facilities, and MHM blocks. Recently, boys have also been facing the fear of armed groups and forced recruitment into these groups. This results in a deteriorating trend in the enrollment of adolescent children starting from age 12. The number of boys and girls not enrolled increased significantly from age 12 onwards, with girls at age 18 having the highest percentage of out-of-school (49%). Some further challenges include the availability of income-generating opportunities, which especially encourage boys to engage instead in educational activities, the increased rate of private education, and the fact that this informal education has no future impact. On the other hand, children with disabilities have additional barriers to accessibility (camp, road, and geographical terrain) and a lack of inclusive teaching-learning materials. They are less likely to attend learning facilities. The lack of qualified and trained teachers from the Rohingya community is also one of the significant challenges now.Equitable and sufficient learning facilities are also absent in the Rohingya camps, which is also a hindrance to the children engaging in educational activities. However, extending the educational facilities to new spaces is hardly possible due to the lack of space availability. Accommodating the ever-increasing number of children in learning facilities without vertical expansion won't be possible. Besides, the construction materials of the learning facilities make them more vulnerable during any disaster. These structures need to be reinforced to ensure the safety of the attending learners.On the other hand, the specific need to support pre-schooling aged and adolescent learners in the host community is significant. According to the Bangladesh Annual Primary School Census, there are no sufficient pre-primary schools that can help children aged 4-5 in their pre-schooling development and their smooth transition to primary school. Besides, the dropout rate of adolescent children, especially girls, is high due to age and gender-based violence, disrupted educational prospects, widespread psychosocial distress and worry, and limited health service uptake, which results in a lower retention rate in adolescent age girls. Regarding quality of teaching, lack of proper training, and availability of higher paid jobs in other sectors play as demotivating factors for the teachers. Additionally, increased awareness efforts are essential to encourage consistent use of these facilities among families and the wider community.Engaging community members in both Rohingya and host areas is crucial for addressing key educational challenges, poor school attendance, the quality of education, girls' enrollment, and child protection. To effectively tackle these issues, it is essential to implement capacity-building initiatives that empower community leaders, parents, and local stakeholders with the knowledge and skills needed to support educational programs. Regular meetings and forums should be established to foster open dialogue and collaboration among community members, enabling them to identify barriers to education and develop targeted solutions together. These initiatives can also enhance awareness about the importance of education for all children, particularly girls, and promote protective measures that safeguard children from violence and exploitation.

BRAC promotes diversity and inclusiveness in its projects and workplace environments by engaging various stakeholders. 2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

BRAC recognizes the critical importance of involving affected communities throughout the entire project. From the design phase to final evaluations, the participation and inclusion of the affected populations will be prioritized to ensure that their voices are heard and their needs are addressed. Community-level meetings will be organized to facilitate this engagement to share relevant and actionable information with community members, fostering transparency and collaboration.Inclusive community groups will be established, comprising individuals of diverse backgrounds regardless of gender, age, disability status, and other factors. These groups will play a crucial role in identifying education-related needs, discussing vulnerabilities, and proposing solutions that reflect the unique challenges faced by their communities. By empowering these groups, BRAC aims to enhance ownership and accountability in project implementation.Strong community feedback systems will be put in place to ensure continuous feedback and improve service delivery. These systems will allow affected individuals to assess and comment on the performance of services, including sensitive issues such as sexual exploitation and abuse, fraud, and corruption. Regular consultation meetings will be organized to facilitate open dialogues, enabling community members to voice their concerns and suggestions.The monitoring team will conduct periodic evaluations to effectively capture community feedback. These evaluations will provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of programs and the experiences of community members, ensuring that any necessary adjustments can be made promptly. BRAC's project team is committed to responding constructively to community feedback, using it as a tool for continuous improvement. By fostering an inclusive and responsive environment, BRAC aims to enhance project outcomes and ensure that the needs of the affected populations are met with dignity and respect.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC maintains a safeguarding policy and 5 subset policies including PSHEA policy, Child and adolescent protection policy, Whistle blowing policy, Prevention of Workplace Bullying and Violence Policy and Adult with special needs policy aimed at preventing PSEA and child abuse across stakeholder interactions. These policies emphasize organization’s commitment to creating a safe and supportive environment for everyone involved, staff, volunteers, contractors, programme participants, vendors, suppliers and service providers receive Safeguarding and PSEA training to equip with necessary knowledge and skills to recognise and address safeguarding concerns effectively. Additionally, BRAC’s Child Protection and Protection sector collaborates closely with educational programmes to mainstream safeguarding practices throughout educational initiatives, ensuring that educational environments priorities safety and well-being of children. BRAC encourages programme participants and volunteers to provide feedback through various accessible channels, that essential for preventing and mitigating adverse situations. BRAC offers three options to facilitate reporting, including a whistle-blowing mechanism that ensures confidentiality and protects. Furthermore, organisation promotes awareness by displaying different communication materials that provide detailed information about designated focal points and Community CFRM box at every facility. All measures ensure that staff, volunteers, and community members can easily report incidents or concerns.Building on its 2024 successes, BRAC will continue to priorities safeguarding and PSEA in 2025. This include ongoing staff training, programme participant’s awareness sessions, and standing agenda of PSEA and Safeguarding in staff meetings. A strong response mechanism will be in place to support survivors, and CFRM will be maintained in every school. Additionally, SEA risk assessments and dissemination of awareness materials will be ongoing.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Considering the disaster risk assessment conducted in the camps, this program equips volunteers, teachers, and community members with comprehensive Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) training. This initiative aims to empower these individuals to act as early responders during a disaster, enhancing community resilience and preparedness. By building local capacity, BRAC seeks to ensure that communities are better equipped to manage emergencies, respond effectively to crises, and safeguard the well-being of all residents.The Environmental and Social Safeguarding Framework (ESSF) delineates the scope and implementation modalities for identifying and mitigating potential environmental and social risks associated with its projects. This framework emphasizes the importance of safeguard requirements, ensuring that all activities align with BRAC's commitment to environmental sustainability. Integral to this commitment is BRAC’s environmental policy, which embodies the “Do Good” criteria and the “Do No Harm” principles.BRAC is dedicated to avoiding, minimizing, or mitigating adverse environmental, social, or economic impacts from its initiatives. According to the ESSF, BRAC will not support any activities that could lead to the significant conversion or degradation of critical natural habitats, ensuring that environmental sustainability remains a priority. Moreover, BRAC is committed to implementing practices that protect biodiversity and ecosystem services, recognizing their vital role in sustaining the communities it serves.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

BRAC has an individual technical group specifically designed to assess emergency preparedness and develop effective response strategies for mitigating the impact of disasters within the camps. In adherence to the education sector standards, BRAC has implemented comprehensive emergency preparedness and response plans alongside standardized operating procedures for the learning facilities. Additionally, BRAC has facilitated emergency preparedness and response training sessions for teachers and facilitators while formulating a strong contingency plan and response mechanism system aimed at providing crucial support to vulnerable groups in times of crisis. In anticipation of emergencies, dedicated funds have been allocated to ensure swift and effective responses when the need arises, guaranteeing prompt access to crucial resources. Moreover, BRAC has made a steadfast commitment to providing ongoing support to affected communities in the aftermath of any situation that may arise in the relevant areas.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

In 2025, BRAC's education initiative will support youth aged 15-17 in both Rohingya and host communities through targeted programs emphasising leadership, empowerment, and inclusivity. The project will engage 600 adolescents in mentorship programs designed to develop leadership skills and raise awareness about key social issues, empowering them to address challenges in their communities. To ensure a supportive and inclusive learning environment, BRAC will prioritise the construction of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) blocks and repair 140 gender-segregated toilets, benefiting adolescent girls and fostering a safe space for education. These efforts will be particularly focused on 15 Learning Centres (LCs) that will support 600 adolescent girls. Additionally, the initiative will distribute essential educational materials, such as notebooks, pens, and science equipment, to enhance the learning experience for all students. Community engagement activities will involve parents, leaders, and school management committees to promote gender equality, encourage adolescent education, and address socio-cultural barriers.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by providing educational opportunities that empower learners for sustainable livelihoods. The curriculum includes practical skills like communication and problem-solving, enabling refugees to reduce aid dependency and pursue income-generating activities. The project modality emphasises community participation, and self-reliance. Activities like preparing DRR posters and training on disaster preparedness equip refugees to address challenges, enhancing their resilience to natural and man-made disasters. Rohingya teachers are supported through training in teaching methods and classroom management, improving their skills and fostering inclusive learning environments. For example, they adopt interactive techniques like storytelling and positive discipline strategies. These initiatives also promote resilience by challenging social norms, encouraging female participation in education, and empowering the community.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, BRAC’s project activities are closely aligned with the development priorities outlined in the District Development and Growth Priorities of Cox’s Bazar. BRAC’s pre-primary program prepares young children (ages 4-5) for enrollment in government primary schools, enhancing the district’s educational coverage and supporting formal education goals. This activity aligns with district plans to expand access to quality education and strengthen educational outcomes. The mentorship program promotes skills like leadership and social awareness, which contribute to youth empowerment and contribute to social cohesion improvement and gender equity. These activities are evident in BRAC’s focus on ECE, adolescent mentorship, and educational infrastructure, which directly contribute to host learners' retention and improvement of girls' attendance. They also impact and contribute to the overall national education system in Cox’s Bazar district. Prepare for a better future community.
