Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) strategically enhances dignified, equitable, and durable WASH services in Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. This project will ensure the maintenance and renovation of WASH facilities at Camps- 17, 20, 25, and 27, to sustain safe water, sanitation, and waste management systems. It will emphasize household water quality, fecal sludge management, and health hygiene which are also essential for community health and well-being. SC will promote tap stand water as a safe drinking source and encourage the use of safe water points. SC will identify all unsafe water sources, such as private line water, shallow tube wells, etc., by testing the water quality and disseminating messages well ahead of alternative sources before decommissioning all unsafe water sources, as there is a correlation between unsafe water and diarrhea. SC is committed to applying back better policy for building disaster-resilient infrastructure and supporting self-reliance in Rohingya communities, with a focus on sustainability and preparedness for future challenges.
SC recognizes the diverse needs of community members and plans to establish gender-specific WASH spaces prioritizing safety and privacy, especially for women and girls. This approach will enhance the effectiveness of WASH services by addressing unique hygiene requirements and ensuring inclusivity and equality.
To maintain high service standards, SC sets quality benchmarks and will conduct bi-weekly functional monitoring to track the quality of activities. A complaint feedback system will be followed to track issues and ensure timely resolutions, focusing on the needs of vulnerable community members. Regular assessments will allow SC to respond to emerging challenges and maintain service quality.
SC will implement robust solid waste and fecal sludge management services, including green treatment plants that use innovative, chemical-free methods. Treated waste will be repurposed for vegetable cultivation, linking health, nutrition, and local livelihoods.
In line with its commitment to climate resilience, SC will prioritize renewable solar power for WASH facilities, reducing reliance on fossil fuels and aiming for zero waste and carbon emissions. This approach will enhance the sustainability of WASH services and exemplify eco-friendly practices in resource-limited settings.
SC employs a cost-efficient, participatory model to encourage community ownership of WASH services. SC fosters a sustainable framework to enhance resilience and self-reliance by involving residents in planning and implementation. A local maintenance committee will be formed to engage community members in maintaining hand pumps and conducting water quality tests.
Hygiene promotion remains critical, especially for children and adolescent girls. SC will provide menstrual hygiene management training, empowering participants to create reusable sanitary pads. SC will use storytelling and role-play to make learning enjoyable and effective for children.
Cleaning campaigns led by WASH committees and community volunteers will encourage active participation, involving children, adolescent girls, and local youth to inspire responsibility for clean environments. SC will follow a comprehensive approach, which includes contingency planning for emergencies and natural disasters.
SC will closely coordinate with the WASH sector, camp management, and DPHE to ensure effective implementation. SC is committed to accountability, ensuring the voices of community members especially the most vulnerable are integrated into program design. SC aims to provide dignified and inclusive access to safe water and sanitation services for Rohingya refugees and host communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Access to clean water, sanitation, and hygiene is a fundamental human right, crucial for preventing diseases such as cholera and dysentery. Overcrowding and poor living conditions, particularly in refugee camps, exacerbate health risks, especially for children, who make up half of the Rohingya population. Effective waste management is key to preventing disease outbreaks, but challenges remain in waste collection and sanitation access. For instance, while 84% of households use a two-bin system for waste segregation, 36% of households in Camp 27 report a lack of waste collection services, and 23% lack two bins for segregation at the household level (ISNA, 2024).Access to sanitation facilities is particularly difficult for persons with disabilities, and 69% of beneficiaries report that their WASH needs are being met (MSNA 2023, SCI). Safety concerns are also prevalent, with 58% of respondents citing inadequate lighting as a primary reason for women and girls feeling unsafe, and 32% of women reporting feeling unsafe using communal latrines at night (ISNA 2024). This insecurity can lead to risky behaviors, such as using bathing areas for defecation and urination. Furthermore, funding shortages have impacted critical WASH interventions, especially those addressing menstrual hygiene management, which increases health risks for women and girls.Water access remains a pressing issue while 68% of beneficiaries use tap stands for drinking water, considered safer than hand pumps, 26% rely on hand pumps, particularly in Ukhia. In Teknaf, 94% of households use piped treated surface water, but challenges in water availability persist. Despite an overall satisfaction rate of 85% regarding water access, beneficiaries receive an average of only 23 liters per person per day, with Camp 27 receiving even less—just 16 liters per person per day, far below the emergency standard of 20 liters per person per day. Teknaf also faces recurring water crises every year, requiring additional boreholes for sustainable groundwater access.Hygiene management is complicated by high supply costs and inadequate hygiene practices. SC serves over 18,210 individuals across four camps and host communities, working to improve access to WASH facilities. However, infrastructure damage during the rainy season, along with recurring water shortages in camps 27 and 25, underscores the need for emergency interventions. WASH facilities are often not accessible for persons with disabilities, with 79.21% of them facing difficulties accessing assistance. Older individuals also experience challenges with accessibility and privacy in WASH facilities. Additionally, while 9% of households own private latrines, many are poorly managed, overflowing, and pose a significant hygiene risk (ISNA, 2024).Inequities in WASH access disproportionately affect women, girls, older individuals, and people with disabilities. Communal facilities often lack lighting, gender-segregated toilets, and safety measures, leaving 38% of women feeling unsafe at night (JMSNA-2023). Despite 83% of women and girls receiving hygiene kits on time, many still lack private spaces to dry menstrual clothes, increasing the risk of infections due to taboos around menstrual hygiene (ISNA-2024). Access for people with disabilities remains limited, with only 50% having adequate sanitation facilities, and older adults face challenges accessing distant WASH points that do not meet their incontinence needs (ISNA-2024).Maintaining existing WASH facilities is critical, as much of it was built in 2017 and 2018. Ongoing hygiene promotion and behavior change initiatives are essential to improving health practices. SC focuses on community engagement in site selection and intervention implementation to ensure the sustainability of projects. The needs analysis conducted by SC also incorporates gender, age, and disability considerations to ensure that projects are inclusive and responsive to the needs of the most vulnerable voice.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

WASH in communities often overlooks gender-related needs, resulting in poor basic services and increased risks for women and girls. SC believes it is important to address the needs of girls, women, children, the disabled, and other vulnerable groups in preventing WASH, Dengue, and other infections in healthcare settings. Save the Children requires all staff and volunteers involved in the project to take pieces of training on safeguarding and discuss preventive measures. If cases arise, they are reported in accordance with the guidelines and privately addressed by the person in charge. To this end, safeguards will be integrated into all phases of the project to ensure that the needs, risks, and threats of women, girls, and other vulnerable groups are considered and addressed. All ‘Save the Children Staff and Visitors receive mandatory ‘Safety and Security’ orientation training by the Safety and Security Manager on the first day of their office. In the Cox’s Bazar Area Office, the security manager develops the context-specific security plan and SOP. The Security Manager is responsible for the safety of the field staff. S/he coordinates and collects security information from ISCG, government agencies, military, Law Enforcing Authority (LEA), and other organizations, monitors and analyzes the security situation, and then shares the synthesized information with the management and disseminates information and advisory to the field staff on ‘need to know’ basis. SC will establish a useful feedback and complaints mechanism for all users including children and adults, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries, to collect feedback and modify the program as needed. SC WASH strongly links with safeguarding and protection staff to address any protection issues. Following the updated risk assessment tools, the safeguarding and gender considerations will be mainstreamed across all phases of activities under the project.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Save the Children’s initiative focuses on addressing the unique WASH needs of at-risk groups, including persons with disabilities, older individuals, adolescent girls, and women within targeted areas. Using the Washington Group Questionnaire, SC identifies barriers to accessibility and privacy, prioritizing segregated facilities that meet the diverse needs of these risk groups. The initiative will continue to explore physical and social obstacles, ensuring that the experiences of affected individuals guide the development of practical, inclusive solutions. SC aims to create targeted WASH services that adhere to sector standards, including constructing segregated facilities for different disabilities and genders. Collaborating with site management, this project will enhance safety and security by ensuring improved lighting and infrastructure, significantly mitigating the risk of abuse and creating a more secure environment. The project will also continue emphasizing educating communities about the hygienic use of WASH facilities and promoting the production of reusable sanitary pads by adolescent girls, fostering independence in hygiene management. Community volunteers will be trained in gender and inclusion to ensure effective advocacy for diverse groups including risk groups. The initiative will promote the active participation of women, girls, older individuals, and persons with disabilities in WASH committees, ensuring inclusive decision-making processes that reflect community needs. Resources will be allocated strategically to address the specific challenges these populations face, and collaboration with various sectors will enhance technical support. SC is committed to incorporating the voices and needs of persons with disabilities in all Joint Response Plans, ensuring they are actively involved in the decision-making process.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Save the Children (SC) prioritizes affected populations' needs through participatory assessments, including the 2024 Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA). SC emphasizes an accountability mechanism that encourages community feedback via multiple channels, ensuring that community voices, especially those of children, adolescents, and marginalized groups, are integral to the decision-making process. To strengthen communication and responsiveness, SC promotes face-to-face interactions and household visits and establishes complaint boxes and information desks for community feedback. SC also works for a standardized messaging approach in four languages to enhance accessibility. SC actively involves the community in identifying needs and will continue prioritizing assistance to ensure transparency in beneficiary selection. Regular communication will be maintained to ensure that selection criteria are clear and understood. SC plans to hold consultations in camps to explain response plans and clarify support needed for implementation, fostering trust through regular updates on aid delivery and responses to feedback. Monitoring community perceptions of the humanitarian response is essential for SC's improvement efforts. Utilizing existing feedback mechanisms and perception surveys, SC aims to evaluate the quality of information and community engagement in decision-making. This feedback will guide service adjustments. SC is committed to inclusivity, ensuring that all demographic groups, especially women, older people, people with disabilities, children, and youths, are involved in planning and implementing accountability measures. By addressing the diverse needs of their communities, SC always seeks to create an equitable and effective humanitarian response.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Save the Children will mainstream the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in the program implementation. In addition, safeguarding the children and adults is a crucial priority for all Save the Children’s staff. SC follows a safeguarding policy by using safeguarding risk assessment tools and reviewing them periodically. Orientation on safeguarding and PSEA is mandatory for all staff each year. These policies function under the Safeguarding Policy Framework and have been developed specifically to protect adults and children. This policy outlines Save the Children’s commitments to prevent and respond to sexual misconduct to ensure that all children and adults living in affected communities, even those who may not be directly involved in our program activities, are protected from Sexual Abuse, Exploitation, and Harassment. This includes putting the safety, well-being, needs, and interests of the victim/survivor first. Zero tolerance will be strictly maintained for sexual exploitation, abuse, and/or harassment by any of its representatives in program implementation, materials supply, or any other dealings with the project. All credible concerns and allegations will be addressed by policy. Several channels have been opened for reporting of any cases.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Save the Children has developed a comprehensive, integrated contingency plan for all sectors, including WASH, to respond effectively to disasters. This plan includes regular training and simulations for the Emergency Response Team (ERT), ensuring staff are prepared for various crises. Key sectors have defined and agreed upon preparedness actions, and SC maintains a stockpile of essential supplies, such as construction materials, water treatment plants, hygiene kits, dignity kits, kitchen kits, and clothing, to enhance our emergency response capabilities.SC will train volunteers and community committees in camps to strengthen emergency preparedness. In designing response interventions, it considers local risks and vulnerabilities, adheres to do-no-harm principles, and ensures environmental compliance. Our humanitarian sector focuses on preparedness, early action, and disaster response, with ongoing Emergency Response Training and Emergency Preparedness Planning workshops for the WASH team to maintain high standards.Additionally, Save the Children emphasizes making WASH facilities climate-resilient and inclusive, particularly for individuals with disabilities. Our approach facilitates effective community responses while prioritizing climate resilience by reducing reliance on fossil fuels and promoting renewable solar energy for WASH facilities. We aim for zero waste and zero carbon emissions to enhance the sustainability of our services.To support our commitment to eco-friendly practices, all WASH staff will receive training on climate-resilient WASH programming in 2025. This initiative underscores our dedication to integrating sustainability into our operations and equipping our teams with the necessary skills to implement climate-conscious solutions in resource-limited settings. Through these efforts, Save the Children strives to create a more resilient and sustainable future for communities affected by disasters.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The project emphasizes a comprehensive approach to Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) to ensure water, sanitation, hygiene, and waste management services are in continuity and safe during emergencies. Save the Children has established an Emergency Preparedness Plan (EPP) that incorporates robust measures, including a detailed business continuity plan and standardized operating procedures for emergency response in Rohingya camps, Cox's Bazar and Bandarban district. This framework is designed to maintain uninterrupted WASH practices even in crises. SC has a dedicated trained Emergency Response Team on standby, ready to deploy to provide swift support to the affected communities in Cox's Bazar and Bandarban districts covering the Cox's Bazar Area Office.To minimize disruptions in learning, SC has strategically prepositioned essential educational and emergency supplies in critical locations, enabling rapid response capabilities. Additionally, contingency funding positioning from humanitarian sources will be allocated to address unforeseen circumstances, ensuring that resources are promptly available for effective intervention needed, with preliminary budget requirements outlined to support various potential scenarios. This proactive approach contributes to building a resilient WASH system that can adapt and respond to emergencies, particularly within the challenging context of Cox's Bazar.Furthermore, SC has established several trained volunteer groups to sustain WASH activities, including water supply operations, during emergencies. The project staff will utilize emergency communication channels to address urgent situations effectively. This strategic planning will ensure that SC can respond dynamically to the evolving needs of affected communities, reinforcing our commitment to safeguarding the health and well-being of the beneficiaries.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Save the Children (SC) actively engages children and youth aged 15-24 in program implementation and decision-making processes. Recognizing the unique challenges faced by children and adolescent girls regarding menstrual health, SC provides comprehensive menstrual hygiene management training. This initiative empowers participants to create their reusable sanitary pads, promoting sustainability and self-sufficiency. By utilizing engaging methods such as storytelling and role-play, SC fosters an enjoyable learning environment for children that demystifies menstruation, reduces stigma, and builds confidence among girls. Additionally, SC has formed a group of non-paid youth volunteers who enhance community engagement through training in disaster preparedness and response. These volunteers participate in program implementation by organizing cleaning campaigns, facilitating waste segregation, and raising awareness about hygiene practices. Their involvement strengthens community ties and empowers youth to take ownership of local health initiatives. SC also emphasizes accountability by incorporating children and youth feedback in program design and implementation, ensuring their needs are addressed effectively. Through these efforts, SC fosters a culture of hygiene and health awareness that benefits the entire Rohingya community of the assigned areas.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

SC’s cost-efficient model for WASH services prioritizes self-reliance and community resilience by actively involving residents in planning and implementation. This participatory approach fosters ownership and responsibility, with local maintenance committees ensuring the upkeep of essential facilities through targeted training for community members. SC also produces compost fertiliser from solid waste management, supporting local gardeners and youth groups. Additionally, it plans to distribute FSTP effluent to vegetable gardens via underground pipe networks, enhancing nutrition and livelihoods by promoting kitchen gardens. Engaging community members in constructing WASH materials, SC boosts food security and creates livelihood opportunities, empowering individuals for self-reliance and resilience in the Rohingya community.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

SC is committed to improving water and sanitation facilities in host communities near camps. Our initiatives include repairing and upgrading tube wells and conducting water quality tests, with selection based on community meetings involving union parishad representatives. SC’s water and sanitation experts will focus on vulnerable groups, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities when selecting the sites. We will also repair and maintain latrines, guided by local leaders and community consultations. To promote sustainability, SC will establish WASH committees, providing training on maintenance, water quality testing, and community engagement. This holistic strategy aims to enhance access to safe water and sanitation while fostering community ownership. While these activities may not directly align with the district development plan, they are designed to complement existing efforts without conflicting with the overarching development goals of the district.
