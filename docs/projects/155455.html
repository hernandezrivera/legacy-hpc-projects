Needs

Zimbabwe is at risk of multiple hydro-meteorological hazards namely drought, flooding and diarrhoeal diseases epidemics (Cholera and Typhoid). The projection of the El Niño phenomenon will negatively affect the provision of safe water thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable communities. Currently, the country is experiencing a cholera outbreak in Harare and 17 other districts. As of 07 December 2018, the country recorded a cumulative 10,550 (10,269 suspected and 281 confirmed) cases and 59 deaths with a case fatality rate of 0.56%. With the eminent El Nino phenomenon, Save the Children is proposing an integrated response strategy to the WASH needs and risk of WASH related diseases. The intervention will be carried out in the following: Harare, Epworth, Binga, Kariba, Gokwe North, Gokwe South, Kwekwe and Mt Darwin districts. The 7 districts are part of the 30 priority districts identified to be the most at risk and fall between scores 3-5 in the Zimbabwe drought severity rankings. Harare has been included as it was the area worst hit by the recent cholera outbreak. The total number of people at risk in the 8 proposed areas of operation has been estimated to be 170 599. With the eminent drought there is a need to Improve access to sufficient water of appropriate quality and quantity to vulnerable communities for them to fulfil their basic needs. This will be done through the rehabilitation of non-functional water points so as to improve access to clean safe water. Currently only 7,7% of households nationally treat their water before drinking. With that in mind it is important to ensure that households get access to safe water to reduce the prevalence of WASH related diseases. There is a need also to Increase awareness of safe hygiene and sanitation practices, nationally a total of 18 districts (7 proposed districts included) have more that 50% of households practicing open defecation and hence vulnerable people in those communities are at risk of WASH related diseases. The proposed intervention will focus on participatory health and hygiene education (PHHE) using the school health club and community health club approaches. Save the children has recently successfully implemented Cholera response projects in Harare, Chitungwiza, Gokwe North, Seke, Buhera and Mt Darwin districts using the school health club approach. We are therefore proposing to use the model together with the community health club approach to increase awareness of safe hygiene and sanitation practices. The last part of the proposed intervention will involve the distribution of NFIs to schools and the most vulnerable families in the targeted areas. The distribution of hygiene enabling NFIs and hygiene education pertaining to safe and hygienic child care and feeding practices will be done to prevent occurrence of diarrhoeal and other water related diseases. In order to promote sustainability, water point committee members will be trained on community based management of water points. The project will also further support district level stakeholder participation by supporting DWSSC and PWSSC meetings The response is specifically targeted at WASH needs arising from and aggravated by the current cholera and eminent drought.

Activities

1 Rehabilitation of 150 non-functional water points2 Solar mechanisation of 15 water points3 Water quality testing of 250 water points4 Inline chlorination of 40 community and institutional water points in Harare and Epworth5 Training of 165 water point committees (1155 members)6 Refresher training of 90 pump minders on rehabilitation and repair of water points7 Refresher training of School Health Coordinators8 Refresher training of EHTs and Village Health Workers on PHHE9 Forming/reviving school health clubs10 Forming/reviving community health clubs11 Community awareness campaigns led by school health clubs and community health clubs12 Supporting DWSCC meetings at district level13 Dissemination of critical hygiene messages, IEC material development & distribution, Dissemination of messages focused on sanitation improvement, household water treatment and water conservation, 14 Monitoring diarrhoreal diseases, 15 Distribution of NFIs to 16 000 Households and 200 schools, including logistics and monitoring
