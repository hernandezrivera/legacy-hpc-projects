Project Summary

The project will focus on integrated protection monitoring with several sub-sectors including Mine Action, Trafficking and legal documentation. Following 2024 camps closures/other alarming protection concerns in Borno and Adamawa on top of rapid protection assessment for new areas, DRC will focus on adjusting its responses programmes according to protection concerns identified. IPA will still be provided as a direct response while DRC protection desks/centres will initiate a generalised responses to cases through further individual counselling in some areas as a pilot approach. Protection response services will be delivered at DRC Protection desks/Protection centers and the pilot of case management in cooperation with specialized (CP/GBV) case management services by strategic/operational partners will lead to a network of practictionners in emergency which will collaborate into exchanging good practices. DRC will also reinforce capacities and awareness on general PSS services through specific curricula adapted based on protection monitoring findings. DRC will especially pay attention on survivors of trafficking and SEA to follow proper referrals pathways and provide further support on spotting the signs of risks associated with it. While the project will strengthen the community-based approach and support the community based structures in choosing their favourite forms of community engagement (awareness sessions, drama, songs etc), DRC within this project will expand its PSS services for the elderly and people with limited mobility opportunity to feel included.
DRC will train relevant service providers at a community level, such as teachers, health workers and community structures, on MHPSS and work with the CB structures to reduce stigma around mental health in Borno and Adamawa.

The innovative approach that consists of integrating protection monitoring, trafficking and age concerns into the protection response will particularly materialize through the implementation of structured PSS, support of community based networks in identification of marginalised persons and an age, gender, diversity sensitive protection monitoring tools, and adapted curricula for all PSS age and gender groups.

1 - Needs

Borno State is affected by the conflict and insecurity caused by the non-state armed groups (NSAGs), the government forces, and criminal groups. The attacks target both civilian and military personnel, resulting in killings, abductions, and displacement. The security situation also hampers humanitarian operations, as most roads are unsafe and prone to attacks. The affected population faces movement restrictions, food insecurity, insufficient humanitarian aid, and economic hardship. The high presence of government security forces (Military, Police, and CJTF) and local vigilantes in the communities also poses protection risks, such as physical assault and restriction of movement. Ngala, Bama, Gwoza, MMC and Jere LGA host a large number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and refugee returnees who have fled from NSAG attacks and insecurity in their areas of origin. These locations face various protection risks, such as killings and abductions, sexual violence, domestic and intimate partner violence, child labor, and discrimination.These risks are influenced by the presence of NSAGs, the lack of adequate standard of living, the lack of access to services and assistance, the conservative environment, and the marginalization of some groups. These risks affect the health, dignity, and well-being of the affected population, and expose them to trauma, stigma, and loss. The report gives some insights towards how the affected population has been coping to mitigate protection risks and the various needs and gaps related to humanitarian interventions.Adamawa State is relatively calmer than Borno State, although there are still some attacks happening between herders and farmers that could lead to social cohesion issues. GBV is also the highest risk identified in Adamawa, but no significant attacks have been recorded. However, there are still some suspicions towards youths and men and their relations to NSAGs. The affected population faces food insecurity, insufficient humanitarian aid, and economic hardship, which affect their health and education. The affected population also faces discrimination and marginalization based on their ethnic background, occupation, or physical impairments. The affected population needs more humanitarian actors to address these urgent needs, especially in locations where there is limited or no humanitarian presence. 2024 marks the 14th year since the conflict started in Nigeria with BAY states being mostly affected. Borno and Adamawa states in northeastern Nigeria have been facing a severe humanitarian crisis, primarily driven by the insurgency of two main NSAGs and associated conflict-related displacement. The region has been a hotbed of violence, with frequent attacks by extremist offshoots of Boko Haram, JAS (Jama’atu Ahlis Sunna Lidda’adati wal-Jihad), Ansaru and ISWAP (Islamic State in West Africa Province), leading to significant displacement of civilians and loss of lives. Among key protection risks identified the 3 main protection risks in Borno in areas of operations are child labor, abductions, and killings while in Adamawa, sexual violence, early marriages and abductions prevail as the highest protection risks. Based on those needs an appropriate PSS curriculum for populations will be needed to address high level of distress linked to PTSD and high level of stress and protection monitoring will need to be strengthened knowing that a lot of protection concerns could be linked to further areas of protection such as trafficking etc. DRC will address all immediate needs through effective referrals pathways and IPA support but will also be able to provide direct support through its general protection expertise on PSS.

2 - Activities

- Protection Monitoring: Activity 1: Protection Monitoring through HH surveys – DRC will continue using the Protection Sector North East HH survey through Kobo to ensure a better comprehension of the overall situation of Borno especially related to sudden returns, displacements and others difficulties as to have a specific entrance into more global advocacy. They will also feed the reporting incident tool when violations are being reported. Activity 2: FGD and KIIs are conducted – DRC will conduct FGD and KIIs regularly when trends are being seen and communities need to be heard to readapt our own programs but also to provide further snapshots that can be reported to different platforms and actors including the PSNE, INGO forum etc. Activity 3: Referrals to specialized services and assistance – DRC already has FP and a service mapping of different services available including DRC activities in other sectors: this will be updated on a quarterly basis from each LGA. Therefore, referrals tracking and information dissemination about different basic services and more specialized services will continue to be provided through the information protection desk. - Structured PSS and Recreational activities:According to DRC guidelines on MHPSS, we are involved in the 3 first layers of the MHPSS pyramid. Although DRC would not provide individual psychological counselling, the PSS assistants would be able to provide psychological first aid as a non-specialised support and include different persons according to their gender and age groups in various sessions: In order to cope with changes and transition: DRC will provide adults with a structured PSS-Stress management curricula, another one on self-awareness and self-esteem. This will comprise of one session per week per group targeted for 8 weeks. It will complement the life-skills and vocational training activities that brought confidence into the participants and were used as an entry point for this type of protection activity that will work closely with the Economic Recovery department. DRC ECREC team will also identify households that have a level of food insecurity level 5 DRC protection and EcRec teams will also ensure that adults will be able to explore practical and emotional challenges they face and the emotions their children go through through exploring a concept of ‘learning through stress management strategies’. We also we submitted under the FSS module complementary activities to some of the PSS activities. - Individual protection assistance (in kind/cash):Through protection by presence (protection safe spaces, mobile desks, home visits) in the state and LGA identified as priorities, DRC will identify vulnerable individuals exposed to high protection risks through protection monitoring. DRC staff may use some principles of case management related to data protection and analysis of IPA cases, but IPA is a very different activity than CM, this is an assistance aimed at preventing, reducing or mitigating exposure to protection risks, or limiting the effects of violation on victims, which can be addressed with individual protection assistance. - Awareness sessions: DRC has been working with several community-based groups for several years in the different locations targeted and has been working with community-based outreach workers which were trained on general protection topics. DRC will accompany the CBP to pilot conducting some simple awareness sessions on general protection topics such as child labor, anger management with an audience of 8-15 participants per sessions and would be limited by 2 to 3 sessions per week throughout the timeframe of the project. Each of those sessions will be supervised by protection assistants for the first three months of the project and will then be handed over to community structures FP.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

DRC will build on its position of leading protection actor in Northeast to keep ensuring protection is mainstreamed throughout its programming, putting the Do No Harm principle at the center of its interventions, and paying attention to prevent & minimize any unintended negative effects of its intervention. DRC’s protection mainstreaming package includes comprehensive training for all staff on protection principles & mainstreaming, including non-discrimination, respect, confidentiality, security, transparency in beneficiaries’ selection, as well as on PSEA. Each training will be contextualized depending on the role played by each actor involved in the project (local authorities, community-based organizations, community volunteers, DRC’s staff, other organisations' staff, etc.) to ensure a maximized ownership of such principles. Particularly, DRC will ensure that community based structures and other staff from organisations with whom we collaborate with are properly trained and can mainstream protection while implementing their specific activities. DRC will ensure safe, dignified, and impartial access to the assistance provided, with private spaces for activities that require confidentiality such as individualized PSS discussions, identification of protection concerns (especially GBV), etc. PSS activities and awareness-raising sessions on GBV and EORE will be informed by inclusive protection monitoring allowing both individual and collective participation through information sharing, and will be adapted to the specific needs of each localities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC will ensure constant and meaningful participation of girls, women, boys, men, and key stakeholders such as community-based organizations at all stages of the project. DRC will inform communities in advance and in a transparent manner about the project. They will conduct regular activities to ensure learning and adaptation of the project activities based on the lessons learned. Through other feedback and complaints platforms, the implementation teams will ensure design is well suited to the needs and context of the affected communities, engage communities in monitoring and evaluating the action and gather feedback to adjust implementation through disaggregated FGDs to further ensure the quality, impact and appropriateness of planned responses, and to allow for timely adjustments.
