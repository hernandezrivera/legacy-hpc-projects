Project Summary

The proposed project aims at addressing emergency and more sustainable NFI needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northeast and Northwest Syria. The intervention will target a total of 213,336 beneficiaries.
In NWS, ACTED will provide cash for fuel to better equip the residents for winter in western camp clusters in Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates. In NES, ACTED plans to cover the fuel needs of all the households residing in Areesheh and Serekaniye camps, in Al-Hasake governorate through the provision of heating fuel and kerosene and diesel heaters respectively. In addition, ACTED will preposition itself to cover the winterization needs, according to the budget availability, of the sites targeted with the provision of mobile CCCM services in the region, should emergency needs arise throughout the project.
ACTED will take into account the different risks and concerns affecting women, men, girls and boys and people with disabilities when designing this intervention. With regards to the latter, ACTED will ensure that the distributions are conducted respectfully of the needs of the most vulnerable, with specific distribution lines dedicated to women and elderlies. ACTED will also conduct the distributions directly at the HH level when needed.

3 - Needs

Across Syria, over 5.7 million persons are expected to be in need of NFI assistance in 2023, thus a 5 per cent increase since 2022, as per the (unreleased) 2023 HNO. Due to limited access to livelihoods, camp residents, IDPs and conflict-affected people are highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs, particularly with regards to non-food items (NFI) and winterization, as limited purchasing powers means households must prioritize food and water at the expenses of NFI. Winter clothing, mattresses and blankets have witnessed a significant increase in inaccessibility in 2022 when compared to the previous year. With the security environment remaining somewhat volatile, deterioration in economic conditions, compounded by a depreciating Syrian pound, has led to further intensification of the severity of need for NFI support. IDPs in camp settings in NWS endured an exceptionally harsh 2021-22 winter. As formal camps face critical capacity issues, newly displaced households turn toward informal settlements, where access to NFI items is the most limited. This is compounded by continued displacement trends, particularly across NES. This highlights needs for in-camp NFI support.

4 - Implementation of activities

ACTED’s intervention will focus on providing seasonal NFI items as well as heaters to 213,336 conflict-affected IDPs and camp residents across Northwest and Northeast Syria to better equip them for winter. In NWS, ACTED will provide cash for fuel to better equip the residents for winter in western camp clusters in Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates. In NES, ACTED plans to cover the fuel needs of all the households residing in Areesheh and Serekaniye camps, in Al-Hasake governorate through the provision of heating fuel and kerosene and diesel heaters. Under this assistance, each household will receive a total 100 USD, which will be distributed in one or two instalments at the beginning and at mid-winter season. In addition, ACTED will preposition itself to cover the winterization needs of the sites targeted with the provision of mobile CCCM services in the region, should emergency needs arise throughout the project.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups’ needs during project design/implementation. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach. ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct covering Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV),and ACTED implements Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Information on the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be communicated to camp manager, community leaders, camp residents. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints (WhatsApp group, letterbox, desks).. The presence of women staff in the teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Theft: NFI kits procured for the project could be stolen in transit to or from warehouses. Ground-level storage spaces below residential properties are used as and when possible, warehouses are protected by a security guard 24 hours a day, and keys to warehouses are solely in the possession of the Storekeeper who is responsible for all stock movements. Warehouse locations are carefully considered and when possible located outside of the town perimeter and near checkpoints for additional security. Diversion of assistance: ACTED has a robust and established Standard of Procedures for its activities to track the assistance to ensure it reaches the targeted beneficiaries. ACTED and partners’ security teams will conduct thorough security assessments in each target area to identify secure distribution sites. Distribution schedules will be set up to ensure that there is only a small number of beneficiaries per day and the location of the distribution site is close to the area where beneficiaries are. Monitoring and evaluation units also conduct 100% verification and extensive Post-Distribution Monitoring to track the assistance provided. Access and security to the sites, for in-camp contexts: Security and Political, Expansion to new areas: in-depth access mission from the ACTED Security team, formal approval required for daily movements and daily sharing of security updates.
