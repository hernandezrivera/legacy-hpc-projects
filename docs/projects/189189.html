Project Summary

The project aims to ensure that Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant WASH structures and infrastructure in the flood prone areas, and Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition focusing on SAM children/caretakers and PLW provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion.
The target communities and villages shall be identified in consultation with the WASH Cluster subnational coordinator and with the support of the Nutrition partner, and households with a pregnant and lactating woman, female-headed household, households with a disabled and/or elderly shall be targeted as this will improve access to safe water especially in payams/communities with high GAM prevalence and high vulnerability to WASH. The project shall also assess the WASH conditions at nutrition facility level and support nutrition partners with the provision of safe water, hygiene promotion activities and complementary sanitation services where possible in the facility to facilitate nutrition programming and reduce the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission.

1 - Needs

An estimated 68,000 people’s living standard and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in Canal-Pigi, Jonglei State (WSC, 2021). Despite the fact that major waterways were replenished by the long rainy season, only 39 per cent of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34 per cent in rural areas (FSNMS, 2021). Difficulty in securing enough water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49 per cent of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. The situation is particularly concerning in Canal/Pigi where virtually all households relied on surface water (FSNMS, 2021). Surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods, when latrine pits seep into waterways. Limited access to water is also compounded by 14 per cent of households reporting having at least one jerrycan and/or bucket to safely store water (FSNMS, 2021).Displacement, insecurity and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. Insecurity is also a factor that has affected water access. Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points, with 18 per cent of households, both rurally and in urban settings, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water in the two weeks prior to data collection (FSNMS, 2021). Of those, 12 per cent reported of women and girls reportedly avoiding water points due to GBV concerns (FSNMS, 2021). Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. Overall, 80 per cent of households did not access a latrine (FSNMS, 2021). In Jonglei region, the percentage of households accessing a latrine is consistently below 10 per cent, with open defecation the norm (FSNMS, 2021). Several factors contribute to this bleak situation. Long-lasting development challenges largely explain the fact that South Sudan has historically displayed one of the lowest sanitation coverage globally (WSC, 2021). Secondly, using a latrine is considered a taboo in many parts of the country, especially in states with a larger rural and/or pastoral population (WSC, 2021). In some locations, constructed latrines are hardly used due to societal pressure, with people preferring instead to walk a great distance even at night to practice open defecation (WSC, 2021). Moreover, floods regularly damage existing sanitation infrastructure and, given their annual occurrence and paired with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by communities to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities.Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Overall, only 16 per cent of the households reported owning soap and this figure was as low as 4 per cent in Jonglei State (FSNMS, 2021).

2 - Project activities

The project will build quality sustainable infrastructures such as flood-resilient facilities, robust supply systems and self-sustained maintenance mechanisms through enhance community engagement and ownership by undertaking sand filtration household facility construction. The SAM children and their care takers as well as Pregnant and Lactating women shall be targeted at nutrition facilities with WASH services and NFIs distribution to reduce disease transmission at household level.WASH NFIs, hygiene promotion, emergency sanitation and water supply shall be targeted in communities showing a high risk of outbreak and high malnutrition prevalence.Protection mainstreaming and GBV mitigation measures will be mainstreamed throughout WASH programming.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback shall basis for informed decision making to ensure all groups are reached and served equitably. Beneficiaries need and have the right to timely access relevant information on issues related to the proposed project and what they would expect from it, and how to lodge a complaint and provide feedback about the projects. To ensure feedback and complaint mechanism, Post-distribution (PDM), post- intervention (PIM) monitoring will be done across the project where areas are accessible and secure. PDMs/PIMs give clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports shall provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve overall project quality. Additionally, regular community meetings with leaders will help in collecting feedback.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project shall undertake FGD and the training of hygiene promoters selected from within the communities to share information about the project and also implement recommended strategies reached, accepted and developed after collecting feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project shall ensure conduction of safety audit and Focus Group Discussion led by women and girls are conducted through which critical information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) shall be shared with them.1. The organization policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse shall be oriented to all staffs and ensure that all adhere and uphold the principle of 'Do No Harm' while serving the beneficiaries. 2. Through collaboration with Protection partners, the beneficiaries are enlightened and sensitized on information regarding SEA including information on fast channels available to address SEA cases in the communities. 3. Access to PSEA visibility materials in public places like office notice boards, markets, water points, sanitary facilities, health care centers and schools. 4. Events like community or individual meetings / workshops, drama or music events would provide a good platform for sensitization of beneficiaries. Additionally messages on PSEA can be embedded in Hygiene promotion messaging to reach the entire community.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project shall undertake FGD and the training of hygiene promoters selected from within the communities to share information about the project, provide timely feedback and complaint from beneficiaries about the project.
