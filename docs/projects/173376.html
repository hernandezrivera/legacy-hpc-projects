Project Summary

Lifesaving and comprehensive health care services including Non-communicable disease (NCD) care and Cervical cancer screening using NIROG electronic medical record (EMR) system.
Major goals are to improve NCD care delivery (e.g. Hypertension, Diabetes, Asthma, Anemia, Gestational diabetes, pre-eclamptic hypertension, etc.), detection and treatment of precancerous lesion for cervical cancer using 'see-and-treat' module, and expansion of paperless, digital EMR system to strengthen coordination among the service providers in the FDMN camps for better information management and monitoring to help achieve standardized, quality and accountable health service delivery.
HAEFA’s medical teams have provided healthcare to over 130,000 Rohingya Refugees living in the camps since 2017. HAEFA received the 'Stars in Global Health' from the Grand Challenges Canada in 2018-2019 for the RMG healthcare services including EMR (electronic medical record)-based NCD (HTN, DM, Pregnancy with gestational DM, hypertension in pregnancy/PET) and cervical cancer screening using mobile colposcopy method and treatment to more than 10,000 RMG workers at their workplace. HAEFA imported 10,000 pieces of essential PPE and distributed throughout Bangladesh and provided "Protective Health and Hygiene Packages" that include face masks, bar soaps, washing aprons, toilet brush, etc. to the most vulnerable COVID-19 patients with comorbidities such as HTN, DM, Asthma/COPD, TB, and above 65 years of age. HAEFA also pioneered a COVID-19 Competency Training of Trainers program in collaboration with Project HOPE. This training was aimed at the healthcare professionals at all levels and institutions including physicians, nurses, midwives, public health workers, ER/lab/ICU/OR, hospital directors, and policy makers.
With partnership of Project HOPE, HAEFA also provided and is continuing to provide medicines and medical supplies to the medical teams treating health problems of the population impacted by the devastation of Cyclone Amphan in May 2020.

1 - Identified Needs

1) Needs of FDMN (Rohingyas): Ensuring adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities is a continuous challenge for FDMN (Rohingyas) population. Moreover, the arrival of a pandemic to the camp setting created fear, distrust, spread of misinformation, and access to health services more difficult. Specially people with disabilities and women, children, transgender people are facing increasing barriers in accessing health services. Crucial services such as sexual, reproductive and maternal health services have been reduced. Present containment measures made it harder for them to access services like menstrual hygiene management (MHM), sexual reproductive health, women and girl friendly spaces and access to protection and justice for GBV survivors. Such context of uncertainty has created feelings of insecurity and stress which lead to widespread mental health issues among everyone particularly among men and boys. These created additional need for community surveillance, immediate measures to address fears and misperceptions regarding isolation facilities triage system, specialized treatment and referral pathways to face the challenge and stigmatization created around COVID-19 among the community has been created.Moreover, there are shortages in Non- Communicable disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity, screening for diseases among the survivors such as cervical cancer, and specialized services (skin & gestational diabetes and hypertension in pregnancy, and other health care services for people with palliative needs).There are needs for increase in the provision of mental health support to everyone, increase in the number of door-to-door community health worker volunteers ensuring a gender balance and representation across diverse communities in raising awareness. Gender sensitive and protection training among health actors, making all critical services and supplies for all forms of sexual and reproductive health care accessible to all are pressing needs.There are also critical gaps in comprehensive emergency obstetrics & newborn baby care, family planning provision and availability to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management for gender-based violence survivors. 2) Needs of FDMN (Host Community): Many of the same needs are observed in the host community particularly regarding health care, NCD management, laboratory diagnostic facility, reproductive healthcare, moreover, host communities reported greater challenges to accessing health facilities, with only 40% reporting safe and easy access to health facilities.In light of COVID-19, the need for continued messaging, testing, and management created challenges to service provision for the host community as well. The gap in knowledge amplified the fear and stigmas surrounding COVID-19 immensely among the host community. Same fear of COVID-19 and isolation as present within the camp setting that created movement restrictions and closed facilities made the host community also to face further barrier to health-seeking behaviours, resulting in fewer presenting to facilities for timely care.Fear of being infected with Covid-19 at health facilities is also restricting them from getting treatment on non-COVID-19- related illnesses, even when these were critical. Financial impact of Covid-19 had decreased the ability of poorer households to seek health services and be tested for the virus. Access to maternal, sexual, reproductive health and rights services is even more restricted for host community women. Stigma and discrimination hindered transgender people, persons with disabilities, elderly people and children’s access to health services in the host community too. Increasing mental health issues and a sense of insecurity and stress were recorded high in numbers, especially women experiencing high levels of stress and mental health issues due to the increase in GBV, household work and financial problems.

2 - Activities or outputs

1.Essential health care services including preventive and curative care for NCDs like Hypertension, Diabetes, Asthma, Anemia, Gestational diabetes, pre-eclamptic hypertension, will be provided. 2.Strengthening health care services including laboratory and diagnostic capacity for both community. 3.Health awareness aimed at providing right health and nutrition information related to NCD and reproductive health will be provided. 4.Using Mobile-ODT and Thermo-coagulator, in collaboration with Brown University and an oncologist will provide Cervical cancer screening and improvement in health-seeking behaviour for female patients aged (30-65 years): 3000 women in a year. Also family planning awareness programs, modern methods of contraceptive to the consenting adults. 5.Using our existing field-proven paperless digital EMR system [NIROG], provide high quality healthcare by systematically capturing demographic information, age and gender, health data, diagnosis and treatment plans for all patients examined. The NIROG EMR allows the HAEFA team to be completely in line with the accurate reporting requirements established by the Government of Bangladesh (GOB) DGHS, MoHFW national authorities. 6.During the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, HAEFA has been providing special care and medical services to patients with NCDs (esp, DM, Asthma, heart conditions, pregnancy, etc) who may have severe COVID-19 outocme and provide with immediate necessary treatment and referral. In collaboration with CPJ (Center for peace and Justice) HAEFA will be monitoring symptoms of presumptive cases for COVID-19 for 14 days. 7.With the effort to minimize the spread of the disease among family members COVID-19 patients' identified by HAEFA will continue to provide and distribute "Protective Health and Hygiene Packages" to the most vulnerable COVID-19 patients with comorbidities such as HTN, DM, Asthma/COPD, TB, pregnant, and above 65 years of age. 8.For Local host communities: HAEFA provides services for NCD, cervical cancer screening, diagnostic facilities, and communicable disease, including TB screening to the host community living in close proximity to the camps and nearby unions. 9.HAEFA regularly takes part in Health Sector Emergency Preparedness & Response (EPR) group meetings and discussions and will continue to do so. 10.HAEFA will continue to take major roles in COVID-19 local and global Responses, trainings and resource allocation as it has already been doing from the onset of the pandemic. HAEFA imported masks, protective goggles, face shield, gowns distributed free of cost to the healthcare professionals in the FDMN camps, nearby subdistrict and district hospitals of Bangladesh. HAEFA also conducted “COVID-19 Competency TOT” courses in collaboration with Brown University, USA. 11.HAEFA will continue to ensure equal and inclusive access to health that address the specific needs of women, girls, men, boys and other marginalized populations (transgender, persons with disabilities, etc.) as well as the socio-cultural context. HAEFA maintains gender-balanced health staff/volunteers where it recruits and train both female and male in community health committees, community awareness and social norm change activities. 12.HAEFA will introduce regular door to door awareness programs which will focus on mental health issues and maternal health care which will encourage antenatal, post-natal checkups along with awareness in increase of health seeking behavior among all genders and age group of people, especially women. 13.HAEFA will maintain a feedback and complaint mechanisms that are child- and adolescent- friendly, gender-responsive, inclusive and confidential. An exit pole system is in place which accumulates the patient’s feedback after their visits in the health centers. Also,in collaboration with CPJ, HAEFA trained volunteers for regular follow-ups especially on presumptive COVID-19 patients.
