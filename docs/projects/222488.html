Project Summary

Action Against Hunger through its Food Security and Livelihood sector will support vulnerable and crisis affected population through multi-purpose cash assistance and agricultural input support including capacity building.
The principal objective of the Action is to provide emergency multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to address critical needs of the crisis-affected population and strengthen local capacities and resilience to withstand shocks in South Sudan. Using a gender and localization responsive approach and providing MPCA to over 51,271 IDP’s, returnees, and host communities, complimented with other sectorial resilience building interventions, that aim at meeting the basic needs of the most vulnerable household.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity - classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 Catastrophe). The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. The case is double in Aweil East and Twic, counties that have direct boundaries with Sudan which continue to receive high numbers of returnees that are fleeing Sudan crises keep on rise. This has put the pressure on the host communities to provide land for returnees and refugees settlements beside sharing the resources. This has also increased prices in the local markets, noting that 80-90 percent of commodity supplies previously come from the neighboring Sudan and this has cut amidst the increasing insecurity and road blockage by the armed fractions. This is not different in Fangak where people with severe food insecurity resulting from climate induced challenges such as seasonal flooding, poor rain distribution, pest and diseases including man-made disasters such as communal conflict, cattle raiding and revenge killing some of which does not target the aid workers and carry little implication on the humanitarian operation.

2. Project activities

Action Against Hunger will support targeted and crisis affected population through lifesaving and resilient building activities as align with FSL cluster objective for the counties in priority 1 & 2 as guided by IPC report with special focus on population on IPC 3 & 4 including pocket of 5. The activities will include provision of multipurpose cash assistance where there is market functionality to crisis affected population including vulnerable host population, IDPs and returnees and persons with disability. The MEB will be demand using JMMI data of the prevailing months. The project will also streamline component of resilience by providing target farmers with Agriculture inputs with the objective to support food-insecure smallholders and communities to build resilience and enhance their livelihoods. The goal is to build the resilience of rural households to seasonal climate induced shocks. Action Against hunger will integrate the farmers capacity building and Livelihood activities blended with complementary programs such as Health, nutrition, Wash, and Gender, that will help prevent a deterioration in the food and nutritional status and reduce the communities from further prevalence of food insecurity crisis through building community resilience to further shocks by community asset creation and livelihood. The proposed intervention is also designed to build resilience and sustainability among the most vulnerable communities in the target locations.
