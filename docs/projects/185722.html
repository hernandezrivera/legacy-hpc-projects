Project Summary

The proposed project is aimed at strengthening the WASH response system in the BAY states, (Borno, Adamawa, and Yola); through the provision of access to a safe water supply via rehabilitation and upgrading of nonfunctional boreholes, provision of access to safe sanitation facilities through rehabilitation of non-functional latrines, and showers, provision of access to kits and top ups, distribution of hygiene kits, COVID-19 Personal Protection equipment, and gender-sensitive, age-appropriate, and community tailored hygiene promotion to reach a total of 150,000 internally displaced men, women, boys and girls, construction of 300 handwashing points in IDP camps and communities across Kala/Balge, and Mafa LGAs, in Borno State, Yola South and Girie LGAs, in Adamawa state and Geidam LGA in Yobe State within one year.
From the total target, 50,000 vulnerable household heads (60% men/adolescent boys & 40 women/adolescent girls) will be reached with sensitization on positive hygiene habits via a door-to-door approach in Kala-Balge LGA while 100,000 individuals will be reached with bi-monthly awareness-raising outreaches on positive hygiene habits using a motorized speaker & megaphone approach in the five LGAs. Key messages in both approaches will include prevention and control of COVID-19, Cholera, and Lassa fever.
GOALPrime, through funding from NHF, has established WASHCOM in Kala Balge, this will be replicated and/or strengthened across the five LGAs taking gender-incision into consideration hence a ratio of 60:30:10 women, youth, and men would be used. All will be trained in the construction, maintenance, repair, and management of the WASH facilities.
GOALPrime through the support of the WASH sector common pipeline will distribute 15,000 hygiene kits and PPEs to vulnerable household heads which will be reached with door-to-door sensitization on Chorale, Lassa Fever, and COVID-19.
GOALPrime will rehabilitate 150 non-functional latrines to reach a total of 900 individuals, and 100 household showers to reach a total of 600 individuals across the LGAs.
All WASH facilities will be designed considering the disposal of used menstrual hygiene products, accessible to children and the physically disabled, sex-disaggregated, and clearly marked. They will be locale from the inside and well-lit, especially at night. Lighting will be done in such a way as to reduce the gathering of youth and men at WASH facility sites. GOALPrime is Active in Yola South and Girei LGAs in Adamawa State, Geidam, in Yobe state, and Kala Balge & Mafa LGAs in Borno state providing rehabilitation of latrines and showers, hygiene promotion door to door, speaker campaigns, rehabilitation of boreholes, distribution of kits, water testing and analysis and disinfection of cholera infected households

1 - Needs

WASH needs, mainly access to clean safe water for drinking, hygiene and sanitation have continued to increase in scale and severity across North-East Nigeria. The complexity of needs due to the COVID 19 outbreak continues to impact on needs for hygiene promotion and awareness. Access to safe water has been constrained as a result of new and protracted displacement as well as returns (self-organised and government-led) resulting in host communities registering a reduced resilience due to high pressure on the existing and already over burdened water points and sanitation facilities. New displacements and challenging groundwater potential in some areas have resulted in need for emergency water provision through water trucking, and increased costs for in-depth hydrogeological studies and optimization of water points to ensure water resources are used optimally. Access to sanitation continues to be diminished due to wear and tear of emergency type construction, continuous displacements and seasonal flooding leading to high open defecation and reduced handwashing practices. Sanitation needs for persons with special needs, women and children to ensure safety and equitable access are a critical gap as the sector which requires additional resources as other issues of proximity and design of the facilities are considered.WASH and Health continue to work together on disease early warning and cholera prevention campaigns coupled with environmental hygiene to prevent high AWD cases and mitigate cholera outbreaks. Recently, the WASH sector has further rationalized partners operations to ensure more accountability to beneficiaries and better coverage of needs with reduced funding, which has improved the quality of the response.An estimated 2.8 million people across NE Nigeria remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to clean water and dignified hygiene and sanitation services.For Access to safe clean water; 1.02 million people receive less than 15 litres per person per day while about 1.06m people have intermittent water access with over 500meters walking distance and over 30 minutes queuin time at water points.Access to hygiene and sanitation for estimated 2.8 million is hindere as the households have no latrine and practice open defecation and share a single latrine with more than 20 others, with limited access to handwashing with soap and water and no personal hygiene materials.The fecal sludge management is poor, with low infrastructure to support liquid waste from desludging activities, which is considered as one of the reasons for yearly outbreak of cholera. Additionally flooding is a major concern as most of the fecal sludge worsens with households facing exposure to untreated waste.The high need for WASH services amongst IDPs and returnees has had an increase from last year, mainly due to continuous displacement and the high volume of expected returns. In 2021, the BAY state have face the most critical cholera outbreak in the history of the response. There is critical gaps remain in the water, sanitation and hygiene efforts to reduce non-communicable disease spread, respond to ongoing emergency, and sustain the current level of services across priority areas in the BAY states of North-East Nigeria. The Borno State Government (BSG) resumed the closure of several IDP camps located across the metropolitan city of Maiduguri. In July the BSG relocated IDPs from Farm Center, Muna Garage and Custom House II camps in Maiduguri, the state capital, to Mafa LGA, approximately 40 kilometers to the north-east. As of 16 July, over 6,000 IDPs had been moved with many settling in Ajiri village, Mafa IDP camp and the host community. Over 60,000 IDPs across Dalori 2, Stadium and Gubio camps in Maiduguri have also been relocated. Although the humanitarian community remains concerned about returns that may not be sustainable and aligned to the Borno State Returns Strategy and coverage has to widen to cover the hard to reach LGAs

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GOALPrime will continue to mainstream protection throughout the implementation of this project by aligning every approach with the protection and Do no Harm principles and PSEA, promoting equity, safety, and dignity of both the beneficiaries and that of employers and volunteers. GOALPrime, just like in our ongoing projects will continue to prioritize safety, dignity and avoid causing any form of harm to both beneficiaries and workers. The project will raise safeguarding awareness at the community level and put in place reporting and response mechanisms for redress in case of PSEA. The project will also conduct referral mapping for SGBV cases referrals across all the implementation locations GOALPrime will also strengthen already established mechanisms through which women and girls can be served effectively in target LGAs with systems that allow addressing concerns and complaints. GOALPrime will continue to ensure that the target population accesses the service in proportion to needs and without barriers and discrimination. GOALPrime will work with the community to develop sensitive messages to talk about SGBV, gender inclusion, and other concerns influenced by culture and environment. GOALPrime will continue to pay attention to different needs of girls and boys, women and men including girls and boys with disabilities who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, hence, maintaining an environment that enhances integration and equal considerations of the needs of boys, girls and those with disability. In the Life-skills based psychosocial support services component of this project, GOALPrime will integrate SGBV/SEA Risk Mitigation activities to minimize or mitigate SGBV/SEA risk to beneficiaries. GOALPrime will train employees, and volunteers on child safeguarding and safe child participation, and work with men and boys as ambassadors for the safe participation of women and girls in our project activities. GOALPrime will continue to work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. GOALPrime will also ensure that all activities are carried out in compliance with the Nigerian Center for Disease Control (NCDC) and Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) guidelines against the spread of, and community transmission of COVID-19. GOALPrime will ensure zero sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) via training and compliance of all staff, volunteers, contractors to GPON's PSEA Policy and code of conduct and also ensure that defaulters are held accountable via GPON's Investigation mechanism

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime will establish or strengthen new or existing feedback and complaints mechanisms in target LGAs. GOALPrime will hold bi-monthl feedback focus group discussions with girls and boys including adolescents’ beneficiaries and their caregivers to understand the project's impact on them and also receive suggestions from them on areas they want project improvement. The Focus Group discussions will however be in compliance with the COVID-19 regulations of maintaining social distancing and the use of face masks. GOALPrime will also carry out at least two child-friendly surveys –one at the beginning of the project and another one at the end of the project to collect perceptions and feedback from children that should guide project activities and strategy improvement. In addition, GOALPrime will still have a complaint and suggestion box kept at strategic positions within the project environment for beneficiaries and their parents to drop their complaints if they feel more comfortable with making anonymous complaints and also their suggestions on how they want the project to serve them better. GOALPrime will provide feedback of complaints to the beneficiaries, parents, and other relevant community stakeholders as soon as complaints are received and reviewed. There are suggestions also will be taken into consideration in the review of Programme approach. Effective and timely actions shall be taken to address concerns received through our established feedback mechanism. However, lessons learned from past projects are. that complaints received via the complaint box mechanism are mostly not related to the project being implemented. To address this, GOALPrime will intensify sensitization of the project to both the target beneficiaries and the community people so that they understand the expected project outcomes and make relevant complaints related to the project.
