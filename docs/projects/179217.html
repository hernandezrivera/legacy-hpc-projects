Project Summary

The project is designed for promoting cohesion and recovery by reducing shelter vulnerabilities of residents, and IDPs for camps by reducing the impact of the negative effect of fire incidents for the IDP's living in informal settlements in Aleppo and Idleb governorate.
35000 IDPs (7500 Men, 8000 Women, 9500 Boys, and 10000 girls ) will be benefitted from the proposed fire response intervention in the 50 targeted camps, in addition to 100 IDPs will be benefitted from the technical capacity building for fire prevention.
All the IDPs will also be benefitted from the formed emergency intervention groups.
The project will cover the enhancement of the capacity of the camps' representatives in the targeted informal settlements to prevent, mitigate and respond to the fire incidents by conducting FGDs and awareness-raising sessions with the camp management and beneficiaries taking into practice and account the COVID-19 preventive measures during the awareness-raising sessions and FGD's along with developing fire warden systems in the targeted camps. Additionally, the project will ensure the provision of fire extinguishers and fire blankets to enhance the capacity of the people on firefighting by providing them with the needed materials in the targeted informal settlements in case of any fire incident occurring by Constructing of fire units in camps and equipping them with recommended tools and equipment by Fire TF.
The main activities:
-Forming Fire Warden Representatives committees FWRs with female representatives.
- Provide capacity building and ToT for community leaders and committee members for fire prevention.
- Establishing 50 equipped fire points and identifying the emergency procedures for any possible incident with setting up a fire response.
- Distributing fire blankets
-Conducting 100 fire prevention training sessions.
- Awareness-raising campaign for fire prevention for 35,000 IDPs in the camps through training for HHs and camps committee taking into account all the CCCM, SNFI and WASH clusters recommendations to ensuring physical distancing and COVID-19 related measures.
Regarding vulnerability criteria, the activities will be for all the IDPs inside the targeted camps, however, the selection criteria for the committees will be focused on: (after coordination with the CCCM cluster)
1- Community acceptance from the sector in the camp.
2- Age between 25 to 45 years.
3- The ability to act as a focal point for any issue related to the camp and assist in the mediation of disputes.
4- The ability to liaise between IDPs and humanitarian service providers;
5- Ensure effective information dissemination among site residents and empowers IDPs and encourages participation in decision-making.
All the mentioned activities are a top priority under CCCM. Forming FWRs committees, and establishing equipped fire points with Awareness-raising will be prioritized if HRP won't be fully funded.

4 - Needs

According to CCCM reports, More than 2.7 million are Internally Displaced People (IDPs), many of whom are living in over-crowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone). Women and children represent 80 percent of this caseload while more than 22,000 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs. This must also be considered against the lingering effects of the massive displacement of nearly 1 million people in early 2020. ATAA conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates across 65 villages and camps across 178 camps administratively belongs to 41 villages between 14th September and 9th October/2020. Based on the main findings of these assessments, - All of the target camps are random and heavily condensed, moreover, 6 out of 14 target camps have reported fire incidents previously, taking into consideration that none of them have firefighting equipment nor centers, or wardens for firefighting. Consequently, provide them with equipment, training, and establish a firefighting mechanism is of high priority.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

This project was designed in a manner that enables all gender and age groups, in addition to PwD to access the project services. ATAA will reflect the age and gender of Project beneficiaries in the beneficiary lists. A referral system will be in place in coordination with Protection Cluster to refer any GBV case and provide needed support. Moreover, a feedback and complaint mechanism (complaint boxes, WA number, email) will be established and shared with all Project beneficiaries to ensure ATAA can receive any Project-related complaint, including PSEA and CSG related complaints. ATAA will train all project staff on its protection policy, including the child safeguarding policy and the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse policy, and when ATAA will conduct the training to the camp managers, it will include PSEA training and CSG. ATAA also will raise the awareness about PSEA, in coordination with PSEA Network as Ataa is an active member in PSEA Network, led by IOM. - ATAA will mainstream PSEA policy including training of ATAA`s personnel in addition to signing ATAA protection policy and COC by all of ATAA personnel, volunteers, partners, suppliers, services providers, and all relevant persons. - Activating the complaint mechanism including protection incidents and penalty procedures. - ATAA will assign a PSEA focal point to ensure the implementation of all protection principals, prevention any abuse, and to conduct the protection training and awareness-raising sessions related PSEA and CSG topics, with coordination and supervising of ATAA Protection Program.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Mitigations: 1- Overlapping, losing some locations due to other NGOs intervention, loss of access. - will perform multilevel coordination (Cluster, Field) - coordinated with all the stakeholder during the design phase 2- Risks related to HLP and propriety conflicts - Ensure validation of the documents during the project - communicate clearly with all the stakeholders - Involved the camps management committees, KIs in the process in case of any conflict 3- Because of the conditions on the ground that may force some beneficiaries to displace. - ATAA will be monitoring all updates on the ground and if we noticed that any of the beneficiaries displaced we will change the selected beneficiaries and choose other beneficiaries who meet the criteria in the same location. 4: Increased number of cases infected with COVID - Training ATAA teams about best practices including social distance, sterilization and wearing face masks. - Providing teams with PPE kits (gloves, sterilization and face masks). - Printing awareness posters on food baskets about COVID-19 and best healthy practices to avoid it. - ATAA distribution teams will organize beneficiaries and make social distance between them during commodities distribution. 5- Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to sexual exploitation and abuse, and reporting via established reporting mechanisms of any concerns or suspicions regarding sexual exploitation and abuse, and investigation if necessary (hotline). A female employee will be assigned as a focal point
