Project Summary

Rationale and Justification for Al lait Intervention :Since May 2016, Alliet locality has seen a steady influx of South Sudanese Refugees, fleeing to Sudan from Northern Bar Ghazal and transiting the border states of East Darfur and West Kordofan to settle in North Darfur. South Sudanese men have traditionally travelled to the locality to work on local farms during the rainy season and after completion of harvest they return to South Sudan. This situation continued following the separation of South Sudan, however, since May 2016, local NGOs operating in the area and community leaders reported that thousands of South Sudanese families were arriving in the locality seeking asylum as a result of food insecurity and armed conflict in their home areas.
However humanitarian access of Alliet was denied until February 2017 when a first interagency mission led by UNHCR was approved to assess the humanitarian situation.
SSR population figures remain contested with considerable fluctuation rates. In February 2017 19,531 South Sudanese refugees (school-aged population 6,077) were registered in 11 communities in addition to 7,554 IDPs (school-aged population 2,643) that arrived due to inter-communal fighting between farmers and nomads (IOM registration Feb 2017). In January 2018, UNHCR conducted a second registration exercise focusing on South Sudanese refugees in both Alliet locality and El Fasher Town. 11,229 refugees were registered with 929 individuals settled in El Fasher town and 10,300 individuals in Alliet locality. UNHCR estimates this represents 82% of the actual SSR population. These figures show a significant decrease from the 2017 population which can be explained by the continuous population movement related to the economic agricultural-focussed livelihoods as well as the hesitation of SSR to be registered formally. The population in need of humanitarian and livelihood support remains significant: According to UNHCR data, 20% of the SSR (or 2,246) are primary school-aged children aged 6-13 and 6% (or 662) are children of pre-primary age 4-5. Out of these only 24% (700 children) are currently enrolled in education.
South Sudanese refugees and newly arrival IDPs are welcomed by the host communities and are sharing resources in Allait and El Fasher, however facilities and services are overstretched and require urgent multi-sector interventions including education. The SSR in El Fasher and Alliet live formally out-of-camp, however the land provided to them by the communities are commonly gathering sites on the fringes of villages, which are largely un-serviced and in considerable distance to village centers/ access to services (i.e. water sources, schools). Due to its remote location and access issues, Alliet locality has received limited support from the humanitarian and development community in recent years. The host community is vulnerable and in need of assistance in service provision across all sectors, including education. The majority of the host communities are farmers cultivating cash crops such as groundnut, sesame and practice livestock rearing (cattle and sheep).

2 - Activities / Outputs

Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for the most vulnerable children (pre-school and primary school age children and adolescents) affected by new emergencies and ensure continuity of relevant quality education during sudden onset and aftermath of emergencies.
