Project Summary

AVSI's 2023/2024 intervention utilizes an integrated approach to address the food security and livelihoods needs of the population in Eastern Equatoria State (EES) (Ikwoto, Torit, Lopa Lafon, Magwi, Kapoeta East, South and North) and Lakes State (LS) (Cueibet, Yirol West, Wulu and Rumbek North) and CES (Juba and Yei). The overall objective of the interventions will be to reduce food insecurity of the selected areas sustainably, having as main targets food insecure communities classified in IPC 5, 4 and 3 with special attention to single mothers, returnees and vulnerable households. FSL service delivery will integrate emergency to reliance interventions, aiming to strengthen the productive bases of agricultural communities to enhance overall production. At the same time, targeting the most vulnerable by integrating them into active roles within more developed groups and production systems. This model also uses the “model-farmers” approach, where successful local farmers set the example for other community members. Activities will target both the local institutions (with a strong component of capacity building and awareness) and the local communities (with direct support, capacity building and accessto improved agricultural inputs and services). At the institutional level (county and local authorities), the interventions will focus on creating and strengthening monitoring systems in the fields of livestock (disease early detection and outbreak prevention) and agriculture. IDPs/returnees and ‘host’ communities are both considered in order to avoid conflict. For the former, activities will focus on their integration/interaction through agriculture activities, enhanced access to quality and improved agricultural inputs (seeds and tool kits) and general services (health services, accessibility to water, school fees). For the second group, a wide range of activities will be implemented including capacity building in agriculture using the Farmers Field School (FFS) approach. For both, the diversification of livelihoods activities will be supported (eg. fish farming, bee-keeping, vegetable production, agro-forestry etc). Special attention will be given to climate change adaptation and smart agriculture practices with the aim of fostering a sustainable increment of production with constant attention to preservation of natural resources and restoration of lost resources. With the lessons learnt from compounding effect of COVID 19 pandemic to already existing challenges, several revised/new guidelines and approaches shall be adhered and implemented in 2024 to ensure continuity of equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services in the context of COVID -19. The project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programming with other relevant sectors such as nutrition, WASH, health, protection and education through synergies and cross-cutting activities (i.e.; school gardens, mother gardens, water points creation in schools and nutrition centres, GBV prevention and response) that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to multi-sectoral basic service which will further reduce food insecurity and increase resilience. Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring that programming of FSL services approach is community/people centred in such a way that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation to ensure that the intervention is responsive enough to the arrays of the changing needs.

1. Needs

As per 2023 HNO, 9.4 million people requires humanitarian assistance in 2023: 76% of South Sudan's population, an increase of 500,000 people from 2022. The last IPC updated to November 2023, projects that 7.1 million will face severe acute food insecurity – at the Crisis level (IPC 3) or higher during the lean season April-July 2024. Of the 7.1 million, 2.34 million people will face Emergency (IPC 4) acute food insecurity, and 79,000 will be in Catastrophe (IPC 5) acute food insecurity in Jonglei and Unity States. The rest, 4.8 million people, will face Crisis (IPC 3) conditions. The projected 9.4 million people in need for 2023 include 1.9 million IDPs, 1.4 million returnees, 5.8 million host community and 337,000 refugees. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. Nearly 15 per cent of the total people in need are PWD.The inter-sectoral analysis conducted using cluster people in need (PiN) revealed that there will be 7.6 million people experiencing extreme need in 66 out of 78 counties and 1.7 million people in severe need in 10 out of 78 counties. Climate change, conflict and economic shocks as well as other household-level stressors, like gender-based violence (GBV) and poor WASH services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in South Sudan. The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding has contributed to the destruction of, and damage to water facilities, increasing the likelihood of water-borne diseases and intensifying the existing vulnerabilities of the affected people, which include high poverty rates, widespread displacement and limited access to basic services such as health, nutrition and education.Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.17 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence is projected to be above 15% in 59 counties in 2023.It is evidenced in AVSI 2023 Multisector survey Conducted in Kauto Kapoeta East County that 48% HHS livelihood’s condition had worsened over the last year due to loss of crops/harvest(31%) and loss of livestock(24%),13% HHs face severe hunger, 17% is experiencing moderate hunger, Livestock disease (68%),human sickness (61%) high commodity prices of food items (42%),64% HHs adopted crisis coping strategies, only 4% HHs have an acceptable FCS, while the greatest majority (71%) has a poor one.The current economic crisis is impacting people’s ability to access markets (HNO 2023), while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services. An increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Lakes State which will likely continue to suffer disruption of livelihoods and trade and increment in humanitarian needs in 2023. Access to justice and the rule of law are limited for many people who experience crimes and violations. Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a threat; women and girls are exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities, which is further exacerbated due to flooding that limit livelihood opportunities, for instance collection of firewood and charcoal production. Feeling unsafe, some women and girls avoid areas such as water points, latrines, distribution areas, markets and firewood gathering sites. In South Sudan, where 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry, climate shocks are likely to lead to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the availability of farmers’ land. This will also lead to a disruption of the traditional seasonal migration routes, less land will be available for cattle grazing, by increasing the risk of conflict in affected areas.

2. Project activities

CO1:Food distribution targeting especially schools in Lakes State and Eastern Equatoria.ACL activities, by using Food For Assets and Cash for Work (i.e.; rehabilitation of roads, drilling boreholes)CO2: : Distribution of main season agricultural seeds (vegetable, crop and fishing) and dry season response kits (veg and fishing)The distribution will target vulnerable beneficiaries HHs; IDPs, returnees and Host community. Immediately after transportation of inputs to the project site, beneficiaries’ registration undergoes a process of identification with involvement of department line government officials (i.e.; CAD, RRC, Agriculture Payam extension workers and local authorities) for transparency and accountability to the community. Provision of technical guidance to beneficiaries on vegetable/crop production through setting of demonstration plots at Payam level or OTP for nutrition sensitive agriculture (kitchen gardens). Demo to be located at a primary road or health facilities to act as a showcase to the communities. Demo will be accompanied by awareness and Social Behavioral Change Communication.Training of beneficiaries both males and females as TOTs who shall then act as extension works/community agriculture/fisher folk facilitators within the community on Climate Smart Agriculture and on the use of the kits, fish handling processing, preservation and marketing. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted by the M&E team two weeks after distribution of inputs to ensure inputs are physically in good conditions. Post planting will be done one month after distribution by the partner and the M&E team to make sure that the inputs are use appropriately by the beneficiaries in the various locations. Livestock treatment and vaccination: livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonosis (i.e.; vaccination of animals in the four counties of Torit, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta South and Ikwoto; Conducting mass treatment and deworming of livestock during disease out break; Participating in stakeholder workshop with state, county and national veterinarians & other animal health personnel on the project objectives and goals and needs; Awareness campaigns through dramas and radio talk show, community dialogue to educate the local communities on the importance of vaccination and treatment of livestock, create community and livestock owners’ self-alertness for disease early warning campaigns, zoonosis and TADs that includes anthrax, brucellosis, Rift valley fever, Tuberculosis, hemorrhagic septicemia, black quarter; Support in the eradication and control of livestock disease within the Cluster Areas; Training of CAHWs in sampling, testing, cold chain management, and reporting using an approved training curriculum (covering the “One Health” approach) & functional in support of a national network cool chain management at County and Payam level), as well as Training of CAHWs and other treaders for the implementation of cost recovery system, facilitation opening of vet shops by CAHWs at Payam level.CO3:Training on animal traction/ox-plough to willing and selected beneficiaries to improve on the farming technologies, hence extensive farming. use in the appropriately by the beneficiaries in the various locationsVSLA and Market Linkage: through value chain assessment especially in EES and Juba.Natural Resource management and agro-forestry (i.e.; Formation and training of natural resource management committee; Establishment of fruit tree seedling; Conducting community level sensitization on natural resource mapping and utilization with minimal conflict; Encouraging use of improved cooking stove reducing the amount of emission into the atmosphere; Roundtables to discuss on possible resolutions and by-laws and to ensure women empowerment and leadership).
