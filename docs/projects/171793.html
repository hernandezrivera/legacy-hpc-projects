Project Summary

Due to socio-economic inequalities exacerbated by multiple conflicts, famine, and widespread insecurity in Somalia, constant displacement and instability forces more than three million vulnerable boys and girls are out of school due to lack of an enabling learning environment. With one of the lowest primary school enrolment rates in the world, girls are at a greater risk of being deprived of their right to education due to lack of a positive environment for women, cultural biases towards boys, and the prevalence of GBV in form of psychological, economic and physical abuse for adolescent girls who have a higher school drop out rate. In lieu of proper water and sanitation facilities, adequate teachers, books, and classrooms the quality of education is prone to be poor. These hardships are worse for children with disabilities who also have to maneuver physical challenges in their pursuit of education. The right to education can be realised by improving these children’s access to quality learning and education-related services and facilities to allow both boys and girls between 5 to 18 years old (with special emphasis on the inclusion of children with disabilities and girls), to ensure increased gender-equal school completion.
This proposed intervention is designed based on critical education needs in Somalia. The program seeks to build on existing knowledge of the targeted communities and the capacity of ministry officials with the aim of achieving increased access and retention of out of school and children from vulnerable households. The proposed activities will focus on strengthening education in Somalia and Somaliland regions in an attempt to help out of school children and those who are economically disadvantaged.
The project aims to ensure a safe and protective learning environment with good quality teaching and increase access and retention of crisis-affected and IDP children through an integrated package of school-based emergency assistance.
The project sought to increase access and retention to education and will include establishment or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces with gender-friendly and inclusive WASH facilities ((gender-sensitive latrines, water points, girl-friendly spaces), provision of school furniture, teacher incentives, and teacher trainings in pedagogical skills, handling special education needs children and psychosocial support, inclusive, protective and socio-emotional teaching and learning, as well as training of Community Education Committees in school management and school safety and preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement and disability through advocating for the inclusion of IDPs and people living with disability in decision-making processes and removing barriers associated with accessing education services and facilities for children with disabilities.
Additionally, the project will provide awareness-raising and community mobilization. In the targeted locations the project will be integrated with other Mercy-USA’s programs implemented. The main objective is to increase school attendance and retention through the provision of quality basic education to emergency and drought-affected school children and support the host community in the targeted regions by providing a safe and supportive learning environment.

Project Activities

The project aims to ensure emergency and crisis-affected children and youth have access to safe and protecting learning environment. This will be achieved by addressing barriers to quality education which includes enhancing the safety of schools and mitigating potential risks by establishing risk mitigation measures, mapping of school facilities to identify any barriers to safe access, and training of stakeholders, including Community Education Committees, teachers and Ministry of education officials.Mercy USA will conduct community mobilization to increase community participation in schools and employ these forums to mobilize community leadership and local authorities on their roles and responsibilities to protect and support all children in ensuring safe access to school.The project activities will address the needs of targeted 20 schools which includes support for infrastructural and learning materials and teacher’s incentives. Through the collaboration of all stakeholders including the ministry of education and other NGOs working/supporting education in the target regions. The proposed activities will entail implementing the recruitment of additional teachers with monthly incentives, training of new teachers, training of school management committees, quarterly supportive supervision with officials from the ministry of education. Construction and rehabilitation of learning classrooms, improvement of WASH infrastructure, and distribution of hygiene kits to targeted schools in Somaliland and Galmudug regions. Project activities include:• Community mobilization for child protection, safety, enrolment and retention• Rehabilitation of infrastructure in primary schools• Sanitation facilities support• Connection of water supply system• Provision of teacher’s incentives• Provision of TLM• MHM and distribution of sanitary kits to adolescent girls• Training of teachers and other stakeholders on child protection and school safety

Mitigation Measures

Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA:• Mercy-USA, guided by internal PSEA and Child Safeguarding Policy and international best practices, will safeguard gender justice and equity and promote gender equality. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. • Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. Mercy-USA will have three forms of confidential feedback mechanisms to ensure that all community members are reached, including illiterate people, those who cannot physically travel, and finally, those who do not have resources to feedback. The follow mechanisms will be in place:• Suggestion boxes • Hotline numbers• Focal persons at the community level. • Community mobilization will be intensively carried out to ensure the uptake of the formal feedback mechanisms established.• Mercy-USA will continuously monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of the mechanisms established and make improvements continually. Additionally, to ensure protection and inclusion and mitigate the risks, Mercy-USA will provide schooling close to settlements or in the central areas of camps, provide classes for different age and skill levels, provide appropriate clothing and sanitary supplies to girls so that they can attend school and ensure other obstacles to increasing the enrolment and attendance of girls are addressed.

Please provide further explanation

Mercy-USA will ensure that WASH responses use technologies and approaches compatible with the needs, knowledge, and circumstances of the receiving population, fundamentally addressing critical health hazards. All WASH infrastructure will be climate-resilient. Mercy-USA will protect water and sanitation structures from contamination, and structural damages resulting from floods and groundwater sources are dug deep enough to provide water during the dry seasons. Mercy-USA will ensure that facilities are designed and located so that they are robust to climate uncertainties alongside other pressures on resources, systems, and services.During project implementation, focus group discussions prioritize the disabled, and other minorities to ensure the needs of all vulnerable groups are being addressed during program implementation. Separate toilets and handwashing stations will be provided for boys and girls. Doors to latrines can be locked from the inside. The project will increase the protection of women and girls from abuse by ensuring that sanitation facilities will be located in safe and easily accessible places. The project will also ensure that all facilities such as latrines are designed with adequate private space for women and girls to clean and dispose of sanitary materials with dignity. For enhanced protection, teachers will be trained to detect, respond to, and refer cases of violence and abuse, and exploitation of boys and girls.
