Project Summary

Humane Aid for Community Organisation (HACO) works with vulnerable communities to alleviate suffering caused by multiple triggers such as displacement, war, disease, amongst others. HACO works in the locations of Unity, Lakes and Western Equatoria with bases in the counties of Rubkona, Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Mayom and Panijiar (Unity State), Yirol East (Lakes) and Yambio, Tombura and Maridi (Western Equatoria). Dire WASH needs have been observed in the locations targeted, mainly as a result of displacements, communal violence and lack of WASH infrastructure in majority of the locations. The project seeks to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees. With protection at centrality of the implementation, WASH related GBV will be mitigated through WASH specific activities tailored for GBV mitigation.
The chronic persistence of malnutrition in the areas targeted has been traced to poor WASH standards, resulting in AWDs. The project will further provide WASH in nutrition minimum package of intervention for famine prevention, mainly at the OTP/SC.
Provision of WASH services as a public health strategy to avoid occurrence of epidemics, or at the occurrence of epidemics to avoid spread is planned. The areas targeted are all disease prone, having experienced previous outbreaks of cholera, and some neighbouring Ebola stricken Democratic Republic of Congo.
The target group consists of IDPs, vulnerable host communities and returnees. A total of 12,120 people are targeted with SPHERE standard WASH services, 48% of these female. 60% of the beneficiaries planned for are from Unity State, 30% in WES and 10% in Lakes. 56% of these are IDPs, 32% host communities, 12% returnees and other groups. 57% of the WASH component shall be centred around safe and clean water supply at household, community and institutional level, 43% on Sanitation with all beneficiaries receiving support in hygiene promotion.

Needs

Parts of Unity and Western Equatoria State state bore the brunt of the conflict that has spanned over 5 years, majorly affecting women and children. Chronic WASH needs have emerged that despite efforts to alleviate, have been exacerbated by multiple displacements, erratic weather patterns, economic hardships and food insecurity. The counties of Rubkona, Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Mayom and Panyijiar jointly host populations of over 500,000 people of which more than 50% are IDPs. The similar trend is observed in Yirol East, prone to inter communal clashes in quest for livelihoods. Pockets of IDPs and communities in need have been identified in Yambio, Maridi and Tombura, communities at risk of epidemic outbreaks due to poor WASH standards. WASH cluster estimates place the number of persons in need of WASH services in the locations targeted at more than 400,000 (57% female). Multiple displacements of populations, coupled with food insecurity, malnutrition, and disease outbreaks have all exacerbated the already dire humanitarian situation in parts of Unity, Lakes and WES states majorly affecting women and children, who prior to the current situation where already living below globally accepted thresholds in WASH, Health, Nutrition and Food Security. There’s been a destruction of WASH infrastructure, and a tendency of communities to resort to unsafe WASH practices thereby increasing likelihood of water related diseases. Despite humanitarian interventions, recent assessment reports (IRNAs, REACH Assesments) from various targeted locations especially in Unity and Lakes indicate dire WASH, FSL, Health and Nutrition needs especially amongst women and children who bear the rigor of collecting water from far locations due to insufficient water points in the locations targeted. Majority of the populations settled in various locations resort to usually nearby unsafe stagnant surface water, to meet their WASH needs. The insufficiency of water points was further exacerbated by increased concentration of IDPs in safe havens, deliberate destruction of water points by armed forces, breakdown due to over usage and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitations and repairs. Increased caseloads have further strained available sanitation and hygiene facilities, with majority of these requiring rehabilitation and/or upgrade. Unsafe sanitation and hygiene habits are a common practice with open defecation widely visible in areas where large groups of IDPs are concentrated. Protection risks have been increased to girls and women who have to stray into nearby bushes due to lack of gender appropriate facilities. (Protection Cluster, 2018)Insufficient WASH facilities have played a big role in the rise of water related and diarrheal diseases, which have undermined efforts to reduce levels of malnutrition that are far beyond the WHO thresholds of GAM in Unity and Lakes States. This has had a contributory effect on food insecurity in these areas, that are expected to deteriorate into Emergency phase according to the latest IPC projection data. Significant disruptions of markets and high inflation rates in Unity, Lakes and WES has further lessened opportunities of already vulnerable communities to access basic WASH supplies such as soap, PuR tablets, Hygiene Kits amongst others. Provision of emergency WASH services will reduce likelihood of deaths due to water related diseases.

Activities

The Following activities are planned in the locations of Rubkona, Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Mayom Panyijiar, Yirol East, Yambio, Tombura and Maridi in line with the collective WASH cluster strategy. WASH activities for IDPs/host communities and returnees • Provision of timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations. • Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH- related diseases.• Enabling of vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services WASH activities to Mitigate WASH- related GBV • Gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers • Provision of safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis • Provision of safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention interventions. • Provision of safe drinking water and nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM. • Provision of WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC till the household level • Ensuring smallstock water requirement and promote appropriate hygiene practices to prevent smallstock open defecation at household level WASH in Health for outbreak preparedness, response and risk reduction. • Provision of a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad- hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community • Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

HACO engaged communities with this potential responses. HACO adheres to community participation and involving communities before, during and after the project has been successful in the past. The beneficiaries will be informed again during the project inception or launch. Past experience shows that providing beneficiaries with information is very healthy and it helps in tailoring community driven project and encourages sense of ownership and sustainability. Beneficiaries mentioned the type of services and assistance that they need. Non- discriminatory and inclusive approaches are critical to successful implementation and exit.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are designed in gender and age sensitivity manners taking into consideration cultural and community issues. Some messages will be translated into local languages and some community leaders have expressed willingness to assist with the translation as part of the local contribution.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

GBV mainstreaming is a crosscutting component of all our projects and these ones are no exception. PSEA is global harassment which must be stopped by having policies and procedures in place and followed by all. Community based approach to minimize protection from sexual exploitation and abuse will be in place. Having Community PSEA voluntary who are tasked to monitoring and reporting PSEA concerns with evidence to the relevant local bodies entrusted with these roles. HACO has zero tolerance on PSEA and it is incorporated into HACO Code of Conduct signed by all staff members. Key messages will be developed about PSEA and intensive awareness raising about PSEA will running throughout the project cycle.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

All the stakeholders were consultations as part of the coordination efforts to avoid duplication of efforts. Women groups from both IDPs, Returnees, and host community were involved and their views and needs taken into programming. Local authority, youth leaders, people with special needs, religious leaders and partners were heavily consulted to see how the work they do complement each other.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Complaint desks will be established to receive constructive feedback from the beneficiaries. HACO will ensure they are complaint focal point in each boma or payam at least one female and one male who will make sure that beneficiaries complaints are listened and addressed in a timely and respectful manner. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to file their complaints without fear and they have right to register their complaints and this helps improve service delivery in their interest.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community engagement is a centered approach used by HACO. Community participation and engagement determine the success of a project. Community will be interviewed to determine the overall success of the project. All categories of the beneficiaries will be included into the monitoring and evaluation of the project through KIIs, FGDs and interviews.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project takes account of protection risks especially in terms of differential protection needs of the vulnerable population for example providing gender segregated toilets in locations were there will be need for construction of toilets.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project fully understands the tendency among the population to deny services to other sections of the community. to forestall this; WASH services will be rendered equitably to communities that sometimes fail to agree on sharing services or resources. Affirmative action will be applied so as to uplift the weaker gender.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will address these inequalities by equitably providing WASH services to communities based on observed deprivations within and between communities. In other words the spread of services will be focused on taking care of the neglected as well as those that are deprived. A committee comprising of representation of all genders will be formed to oversee the allocation of the resources

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities seek to address inequalities like GBV by providing gender aggregated toilets, providing water points at nearby locations especially among communities where they have to travel long distances thereby making them vulnerable to GBV

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The monitoring of the project will assess performance basing on the number of beneficiaries of the different services like the number of those reached with hygiene messages, the number of those receiving water services or those getting support for MAM and SAM.Data collection will be done per activity so as to monitor attendance of the beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring does measure the safety of the beneficiaries in that it takes account of the different safety needs of the categories of the beneficiaries; that is to say for women and girls it takes care of their safety needs. for communities that are not friendly to one another, services are provided equitably and if difficult, community engagement mechanisms are in place to foster peaceful coexistence within or between communities.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The beneficiary dignity is at the center of this project in that the project addresses needs of the different categories of the population; this with regard to their vulnerabilities and addresses them for example those being deprived before for services will be provided with WASH services. While providing these services; there is need to respect the dignity of those for whom services are being provided and not let them feel bad about their situation.
