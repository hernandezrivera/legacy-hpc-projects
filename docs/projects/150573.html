Project Summary

The overall objective of the project over 2 years is to respond to the immediate shelter needs of vulnerable populations in E. Jerusalem. This response will be through self-help approach for the most vulnerable families to provide shelter repairs and transitional shelter solutions close to existing homes, in order to: 1) Promote self-reliance, maintain, and support most vulnerable families who live with the effect of violence, coercion, and deprivation with shelter related solutions. 2) Enhancing the protection and reducing the likelihood of displacement or forced eviction. 3) Contributing in solving the problem of overcrowding houses by creating new spaces through rearrangement to ensure privacy and dignity 4) Reduce the cost of living by adopting green approach. 5) Extend the life span of existing shelters; in sight of the high cost of the issuance of building permits in EJ. Thus, complying with the second HRP strategic objectives “SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services”. And complying with the shelter cluster strategic objectives “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity” and “Capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased”.
This project comply with PHC’s Rehabilitation Program objectives, aiming at contributing of elimination some poverty effects on vulnerable women HH’s, improving the quality of the proper housing stock, reducing land designation for housing and infrastructure by restoring the existing housing stock and improving the social, economic, and health conditions for the beneficiaries.
The project will provide urgent housing rehabilitation to 50 FHH or vulnerable families in E. Jerusalem whose shelter is unfit for weather conditions, overcrowded, and/or unsafe. In the first year, PHC will commence to respond to 25 vulnerable families in E. Jerusalem as described hereafter.

Needs

The Israeli government and municipal policies in East Jerusalem have negatively affected Palestinian communities especially women’ and children’ lives and rights. These policies significantly increase the humanitarian vulnerability of Palestinians, and affect their residency status, economic situation, access to education, health services, and their ability to plan and develop their communities. The lack of planning and the high cost of issuance of building permits (370 permits in average per year while an annual housing demand is around 1,850 units/year) increases the likelihood of displacement and forced eviction of Palestinians. Displaced women and girls lose the space where they enjoy privacy, autonomy, social networks and the center of their lives and are negatively affected by the occupation-related political, social and economic oppression. Safety, security risks and threats, including the violence perpetrated against women, the restrictions imposed on their freedom of movement, and the family forced displacement, are direct consequences of the Israeli occupation policies and measures, as perpetrated by the Israeli military, settlers and systematic discrimination. These threats are reflected as domestic and social violence for females, and military violence for males. In addition to the declining economic conditions across Palestine led to a situation of a chronic shortfall of housing, and an inability of vulnerable families to maintain their current housing units.According to 2018 HNO: At least 1.9 million Palestinians experience, or are at risk of, conflict and violence, displacement and denial of access to livelihoods, among other threats. The most vulnerable Palestinians are currently denied or restricted in their access to essential services. Elderly women are more vulnerable to poverty, disease, and have limited access to services. There is no social security system in place to ensure a decent life for the elderly and to provide the different life requirements, such as housing, healthcare, income, disability needs. Palestinians are expected to continue to depend on their children in their old age instead of social security, and the burden of the career role is often placed on the shoulders of women. Disability remains a critical source of vulnerability, that can lead to gender based discrimination. Women and girls with disabilities suffer from amplified forms of GBV, and have limited access to services.In August 2018, PHC updated its assessment that was divided into 1) technical; in sight of minimum shelter standards 2) social; in sight of separate individual interviews with women, men, boys, girls and with different age groups. This assessment concluded 50 (50% FHH) vulnerable families in EJ in urgent need, this update assigned the most needy 25 (13 FHH) families that shall be urgently targeted in 2019. Through the selection criteria, PHC Grading System identifies the neediest beneficiaries, this system assigns that divorced and widowed women or FHH have high weight that leads to increase their benefit chances in 2019. Furthermore, this system designed to respond to different segments of the beneficiaries (women, men, boys, girls & elderly) and their different needs.Findings of the assessment in EJ mainly were:1.Inadequate housing conditions increase the likelihood of displacement from EJ to outside the city, in particular for FHH’s families. 2.Some families lack the safety, health and comfort requirements due to overcrowded housing units and poor living conditions that affect family members privacy and dignity and children’s welfare that resulting in intergenerational transmission of social inequality. 3.Families invests all spaces in the house especially in the old city due to the limitation and costly of building permits and restrictions, land confiscation.4. Weak capacities for self protection and limitation of doing their basic repair works themselves.

Activities

PHC’s methodology ensures the participation of different gender/age groups in the project cycle; starting with the needs assessments, to implementation, monitoring & evaluation. PHC will develop a proactive approach to emergency preparedness, in order to ensure early action is taken when required. This approach includes risk monitoring, establishment of coordination & management arrangements & preparing for joint needs assessments along with the Palestinian official bodies especially MoSD, CBO’s. The official bodies will provide and support services inside the community using available resources, voluntary and skills, and will manage adverse conditions that potentially could lead to emergency, this will lead to increase beneficiaries coping capacity and contributing to building resilience.PHC aims to avoid, minimize and mitigate the risks associated the delivery of this project, by identifying risks of the targeted beneficiaries in terms of safety, dignity, do no harm by mitigating physical and sociological threats through prioritizing intervention to provide the most appropriate assistance to beneficiaries without discrimination or exposing them to further risk. PHC approach is also guided by Meaningful access related to the provision of its assistance (i.e. handicapped, elderly). –Accountability: maximizing transparency and setup inclusive and accessible feedback & complaint mechanism. Beneficiaries’ participation and empowerment: ensuring that the beneficiaries are actively involved & participated in all the process; decision-making, implementation, monitoring, evaluation & management. They are the key element to analysis their needs and the means of solving their problems. Since the cost of the building permits is very high, the project will contribute to reduce the likelihood of displacement of vulnerable families outside the wall through upgrading existing unsuitable shelters to avoid replacement of place of residence outside the wall.O1. 25 shelters of FHH’s and vulnerable families in EJ are made adequate for living.1. Data collection & selection of beneficiaries: The project Committees will list the eligible cases. Through this process, gender has been taken into consideration where PHC pays special attention to vulnerable FHH i.e. widowed or divorced besides the main target groups that include individuals with disabilities, children & the elderly as mentioned in the needs section. 2.The project team will revisit 25 assessed vulnerable families to define the rehabilitation budget for each beneficiary, & to prepare an accurate BoQ’s for each shelter.3.The project team will prepare the necessary technical work documents, specifications and BoQ’s. 4. PHC will sign agreements with beneficiaries & will determine the amount of each grant according to activity 2 with an average amount of $7,200 taking into consideration that 10% of the grant amount will be a participation of beneficiaries either financial or physical labor contribution.5. PHC will determine the number of payments for each beneficiary according to their grant. The payments will be paid according to each subproject progress.6. Work in each shelter will be monitored & supervised on daily basis; the final payment will be conditional by the completion of all works.7. M&E: Please see the indicators sectionO2. Community capacity building & awareness raising for self recovery.A1. The project team through technical workshops will assist 25 beneficiaries in: negotiation process with workers & contractors, in getting voluntary works from the local community. In addition, the social workshops are tailored with discussions concerning their living standards, it will help people to maintain their dignity, & it will ensure better life for elderly, less domestic and social violence for women, less military violence for men, & more privacy for girls & boys. Next year, PHC will target another 25 vulnerable families with the same mentioned activities and outcomes

Indicators

PHC aim to achieve the optimum results regarding beneficiaries by defining the aptness support for emergency vulnerable families with a reasonable cost. Previous phases of the program showed that small grants make a significant difference in the lives of vulnerable families at several levels:At the community level, PHC formed the Advisory Committee, which consists of community female and male representatives who acquaint with the areas of the target group and familiar with their needs & challenges; to support the program implementation and participate in the selection of the beneficiaries according to housing conditions & socio economic situation of families.PHC involves beneficiaries in the different stages of the project cycle. One of the assessment criteria is to involve the families in determining their needs according to their gender & age (vulnerable FHH’s, the elderly, etc) or individuals with special needs. At the beginning, kick off workshops with beneficiaries will be conducted to discuss the project components & to take into consideration any recommendations from beneficiaries’ side in project design & implementation. During the project, another workshops will be conducted to gather their feedback, complaints & lessons learned in order to take the suitable action accordingly. At monitoring and evaluation level, PHC will monitor the progress of works on daily basis. Monitoring process for this project will be based on regular field visits (PHC, SC, Donor), and periodic reporting. As a result of the field visits reports the Project Committee will share feedback and information concerning challenges and issues raised on site to partners. PHC always aims to provide assistance in ways that are supportive of recovery and long term development, and do its best to “do no harm” by concealing them from additional riskEvaluation process will be based on a questionnaire, financial audit, meetings and quality assurance process. It is worth to note that the questionnaire is a tool designed and used in the recent phases of the program to indicate the impact on the living conditions of the most vulnerable families, the degree of safety and adequacy, quality of work, and the progress achieved. And in order to assess the changes that were attributed to the project intervention, as well as to examine whether targets have been achieved. Knowing that one of the Project committee mandate is to implement and monitor the project to be gender balanced. Through the evaluation process, the changes occurred in terms of safety, dignity & integrity will be measured separately for women, men, girls and boys. According to this project 154 are direct beneficiaries; 86 females, 68 males, 60 Children, 14 elderly.At the Cluster level, PHC as the only SC member who worked in EJ; will implement this project to ensure that the SC objectives to be achieved in coordination with the cluster.PHC pays special attention on the environmental issues; neutral impact on environment with mitigation or enhancement, to achieve this PHC improved technical practices for basic repairs, resulting in more energy efficient and environmentally friendly living environments. PHC will take into consideration measures to promote more environmental friendly housing, cost and energy efficient building practices.To achieve this, the indicators of the project will be:Shelter upgrading through providing cash or materials assistance & technical support* 25 HH's assisted to upgrade substandard housing unit in EJ in 2019, 25 in 2020Provision of winterization assistance to vulnerable households at risk of exposure: * 13 HH's provided with climate protection assistanceCommunity capacity building and awareness raising:* 25 HH's supported with training and tools for self recovery or protection measuresFamilies under protection concerns or at risk of displacement provided with protective shelter materials: * 3 HH assisted with shelter protective materials.
