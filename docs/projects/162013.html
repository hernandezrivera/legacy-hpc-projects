Project Summary

"WaSH project is continue to ensure inclusive and equitable provision of life saving water and sanitation services building on infrastructure already constructed in the previous phase in 2019 (in the Camp no 12,13,14 and 15) and strengthening behaviour change communication (BCC) by encouraging community-based collective measures. The previous phase of the intervention has contributed to an increase in the appropriate usage of hand washing. Almost 79% of the respondents from the end of project evaluation reported that hand washing points installed under the project were utilized for the purpose of hygiene maintenance. In terms of safe and inclusive access to water, the water point accessibility for PWD, pregnant women, adolescent girls and aged persons has been enhanced. The increase in ease of accessibility for pregnant women rose from 6% in baseline to 48%, similarly, for adolescent girls, it improved from 24% to 83%. It was also found that 98% of the latrines constructed were also being used regularly. So maintenance of these essential WaSH facilities and services are vital to reduce the risk of disease outbreak.
Bear in mind the gravity of the issue, CAID will continue to ensure lifesaving water and sanitation services to the Rohingya and host communities in 2020. Focus will be provided to address the key challenges facing WaSH service such as congestion at water points, unequal distribution, inclusive access and protection concerns in bathing cubicles and latrines.

Objectives

1. To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs. 2. To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable sanitation (including household waste) facilities to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times.3. To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviours through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities.

Needs

In the initial emergency phase many agencies in camp 12, 13, 14, 15, 18 & 19 installed emergency and semi-permanent latrines, which means that currently the needs have shifted towards decommissioning, or improvement of these latrines. The decommissioned latrines need to be replaced by new latrines for beneficiaries, accessible for women and some for PWD. Overall, there is huge gap in bathing cubicles for women. There is also a gap in hygiene promotion and BCC awareness. Hygiene practices remain low within the Rohingya community and as host community. According to a survey conducted by WASH Sector follow-up assessment covering the camps there is lack of (43% of households are facing problems accessing or collection water ) for the Rohingya population, which results in bad hygiene practices. In the host community the situation in terms of hygiene practises is not much better. We propose to continue with Hygiene promotion activities and provision top-up or full hygiene promotion kits. Waste management system has been set up but will require scaling up and continuation of current activities as initiated by Christian Aid in camp 15. We propose to create a similar system in camp 14 and camp 13.
