Project Summary

The HNO 2022 estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's p opulation, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. There are increased and new threats of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect among the population due to increase in displacements occasioned by floods and internal conflicts, therefore a critical need for direct Child Protection, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support interventions. War Child Holland has been implementing Child Protection and Mental Health Psychosocial support projects for more than six-years in Jonglei and Upper Nile States targeting Vulnerable community members in Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in camps, PoC and host communities. WCH is an active member of CPSC both at national and state levels and Co-lead of National PSS TWG and Upper Nile State CPAoR co-chair. In that regard, the following proposed activities are based on practical experience and an understanding of specific needs of the communities in the proposed Malakal (Town and POC), Melut, Pigi Canal, Tonga/Panykang, Fashoda and Bor South Counties.
War Child Holland (WCH) proposes to provide practical life saving Child Protection (CP), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services, through direct interventions reaching 26,408 individuals comprising of approximately 18,486 children (11,091 girls: 7,394boys) and 7,922 adults ( 4500 women: 3422 men).This will be accomplished through provision of community based Child Protection CP, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, Case Management and Cash based programming with focus on Child Protection risk prevention, mitigation and response. The proposed response is also fully aligned with Child Protection sub -cluster strategic objectives and selected Child Protection Sub- Cluster (CPSC) indicators for 2023. This project will therefore increase and influence access to Child Protection, Mental Health Psychosocial Support through Focused and Non- Focused MHPSS activities which will aid in inclusion of children with disabilities who are most times left out or discriminated against during activities in schools and communities.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2022, identified, humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement which made it difficult for returns to the country for both IDPs and those who were refugees in the neighboring countries. Persons with highest needs comprise of IDPs, separated and Unaccompanied children (UASC), GBV survivors, children associated with armed groups and forces (CAAFAG) and communities living in counties severely impacted by the protracted crisis. Furthermore, the sub-national violence that escalated in May 2020 caused displacement of about 80,000 people. Jonglei was further affected by the June 2020 violence, which was compounded by the prolonged flooding between August and December 2020 (HNO 2020).In addition, humanitarian access to essential services, including health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, remains a challenge in an already complex context. Between January and December 2021, 591 humanitarian access incidents and impediments were recorded and the same trend continues to be witnessed to date. As a result essential services to most vulnerable groups including access to food are unnecessarily delayed or altogether denied. Perpetual food insecurity further complicates lives of the most vulnerable groups. Consortium assessment (IRNA) conducted in September 2021 in Fashoda for example further highlighted the precarious situation facing children including separation, child marriage, child labor exercabated by severe flooding, food insecurity and increased pressure on diminished household resources. The impact of COVID-19 pandemic also increased to unprecedented proportions the risks to which children, women, girls, families and communities were exposed to Psychological distress, partner violence, family separations, violence against children including child labour and child marriages and psychological distress. Mental health problems including suicidal tendencies have also been reported. Ripples effects of the Covid 19 pandemic in the country continue to be felt among children. A disrupted school cycle contintues to aggravate the already severe mental anguish that children and adolescents are going through.Additional concerns are also faced by children living with disabilities. The six (6) targeted locations of Bor South, Pigi/Canal, Malakal, Melut, Tonga/Panykang, and Fashoda are impacted by all the above mentioned crises and are categorized as Child Protection Severity Level 3. Specific CP and PSS support issues affecting children in the proposed Counties include: - Increased risk of girls and boys to recruitment, exploitation, robbery and street life, sexual violence, school dropout, neglect, child marriage, child labour and mine risks.Existence of large number of children affected by separation and neglect due to population movement and stretched caring capacity of families. Psychological distress and self-harm behaviours among children and caregivers due to exposure to violence, displacements, livelihood disruption continue to be witnessed. • Glaring gaps in CP and PSS support services available in the proposed locations including technical capacity gaps of relevant ministries and community based structures. • Deterioration of traditional support mechanisms for children and other vulnerable community members due to the impacts of conflict, displacement and effects of Covid 19. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. The other main challenge is lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout.

2 - Project activities

War Child Holland has been implementing child protection and Mental Health and Psychosocial support programmes for more than six-years in Jonglei and Upper Nile States, targeting Vulnerable community members in IDP camps, PoC and host communities. WCH is an active member of CPSC both at national and state levels. Referring to such, the following proposed activities are based on practical experience and understanding of specific needs of the communities in the proposed various locations. The following key proposed activities are based on the local community needs identified on the locations.While in Coordination with others, War Child Holland conducted Child Protection risk assessment to map and identified protection concerns and needs in the proposed 6 locations with focus on girls and women and including vulnerable(Persons with disable) and vulnerable old women/menOrganize Project feed backs meetings and project launching meeting with participation of all the key community level actors and stakeholders in respective locations; Develop child protection action plan based Consultation with relevant stakeholders including children and community representatives; Recruit, train and deploy 72 community level CP and PSS facilitators (36 M: 36 F) in 6 project locations;Train, mentor and support 180 Caregivers, community leaders and representatives to strengthen community based CBCPN in 6 target locations; Facilitate child protection and psychosocial support in emergency in relation to capacity development training to 180 community actors(30 per community), social workers and key stakeholders to strengthen child protection and PSS response in the target 6 Counties; With age, gender and disability sensitive CP and MHPSS and PM+ messaging targeting IDPs, returnees and host communities, reach 26,408 individuals comprising of 18,486 children and 7,922 adults through Community awareness raising sessions, Radio talk show and events. Participants will include parents, Techers, caregivers, community leaders and different categories of duty bearers in the 6 target counties of Bor South, Pigi /Canal, Malakal, Melut, Tonga/Panykang & Fashoda.Reach 6,000 children (3000 boys, 3000 girls) through static and mobile CFS based recreational and PSS support services in Bor South, Canal/Pigi, Malakal, Mel (6 counties); Provide Case Management services to 300 vulnerable children (162 boys: 148 girls) in 6 locations; Provide PFA and community based PSS services for children and caregivers in need of additional support; Organize PSS sessions for 2400parents/caregivers (1200men: 1200female) experiencing psychological stress and facing difficulty to provide adequate emotional support to their children; Facilitate development of referral pathway and service coordination mechanisms in 6 Counties;Identify, assess and provide CM support to extremely vulnerable children especially girls including referral to other service providers; Facilitate FTR support and services to identified 60 UASC (32 male:28 female) in 6 target counties; Provide unconditional cash support for 234 extremely vulnerable children and their caregivers (34 male; 200 fem) in the project locations using broader case management vulnerability criteria (HHs with child labour and disability cases, children facing neglect and malnutrition, children in female headed HHs etc); Provide child protection and PSS mainstreaming training and technical support to 14 non-protection actors in the 6 locations; Provide periodic update on grave child rights violations through monitoring and reporting mechanism (MRM); Strengthen feedback/complain mechanism to ensure accountability to affected population including children with disabilities; Conduct monthly monitoring/quarterly and support visit to target communities and organize quarterly project progress review meetings with stakeholders in the 6 Counties; Submit periodic activity progress and budget utilization report using agreed templates.

1. Number of, girls, boys and including children with disabilities provided with focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services: Using individual interviews and FGDs, this indicator will be assessed by measuring psychological wellbeing improvement among children attending PSS sessions. Adherence to the IASC CP and MHPSS in emergency minimum standards will also be assessed. 2. Number of women and men who receive psychosocial support, parenting messaging and participate in parenting sessions:Narrative reports detailing number of parenting sessions held, topics covered and pre-post results of the trainings will be used to measure delivery of the dialogue sessions. At project evaluation, a sample of parents and caregivers who have gone through the dialogue sessions will be interviewed using brief semi-structured questionnaires and other tools. Results will be analyzed especially on their ability to apply the knowledge gained. 3. Number of women, men, boys, girls and including other children with disabilities with protection concerns are supported through case management, referral services and CBA: This indicator measures the extent to which WCH, in collaboration with other actors to provide appropriate case management and referral services to the targeted children and/or caregiver with severe protection concerns – including those living with disabilities (CLWD), identified either through WCHs activities or referred to WCH by other actors. Data sources will include Case Management Plans, Referral Forms, and FTR Forms, CPIMS registration, Monthly Narrative Field Reports, Success stories and Case studies. KIIs results from girls, boys, adolescents and adults including those living with disabilities will be used for data verification. 4. Number of women and men trained on child protection in emergencies (Copied) including case management, MHPSS, FTR CAAFAG, MRE etc. This indicator will measure the extent to which the targeted duty bearers in 4 counties including local government representatives such as the MoGEI, MoGCSW, police officers in charge of children cases have been trained in CP/MHPSS, PFA, FTR, MRE and Covid 19 prevention and response in line with the IASC MHPSS Standards, CP Minimum Standards(CPMS), UNCRC Guiding Principles, and the Covid 19 Risk Communication andCommunity Engagement (RCCE). The indicator will be tracked through attendance registers with data disaggregated by age, sex and disability and by the mix of relevant duty bearers included. Training content and methodologies will be assessed for adherence to the earlier referenced standards and Guiding Principles. Application of the learnings will be verified through KIIs and FGDs. 5. Number of women, men, girls and boys including those living with disabilities reached through awareness raising activities in the community and Radio talk shows. This indictor will measure the level of community engagement in CP, MHPSS and Covid 19 risk communication through social mobilization and awareness raising reaching targeted individuals and communities of various age ranges and forms of disabilities, including parents/caregivers, community leaders, Teachers, PTAs, and various others - to challenge traditional negative coping practices, behaviors and social and cultural norms that exacerbate CP and MHPSS concerns to children. Narrative reports on the CP/MPHSS messages covered, Activity feedback /evaluation results and attendance lists will be used as data sources while KII and FGD with girls and boys including CLWD will be used for data verification during project evaluations.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

As we experienced from other WCH’s on-going Projects and partners’ humanitarian assistance in the 7 counties, both in-kind and cash assistance provided under this project are based on individual needs, are small and deemed unlikely to disrupt any market functionalities in the targeted locations. However, programed and a rapid market survey will be conducted ahead of either in-kind or in-cash grants.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

This project will provide both in-kind support and small cash grants, both of them are determined through a cost-benefit analysis. The cash support will be provided as IPA support where referrals to other services is not feasible for example where no other actor can provide support, and the child protection risk can only be resolved or reduced through this means. While the sole aim is to reduce, prevent or respond to a high protection risk. Cash support will not be provided to only children but rather to an adult who is vetted and approved of by case worker and Community Based CP system in place and will be provided only if deemed safe for the person receiving it.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WCH will strengthen the existing feedback and complaint mechanism to ensure the participation of vulnerable groups including individuals with disability, illiterate community members, women and girls. Assessment will be conducted to identify the most appropriate feedback channels to ensure access to vulnerable groups. Based on the assessment findings, each group will be given an opportunity to provide feedback and complaints about the project using their preferred option. WCH will conduct weekly and monthly follow up to ensure that the preferred feedback options are functional and reliable.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Targeted and identified communities members will be engaged and informed on the criteria to be used for selecting children and caregivers in highest need of Support/protection concerns. Identifying and responding to at-risk children available services that they can expect and also on how they can launch complaints or provide feedback to WCH and its partners in a confidential manner.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All staff, volunteers and workers will be trained on PSEA, code of conduct, as well as be oriented and sign to WCH Child Safeguarding Policy which includes information on how to confidentially report any suspected or identified cases of PSEA and have update and active referral pathways.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Analysis of the beneficiary feedback results will be undertaken on a regular bases and included in topics discussed during project reflection meetings, and where feasible be used to improve project delivery outcomes including project ownership by the communities.
