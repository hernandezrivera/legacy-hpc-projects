Project Summary

Twic and Pibor Counties are among the hard to reach counties in South Sudan as stipulated by the CCCM Cluster and UNOCHA with demanding CCCM needs particularly among internally displaced and returnees. The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 8.5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection services (According to HNO-2024). The purpose of this project is to provide timely and critical lifesaving Camp Coordination and Camp Management targeting a total of 15,000 conflict affected IDPs, and returnees including people with disabilities and marginalized groups in Counties of Twic and Pibor. The CCCM condition is also demanding in these Counties with critical needs in access to CCCM services to the IDPs and returnees. PCO intends to scale up CCCM services to this vulnerable group of people through an integrated approach in FSL, WASH, Protection (CP/GBV) NFIs, Nutrition and Health sectors to a population in priority 1and 2. PCO intends to scale up CCCM services to this vulnerable group of people through an integrated approach in FSL, WASH, Nutrition, Health, Education, NFI and Protection (CP&GBV) sectors. Scaling up of Camp Coordination Camp Management will be addressed through Establishment of the CCCM team per site/area, Provision of site management services including site/area level coordination, service monitoring, community engagement, Coordinate the operational entities, provision and access to services and protection in accordance with international standards aimed at guaranteeing the dignity of people and equitable access to basic services and fundamental rights, Establish a CCCM referral mechanism per site/area, Conducting joint safety audits at site level and new displacement sites, Developing site level multi-sectoral DRR informed contingency plans, site infrastructure care and maintenance, Set up of Transit Center for temporary accommodation and critical services which includes site planning, ground works and construction of basic site infrastructure and communal facilities. Establish complaints and feedback and complaint mechanisms at site level, Conduct bi-annual survey on complaint, feedback and referral mechanism, Conduct awareness-raising activities in collaboration with the site community. Establish and support representative and inclusive community committees and leadership structures ensuring inclusion of gender, PWD and minority groups, Provision of capacity building to community committee members, site leadership and relevant authorities, to participate and eventually take-over of the management of the site, Mobilization and participation of IDPs site population. Ensure care and maintenance of the site infrastructure by taking action to mitigate the impact of environmental degradation, Provision of CfW incentives for people involved in site maintenance activities, Coordinate the solutions for the IDPs in sites to include return, safe and dignified camp exit, community engagement and consultations, Collaborate with relevant humanitarian, peace and development actors, Quarterly IDP site data collection and update of site profiles.
PCO has been operating in Pibor GPAA since 2014 implementing various sectorial activities in Education, WASH, FSL, Protection (CP&GBV), and NFI being supported by SSHF/CHF, FAO, Cordaid and UNICEF. PCO in partnership with IOM implemented CCCM activities in Pibor GPAA under SSHF SA1 funded by UNOCHA from 2022 to 2023, In addition, PCO is currently the CCCM Cluster Coordinator for Pibor GPAA. PCO is the current FSL Cluster Core Pipeline Manager in Pibor GPAA since 2022 to date. Furthermore, PCO has been a long-term partner with UNOCHA in CCCM, Education, FSL, WASH and an active member of the relevant clusters. The Knowledge and experience gained during this period will enable PCO to successfully implement project of the same nature.

1. Needs

The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 8.5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection services (According to HNO- 2024). The HNO-2024 estimated that 1.63 million people are in need of emergency life-saving integrated Camp Coordination and Camp Management services. South Sudan Humanitarian Response Monitoring Report 2024, states that as of October 2024, over 880,000 people remain in 124 formal and informal displacement sites across South Sudan, facing severe vulnerability due to limited alternatives. South Sudan’s CCCM system has been negatively affected by protracted displacement, continued insecurity, recurrent outbreaks of subnational violence, persistent uncertainty about the political process to achieve peace, high impact of natural disasters i.e. drought, flood, disease outbreaks have over the years eroded households coping mechanisms and livelihood options, economic crisis, inadequate access to CCCM quality services, prolonging displacement increasing their reliance on humanitarian assistance. Unplanned, self-settled IDP sites are particularly at risk of being excluded from essential services, with more than 320,000 individuals living within displacement sites (PoC, IDP camp-like settings) lacked CCCM support due to resource constraints, resulting in limited access to coordinated services, community support, information, or site maintenance. The living conditions in camps, PoC and Camp like settings remains at critical levels across the country, however, there are very few people returning to their places of origin because of lack of basic services and insecurity. The continued influx of returnees from Sudan has continued to stretch the already dire resources. Additionally an estimated 520,000 South Sudanese may have returned since the start of the Sudan crisis. Based on monthly site verification, the CCCM Cluster anticipated that around 20 per cent of these returnees will settle in existing IDP sites throughout the country and to ensure that the needs of both IDPs and returnees access to life-saving assistance and protection is met. The integration of these additional returnees in existing IDP sites will require significant efforts to provide decent living conditions, protection, and humanitarian assistance. CCCM support is also needed in ensuring displacement sites are safe and protected from the damage caused by increasing floodwaters and conflict. The proposed project is to provide timely and critical lifesaving CCCM service targeting a total of 18,000 conflict affected IDPs, and returnees in Pibor GPAA. The target population has experienced a cycle of violence and flooding that has made it difficult to access for a long time. As a cross-cutting sector, CCCM is well-positioned to address the multidimensional needs of IDPs, returnees, promote comprehensive and durable solutions, to ensure that IDPs' needs and intentions are integrated into planning, implementation, and decision-making processes. The regular deployment of the comprehensive site profiling tool will provide site-specific information essential for solutions programming, benefiting a range of partners working through the nexus approach. The host communities in these areas are getting it hard to cope with the number of IDPs and returnees who are in need of their support and care with the little resources that they have. Several strategies will be used including joint interventions with other clusters such as Protection, Education, FSL, WASH, NFIs, and ensuring that our activities and the whole intervention complements other sectors and partners activities and no duplication.

2. Project activities

The overall objective of this proposed Humanitarian Response plan is to facilitate safe access assistance and protection for internally displaced and returnees populations in displacement sites and provide informed, evidence-based support to displaced communities to find contextually appropriate solutions for themselves and provide Camp Coordination and Camp Management services to conflict/flood affected vulnerable IDPs and Returnees to 15,000 individuals through implementation of 4 Cluster Objectives with the following key activities :• Establishment of the CCCM team per site/area, • Provision of site management services including site/area level coordination, service monitoring, community engagement, • Coordinate the operational entities, provision and access to services and protection in accordance with international standards aimed at guaranteeing the dignity of people and equitable access to basic services and fundamental rights, • Establish a CCCM referral mechanism per site/area, • Conducting joint safety audits at site level and new displacement sites, • Developing site level multi-sectoral DRR informed contingency plans, site infrastructure care and maintenance, • Set up of Transit Center for temporary accommodation and critical services which includes site planning, ground works (e.g. clearance, levelling, etc.) and construction of basic site infrastructure and communal facilities. • Establish complaints and feedback and complaint mechanisms at site level, Conduct bi-annual survey on complaint, feedback and referral mechanism, • Conduct annual survey with the female committee members on their participation in the decision making process, • Conduct awareness-raising activities in collaboration with the site community. • Establish and support representative and inclusive community committees and leadership structures ensuring inclusion of gender , PWD and minority groups are represented and participate to the established representation structures, • Provide capacity building to community committee members, site leadership and relevant authorities, and eventual take-over of the management of the site, • Mobilization and participation of IDP site population, including through cash for work and Community-led quick impact projects to address immediate needs of both IDP, returnees and host communities, that are identified by the communities themselves • Ensure care and maintenance of the site infrastructure while preventing or taking action to mitigate the impact of environmental degradation (including through community-led projects and CfW). • Provision of CfW incentives for people involved in site maintenance activities, Coordinate the solutions for the IDPs in sites this may include safe and dignified camp exit, go and see visits, community engagement and consultations, • Identification and implementation of self-reliance activities in IDP sites, • Collaborate with relevant humanitarian, peace and development actors. • Data collection on the views of IDPs, Quarterly IDP site data collection and update of site profiles.
