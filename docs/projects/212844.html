Project Summary

North-East Syria (NES) is a region that is home to an estimated 3 million people including 710,000 people displaced from the earlier phases of the conflict, which included offensives against the ISIS caliphate. Following the start of the Turkish offensive on 9 October 2019, more than 190,000 women, children, and men have been displaced.
Cities, camps, and informal settlements have been particularly affected by the new wave of IDPs, putting further stress on the local infrastructure, and capacities of communities, and overstretching the already limited humanitarian relief efforts. While there have been increased efforts to provide emergency relief to affected communities, the condition of the infrastructure particularly in camps is alarming.
The aim is to include assistance to these families in our response in Areeshah camp by responding to the children and their families by providing child protection services and early child care and development for children aged (3-5 years).
Needs: the implementation of ECCD Project in Areesha Camp, is crucial based on the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024 and the severity of the situation in these areas.
Child protection needs: The HNO 2024 highlights the alarming rates of child protection risks and increased the risk of violence, abuse, and neglect, including GBV, child labour, child marriage, vulnerabilities to family separation and human trafficking. Children with disabilities remain underserved due to limited skills and competencies among partners to deal with other forms of disability beyond physical disability. Out-of-school children, are at risk of violence, particularly child labour and child marriage. There is an increase in children in street situations, leading to exposure to violence, sexual abuse, and exploitation, with a high number of children in contact with the law for petty and grave crimes.
Education needs: The HNO 2024 highlights There are 470 schools damaged in north-east Syria, Availability of schools is further reduced with sudden onsets of displacements when schools are used as temporary shelters in some areas.
The main drivers of needs relate both to the supply and demand of education. Across Syria, the main driver reducing access to education is economic, affecting both the households and teaching personnel. Although education is officially free, including textbooks, financial hardship causes the family to pull their children out of school to reduce spending on indirect cost of education (such as school supplies and transport) and resorting to negative coping strategies, exposing children to gendered protection risks such as child marriage and consequent early pregnancies.
The project goal is for Children and caregivers to have improved holistic early childhood development opportunities and support through increased access to quality ECCD services, ensuring that children benefit from school feeding programs, training teachers and education personnel to provide safe and structured learning proccess, and, Support the provision of formal education to children and youth. the project will work to Provide access to safe and protected early learning opportunities, Improve the quality of early childhood learning, and Build the capacity of parents and caregivers to support early learning at home.
the intervention will include Conducting assessments, School Safety Plans training, Distributing emergent literacy and math (ELM) materials, Conducting ELM activities in ECCD, ELM at home for caregivers, Distributing ECCD kits, Distributing Hygiene kits, Parent Teacher Association (PTA), and Teacher Learning Cycle (TLC). also, awareness raising and community events to prevent and respond to child protection issues.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis:1. GBV Risk Assessment: A thorough assessment of the specific GBV risks and vulnerabilities in the targeted Camp will be conducted. This assessment will consider factors such as social norms, power dynamics, displacement, and access to basic services. It will provide critical insights into the prevalence and forms of GBV in the project areas.4. Risk Mitigation Strategies: Based on the identified protection risks and vulnerabilities, appropriate risk mitigation strategies will be developed and integrated into project activities. These strategies may include community engagement and mobilization, awareness-raising campaigns, capacity-building initiatives, and the establishment of referral mechanisms to ensure that women and girls can access timely and appropriate support.5. Do No Harm Approach: A "Do No Harm" approach will be adopted to ensure that project activities do not inadvertently exacerbate protection risks or harm individuals. This approach involves regular monitoring, feedback mechanisms, and continuous learning to adapt interventions and minimize potential negative impacts.PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy:1. Policy and Code of Conduct: Salam Organization will develop and implement a clear PSEA policy and code of conduct that explicitly outlines expected behavior, zero-tolerance for any form of sexual exploitation and abuse, and the consequences for violations. All staff, volunteers, and partners involved in the project will be required to adhere to this policy.2. Training and Capacity Building: Comprehensive training on PSEA will be provided to all project staff, including ECCD personnel, mobile unit staff, and volunteersEffective monitoring and evaluation are crucial for the success and sustainability of the project. To ensure that M&E is effectively integrated, the following steps will be taken:•Data Collection: Relevant data on project activities, outputs, and outcomes will be collected using standardized tools and methodologies. This will include information on the number of beneficiaries reached, types of services provided, and feedback from accessing the services.•Regular Monitoring: A dedicated M&E team will be responsible for monitoring the implementation of project activities. They will conduct regular visits to ECCD, mobile units, and other project sites to assess progress, identify challenges, and provide support where needed.•Quality Assurance: The M&E team will ensure the quality and adherence to established standards in the delivery of GBV, and CP services. They will work closely with service providers to maintain high-quality support and address any gaps or issues that may arise.•Outcome Evaluation: Periodic evaluations will be conducted to assess the impact and outcomes of the project. This will involve measuring changes in knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors related to CP, as well as assessing the effectiveness of the services provided.
