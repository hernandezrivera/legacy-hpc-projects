Project Summary

Kassala State is one of the most affected areas by the war in Sudan and the population displacement, hosting around 157,000 IDPs as of January 2024 (OCHA situation report). Kassala State had existing IDPs from the pre-war period, more than 200,000 people who were displaced from the eastern and southern parts of the State since 2019 and refugees from neighbouring Eritrea.Project Description
The project aims to enhance the protection and resilience of communities affected by HLP violations and basic service gaps in Kassala state, Sudan by strengthening the capacity of community-based networks to:
raise awareness on HLP rights,
monitor and report on HLP violations,
prioritize their shelter and basic services needs, and
manage the community block grants for the prioritized actions for the improvement in shelter and basic services.

This is to be achieved through the following:

1- to train and capacitate existing community-based networks to track and monitor HLP violations in Kassala State; and to raise awareness of community members on their HLP rights through dissemination of information and awareness materials. whose result is the development of HLP briefings, to be updated periodically to different coordination bodies, including United Nations Country Team (UNCT), Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), Protection Sector and other cluster working groups.

2- to train and capacitate community-based networks at neighborhood level to identify and prioritize their needs. Prioritization exercise is conducted through the provision of capacity building for existing community networks on the use of Rapid Urban Sector Profiling for Sustainability (RUSPS) tool; RUSPS tool is a methodology developed by UN-Habitat to assess the urban situation and needs in various conflict-affected settings. It aims to provide up-to-date, holistic, and participatory analysis of the impact of the crisis on key cities, and to identify priority areas for intervention. It covers four main themes: urban governance; conditions of shelter and informal settlements; gender, urban environment; and local economic development and livelihood situations.
3- to train the community-based networks on the management of the community block grants. These grants will be based on the prioritization through the RUSPS exercise, and provided as selected package grants that may include shelter support, including cash for rent or rehabilitation, and improvement in Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) facilities including construction or rehabilitation of public pit latrines, improvement in water supply infrastructure, and Solid Waste Management basic units.
The Proposed location for the intervention is Kassala State, a state that is hosting 157,000IDPs (OCHA, 2024), with the prospect of further increase in the arrival of IDPs as people are re-displaced from areas where fighting spread since the last months of 2023. The target location for HLP component is Kassala state as a whole (2,519,071 persons), while community block grant activities will be implemented in 2 localities, Kassala Rural locality and New Halfa locality, to support 58,500 persons. The selection of these two localities is based on the results of assessment shared by UNOHCHR
The project interventions are in alignment with the Strategic Objective 2 of the Protection Sector in HRNP 2024: Community-Based Protection. The relevant indicators under the Objective 2 of the Protection Sector Strategy include: Number of community-based structures established or supported;
Number of community support activities/projects completed;
Number of service providers, including government officials and other duty bearers, community representatives and humanitarian staff, trained in protection issues and safe and inclusive service delivery; and Number of persons reached through information, awareness raising and community outreach activities on general protection (including civil documentation, HLP, peaceful co-existence).

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection is mainstreamed during the whole project cycle by ensuring access, participation, safety, and dignity for the target beneficiaries of IDPs, host communities, and vulnerable groups such as women, male and female youth, people with disabilities and female-headed households. The following principles are adopted in the implementation of project interventions.• Putting safety and dignity first and do no harm: Risk analysis will be conducted to assess the possible negative impacts of the intervention that can increase people's exposure to physical and psychosocial risks, and measure to avoid or reduce them. • Meaningful Access: The project will ensure that people can access the assistance according to their needs and without any obstacles or discrimination. with special attention to vulnerable groups of women, IDPs, male and female youth, and host communities who might have challenges accessing assistance and services.• Participation and empowerment: the Project will support the development of self-protection, capacities on HLP through training sessions, raise awareness and share information on HLP rights/violations and help people to assert their HLP rights, including rights to adequate housing. And empower communities to take leading role and ownership to management of shelter and basic services upgrading.Need assessment and risks analysis: UN-Habitat is adopting participatory approach/tools including RUSPS in needs assessment to ensure meaningful participation of IDPs, host communities, women, youth, people with disabilities, eldery, local CSOs, and local authorities, to involve them in assessing their situations, identifying possible threats and vulnerabilities, and understanding their capacities and coping mechanisms. The specific protection risks of their involvement will be addressed via many modalities of participation, including face to face key informant interviews, focus groups, online anonymous surveys. Project design: the interventions are prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding doing harm, through conducting risk analysis and mitigation measures prior to implementation and adopting conflict sensitive approach. Resources allocated for capacity building and protection of communities in the project budget Project implementation: The project will be implemented according to protection mainstreaming plan and take into account all preventive and mitigation measures to ensure meaningful access, safety, and dignity of targeted population. Special arrangements will be into place for individuals or groups who may face barriers or challenges in accessing the assistance, the modality of implementation that utilize participatory tools will promote social cohesion between displaced and host communities to identify needs and manage block grants together.Monitoring and evaluation: The Project will assess impact of the protection mainstreaming activities through quarterly monitoring, and feedback collection/evaluation.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Community feedbackThe project main aim is to enhance the protection and resilience of communities affected by HLP violations and basic service gaps in Kassala state, Sudan, by strengthening the capacity of community-based networks to monitor and report on HLP issues, as well as to prioritize their needs for basic services and manage rehabilitation of basic services through block grants. Therefore, UN-Habitat proposed an intervention to achieve strengthening of community-based structures for:• Community-driven prioritization and management of basic services rehabilitation to bridge the gap in access to critical basic services.This is to be achieved through training and capacitating community-based networks at neighbourhood level to identify and prioritize their needs. Prioritization exercise is conducted through the provision of capacity building for existing community networks on the use of Rapid Urban Sector Profiling for Sustainability (RUSPS) tool; RUSPS use to assess the urban situation and needs in various conflict-affected situation. This will provide up-to-date, holistic, and participatory analysis of the impact of the crisis on key cities, and to identify priority areas for intervention. UN- Habitat will exercise the following guidelines of community feedback:1. Community Consultation and Collecting feedback: UN- Habitat trained community-based networks will support in collecting feedback from target communities including IDPs and host communities at New Halafa and Rural Kassala Localities in a systematic way, on issues related monitoring and evaluation on HLP issues and Community-driven prioritization and management of basic services rehabilitation. Different participatory approaches of feedback collection will be used, such as surveys, interviews, focus group discussions and observation. Inclusivity will be considered in feedback collection process.2. Analyse the feedback collected by the project’s established community based- networks and identify the key insights that emerge. The project team will use different tools and techniques to organize and interpret feedback data. The project team will also compare and contrast the feedback from different sources and perspectives, including local government and NGOs operating in Kassala state. 3. Apply the collected and analysed community feedback to inform the project decisions and actions specially when it comes to selection and implementation of basic services. The project team will use the feedback to improve the project implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Communicated the project team feedback and share it with the target community and other relevant audiences. The project team is preferring to communicate feedback findings through community meeting validation and established community- based networks as they would tailor communication content to suit communities at grassroot levels.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Inter-agency accountabilityThe Project is fully committed to inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms to ensure protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. The Project intends to actively engage with inter-agency mechanisms in the project locations to receive complaints, including sexual exploitation and abuse, aimed at providing support to survivors of sexual exploitation and abuse, ensuring reporting and allegation referral, and ultimately preventing sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness-raising. The Project will appoint a focal point within the project team, ensuring that the person has proven integrity, objectivity and professional competence, capable of maintaining confidentiality and cultural sensitivity. The focal point has experience in working directly with local communities with proven communication skills. The focal point is responsible for liaising with the inter-agency mechanism in the project locations, participating in any coordination meetings, familiarizing him/herself to the procedures, providing inputs into the development/strengthening of the procedures as needed, and ensuring reporting and follow-up. The focal point will also ensure that all actors and stakeholders of the Project are aware of the purpose and functioning of the inter-agency mechanism, including implementing partners, community networks, contractors, and any other actors. Towards implementing partners and contractors, the Project will require a commitment to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and include it in the contractual language. The focal point will also ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the purpose and functioning of the inter-agency mechanism to receive complaints, including what measures are taken to protect known or potential survivors of sexual exploitation and abuse, whistleblowers and testifiers. The Project will commit financial resources for staff training, maintaining entry points for receiving complaints as well as providing facilities (e.g. meeting rooms, etc.). The Project will ensure that an internal complaint mechanism and standards of conduct are available and known within the Project team. Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse is also included as staff onboarding briefings and contractual processes.
