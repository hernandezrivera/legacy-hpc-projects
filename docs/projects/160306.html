Project Summary

This project will focus on providing life -saving gender based violence assistance to IDPs, returnees and host communities in Aweil West, Aweil Centre, Kajo Keji, Budi and Kapoeta South and Ulang Counties, South Sudan.
ARC has on-going GBV response and prevention interventions in all proposed locations except in Aweil West where ARC plans to establish GBV to be integrated with on-going nutrition activities. Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) funds will be used to complement OFDA support in Kapoeta South and East, Budi, Kajo Keji and Ulang. In Aweil Centre and Kapoeta South, GBV activities are funded by WFP and the funding will be ending on November 30th, ARC views HRP as an opportunity to sustain the project.
In addition, HRP funds will be used to broaden the scope of GBV activities to reach more beneficiaries especially in locations where ARC has on-going activities (Budi, Ulang, Kajo Keji and Kapoeta South). Also, there are emerging gaps and vulnerabilities to GBV due to evolving context as a result of floods and related displacements of IDPs, returnees and host communities against a background of limited funding for GBV.

1 - Needs

Kajo Keji: Findings from assessments in 2019 highlights the humanitarian needs and gaps in service provision to affected population who are scattered in various locations within Kajo Keji. General fear and insecurity still prevails. Humanitarian organizations including ARC are providing services to IDPS in Korijo, with few partners supporting the scattered population. Reports from IOM indicate that the population in Korijo IDP camp has significantly reduced to less than 12,000 individuals due to water stress. HRP funding will broaden the scope of GBV services (Case management, PSS, risk mitigation and awareness) to those living outside Korijo camp in logo and Kerwa and complement OFDA funding for GBV and WASH in Korijo where ARC is the main GBV service provider. Kapoeta South & Budi: KS and Budi tend to experience similar GBV/protection challenges. ARC is the main GBV partner in Kapoeta and Budi region. ARC’s presence is critical in providing support to GBV survivors and those at risk through case management, PSS activities and other WGFS services. ARC received 288 GBV survivors in Kapoeta South, East and Budi combined from July to September 2019, an average of 96 cases per month. This reflects the high number of survivors receiving support from ARC and the need for sustained presence. ARC will enhance monitoring of protection issues related to the presence of a cantonment site in the region. Protection risks associated with firewood collection among women are common in this region highlighting the need for fuel efficient stoves to mitigate GBV risks. Cases of early child marriage are rampant among nomadic Toposa community. This calls for sustained awareness raising on GBV as a joint effort to dismantle retrogressive cultural norms and practices. Also, ARC’s presence in Kapoeta is strategic in supporting the GBV coordination together with the Ministry of Social Development.Aweil Centre and Aweil West: ARC is currently implementing a GBV response and prevention project funded by WFP. The project is ending on 30th November. ARC exiting in Aweil Central will not only leave a gap in GBV service provision (case management, WGFS services, risk mitigation and awareness raising) but also in coordinating GBV since ARC is the GBV Sub Cluster lead partner. HRP funds will be used to fill this gap. Also, ARC foresees an opportunity to expand GBV services to Aweil West County where GBV services are unavailable. ARC is implementing a nutrition project in Aweil West and ARC views this as an opportunity to integrate the two sectors and strengthen inter-sectoral linkages and improve access to GBV services in remote areas. Ulang: Ulang experienced inflow of returnees from Ethiopia and IDPs from surrounding Counties. Ulang hosts 300 returnees, 721in Yomding and 2,930 in Kuich and numbers keep increasing. Conflicts over scramble for water is a major security risk. Also, this have impacted on the food security situation as host community share their food rations with returnees and IDPs. Needs continue to multiply with the recent flooding in Sobat corridor overstretching the capacity of partners. From IRNA assessment, 25, 758 people have been affected by the floods and majority have been displaced with no facilities to support them, increasing vulnerabilities and generally population in need of assistance. The four WGFS and one youth center continue to receive overwhelming numbers of beneficiaries. Dignity kits are lacking. GBV services are needed outside the four payams where ARC is operating. ARC is refocusing its existing GBV activities to address emerging needs and gaps by introducing unstructured PSS activities where more women and girls can participate. However, consumption of WGFS supplies such as sugar is unsustainably high. Also, GBV services remain limited to 4 out of 20 payams in greater Ulang. With ARC being the only service provider GBV gaps are immense. HRP funding will be used to fill these gaps and expand to other payams.

2 - Activities

Cluster Objective 1: Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people Activity 1.1 Setting up systems and procedures for case management in Aweil WestARC has on-going GBV response and prevention activities in all locations except in Aweil West where GBV project will be established. System set up will include; recruitment and training of Caseworkers, establishing private rooms for case management, procurement of necessary supplies printing tools needed for documenting survivor’s stories . Activity 1.2: Comprehensive case management and referrals Survivor-centered and confidential case management services to GBV survivors will be through individual and group counselling and consent based referrals. 300 survivors are targeted by this project. Referral pathways will be critical in supporting effective referrals of GBV survivors. To this end, ARC will work with referral pathway partners to ensure that a localized and updated pathways is in place.Activity 1.3 Staff and community frontline trained in case management190 participants will be trained in case management by ARC with support from SSHF. Participants will be GBV staff and other frontline service providers in the referral pathways. ARC will use the staff already trained as Training of Trainers (ToTs) in case management to roll out this training/organize refresher trainings as needed at the County level.Activity 1.4: Establish, equip and operationalize one WGFS in Aweil West ARC will construct, equip ad operationalize one WGFS in Aweil West. WGFS activities will conform to UNICEF’s WGFS guidelines for South Sudan.Activity 1.5: Provide PSS for women and girls at the WGFSWGFS will be an entry for women and girls to access other services. The main activities will include; case management, age appropriate information sessions, empowerment/skill building activities, and recreational activities among others. ARC will identify a fully dedicated WGFS facilitator from the beneficiaries to coordinate skill building and PSS activities so that caseworkers can fully focus on case management. This will also foster sustainability and ownership of WGFS activities by the community. 7200 women and girls are expected to access WGFS services in all six locations (1200 per location).Cluster objective 2: Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilienceActivity 2.1: Community sensitization and awareness raising on GBVGBV prevention programming will involve awareness raising on services available and referrals. ARC will adopt different strategies in passing GBV key messages to the community such as house to house visits, use of posters and community conversations. 24,833 women, girls, boys and men will be reached with GBV messages through awareness raising.Activity 2.2: Distribute dignity kits ARC will procure, transport and distribute dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age. as a GBV risk mitigation measure. 600 dignity kits will be distributed to vulnerable women and girls. The dignity kits contents will be guided by the GBV Sub Cluster.Activity 2.3: Conduct safety auditARC will lead joint routine safety audits with other sectors to strengthen integration and develop a holistic approach in responding to GBV. Safety audit findings will be shared with all partners with key actions for each sector. Activity 2.4: Train Community Mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warningARC targets to build the capacity of 60 community mobilizers on hot spot mapping, safety audits, disaster reduction and preparedness among other topics. Cluster objective 3: Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population Activity 3.1: Training on fuel efficient stoves as a risk mitigation measure150 women and girls (25 per location) will be trained and will be supported to cascade the training to others.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To mitigate the risks, ARC will ensure that women, men, boys and girls are involved in the design, implementation and monitoring and evaluating the project to reflect their needs at every stage of the project cycle. This will include ensuring that the needs and priorities of vulnerable and minority such as elderly, people living with disabilities, women, adolescent girls are taken into consideration and they are included as beneficiaries of this project. ARC will sensitize beneficiaries on services available, where and how they can be accessed. To do this effectively, ARC will ensure an updated and localized referral pathways in place and will be disseminated widely among the service providers and to the community. Ethical considerations in regard to handling of survivor’s information will be upheld. Survivor’s information will be safely stored in lockable cabinets and case management rooms will only be accessed by key staff. GBVIMS tool will be used to safely collect, store, analyze and share GBV data in an ethical manner.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ARC's commitment to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) focuses on the effective implementation of three long-held principles: 1) our customer is the IDP/host community/returnees, 2) IDPs/host communities/returnees define quality and 3) we solve problems alongside IDPs/host communities/returnees. These principles hold us accountable to improving the quality, relevance and impact of our work in the interest of beneficiaries. To demonstrate ARC's commitment to AAP in practice, ARC will ensure strong accountability in its emergency responses toward communities and individuals directly affected by disasters by involving them directly in program design and encouraging them to provide feedback on how our interventions are influencing them. ARC will use periodic focus group discussions (FGDs) and an ongoing feedback/complaint response mechanism throughout this project to obtain feedback from beneficiaries. Feedback from beneficiaries will be analyzed from time to time and ARC will coordinate appropriate action internally and bilaterally with respective partners.Feedback/Compliant Response Mechanism: ARC places a strong emphasis on receiving feedback from the individuals and communities that we seek to serve in order to improve ownership and the quality of our programs. A feedback/complaints response mechanism that both receives inputs from beneficiaries and regularly provides feedback will be utilized during this project.PSEA: ARC staff have signed a code of conduct which includes zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse policy. ARC commits to continuously educate beneficiaries on PSEA and other violations and to inform them on reporting mechanisms in place when such cases occur. WGFS are used as entry points for reporting PSEA cases. ARC will support survivors of PSEA to access support in a manner that does not compromise their dignity and privacy.
