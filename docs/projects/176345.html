Project Summary

The project will reach the most vulnerable 120,000 people (43,200 girls, 28,800 women, 21600 boys and 26,400 men) which corresponds to 20,000 HH, (Nassir 5000HH, Maiwut 3000HH, Akobo 5,000HH, Leer 3000HH and Fangak 4000HH). We shall contribute to FSL cluster objective 2 by providing emergency complementary production inputs (crop kits, fast growing vegetable kits and fishing kits) and also to FSL cluster objective 3 “Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks “ through Capacity building on, natural resource management, value addition, post-harvest handling and marketing. This will improve food production and provision of fishing kits will contribute to better nutrition at the HH level and in other cases improve HH income due to the sale of excess fish, this will take place from January 2021 to December 2021. We shall also provide unconditional cash assistance in line with FSL cluster objective 1 Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19. This response plan is aimed at an effort to prevent an even more severe food shortage as well as strengthening the coping capacities of the vulnerable host community and IDPs. Nile Hope shall liaise with FAO to get in kind support for the crop fishing and vegetable kits for the emergency responses but will procure for the other ongoing developmental projects
The production inputs will be given to 15,500 HH (9,300 Female HH and 6,200 Male HH) 3500 in Akobo,3000 HH in Leer,3000HH in Fangak,2000 HH in Maiwut and 4000 HH in Nassir ,which corresponds to 93,000 beneficiaries vulnerable Host Communities and IDPs and unconditional cash assistance to 4500HH (1000 HH in Maiwut,1000HH in Nassir,1500 in Akobo and 1000HH in Fangak) which corresponds to 27,000 beneficiaries. Special focus will be given to people with disability to make sure they are not left out. Capacity building training will be conducted on improved crop, vegetable and fishing practices ,value addition ,marketing and continuous practical sessions in the farms to selected recipient beneficiaries at the Payam level as TOTs to the rest of the Community for sustainability purposes targeting 4,100 people (2460 women and 1640 men). The overall project objective is improved food security and livelihoods at house hold level for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition

1 - Needs

Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in South Sudan with increased & protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic (IPC Nov 2020). The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic significantly decreased commodity movement, market functionality, cross-border trade, daily labour opportunities, reducing HH purchasing power and food access .7.24 million people face acute food insecurity, 1.9 million acutely malnourished women and children & 1.47million IDPs, (WFP Situation report #283. Akobo is in IPC 4 and at risk of famine this is due to the protracted conflict and the massive flooding which has destroyed people’s livelihoods and caused large displacements. All the cultivated lands have been destroyed by the current floods resulting to loss of farm produces .This is expected to have consequences on food security for vulnerable IDPs, returnee &host community. Immediate scale-up of humanitarian assistance is needed to save lives and prevent total collapse of livelihoods. Leer County has been indiscriminately affected by ongoing conflict, recurrences of flood and the impact of COVID-19 .It is placed in IPC 3 (IPC Nov 2020). The County is predominately agro-pastoral community whose main livelihoods depend on rearing of livestock, subsistence farming, fishing and petty trade. Fishing activity remains to be the most well fitted livelihood activity. They also engage in rain-fed agriculture (maize and sorghum) once a year during the main season, from the different support the County has been getting from humanitarian actors, the population is gradually progressing well. The swamps create opportunities for irrigation and fishing activities to take place both in dry & main seasons. There is need to continue supporting the community to ensure they do not slide back to severe state. Fangak is classified in IPC 4 (IPC, Nov 2020). The community there is vulnerable &It is hosting IDPs fleeing fighting In Unity state &returnees from Sudan. We also have the recent floods in 2020 that have displaced more than 6356 people (IRNA 0ctober 7th 2020) .This has exposed the community to further vulnerability. There is also decreased access to food &disruptions in the supply chain due to the conflict between government & IO forces over control of the Tonga market which supplies Fangak. Maiwut is classified in IPC 4 (Nov 2020 IPC). The community has experienced recent shock caused by flooding that caused mass displacement which left them extremely vulnerable. They are also trying to recover from the effect of the conflict between the warring parties over control of the area & is also hosting high number of returnees from Ethiopia. The population is in dire need of immediate humanitarian support. Nasir County in IPC 4 remains a high priority location as a result of protracted conflict which has led to multiple displacement which prevents the people from fully engaging in food production coupled with poor harvest due to prolonged dry spell &then later on flooding in 2020 due to heavy rains. The already vulnerable host community is extremely overwhelmed by the many returnees coming from Ethiopia & IDPs who previously ran from the conflict hot zones. The compounding effects of widespread violence & sustained economic decline that have further diminished the capacity of people to face threats to their safety &livelihoods. Nile Hope aims at preventing food shortage by providing production inputs (fishing, crop &vegetable kits), & also scale up the ongoing unconditional cash assistance to ensure the community access food & other basic needs. We shall also continue building their resilience by supporting producer groups through capacity building training on improved production, post harvest handling, value addition & marketing .We have ongoing partnership with DCA and FAO to continue supporting the vulnerable communities but the gap is enormous.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

We have an established safeguarding taskforce in Nile Hope to ensure all the issue of sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse (SHEA) are prevented .We carry out safe guarding monthly sessions to ensure all our staff are on board and are observing the PSEA policy that includes also the code of conduct which is annexed to staff contracts and emphasizes on zero tolerance on any sexual misconduct. We also ensure we advocate for gender balance as early as at recruiting level of staff and of during the formation of project steering committees aiming at 50% female where possible, this is aimed at creating safer space for our women beneficiaries to raise any PSEA issue that may arise . We also work closely with our gender department to broadcast PSEA localized messages at distribution sites as well use the existing referral pathways in case any cases of sexual abuses are reported at the community level

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During the composition of the feedback committees ,we shall ensure that all the vulnerable groups are represented .This is aimed at ensuring that feedback from all the groups is collected and responded to. Also in the M&E tools for collecting feedback ,questions will be tailored to be inclusive and representative as well making sure all the groups are included in the sample selected to respond to the questioners

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community is an active part of the project. Nile Hope endeavors to engage with them through the locally recruited staff (local knowledge), local leaders, community representatives and relevant ministries at the county level. This is done via regular stake holders meeting as well as through consultative sessions with the locally constituted project steering committees, beneficiary selection committees and cash distribution committees. Those committees are also tasked with collecting feedback and complains from the community and forward to Nile Hope Focal point at the field level The MEAL team in Nile Hope also conducts joint monitoring with the selected stake holders and the project steering committees. This is aimed enhancing community ownership and participation

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

We shall use the feedback collected during the quarterly beneficiaries feedback sessions and this information collected will be used when reviewing and improving the project through out the implementation

8.11. Mitigation Measures

We promote meaningful access, safety and dignity of our beneficiaries We shall ensure all the distribution sites are selected in consultations with all the stake holders and the local authority to ensure they are safe and accessible to all the groups targeted. Appropriate distribution time will be agreed upon with all the stakeholders and the beneficiaries’ representative to ensure it is convenient for the target beneficiaries and is not too early in the morning or late in the evening, which can expose them to insecurity and other protection issues We shall also work in collaboration with our GBV sector and other actors on the ground to ensure that we work with the existing structures to combat any protection or GBV related issues ensuring we link our project to the established referral pathways . In the project committees we shall have a well detailed selection criteria to ensure it is inclusive and representative .We shall also deploy the vulnerability matrix during selection of the beneficiaries to guide the process and our team will use the set criteria to verify the people to be targeted. The Feedback and complain mechanism will be localized and will also encourage the use of pictorial illustrations to ensure the community understand and utilize the established mechanisms. In the feedback and complain committees, we shall ensure all the groups and gender are represented to ensure the feedback is not biased.Nile Hope objectively works and cooperates with the Community through their representatives and the local authority to ensure they are no conflicts arising from selection of the beneficiaries ,targeting of the payams and bomas . This is aimed at ensuring that the decisions made includes all the stake holders and are not left to the local authority to decide, this approach has build trust in the areas we are serving .We remain neutral and only focuses on the needs identified in the community
