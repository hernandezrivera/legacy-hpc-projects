Project Summary

The main objective of the shelter coordination is to ensure that vulnerable Palestinians who are displaced or at risk of displacement, and people under constant protection concerns obtain secure and adequate shelter solutions, through coordination and capacity building of actors working in the shelter sector. Thirty-one and eighteen various shelter member organizations are currently registered at shelter cluster in Gaza and West Bank respectively, including INGOs, NNGOs, UN agencies, and Government counterparts and are benefiting from the shelter cluster services such as coordination, information management, strategy and standards, monitoring and evaluation, contingency planning, advocacy, communications, and crosscutting issues. The Project is in line with the HRP 2023 three strategic objectives and allows Shelter Cluster to continue its role by ensuring that the IASC cluster core functions are fulfilled and well designed to suit the context in Palestine.
Considering the cluster core functions , the cluster will conduct regular coordination meetings where all the participants has a one platform to discuss different topics involving the relevant ministries, the meetings allow also the partners to address different experience, lessons learned approaches used in implementation and monitoring. At the IM part, the cluster FP will ensure consistent and regular data collection and analysis to update the progress of the HRP objectives and indicators, 5Ws regular update, producing factsheet, eliminate any overlap, and supporting the partners in verifying and checking the targeted beneficiaries list. The cluster with the partners support will develop and review the cluster strategy on yearly basis that clarify the main priorities and strategic directions for the work, in addition to the development of the different inputs for HPC process. In addition, the cluster will work to have sound and systematic monitoring and evaluation for the shelter interventions with special focus on outcome indicators measurement, correction actions and sharing experience between the partners.
For the contingency plan. The cluster is keen to have operational and practical plan that includes all most likely scenarios, impact analysis a, mapping the available capacity, possible gaps in the resources. For this purpose, the cluster will review and update two contingency plans one for Gaza and one for WB with active participation of key actors in the field of emergency responses. Besides the regular work to track the available NFIs stockpiles.
For the cross cutting issues, the cluster will coordinate for any opportunity to increase the partners capacities. The cluster with cluster FPs will produce tip sheets for gender and protection mainstreaming in shelter interventions, ensures the partners give special attention to the vulnerable groups needs such as PWDs and elderly and commitment to PSEA through encouraging them to have internal polices and sufficient knowledge and referring system for the affected cases.

1 - Needs

The Shelter Sector faces different challenges in Gaza, the West Bank, and East Jerusalem that require continued collaboration between actors as well as representation to the HCT and donor/diplomatic community to remove barriers and ensure assistance to the most vulnerable Palestinians. The Cluster collects regular updates from partners to identify these challenges and outstanding needs, as well as using data from cluster and partners’ evaluations/assessments such as the damage assessments after May 2021 and August 2022 escalations in Gaza, MSNA 2022 findings and analysis, and OCHA data including IDPs and demolition databases as well as PCBS information where relevant. In Gaza, the recurrent escalations, high unemployment rate, Israeli restrictions on the import of building materials and equipment and poverty exacerbated by the consequences of limited income resources hinder the people to meet their basic needs including adequate housing. Therefore, intensive coordination efforts are required between different actors to address the urgent gaps and needs and to list the needs based on clear and transparent criteria for selection and targeting, this requires special and close coordination with the different ministries to cross check the lists and to align the interventions with existing strategies. The needs mainly resulted from the existing and frequent damages from escalations, and the large numbers of substandard shelters conditions, continued information tracking, and emergency preparedness is required to promote adequate and timely support to various vulnerable people in Gaza Strip. The Shelter Cluster has played a vital role in gathering and triangulating data and sharing this to internal and external stakeholders. Chronic housing shortages and the dire economic situation as a result of the blockade, loss of jobs, frequent conflicts, salary cuts, delay in social net payments and poverty have compounded concerns for adequate shelter, protection of acutely-vulnerable families, as identified in the MSNA 2022, MPWH assessments and MoSD updated database. Continued efforts are needed to consolidate, harmonize and improve databases and information sharing to ensure prioritization and efficient use of resources. In the West Bank and EJ, addressing the shelter needs for the communities at higher risk of displacement, demolition and forcible transfer particularly in area C and East Jerusalem, in addition to the preparedness actions and ensuring adequate Shelter/NFIs stockpiles are key priorities for the cluster. The advocacy for the housing rights is another key function of the cluster to allow the people enjoy the rights to have adequate and protected shelters. Careful coordination is required to address these needs. Several agencies are currently working to address emergent needs in Gaza as well as the regular and timely demolition responses in the West Bank through emergency shelter provision as well as advocacy and legal advice. Inter and intra cluster work is ongoing to increase predictable and coordinated response, improve technical standards and sustainable solutions, and engage with donors and authorities on finding efficient ways to support the people. In both contexts, children are particularly vulnerable, as are the elderly and those who are disabled. Women face a particular burden as primary care givers and face specific vulnerabilities accessing their housing land and property rights. A cluster gender and protection focal points were appointed to address different concerns and to provide support and guidance to partners on gender-responsive interventions and protection mainstreaming, as well as revision of NFI standard packages and kits is made frequently to fit with the needs of various vulnerable groups including children, women, girls, elderlies and PWDs. Shelter sector coordination across the oPt has contributed to improved collaboration, common standards, and clearer guidance, and will continue to work on greater preparedness and coordinated response. There has been good engagement and coordination with local government that should continue to increase understanding of humanitarian principles and overcome barriers to shelter provision to the most vulnerable. Across the oPt, the poverty of marginalized communities and groups, in addition to Israeli practices generate extra pressure for families living in substandard shelters to create hygienic and appropriate living space and to find alternatives for the shared kitchens and toilets with other extended family members. For an effective, efficient, and timely response to unexpected and frequent escalations as well as unforeseen emergencies, the cluster works to increase the partners’ capacities to respond to the emergent needs. This involves NFIs stockpiling, community capacity building, improving urban displacement setting to host IDPs, and maintaining the existing DES (Designated Emergency Shelter) to ensure adequate functionality at time of emergency. With support from PSEA network, cluster partners will participate in various sessions to improve their knowledge and capacity on PSEA. The cluster will ensure that the partners include and track specific PSEA indicators and integrating PSEA related clause in their contracts. Practically, the field staff should receive more in-depth training on causes and consequences of SEA in addition to any available field reference materials. The beneficiaries should be aware of the referral and confidential reporting system for any SEA. The cluster will continue to receive, process act upon Shelter-related requests for help, feedback and complaints. Complaints, allegations concerning SEA will be referred in accordance with the PSEA referral protocol and SOPs. The cluster will foster the endorsement of the SOPs by all members and liaise with the PSEA Network for technical assistance.

2 - Activities

NRC has led the Shelter Cluster for several years, bringing together the work across Gaza and the West Bank and supporting the ‘one Palestine’ approach. Throughout the current and previous years, the Cluster has produced regular information updates that have been highly valued and relied upon to track the status and progress of the shelter response, updated dashboards published regularly to summarize and show the responses and remaining key gaps, emergent needs, and support advocacy and resource mobilisation. The cluster hosts and activates different Working Groups (WGs) like the Strategic Advisory Group (SAG), Contingency and NFIs WG, and minimum standards development and revision WG, and participates in other technical groups with MPWH and partners interested in reconstruction and repair of damaged shelters. The Cluster is a respected and relevant forum through which relationships between key stakeholders are strengthened, reliable information is collected and disseminated and common approaches and standards are developed that reduce inequity and provide safe, adequate and dignified shelter solutions to affected and displaced populations in Palestine with encouragement to include local partners in CBOs and informal project committees . The overall objective of the program is to ensure that displaced people and people at risk of displacement, and people living in unhygienic substandard shelter conditions in oPt obtain secure and adequate shelter/NFIs solutions, by continuing to support service delivery through monthly Shelter Cluster meetings, clear and regular information sharing, addressing Shelter related needs and informing strategic decision making through assessments, strategy development, developing different standards, activating different working groups and ensure stakeholders engagement. The Cluster continues to provide a forum for data collection, analysis and dissemination; contingency planning and preparedness; and information management support. And promotes the mainstreaming of protection, PSEA, GBV and gender in cluster activities through dedicated workshops and tailored support from the gender and protection focal points at local and global level. It continuse to play a role in strengthening M&E to ensure adequate impact and outcome analysis and to ensure the overall quality assurance for the provided assistance, sharing good experience, involving and providing guidance for local organizations as relevant and needed, while highlighting gaps and advocating for appropriate and effective solutions to meet the needs of affected populations. The Cluster will focus on technical support to partner organizations in relation to contingency planning, shelter impact, and gender needs. The cluster also aims to support the partners to target and prioritize the poor families and groups FHHs, PWDs, and the Elderly and keep monitoring and updating the gap and caseloads for different needs including IDPs. 3 - Indicators

The Cluster in 2023 will coordinate activities related to Strategic Objectives 1, 2 and 3 to mitigate the risks and impact of displacement, protect against the effects of severe weather impact, and build preparedness and response capacity. In addition, activities that focus on ensuring access to a basic level of adequate shelter will contribute to Strategic Objectives 1 and 2. The Cluster will also work to promote the inclusion of cross-cutting issues in the work of its members and continue to pursue transition options through building partnerships and linking with long term actors and government .Accountability to members will be fostered through regular feedback and internal performance mid-year review, while overall cluster and cluster members' performance will be monitored by OCHA through the Periodic Monitoring Review (PMR). The Cluster through the SAG will develop a strategic plan at the beginning of the year (Jan-Feb) to identify partner priorities and layout concrete and time-bound activities in an annual workplan. The cluster will continue to increase knowledge and capacity for national authorities, local NGOs and build relationships with NRC Global and the Global Shelter Cluster in order to access surge support where required and ensure cluster performance oversight. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is an essential practice in shelter work. For this purpose, the cluster developed a protection mainstreaming checklist to help partners in this field. Emergent COVID 19 protection concerns also have been included to protect the people living in substandard shelters through guiding the partners to ensure the families have adequate space and isolation location, and distribution of hygienic and cleaning materials as well as awareness leaflets and brochure for different people needs. PWDs is one of the main vulnerable groups identified from the MSNA and will be targeted and prioritised within the shelter interventions. Shelter adaptation works to help and serve the PWDs and the elderlies with access issues through the provision of ramps, handrails, wide doors, and suitable latrines. In addition cluster partners are encouraged to strengthen their knowledge and understanding of the rights of the PWDs to enable them to participate actively in all processes of humanitarian programming and try to eliminate all the existing barriers that prevent PWDs from accessing services and addressing their needs. Moreover, appropriate indicators have been included and monitored for the inclusion and targeting the PWDs. For 2022, the Cluster partners will adopt a multidimensional prioritization approach based on the MSNA results, May2021 escalation damage assessment, partners updated assessments, displacement figures, people and communities at higher risk of COVID-19 pandemic, and the geographical areas targeted for the proposed interventions. The Cluster will give priority to projects that target particular vulnerable groups such as herding and marginalisd communities located in Area C, and the social cases in area A and B as registered and recommended by MoSD (Ministry of Social Development). It will also prioritise the people with disabilities (9,000 people) particularly in northern Gaza, vulnerable FHH (41,000 people) in north Gaza , vulnerable households in areas at risk of flooding and weather extremes, in addition to the recent and previous IDPs who suffer from multiple displacement, and families evicted from their homes due to the inability to pay rental costs. In addition, the Cluster will prioritise projects that aim to reduce the number of people in need and decrease recurrent assistance like shelter repair and restoration for the uninhabited damaged homes. COVID-19 shelter-related responses and needs will be addressed and prioritised based on the government, Health Cluster guidance, and the evolution of the pandemic situation. Provision of safe and dignified shelter solutions for differentiated needs of women, girls, boys and men is one of the most fundamental principles of protection and GBV risk mitigation. Shelter is the place where people seek wellbeing, privacy, and safety. The Shelter Cluster has advanced steps in gender mainstreaming through active participation ofthe beneficiaries, especially adolescent girls, women and girls with disabilities, female headed households, older women, in the needs assessment phase and determining the needs of different groups respecting the local culture and norms. Being experienced in the field, cluster partners understand the social and gender dynamics that could help or hinder getting shelter as ‘right’ as possible. Vulnerable FHHs are normally one of the targeted groups in the shelter projects. Designing gendered NFIs packs for IDPs, achieving privacy, consulting women and girls on HH needs like kitchen and sanitation facilities design and location are common practices of gender mainstreaming and active participation of the affected communities. Shelter partners will work together with protection cluster and GBV AoR to integrate GBV risk mitigation efforts into Shelter/NFI programming (i.e. safety audits, gender sensitive, participatory shelter designs etc.)
