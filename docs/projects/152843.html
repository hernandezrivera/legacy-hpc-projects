Project Summary

Juba has become a place of refuge to those fleeing from their war torn counties, the focus for years has been IDPS with in the UN POC yet the IDPs staying outside the UN protection of civilian (PoC) compounds had indicated significant number of conflict-related family separation cases that required assistance. Anecdotal evidence suggests that child, early and forced marriage has increased during the conflict as families use it as a coping mechanism to alleviate economic pressures, clear debts, buy protection or settle disputes. With the disruption of routine activities, change of environment, dropping out of school and missing out on friends, distress increases among both boys and girls. Therefore there is a need for psychosocial support using creative structured age and sex appropriate activities. This project will alleviate the impact of the conflict on children within Juba through the provision of targeted psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification services targeting children and caregivers with an aim of rebuilding ability to reconnect and cope positively.
Renk has also become a safe haven for IDPs and returnees. In Renk and Juba MTT will do the following;
1. Provide structured community-based psychosocial support activities: The conflict has severely affected the psychosocial wellbeing and mental health of children, with some witnessing brutal murders. Additionally, they and their caregivers daily experience difficulties in accessing food, water, education, livelihoods and recreation. In this project, MTT will administer psychosocial support to vulnerable children—including Psychological First Aid and other forms of non-formal psychosocial support as they reintegrate into communities. CFSs will be made to coalesce into communities through community based child protection networks (CBCPNs).
2. Provide parenting skills session to caregivers and community members based on existing tools: Conflict-affected and displaced children risk becoming involved in the worst forms of child labour such as child trafficking, hazardous work or begging in the streets. Through training foster caregivers, MTT will increase the level of children care and protection. This is in realization of the fact that children outside parental care are at more risk of exploitation. The capacity of communities will be increased to identify, report, and refer children with urgent child protection concerns through CBCPNs and existing referral pathways.
3. Support community-based child protection committees to engage in child protection activities:
The project has considered gender and age in planning and implementation. Boys and girls with special needs are targeted. The project will provide life skills and resilience programming to adolescent girls and boys who will be supported to organize peer support activities with children. Women and men will be duly represented in all decision-making organs to provide avenues for maximum input from all groups who will be encouraged to participate in every component of the project with the dynamics of the gender roles in mind.
4. Capacity building of non-child protection workers on CPiE: In collaboration with education and health clusters, capacity built of health workers and teachers with emphasis on girl-child education and provision of dignity kits. Feedback mechanisms are embedded for project flexibility. Nearby primary schools will be identified as partners with whom to mainstream Child Protection and psycho-social support in schools through capacity building events while observing the: "do no harm"

MTT will increase the level of protection and care for children by training foster caregivers within the CFSs. Even as we increase the capacity of communities to identify, report, and refer children with urgent child protection concerns, we will strengthen and establish community-based child protection mechanisms, and raise awareness about existing referral pathways.
