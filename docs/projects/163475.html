Project Summary

This project aims at ensure that vulnerable persons affected by the Displacement in the East Region have ability to exercise and enjoy legal identity and rights to housing, land and property (HLP). Main target are women, men, boys and girls, refugees and host communities. Core activities are Legal identity support and HLP rights protection.

Besoins / Needs

Access to legal identity: the Legal Identity (LI) issues are not only related to the availability of services but also to the high demand. In fact, most of civil registration centers were not functional even before the refugee’s influx (lack of civil servants, lack of equipment). In addition, the host population seems not to value birth registration and other matters related to civil status. According to the preliminary results of the multisectorial assessment conducted by NRC in August 2019, 60% of women and 40% of men reported that they have never possessed a civil or identity document. The situation is even worse for refugees who are coming from a country where the registration system has not been functional for a very long time and who may not be aware of the LI-related legislation in Cameroon. Protection of house, land and property: the arrival of refugees from CAR who are partly composed of pastoralists have increased the friction and conflicts between farmers and pastoralists. During the multisector assessment conducted by NRC, it was reported that those conflicts can be particularly violent, causing secondary displacement of refugees, or back and forth movements of host communities. The access to HLP rights for refugees, especially to farming land and housing is very complicated as some authorities consider that improved access to those rights may be a ‘retention’ factor (i.e. that refugees wouldn’t repatriate if they have access to land and adequate housing). For refugees who need farming land, the access mode is through buying customary rights (28%) and renting (20%) .
