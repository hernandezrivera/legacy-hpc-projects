Project Summary

The project will ensure affected population groups in Somalia have protection, privacy and improve their living conditions through the provision of appropriate emergency shelter assistance, non-food items (NFI) and provision of more durable shelter support and community infrastructure:
Through the construction of 2500 new durable Shelters (15,000 individuals) and infrastructure for returnees, IDPs and host communities. Provision of NFIs to 100,000 households in kind or cash and provision of emergency shelters to 16,500 households in Kind or cash.

Project Activities

Loss of property and lack of security of tenure are common consequences of displacement and major factors in exposing families to risk. The provision of shelter is, therefore, essential to anchoring and sustaining return and reintegration in Somalia. Returnees need adequate, dignified, safe and secure shelter with lockable doors. However, they are frequently constrained by their limited resources, capacities, and skills to construct or undertake desired improvements to existing dwellings. Rental arrangements are often used by returnees where no adequate immediate transitional or permanent shelter is available. Expenditures on rent prevent families from meeting other needs such as education or health. In parallel, land tenure is a key source of insecurity for returnees, and for receiving host communities. Land tenure is also a requirement to protect returnees from forced evictions which have become a regular feature in IDP and other urban settlements, particularly in southern and central regions. As of August 2019, UNHCR statistic indicates that over 91,000 people have voluntarily retuned to Somalia since 2014. In 2020 the operation will priorities to support 17,000 new Somali Returnees. According to shelter cluster Humanitarian Needs Overview, most of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters which offer limited privacy and protection from weather conditions and about one third of population lack NFI. 2.2 million people will require shelter and non-food items assistance in 2020. Of these, 1.55 million are IDPs, 600,248 are vulnerable non-displaced people, 42,000 are refugees and asylum seekers and 17,000 are refugee returnees UNHCR will continue to respond to displacement and emergencies through its tri-clusters approach with the provision of core relief items and emergency shelter kits. UNHCR will use age, gender and diversity approach to address the needs of the affected population. Different population groups including men, women, children, older people and disabled will be consulted and their specific needs will be considered in planning and implementation of the planned interventions. UNHCR will target a total of 699,000 of the 1.4 million targeted individuals with target assistance of basic needs and essential services. The targeting will occur based on UNHCR and clusters assessments for those most in need and highly vulnerable. UNHCR, as part of the Shelter/NFI and CCCM Cluster, will coordinate with other actors to provide assistance as needed. UNHCR will establish and carry out post distribution monitoring to obtain feedback and suggestions and ensure that the right beneficiaries receive the items. Activities: Construction of new durable Shelters and infrastructure established, improved and maintained to support reintegration of returnees, IDPs and host communities. For returnees, UNHCR plans to construct 2500 durable shelter and will explore the provision of cash for shelters and encourage owner-driven approach where applicable. In line with the Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF) approach, UNHCR will aim at developing integrated settlements together with a wide range of partners, including government agencies, other UN agencies and humanitarian partners, as well as the beneficiaries. The integrated settlement will include shelters, schools, health centers, police posts and market/ communal areas. Together with partner organizations, UNHCR will advocate with the government to acquire land for the returnees and IDPs Given the protracted nature of displacement, provision of basic and domestic items remains a priority. UNHCR Somalia will targets to support 100,000 households with NFIs, either in kind or cash and 16,500 households will be supported with emergency shelters kits.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures: (3a) Protection concerns stemming from displacement Forceful evictions remain one of the most severe and prevalent protection concerns throughout Somalia, particularly for IDPs and poor households, due to an underdeveloped regulatory framework, hampered access to justice, low awareness of and respect for HLP rights and a weak protective environment. Both government and private land owners contribute to the forceful evictions of thousands of displacement communities. Families are often displaced without substantial household items. UNHCR aims at countering evictions through different activities.-UNHCR will undertake awareness raising on relevant HLP laws, to ensure that IDPs are aware of their housing, land and property (HLP) rights. -UNHCR will also advocate with government for non-discriminatory application of HLP laws in coordination with partners/clusters. -Inclusion of IDPs in the planning process will be crucial for planning peaceful coexistence activities and achieving implementation that enhances integration. -Awareness raising amongst host communities and IDPs will take place to promote peaceful coexistence, in coordination with peaceful coexistence activities.-Host communities will also be supported alongside the IDP for peaceful coexistence. 3b): Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance -The selection criteria for shelter, CRI and NFI assistance will take into consideration the vulnerabilities and specific needs of the beneficiaries. -Female-headed households with Specific Needs, Persons with Chronic disease and elderly persons are prioritized. -The most vulnerable Returnees and IDPs will be identified through collaboration with Returnees and IDP committees and provided with permanent shelters and support for sustainable livelihoods and basic services. -The design and construction of shelters will preserve the safety and dignity of the affected population.-Gender balance will be considered when setting up distribution teams and committees to ensure enough women representation.- Apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries for distribution to ensure vulnerable persons such as older people are not excluded. (3c) Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services) Displaced women, men, girls and boys in Somalia remain victims of various protection incidents and other human rights violations. Violence is occurring in places of refuge and in areas which are controlled by non-state actors and which are not accessible by humanitarian actors. Continuous displacement and lack of livelihoods has also reduced the capacities of these uprooted communities to access humanitarian and medical aid. -UNHCR will continue to monitor closely all protection incidents occurred within the context of forced displacement in Somalia. By advocating and putting in place response systems, every protection incident should get a referral or direct assistance. - UNHCR will ensure shelter and NFI assistance reach the most vulnerable groups.-Influenced by the HCT’s Centrality of Protection strategy, UNHCR will pilot monitoring in areas which are occupied by armed non-state actors. Especially in South West State and Jubbaland State, individuals will be identified with strong connections in areas which are currently not included in humanitarian programming and assessments. Expected is that these Independent Monitors will feed protection related information to UNHCR and the wider humanitarian community on specific protection needs among these communities.-Central distribution locations will be identified away from conflict areas and at safe distances from the settlement areas. -Post distribution monitoring’s will be conducted obtain feedback and suggestions.

Please provide further explanation

UNHCR, government and partners are seeking to create bridges to bring displacement on to the development agenda and find permanent solutions through local integration. In advancement of the principles of the CRRF, and in line with the whole of society approach, UNHCR will enhance its strategic links with key Government Ministries at the federal level and regional levels. In line with the Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF) approach, UNHCR will develop integrated settlements together with a wide range of partners, including government agencies, other UN agencies and humanitarian partners, as well as the beneficiaries. The integrated settlements will include shelters, a school, a health center, a police post and a market/ communal area. UNHCR will construct durable shelters in return areas and IDPs relocation sites, while the operational partners will construct the structures to provide the other services. Together with the partner organizations, UNHCR will advocate with the government to acquire land for the returnees. UNHCR through its implementing partners will continue to provide permanent shelter to facilitate their reintegration. The Ministries will be responsible to take over the responsibility of the integrated shelter.
