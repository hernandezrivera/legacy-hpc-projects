Project Summary

Objective:
build the resilience and healing of conflict-affected communities NW Syria subdistrict by enhancing their adaptability and recovery capacity.
Outputs:
1. The restoration of agriculture, livestock production, labour market through transitional extension services.
2. Structural rehabilitation of water and sanitation systems mitigaring impact of climate change.
3. Enhance peaceful conflict mangement and social cohesion through Effective early warning and early response mechanisms.
Activities:
1.1 Vegetables value chain support by supporting green housing and solar-powered dripper irrigation systems through voucher modality.
1.2 Promotion of eco-friendly hydroponic agriculture of Azola plan for hyprid livestock fodder
1.3 Market-driven vocational training and startup/ scale up business support for women, youths and men.
2.1 Rehabilitation of irrigaation infrastructure e.g. repair/ provision of irrigation equipment, rehabilitation and seasonal maintenance of cannels, etc.
2.2 Repair and rehabilitation of Jarablus sweage treatement station.
2.3 Rehabilitation and improvement of local landfills to mitigate environmental and public health negative impact.
2.4 Expansion and repair of water supply system in Jarablus subdistrict.
3.1 Establishing 2 early warning and early response networs (1 for women, 1 for men) addressing drivers of conflict and constrains of social cohesion.
3.2 Capacitate local community on conflict transformation, intrest-based negotiation, and community mobilisation.
3.3 Develop peace action strategy adn plan to promote tolerance and coexistence.
3.4 Support community led social cohesion initiatives.

3 - Needs

Households across the country face a gap between incomes and expenditures on essential needs, with 77 per cent of households reporting that their income was insufficient to cover the cost of basic needs (up from 70 per cent in 2021). To cover costs of living, incomes would need to increase by 67 per cent in GoS areas, 53 per cent in NES, and 43 per cent in NWS. This trend further demonstrates the existence and possible expansion of “working poor” households, whose aggregated income is insufficient to meet rising costs of basic needs. This dynamic points to the need for interventions that can raise incomes at the household level in GoS areas, and create livelihood opportunities particularly in the north-east and north-west, where employment rates at the household level are lower.Regarding access to electricity, 73 per cent of households reported having electricity for less than one third of the day, and 15 per cent of households reported having electricity for less than three hours per day. Among households with access to renewable energy sources, 53 per cent have access to electricity for more than 9 hours per day, though may be reliant on multiple sources such as the main network, generators, or batteries to reach this amount. Data on access to electricity and ability to meet basic needs indicate that households having access to more hours of electricity per day are also better able to meet basic needs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks will be analyzed throughout the project life-cycle. According to the project activities, the following protection risks and mitigating measures are presented:1)- Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services Mitigating measures: Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. 2)- Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers)through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low-quality. Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards 2. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ONSUR strongly considers gender aspects at every step of its project design and implementation. A gender analysis was included in this project’s needs assessment. At every stage of the program, men and women will be included engaged, with consideration to the “do no harm approach”, especially those that are influenced or affected by project performance. Furthermore, ONSUR proposes by our project to engage women in decision making, particularly regarding the location of project activities. ONSUR will ensure rigorous follow up through monitoring and evaluation of projects. In addition, Rigorous M&E frameworks will be implemented to record gender differences in project implementation and results. Information will be disaggregated according to gender and results will be shared at the cluster and community level to assist in decision-making. ONSUR will never tolerate any abuse by anyone towards a woman or a girl. As well as, all field staff will signed on the code of conduct, and they will attend PSEA training
