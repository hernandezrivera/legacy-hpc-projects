Project Summary

The project addresses the protection and well being needs of returnee and IDP children and their care givers in Ninewa and Erbil governorates. A variety of tailored community-based psycho-social support activities will be conducted, aimed at strengthening child protection, community resiliencies and peaceful co-existence in the target locations.

Needs

Participatory psycho-social needs and risks assessments conducted by DCA and partners with community members in Mosul and Erbil (May 2018), along with Protection Cluster Needs Assessments, have indicated that returnees and IDPs are in severe need of child protection focused activities. Interviewed parents and feedback from a cross section of community members identified the characteristic behaviours in affected children such as unwillingness to go to school, withdrawal and isolation, difficulties in concentrating, prolonged sadness and unusual crying or screaming. An additional wider range of challenges was identified among boys who were reported to be disruptive, acting out and portraying “more aggressive behaviour”. Girls face difficulties in accessing education and services due to cultural norms and practices, where priority for limited education opportunities in the families is most often given to boys. Girls are at risk of early marriage, meanwhile boys are frequently engaged in child labour. The consequences of prolonged displacement and lack of livelihoods opportunities for adult family members on daily life, have had significant, long-lasting psychological and emotional negative impacts on children. There is a need to keep children safe and enable them to access education through sustainable solutions. Hence, the project, through a variety of PSS and well-being activities, addresses existing stressors and trauma to enable children to feel more calm, increase their communication skills with others, increase their ability to concentrate and thereby help prepare them to re-enter the education system. Boys and girls of all ages continue to suffer protection risks in displacement and upon return to their home with limited after-care support, which can have extremely damaging physical, emotional, social and psychological consequences. Exposure to violence, abuse and exploitation is ongoing with negative impacts on psycho-social well-being and development needs of children. 35,000 children are assessed to be in need of case management services in 2019 (CP Sub-Cluster). Through PSS services, the project identifies extremely vulnerable children to refer to case management. The target communities continue to be disrupted due to the high presence of IDPs and returnees, which causes peaceful co-existence and community-based structures and support to be affected. Often government and community structures are unable to address the care needs of affected children and their families. There is an ongoing need for psycho-social support for at-risk children and their families as well as capacity strengthening of community structures to address these needs in the future.

Activities

Project Activity 1: Project staff will identify children at risk and refer them to case management services and specialized services. The children will be identified during the structured and sustained PSS activities conducted by project staff in community centers.Project Activity 2: The project will provide structured and sustained psycho-social support (PSS) and resilience activities to boys and girls, following a needs-led and culturally sensitive curriculum, including the following activities drama, art, music, games and group activities. Through structured play, children will discover strengths and connect to inner and outer resiliencies that promote wellbeing. Project Activity 3: Project staff will provide skills and knowledge for parents and caregivers to better care for and protect their children. The project will provide community-based family support activities that also promote peaceful co-existence, trust and positive relationship building. Activities will include awareness raising and training on good parenting, stress management, how to recognize signs of trauma in children and how to provide helpful assistance. Project Activity 4: The project will conduct community-led awareness raising activities on child protection issues, focusing on prevention and behaviour changes. Activities will raise awareness about Child Protection issues and enhance community based psychosocial support networks. Activities will be tailored to parents, caregivers and teachers. Project Activity 5: Project staff will organize and facilitate larger, non-structured creative community-based activities that promote trust, positive relationship building and creative interaction between different sections of the community, including peaceful co-existence activities. Examples of such activities may include art exhibitions, music events and sporting events.All project activities are in-line with the Iraq HRP 2019 Strategic Objective 2: Ensure centrality of protection through mitigating protection risks, harmonizing protection approaches, and advocating for adherence to protection norms and applicable legal standards; as well as with the Protection Cluster Objective 1: Provide specialized protection assistance, and community-based support, to vulnerable conflict-affected individuals, thereby enabling them to secure their rights, access humanitarian assistance ad public services, and overcome barriers to achievement of durable solutions.

Protection Risk Analysis

1: Activities intended to promote peaceful co-existence may be perceived as favouring some groups over others and contribute to heightened tensions between different sections of the targeted community. 2: Safety and security risks in project locations can both impact project implementation and not least the security and safety of the target population and project staff.3: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. 4: Low capacity of staff in community centres can cause harm to service users due to risks of re-traumatising and lack of referrals when needed.5: Lack of staff care can cause extreme stressors and burnout among frontline staff. 6: Lack of family support to the beneficiaries participating in the project will limit the impact/benefits.

Mitigation Measures

1: Participatory mapping and in-depth conflict analysis prior to co-development of activities will inform the nature and location of activities. In addition, DCA and partners will conduct all aspects of the project in a non-partisan manner, including the selection of project staff and beneficiaries, following the humanitarian principles of neutrality and impartiality and based on need.2: DCA and implementing partners have designated security focal points who monitor and analyse security threats and trends, and provide regular training to staff. When security tensions are high, project activities are temporarily suspended, to ensure project beneficiaries are not put at risk. 3: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who the services are for. DCA will provide regular training to DCA and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).4: DCA technical advisors monitor staff capacities, provide training as necessary and focus on unlearning of unhelpful, bad practices. Capacity strengthening of staff is ongoing throughout the project period. 5: DCA engages partners to increase their staff-care and facilitates workshops on the topic. DCA also facilitates workshops on staff-care for its own staff.6: The project approach is systemic in sensitising and creating awareness for families in order to get their buy-in.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Communities and local authorities are engaged in community baseline well-being assessments to ascertain needs and enable greater community awareness and ownership of activities that address those needs. Regular follow up and FGD feedback forums are conducted to ensure effective communication is enabled. Changing needs are identified and addressed, with technical support from the Community Based PSS Advisor. Through feedback loops, including the Complaints Handling System, protection concerns will be addressed in a timely manner. Children themselves are enabled to have a voice in assessing their own needs. As a priority for 2019, DCA and partners will work more systematically on engaging children in the design phase.The project M&E places children and parents at the centre of gauging and measuring their own impacts and resilience strengthening. It employs quantitative and qualitative assessments that include: community well-being baseline tool, Focus Group Discussions, pre and post assessments, photo monitoring, facilitator observations, external evaluation. Child safeguarding and PSEA policies are in place and all project staff will be trained on this at the beginning of the project period. DCA and partners establish clearly visible complaint and feedback boxes outside community centers. Committees are set up to review and address complaints, providing timely feedback on how the complaint has been addressed, if appropriate. Sensitive complaints are addressed in a confidential manner by management staff. The Complaints Handling System will be regularly reviewed and any issues arising will be systematically followed up on. Regular action-reflection sessions will identify lessons learnt and be used to feedback into planning.
