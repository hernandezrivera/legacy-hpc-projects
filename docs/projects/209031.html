Project Summary

The focal point of the 2024 nutrition intervention will evolve around the scaling up interventions and enhancing the quality-of-service delivery by execution of life-saving nutrition programs and improving resilience among women of childbearing age and caregivers of children under five years. To achieve this, UNICEF would implement programmes by ensuring the humanitarian-development nexus encompasses the provision of micronutrient supplementation, treatment of children under five with wasting and oedema in the inpatient care and outpatient care, and the provision of the infant and young child feeding programme in emergency. Insights from the 2023 program are crucial in shaping the direction of the 2024 Humanitarian Program Cycle (HPC) Project, steering its alignment with the dynamic landscape of nutrition in Northeast Nigeria. These lessons have profound implications for the Northeast (NE) 2024 Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC), Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) targets, and the NE Nutrition Strategy.
The strategic positioning of the 2024 project involves a deliberate emphasis on addressing the urgent needs within specific Local Government Areas (LGAs) characterized by elevated Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates exceeding 10%. Notable among these LGAs are Maiduguri, Jere, Damboa, Gubio, Kaga, Konduga, Mafa, Magumeri, Marte, Monguno, Nganzai, and Mobbar in Borno; Geidam, Machina, Nguru, Yunusari and Yusufari in Yobe. These areas, often transitioning into serious and critical phases of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN) during lean seasons, harvests, and post-harvest periods, are focal points for intervention.
Particular attention will be accorded to high-risk and hard-to-reach locations, including Kala-Balge/Rann, Bama, Gwoza, Ngala, Dikwa, Marte, Monguno, Nganzai, Mobbar/Damasak, and Guzamala LGAs, housing a substantial number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnee populations. In 2024, UNICEF's primary focus will be on scaling-up and strengthening quality services to acutely malnourished children and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) identified as vulnerable and at significantly high risk of mortality. The nutrition response extends to all under-five children and PLWs through preventive and curative nutrition services such as micronutrient supplementation, ensuring that acute malnutrition levels remain below average thresholds, thereby minimizing morbidity and mortality.
Furthermore, the nutrition response will deliberately prioritize households with persons with disabilities, recognizing their heightened vulnerability and the barriers they face in accessing health, nutrition, and Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services. These individuals are disproportionately food insecure due to pervasive levels of exclusion. Notably, the nutrition status of the targeted population in the BAY states undergoes a substantial decline from May to September each year, coinciding with the lean season and the onset of the rainy season. This period witnesses heightened rates of acute malnutrition, medical complications arising from limited food access, and an increased prevalence of diarrhea, malaria, and measles outbreaks.
To effectively navigate the challenges of the lean season, UNICEF will proactively prepare by securing a robust pipeline and strategically prepositioning supplies. This anticipatory approach aims to augment the overall sector capacity to address acute malnutrition, with a specific focus on inpatient care and Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E). 1 - Needs

Based on the North-East Nigeria Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in June/July 2023, the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates in Borno have seen a slight decrease from 12.3% in September 2022 to 10.2%, while Yobe has experienced a decline from 10.6% to 8.0%. In Adamawa, the GAM rates have remained relatively stable at 4.0%, staying within an acceptable threshold. However, the December 2023 Humanitarian Situation Monitoring (HSM) reveals serious levels of acute malnutrition among new arrivals from inaccessible areas, with overall GAM rates at 16.0% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) at 6.9%. These high rates signify an extremely stressed population grappling with food insecurity, limited water and sanitation access, and suboptimal health conditions as the primary drivers of acute malnutrition. Projections for 2024 estimate that over 1.5 million children under five and 206,779 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will suffer from acute malnutrition, necessitating highly specialized nutrition, supplemental feeding, and health services to prevent a surge in mortality. To address this, there is a critical need to significantly strengthen and scale up nutrition treatment services, including outpatient therapeutic programs, supplemental/blanket feeding programs, micronutrient supplementation, and the promotion of maternal, infant, and young children feeding practices.

2 - Activities

Proposed Activities to Address Nutritional Challenges: To effectively meet the nutritional needs of children under five and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in the BAY States, UNICEF proposes a comprehensive set of activities aligned with specific strategic objectives:1. Strategic Objective 1: Strengthen Preventative Nutrition Services• Distribution of Micronutrient Powders (MNP) for point-of-use fortification among children aged 6 - 23 months.• Distribution of Vitamin A supplementation for children 6 – 59 months of age• Skilled Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counseling for PLW.2. Strategic Objective 2: Improve Access to Quality Curative Nutrition Services• Procurement of nutrition supplies/commodities (RUTF, F75, F100, ReSoMal, weighing scales, height boards and medications).• Management of children aged 0 -59 months with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and Severe oedema through Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP) and stabilization centers (SC).3. Strategic Objective 3: Reinforce Appropriate Coordination and Strengthen Situation Monitoring• Ensuring proper Nutrition Sector Coordination. • Conducting nutrition surveys and joint assessments in collaboration with other sectors such as Food Security, WASH, and Health.• Implementing Cash & Voucher Assistance (CVA) for nutrition outcomes.These proposed activities aim to enhance the management of acute malnutrition in BAY states by ensuring timely screening, referrals, and comprehensive care for SAM children, both with and without medical complications, within outpatient therapeutic centers (OTP) and Stabilization Centers (SCs). The project emphasizes the integration of SAM case management and MIYCN into existing health services, utilizing government staff at the local government area (LGA) level.
