Project Summary

Action Against Hunger proposes an integrated intervention through livelihood based project in responses to the impact of conflict and climate change in six counties known for consistent severe food insecurity and high rates of malnutrition in the three states of South Sudan. The proposed intervention aims at saving lives and addressing the immediate, medium and long term needs of the affected population while improving access to improved agriculture production and adaptation to climate change to increase community preparedness, resilience and adaptability capacities to enhanced agricultural production, food and nutrition security, livelihood improvement, and economic strengthening of households, there by improving the food and nutrition security of the most vulnerable affected internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities. Action Against Hunger in response to the ongoing food insecurity proposes a livelihood and climate change adaptation project for the ongoing conflict and flood associated negative impacts in the six counties of the three states of South Sudan. Through this intervention, Action Against Hunger will equip communities with knowledge, technical skills, support services, and tools they would need to prevent future shocks and sliding into food insecurity and ultimately into famine ensuring that the communities understand the effects of climate change, are disaster ready and can adapt to climate change impact, utilizing local driven innovative solutions to mitigate the risks they face. The proposed action aimed at addressing the immediate and medium term needs and building community resilience by improving agriculture livelihood service therefore improving the food and nutrition security of the conflict and flood affected communities and increasing their resilience to hunger and disaster risk and shocks mainly intercommunal violence, the impact of Sudan crisis and flooding.
The project target primarily conflict and flood affected communities of IDP, returnees and host communities affected by the negative impact of prolong climate change and conflict related crisis in IPC 3,4 to 5, particularly limited access to agriculture inputs and cash for essential food and HHs needs. The project aim to increase climate smart production and climate-risk reduction actions for enhanced adaptation of communities with greatest vulnerability to the negative impact of climate change. The key interventions proposed through this action are cash based assistances and livelihood support as part of rapid responses; agriculture production, fishing inputs provisions and climate adaptation measure with training packages on improved agronomic practices including improved kitchen gardening; nutrition sensitive agriculture, anticipatory action preparedness (DRR), financial inclusion and marketing participation support and pest/disease of economic importance responses.
The proposed design is informed by the findings of the IPCs October 2023 and multi-sectorial assessments, conflict sensitivity , security and risk analysis and recommendations which have been conducted and released in the last six months.
The proposed response is designed in line with Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) of FSL Cluster and contribute to the HRP 2023 Strategic Objectives (SO1, SO2 and SO3). The Action aims at reaching out to 149,618 people (at least 60% female), which fall under IPC 5, 4 and 3 in three States of South Sudan, more specially Northern Bahr ElGhazal (Aweil East, Aweil Center), Jonglei (Fangak) and Warrap (Tong East,Twic, Gogorial East) with a budget of 2,514,045 dollars.

1. Needs

South Sudan compounded by complex and interlinked causes, exacerbated by armed and inter communal conflict, annual floods over the past four years. Mass displacement, high levels of food insecurity and disease outbreaks including COVID-19 triggered an escalating humanitarian and protection crisis. The country remains one of the most food insecure countries globally and in the East Africa region, with over 60 percent of the population anticipated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes expected to be acutely food insecure between June and September 2024. The 2023 South Sudan Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) projected 9.4 million people in need (PIN) of humanitarian assistance. Of this are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children (2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys) and 15% disabled persons. In comparison to 2022, the number of PIN has increased by 500,000 individuals (OCHA 2022). This increase is largely attributed to protracted conflict, worsening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, recurrent flooding, and inadequate access to basic commodities and services. The November 2023 IPC report further indicates 5.83 million people (46.3 %) experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity( IPC Phase 3) or worse with 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe).The same report indicated that 1.65 million children (6-59 months) and 870,000 pregnant/ breastfeeding women are acutely malnourished. The September 2023 FEW NET report highlighted the severity levels of 28 counties of severe concerns expected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) at the peak of the lean season. The main drivers of the crisis were categorized in to two; i)covariate climatic, conflict & with economic shocks such as floods and dry spells, high food price rises, and the continued disruption of livelihoods ; ii) household level idiosyncratic stressors such gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services. These perennial issues and other household (HH) stressors such as gender-based violence (GBV) and protection issues constitute key drivers of South Sudan’s humanitarian needs. In addition, the outbreak of conflict in Sudan in April 2023, has led to influx of 20,610 refugees and 224761 returnees putting pressure on already scarce resources compromising the already dare situation of the vulnerable communities . Analysis of Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives (JMMI, September 2023) and WFP/VAM (November 2023) shows the increased trend of market prices of basic commodities (food and non food) in target locations worsen by the blockage of supply routes from Sudan . An intersection of the crisis of community conflict, sub-national organized violence, prolong dry spelt, and flooding exacerbated the food insecurity, malnutrition and already extreme levels of GBV and other protection mainly sexual abuses in women and girls in target locations including people with disabilities among the most vulnerable due to shocks. ACF in response to the 2024 HRP will support 6 counties, specifically Aweil east, Aweil Center, Gogorial West, Tonj East, Twic and Fangak selected based on acute food insecurity analysis that falls under IPC 5,4,3.ACF has operational presence and experiences in the areas, good partnership and potential integration with ongoing multi-sectoral programs in the areas and it's against this backdrop that ACF proposes this Action in response to the humanitarian crisis by implementing life-saving, short term emergency and recovery livelihood based interventions in the 6 target locations. The proposed response is developed in line with the Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) of the FSL Cluster and Strategic Objectives (SO1, SO 2 and SO3) of HRP 2024 considering minimum guidelines priorities and standards relevant building on each CO. The action targets 149,618 food insecure people in the IPC 5,4 and 3 locations for 12 months.

2. Project activities

Action Against Hunger proposed all the three cluster objectives with aim CO1 will build on CO2 and CO2 to build on CO3 in the subsequence year hence the cash and livelihood based intervention in meeting its specific objectives 1; Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for 38,684 vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5). outcome1.1: improved access to food and income needs of the most vulnerable to reduce high vulnerability to food insecurity and malnutrition of HHs. The outputs and activities under cluster objective will contribute to HRP 2023 SO1:output1.1 Target household received food assistance to meet immediate food and income needs .Activity 1.1.1 Provision of food assistant jointly with WFP. Activity 1.1.2 Provision of cash assistant through Assets creation in areas affected by climate change impact and with functioning market, Activity 1.1.3 Awareness and sensitization on nutrition foods values and importance. To respond under cluster Strategic objective 2, The outputs and activities under cluster objective 2 will contribute to HRP 2023 SO1 and SO2:Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs and fishing support, out put 2; support and engage HHs in nutrition sensitive climate smart agriculture food production to meet short and medium term food needs. Activities 2.1.1 Distribute emergency crop kits (local sourcing through seed fair) and purchase in areas with limited seeds, 2.1.2 Distribution of vegetable kits to engage HHs in vegetable farming /kitchen gardening, Activity 2.1.3 Distribution of fishing kits in areas of potential access for fishing activity mainly along the river and swampy areas, Activity 2.1.4 Training of agricultural input and fishing kit beneficiaries on improved farming and fishing practices and use of newly adapted technologies in a way that minimize risk of environment pollutions, Activity 2.1.5 Training of community nutrition volunteers and mother to mother support groups in the production of nutritious foods (mainly diverse vegetables), cooking and feeding practices including practical cooking demonstration .outputs and Activities under Cluster objective 3 which will contribute to HRP 2023 SO3: Output 3.1 Increase resilience to disaster risk and shocks by supporting 110,934 people with inputs and trainings. Activity 3.1.1 support HHs with improve agronomic practices (FFS, demo plots, post harvest management). Activity 3.1.3 Support vegetable producers with irrigation equipment and trainings, Activity 3.1.4 Support small holders producers with ox-ploughs to increase acreage of land cultivated. Activity 3.1.4 support seed multiplication groups by establishing local seeds storage facility using available local materials (cost sharing initiative). Under out put 3.2, Activity 3.2.1 provide start up capital and mentorship to IGA groups .Activity 3.2.3 Support community with VSLA kits and training and linking to IGAs. Activity 3.2.3 Support HHs participation in product aggregation, marketing and market linkages. Under output 3.3 Activity 3.3.1 HHs participate in construction and restoration activities through cash to respond to building resilience to potential increasing climate risk and shocks, Activity 3.3.2 Support communities to conduct forecast based Anticipatory actions against climatic shocks, Activity 3.3.3 distribute storage bags to HHs(hermetic bags, tilted buckets, grain bags), Activity 3.3.4 Support HHs with climate smart flood resilience inputs and approaches (low land rice, floating gardens) to enable access to food needs. Activity 3.3.5 Provide training on DRR, NRM and agroforestry and construction of DRR infrastructures. Activity 3.3.6 support HHs to construct fuel saving energy stoves using locally available material. Activity 3.3.7 Support HHs to established economic tree seedling nursery bed and managements.
