Project Summary

The WHO project aim is to decrease the avoidable mortality and morbidity related to water, vectors, and hospital acquired infections among 1,937,500 affected population in Blue Nile, Central Darfur, East Darfur, Gadaref, Kassala, North Darfur, Red Sea, South Darfur, South Kordofan, and West Darfur.
WHO will also support the MoH and partners to implement integrated vector management campaigns in response to acute events (such as outbreaks of vector borne diseases) and for the mitigation of the imminent health threats created by floods, and displacement.
To decease avoidable mortality and morbidity it is essential to reduce the burden of vector and also water related disease below the epidemic threshold among targeted IDPs and vulnerable communities in Blue Nile, Central Darfur, East Darfur, Gadaref, Kassala, North Darfur, Red Sea, South Darfur, South Kordofan and West Darfur. This will be achieved through a comprehensive package of water safety activities: water quality monitoring and surveillance in targeted high risk locations; community based integrated prevention and control of water and vector borne diseases, and biomedical hazard control at health facility level.
The water quality activities will be implemented by the MOH and WASH partners at state and national level with sustained support from WHO, complementing the UNICEF WASH intervention. This year, the focus will be at locality level, to promote efficiency by reducing the cost of sample transport from field to states capital cities and enabling the local authorities to conduct regular water sampling and testing in prioritized high risk locations. The main activities related to this component includes training of environmental officers/workers (MOH and WES), community volunteers and hygiene promoters on water testing using portable kits; sanitary inspection of water sources; proper water sources maintenance and house-hold chlorination, treatment and storage. The results of the regular monitoring of water sources quality and sanitary conditions will allow timely identification of health threats and implementation of corrective measures through sharing with all relevant partners. Close information sharing and consultations with Health sector will facilitate the early warning on identified public health alerts related to water and vector transmission and joint planning of control measures.
WHO comprehensive package of vector control activities includes; training of the environmental health officers, CHW workers from MOH/WES in the selected 16 localities/locations on the integrated vector management and entomological surveillance, printing of the IEC material on the common vector, technical and logistic support to vector control campaigns and vector surveillance mission. The implementation will take into account the specific demographics of the IDP camps in targeted states with high population density, and also be directed by health information data on prevalence and trends of the diseases.

Needs

Access to effective sanitation facilities and the practice of good sanitation and hygiene behaviors are critical for human health, dignity and economic development. The situation in Sudan is complex with a combination of protracted and new emergencies in rural and urban contexts and challenges in accessing all areas, particularly in JM areas in Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile. As per the last MICS (2014) and also supported by several recent assessments 29% of the population of Sudan currently still practices open defecation, only 33% have access to improved sanitation (excreta disposal), 68% have access to an improved water source and only 40% of the population have a dedicated location for hand-washing, with only 29% also having water and soap at this location. It is estimated that only 62% of schools and 59 % of HFs(HFS 2017 FMOH) have latrines and 20% have a place for hand-washing with soap and 85% have garbage around the school. Environmental health management and infection control in health facilities faces many challenges (51% have no water supply) including a lack of allocated budget for maintenance, lack of monitoring and auditing system, although work is on-going to improve the situation. During 2018 there were large scale multiple outbreaks including; vector borne diseases; chikungunya with 40,000 cases, malaria 1,7 million cases, dengue fever 1000 cases and suspected Rift Velley Fever and expectations of increasing challenges with the progress of climate change. Vector related surveillance capacities to predict/warn on potential outbreaks are weak or non-existent in most targeted States and regular maintenance of spraying equipment requires improvement. There is increasing resistance to key insecticides and a need to build community capacities to use mechanical means of protection. Water borne diseases including large outbreak of AWD, dysentery (225,323 cases), typhoid (170,000 cases) remain a big challenge triggered by use of open sources and water transfer by donkey cart. Only 35% of the targeted Localities have some drinking water surveillance system. There is a lack of trained human capacity at Locality level as well as limited equipment and supplies (source is WHO/MoH water quality monitoring system 2018) Most health facilities do not have strong EH/S&H/WASH systems, with most medical waste being mixed with general waste and disposed of at landfill. Where incinerators exist they tend to be traditional single chamber versions. Health care and hazardous wastes pose particular challenges for Sudan. Currently there is limited segregation of biological and House Hold waste and much is disposed of in general landfill or standard disposal sites.During the planning phase of the project different age groups are involved in the designing of the activities and targets, this was through the interviews and lesson learned during the previous outbreaks and the meetings with WASH committees developed by the WHO and partners.

Activities

Integrated Vector control: • Including flies and mosquito control, and hygiene promotion campaigns for behavioral changes, indoor treatment of breeding sites. The use of female promoters with increased utilization by female audience at HH level •Analysis the data collected through vector surveillance linked to epidemiological data for targeted interventions response to outbreaks and during the raining season. • Revision, printing and dissemination of the vector control guidelines. • Capacity building of the environmental health staff (MOH and NGOs) in integrated vector control management in all targeted States. • Promote and facilitate with MOH, and municipalities an integrated approach for vector control activities • Continue the establishment of vector surveillance sentinel sites in all major camps – define the SOPs, provision of tools and supplies, training of the relevant staff, collection and interpretation of data, and logistic and operational support. Water quality: •Conduct reactive water sources monitoring and quality testing for identification of sources of infection during alerts of outbreaks of water borne diseases. Disseminate the results to all relevant WASH and Health partners for guiding control and response activities. • Together with the WASH sector/UNICEF/WES/MOH implement the water safety plans in all major camps in targeted states. • Increase the capacity of FMOH, SMOH, WES and partners for water quality testing, water source surveillance and water safety plans • Supplies and reagents for 3 state water quality laboratories and construction and establishment of 5 new water quality laboratory. • Update, complete, and disseminate 100 % of water sources surveillance for Red Sea, Gadaref and Kassala State• Monitor, supervise, and undertake water sampling and testing and sanitary inspections of sources in targeted camps and vulnerable communities jointly with state MOHs and NGOI partner's support.

Impact on environment/natural resources

WHO is giving special attention to the protection of the environment and natural resources during the implementation of project with medium impact on the environment. The main hazard is the waste generated by the vector control chemical activities. This hazard is reduced with several strategies; promote alternative methods to the chemical management and usage of the insecticide such as the habitat and environmental control, and reducing contact with the incriminated vectors. However chemical control is still considered as the most important element in the integrated control of vector borne diseases especially during the outbreaks where the vector density exceeded the reference threshold. During such situation WHO has already developed plan for the proper disposal of the chemical generated during the process and accordingly WHO will ensure that the staff are trained and the necessary supplies are made available.
