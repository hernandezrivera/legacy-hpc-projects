Project Summary

The project will improve access to water through rehabilitation of existing water systems. NCA will implement a comprehensive approach to WASH by coupling infrastructure development, community mobilization and behaviour change communication to improve sanitation and hygiene practices. WASH committees will be established and trained in community mobilisation, while capacity building training will be conducted for the local water mechanics who have been working with NCA on the previous projects.
This project will address multiple issues such as WASH, Livelihoods, Health, Nutrition and Shelter/NFI with an integrated approach. Activities will be geared towards improving food security and access to water. , Women and young girls particularly will be less vulnerable to GBV. Victims of GBV will have access to quality treatment and referral options.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in South Sudan continued to cause displacement of large number of people in to neighboring countries. Sudan is now home to 854,000 South Sudanese refugees whose number started to increase in 2017, and of which about 24,600 are living in Both Al Radom and Bilel refugee camps in South Darfur. The arrival of the South Sudanese refugees has started to create pressure on the already weak infrastructure in Darfur. Basic infrastructure in Darfur is damaged or destroyed and disrupted trade, employment and investment in much-needed development of the region has diminished because of the Darfur conflict. The South Sudanese refugees in Darfur have limited access to livelihoods, WASH and Health services. This combined with impacts of the ongoing economic crisis in Sudan which highly affected purchasing power of refugees calls for immediate attention. The health status of many refugees is still poor, and no access to specialized Sexual and Gender-based Violence (SGBV) treatment services in both camps. NCA is part of the UNHCR’s 2019 and 2020 South Sudanese Refugee Response plan for Sudan. In line with the response, NCA will focus on achieving the following objectives: (i)Addressing ongoing and unmet protection and basic services needs among the existing refugee caseload and improving service provision to meet sectoral standards; and (ii) Contributing towards building self-reliance among refugees and host communities and sustainability of interventions across the response. NCA will build on successes and draw lessons from the ongoing multiyear project, which is funded by SIDA through ACT Church of Sweden (ACT Church of Sweden ref nr 501066). NCA will work towards improving access to WASH, Food Security and SGBV services for an estimated 44,600 (24,530 Male, 20,070 Female) South Sudanese refugees and host communities..

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Solid waste of all households of the settlements as well as commercial waste is removed stored safely and removed regularly with equipment and frequency- to avoid environmental pollution. Risk of solid waste pollution of environment is kept to a minimum through communities’ sensitisation and campaigns.Medical waste at the PHC is managed separately according to standards tproo minimize risk. Household latrines are designed in way that is not affecting the water bearing formation (depth should not exceed 3 meters to ensure safety of environment and leave 0.5 meters on top before filled up to ensure safety of people from contamination). The FSL sector will focus on introducing agricultural techniques such as homestead gardening and mulching with positive impact on the environment.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Given that NCA is not only a certified member of the CHS but also a signatory to the Protection Call to Action, a Do No Harm (DNH) approach is taken throughout the project cycle. Gender-sensitive responses have been included into the proposed action and are based on the varied needs of men, women, boys and girls. Thus, gender and age disaggregated data has been provided in this proposed action and will continue to be shared through all future reporting. Given the Darfur context, protection needs specific to women and children have been considered and included in this project. This has been done to ensure their safety, security and dignity are maintained and can be exemplified by GBV responses included in the proposal. GBV is a real problem, often very inadequately addressed within these communities, where traditional norms may prevent appropriate support and counselling being sought.Plans have been made to incorporate sensitization messages on the importance of women’s inclusion in community decision making processes and awareness sessions on SGBV.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As certified towards the Core Humanitarian Standards on Quality and Accountability (CHS), NCA commits to implementing the CHS commitments in all aspects of our work. In line with its efforts to ensure programme sustainability, NCA is committed to safeguarding NCA’s internally defined sustainability standards related to gender and conflict sensitivity, environmental impact, accountability, inclusion, human rights and anti-corruption. Addressing anti-corruption as a key sustainability issue. NCA verifies the financial management skills and competences of partners as well as follow all NCA standardised procedures on procurement. NCA ensures that Do No Harm principles, promoting non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety, dignity, participation, and empowerment and accountability measures become an integral part of every response. NCA considers differentiated needs that women, men, girls and boys may have and gives attention to persons with special needs, such as elderly, disabled or chronically ill people. NCA prioritizes (1) Safety & security, and dignity; (2) Meaningful access, including for women, children, persons with disabilities or other persons with special needs; (3) Accountability, for example through community-based feedback mechanism; and (4) Information, consultation, participation and community empowerment. More information on beneficiary selection, gender and disability inclusion is in the introduction section of this proposal. Protection and feedback/alert systems are detailed in the attachment on the Code of Conduct implementation plan. NCA works with grassroots community structures such as the WASH committees, Nutrition volunteers and lead farmers who form the backbone of the community feedback mechanism. Regular meetings with communities will be conducted to assess progress made and receive any feedback from the communities. This will be used to review the project approaches and further fine tune activities.
