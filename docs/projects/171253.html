Project Summary

The proposed interventions aims to increase the resiliency and coping capcities of vulnerable populations in Area C of the Jordan Valley and Tubas, at risk of displacement, to deal with threats related to their protection from natural and manmade hazards, while simultaneously responding to COVIDfinancial impact and PAs reduced capacity to address the service gap needs in particular for Bedouin and herding communities in Area C.
The response will achieve this through a tailored and integrated modality of assistance that responds to HRP SOs 1,2 &3, and Shelter Cluster Priorities (1,2 & 3) addressing the needs of 17 shelter and protection vulnerable marginalized sub localities in the governorates of Jericho and Tubas (AL Dawaheek Near Aqbet Jaber Reugee Camp /Fasayil al Fauqa/ An Nuwei'ma (Al Foqa) AL rashayedeh /Al Jiftlik-abu al 'Ajaj (Tal Smadi) /Al Jiftlik-ash-Shuneh / Mantikat Masna'a Al Hadeed/Al Mashru' Bedouins - Al Jahaleen Community /Al Mu’arrajat east/ Khirbet Tell el Himma ((khirbet al himeh + khirbet hamad)/Humsa - Al Bqai'a ( Humsa al Foqa)/Khirbet Samra /Ibziq /Al Farisiya-Nab'a al Ghazal /Al Farisiya-Khallet Khader/Ein al Hilwa/ Al Farisiya-Ihmayyer)
In particular, the response will support the mitigation of immediate harm for displaced/at risk of displacement populations through:
• Provision of protective shelter materials to 50 families under protection concerns (settler violence )
• Provision of temporary shelter assistance to 74 Families suffering from temporary displacement
Further the response will contribute to ensuring “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity with a particular focus on COVID-19 mitigation” through:
• Expansion &/or Rehabilitation of 136 substandard shelters or in need of winterization summarization mitigation including adaptation meet needs of PWDs, and r gender privacy COVID-19 isolation spaces and HH WASH facilities.
• Provision of dignified HH hygiene & disinfection materials to 250 identified HHs including those benefiting from protection materials in alignment with health standards and guidelines for COVID-19 mitigations.
Lastly, the response will work to Increase local capacities to respond to shocks in a timely, efficient and effective matter through:
• Capacity building of local NGO’s, community committees, municipal forums, and volunteers, by training and tools for emergency response, self-recovery, or protection measures to be able to support IDPs and affected people during and post emergencies
o 16 gender segregated Training of established CBPSCs as a COVID-19 and protection task force in localities, and legal mitigation in cooperation with legal aid actors.
o 34 gender segregated COVID-19 guidance trainings for target communities in partnership with Directorates of health and other health sector actors.
MA’AN adopts shelter response protection and risk mitigation measures in alighment with “ Do No Harm” priciples and will ensure the privacy and needs of marginalized groups including ( women, children, elderly and PWDs) are accounted for in tailored individual HH level assistance, further informed by findings of conducted needs assessments and previous experience. The proposed interventions aims to complement repsonses proposed by MA’AN partners and other aid actors amidst increased threats to steadfastness of Area C populations on their land and exacerbated impact of COVID-19 on their living conditions. The intervention at the community level assistance will be coordinated with all relevant stakeholders to ensure an effective approach including MoH and directorates, Legal and protection aid actors, and community members.
The intervention has been coordinated with other shelter response actors to ensure no overlap and complementarity with previous HF and current HRP submissions. ( elaboration in top indicators section due to limited space)

1 - Needs

The fragmented Palestinian territorial Sovereignty has many ramifications including inability to enforce the rule of law, undertake physical and socio-economic development planning, construct residential and public service facilities, or provide adequate level of public services and infrastructure. The pervasive poverty and unemployment is compounded and caused by occupation regimes restrictions and COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated existing vulnerabilities for those living below poverty line. MA’AN and shelter cluster partner informed the 2020 Shelter vulnerability assessment in Area C. The updated assessment for these communities as of August 2020 showed that there is an urgent need to rehabilitate at least 3,200 existing shelters. The oPt 2020 Gender analysis further stated 457 structures were demolished in WB and EJ and 547 Palestinians displaced in 2020 through 7 September, emphasizing that Female-headed HHs are also at particular risk as economic insecurity may leave them vulnerable to forced displacement or eviction. In addition, MA’AN has ongoing shelter projects in targeted governorates and an inhouse database for all shelter applications and assessments. alongside referencing OCHAs settler attacks DB, MA’AN has conducted assessment field visits during Sep to targeted localities and was able to identifiy from the collective findings of the assessment 17 communities located in the governorates of Tubas and Jericho with Demonstrated med-severe shelter vulnerability, which has been exacerbated by the occupations annexations plans,discriminatory planning procedures and practices, Expansion of settlement and increased settler violence.Specifically MA’AN has identified 50 HHs from 4 localities which have experienced contained harassment(settler intimidation, demolition of livelihood & residential infrastructure,) demonstrating a protection risks due to settler violence as follows:• Al Farisiya Nab’a al ghazal: 14 HH The community of Al Farisiya Naba’ al Ghazal lives across the street from Rotem settlement and has a history of incidents with settler violence practiced against them. In the area of Al Farisiyeh and especially the area of Al Farisiya-Khallet Khader and Al Farisiya-Ihmayyer, 2 new settlement caravans have been established nearby the communities and are preventing herding activities and narrowing the landscape around them, which eventually threatens their existence as herding is their main source of income. • Khirbet Tell el Himma: 20 HH in (khirbet al himeh + khirbet hamad) A new settlement/farm for one settler family has been established nearby the location. This impacted the herding spaces as the settler does not allow herding activity near his settlement. This is issue is alarming for future possible escalation and attacks on the families of Khirbet tell el hima . • Khirbet Samara:6 HH 2 new New settlement caravans have recently been established near the community.• Ain El hilwa: 10 HH Ain El Hilweh community is very close to Muskoyot settlement and has been long suffering from settlers attacks preventing the community from reaching the natural spring that is the only source of water for their animals. In addition to the need for protective shelter materials, Timely access to health services in cases of emergencies is limited, distribution of protective assets including first aid kits,fire extinguishers are a priority.MA’AN has also identified 74 HH in 4 Tubas localities (Humsa - Al Bqai'a ( Humsa al Foqa),ibZiq, Alfarsiya- khalet khader, Alfarsiye-ihmayyer) in need of temporary shelter assistance due to IOF closures. According to consultations with Bet’selem, the identified HHs are temporarily displaced by an average of 3 times per year and are in need of protective shelter materials including bedding. The COVID-19 Pandemic has further increased shelter vulnerability as socio-economically disadvantaged poor families living in Area C substandard shelters struggle to find suitable isolation areas within their homes due to overcrowding and lack of separated spaces. They are in need to improve the covered areas, provision of the hygienic and disinfectant materials and separating the shared facilities like toilets and kitchens The risk is further heightened if temporary bans on Palestinian workers in settlements and Israel are lifted, as their HH members will be at a higher risk of contagian. MA’AN has identified 126 HH in the localities of ( AlMu’arrajat East, Aldawaheek near Aqbet Jaber camp, Tal smadi in Ajjiftlik abu ajaj, Mantikat masna’a al hadeed. Aljahaleen in alMAshrue Bedouins, Al jiftlik ashuneh, Alrashaydeh in An Nuwei’ma Al foqa, Fasayil Al,foqa) in need of substandard shelter upgrade and/or protection and privacy spaces. Some have further shown need for rehabilitation of WASH facilities. Further all identified HHs are in need of support with material assistance to mitigate COVID-19 as disinfectant materials are expensive and more frequently consumed. As construction in Area C is limited due to IOF discriminatory planning, there is an evident need for temporary isolation spaces for home quarantine. MA’ANs assessment with communities revealed that HHs would refrain from reporting cases of contagian to not distance family member. This practice would however place other HHs members in danger of contagian. MA’AN consulted communities on the acceptability of provision of individual isolation tents/ bedding and hygience kits. Further, the lack of appropriate WASH structures in homes pose a burden for managing daily household tasks for women. In HH with multiple wives, women suffer from lack of privacy comfort & lack of decent living spaces. Large HH size and small shelter also poses a challenge for the privacy and safety of boys and girls as in some cases over 9 children occupied only one room. Female headed HHs, the elderly and disabled have less capacity to respond to emergencies and ensuring protection of their children.

2 - Activities

MA’AN will lead a coordination meeting with Shelter response stakeholders to map out plan and explore special regulatory considerations for program implementation in the 17 target localities. Further, Community Based Project Support Committees will be formed in each location , a known MA’AN approach, CBPSC’s inform the needs of the communities and the different marginalized groups. The CBPSCs will serve an integral role in increase accountability, information dissemination and transparency and report protection concerns throughout the response and will be comprised of representatives from ( local council/municipality, schools, clinics, and community members including Women and PWDs). The project will also seek coordinated support from partner organizations for legal follow-up and specific protection concerns, taking into account socio-economic and protection vulnerabilities through responsive, remedial and preventive support. Through monitoring in the field, any arising case of HH in need of legal aid support will be referred to WBPC partners and sub-implementers (NRC, JLAC, St. Yves, etc.). Protection cases (ie GBV, child protection concerns) will be safely referred to relevant protection actors through the Protection cluster and existing referral pathways. Risk assessments will be conducted with communities to mitigate risks associated with provision of humanitarian assistance in Area C, including legal advice to partners and beneficiaries to ensure safe intervention,, legal awareness sessions in target communities to inform beneficiaries of risks, legal avenues and resorts before, during and after the intervention.1.1 Provision of protective shelter materials to 50 families (275 individuals) under protection concerns (settler violence ) in 4 identified communities (Khirbet Tell el Himma , Khirbet Samra, Al Farisiya-Nab'a al Ghazal, Ein al Hilwa). 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Addressing the needs of full community clusters and involving community members as leaders throughout the project’s implementation, the proposed intervention seeks to minimize implementation risk and enforce protection standards for the “do no harm” policy. Community leaders will be involved throughout every phase of project implementation. The security situation is monitored closely on an ongoing basis by MAAN to ensure timely advice and relevant security precautions can be implemented. Further MA’ANs local coordinators and continoues presence in the communities along with the CBPC members will report regularly on any risks to safety and security during implementation to mitigate time and effectiveness impact. When needed, MA’AN has depended security and safety approaches to infrastructure work in Area C where. To ensure safety and security of HH’s and beneficiaries during implementation and in line with the “Do No Harm” principles, MA’AN ensures that infrastructure work in all its forms in Area C do not affect the arial view of shelter dimensions in the community to reduce chances of future demolition orders. Further, during provision of Materials, MA’AN ensures all material provided are hidden under existing structures to avoid confiscation. When needed, provision and rehabilitation work is done during night hours as a response to security and financial threats in the target Areas. The communities targeted by the project are very risky locations (surrounded by settlements or under ICA threat of demolition), therefore MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during week-ends) in coordinated times with the community committee and beneficiary to assess the security situation of the community. -MA’AN mobializes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. And when possible in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines procures from local sources. - MA’AN has been working for many years in the Jordan Valley, including for shelter projects, and have a good relationship with the communities, so they will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time. Further MA’AN will remain in contact with various partners working on protection and legal cases and reporting incidents. Aligning with the “Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through Self-rehabilitation and project is completed with project closeout. In instances where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the shelter rehabilitation, MA’ANs technical team will directly supervise the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households , persons with disabilities …etc).

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The response activities have been designed to address the tailored needs of community members. The criteria will focus on the socio economic conditions of the households as well as on the housing conditions and the gender make up of the household. As women are responsible for the household in Palestinian society, the number of females in the household will also be an important part of the application process. women headed households, seniors living alone, and PWDs are disproportionately affected by poor housing conditions, such as those in the selected villages thus these groups will be a prioritized during the selection of beneficiaries. Further, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through Self-rehabilitation and project is completed with project closeout. In instances where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the shelter rehabilitation, MA’AN technical team will directly implement the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households , persons with disabilities …etc). Moreover, protective assets will be distributed to HHs meetings this criteria based on the rapid technical assessment of the communities. Shelter rehabilitation/ upgrade and winterization will be conducted for these groups by the projects technical teams including provision of material, and conducting the rehabilitation work instead of provision of material and supervision. The shelter technical needs for selected households will be evaluated individually and customized to meet the needs of the groups in terms of access, safety, protection and privacy rather than a standardized implementation for all shelters. Moreover, the CBPC will ensure representation of these marginalized groups and their needs will be accounted for in the customized protection of DRP trainings in communities.
