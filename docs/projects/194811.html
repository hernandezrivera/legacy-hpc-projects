Project Summary

The IRC’s Integrated Protection program in Gedaref and Blue Nile, will provide lifesaving specialized gender-based violence prevention and response services to support the old and new returning refugees and other most vulnerable women and girls at risk, such as elderly women, pregnant women, breastfeeding women, women and girls with disabilities, and female-headed households (HHs). The project will support the integration of reproductive health and GBV services to ensure gender-based violence (GBV) survivors can access safe, confidential, and timely clinical care for the harmful health consequences both physical and emotional. The project will also focus on delivering lifesaving Child Protection (CP) services including specialized case management and PSS, prevention and response to child separation from family, and promoting family-based -care in the child’s best interest to ensure that children are safe in their homes and receive support when they experience harm. The IRC will implement this intervention through existing Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) in Tunaydbah refugee camp where the IRC's Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) curriculum will be implemented as well as integrate/set up temporary SHLS within the health facility in Blue State. The IRC will also promote community participation in all stages of the project and engage community members to further mitigate potential risks for children and adolescents.
Additionally, the IRC will introduce activities to assist in reducing the risk of water-borne diseases and vector control within the catchment areas of the supported Health Facilities through risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) activities by involving IRC Integrated protection. The IRC will target the refugees in both Blue Nile and Gedaref states, affected by WASH-related diseases and outbreaks as well as flooding and malnutrition, to address their dire needs in terms of WASH support. The IRC prioritizes gender and protection as cross-cutting themes across all areas of intervention to ensure that services are equally accessible and safe for clients and that the unique needs of individuals are addressed. especially children under 5 and pregnant, lactating women (PLW), and people with disabilities. WASH activities will be bolstered by life-saving, integrated health and nutrition services to reduce excess morbidity and mortality for the refugee population. This will be done through primary health care facilities and referral of complicated cases to secondary facilities. The health package will include reproductive, maternal and child health services, treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases, mental health and psychosocial support, emergency preparedness and response including response to COVID-19 pandemic and other outbreaks. The IRC will provide life-saving treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), referrals to stabilization centers and counseling of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) on infant and young child feeding (IYCF). The IRC will work directly in the camps to provide services, and through established Ministry of Health (MoH) facilities to scale up services to refugees in urban areas including capacity building to healthcare providers and basic health infrastructure improvements to deliver quality health and nutrition services for the refugees.

1 - Needs

Protection: As of February 2023, Gedaref Tunaydbah refugee camp hosted approximately 23,750 refugees (UNHCR IMS report) displaced from the Tigray conflict in Northern Ethiopia, this number has subsequently increased to 24,858 individual refugees by mid-May 2023 following the armed clashes between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF)which has caused massive protection crisis. while Blue Nile state is hosting approximately 13,254 refugees from Ethiopia and South Sudan. Across Gedaref and the Blue Nile, common child protection concerns for refugees include large-scale family separation especially the fleeing refugee and internally displaced families, resulting in many unaccompanied and separated children. Children, especially adolescent girls are at risk of sexual violence,, child labor, early marriage, and other vulnerabilities including the risk of being recruited into the armed forces/groups. the revised Sudan 2023 HRP report states that displaced and conflict-affected women and girls are facing increased risk of GBV including sexual violence during displacements and temporary accommodation, augmented by the increased presence of the armed actors, domestic violence has increased due to limitations in mobility, increase psychosocial distress, shrink access to basic needs increase vulnerability to sexual and other forms of exploitations, as well as the elderly, people living with disabilities, and survivors of GBV at risk boys and men.WASH: According to UNHCR, water supply in Gedaref and Blue Nile refugee camps is not sufficient due to low production capacity. Refugees are now receiving as an average 17 l/p/d which is less than the standards for the minimum amount of water (20l/p/d). Excreta disposal remains a key gap in the camp, as access to sanitation facilities is limited and far below industry standards and is anticipated to worsen due to increasing population and poor soil permeability. At maximum capacity of 35,000 refugees in Tunaydbah camp (Gedaref) and more than 8000 in Blue Nile, refugees will need 980 latrines and 700 bathing shelters. Yet, the current amount is 570 stances of improved latrines and showers serving 20,500 people in all locations for a with coverage 1 latrine to 50 people below the SPHERE standards. Hygiene promotion is a key gap in the camps with poor hygiene practices impacting the health of refugees. Women and girls are affected by the limited access to menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and safe, private, and hygienic sanitation facilities supportive of MHM.Health and Nutrition: The health and nutrition mid-year summary report for Gedaref highlighted the fragility of the health system in the state characterized by multiple outbreaks of diseases including hepatitis E and monkey pox, which further compounded the devastating impact of COVID-19 outbreak on the healthcare system. A WHO survey also evaluated the availability of essential medications in Sudan in 2022 and found out that on average, only 31% of critical medications were available in public health facilities and 30% in private health facilities. IRC clinic data in Tunaydbah camp indicates malaria is still a leading cause of morbidity for the refugee community with 296 confirmed cases recorded in 8 months. Moreover, due to their traumatic experiences, mental health conditions are quite frequent among refuges commonly manifesting as depression, anxiety, and post-traumatic stress disorders with limited services available. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) for Sudan is projected to remain high in 2023 at 13.6%, with 64 localities classified by WHO as very high malnutrition prevalence rate of 15% and above, and 9 localities falling into catastrophic levels of GAM 30% and above. Moreover, most of the nutrition centers require infrastructure upgrades to expand storage facilities for nutrition supplies and waiting shades, and human resource capacity enhancement in terms of additional staffing and training.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project will have no negative impacts on the environment. For WASH:The project will not impact negatively on the environment. Although latrine construction activities are likely to have minimal on-site environmental impacts, most of the negative environmental impacts are likely to be seen off-site, where construction materials such as sand, fired bricks and timber will be sourced. Therefore, all tender/bidding documents for latrine construction activities will include clauses to ensure that contractors/suppliers obtain construction materials from authorized/certified sources. In addition, where appropriate IRC will promote the use of soil stabilized blocks (SSB) in lieu of fire bricks and contractors/suppliers will be required to adhere to the stipulated environmental mitigation measures below which will form part of the contract conditions The IRC will support safe disposal of non-medical wastes like papers, plastics and medical wastes like soiled swabs and organic materials using the colored bin systems to ensure proper waste segregation before final disposal and sharp containers for disposal of all needles, sutures and blades. Medical wastes will be stored away from reach of children in secured or fenced area and incinerated promptly, and the remnant ash disposed safely. Organic waste like placenta will be properly disposed in placenta pits. The project will prioritize installation of solar systems for health facility lighting to promote sustainable renewable energy sources and minimize emissions. The IRC health team will closely work with WASH colleagues to ensure appropriate IPC measures instituted at all service delivery points.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project aims to ensuring protection exerts effort to deliver and address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. In all aspects of the project, from design to implementation and monitoring, prioritizing fundamental protection mainstreaming principles of ‘Do No Harm’, ensuring meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation and empowerment of the target beneficiaries and stakeholders is mainstreamed.. Community mobilization and outreach activities will include information dissemination on the prevention and mitigation of gender-based violence and child protection risks. All staff working on the project will be trained on underlying protection concepts, gender mainstreaming, and referral systems. The IRC will integrate and coordinate activities within its referral mechanisms, permitting the team to refer potential cases to the health team. All field-based staff will receive information about available protection and response services to make safe and ethical referrals upon beneficiary voluntary informed consent.The IRC health team works closely with protection colleagues to identify protection risks, vulnerabilities and referral pathways for services. The action will ensure availability of clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS) through the training of health facility staff and preposition of supplies for clinical management of rape (CMR). Providers will be trained on ensuring privacy and confidentiality including in the health information system (HIS). The IRC will also train key providers at the health facilities and in the communities on psychological first aid to offer mental health support and enhance rights of individuals with mental illness. The WASH activities will be inclusive of feedback and participation from target beneficiaries, particularly women, including through focus group discussions and by ensuring their inclusion within the WUCs and as hand pump mechanics. The IRC will increase the registration and identification of persons with disabilities and the elderly to ensure that unique needs are addressed, while also strengthening existing community support structures for these groups, such as surrogate water collectors. Hygiene promotion activities will also include information on services available for survivors of abuse and sexual violence

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The IRCemb embraces a client-centered approach that puts the target crisis-affected communities and vulnerable group at the center of the intervention. Regular multi-sector feedback sessions with the clients including caregivers, women and girls will be conducted within the safe spaces to ensure activities and services are adequately addressing the needs of the crisis-affected population. Women, girls, parents and caregivers will be engaged to participate in the decision-making regarding the location and activities . Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with survivors of GBV on a voluntary basis. The IRC team will engage with women, girls, children, and caregivers from crisis-affected communities in planning and implementing approaches to prevent, minimize, and respond to the consequences of CP risks and GBV. IRC will ensure its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback schemes ensure full community participation in the design and re-design of the project . The IRC will carry out community outreach and awareness raising intervention to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the integrated protection available services, IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, the process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). Across all sectors, the IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including focus group discussions (FGDs), KIIs, feedback boxes, and a hotline, which will inform project design and implementation. The IRC also has a neutral department called Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, who is responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
