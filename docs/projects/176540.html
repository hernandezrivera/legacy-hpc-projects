Project Name

Supporting the vulnerable IDP, returnee, and host communities in Jonglei, Lakes, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Eastern Equatoria, and Central Equatoria States and hard-to-reach areas across South Sudan with emergency SNFI through a combination of static and mobile responses and with disaster risk reduction.

End Date

Project Summary

In line with the SNFI cluster strategy, through static and mobile interventions, ACTED will utilize in-kind and cash-based modalities to cover SNFI gaps for IDPs, returnees, and host communities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups and people with special needs (PSNs).The activities will include distribution of in-kind NFI and Shelter kits, cash/voucher assistance for emergency shelter and NFIs, cash for work activities for shelter construction, and construction of communal shelters to respond to large scale emergency influx of displaced populations. Moreover, ACTED will conduct disaster risk reduction activities to increase resilience of vulnerable populations.
The interventions will contribute to the following cluster objectives:
Sectoral objective 1: Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities.
Sectoral objective 2: Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs.
Sectoral objective 3: Support the most vulnerable returnees and host communities/affected non-displaced with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions.
Sectoral objective 4: Support recovery, resilience building and access to land, housing, and property.
Sectoral objective 5: Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills.
For all its activities, ACTED will implement COVID-19 prevention measures. This will include increasing engagement with local authorities by explaining in detail mitigation strategies in place for each activity. ACTED teams will receive trainings on COVID-19 preventive measures and a full set of PPE stock to be used during project activities. During distributions, ACTED will ensure the beneficiaries and ACTED staff follow social distancing measures. Moreover, ACTED will install handwashing stations at distribution sites for beneficiaries to wash their hands before and after the receipt of the items.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, there are currently 2.1 million people in need of emergency SNFI assistance in South Sudan. This number has increased by 100,000 people in 2021, mainly due to the increased number of returnee settling back in the areas of origin. An estimated 70% of newly displaced people outside IDP camps and collective centres, as well as over 50% of returnees are in need of emergency shelter and NFI support. Therefore, ACTED will continue implementing SNFI interventions in 2021 in line with the following cluster objectives:Sectoral objective 1: Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities.Sectoral objective 2: Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs.Sectoral objective 3: Support the most vulnerable returnees and host communities/affected non-displaced with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions.Sectoral objective 4: Support recovery, resilience building and access to land, housing, and property. Sectoral objective 5: Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills.

2 - Project activities

In line with the SNFI cluster strategy, ACTED will utilize in-kind and cash-based modalities to cover SNFI gaps for IDPs, returnees, and host communities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups and PSNs. This will be done though distributions in areas where ACTED has a static presence, such as Juba and Bor former PoCs or through mobile interventions in hard-to-reach areas. ACTED has an established emergency response mechanism to ensure a timely response and effective coordination as part of its mobile responses which include SNFI, WASH, CCCM and protection components. A shock is flagged through clusters, working groups, ICCGs, and/or humanitarian coordination fora. After a shock, ACTED is able to deploy a team in 3-5 days to conduct a scoping assessment, which will last apprx. 3 days to complete. Tools such as site profiles and site monitoring matrix 3Ws are shared with partners. In line with pipeline availability, ACTED will immediately launch a multisectoral response as per the identified needs of the targeted communities. The intervention duration will usually last between 4-6 weeks. ACTED will coordinate with all the clusters, working groups and humanitarian coordination fora as well as reaching out to ECHO and RRM partners to avoid duplication. ACTED will also coordinate with RRC, local partners, mobile partners, and cluster state focal points. In addition to activities surrounding service monitoring, ACTED will train local partners on coordination mechanisms to avoid duplication of response. Apprx.,2-3 months after an intervention is complete, ACTED will return to the location to conduct a post intervention/distribution monitoring and will share the results with the relevant clusters.The activities will include distribution of in-kind NFI and Shelter kits, cash/voucher assistance for emergency shelter and NFIs, cash for work activities for shelter construction, and construction of communal shelters to respond to large scale emergency influx.Moreover, ACTED will support communities in locations prone to man-made or natural disasters with DRR preparedness activities. The deployed mobile teams will adopt Community Managed Disaster Risk Reduction (CMDRR) modality to implement both sub-components of the disaster preparedness – comprehensive mitigation actions and contingency planning. CMDRR entails the community led hazards mapping and corresponding actions, whereas the programme team acts as a technical lead and facilitator.As part of the mobile interventions, ACTED will ensure protection mainstreaming and follow a do no harm approach. This will be done through the inclusion of at least 1 Protection Officer to ensure protection mainstreaming measures are incorporated into response. Protection Officers will do awareness raising on HLP and SGBV. For mobile interventions, a protection scoping report will be prepared and shared with other partners for advocacy. These will be supported with the beneficiary selection process which includes suitability and vulnerability criteria and is supported by PMCs, which are composed of 50% women. The mobile responses will establish a protection pathway through its CCCM components which is handed over to the community at the end of the intervention. ACTED mobile responses will entail service monitoring and will where possible establish an advisable pathway through which community can make referrals to mitigate cases, HLP issues, and legal assistance.ACTED will prioritize to minimize any HLP issues arising from its shelter interventions, including eviction. At the start of each intervention, ACTED will conduct FGDs and KIIs with boma chiefs and local authorities to discuss any issues related to HLP. Moreover, the assessment period looks at HLP issues and informs the design of the intervention. As part of ACTED’s selection criteria, the HLP status of the HHs is determined and only included in the intervention if there are no land disputes which may cause escalation of tensions.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market analysis is a crucial part of ACTED’s cash and voucher activities and are incorporated into the response. Before undertaking any cash or voucher activities, ACTED will conduct a detailed market analysis and routinely monitor prices to ensure the market is functional and has the sufficient capacity to meet the needs.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and voucher programming is used as complementary to the in-kind SNFI items provision in ACTED’s programming. Cash and voucher modalities will be preferred where possible. This includes preference from beneficiaries and availability of items in local markets as well as capacity of the market to absorb such transfers as in areas directly affected by disaster, functioning markets are either not available or slow to recover from the shock with limited availability of goods due to hampered access or damaged infrastructure. ACTED will implement both conditional (cash for work) and unconditional (emergency needs for the most vulnerable households). The conditional modality, which is cash for work for emergency shelter construction, will have a two-fold benefit; supporting the households financially while addressing shelter needs. The unconditional modality will facilitate access to a wider range of items and empower the beneficiaries to choose the specific items that their household needs, in locations where the markets are functional.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During sensitization of beneficiaries on CRM mechanism, ACTED informs beneficiaries about PSEA and provides clear instructions on how to report such cases utilizing CRM present at the site of implementation. ACTED explains how such sensitive cases are handled to encourage reporting.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As per its standard operating procedures for CRM, throughout the project, ACTED will ensure that multiple channels of communication (e.g.: help desk, staff registering complaints, hotline etc,) are available for ACTED to receive feedback from communities including the most vulnerable members such as individuals living with disabilities and individuals who are not literate. Furthermore, ACTED will have at least one women staff member in help desks to encourage women to register their feedback to ACTED.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the implementation project activities, beneficiaries will be sensitized on the complaints and response mechanism in camps, and on how to submit concerns or complaints to ACTED. Moreover, help desks at project sites will be installed at central locations and equipped with visibility materials. Multiple avenues will be used to spread the awareness of the CRM from key messaging using megaphones during activities, to engagements at community meetings, house-to-house visits conducted by outreach workers, radio and visibility material (banners, stickers, business cards…).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ACTED’s independent AMEU will continuously register the complaints and feedback of the target population and insert them into ACTED South Sudan’s learning database. The findings will be discussed at least on a monthly basis with the project implementation and project development teams to ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the ongoing project as well as for the design of upcoming projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Exposing beneficiaries to violence: ACTED counts on a robust internal mechanism to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA. All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which includes ACTED’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy and Child Protection Policy. The staff will receive refresher trainings on these policies on a regular basis. ACTED will take the necessary measures to ensure that the communities receiving NFIs will have safe access to distribution sites by selecting an accessible location. Unethical management of data: Beneficiary data will be held and transferred according to data protection standards (e.g.: using password protected documents, anonymizing, closed access etc). Inclusion of marginalized groups in consultations: Vulnerable and/or marginalized groups will be included in community consultations to ensure they are included in decision-making processes and receive the assistance provided by ACTED. Interference from external actors: ACTED will conduct inception meetings with external stakeholders including local authorities to ensure that the scope of the project and any selection criteria is widely understood at the beginning of the project. Coordination with all stakeholders will continue throughout the project to manage the expectations of the communities and avoid any interference to project activities.
