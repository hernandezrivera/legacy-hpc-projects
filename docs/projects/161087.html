Project Summary

Although there were improvements in 2019 in security with the R-ARCSS, persistant localised insecurity resulted in displacements and IDPs in PoCs remain due to uncertain security preventing return to home locations. There have been returnee arrivals due to instability in Sudan and improvements in security in urban areas. Bentiu PoC still has high population of 115,960 people. Intercommunal tensions continue to result in displacement particularly in Rubkona, Guit and Koch. Continued conflict in Yei and surrounding areas continue to result in displacements. Concern’s SNFI response in 2020 will provide life-saving assistance to under-served vulnerable IDP, returnee and host community populations through mobile response in Rubkona, Guit and Koch in Unity and Yei and surrounding areas in Central Equatoria. Concern will build on its success with CBIs utilising a voucher modality to provide access to SNFI materials enabling beneficiaries to have flexibility to meet needs. Assessments with communities will determine which items are most in need with assessment information communicated to the cluster. In coordation with partners, activities will be planned to reduce gaps and avoid duplication. For items requested by the community which cannot be provided by markets, requests will be made to the SNFI core pipeline and distributed in-kind. In Bentiu PoC, where Concern has been working since December 2013, newly arrived IDPs will be provided with SNFI kits and living conditions will continue to be improved with repairs and rehabilitation of shelters and replacement of NFIs. Concern was one of the first organisations to respond as part of BBR will continue to provide assistance. Concern has also re-established operations in Yei in 2018 following needs assessments and critical gaps in SNFI assistance. Focus in Rubkona and Yei urban areas for vulnerable resettiling IDPs and returnees and host communities will be on durable shelter constructions based on traditional designs. Concern will provide shelter materials through a mix of in-kind and CBIs that will provide beneficiaries with more choice to meet shelter needs and support market recovery. As markets are still recovering, assessments will determine which SNFI materials can be sourced locally and which will need to be provided in-kind. The response will improve living conditions through integrating with Concern’s and other partners’ WASH programme. Concern and community shelter committees will supervise construction of quality shelters that provide dignity and privacy for women, men, girls and boys. Provision of NFIs such as Jerry cans and kitchen utensils will contribute to better hygiene. In Bentiu PoC, Concern and WASH partners will ensure provision of water, sanitation, environmental health services and improved hygiene practices to SNFI beneficiaries. Concern considers accountability early and often throughout the project process. Communities are consulted during the design stages to establish expectations, targeting, and Concern’s ways of working which are in line with the national SNFI cluster.
In Yei, Concern has contracted a local NGO, ARD to provide trainings on ecostoves and briquette making beneficiaries. In Rubkona, Coalition for Humanity has supported Concern in fairs and distributions. Concern aims to develop partnerships with ARD and Coalition for Humanity in 2020 for complementarity in this response. ARD have a strong local presence and access to remote communities in Yei. Coalition for Humanity have a strong experience in protection responses and will support Concern staff in integrating protection in this response.
Concern will continue to participate in the national SNFI Cluster and remain as Unity State SNFI Cluster focal point and Yei SNFI Cluster focal point. Concern’s active participation in coordination mechanisms will ensure that information is considered, such as population influxes in programme locations or the establishment of Humanitarian Hubs.

1 - Needs

Unity has the highest number of IDPs at 247,278 (OCHA, Sep 2019) with 115,960 residing in Bentiu PoC (IOM DTM Sep 2019). There are 12,061 temporary shelters with most constructed by Concern in 2015 and it was not envisioned that they would have to last 3-4 years. Subsequently, wood posts rotted and materails become damged. Most shelters have an average of 9 people sleeping and 1.6 households per structure. Most IDPs do not feel safe to leave the security of Bentiu PoC and there is minimal markets for shelter materials in the PoC for IDPs to sel-repair and aid dependency is high. There is a need to also ensure provision of NFIs that have become damaged, including jerry cans and kitchen utensils to ensure hygienic and dignified living conditions particularly where congestion risks disease outbreaks. The main NFIs highlighted as priority items are solar lamps, cooking sets, blankets, and mosquito nets. During the wet season plastic sheets, mosquito nets, and solar lamps are identified as priority items (Concern SNFI assessments 2019). Since R-ARCSS more localised conflicts have been observed, notably around Guit and Koch with intercommunal violence and cattle raids. People fleeing violence are often forced to live under trees or poorly constructed shelters with limited protection and dignity and many houesholds are female-headed. Many household items are abandoned or looted during displacements and IDPs lack the means to replenish these items. On top of a lack of household items, a lack of access to clean jerry cans, kitchen utensils, and mosquito nets increases health risks to waterborne illnesses, malaria and subsequently malnutrition in children and PLW (Concern assessments, 2019). In Rubkona, IDPs have relocated from Bentiu PoC due to congestion and other locations due to Bentiu and Rubkona urban areas being perceived as safer and lack the means to construct more durable shelters. With improvements in security, returnees nearly 20,000 returnees have arrived in Rubkona often with minimal means for recovery (IOM DTM, Jun 2019). The protracted and unpredictable nature of the humanitarian context due to continuing conflict between armed groups in Yei and surrounding areas in Central Equatoria requires a flexible response strategy to address acute needs while avoiding undermining communities’ resilience and coping strategies. Needs assessments by Cocnern and partners in March 2019 identified unmet need for lifesaving NFIs (blankets, cooking sets, and mosquito nets) and shelter assistance (materials and tools). The host communities were supporting newly displaced IDPs, including identifying and allocating abandoned and vacated homes, with the agreement that if the owner of the shelter returns, the IDP household would vacate the shelter if asked within a set timeframe as agreed with the owner and chief. In many cases, this resulted in multiple displacements, increasing IDP’s vulnerability each time. The assessments found that vulnerable returnees are in some instances returning to their homesteads to find their shelters destroyed or burnt, resulting in vulnerable returnees residing in abandoned and vacated homes (insecure tenure and poor living conditions). Insecurity, socio-economic factors, and limited access to functioning markets (outside key centers) are key constraints households face when trying to meet their life-saving SNFI needs. Concern’s needs assessments in Yei have noted that households are engaging in negative coping strategies, including the gathering of wild food such as leaves and roots increasing GBV risks, consuming one meal per day and reducing portion sizes. Concern conducted a market assessment in Yei in March 2019 to assess the feasibility of a multipurpose cash intervention, which shows local markets in recovery stage, but a lack of purchasing power and demand were among the biggest challenges for the markets to fully recover.

2 - Activities

CO1: Concern will continue to provide essential SNFI kits to newly arrived IDPs in Bentiu PoC and humanitarian hubs, should they be established. Selection criteria will be agreed with the SNFI Cluster and communities based on assessments. Concern will coordinate with CCCM and CHC for distributions. Concern will continue mobile response following alerts of displacements or returnees influxes in Rubkona, Guit and Koch in Unity and Yei and surrounding areas in Central Equatoria. Assistance will be a combination of in-kind and CBIs to provide access to life-saving SNFI materials to meet needs of women, girls, men and boys that are as per social and cultural norms. Following an alert, Concern will conduct a multi-sectorial assessment through the cluster where it is State focal point in Unity and focal point in Yei. Targeting will use community defined criteria such as vulnerable female-headed households, households with PSNs and households with 3 or more children under 5, verified using IOM DTM and CRM. To reduce dependency focus will be on providing training towards skills transfer so communities can do repairs for themselves and others in their communities with tools provided. Where markets cannot provide shelter and NFI materials, Concern will provide items as in-kind assistance. This will be done with support from the SNFI Cluster Core Pipeline where items can be accessed in agreement with the pipeline, and distributed by Concern.. CO2: In BBR locations and Central Equatoria, focus will be more durable shelters constructed as per traditional designs with a combination of vouchers and in-kind distributions. Beneficiaries will be provided with vouchers to purchase shelter materials from traders and in Yei unconditional cash transfers to access NFIs with the value according an estimatinon of beneficiary NFI needs and the CWG’s MEB. Concern’s Construction Supervisors and community shelter committees will train and supervise community shelter committees to build quality shelters. Shelter rehabilitation activities will focus on utilising locally available or community-sourced materials (poles, mud and grass), local labour and local building techniques.CO3: In Bentiu PoC, Concern will focus on ensuring dignified living conditions for households through providing access to SNFI items should they fall into disrepair or damaged and need replacing. This will be through camp management’s CRM where complaints will be sent to Concern who will assess shelters and then request needed items from the core pipeline ensuring they are appropriate to female and male beneficiaries’ needs. Concern’s SNFI programme will consider additional needs, namely in WASH where service provision is often limited. This will include integrating with Concern’s other partners’ WASH programmes. In BBR locations and Yei, IDP and returnee sites targeted for activities will be selected in coordination with CCCM and WASH partners to ensure essential services are provided. CO4: Concern will support vulnerable protracted and resettling IDPs, returnees and host communities through integrating with its WASH programmes to build resilience to shocks. Beneficiaries will be consulted on shelter design to make them more durable to weather conditions, particularly heavy rains and construction will be supervised by Concern’s Construction Supervisors. In more stable locations shelters will be traditional tukul designs that beneficiaries are familiar and more durable. Provision of NFIs such as kitchen utensils and Jerry cans will improve hygienic living conditions. In Unity, Concern will develop a partnership with Coalition for Humanity who have strong experience in protection and good local knowledge, will support Concern to improve reduction in beneficiary protection risks. In Yei, Concern aims to partner with a local NGO ARD, whom Cocnern contracted to conduct trainings in 2019. ARD will provide trainings on ecostoves and briquette making to reduce fuel consumption.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Although the programme focuses on provision of essential and life-saving SNFI assistance, there are some sustainability considerations in the programme, namely the resiliency provided by supportive supervision during the construction process and technical trainings on repairs. Outside of Bentiu PoCs and humanitarian hubs if they become established, the focus will be on durable and quality shelter construction to reduce dependency on humanitarian agencies for repairs. Concern’s approach utilises community labour to carry out construction and repairs with close technical supervision. Shelter committees are trained specifically on small repairs, which they can carry out on their own shelters, prolonging the lifespan of those materials, or be carried out for neighbours for a small fee. Each stage, from land clearance to shelter construction, is carried out by the community in groups and verified by technical supervisors, and these extra levels of accountability including phased supervision and collective responsibility, aimed to prevent distributed tools or supplies from being sold, ensure a higher quality of completed shelters and stronger resilience of the community due to the hands-on components of the project. Concern will where feasible provide assistance through fairs or cash transfers to provide beneciaries with more choice to meet needs and support market recovery. Locations of shelters will need to be done in coordination with WASH partners to ensure water and sanitation services are provided and ensure clean living and dignified living conditions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

During assessments, equal participation of females, males and PSNs will ensure their respective specific needs will be considered for composition of SNFI kits and MEB. While displacement can reinforce traditional gender roles due to family separation, patriarchal patterns can be broken needing renegotiation of gender relations with women assuming roles as head of households. This is an opportunity to increase leadership of women in SNFI responses. Experience from previous programmes has found that nominating the senior female to receive items, encourages joint decision-making with spouses. Concern will prioritise improving composition of shelter committees so that there is stronger female representation to raise key issues, ensuring training opportunities are not only provided to males. Concern will coordinate with CCCM partners, local authorities and community leaders to allocate land for beneficiaries and in Yei coordinate with the South Sudan Law Society fo HLP integration. All those involved in Concern’s programmes sign Concern’s Code of Conduct and Programme Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-trafficking in Persons Policy; and included in these is mandatory reporting of breaches. During assessments, Concern will coordinate with CCCM and Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Fair and distribution sites will be located in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe locations. Prior to distributions, Concern will conduct sensitisations on how beneficiaries can reduce protection risks such as travelling in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Information on fair and distribution days will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulation. The CRM will be used to identify protection cases and make referrals to Protection partners. Concern will provide assistance to PSNs such as disabled to transport SNFI materials.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

In Bentiu PoC, the camp manager, IOM manages the CRM. Prior to the programme, sensitisations will include how beneficiaries can provide feedback, the CRM and how monitoring will be an opportunity to provide feedback. Complaints received will be referred to Concern for verification and action. In BBR locations and Central Equatoria, Concern has a CRM in place for registering complaints either in person to a Concern staff or in writing and submitting through CRM boxes which are located in programme locations. Complaints are assessed and verified by a committee comprised of staff members from different teams independent of the SNFI team and complaints validated are referred to the Programme Manager for action. Sensitive complaints such as on breaches of Concern’s Code of Conduct and P4 or fraud are confidentially referred to the Country Director. During assessments Concern will collect feedback on the types of SNFI materials preferrd by females and males Targeting will include a verification process where complaints can be assessed by beneficiaries not targeted. Monitoring activities will be used as an opportunity for beneficiaries to provide feedback. PDMs will be conducted 2-4 weeks after fairs and distributions depending on security. If insecurity prevents conducting of PDMs after fairs, Concern will conduct a rapid response monitoring on fair and distribution days using the SNFI Cluster tools. PDMs will contain questions on beneficiary satisfaction levels with the response, security and protection concerns. In addition, the PDMs will be used to identifying how the assistance met the needs on specific types of beneficiaries such disabled or elderly. Separate female and male FGDs will allow for women and men to have a safe space to express opinions of the project. PDMs will also be conducted with traders participating in voucher fairs to understand their satisfaction levels with the fairs process. Concern will ensure that staff participate in cluster AAP trainings.
