Project Summary

This is intended to render life-saving activities for vulnerable women and girls, persons living with disabilities who are at risk in the context of armed conflict and COVID-19 in Cameroon. The interventions under this Action aim to reach 300,000 internally displaced persons including persons with disabilities and members of host communities with integrated protection services (medical, mental health, psycho-social, legal and protection) and integrated sexual and reproductive health services.
Furthermore, the Action will ensure the continuous implementation of infection prevention and control measures (IPC) in 16 health facilities (5 in the NW, 6 in the SW and 5 in the Littoral) in order to reduce the risk of transmission and spread of COVID-19 in hospitals and in local communities. The Actions interventions will cover 3 regions of Cameroon and will target 12 more affected Divisions: Mezam, Ngoketunjia and Bui (North West), Fako, Manyu and Meme (South West), Wouri (Douala ,,2,3,4,5 & 6). Through this project, 100 service providers from the 16 health facilities, including their maternity wards, in the targeted Health Districts will benefit from capacity building in IPC as well as provision of materials and equipment for infection’ protection and control - in particular personal protective equipment (PPE), emergency reproductive health kits for the management of rape and the safe management of deliveries and complications. In addition, this Action will help to facilitate access to quality remote and timely medical and mental health assistance with Specific attention on lifesaving services to GBV survivors with disabilities. For women and girls with disabilities, the intersection of gender and disability increases their vulnerability to violence. In addition, social norms often designate women and girls as caregivers of people with disabilities, which can reinforce their isolation and further limit their access to social, economic and material support, increasing their vulnerability to violence and exploitation.
In the Littoral Region, the level of violence exacerbated by forced displacement and lack of adequate lifesaving GBV services put women and girls at heightened risks. In 2022, GBV partners will provide holistic assistance and support to vulnerable groups. The 2021 protection analysis has shed light to the fact that a vast majority of the GBV incidents taking place in the Littoral include economic violence exacerbated by poverty, limited access to resources and intimate partner violence and violence committed by other family members and relatives.
The crisis in the NWSW regions is characterized by secondary forced displacement to neighbouring regions. The Littoral region is one of those regions hosting many IDP women and girls who have sought refuge. As a result of poverty, couple with an increase in house rents many of these women and girls stay in overcrowded homes with little or no privacy. They are often exposed to sexual exploitation and abuse. With reduced means of subsistence, many IDP children don’t go to school because their parents cannot afford school fees and most often boys are prioritized limiting girls access to resources. This cause them to adopt negative coping strategies which often expose them to, sexual exploitation, sexual violence, early marriage and early pregnancy.
It is therefore key the GBV actors fully support all humanitarian sectors/clusters throughout implementation of their response in line with the principle of do no harm but also to prevent identified GBV risks. Sexual violence has also been exacerbated by situations of displacement and family separations, forcing families to live in increased risk with relatives or within the host community and creating risk of sexual exploitation, sexual violence and assault against young girls particular.
Poverty and ignorance limits women’s access to available GBV lifesaving services in many areas in the littoral.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Risk of GBV and SRH shall be reduced and quality of response improved and sustained. Outputs 1: Participation of Communities/ Awareness raising campaigns on GBV prevention and ResponseActivity 1.1: Establish Protection Committees, Women’s Committees and CBOsThey played a crucial role in awareness raising and somehow facilitated case referral. The vulnerability of refugees, social norms and gender discrimination in their environment, continue to expose women and girls to various forms of violence. The women’s committees help enhance women’s commitment to GBV prevention and response activities, and give women an opportunity to express their opinions and stand up for their rights. The CBOs will then conduct GBV prevention activities in collaboration with other volunteers in order to change the behavior characterized by cultural practices that favor male dominated decision making roles. Activity 1.2: Engage men and boys in community activities Most if not all the cases of GBV in the Communities are perpetrated by men and boys. But little or nothing is being done to involve them in GBV activities..Activity 1.3: Support dissemination of GBV messages through community radios and listener groupsInformation on GBV will be disseminated via the community radios in the different localities. Social workers and community mobilizers will share information on GBV topics in the local languages and the translation will be done in English/French. Activity 1.4: Conduct awareness raising campaigns during International DaysThe GBV program will focus sensitization and awareness raising activities on group discussions, round tables, and educative talks to commemorate 03 International Days in 2022 in collaboration with GBV AoR partners. Output 2: Support GBV/SRH services provided to GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girlsActivity 2.1: Rehabilitate Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS)UNFPA will rehabilitate the existing WGSS in North West and South West and mainstreaming. These WGSSs provide better comfort in safe spaces for women and girls to express themselves, interact with other females, and participate in recreation (singing, dancing etc.) and skill-building activities. Activity 2.2: Provide Mental health support, psychosocial counseling, case management and SRH services to GBV survivorsIndividual counseling will be provided on the risks linked to GBV to all GBV survivors and all other vulnerable persons in the community. The GBV Supervisors will provide regular coaching; on-the-job supervision and competency checklists to Social Workers to make sure guidelines are respected. . Trained social workers will provide individualized case management to survivors through each of six steps. Dignity kits with COVID-19 preventive items will be provided to survivors and to vulnerable women and girls at risks of GBV based on their needs; social workers will always assess the needs of beneficiaries before providing kits. Activity 2.3: Support skill-building activities and empowerment initiatives for women and girlsSkill-building activities will be organized at the WGSS and other relevant places to promote women’s self-empowerment and resilience. Activity 2.4: Cash base TransfersLack of financial resources and economic opportunities are the contributing factors of GBV. CBI may be an appropriate modality to respond to and mitigate the risk of GBV. Output 3: Capacity development supportedActivity 3.1: Capacity building sessions for GBV Actor and other partners In order to ensure quality service in the prevention and response to gender-based violence all stakeholders should have a better understanding of the core concepts of GBV guiding principles, GBV causes and consequences, survivor centered approach.
