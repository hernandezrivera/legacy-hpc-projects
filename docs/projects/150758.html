Project Summary

The project overall objective is to ensure that most vulnerable households with protection concerns due to their substandard housing condition are supported to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity by providing rehabilitation/adaptation assistance (Shelter and NFIs Cluster Objective 2). Providing essential shelter support meets basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation (Strategic Objective 2) and strengthens their capacity to cope with the protracted crisis (Strategic Objective 3).
250 families (1,400 individuals) with the worst combination of socioeconomic vulnerability and housing conditions are selected for support. Factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability include socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD-headed), income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets. Factors reflecting shelter vulnerability include gaps in core shelter items based on the Shelter Cluster agreed Minimum Standard for housing in Gaza.
The rehabilitation/adaptation support provided by NRC includes core items that ensure physical & climate protection, privacy and dignity, safety & security and reflect gender & protection concerns & special needs like those of people with physical disabilities and access-concerns. Affordable off grid and easy to repair solar systems will be provided to beneficiary households to meet essential household including WASH needs and reduce protection risks. Complementary and based on need, essential household NFIs such as water storage, household assets and equipment, bedding set, kitchen set, dignity kit, hygiene kit are provided.

Needs

The collapsing financial situation, electricity crisis & impact of the Great March of Return (GMR) severely impact the ability of vulnerable households to afford their right to safe & adequate housing, mitigate protection threats & meet basic needs. Most affected are families living in acute poverty and exposed to protection threats due to living in substandard shelter conditions. This includes GMR-affected with long-term disabilities and exhausted coping capacity whose shelters are not safe and adequate to accommodate their lack of physical mobility & wheel-chair use. Families living in acute povertyNRC engaged a sample of male and female households especially youth and elderly as well as PWDs through interviews and household vulnerability assessments to reflect shelter and NFI needs including gender, age, protection, and special needs.Based on data from MoSD (Sep 2018), 539 hardship-households are in urgent need for Shelter support. In Oct 2018, NRC conducted household vulnerability assessments of a sample of 41 out of these 539 households in 3 vulnerable areas in Middle Area (Deir Al Balah Al Berka neighbourhood), Gaza (Al-Shati refugee camp) and North Gaza (Jabalia refugee camp). In addition, NRC conducted KIIs & household vulnerability assessments of 10 male & female GMR-affected households in Gaza and Khan Younes Governorates (Sep/Oct 2018). The conducted assessments show the following findings with GMR-injured as most vulnerable in most aspectsOverall vulnerability of the assessed households is moderate (62%), high (7%), severe (5%; GMR: 10%).Socioeconomic vulnerability is largely moderate (48%) to high (11%; GMR: 20%)- Heads of household: 21% have physical disabilities (GMR: 30%), 5% mental disabilities (GMR: 10%), 12.6% widowed (GMR: 30%), 73% unemployed (GMR: 100%)- 32% live in overcrowding conditions with negative impacts especially for women (see GAM) - 59% are dependent on food assistance - 57% live in shelters with poor roofing (asbestos, corrugated sheets, combined)- Large number of families lack basic household assets, such as furniture, household equipment, kitchen set, bedding set - Largely similar coping strategies are used: selling household and productive assets, receiving assistance from family/friends, stopped paying utility bills, accumulated debts, spent past savings, begging/child labor. The shelter vulnerability is moderate (25%), severe (7%), high (6%) - Major gaps in core shelter-items necessary to provide a minimum level of physical and climate protection, safety, privacy, dignity, health & wellbeing of male and female family members of all ages and especially family members with physical disabilities (doors, walls and roofs, windows, water supply and storage, shower/toilet, kitchen, electrical connection, access to housing unit & WASH facilities). Another key finding of KIIs conducted with GMR-injured is their lack of legal information on their rights, available legal procedures to seek justice and/or compensation, or legal actions to receive adequate medical treatment. Female widows as a result of the GMR are now more vulnerable to eviction as their HLP rights and security of tenure depended on their husbands, vulnerable to overcrowding & loss of privacy, vulnerable to increased responsibilities as they will be taking care of injured or disabled members of the family, and to shifting roles to become the family breadwinner.

Activities

The identified protection threats in poor shelter conditions are the result of lacking safety & security, privacy & dignity, especially for those households with special needs reflecting gender, age, and disability. To mitigate identified protection threats which acutely vulnerable households with exhausted coping capacities are exposed to, NRC will provide shelter rehabilitation/ adaptation support for most vulnerable 250 families. It includes items that ensure physical & climate protection, privacy & dignity, safety & security and reflect gender & protection concerns & special needs like those of people with physical disabilities and access-concerns: sealed roof, external walls and door (physical and climate protection), bedrooms and bathrooms with lockable doors; door width suitable for wheelchair access; separation of sleeping and living spaces; sleeping space separation considering sex and age (privacy and dignity), handrails for stairs and roof to protect children and elderly, LED emergency lighting to reduce risk of accidents and injuries at night and reduce GBV; solid lockable external door and lockable windows; entrance ramp to housing unit for PWDs and barrier-free access to WASH facilities to ensure hygienic conditions for injured (safety and security), smooth and cleanable floor and wall surfaces; proper ventilation; hygienic food preparation and cooking space; separation of kitchen and bathroom; access to water supply (hygiene). Affordable off grid and easy to repair solar systems will be provided to beneficiary households to meet essential household including WASH needs and reduce protection risks. Complementary and based on need, NRC provides essential household NFIs such as water storage, household assets and equipment, bedding set, kitchen set, dignity kit, hygiene kit.In case of needs and protection threats to which NRC cannot respond, NRC project staff is trained to refer cases exposed to protection threats including GBV. This includes children in families with GMR-injured with post-traumatic stress. NRC engages male & female beneficiaries of all ages in designing the best technical response to each households’ specific needs and protection concerns & supports them in prioritizing their needs considering budget limitations. Beneficiaries are involved in identifying the most suitable modality of implementation (self-help approach/contractor). NRC’s post-project-surveys of male and female beneficiary-households indicates that the large majority of households prefer the conditional cash modality for repair/rehabilitation/adaptation while in certain circumstances, especially female, widow/divorced/elderly/disabled-headed households prefer a contractor-approach.Based on social hardship cases registered in MoSD, the most vulnerable in terms of socioeconomic & shelter vulnerability will be selected for support based on a detailed household assessment. The factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability include socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household (especially female/widow/elderly/PWD-headed), income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets. The factors reflecting shelter vulnerability include gaps in core shelter items within 12 set categories based on the Shelter Cluster agreed Minimum Standard for housing in Gaza. Those with the worst combination of socioeconomic & shelter vulnerability will be prioritized for support. Synergies with NRC’s Information, Counselling, and Legal Assistance Programme will be ensured for legal protection. This can consist of raising awareness about HLP rights and responsibilities and support to gather proper ownership documentation especially for women who are disadvantaged in accessing their property & inheritance rights. Both the head of household and the female/male partner sign the contract with NRC to ensure equal access to assistance & information.

Indicators

NRC’s M&E framework ensures that relevant M&E tools are used to meet planned targets with quality. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E unit, ensures that a tracking and reporting system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes. An indicator matrix records data from the field on a monthly basis to measure progress and provide corrective measures where needed. NRC supports quality control measures and accountability towards beneficiaries. In addition, the M&E unit conducts regular field visits to monitor project progress and feedback key findings to project teams. NRC conducts a post-project lessons learnt exercise to continuously improve efficiency and effectiveness in implementation. On a technical level, NRC shelter field and office staff tightly monitor the progress in implementation and provide continuous technical support and monitoring visits to target-households during implementation. Additional technical support is provided if heads of households do not feel comfortable/able/experienced to undertake the shelter works themselves.Community outreach and engagement: during the design phase of the project, male and female households of vulnerable and affected communities especially youth and elderly as well as PWDs were consulted to reflect Shelter & NFI needs including gender, age, protection, and special needs. At the start of the project, essential project information including selection criteria and access to NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism is communicated to the target communities through coordination with municipalities, community representatives, neighbourhood committees, key community members, and local NGOs/CBOs. Project information is communicated to both male and female family members in direct communication and through information leaflets which also informs them about NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism. NRC engages male and female beneficiaries in its regular monitoring visits throughout project implementation to ensure the quality of the intervention. NRC conducts post-distribution monitoring and post-project surveys with beneficiary households to measure the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance and the satisfaction of male and female beneficiaries of all ages. NRC’s local partner IDCO is conducting the activities related to the communication with communities at the beginning of the project, and supports the post-distribution monitoring and beneficiary satisfaction survey at the end of the project. A close coordination with the Shelter Cluster partners ensures to maximize efficiency and impact in target locations and avoid duplication of assistance. NRC keeps the Clusters informed about the implementation, achievements, and lessons learnt. In the monitoring and evaluation stage, post-distribution monitoring surveys, baseline and endline data collection considers sex and age.
