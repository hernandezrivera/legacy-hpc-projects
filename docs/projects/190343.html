Project Summary

The project shall contribute to the objective of provision of access to well-coordinated and disability, gender sensitive quality child protection services by 2023 to boys and girls including adolescents and children with disabilities facing life-threatening risks in the targeted locations in Tigray, Amhara and Somali region. Alight aims to reduce increased risks children and adolescents face in drought, disaster, shock, and crisis displacement situations due to their age, vulnerability, and developmental stage. Children and adolescents are often become more vulnerable during drought and displacement situations due to disruptions in normal caregiving practices, the inability of families to positive adopt positive coping strategies, interrupted learning, and weakened child protection systems in conflict and drought affected woreda in Amhara, Tigray and Somali region. Some girls and boys are particularly vulnerable because of their age, ethnicity, gender, religion, sexual orientation other rand other factors that increase their marginalization. Alight will respond to child protection threats including but not limited to GBV, child abuse and exploitation, psychosocial distress, injuries resulting exposure to disaster debris, child recruitment to armed groups, and deprivations of rights among others. in response to above, Alight will scale up activities such as identification, documentation, tracing, and reunification. Alight will mobilize and work with communities; to provide alternative care system for UAC, enhance reintegration support for UAC and children formerly associated with armed forces or armed groups. Alight will conduct training for Child Protection working groups, participate in CP meeting and develop interagency referral pathways for child protection partners to improve service delivery as well as engaging parents and care givers on positive parenting. Alight will work with trained social workers and counselors to support child led initiatives at schools to promote violence free environment, build children’s self-esteem to report cases of abuse, establish Child Friendly Safe spaces for children and adolescents to socialize, learn, develop, and be protected from child abuse and exploitation. Overall, Alight will strengthen community-based protection initiatives for displaced, conflict and drought affected children with a special focus on children with disabilities, children from marginalized communities and those adopting to negative coping mechanisms. In order to ensure child safety, Alight will conduct child safety walks/mapping exercises to identify the safety risk for children affected by drought and the displacement, therefore, strengthen the CP referral pathways to ensure integrated interventions at schools are safe, accessible, inclusive and protective for all children. Alight will collaborate with CP AoR, relevant regional government institutions and partners to update and operationalize integrated referral pathways for CP services and train child protection and other actors on safe and timely referral. Alight will establish and strengthen community-based Child-friendly spaces to ensure the safety and wellbeing of children at school and community level; enhance the provision of structured psychosocial support and recreational activities such football, drama, songs, exercises and competition to school-going children and children in the IDPs camps and their caregivers will be involved community-based prevention sessions that will discourage harmful effects and harmful traditional practices.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Case Management: Alight will enhance provision of one-off cash assistance of $30 to 1500 caregivers for support to access to case management activities for identified vulnerable children. This cash will be used for transport and access to PSS. the target breakdown 1500(900G, 600B) crisis and displacement effected Children from the target IDPs will be provided quality, confidential and comprehensive case management that meets their need throughout the 12 months project period. The case management services will be equally provided cases identified or referred from the existing facilities supported by Alight and other humanitarian partners and surrounding vulnerable host communities in Amhara, Tigray and Somali region.Actv 2: 500 UASC children reunified with their primary caregivers or in appropriate alternative care Alight to support provision of one-off cash assistance of $100 to 500 caregivers for support to 500 UASC children reunified with their primary caregivers or in appropriate alternative care, the trained Professional psychosocial counsellor, social workers and CPN will carry out tracing, registration and family reunification to the conflict and drought displaced population.Activ3: Training of the Community-Based Child Protection Mechanism (CMCPMs)Alight will establish 8 groups of CMCPMs committees each comprised 20 members, and then Train 160 (80M, 80F) CMCPMs in the target location to support conflict, drought and emergency effected children. The training will take three full days maximum and will be conducted the initial month of the project kickoff. The identification of the of the Community-Based Child Protection Mechanism (CMCPMs) committees will be carried out from IDPs Tigray, Amhara IDPs, and Somali targeted Woradas in discussion with community leaders, then, Alight will ensure to obtain advocacy-oriented members from the community who can convey appropriate messages to the community once fully trained.Act - 4: Activity: Training on Identification, documentation, tracing and reunification (IDTR) for case workers, CPN and Child Protection Sub AoR in Tigray, Amhara and Somali region. Act -5: Conduct awareness-raising and outreach on key child protection risks, social norms, harmful practices, MRE and related information on CP service and mitigation measures.Act - 6: Creation of child friendly spaces within targeted communities in Amhara, Tigray and Somali region.Act - 7: Conduct safety Audits to regular monitoring of violations of children's rights in the target regions.Act -8: Conduct Psychosocial Support Service (PSS) for girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities in the target regions.Act - 9: Sourcing and selecting Community based Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) Volunteers in the target location.Act-10: Activity: Training of PFA volunteers: A three-day intensive training for Psychosocial support and psychological first aid for the 180(90W, 90M) community based Psychological First Aid volunteers will be conducted in target locations. The training is aimed to build the capacity of community volunteers to response to the impacts of the crisis and displacement. The Psychological first aid training will help them (PFA volunteers) know how to support children and the community affected by the displacement and traumatizing events of loss and stress. In the training volunteers will learn how to be a compassionate helper, provide confidential, compassionate and competent care to affected communities, the training will strengthen their skills in being a good listener, and in offering practical help without encouraging dependency. The training is aimed to empowers volunteers to have the needed skills and knowledge to support their community and children in need. Act-11: Provision of PSS services to the community in Amhara, Tigray and Somali IDPs and vulnerable host communities. Act-12: Provision of transport Allowance to vulnerable children for medication and other services.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

ALIGHT together will involve the leaders in the community with the affected population in all planning and preparation related to the activities proposed. ALIGHT will maintain a strong relationship with the government and stakeholders in Tigray, Amhara and Somali region, which will allow us to approach the community with acceptance from local leaders in order to gain the trust of the local community and provide the most effective services. In addition, feedback mechanisms will be put in place to involve beneficiaries in target communities. For example, listening sessions with Guidian will be conducted to get their feedback on services provided to them and recommendations for future programming.ALIGHT will ensure accountability of the interventions provided by setting up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. These mechanisms will include following up and assessing the effectiveness of established Complaint Mechanism including placing a complaint box in all service centres and informing beneficiaries, creating hotline for complaints and feedback, Kuja Kuja customer feedback and conducting survivor exit interviews to assess the satisfaction of survivors with the services provided, assess staff attitudes towards beneficiaries, including survivors charged with provision of services, as well as garner information on how to improve services. Further, the Protection sector will work with the Health and WASH sectors to support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights. Overall, ALIGHT’s key interventions will promote age appropriate, inclusivity in decision making and accountability processes, such as including marginalized females into PFAs and CFs and safe space Activists, GBV focal points and CBPNs committees at camp levels. Staff will be sensitized on how to identify and ensure that members from marginalized groups are accepted and treated with the dignity they deserve; providing infrastructure that caters to their wellbeing, such as constructing ramps at entrances to the health facilities and at the latrines for those with mobility issues; promoting recruitment of persons from these groups by ensuring that all recruitment advertisements bear clear information encouraging the receipt of applications form qualified persons including those with disabilities; and developing IEC/BCC materials that promote community understanding and inclusion.
