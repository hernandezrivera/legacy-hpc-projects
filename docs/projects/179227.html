Project Summary

Mercy Corps aims to strengthen our protection interventions to support the overall protection cluster objectives towards the provision of quality integrated protection services with a focus on community based approaches and vulnerable adolescents and young people. Mercy Corps will continue to expand on its commitment to integrating protection outcomes into other sectoral programming including cash, food security and livelihoods, WASH and social cohesion. Mercy Corps will do this through direct implementation and local partners in north Syria.
Mercy Corps projects aim to ensure vulnerable adolescents and young people (i.e. early marriage survivors, adolescents involved in child labor) exposed to acute and/or chronic crises are safe, protected and more resilient and have the capacity to address root causes of inequalities. This will be achieved through three pillars of integrated programming, 1) adolescents and young people are supported with structured psychosocial interventions, life skills and mentoring to improve their positive coping and personal development to self-efficacy and well-being whilst being provided with opportunities in livelihood and resources including cash grants or cash assistance; 2) Communities are supported with interventions to unlearn harmful values and social norms and to adopt new values and attitudes and become more committed to equality and diversity and become champions of adolescents and young people 3) Adolescents and young people who are survivors and marginalised understand their rights, exercise self-efficacy and are referred to specialised actors. These interventions will be complementing existing sectoral programming where Mercy Corps or its partners are operating in and will not be standalone.
Mercy Corps will also continue to ensure protection mainstreaming is embedded across all our activities and programming. This will include strengthening data collection and ensuring it is disaggregated to inform programming; referral systems and service mapping are in place to refer identified protection cases to specialised actors; Mercy Corps staff and partners are trained in protection mainstreaming principles and approaches, protection risks analysis and safeguarding/PSEA; Mercy Corps community accountability feedback and response mechanism are designed in consultation with communities and feedback received are use to track red flags; community awareness sessions to inform the communities of their rights and entitlements, complaints and feedback mechanism and the code of conduct of all individuals providing aid.

4 - Needs

Diverse and interlinked protection needs are widespread in Syria. Inadequate living conditions in north-west and north-east Syria, as well as the loss of livelihoods, traditional safety nets, property and assets compound gender-based violence (GBV) risks and child protection concerns. This protracted crisis has disrupted social services and public service, strained coping mechanisms and community safety nets and resulting in psychological distress. HNO estimates 13.1 million PIN of protection assistance while Idlib is hosting the highest number of IDPs and returnees. Across Syria, 27% of children in households are displaying signs of mental distress, doubling from 2020. Mercy Corps’s assessment also indicates a rise in suicides and drug use among children and young people. Covid-related impediments disproportionately affected girls and women and the decreased remittances entering Syria, fuel shortage and bread crisis are pushing the Syrian economy to the brink of collapse. Protection monitoring reports in the north of Syria suggested that child labour, including recruitment of children in combat and support roles is a persistent concern. Girls and boys, including adolescents and children with disabilities are also at more risk of other forms of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. Psychosocial needs of many girls and boys are also left unmet. Voices of Syria 2021 reported that GBV remains a major protection risk and pervades the lives of women and girls, particularly domestic and family violence, early/forced marriage and sexual violence and harassment. Protection monitoring reports indicated that GBV responses and prevention activities remains a need in the north of Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

Interventions will target adolescents and young people and women and girls using an integrated approach. Activities will aim at enhancing their capacity and the protective environment. Programs will be designed in collaboration with girls, boys and young men and women in a participatory manner including PWDs. Monitoring will be conducted at the end of each cycle of interventions and baseline and endline (i.e. functioning and KAP) will be collected.Structured PSSl support, life skills and mentoring: Adolescents and young people who have experienced acute/chronic stress are more likely to experience symptoms of mental health and engage in negative coping strategies. Through PSS support they will be able to improve functioning, strengthen positive coping and build social connections. They will also have the opportunity to learn new skills and be supported by mentors and peers. Vulnerable adolescents and young people without opportunities are also more likely to feel disenfranchised and be recruited into fighting and exploitative labor conditions and young girls or women may be forced to marry or engage in risky behaviors. Hence, in addition to psychosocial support they will also have access to multipurpose cash and livelihood opportunities so as to promote resilience.Community based interventions addressing GBV and inequality: Through community based social behaviour change interventions Mercy Corps will work with girls, boys, women and men to unlearn harmful attitudes and behaviors driving GBV and inequalities. The aim is for communities to become committed to equality and diversity and become champions to those marginalized (i.e PWDs, people with perceived affiliations, girls and women).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Interventions include different community actors thereby increasing risks of SEA (high impact, medium likelihood). Mitigation: 1) on-boarding and training on PSEA and COC, 2) community awareness on rights and entitlements and COC of aid workers 3) feedback and complaints mechanism designed in consultation with community, 4) proper recruitment procedures, 5) service mapping and safe referral are in place. Recruitment of children into armed groups remains a risk (high impact, medium likelihood). Mitigation 1) identify safe locations and timing in consultation with community, 2) ensure staff are trained to identify risks and refer to specialised actors 3) build capacity of adolescents and young people 4) monitor red flags i.e. school dropouts 5) parents understand risks and red flags 6) regular mentor-parent check-insMental health is highly stigmatized (High impact, medium likelihood) and poorly understood. Mitigation 1) existing relationship within targeted community and community is consulted 2) program uses wellbeing instead of mental health 3) program targets a wide section of the community and not single out particular groups.Promotion of gender equality and rights of marginalised groups and changing gender roles and responsibilities may excerbate GBV (High impact, medium likelihood). Interventions may cause harm if not properly managed. Mitigations 1) updated gender analysis, 2)consult men, women, girls and boys and marginalised groups on project design, 3) ongoing monitoring of red flags through audits, 4)strengthen feedback and complaints mechanism 5) service mapping and referral to specialised actors, 6) staff trained on supporting survivors 7) SADD data collected and analyse

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Approval from local authorities (high impact, low likelihood)Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions. Where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavor to support partners in their intervention methodology and engagement with local actors to increase opportunities for operating.Increased insecurity (high impact, medium likelihood)Situations in the northeast and particularly northwest remain volatile. Due to insecurity Mercy Corps might experience issues in accessing certain communities and lead to project delays. Mercy Corps security team and humanitarian access team to scenario plan and adjust the project and intervention to minimise impact and risks to affected communities.Covid-19 pandemic (high impact, high likelihood)Ongoing covid-19 pandemic had resulted in delayed delivery of services and project adaption to ensure public health measures are observed. This is expected to continue in 2021. Over the past year Mercy Corps has strengthened its remote monitoring and implementation and public health adaptations and SOPs put in place 2020 remains in 2021 to protect affected communities. Mercy Corps will also ensure project work plan accounts for potential delays due to lockdown and closures.
