Project Summary

The project aims to enhance equitably and quality access to education for crisis-affected IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable children aged between 5-17 years. Schools are destroyed, and some children feel unsafe on the way to school or while in school due to the lack of electricity, especially for the second-shift students. Families have had to be displaced to areas where schools are available; as a result, schools in safe areas are overcrowded, and lack of documentation hinders the children from accessing primary and/or secondary national examinations.
The earthquake has weakened the capacity of the education system to address basic education needs more than ever. According to the Ministry of Education, 229 schools were damaged in the affected governorates. Additionally, 92 schools were used as collective shelters in February 2023. Thousands of children were not able to go back to school in the affected governorates, leading to dropping out and a setback in learning.
UNHCR plans to provide non-formal education activities to over 169,000 children and rehabilitate around 1,050 classrooms.
Needs assessment.
The protracted crisis in Syria has hindered children from accessing and enjoying their full rights to education. According to the 2023 HNO 7.2 M children/youth are in need of education. Economic factors and currency depreciation, including children working to support households, are key factors for children not attending school. The formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school-age children. Existing services are unable to physically accommodate or meet the different learning needs of IDP returnee children, including those with specific needs. The limited availability of non-formal education services continues to reduce the opportunities for children to return to learning at age-appropriate levels. UNHCR believes that children with access to education are less exposed to harmful negative coping mechanisms such as early marriage, child labor, and other forms of exploitation. The routine and milestones of going through the education process offer children stability and a sense of normalcy.
Implementation of activities
Education interventions will be in line with the “No Lost Generation” initiative launched in 2013. With over 2.45 million children and youth out of school. UNHCR will participate in the EWG to coordinate education interventions and will respond to the identified needs with due consideration of security and resource limitations. The following education interventions build on the priorities and the identified needs of the Education Sector. Education interventions will include non-formal education programs such as remedial classes, catch-up classes, and homework cafe activities for all children, including those with disabilities. Activities will take place in UNHCR community centers and education facilities. Interventions will adhere to the MoE and Education Sector guidelines. School rehabilitation will be limited as a last resort and as part of comprehensive intervention in a priority return area. Access to persons with disability will be taken into consideration in school rehabilitation, and UNHCR will coordinate with the WASH and Shelter Sectors and ensure that the re-tabulated schools have WASH facilities in place for both genders and are disability friendly to safeguard the protection of boys and girls. The activities will be monitored through eld visits, partners' monthly reports, and the Partners Reporting Monitoring tool.
