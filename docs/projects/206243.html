Project Summary

In 2023 an estimated 9.4 million people, more than two thirds of the country’s population, are in need of humanitarian assistance – 53% of people in need are children. In this context, education plays a crucial role for South Sudan’s children. Education provides safety, keeping children and youth from physical harm, whilst also contributing to psychosocial wellbeing and health, when complemented by WASH and school feeding interventions. Schools provide safe spaces that also function as a platform to deliver lifesaving interventions, such as food, water, sanitation, mental and physical health services. Education programmes mitigate the impact of humanitarian crises by building resilience among children, young people and their communities. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) programmes in education curricula and preparedness plans equip and strengthen children’s ability to deal with emergencies and mitigate the impact of future disasters. WV will implement EiE projects in the counties where it has a strong operational presence, characterized with very high needs, especially in education and other related sectors: CES (Juba) Upper Nile (Malakal, Melut, Renk), WES (Yambio, Nzara, Tambura), Warrap (Tonj North, Twic, Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Tonj South and Tonj East), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil North, Aweil South) targeting populations affected by conflict or disaster in 2023.
WV is currently implementing education programs in partnership with ECHO, Education Can't Wait, EMPOWER Irish Aid, UNHCR and GPE. To consolidate on current efforts while reaching further and deeper into the areas with dire needs, in hard-to-reach areas WV will continue to focus on emergency affected rural and urban areas.
In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, WV will ensure that through this project, 28,200 children will have access to safe and equitable quality education; 600 teachers, and 650 PTA’s will be trained and supported with supplies and materials; 1500 youth will be provided life skills and vocational training with starter kits. Out of the identified learners, the project will target about 400 children (200 boys, 200 girls) with disabilities. The learners will also be categorized under social/residential status as internally displaced, host community, or refugees during the actual enrolment in the project.
WV will also prioritize establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that are supportive and enhance motivation of children to go to and remain in school with a particular emphasis on girl’s education. In addition, recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and promote their overall wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite Gender, disability or ethnic background.

1. Needs

In 2023 an estimated 9.4 million people, more than two thirds of the country’s population, are in need of humanitarian assistance – 53% of people in need are children. In this context, the role of education is more important than ever for South Sudan’s children. Education is fundamentally protective, keeping children and youth safe from physical harm, whilst also contributing to psychosocial wellbeing and health, when complemented by WASH and school feeding interventions. Schools are safe places that also provide a platform to deliver lifesaving interventions, such as food, water, sanitation, mental and physical health services. The education in emergencies response in SSD supports continuity of education for crisis affected communities, but also contributes the building blocks of an inclusive and resilient national education system. The needs indicated are:2.8 million children are out of school; 23% of schools have classes under trees; 77% of schools reported a lack of separate toilets or washrooms for girls and boys; 60% of classrooms and 50% of latrines are not accessible. Less than 25% reported having a general protection referral mechanism; Only 34% of head teachers reported teachers had been provided with PSS training; The needs overview summarizes top line education in emergencies needs in South Sudan, drawing on several key data sources including the 2023 and 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the Education Cluster’s 2021 Education Needs Assessment (ENA) SSD report, and the MoGEI’s 2021 National Education Census Report. The overview of education needs presented here was validated and prioritized by members of the Education Cluster SAG as part of the strategy development process.WV will also prioritize establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that will support and motivation of children to enroll and remain in school with a particular emphasis on girl’s education. Recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite gender, disability or ethnic background. WV through its partnership department, will enhance capacities of CSOs, CBOs and national organizations through collaborative implementation and knowledge sharing. WV will implement EiE projects in the following counties where it has strong and operationally meaningful presence, with very high needs, especially in education and other related sectors: CES (Juba) Upper Nile (Malakal, Melut, Renk), WES (Yambio, Nzara, Tambura), Warrap (Tonj North, Twic, Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Ton South and Tonj East), North Bar El Ghazal (Aweil North, Aweil South) targeting populations affected by conflict or disaster in 2023.

2. Project activities

WV will implement the following Activities: Under CO1 Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level and need. Enroll children in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers, ALP, functional literacy, life skills for children, life skills for youth, life skills for life, TVET; Establish and/or rehabilitate Semi-permanent & temporary learning spaces and classrooms; Establish and/or rehabilitate gender-segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion - Procure Provide crisis affected children with quality education recreational materials/kits. Under CO2 Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle: Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents; Train teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching - learning process for crisis affected children; Train and update teachers and learners on PSS; Provide life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents; Train teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, health and nutrition; Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) Under CO3 Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response. Organize conduct back-to-learning campaigns; Provide cash transfers to volunteer teachers as incentives; Procure and distribute PPEs, relevant hygiene, and handwashing facilities to maintain school health practices and disease prevention. The project results will be measured with the following indicators: # of school children who got access to gender sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards. (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls) # of school who children benefited from rehabilitated gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards. (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls) # of learners who received sanitation and hygiene materials # of teachers trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies # of teachers trained and updated on PSS, CP and referral mechanisms # of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) who benefited from life-skills and basic vocational training # of community education committee members and local education officials trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) # of children, teachers and PTA members provided with appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials and information on prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases # of school campaigns organized on back to school/learning. # of national/local organizations that are supported with their institutional capacity gaps and needs
