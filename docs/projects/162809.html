Project Summary

WV FSL program intervention is aiming to provide humanitarian assistance to IDPs, Returnees, and Host communities to improve their food security and likelihoods opportunities. The Overall Objective is to Support in the improvement of the food and livelihoods security status of disaster affected, vulnerable and small scale farming households through Provision of life-saving emergency agriculture and livestock/veterinary inputs, extension services and kits during or in the aftermath of a shock. Building, restoring and maintaining productive assets, through provision of inputs and capacity building for climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary services to vulnerable small-scale farmers, and Provision of income generating support and vocational trainings

1 - Needs

The food security situation in Sudan continues to be in a bad state as a result of many years of conflict coupled with limited livelihood options, environmental degradation, climate variability, and low agricultural productivity. Of late localized crop failures and substantial increases in food prices have made the situation worse. For instance, the price of sorghum increased 77% from January 2017 to January 2018. The cost of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, tools, fertilizers, and animal medicines, has similarly increased thereby contributing to the reduction of purchasing power at the household level. The shortage of fuel (40-65% of required fuel was provided by government ) and high prices of inputs during the planting season meant that lower yielding crops were planted in the past agricultural season and this reduced the yield potential of the season that witnessed normal rainfall amounts. The combination of all these factors has a negative bearing on the majority of IDPs, refugees, returnees, and host communities resulting in limited access to food. Desertification and reduced fertility of previously agriculturally productive areas is evident in South Darfur. This is due to poor soil fertility management, climate variability and loss of top soil due to erosion. Above average rainfall was received in South Darfur in the just ended agricultural season and this was favorable for crop production and pasture regeneration. As a result food security outcomes were expected to improve between October 2018 and January 2019 and most areas in South Darfur would maintain or improve to Minimum (IPC Phase 1) and Stressed (IPC Phase 2) on the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification methodology. However, it is anticipated that the food security situation will deteriorate to Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in areas hosting IDPs and conflict areas such as Jebel Mara. In Nitega locality which boarders Jebel Mara, for example, a harvest assessment conducted by FAO, the ministry of Agriculture, and NGOs (including World Vision) in December 2018 showed that there is already a grain deficit of 24,317 metric tons before the lean season has even begun. The condition of pasture, water and livestock was also found to be poor. Prospects of a normal rainy season in 2019 are not good according to FEWSNET and local experience which believe that after 2 relatively good consecutive rainy seasons in 2017 and 2019 the region is due for a poor one. This calls for concerted efforts to adopt climate smart agricultural techniques that will minimize the effects of such an eventuality. World Vision Sudan conducted a multi-sector needs assessment conducted in November 2018 in Buram, Kass, Beliel, Nyala North, Mershing, Nitiga, Almalam, Rahead-Albirdi and Alradoum localities. The main source of food as reported by families is purchasing from the market (70%) while 26% mainly get their food from their own production

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is going to address the environmental issues by considering the local environmental context of the targeted area, and a continuous assessment for potential environmental impacts will be carried throughout the project life. The project is also going to do mitigation measures by compensating for negative impacts (if any), and enhance environmental benefits in the project by practicing some measurements, the followings are part of it: • Replace the use of fired bricks with Soil-Stabilized-Blocks (SSB) or Cement-Stabilized-Blocks (CSB). • Locally available material is recommended provided that it is not impacting negatively on the environment. • Include environmental awareness sessions in projects, and support solid waste management schemes • Reduce packaging material and discourage use of plastic bags to reduce the amount of waste generated. • Promote alternatives such as Fuel-Efficient Stoves to reduce use of wood, solar panels for lighting and pumps. Include fuel efficient cooking techniques (e.g. pre-soaking beans, sheltering cooking fires, etc.) in training. • Raise awareness on environmental protection and conservation • Reduce wood consumption by supplying iron pipes and using environmentally friendly material for constructions and rehabilitation; and promoting alternatives such as Fuel Efficient Stoves, and fuel efficient cooking techniques. Consider also alternative energy sources such as solar panels for lighting and pumps.• Enhance environmental benefits in the project by Tree planting, etc

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will both be mainstreamed into sectoral activities. As mainstreaming issue, the project will ensure that each activity will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults.Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staffs or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children.To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staffs will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability All project staff have built and maintain channels for constant communication with affected communities.
