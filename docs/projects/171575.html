Project Summary

In Palestine, increasing prIn Palestine, increasing proportion of women and girls with disabilities (WGWDs) cannot access GBV services provided at state and non-state levels, due to lack of environmental adaptations, limited trained staff, and communication and mobility barriers. Also, WGWDs experience various forms of psychological, social, physical and sexual violence due to a range of complex factors related to the composition of society, the prevailing culture, the structure of service delivery and discrimination on the basis of disability and gender.
The project will contribute to eliminating the GBV against WGWDs in West bank and Gaza strip and increasing their access to GBV services through: economically empowering the WGWDs survivors of GBV; raising awareness of the WGWDs and their family and community members about GBV and disability; build the capacities of GBV and non GBV service providers around adequate service provision to WGWDs; and at last promoting the results of the project through producing and disseminating briefing notes utilized to promote the inclusion of WGWDs in the filed of GBV.

1 - Needs

Due to gender & disability intersection, violence against women and girls with disabilities (WGWDs) is higher & may manifest in different ways when compared to non-disabled women & male peers. The causes of violence are directly linked to attitudes & structures that promote &/or condone discrimination & unequal power, the non-accessible physical infrastructure, limited trained staff, communication barriers, limited inclusive GBV support services & the lack of awareness on acts of violence & appropriate responses, in which WGWDs & their families often lack information about taking precautions against violence. The 2019 PCBS Violence Survey indicated that 37% of WWDs who are currently married or who have previously been married have experienced violence by the husband at least once for the 12 months preceding the survey. 50 WWDs & 134 service providers, decision makers working in the fields of gender, disability & GBV have participated in a qualitative national study conducted by Al-Marsad & SHS in 2020 entitled “The Reality of Prevention & Protection from Violence Services for WGWDs”. The results of the study found that the National Referral System for protection from violence neither considers the special needs of WWDs nor adapt its services to accommodate their needs. As such, the protection services provided to them are incomplete. Even NGOs against violence did not target WGWDs as direct beneficiaries except on rare occasions whereas %83.8 of the provided services were awareness-raising. Yet, when GBV service providers were requested about providing services responding to the needs of WGWDs (in-terms of adequacy, accessibility, qualified staff) it was very clear that they mostly lacked understanding of the disability mainstreaming concepts & practices. Moreover, in the most threatening situations like the current COVID-19, WGWDs are extremely vulnerable to risks & marginalization as there is no specific plan or services tailored to respond & meet the needs of WGWDs during the times of crisis like C19. The data collected from the field has extremely highlighted the huge gap on the service provision level as in 2020, SHS launched a survey in WB & GS to assess the needs & situation of WGWDs during COVID 19. The first stage of the survey demonstrated that: 39% of the surveyed 350 WGWDs face financial difficulties & physical difficulties as a result of the state of emergency; 29% face difficulty in accessing medication, hospital treatment, & obtaining medical supplies including assistive devices; & 11% expressed their need of psychological counseling as a result of the additional pressures. Also most WGWDs indicated that there is great difficulty in mobility to get the the basic needs; while the working WWDs are extremely suffering in order to provide for their families as they are the breadwinners. Most of the families interviewed were suffering from difficult economic conditions & are below the poverty line. On the other hand, no official body, whether at the level of a ministry or a department, has contacted any WGWD in order to examine their needs, or to check on their conditions in light of the emergency, whether in the WB or GS. This indicates that WWDs have disappeared from the official agencies' map and emergency plans. The survey revealed that there are several cases of WWDs who were cut off from cash assistance provided by the Ministry of Social Development, which further deteriorated their living conditions. Throughout the ongoing C19 survey, up to the moment, out of the surveyed 815 WWDs 68 WGWDs indicated that they experience different types of violence due to the increased pressure on the family & WWDs in light of the current situation. It was also noticed that the WGWDs are not equally treated as their male siblings with disability, where discrimination on the base of gender & disability was prevailing in some families during the times of crisis. In general, the vulnerability of WGWDs are not basically linked to geographical or social factors, but gender-disability are the key drivers for marginalization. Yet, the risk of GBV & economic fragility is higher in Gaza strip if compared to West bank due to the siege & longstanding challenges.The design of the project was basically built upon the inputs and feedback collected from more than 1000 WWDs and their families of whom participated in different training courses, focus groups and needs assessment exercises in which their needs and concerns were integrated into the proposed interventions and activities. For example, 82 % of 141 WGWDs & their families participated in SHS- EVAW awareness raising actions in 2020 were not aware of the factors that make WGWDs more vulnerable to GBV & less than 30% knew about the available channels for support seeking or violence reporting. As due to the high extend of exclusion of WGWDs and huge gap in the service provision documented through C19 emergency situation, a major bulk of the proposed interventions are customized to respond to the very alerting needs of WGWDs. As most of GBV service providers lacked the knowledge and experience in dealing with WGWDs, particularly during emergency C19 situation, a good proportion of the capacity building component will respond to this gap and provide specific topics related to targeting PWDs through the emergency response actions. Additionally,as most of the targeted WGWDs and their families might experience movement restrictions due to C10,SHS will apply its module of remote intervention that was and still applied during the C19 emergency situation.In general, during all in-person gatherings, sessions & trainings, the C19 related precautions will be followed including wearing masks,using hygiene kits & maintaining physical distancing.For the remote interventions like the online/zoom awareness raising sessions,SHS will make sure that all materials are accessible,disability friendly &can be accessed by people experiencing all types of disabilities

2 - Activities

Most surveyed WGWDs clearly stated that their socio-economic fragility & dependency is a crucial factor that causes & heightens violence. SHS GBV-Disability advisors recommended to provide apprenticeship & VT to empower the WGWDs & mitigate the impact & causes of violence. The project shall include an economic empowerment component implemented in partnership with the Economic Empowerment Program for (PWDs) at Bethlehem Arab Society for Rehabilitation. Thus the project will focus on:• Providing apprenticeship/ internship to 25 WWDs survivors of GBV in West bank (WB) & Gaza strip (GS) in which to enhance & practice their employability & technical skills • Providing vocational training to 15 WGWDs survivors of GBV in (WB) & (GS), specifically in the most demanded vocational specializations in the market• Provide voucher assistance to 100 women, girls & boys with disabilities survivors of GBV in WB & GS. SHS will partner with other child protection organization to facilitate the access to boys with disabilities survivors of GBVSHS will agree with the employers on placing the WWDs in IT, digital & admin temporary jobs that can be fulfilled from office & home & agree with employers to support the WWDs with all needed equipment & tools to fulfill the job remotely (C19).SHS will make sure that the WGWDs have access to the online training curriculum through coordination with the VT centers to provide accessible formats & tools. (C19)As most WGWDs & their family members are not aware of the GBV types & assistance seeking channels. Also, in most cases the perpetrator is a family member. The project proposes to:• Conduct of in person awareness raising sessions about the interrelation & specificity of disability & violence, combating & protection mechanisms to 120 WGWDs & family & community members (women, men, boys & girls) in WB & GS• Conduct of remote awareness raising sessions about the interrelation & specificity of disability & violence, combating & protection mechanisms to 120 WGWDs & family community members (women, men, boys & girls) in WB & GS All materials & surveys are digitalized (videos & infographics). SHS FPs who are WWDs will facilitate the online/ zoom sessions & lead the discussions. All participants/parents are approached by phone or WhatsApp to receive technical support. All surveys are transformed into accessible formats & sent to participants via emails or WhatsApp. For sessions that target children (in person or remote), the materials & surveys will be adapted into children friendly formats. The digitalized materials (info graphs, motion graphs, films) will be shared via SHS & partners’ social media channels. (C19) As most of service providers (GBV & non GBV) do not acquire the skills needed to provide adequate services (identification, response, referral) to the WGWDs survivors of GBV., the project will:• Provide disability mainstreaming training (GBV-disability minimum standards) to 25 front line GBV service providers in WB & GS (18 women,7 men)• Provide disability & GBV mainstreaming training (GBV-disability minimum standards) to 40 non GBV service providers particularly humanitarian, DPOs & partners staff in WB & GS (28 women,12 men)The training will significantly focus on building the capacities of several DPOs and disability organizations including the project partners to mainstream the GBV dimension within their strategies, programs and services.The trainings can be provided via zoom or other virtual programs; all training materials & exercises can be digitalized. Work groups can be formulated virtually to provide interactive learning environment. (C19) As advocacy & influencing approaches are essential pillars for change, the project shall utilize the results & outputs of the project activities to highlight the gaps in prevention & response to GBV against WGWDs particularly during C19 situations:• A special GBV-disability two briefing notes shall be produced & disseminated to the different GBV-disability actors on the policy making & service provision levels to promote fulfillment of GBV inclusive approaches. The briefing notes & key findings of the project shall be presented & shared with key players & actors in the domain of GBC & disability at state & non- state level through two round tables (1 WB, 1 GS). The briefing notes shall also be produced in hard & soft copies (visual motion graph) & distributed virtually via face book & social media channels. If it was hard to conduct the roundtables in person, an alternative online roundtables model will be applied, whereas all participants can access the zoom link –owned by SHS- to discuss the briefing notes which also was produced in visual format (motion graph). Based on SHS experience during C19, holding remote focus groups can be very impactful, interactive & feasible. Staff will be trained to refer participants requesting assistance to available GBV sources of support; The safety & security of individuals who provide information about violence will be protected at all times following SHS referral & confidentiality protocol; Each participant will receive a complete explanation of the purpose of the actions & will be informed that participation is optional. Permission to take notes & pictures will be formally obtained. Anyone providing information about violence particularly SGBV will give informed consent before participating in any further actions. To effectively reinforcing PSEA, project staff shall be informed & sign SHS–PSEA & child protection policies. During gatherings, the precautions against C19 will be followed: Frequent cleaning & sanitization; Maintain physical distancing - 1 m. distance; wear masks; Brief guests about precautions before & during the event; Ensure the meeting area is well-ventilated; Minimize crowding by staggering arrivals &departures; Provide all necessary supplies –hand hygiene stations, hand sanitizer or soap & water, tissues, closed-lid bins, masks

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

A number of key principles & measures concerning the “do no harm” approach will be respected to guarantee the safety & security of both the working staff & beneficiaries: ensure information gathering is necessary & justified; Ensure continuing informed consent if actively detecting violence; & Safety should be paramount. The project will insure that safety of participants is paramount, & should guide all project decisions; Ethical obligation to ensure that the findings are properly interpreted & used to advance policy & design interventions; The safety & security of all those involved in information gathering about violence- will be continuously monitored; The conﬁdentiality of individuals who provide information about SGBV will be protected at all times; To protect the rights of the participants at the household & facility level, each participant will receive a complete, standardized explanation of the purpose of the actions & will be informed that participation is optional. Permission to take notes & tape record will be formally obtained. Anyone providing information about SGBV will give informed consent before participating in any relevant actions & other data collection activities. Also the local authorities, committees, & security officers shall continuously be informed & coordinated with to provide protection during the distribution & delivery of assistance. Special confidentiality & documentation procedures shall be followed to guarantee that assistance & aids are delivered to the WGWDs themselves without being exploited by others It is very important to respecting participants’ autonomy under voluntary & informed consent. Which also means to consider the right every individual to make their own decisions. It includes putting mechanisms in place that support autonomy In order to ensure voluntary & informed consent, the staff must provide a simple description of the purpose & the procedures for filling any survey or document The confidentiality of information supplied by participants particularly relevant SGBV must be respected in different stages of the referral & response phases using coding measures will ensure the confidentiality if participants. Fieldworkers will be trained to refer participants requesting assistance to available local GBV services & sources of supportAs the protection approach is mainstreamed in all SHS interventions, all projects beneficiaries will be approached by the SHS FP to fill in the organizational admission forms which includes violence detecting/ sensitive questions, whereas any discovered cases of violence will be referred to the referral & monitoring employee to carry out the further referral & response mechanisms of SHS (SHS referral & response protocol) providing that all information shall be kept confidential, secured & protectedIn addition to having all children participating in the awareness raising activities informed of the types of violence & reporting mechanisms, other precautions will be in place. SHS will have all project staff, consultants, trainers, partners signing the child protection policy so that all beneficiaries of children are protected from all forms of violence & abuse. It also aims to ensure their safety, by establishing clear & specific procedures & ensuring that all staff members are aware of their responsibilities. Also SHS will make sure that that some cases of violence & abuse are reported to the competent authorities & that the needed services are provided for children exposed to violence, abuse, neglect & exploitation Additionally, the consent form for photography or videography signed by parents or legal guardians of the child must be obtained in advance before the child joins any activityIf the parent or legal guardian does not consent to the child's photography or videography, the project management must be informed, & the person authorized to take the pictures or videos should be informed of this

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As equal access to aid is intrinsically embedded in SHS’s work, SHS will make sure to have the project activities and aids accessible to WGWDs and PWDs experiencing different types of disabilities. As well the project will put arrangements in place to guarantee reaching out to PWDs and WGWDs and their families living in remote areas, Bedouin communities, Area, east Jerusalem through communication with CBOs, DPOs and NGOs operation in those areas to provide reach out support. SHS will make sure that all venues, training centers and halls are accessible to PWDs particularly experiencing physical disabilities (i.e. alleviators and ramps). All publications and presentations will be printed in Braille to guarantee access to information by people with visual disability / impairment. All meetings, workshops and roundtables will be supported by sign language interpretation to facilitate communication to people with hearing disability / impairment. The media materials will be accessible in terms of having sign language translation, Braille printings, audio narration, written subtitles for all produced Ads, promos and other awareness products. Since the local transportations are not accessible, a considerable amount of money is dedicated for transportation fees in the budget as the participants with mobility disability need special caps/taxis. Accommodation of the awareness raising materials and media publications – particularly for the remote modality - shall include providing of: Screen readers programs, captions, Word formats, big fonts, audio recordings. Intellectual disabilities shall be considered including providing of specialized facilitators during the awareness raising activities.For PWDs experiencing visual disabilities, a personal assistant shall escort them during project activities and meetings that can be secured by SHS if needed.For Children, special adaptations shall be made to the training materials to facilitate access and understanding including altering the awareness materials into children friendly materials: drawings, comics, few wording, appropriate language.Since the local transportations are not accessible, a considerable amount of money is dedicated for transportation fees in the budget as the participants with mobility disability need special caps/taxisAlso, to allow PWDs particularly WWDs accessing the relevant COVID 19 emergency actions and services, a specific part of the trainings will focus on the inclusion of WGWDs in the emergency plans i.e. environmental accessibility, access to information, inclusion in needs assessments, provision of special protection arrangements &provision of assistive and medical aids.To guarantee inclusion of the boys with disabilities, SHS will coordinate with several local actors in the domain of child protection to reach the boys survivors and provide voucher assistance.
