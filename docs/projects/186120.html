Project Summary

NRC will assist beneficiaries to access civil and legal documentation, through the provision of information, counselling and legal assistance. NRC will use mobile teams to ensure that beneficiaries in hard-to-reach areas can have access to these services. Where it is possible, especially in some localities of the North west and South west, sensitization sessions will be improved through combination of mass media, and community-based approaches to deliver culturally adapted messages related to legal and civil documentation.
One of the main targets groups for these services will be children along with their parents who need birth certificates to sit public exams as well as people affected by displacement who face obstacles to access civil and identity documents to enjoy freedom of movement and access to other rights and services.
In addition to addressing the lack of awareness of the procedures to access documentation and providing legal assistance to deal with the judicial and administrative process of late registration of births, ICLA will address financial barriers impeding vulnerable populations from accessing civil and identity documentation. ICLA will advocate to reduce the cost of judicial late registration of births and facilitate free of charge access to the medical certificate required for late registration . After successfully advocating for the operationalisation of the administrative reconstitution procedure in the crisis-affected areas in Far North, NRC will continue with the support of BUNEC to advocate for the implementation of this procedure in North West and South West regions. ICLA will also contribute to the ongoing national exercise of revising laws and procedures related to identity and civil documentation. Most of the advocacy initiatives will be undertaken through the National Platform on civil documentation that NRC co-leads, as well as intends to initiate those types of platforms in the North West and South West regions. LI Interventions in the Northwest and Southwest regions will be carried out gradually with keen observation in areas where there is reasonable acceptance based on its sensitivity, and continues monitoring of the context to increase coverage accordingly.
NRC will strengthen the capacity of institutions, local authorities, as well as humanitarian actors (e.g., civil registration centres, courts, traditional leaders, etc.) by providing trainings and capacity building on LI, as well as by organising workshops to highlight challenges in accessing civil and identity documentation and to suggest practical solutions through an integrated plan of action. In order to follow up the actions (trainings, recommendation of workshop), joint supervision mission of officials in charge of LI will be made by BUNEC, MINDELVEL and NRC.
Together with relevant authorities and bodies, NRC will explore how to use technology and other innovative approaches to boost birth registration and other civil status aspects.
Access to civil documentation will increase the protection of vulnerable populations and their right to move freely, and the access and enjoyment of basic services, including but not limited to education.
The ICLA programme will benefit the following primary target groups are duty bearers: IDPs, refugees, returnees, vulnerable host communities, and other vulnerable populations facing difficulties in exercising their LI rights in displacement and return areas. Priority will be given to women, including widows, and displaced boys and girls in school without documents, as well as to people with disabilities and special needs, and persons facing challenges in realizing to obtain/recover civil or identity documents due to the conflict/crisis. The secondary target group will consist of duty bearers at national and sub-national levels including local and traditional authorities, mayors, civil registration officers, government technical and administrative services, and the court.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In North West and South West, lack of identity and civil status documentation is one of the major challenges IDPs are facing. In fact, secession claims are one of the causes to the crisis in these two regions, making citizenship a major concern, and thus LI is a sensitive issue. In addition, the survey carried out in August 2020 shows that 92% of the respondents indicated that they have faced or are facing difficulties in obtaining birth certificates while 80% indicated they have faced challenges in obtaining national ID cards. As a result of the current socio-political crisis, there has been widespread destruction and subsequently many children have lost their birth certificates, and access to this service has deteriorated. This has greatly reduced their freedom of movement and consequently access to basic services. This adds to a situation which was already problematic as far as legal identity and civil documentation is concerned in the two regions. In fact, it seems there were already many malpractices including tampering with civil status records in order to reduce age, and other fraudulent issues with regards to LI. According to results of an LCD assessment conducted by NRC in the South West Region in August 2020, a great proportion (47%) of children in the households in which the data were collected did not have access to civil documentation. In deepening the analysis according to the situation as per displacement and sex, it emerges that IDPs represent the group that has the least access to civil documentation, i.e. 69% of respondents. There was a slight distinction between girls and boys in the non-possession of these LCD as girls at 51% were seen to be mostly in lack of birth certificates as opposed to 49% of boys. The impact of the reduced access to legal and civil documentation on the displacement-affected population is far reaching. Most children born in the cause of the crisis especially those born in the bushes are not registered, hence, most of them don’t have birth certificates. The situation is similar for most boys and girls in primary 6 who cannot sit for certificate examinations for lack of a birth certificate. Lack of documentation also has an impact on freedom of movement especially for people whose documents got missing or were destroyed by NSAG. Hence, the vast majority of those affected discouraged from moving without documentation for fear of being arrested and detained considering the heavy military presence and numerous checkpoints in the Northwest and Southwest regions. This has also reduced their ability to access humanitarian assistance and basic social services such as financial services, healthcare as well as formal education.
