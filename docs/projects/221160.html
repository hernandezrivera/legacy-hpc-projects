Project Summary

Plan International South Sudan aims to improve the health of vulnerable communities of Lakes by providing comprehensive, integrated primary health care services through static and outreach health facilities in selected payams of Lakes state counties. The service provision is planned to cover Cueibet (Abiriu, Ngap and Achol-malek payams), and in Awerial (Puluk payam) which have health facilities that are not covered with the current HSTP projects and have populations will low access to basic health services. PI will try to increase and expand reasonable coverage and access, especially for displaced or hard-to-reach populations in these locations.
Aligned with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for South Sudan, PI's healthcare interventions are fully aligned with health cluster objective (1) Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality healthcare services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations (2) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases and (3) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. In implementing this project, PI will use the different national guidelines and protocols of MOH South Sudan. The project implementation period will be from January 1, 2025 - December 31, 2025. PI will ensure that most marginalised groups like those with disability, minority ethnic groups, and other groups due to economic, political, social and cultural factors are reached and participated throughout the project cycle.
Key interventions are maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, surveillance and outbreak investigations, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations and approximately 19,504 vulnerable host populations (boys, girls, men, women and PwD) in Cueibet, and Awerial counties of Lakes State.
Furthermore, PI will participate in any joint assessment or sector-specific assessments in operation counties. Besides, to ensure the sustainability and ownership of this project, Plan will prioritise local capacity-building activities throughout the project cycle. Project staff, Community volunteers and local government staff in CHD and health facilities will be capacitated in different ways, including training, involving them in support supervisions, feedback and complaint meetings, and review meetings. Also, the majority of project staff and all volunteers will be recruited locally. Outreach sites, if any are required, will be established in consultation with the community and local leaders. Furthermore, the CHD and communities will be notified that these project interventions are temporary measures that PI is doing, and they should prepare to take over the program step by step.

1. Needs

South Sudan is in a long-term, complex, and severe humanitarian crisis driven by inter-communal conflicts, climate change, a fragile economy, an influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees from neighbouring countries, and limited access to infrastructure and essential services, including health services. As a result, an estimated 9 million South Sudanese are in critical need of humanitarian support in 2024. Health services in South Sudan are mainly dependent on support from international aid and have a significant shortage of resources (human and others). The health sector faces one of the most critical challenges, with a severe health crisis affecting 8.9 million people, mainly in flood- and conflict-affected regions with population movements (displacement and returns) and disease outbreaks, according to the HNRP 2024. Communicable diseases like Malaria and outbreaks of diseases like Measles and cholera are frequent. The situation is being aggravated by flooding and the Sudan crisis. There was extensive violence against women, including physical, sexual, psychological, and economic abuse and forced marriages, according to a 2022 national study. Besides, post-conflict communities of South Sudan are still grappling with high depression rates. Plan International (PI) also conducted a multisectoral need assessment in September 2024 and found that FGD participants identified a shortage of basic health services as one of the key needs mentioned by the communities in Lakes. This is in addition to other problems like severe food shortages leading to hunger, disease outbreaks, insecurity and flooding, and a lack of boreholes or water points. Given the widespread lack of health infrastructure, health workforce, and other resources, frequent outbreaks of diseases and high burden of communicable diseases in South Sudan in General and in Lakes State specifically, coupled with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macroeconomic crisis, conflict, insecurity, it will be mandatory to provide preventive and basic curative health services in the above counties. Furthermore, providing these services will significantly contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality of children, PLWs, and other high-risk population groups and improve the health, development and productivity of the general population.

2. Project activities

The key project activities are:1. Provision of integrated outreach health service - provide integrated health services (preventive and curative services) in community sites that are far from health facilities.2. Community diseases surveillance and report - integrated disease surveillance and response for targeted diseases, outbreaks declared and any emerging diseases in the country3. Provision of preventive and basic curative health service- prevention and treatment of common illnesses in the community like diarrhoea, malaria, RTI, skin infections etc. Besides, provision of disease prevention interventions like immunization, health education and promotion, WASH promotion etc.4. Sexual and reproductive health services. Provision of the SRHR services at different service points like health facilities, outreach sites, schools and others. Availing Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) of life-saving sexual and reproductive health services, including but not limited to Antenatal Care, BeMONC, CEmONC, health facility delivery, postnatal care services, family planning services, STI prevention/protection and management.5. Community Health Services: Community health education and social behavioural Change communication service that on disease prevention, and health promotion activities6. Prevention, control and management of chronic or lifestyle related diseases - hypertension, DM screening and treatment
