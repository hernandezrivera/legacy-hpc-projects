Project Summary

The aim of this project is to ensure equitable access and quality education to adolescent girls and boys and young adults who have not engaged ever with education or currently not engaged in study. This project will provide Inclusive Education and Psych-Social Support (PSS) along with COVID-19 prevention and preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction Strategies. FIVDB will adopt the Cox's Bazar Education Sector curriculum and primers for the Rohingya community and FIVDB’s adolescent primers (aligned with the NCTB curriculum) for the host community. The courses and contents and teaching-learning methodology will be adapted in a way that, the adolescent learners find it relevant for themselves. The adaptation has already been started as FIVDB is implementing 48 both established and home based LCs in the camps with FCDO and AHP funding and our education team is working with the beneficiaries at the learning center level for adaptation. 3200 Rohingya girls and boys will be enrolled in the learning centers in 7 targeted camps, while 25 government or non-formal schools will be selected for rehavilitation from 5 targeted Union in the host communities. The participants' ages will be between 12-24 years. The size of the students in a learning group would be 10 considering the COVID-19 situation so that physical distance could be maintained. Among the learners 70% will be girls and 7% will be differently able person. There will be hand washing facility and hygiene kits for girls and boys separately in each learning center and school. Teachers will be recruited from the communities and will be trained by the FIVDB education team with support from other sector partners, as it is done currently. There will be close monitoring and supervision of the learning outcomes and regular assessment of the progress in learning.
There is a gender equality identified in access to education services,
specially on inclusion of Adolescent and Youth girls (age group,12-24), this project will work to achieve Gender Euality. Different types of services will will be provided to different groups according to the suitability of affected population segments.The teachers will be provided training on protection and inclusion, PSS and disaster risk reduction as they can ensure the participation of all learners including differently able persons and safety of the participants. Location for Learning Centres will be choosen given priority to safety issues of learners, Train teachers in first aid (including psychological first aid) and that first aid kits are available/equipped In every LC, proper drainage system, waste management system and separate washroom facilities for males and females will also be ensured. There will be a 7-member learning center management committee consisting of members from the participants, guardians and community members. At least 3 members of the committee will be female. This committee will be involved in learning center management as well as all kinds of protection activities. Psycho Social support will be ensured by training teachers on PFA and LC Management Committees and SMC members

1 - Identified Needs

Despite significant progress during the last two years in providing safe and systematic access to learning opportunities, there are still gaps in rohingya community in education sector as well as in host community. 27% of household reported a loss or diminished access to education as an impact of the COVID-19 outbreak (J-MSNA, Preliminary findings, 1 October 2020). More than 30 percent of Rohingya children and youth aged 3-24 years old still require access to education. 69 percent of Rohingya refugee households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not benefiting from any learning opportunities. Furthermore, an alarming 83% of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old don’t have access to any educational or skills development activities (Joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment, ISCG, September 2019). Impact of the lockdown on education is a general concern. There was consensus that the disruption of education poses a great concern and aside the loss of education, less regularly daily routine as a risk to children’s wellbeing of both rohingya and host community. All Learning Centers and Schools remain closed, continuing to hamper access to education for rohingya and host adolescents. There is a need to improve education technology to be used as a complementary solution for learning. (Situation Report, September 2020) For the rohingya 9% of individuals that attended any form of learning before COVID-19 outbreak who will not be sent back. And for the host 3% of individuals that attended any form of learning before the COVID-19 outbreak who will not be sent back . 40% of individuals aged 4-24 were reported not to have attended any formal learning before the COVID-19 outbreak. (J-MSNA, Preliminary findings, 1 October 2020)According to J-MSNA preliminary findings September 2020, the main barriers for rohingya community to studying effectively remotely are- Inability of other household members to support children; lack of money to pay for private tuition; support on remote education generally reported to have been limited across the surveyed camps; children needed to help in household. Main barriers for the host community in particular among poor families, inability of parents to support due to a lack of education and lack of money to pay for private tutor. Adolescent girls to be less likely to be sent back to school due to socio-cultural norms and Girls on the other hand may already be helping in the household and instances of child marriage. Children over the age of 10 reported to be less likely to be sent back due to a lack of appropriate learning opportunities. COVID-19 poses High rate of children drops - out and early child marriage. From our observation community people and parents are not engaged in education program in camps and host. There is lack of awareness about the importance of education of adolescents, that is why parents and community people should be engaged in the education program. In the 4 targeted camps (8E, 21, NYP RC and KUT RC) total gap are 18,870 individuals of 3-24 age groups. In the host community there are according to 2020 the target was 7674 approximately in 12-24 age groups and outreach is zero due to closure of the schools because of the COVID-19 outbreak (JRP-2022_EduSector_PIN_Target Calculation V8 (RC HC)). A noticeable fact is that gap between girls and boys are huge among the 6-18 age group. We have targeted 400 adolescent girls and boys (12-24 age olds) from each targeted camps. In the targeted host community 5 union would be selected (Holdiapalong, Jaliapalong, Palongkhali, Whykong and Hnila). for the school rehabilitation and 5 secondary registered schools would be selected from each union.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1 :Site Selection for Learning Centers (LCs): LCs sites will be selected involving relevant stakeholders. In the 8 targeted camps there would 20 Learning Centers (LC) from each camps. LCs would be established in a place which are accessible and convenient for all and considering the COVID-19 situation. Activity 2 : LCs establishment and School Rehabilitation: LCs will be well decorated and equipped with teaching learning materials in camps (8E, 21 NYP RC and KTP RC). Eeasy access for the children with disability and girls will be a priority. The size of the students in a learning group would be 10 learners considering the COVID-19 situation and COVID safety will be strictly followed. Key messages on COVID-19 would be developed and disseminated. 5 schools would be selected for rehabilitation from 5 targeted Unions.Activity 3: Adaptation of curriculum: FIVDB will implement the Cox's Bazar Education Sector curriculum and primers for Rohingya community (English, Burmese and Math) Activity 4: Form LC/School Management Commiittee (CMC) for LCs and Schools: There will be a 7-member learning center management committee consisting members from the participants, guardians and community members (at least 3 female). This committee from both host and rohingya & host community will look after the LCs management and all kind of CP issues. Monthly meeting of CMC to know the progress, maintenance issues and what they have learnt. Purpose of incapacitating CMC is to ensure ownership and active participation Activity 5: Program Participants Selection: This project will be implemented in the existing camps where FIVDB is working currently. For the program participants selection, existing database would be used as well as sector data and various need assessments. The program participants list will be finalized with the consultation of CMC and approval of the CiCs and UNO. Adolescent and youth will be program participants for this project because from this age girls are confined with household work or for child marriage. On the other hand boys are supposed to support their family from this ageActivity 6: Deliver Session: After preparing the LCs and facilitators, learning session would be delivered in 2 shift in a group consisting 10 learners. Activity 7: Psycho-Social Support (PSS): To Improve Psycho social well- being and resilience among vulnerable adolescents and youth, program participants would be provided consistent awareness session on psycho-social support and well being through drawing, group activities and session. Awareness session would be provided on GBV, nutrition, psycho-social well being, mental health and Psychological First Aid (PFA). There will be adolescents friendly recreational activities creating a safe space for children to conduct stress releasing activities like indoor games and crafting. To develop the life skills of adolescent and youth would be provided training on assertiveness, problem solving skill and communication and relationship skill would be enhanced through play activities, family members group discussion providing training on improve networking in society, workshop on how to participate in household work participation, training on enhance relationship with peer, training on enhance relationship with caregivers. Among the learners if anyone suffers mentally or is victim of GBV issues he or she will be refereed to the relevant sector/organizations who are working with such issues.Activity 8 : Reconstruction of LC's : After any natural calamity/disaster this project will allocate a considerable amount of Budget for LC refurbishment.Activity 9 : Monitoring and Follow up: A monitoring framework will be developed. The monitoring and evaluation team to achieve performance indicators and appropriate evaluation. Periodic Monitoring will be conducted using monitoring checklist. There will also use formal surveys, classroom and impact evaluation to measure impacts.
