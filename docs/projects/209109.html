Project Summary

The ongoing conflict in Sudan had negatively affected the education sector. With school closure and lack of access to education in safe and protected learning environment, children in Sudan have been exposed to life-threatening protection risks such as: child recruitment, child marriage and Gender Based Violence (GBV). In addition, pre-existing challenges related to insufficient learning resources and supplies, the crisis has left learners and teachers displaced and traumatized; damaged learning infrastructure and school spaces, and constrained access to schools. All those reasons had affected the schools re-opening which is being also challenged by two main key factors: lack of finances to support teachers’ salaries and the fact that more than 10% of schools are being used as shelter by displaced people.
NRC’s response will focus on two pillars; prioritizing improvement of children’s wellbeing, centered around provision of psychosocial support and lifesaving skills through our First Phase Education Response (FPER) activities and; provision of basic appropriate non-formal education pathways.) and ensuring referral mechanisms are in place to address the holistic needs of children. The response will provide equal access to girls and boys in addition to children with disabilities.
The (FPER) package will support children to remain connected, protected; and learn through NRC’s Child Education Pathways (CEP) emergency curriculum integrated with life–saving education skills and the Better Learning Programme (BLP). The BLP aims to strengthen children’s resilience and wellbeing. Volunteer teachers will be incentivized and trained with the skills to provide BLP and CEP, through child-friendly approaches, including the identification and referral of children in need of specialized service providers. Rehabilitation works will ensure safety and inclusiveness of schools and Temporary Learning Spaces used for education purposes as per INEE standards including WASH facilities and taking into consideration the needs of the children with disabilities. Investing in schools’ rehabilitation will contribute to reduce challenges facing the MoE to re-open schools in the long term and serve as safe learning spaces.
To ensure continuation of learning and to support children to gain back learning losses due to the schools’ closure since April, structured non-formal education (NFE) pathways will be introduced according to the education background and age of the children as the following: 1). School readiness for children between 6 to 7, 2). Catch-up classes: for children between 8 to 14 years old who previously attended school, 3). Accelerated Learning Programme (ALP), targeting children between 9 to 14 years old who have never been in schools before or missed more than 3 schooling years.
Quality will be ensured through the delivery of “Teachers in Crisis Context – TiCC" training to all teachers involved in the NFE activities. The TiCC will focus on introducing new pedagogical techniques focused on active learning and child-centred approaches, including child protection and teacher wellbeing principles. In addition to BLP where teachers will be able to provide basic psycho-social support to children. The distribution of the stationery kits to learners and teaching kits to teachers respond to the challenge of poverty and financial constraint. Provision of dignity kits for use in menstrual health management for girls between 12 to 14 years old will result in increased participation of girls in education. Community engagement through social mobilization and working with PTAs will provide a platform to increase awareness on the importance of education and the promotion of schools/learning spaces as protective environments.
This approach will be carried out throughout the project period as long as formal schools are closed. In the scenario of schools’ re-opening, NRC will support the transition of children attending NFE classes to formal schools.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Do No Harm principle will be strictly followed by NRC who will ensure safe and inclusive programming approach using the risk reduction approach towards mitigating protection and GBV throughout its programming from the planning phase to the delivery of service to the affected population. Safety and Dignity, avoiding causing harm – To prevent/ mitigate tensions between recipients and non-recipients of assistance provided through the proposed intervention, which might be caused by lack of transparency on targeting criteria. Capacity building trainings to education staff and all involved teachers on child protection and safeguarding principles.

NRC will use the feedback received through the above-established channels to improve the execution of the project interventions throughout the project cycle. This will be done in a safe manner without endangering the sources of the information. Any changes or correction measures required will be incorporated into the project to improve its outcomes.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Affected individuals will be able to share their concerns and provide feedback on accountability, safety, accessibility, and participation aspects of the program response through the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM). The main feedback channels will consist of a toll-free hotline, suggestion boxes, and in-person interactions during the delivery of education activities, community engagement and mobilization. The variety of channels provided will guarantee the respect of the AAP principles and will ensure easy access to all the categories of beneficiaries (children with disability or other vulnerable groups). NRC has well established protocols to register, manage, and report feedback from beneficiaries, including handling sensitive complaints. Through community consultations, in the forms of focus group discussions for example, and the post distribution monitoring, targeted beneficiaries will also inform the design and adaptation during implementation. Finally, NRC has developed child friendly tools respecting the age needs for children to report and provide feedback.
