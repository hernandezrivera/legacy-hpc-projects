Project Summary

The proposed SYCAC project meets the main objective of inviting proposals to {contribute to the sustainable development and empowerment of civil society and local communities in Syria} through the project called “Syrian Peacebuilding Club,” which aims to address the underlying causes of conflict and social disintegration in Syria, it seeks to create “safe spaces'' for civil society organizations, activists, local leaders, focusing on women-led civil society organizations. In addition to the local communities to openly discuss their concerns and promote better understanding of peacebuilding issues, conflict management and women’s participation in peacebuilding for the future of Syria, and this project will help to increase awareness about peacebuilding through hybrid session online and physical in NWS on the following topics:
● Peacebuilding.
● Social Cohesion.
● Transitional Justice.
● Citizenship and Common Ground Methodology.
● Strategic Planning.
● Virtual Facilitation Skills.
● Initiatives.
● Communication.
● Community Integration And networking.
Through the project, the objectives of SYCAC are aligned with the specific objectives of the clusters. It focuses on building the capacity of local civil society actors to better respond to needs related to civil society empowerment, including peacebuilding and conflict management in the context in which they work, to propose innovative ideas and methods to meet the needs of society. The needs resulted from armed conflicts that created a major imbalance and injustice in the country, the human rights situation, and the high levels of violence.
Through this action, SYCAC will build capacity and coordinate with CSOs members, civil society actors, grassroots activists, community leaders, and other stakeholders in Syria. In three different control areas, six cities (Damascus, As-Sweida, Ar-Raqqa, Qamishli, Idleb, and Aleppo).The main centers will be in NW Syria (Albab, Afrin) in Aleppo governorate and (Idlib, Harim) in Idlib governorate; however, SYCAC will conduct Ar'raqqa, Damascus, As-Sweida, Al-Hasakeh through online sessions, in order to guarantee the consistency and the safety of the programs and the staff in this cities, to enable civil society actors and CSOs to identify and assess peacebuilding and conflict management issues and challenges in an inclusive, participatory and transparent manner and design. Also, to implement effective responses to meet those needs. This will lead to the adoption of more participatory and collaborative approaches to social work, bringing together local communities and civil society actors to respond to the needs and priorities of society and work towards the improvement, and development of civil society work, peacebuilding, and conflict management issues.
Also, the project’s activities will work in accommodation with the guidelines listed in the cluster where SYCAC designed a comprehensive intervention starting with involving the participants groups in all the projects phases from the need assessment processes to program development, and implementation of the action. The project will mainly support the actors to play a positive role in promoting local peacebuilding approaches and enhancing the social cohesion within the intervention areas and across multiple regions of influence in Syria.

4 - Needs

The overall war and chaos situation has also affected civil society organizations and activists’ groups who are supposed to be the backbone of the response to the communities needs in the fields of peacebuilding and social cohesion. Based on that, SYCAC has designed the project of “Peacebuilding Club” with the ultimate goal of the project is to contribute to the sustainable Peace and equitable development of communities in Syria in the fields of peacebuilding and civil society empowerment, which is planned to be reached to through achieving the outcomes of having 1): Clear pre-identified local needs in the field of peacebuilding through “safe dialogue spaces'' for local identity groups such CSOs, activists, local leaders, civil society organizations networks, and local women's groups to discuss community concerns openly in the field of community social cohesion and peacebuilding, 2): enhanced mutual understanding of the peacebuilding and conflict management issues by the civil society actors, and 3) skilled and empowered Syrian civil society actors who are able to effectively identify community’s needs and respond to them. Based on the needs assessment during 2021, it was found that the targeted communities suffer from problems of social cohesion between members of the same communities, communities between each other, community members and activists. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

All SYCAC programming is based on the “Do No Harm” principle. SYCAC recognizes that providing any service during a conflict in which needs are dire and resources are scarce, has the potential to cause conflict and protection concerns for all actors involved. We will conduct outreach to clarify to the community the scope of the specific targeted communities that we will be targeted and expect that this will generate an understanding amongst the host community. We will focus concerted communication on making sure that the target of the civil society actors understands the nature of the intervention. This will focus on preparing them for the end of the intervention from the beginning so that they make best use of the time that this will support them. This information will also be reiterated during each coordination meeting and coaching session. Program and M&E staff will be present during program implementation to address any concerns that beneficiaries may have. Additionally, during the needs assessment process, program staff will make note of any particularly vulnerable cases or protection concerns and refer those affected confidentially to relative services providers SYCAC is coordinating with.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SYCAC staff will:Ensuring addressed at a minimum the following elements as fundamental to an effective humanitarian response:Routine analysis of gender concerns to develop humanitarian programming and policy processes;Regular and timely collection and analysis of sex- and age- disaggregated data;Support to coordination of gender programming;Integrate gender issues into preparedness and resilience processes from data collection, assessments, planning and capacity-building for partners and communities;Ensure that monitoring and evaluation mechanisms can ascertain if the different needs of women, girls, boys and men have been met in the humanitarian response;Develop communication and advocacy products that capture the different needs, capacities and voices of women, girls, boys and men;Put in place necessary actions to protect women, girls, boys and men from all forms of sexual exploitation and abuse by SYCAC staff.
