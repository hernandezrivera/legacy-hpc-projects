Project Summary

Building on the recommendations of the independent external evaluation and UN Women’s and OCHA partnership and in full alignment with the priorities of the humanitarian response plan and the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) Compact which clearly refers to gender equality as one of the key principles, UN Women proposes to create a “Gender Hub” with the objective of strengthening accountability of all humanitarian actors for gender equality and empowerment of women and girls in line with the IASC Policy on Gender Equality and Women’s Empowerment in Humanitarian Action and its Accountability Framework. The Gender Hub will be led by UN Women in close cooperation with OCHA and all cluster and sub-cluster lead agencies and organizations.

1 - Needs

Over the course of the last year, the situation in Palestine has been marked by multiple, overlapping crises: more than 54 years of Israeli military occupation of the West Bank and East Jerusalem, over a decade long blockade on Gaza, the internal Palestinian political division, the COVID-19 pandemic and its resulting economic crisis and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. On 20 May 2021, after 11 days of the worst conflict since 2014, Israel and militants in Gaza agreed to a cessation of hostilities. Casualties were recorded on both sides, including more than 260 dead in Gaza (23 girls, 43 boys, 40 women and 150 men) according to the Palestinian Authority (PA) and the Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR), along with considerable destruction of residential and commercial buildings. Recognizing the differential impact of crises on women, girls, men and boys, gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls in humanitarian action (GEEWG) has enjoyed significant recent gains in global normative commitments, as well as investment in the creation of system wide resources and tools. These include the World Humanitarian Summit (2015), the Call to Action on Protection from Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (2015), the New York Declaration on Refugees and Migrants (2016) and the Global Compact on Refugees (2018) and the recently adopted Compact on Women, Peace, Security and Humanitarian Action (2021). Such progress demonstrates a recognition of gender in humanitarian action as not only a rights-based issue, but also as an essential contributor to effective delivery of sustainable results across the humanitarian development nexus. Since 2013, OCHA and UN Women have joined efforts to progressively support gender responsive humanitarian action in the occupied Palestinian territory (OPT). The collaboration was solidified in 2015 with the formulation of the first two-year joint action plan for mainstreaming gender in humanitarian action in the oPt. The plan was followed by another joint action plan “Strengthened Gender Focus in Humanitarian Action” (2018-2020). The partnership has supported the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) to ensure that the humanitarian response in OPT is informed by a refined understanding of gender-based vulnerabilities and gender differentiated impact of the humanitarian context on the various groups of the population and that the response is adapted to meet those needs and overcome potential biases in accessing humanitarian assistance and protection. The partnership has been successful on many fronts particularly by the inclusion of gender coordination mechanism in humanitarian coordination architecture; the Humanitarian Gender Group (HGG), the increased attention to gender related needs and concerns in cluster response and the increased financing to women’s organizations through the Humanitarian Fund. In 2021, an independent external evaluation of the contribution of the UN Women and OCHA Joint Action Plan (JAP) (2018-2020) to an inclusive and gender responsive humanitarian action in the oPt was undertaken. Some of the key lessons learned that were highlighted in the external evaluation included: Lesson Learned 1: Systematically and deliberately leveraging the partnership and complementarities between the two organizations such as OCHA and UN Women contributed significantly to the success of a joint initiative on gender equality. It was however not sufficient to ensure complementarity at strategic level. For the impact of such joint initiatives to be maximized, it is important to put in place a management structure for the initiative that involves regular joint reviews and stock-taking exercises. Lesson Learned 2: Adopting a gender sensitive lens and prioritizing gender responsive interventions needs to be tackled as a nexus approach by the UN Country Team. The UN has a system wide action plan (SWAP) on gender equality and women empowerment for adoption across the UN System (2018-2022), which applies to all UN agencies and entities irrespective of whether they are humanitarian or development agencies. Taken together with the UN’s commitment to leave no one behind and to align itself with the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, UN Country Teams have access to stronger frameworks within which they can implement initiatives that promote gender equality and empower women and girls. Lesson Learned 3: Even when a joint plan of action is mainly directed at international stakeholders (as was the case with the JAP that targeted the humanitarian response architecture) it is vital that the initiative engages the national authorities to ensure maximum impact.Lesson Learned 4: Efforts that are made by UN Women, OCHA, and other stakeholders to improve the mainstreaming of gender into the humanitarian response will be confronted with structural limitations in the architecture of the UNCT and HCT and which have not clearly assigned roles and responsibilities on gender, nor defined clear accountabilities related to them. These limitations are beyond the control of any one single agency or stakeholder and are not a feature of the Palestine context only. Lesson Learned 5: Capacity building efforts on gender and gender responsiveness in the humanitarian response need to respond in their design and delivery to the evolving needs of stakeholders, building on the increased knowledge they have already gained. Some of the key recommendations for the development of a new JAP include: • UN Women and OCHA Palestine should liaise with the HC’s office to identify ways in which the accountability for gender equality and women empowerment is clearly identified and system wide. • UN Women, OCHA should lead efforts through the HCT to strengthen the localized approach to gender-responsiveness in Palestine, better reflecting the priorities of national stakeholders including affected population and CSOs.

2 - Activities

Building on the recommendations of the independent external evaluation and UN Women’s and OCHA partnership and in full alignment with the priorities of the humanitarian response plan and the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) Compact which clearly refers to gender equality as one of the key principles, UN Women proposes to create a “Gender Hub” with the objective of strengthening accountability of all humanitarian actors for gender equality and empowerment of women and girls in line with the IASC Policy on Gender Equality and Women’s Empowerment in Humanitarian Action and its Accountability Framework. The Gender Hub will be led by UN Women in coordination with OCHA and all cluster and sub-cluster lead agencies and organizations. The Gender Hub will support humanitarian actors to integrate gender equality more effectively through systematic gender mainstreaming in humanitarian planning and response. The end objective is better service delivery by humanitarian actors for greater empowerment outcomes for women, girls, boys and men affected by the protracted humanitarian crisis in the oPt. In doing so, the hub will also emphasize the application of an intersectionality approach with attention not only to gender but also to age and other diversity factors The Hub will build on ongoing efforts and expertise of the different gender and protection related coordination groups (Protection, humanitarian gender group (HGG), GBV sub-sector, PSEA), also factoring in that UN Women is a member in the protection cluster& GBV sub cluster, co-chairs the HGG, and is member of the steering committee for the PSEA Network. It will take into account analysis and technical guidance emanating from these different groups and ensures that gender is well integrated in the technical advisory support provided to humanitarian sectors, humanitarian national and international actors, UN siter agencies, government and local CSO partners. It will provide technical support to all humanitarian actors to help them translate policies and commitments and various assessments and study recommendations on gender and women’s empowerment into tangible actions.The accountability for mainstreaming gender equality considerations in all internal strategies, work plans, operational guidance, and implementation remains with individual agencies, but the gender hub will provide all actors with hands-on targeted gender mainstreaming support throughout the humanitarian programme cycle from planning, implementation to monitoring upon request from cluster and sub-cluster lead agencies. The gender hub will be a training hub and will develop and deliver training courses for humanitarian workers and local counterparts engaged in humanitarian response on gender awareness and gender sensitivity, gender and diversity, and gender mainstreaming, gender with age marker (GAM), sex and age disaggregated data (SAAD), and gender responsive monitoring and evaluation. The hub will coordinate with the PSEA network to support dedicated trainings on PSEA. The trainings will target humanitarian workers and implementing partners (including women’s organizations) teams and it will also be offered to government counterparts and local partners as necessary.The gender hub will also act as a knowledge management hub of assessments and research that will contribute to strengthening the knowledge and evidence-base for designing interventions that will have impact. It will synthesize research findings and document promising practices that are gender-responsive in digestible forms that stakeholders will more easily be able to relate to, and host knowledge sharing events and roundtables. Based on the knowledge of available research, the hub will also identify research gaps and support the development of cutting-edge research proposals.The primary beneficiaries of the project are humanitarian actors (UN, national and international NGOs including women’s organizations, government counterparts). These humanitarian actors are expected to follow the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Policy on Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women and Girls (GEEWG) in Humanitarian Action that sets out the principles, standards, and actions that humanitarian actors should abide by to integrate gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls into humanitarian work. The secondary beneficiaries are women, girls, boys and men receiving the humanitarian services or are participants and stakeholders of the humanitarian activities implemented by humanitarian actors. The project will strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors to making provision to meet the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys in all their diversity, promote and protect their human rights, and redress gender inequalities through their humanitarian actions. This will contribute to Palestinian women and girls, in the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and Gaza, having equitable access to protection and life-saving assistance and having more opportunities to influence and make decisions about their lives.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

As a member of the Humanitarian Country Team in the oPt, UN Women operates under the SPHERE principles . Do No Harm is a key humanitarian principle under this framework, and guides UN Women’s programme development and implementation. Thus UN Women ensures that assistance does no harm, or minimizes the harm that may invariantly be done. In this regard, UN Women will ensure through the gender hub that all trainings and technical advice on gender mainstream a strong protection lens and integrate guidance on the Do no harm.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UN Women will promote through the gender hub trainings a survivor-centred and inclusive approach to service delivery . This will ensure that survivors’ rights and needs are prioritized first and foremost. The survivor-centred approach aims to create a supportive environment in which the survivor’s rights are respected and in which women survivors are treated with dignity and respect. The approach helps to promote the survivor’s recovery and ability to identify and express needs and wishes, as well as to reinforce capacity to make decisions about possible interventions. Through this approach, women will be provided with options and will have access to information instead of being told what to do. The gender hub will also produce knowledge such as the gender alerts which have a strong focus on gender-based vulnerabilities and strengthening the understanding of the different needs and priorities of women, men, boys and girls.
