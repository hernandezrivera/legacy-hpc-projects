Project Summary

The provision of effective health risks communication and referral systems is crucial in combating diseases such as cholera, malaria, diphtheria, and other childhood diseases. This project aims to establish a comprehensive communication and referral infrastructure to ensure timely and accurate dissemination of health information, prompt detection of diseases, and appropriate referral of patients for treatment and care.
The project will focus on addressing the lack of awareness, misinformation, and limited access to healthcare services, which are significant barriers to disease prevention and control. By implementing a robust communication strategy, we aim to educate and empower communities, healthcare providers, and stakeholders about the risks and prevention strategies associated with these diseases.
Key objectives of the project include:
1. Developing Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials: We will create culturally sensitive and linguistically appropriate IEC materials in collaboration with local health authorities and experts. These materials will provide comprehensive information on disease symptoms, prevention measures, available treatment options, and the importance of early referral for diagnosis and treatment.
2. Establishing a network of community health workers (CHWs): Community health workers will be trained and deployed to disseminate health information, conduct awareness campaigns, and facilitate referrals. They will actively engage with the community members, identify potential cases, and refer them to appropriate healthcare facilities.
3. Strengthening healthcare facilities and referral systems: The project will work towards improving the capacity and infrastructure of healthcare facilities to effectively manage and treat cholera, malaria, diphtheria, and childhood diseases. This includes training healthcare providers on disease diagnosis, treatment, and referral protocols.
4. Developing a real-time surveillance and reporting system: A robust disease surveillance and reporting system will be established to monitor disease outbreaks, track cases, and provide timely information to relevant stakeholders. This will facilitate early detection and rapid response to disease outbreaks.
5. Collaboration and partnerships: The project will collaborate with local health authorities, international organizations, and relevant stakeholders to maximize the impact of the intervention. This will ensure coordination, resource sharing, and sustainability of the project activities.
Through the provision of health risks communication and referral systems, this project aims to reduce the incidence and impact of cholera, malaria, diphtheria, and other childhood diseases. By empowering communities and strengthening healthcare facilities, we strive to enhance disease prevention, early detection, and timely treatment, ultimately improving the overall health outcomes and well-being of the target population.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection mainstreaming approach in this project is underpinned by four Guiding Principles: 1. Prioritise safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimise as much as possible any unintended negative effects of interventions which can increase people's vulnerability to physical and psychosocial risks. 2. Meaningful access: Arrange for people’s access to impartial assistance and services - in proportion to need and without any barriers (i.e. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3. Accountability to beneficiaries: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, or address concerns and complaints. 4. Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

For the general population, we will conduct semi-structured discussions with different groups of men, women, boys and girls on what type of complaints mechanism works for them and use the feedback to inform the mechanism being developed. • For groups with specific needs (single women, children, older persons, persons with disabilities), hold focus groups to understand their attitudes around revealing sexual abuse and to identify the environments and the situations in which they feel most comfortable reporting it. Focus groups are a great tool to reveal topics that are difficult to discuss. Participants will become more comfortable and open up, if these topics are addressed indirectly. The starting point for the conversation could be the ways in which an emergency situation has made people feel more vulnerable, their needs and the ways in which they can feel protected. Once an atmosphere of group trust is created, participants will find it easier to open up about individual and more delicate concerns of SEA. A moderator’s guide should be developed to provide support on conducting focus groups that get the information in an indirect manner, while keeping focus on the goals. Keep in mind the cultural context (i.e. in some situations women do not feel comfortable speaking up in the presence of men or children do not speak out in front of older people).
