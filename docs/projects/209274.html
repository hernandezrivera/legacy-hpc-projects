Project Summary

In line with the 2024 Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) Cluster Response Plan, this project will seek to mitigate the suffering of crisis-affected populations ensuring that they have access to basic water services that are climate change-sensitive, adequate gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, and hygiene services (including vector control, waste management, hygiene kits inclusive of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) items, and risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) for hygiene awareness). The armed conflict that began on 15 April 2023, between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) led to an overall increase in WASH needs, affecting approximately 18.9 million individuals, with the cluster targeting 8.9 million people. The primary focus of the relief effort is to assist those who are recently displaced, individuals stranded in certain areas, and families congregating at designated points. Assistance is particularly aimed at vulnerable groups such as women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities or other vulnerabilities. Additionally, efforts are being made to address the needs of individuals affected by epidemics, natural disasters, and the consequences of climate change, notably water scarcity and floods. This project aims to provide humanitarian WASH assistance to 900,000 beneficiaries including 183,600 women, 176,400 men, 275,400 girls, and 264,600 boys with approximately 12 per cent of the target population estimated to be either elderly, people with disabilities, or individuals with other vulnerabilities. IOM aims to assist approximately 450,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) newly displaced by conflict or disasters related to natural hazards with WASH services and improve and sustain access to safe water for approximately 400,000 individuals including returnees and crisis-affected communities. The project will focus on crisis impacted communities in various states primarily in areas where water infrastructure is damaged or available capacity is unable to meet the critical needs stretched by the influx of the displaced. IOM will also support communities to improve hygiene through sustaining access to safe water and sanitation facilities and promoting hygiene messages to address and increase community resilience to malnutrition, disease outbreaks, and other public health related risks targeting approximately 50,000 individuals. IOM will provide emergency water assistance through the establishment and rehabilitation of water points, access to proper sanitation facilities and hygiene items and campaigns. This will include environmental impact mitigation such as the provision of solar technology to power water points and monitoring ground water to moderate depletion levels. IOM will also participate in the Cholera Taskforce; ensuring access to WASH facilities while supporting management of cholera cases in cholera treatment centres and units through provision of safe drinking water, water chlorination, and water quality monitoring and hygiene promotion/cleaning campaigns. Exit strategies will be developed in more secure states of the country to alleviate aid dependency in protracted emergencies by strengthening self-reliance and facilitating community solutions. WASH committees shall be established, and maintenance tools/spare parts supplied. The project will ensure dignified and equitable access to basic WASH services through the implementation of early recovery approaches where possible, connecting the need for short-term emergency interventions during the ongoing military conflict with long-term solutions by working directly with local communities to facilitate ownership and accountability. The WASH emergency interventions will gradually contribute to socio-economic inclusion of forcibly displaced populations and returnees by directly increasing access to basic services for displaced and host communities and indirectly increasing employment opportunities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project aims to involve the communities directly in the design and implementation of activities, as well as the decision-making process. The goal is to identify priority needs based on factors such as gender, age, and vulnerabilities. This includes the distribution of MHM kits and assessing risks related to accessing WASH services. By adopting an inclusive and participatory approach, the project seeks to incorporate the perspectives of vulnerable women, girls, and persons with disabilities, who are often marginalized or overlooked in community decisions. They face additional barriers in accessing services, particularly when it comes to choosing the locations and features of latrines and water points. By consulting all members of the community, the project ensures that their needs are not only recognized but also that protection risks are identified and addressed.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

In the current context, IOM will be prioritizing the use of Communicating with Communities (CwC) to ensure that there is a continuous process of listening, responding, and adapting programs based on the needs of the community and factor in conflict sensitivity. IOM will seek to include all groups especially women, youth, the elderly, persons with disability, and underrepresented groups from both the IDP and host community and community leaders in assessments and consultations on the best locations for WASH facilities. IOM with the support of local organizations will train and empower WASH management committees to engage in and continue hygiene awareness sessions and activities such as vector control, waste management, clean-up campaigns while collecting community feedback on the efficacy of these activities and adapt or change modalities when needed based on continuous engagement. IOM will also provide IEC materials inclusive of RCCE messaging such as flyers and billboards for hygiene awareness campaigns to support local hygiene promoters from the target communities to disseminate the messages, this community-based activity will include collecting information and feedback on the coverage and efficacy of the messages. During the distribution of hygiene kits, jerrycans, soap, chlorine, there will be registration and help desks established to collect feedback during distributions and provide additional direct assistance or referals to other service providers. After the distributions there will be post distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the level of satisfaction of the beneficiaries through individual and household surveys. IOM will keep a record of the type of feedback or complaint received, time the feedback was received, and how it was addressed. Feedback and complaints received will be categorized into different levels ranging from ordinary level in lower grades such as explaining eligibility and selection criteria to the local community to major dissatisfactions and safeguarding issues (such as reporting suspected cases of PSEA and fraud). Community feedback will be documented in results monitoring frameworks, activity and field visit reports, surveys analyses and actively referred to throughout the project. The feedback received including best practices and lessons learnt will be incorporated during regular project review exercises, to improve the quality of the ongoing work, adapt to emerging needs and changes, or improve planning of future programming.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IOM is committed to making itself accountable to the affected populations. IOM will therefore continue strengthening existing referral pathways to facilitate multi-partner and multisector referral at the site level, leveraging on relevant inter-agency coordination forums and community complaint feedback mechanisms to facilitate two-way communications with partners and project participants. Community feedback will be solicited and incorporated at every step of the program, including design, implementation and the Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) systems. Beneficiary feedback will be collected periodically at three critical stages of the provision of assistance: (1) During assessments information will be collected about the selection processes and additional beneficiary needs. (2) During main activities such as awareness sessions where key informant interviews, face to face meetings and complaint desks will be set up for beneficiaries to make inquiries and provide feedback while getting a response within a specified timeline. (3) During monitoring and evaluation missions, where key members of the community committees and 30-50 per cent sample size of the participating population will be selected for questionnaires about the support provided. There will be follow-ups at each of these stages to ascertain if beneficiary complaints were addressed. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA, as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to handle a GBV disclosure and make safe referrals through existing referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance.
