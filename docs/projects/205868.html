Project Summary

This is for twelve months’ project and will be implemented in Awerial, Pibor, Yei, Lainya, Kajokeji, Renk, Malakal, Mayiandit, Rubkona, Leer and Yirol East counties designed to empower Adolescent girls, young women, women, men and vulnerable community groups in preventing and responding to GBV risks.
The intervention will specifically target to empower adolescent girls, young women, Adolescent women and groups with special needs to have knowledge, information, skills and enhance their resilience capacity to GBV risks and improve GBV support-seeking behavior for survivors. The intervention will also target community supportive structures power holders: community leaders, religious leaders, parents, community social workers, health workers, police officers, government leaders, and teachers to take active measures to prevent GBV. The project will implement both prevention and response actions through capacity building of rights holders and groups at risk mainly: Adolescent girls, Young women, UASC, children and adolescents with disabilities who are disproportionally more impacted and at high risks of facing violence, neglect and exploitation including family-separation.
The project will use gender transformative approaches to provide gender and age-appropriate and tailored support for adolescent girls, vulnerable women, UASC, child mothers, survivors of GBV among the returnees and arriving returnees and work with men and boys through community dialogues, social behavior changes Communication and advocacy approaches and community-driven harm prevention approaches.
The project will use participatory approaches to create awareness in the community on the GBV effect and prevention of harmful practices targeting 24000 people in 10 Counties (8000 girls, 7000 boys,5000 females and 4000 men, disabilities inclusive). The awareness sessions aim to address the root causes of abuse including GBV risks such as inequality in the distribution of power and resources, harmful practices and under-representation of women and girls' decision-making and leadership opportunities. The community mobilization and engagement approach will integrate social behavior change models to empower girls, boys, men, and women for gender socialization and positive masculinity and community actions renouncing harmful practices and GBV risks to adolescent girls, women, and persons with disabilities using the SASA approach.
Conduct mapping and update the GBV referrals and disseminate the information on the referral for the community to be aware of GBV services in the area. Conduct mapping and dissemination of GBV referral pathways in collaboration with adolescents groups, community women groups/women-led organizations and youth groups , safeguarding risks mitigation, and referral pathways including in partnership with adolescents and young people (6 youth groups): The project will engage young people in safety audits and support them to conduct community mappings to identify community-based GBV risks and vulnerabilities also working together to identify ways to prevent and mitigate GBV risks at the community level, updating of existing referral pathways and disseminate them in schools, communities and community spaces. The intervention will facilitate a functional GBV referral pathway with stakeholders, youth and women-led organizations to upscale access to GBV case management, MHPS and capacity building of social workers.
Provide Capacity building on life skills training for adolescent girls and boys at risk of, and survivors of GBV, in school and out-of-school adolescents (10-19 years) 16000 adolescent girls and 14000 boys on life skills and GBV prevention using PALS and Champion of change models for empowering adolescent girls and boys on gender equality, prevention of GBV, Child marriage, MHM support them to stay safe and adopt healthy behavior, the Champion of Change approach will promote positive masculinity and meaningful engagement of men and boys.

1. Needs

About 2.7million individuals are in need of GBV prevention and response services in South Sudan, majority are women, adolescent girls and people with special needs from IDPs, returnees and host communities. There is high need in some of the areas: Cueibet, Awerial, Pibor, Renk, Malakal, Mayardit, Leer, counties of Lakes, Unity and Upper Nile states. Plan International and partners have presence and are implementing projects in these locations on protection, GBV prevention, CVA and food distribution. Gender analysis conducted by Plan International in 6 counties in 2023 reveals GBV is prevalent in the communities contributed highly by patriarchal gender norms, economic hardship, insecurity and conflict, as well as the absence of law enforcement at the community levels. The conflict situations increase the risks of violence to girls and women, where sexual gender-based violence was reported to be the most common form of violence and early forced child marriage. Limited access to basic services such as food, water and sanitation facilities are compromising girls and women's safety, as they are often forced to travel long distances in search of water, thus compromising their security and increasing incidences of rape and sexual assaults and domestic violence when returning home late from fetching water. People living with disabilities often encounter additional barriers in accessing assistance and support, thus making them more vulnerable to GBV. The crisis situation is exacerbating protection risks hence vulnerable children, adolescents and young people are at risk or engaging in negative coping mechanisms such as child labour, traveling to far distances to look for employment or casual work further exposing their children to abuse, sexual abuse, physical assault, psychosocial distress, dropping out of school to ca re for younger ones among others, adolescent girls are dropping from school which put them at high risk of getting into early and forced marriages. The Communities and returnees in Malakal and Renk locations in Upper Nile are facing protection facing risks, Female returnees in Malakal continue to report forms of abuse including sexual violence, rape, early marriage, harassment, domestic violence and intimate partner violence (Plan International RNA, Unity State September 2023). Meanwhile, there is a lack of gender-based violence (GBV) response services available also a lack of or non-existent case management system in health clinics attending to GBV survivors. Poverty, food insecurity, and a culture of impunity in the community are among the contributing factors, many girls and women are at risk of abuse when they go to fetch water, and others are forced into SGBV as coping mechanisms to meet basic needs. Girls are forced into marriage to cultural beliefs and expectations on girls' positions and as a form of economic security. Adolescents are disproportionally impacted by GBV due to their young age and inexperience with relationships which can heighten their risk to GBV. Adolescents and girls living with disabilities are 2-4 times more likely to experience GBV, Also, girls who are out of school, those experiencing food insecurity and those engaged in labour are among the most vulnerable to experience GBV. GBV incidents are underreported, due to low awareness of the effects of GBV, also reporting an incident of sexual violence or rape is hindered by the risk of stigmatization, retaliation, shame or marginalization and oppression from the community, as well as lack of survivor-centred service provision after referrals. In the targeted Counties, there are limited GBV awareness among the communities and returnees, prevention mechanisms and inadequate response services including referral pathways and psychosocial support services to address GBV concerns, also gaps in in technical capacity and coordination.

2. Project activities

The project interventions are in line with the needs of the community on prevention and response to GBV as well as HRP objectives for 2024. The overall objective of the project is to enhance the capacity of women, men, girls and boys, people with disabilities and older persons facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, injury, and severe distress to have resilience capacity and access to specialized life-saving health care, including MHPSS, child protection and GBV servicesSpecific objectives 1: Adolescent girls have access to safe and supportive social networks to enhance their resilient capacity to GBV risks. Specific objective 2: Community structures strengthened to address GBV concerns and create a protective environment. Specific objective 3: GBV survivors, adolescent girls and vulnerable women have access to dignified and safe specialized life-saving quality GBV serviceActivities: 1.1. Establish girls (7200) and boys (6000) group sessions on Lifeskils using Champion of Change modules for adolescent girls and boys on gender equality, GBV, child marriage, peacebuilding, positive masculinity and MHM. 1.2: Sports bonanza and engage 1600 girls and 1600 boys from (CoC clubs) on awareness raising and dialogues with key who will be involved from parents, traditional leaders, and government leaders on gender equality and harmful practices affecting girls and women's rights. 1.3. Establish/ strengthen GBV help desks in schools linking with CBCPCs to support the provision of GBV prevention information, reporting and referrals. The project will support the establishment of 200 GBV desks/ information centers, the desks will be placed in identified safe spaces in schools to facilitate access to information on GBV prevention, information on available services for girls, boys, adolescents, children with disability, also facilitate referrals for identified cases. The GBV desks will be operated by trained community social workers with the support of 1600 people (teachers, PTAs, SMCs) who will be identified and trained as GBV/ CP focal points in schools. 1.4. Distribute emergency dignity kits to 8000 adolescent girls in target locations based on assessed need and vulnerability. These kits will be aligned with the established UNFPA guidelines in the country. Activities:2.1. Mapping of community women-based groups/ organizations and conducting training on GBV, PSHEA risks, and mitigation measures: 2.2. Conduct GBV awareness and 20 dialogue sessions with key stakeholders targeting 12000 community members (6600 females, 5400males) 2.4. Identify, train and engage 200 men champions to led discussion on men engage in promoting positive gender norms and prevent GBV- GBV- awareness conducted through community meetings with men in the area and local radio discussion. 2.5. Train women (800 women) and girl’s leaders (6200 girls) on women leadership, women's participation in family and community decision-making, safeguarding, reporting mechanisms, GBV response and prevention. 2.6. Conduct men’s / boy engagement forum to discuss gender norms associated with negative masculinity and harmful cultural practices such as child, early and forced marriages. 2.8. Conduct positive parenting and life skills programmes to 2000 vulnerable parents and caregivers (100 females,100 males). Activity 3.1. Establish women and girls' safe spaces for psychosocial support and life skills session to adolescent, 3.2. Conduct Mobile GBV services to respond to the needs of the community. The project will collaborate with community social workers and GBV officers to conduct 2 mobile GBV sessions per month to provide information and services to girls, boys, adolescents, women, and men including people with disability to most identified GBV risk areas during mapping. The mobile services will be integrated into other project activities, specifically during food security and nutrition sessions, Sanitation and hygiene promotion meetings.
