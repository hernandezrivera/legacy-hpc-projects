Project Summary

In Somalia, seasonal floods, ongoing conflicts, recurring droughts, poor maintenance of water sources, and the outbreak of COVID-19, have led to continued challenges and high humanitarian WASH needs. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs overview shows that a total of 7.7 million people in Somalia are estimated to require humanitarian assistance. Among these figures, An estimated 6.4 million people need emergency life-saving WASH assistance. So 3.5 million need safe drinking water, 5.1 are in need of sanitation services, and 6.4 million need hygiene supplies. The report showed that also 1.6 million IDPs are in need of WASH services. Due to flash floods, WASH infrastructures were damaged especially among the riverine communities in the Shabelles and Hiraan regions. The lack of latrines that promotes open defecation has had severe impacts on the health of the populations resulting in AWD, as well as poor hygiene and sanitation practices. Lack of access to clean and safe water has exacerbated incidences of water-borne diseases especially cholera which is endemic in Somalia so to combat these diseases more sustainable solutions for water accessibility are needed for both the host communities and the IDPs.
AWI is proposing interventions intended to provide sustained access to safe clean water and promotion of hygiene and sanitation best practices which are key instruments in addressing these problems. AWI will support in rehabilitation of 22 existing shallow wells that will be targeting approximately 4400 households, we will work with the local community to identify those water infrastructures. As per the WASH cluster standards, we will construct 15 new shallow wells in the targeted locations. AWI will also rehabilitate one borehole. In targeting the locations of these wells we will collaborate with the local community. There will be chlorination of wells in case of AWD outbreaks or those areas that are at risk to prevent further spread. The chlorine will be provided to the existing or newly formed water management committees and they will be trained on how to use it. Apart from the lack of safe drinking water, there is also a lack of knowledge and practice related to the safe disposal of human waste and domestic waste. During the rainy season, human and domestic waste is washed down the river, contaminating the water that is used in the next village for domestic use.
We will have the distribution of hygiene messages and hygiene kits to the targeted groups, so as to combat AWD outbreaks. Provision and distribution of 4,800 hygiene kits including bar soap, jerry can, bucket and aqua tabs or chlorfloc in case of turbid river water to the most vulnerable households. Latrines that are gender-sensitive will be constructed and installed with hand washing facilities in the villages with the lowest amount of latrines. We will construct communal latrines in the IDP camp settlements. Each village will form hygiene committees that will be trained on hygiene awareness and promotion. In addressing the needs of the community, we will coordinate with the existing committees and local authorities.

1. - Projects activities

The project interventions will improve immediate needs and long-term access to safe and clean water, sanitation, and hygiene practices and this will include: Rehabilitation of 22 existing shallow wells in selected villages targeting 4400 households in Balcad, Jowhar, and Banadiir. Construction of 15 solar-powered shallow wells targeting 3000 people in Balcad, Jowhar, and Banadiir. Construction of 8 water pans that will benefit almost 720 households in Jowhar. Rehabilitate 1 borehole in one of the riverine villages in Middle Shabelle. Provision of chlorine to the flood/AWD-prone areas. Construction of 300 communal latrines in IDP settlements in Banadiir. Distribute 4800 hygiene kits to vulnerable households. Mobilization and capacity building for WASH committees and promotion of hygiene practices. Provide training to WASH committees on wells chlorination, bucket chlorination as well as household water treatment.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In beneficiary selection, we will work with all the relevant stakeholders in ensuring that all the needs of the vulnerable groups in the target area are identified and their issues addressed accordingly. A participatory approach will be used during selection to ensure the inclusion of marginalized groups inclusive of IDPS thus preventing discrimination. Most of the issues affecting marginalized groups like women and children will be considered and integrated into the project implementation. At times conflicts might arise due to the sharing of resources among the IDPs and host communities so in protecting these vulnerable groups we need to work with the community leaders, existing infrastructure management committees, and other relevant stakeholders. We will engage the community at large and committees present to identify solutions so as to promote a sense of ownership and even improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. The project interventions will be keen to address gender and age issues and the project objectives will be designed to address the needs of the different groups as identified. Also, during data collection, we will ensure it is disaggregated by sex and age in order to provide the necessary information in regards to gender and age. In the selection of community committees, we will have a balanced composition in terms of gender. AWI will ensure that all the WASH infrastructures are located within safe and visible access of the community. Most of the water sources are limited and in far distances and this affects women and girls in terms of exposure to GBV risks and even health-wise as they walk very long distances. We will ensure in the construction and rehabilitation of water infrastructures we consider the distance is reduced. In the construction of latrines as well, we will have gender-segregated latrines and that are lockable from the inside and that will address age-specific needs as well. The latrines will be near the settlements and installed with solar lamps for use at night as well as hand washing facilities. This will support the reduction of GBV and encourage women and girls to move around more freely within the settlements. Hygiene promoters will be also trained to the specific needs of women and girls and will be encouraged to sensitize women and girls to menstruation needs and dedicated hygiene habits. Conflicts and violence affect the access to essential basic services in the community. During our project implementation, we will ensure adequate representation of the groups in the target area inclusive of the marginalized groups. During the selection of the beneficiaries, we will ensure it is inclusive and transparent and that the needs of each group are addressed. AWI will consult both the IDPs and the host communities before any implementation takes place. This will be aimed at reducing any tensions or conflict between the two groups at a later stage. AWI will ensure that all involved stakeholders including staff are well aware and take into consideration actions that might cause conflicts within the community. AWI will work together with the local authorities, community leaders, and religious leaders in promoting harmonious coexistence within the community.
