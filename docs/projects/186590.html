Project Summary

Plan International Nigeria will implement a 12-month project focusing on improving access and coverage of quality lifesaving emergency nutrition services integrated with health, child protection and psychosocial support in BAY states. The project will also strengthen the capacity of government healthcare providers in Health facilities ensuring the continuity and sustainability of these vital services amid the COVID-19 pandemic. The project will treat SAM cases with Medical complication and appetite test failed in SCs and uncomplicated SAM in OTP, conduct active case finding, sensitization, referral of SAM cases, and SBCC MIYCN and others relevant topics. The project will further provide stimulation, psychosocial support and counselling services, child protection and promote Optimal IYCF-E (Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies) practices. The proposed activities will strengthen the management of acute malnutrition in BAY state by providing inpatient management of SAM in hospitals (SC sites) and OTP of SAM through establishment of new SC and OTP sites to provide service for IDP/host communities. Besides, the project will have BSFP and TSFP for at risk and MAM cases respectively. In the course of its implementation, the project shall integrate gender responsive messaging and child safeguarding in its community engagement processes, share information, encourage discussions and address gender and safeguarding issues that impact optimal IYCF. In addition, the project will raise awareness on COVID-19 mode of transmission and gender responsive and inclusive prevention strategies. The project will also provide technical and logistical support and strengthen the supportive supervision and monitoring system in the LGAs. The project will focus on supporting the most vulnerable population groups, specifically children under five years of age, caretakers of SAM children, caretakers of SAM children who are living with disabilities and other vulnerable groups. Plan International will integrate management of complicated SAM cases into existing health services in each general hospital and in the LGA health system using State Primary Health Care and Development Agency (SPHCDA) and hospital staff in all targeted LGAs.
PLAN will strengthen the capacity of the government service providers as well as parents, guardians and care takers to prevent malnutrition and respond to the treatment of complicated SAM cases and we will work closely with government and partners working in each LGA. The project will strengthen accountability to beneficiaries through the establishment and strengthening of an inclusive and appropriate community and facility -based complaints and feedback mechanism that will allow women, girls, men, and boys to safely report concerns of the emergency nutrition response in their respective Hospital. The project also will ensure gender sensitive monitoring and evaluation is undertaken by dis aggregating all data by sex, age, disability and creating complimentary qualitative and quantitative indicators.

1 - Needs

The conflict in North East Nigeria is in its eleventh year and continues to affect millions of Nigerians, particularly women and children, subjecting them to displacement, impoverishment, social instability, disrupted livelihoods, diminished agricultural production, impoverishment, threats of violence and has had severe impacts on the food security and nutrition situation. In 2021 as per the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), a total 8.7 million people (including 2.4 million girls, 2.7 million boys, 1.9 million women and 1.7 million men) need humanitarian assistance. Of which, 1.4 million children in need have extremely limited or no access to core child-protection services.The October 2020 Joint Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in BAY States found that the prevalence of GAM was 12.3% in Yobe, 10.0% in Borno, and 6.2% in Adamawa. Prevalence of GAM exceeded the WHO Crisis Classification threshold for “High” (10%) in 3 domains of Yobe state. In the same survey, the GAM and SAM rate in Northern Yobe including Karasuwa and Yusufari LGAs revealed that 15.8% and 2.4% has exceeded WHO crisis classification threshold “very high) and in Northern Adamawa including Michika and Mubi North LGAs depicts as GAM rate at 7.5% and SAM rate at 1.1%. The IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) analysis issued in March 2021 from May – August 2021 showed in Northern Yobe including Yusufari and Karasuwa LGAs is classified as Phase 4 (critical) and Northern Adamawa including Michika and Mubi North LGAs as Phase 2 (alert). According to the IPC AMN classification, Phase 4 (Critical) and Phase 3 (Serious) indicate that urgent action is required to reduce acute malnutrition levels by significantly scaling up and intensifying treatment and protection activities and IPC, Phase 2 (Alert) indicates the situation is progressively deteriorating with increased levels of acute malnutrition and requires strengthening the existing response. The causes of malnutrition in the BAY states is due to poor food consumption patterns (quantity and nutritional quality), low access to and control of household food and nutrition resource, decreased food accessibility, safe water access with possible outbreaks of acute watery diarrhea/cholera, measles, low rate exclusive breast feeding, poor dietary diversity, poor sanitation and health care, malaria and high morbidity rates (diarrhea and fever). The nutrition situation is negatively impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and its associated socio-economic impacts. Malnutrition in under-five children is a major cause of morbidity and mortality. If it is not treated timely, the likelihood of severe acutely malnourished children to die is 12 times higher than their healthy peers, while children with moderate acute malnutrition are 4 times more likely to die. Children suffering from prolonged undernutrition who survive may become locked in a cycle of recurring illness and faltering growth, with irreversible damage to their development and cognitive abilities. This highlights the importance of preventing a further deterioration of the food and nutrition insecurity in this area. Currently, in Karasuwa and Yusufari LGAs of Yobe state, Michika and Mubi North LGAs of Adamawa states among the total SAM aret,10% of them needs inpatient admission in SC of respective hospitals. If these cases are left untreated they may lead to increased mortality rate in children. To address the needs of children under 5, Plan International will carry out the below activities: treatment of complicated SAM cases in 3 general hospitals, provide psycho social support and counselling, awareness on COVID-19 prevention, screening and referral of SAM cases using MUAC and WFH and behavioral change interventions including IYCF. The number of the targeted beneficiaries are calculated using nutrition sector target for 2020. SC beneficiaries are calculated with the assumption that 10% of the total SAM children will be admitted in SCs at 70% coverage.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Plan International promotes gender equality as a central part of our vision of change. Plan’s Global Policy on Gender Equality and Inclusion requires that all activities undertaken are based on an understanding of the underlying gender-issues and barriers that impede women and girls specifically in benefiting from project activities. Gender equality will be fully integrated throughout the project, starting from targeted inclusive implementation processes to staff recruitment and the participation of both men and women in project structures and evaluation processes. Women and men will be given equal chances during project staff recruitment, with women highly encouraged to apply for positions. In addition, because of their important role in successful community mobilization, the project will deploy as many female workers as possible. While all program staff are accountable for project gender equality and safe programming goals, a Gender and psychosocial support Officer will work closely with staff members to ensure gender and child protection standards integration into interventions.Training/orienting all project staff, partners and stakeholders on the gender-related barriers, child protection and building their accountability on safeguarding standards to address issues and concerns on risk of harm will also take place. In collaboration with the government, awareness/capacity building of health and child protection personnel participating in the project will be raised around the effects of gender inequality, power dynamics harmful traditional practices that have a direct impact on feeding practices and nutrition of children, especially girls as well as the differential experiences and effects of child protection and safeguarding issues on girls and boys through training. Activities proposed are specifically designed to better meet the needs of children of different age and gender groups. Project includes estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. Plan International Nigeria follows the principle of “Do No Harm” which will be considered in all of its activities, which requires the organization to take specific steps and measures to protect and reduce the risk of harm from all intervention processes and activities , such as participatory beneficiary selection, involvement of community members throughout the implementation, informing them about the intervention’s aims and activities, informed consent collection, conducting a risk assessment prior to engagement , as well as ensuring the existence of a functioning and responsive feedback and complaint mechanism at community level that allows timely action to address issues of concern that may cause or put beneficiaries at risk of harm, reputational risk or create conflict in the beneficiary communities.The Emergency Nutrition Project in Nigeria illustrates that it is vital that beneficiaries have a strong sense of ownership of the project and that the ability of men and women to assess their own situation including safety and their ability to take actions to improve their own and their children’s nutritional status. Moreover, identifying gender-differentiated opportunities and constraints for improving nutrition during the design phase of a project often leads to better interventions. In line with this, and building on our ongoing experience, sensitization on gender, child protection and safeguarding concerns is included in any training and awareness raising activity, especially during the inception workshop. Other sensitization measures include the mandatory onboarding signature of the Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct by Plan International Nigeria staff and associates, and the gender responsive safeguarding risk assessments conducted prior to engagement to mitigate all concerns of risk of harm.
