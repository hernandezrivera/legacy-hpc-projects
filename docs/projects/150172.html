Needs

The cow milk production sector is in the West Bank, is one of the most important sectors that contribute to the food security, in addition to providing income to over 1000 Palestinian families. Palestinian food depends heavily on dairy products. In the West Bank there are approximately 27, 000 head of dairy cows, which produced annually the equivalent of 190 thousand tons of raw cow milk and the milk deficit is estimated at 35% of the total quantity of raw milk consumed. Over 1000 families in the West Bank work in cattle. It also estimated that 74% of these families that work in the cattle are small farmers who own 10 heads of cow or less. Between 2005 to 2014 more than 300 cow-mild producing farms closed in various parts of the West Bank, meaning that these families stopped being producers and became dependent on receiving assistance. The main challenges which forced the closure of these farms: • Poor management and lack of expertise • Low fertility in the herd, which led an annual reduction in the amount of milk produced by half, an estimated less than 5 tons per farm • High incidences of infectious diseases, especially diseases that cause high economic losses such as mastitis and pneumonia • High mortality amongst the newly born cow. Estimated around 25% • High production costs, particularly feed, water and veterinary treatments and the drop in the prices of milk • Use of feed protein and energy-poor feeding cows • Cattle pens which are unsuitable for cows because of space constraints • Using simple equipment and tools • The inability of farmers to market milk in a way which enables them return profit • Large agricultural companies competition for small farmers who own less than five cows and medium farm production that have less than 25 head of cattle • Milk suppliers rejecting milk because of manufacturing standards • Milk contains a high percentage of antibiotics that inhibit milk manufacturing operations to other products • The lack of prevention programs to protect herds of cattle diseases and reduce mortality in calves • Lack of clean drinking water for the cows in addition to high prices for water, especially in Central, South and East of the West. The targeted areas of Hebron, Nablus and Tubas are located in Area C and are among the most the marginalized rural areas that lack public services. In the same context, those areas are suffering from increased Israeli Settlers’ violence, not to mention that water and rangeland are controlled by the Israeli Forces who deny the populations’ access. Such measures cause significant impact on cow breeding in addition to drought which increased cow breeders’ reliance on purchasing high priced fodder which is reflected as well on abandoning cows’ possession. In the targeted Areas, around 170 thousand people require assistance in food insecurity. At the beginning of the year 2017 UAWC in cooperation with the directorates of Agriculture of the Palestinian Agriculture Ministry conducted a thorough needs assessment a survey many areas where cattle are raised in Hebron Salfeit Nablus Tubas & Qalqelia. The study examined on many issues that affect cattle including, production processes, manufacturing, marketing and production inputs (feed and water). A very large percentage of employment in cattle are women and children and more than 95% don't use milking facilitates. This causes many diseases related to the spine for farmers involved in milking, mostlywomen, because of sitting for long periods. The study showed that there are many administrative problems, for example it was found that 85% of cattle breeders don't use a detergent and laundry operations and sterilization sterile equipment for dairy maid and resulting in high numbers of bacteria-contaminated milk. The market access of individual and collective selling mechanisms in West Bank create constraints that contribute to reducing the output capacity in the livestock sector and to the current food security issues.

Activities

Union of agricultural work committees in partnership and coordination with the Palestinian Ministry of agriculture and its veterinary services, through its directorates will work to identify beneficiaries and determine ways to make sure project implementation to ensure the implementation of the proposed project and local project committees will be formulated in each localityProposed activities: 1. Rehabilitation of cattle pens and conditions for cattle . Improving cattle feed, water, milking and improving sanitary conditions for the cows will contribute to increase milk production. The project will provide farmers with important equipment and knowledge. The recipients will be selected in coordination with the Ministry of agriculture in different regions and verification through mobilizing applications and field visits. 2. Improve procedures for milk processing and marketing by providing farmers with equipment used in milk processing and collecting and moved from farm to home or factory, with the aim of improving the quality of dairy products manufacturer and verify the integrity of the manufacturing procedures and hygiene and improve the quality of these products which will help with marketing. 3. The distribution and provision of basic equipment and materials to prevent the spread of diseases. 4. Training and involve the training of women in cattle on the following "create inventory of colostrum, mastitis test training early, cervical inflammation test training and training on pregnancy and nutrition methods training Installation of feed and train on municipal milk cheese manufacturing beef, will be among the training activities. 5. Distribute drought resistant seeds, which help provide farmers suitable source of feed for the cows and contributes to reducing the cost of feed. Prior to any project implementation, the organization will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities.Final output: • 400 cattle pens will be rehabilitated the cows who possess them Through qualification (racks and stripes and farm water supply source "plastic tank capacity 2 cups plus qualifying demands calves nurseries to receive modern birth and provide it with means of heating such as bulbs Electric heating) (20% female owned)• 500 female cattle breeders or cattle breeders families will improve the quality of its cheese manufacturing through simple cheese manufacturing equipment distribution (which include "custom jar for transporting milk, filter cloth for milk and cheese, plus 20-litre plastic containers for keeping cheeses and facilitate Marketing and transportation after manufacturing " (25% female)• Improve the health status of 1000 cows through early testing for mastitis and through the distribution of "iodine for sterilizing dairy after dairy maid, providing examine material milk on the farm" California "in addition to distributing paws hands to deal with the cows under sanitary conditions and avoid the transmission of diseases from And to the animal or human. (25% female farmers)• Cultivation of forage crops, 25000 dunum, 300 tons "local barley and vetch" to provide feed for cattle breeders fillers contribute to lowering the cost of feed.

Indicators

Monitoring and evaluation: A baseline survey will be conducted at initiation of the project, which will be updated and compared to a final evaluation. Information collected through semi structured interviews, household visits and field observations. On-going monitoring will allow UAWC to evaluate methodologies and impact of the activities, to identify challenges and best practices; information will be gathered and analyzed on monthly basis. Particular attention will be paid to beneficiary selection, methods of working with local communities, village councils and protections committees. UAWC will ensure proper mechanism of asset distribution, gender sensitivities, as well as the appropriateness of the intervention in each target area. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation; the results communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through ongoing active beneficiary feedback mechanism in UAWC.
