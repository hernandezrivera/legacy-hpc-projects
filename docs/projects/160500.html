Project Summary

This project aims at addressing the needs of the most vulnerable elderly persons, IDPs, people with disabilities and other conflict-affected population in Luhanska and Donetska oblasts (GCA) in terms of water, sanitation and hygiene. The intervention is strictly in line with the goals defined in the WASH monitoring framework 2019-2020, i.e.
- Ensuring immediate and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision, for conflict-affected people;
- Provision of critical WASH-related supplies and information for the prevention of water- and sanitation-related diseases,
This will be achieved through the following activities:
- construction and/or rehabilitation of existing/new water sources e.g. boreholes and water towers;
- repairs of hot water infrastructure (buildings or community level);
- rehabilitation of toilets in institutions (schools, kindergartens and collective centres);
- installation bins for solid waste disposal;
- transportation of solid waste from landfills to utilization points;
- emptying cesspits in private households;
- installation of water treatment systems in social institutions and households;
- water quality testing.
It will enable to cover the needs of up to 95 200 conflict-affected people who will be benefiting from the intervention including elderly persons - 21 000, IDPs - 14 000 and people with disabilities - 5 700. For that reason, it is planning to attract approx. USD 1,67 M of funding.

1 - Needs

After more than five years of an armed conflict in Ukraine, the local population still suffers from its repercussions. Problems related to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) are one of the most acute. According to the latest 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview, approximately 3.2 M people need assistance in terms of WASH in the conflict affected areas, both in Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (GCA and NGCA). Conditioned by the ongoing conflict, critical public water infrastructure located close to the line of contact (LoC) is put at risk of being affected by the armed activities. In an area where 4.6 million people rely on centralized water systems, there were 89 WASH incidents when shelling or other issues directly affected water infrastructure in 2018. Waste disposal and sewage systems Towns and villages near the 'contact line' report problems with garbage, as reduced income for village councils means they can no longer afford to pay to remove it. A similar situation applies to sewage systems in more rural areas close to the ‘contact line’, where people have to empty the septic tanks for their own household. This is more difficult, as pump trucks refuse to visit these highly volatile areas, or charge an increased price for the risk incurred. Old sewage networks and wastewater treatment facilities are also failing, which may lead to water contamination. Within the 5-km range of the 'contact line', some 56 per cent of people have a toilet connected to a sewer network. However, 18 per cent have a septic tank and 32 per cent have an outside toilet, rising to 69 per cent in rural areas.According to the data shared by Education cluster, there are some 45 per cent out of around 3,500 schools, kindergartens and vocational training colleges in the two conflict-affected oblasts, also have substandard latrines, or an inadequate water supply.In relation to the ongoing conflict, the socio-economic situation has also significantly deteriorated in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. Communities and institutions located in the in the region are excessively underfunded from the government’s side. In addition, low level of the incomes of the population do not allow to provide themselves with all the necessary means of personal hygiene, which is especially relevant to families with more than three children.Water pipe systemsThe situation remains critical for major water infrastructure such as pipelines, pumps, and treatment plants. Being established in 1950s-1970s water pipelines and its facilities undergo persistent dilapidation given the critical underfunding and lack of capacity from government of Ukraine. Water companies, such as the “Voda Donbasu” and “Popasnyanskyi Vodakanal” continued to struggle with debt in spite of the Government of Ukraine’s debt release (1.8 bln UAH) of Voda Donbasu at the end of 2017. If water supply, sanitation and hygiene needs are not improved, risks directly impacting people’s health could increase, especially the risk of diarrhea and other waterborne diseases, which disproportionately aff ect very young children and the elderly. Women are more exposed to and are impacted by water shortages than men. In Ukraine, protection issues arise when people are on the move: and lack of water, dysfunctional urban heating and high utility prices represent strong push factors, encouraging displacement.
