Project Summary

The objective of the proposed project is to address the urgent winterisation needs of vulnerable households and improve service delivery through rehabilitation of social institutions and health care facilities in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in both government and non-government-controlled areas.
The project includes both winterisation Non Food Items (NFI) kits and door to door coal distribution to vulnerable families and social institutions with inpatient capacities. Coal continues to be the primary winterisation support requested from the de-facto authorities to humanitarian partners operating in the NGCA. IOM intends to match the amount of support provided to affected communities in winter 2019/2020.
. The coal to be provided to 12, 000 vulnerable households will amount to 3 tons/household in Donetsk and 2.5 tons/household in Lugansk, in line with the amounts distributed by the respective de facto authorities. The amount of coal is estimated to support the targeted households through the most severe months of winter to meet their heating needs, though IOM’s post-distribution monitoring reports have revealed across the years that these set amounts might not be sufficient for all vulnerable families.
Winterisation kits will also supplement the needs of households and in patients at social institutions (to be provided to 3,400 vulnerable households and 95 institutions). Winterisation kits will be comprised of household and personal items that will be determined through needs assessments conducted at the household and institutional level. Post-distribution monitoring reports have highlighted the preference for personal items such as hats, shawls and warm socks as well as other household items including blankets and warm linens. Additionally, in response to frequently reiterated requests for winterization assistance by communities located along the contact line in the government-controlled side of Donetsk Oblasts IOM will distribute much needed electric heaters to 250 households, which are expected to reduce the household heating expenditure and allow family members to use the savings to address other critical needs (food, medicines, clothing, etc.). The inability of households to manage their winter needs as been consistently highlighted across many of IOM’s project interventions during monitoring visits.
Finally, IOM will continue with the rehabilitation of 78 social institutions and health care facilities for winterisation purposes, which is an activity that IOM has long standing experience with. Due to lack of funds in regional and de facto administrations, social institutions suffer from lack of maintenance and infrastructural rehabilitation/improvement, which leaves them unfit to meet adequate indoor temperatures to ensure the comfort and health of residents and patients. The envisaged rehabilitation works will focus on both external structural improvements – such as, amongst the others, roofing, installation of doors and windows to maintain indoor heat – and internal rehabilitations, which are most likely to include the upgrading of heating systems to ensure both water and indoor climate are sufficient to meet heating needs.
All proposed activities are in line with cluster priorities for HRP 2021 on winterisation.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2021), the deteriorated economic situation in Ukraine (lower than predicted national GDP in 2020, increased unemployment rate from 8.7% in 2019 to 10% in the second quarter of 2021), coupled with the consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic and the deriving risk mitigation measures enforced (impact on livelihood due to closure or reduction of the business activities) affect the living standards of the population, who strives to meet their basic needs. The situation in the NGCA is further aggravated by movement restrictions across the line of contact (by over 90% compared to 2020) and the extremely limited inflow of essential goods. As the elderly (65+) comprise approximately 60% of the people crossing the line of contact (LoC), COVID-19 pandemic-related movement restrictions have particularly impacted this group as they have lost significant income in the form of GCA pensions and other social benefits. As estimated by Shelter/NFI cluster, approximately 160,000 people need winterization assistance. Those communities most in need reside in isolated settlements within locations 0-20 km from the contact line in both NGCA and GCA. Social institutions that mange both inpatient and outpatient needs are also highly in need due to consistent underfunding and particularly with the additional strain the COVID-19 pandemic has placed on funding allocation. The need to support social institutions was consistently raised by both health and social policy regional line ministry representatives during discussions with IOM in Lugansk and Donetsk GCA. To supplement limited financial support, social institutions are reportedly often required to rely on the inpatient benefits to procure necessary winterization and other care items to meet their needs. IOM Post distribution monitoring (PDM) of 2019/2020 winter coal distribution found that 95 percent of respondents had indicated the essential need for assistance from humanitarian organizations in the form of coal to help meet their winterization needs. The cost of three tons of coal (average amount needed per household to cover winterization needs during the coldest months of the winter) is on average RUB 10,000 (around USD 140), which is significantly higher than their monthly social payments or pensions which rarely exceed RUB 3,500 (around USD 50). In addition, IOM’s post-distribution monitoring revealed that 67% of beneficiaries highlighted the critical need for warm clothing items and blankets to help them manage winter temperatures. Reportedly, even with the coal provided, they are often unable to raise indoor temperatures above 20-22C due to insufficient heating. This was also voiced repeatedly to IOM by social institutions with inpatients as they equally felt unable to meet the personal needs of their residents owing to inadequate funding to buy and replace essential winter items. In times of heightened risk due to the COVID-19 pandemic, the combination of winterization items, rehabilitations and solid fuel provision will help reduce the likelihood and/or intensity of morbidity among at risk groups. Field monitoring visits in 2020 showed constant needs in winterization rehabilitations in social institutions and healthcare facilities in NGCA. This is mostly due too many years of neglect, lack of maintenance and current unavailability of resources to be allocated for their rehabilitation. This was also confirmed by the results of most recent post-distribution monitoring conducted in February-March 2020. Lastly, with the continued threat posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, the need for adequate temperature in social institutions and healthcare facilities becomes crucial both for curbing the spread of the virus and for contributing to treatment of the patients.
