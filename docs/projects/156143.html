Project Summary

The main objective of the project is improving the management quality and accountability of IDP sites and strengthening household and communal coping mechanisms in IDP sites and developing exit strategies.
The project will also contribute to improving the Camp management quality, accountability, and communal coping mechanisms of IDPs targeted camps through enhancing camp management capacity of the 4 targeted camps and that will be by provision of site management support and trainings.
Also providing communal self-protection by enhancing communal capacity and provision of fire safety measures (fire extinguishers- first aid kits).
In addition that one of the main objectives of the project will be through deliver livelihood assistance in the forms of In-Kind grants, business development services, vocational trainings and build community capacity to IDPS in the targeted camps in Aleppo Governorate, Azaz District.
Thus, The main goal of this project is to Enhancing IDPs resilience in Camps by providing communal self-protection and camp-exit related activities by provision of livelihood assistance and community cohesion supporting projects to IDPs/ refugees and by providing fire extinguishers and First aid kits, Also this project advocate and call on community participation in these camps (Azaz RC, Bab al Nour, Tal Jubeen and Ekdeh) Camps -where Maram already implementing projects there- by providing awareness about IDPs’ role in decision-making to encourage and engaging IDPs in their choices.
Accordingly, this project will seek to empowering IDPs, youth and women in attending the skills development training/ vocational training.
As well as, specific vulnerable groups (elderly, disabled, single headed households, etc.) benefit from resilience-building and livelihood support.
Where IDPs camp committees will be established, with providing training to these committees on various topics.
Also, awareness raising sessions will be conducting for IDPs on Safety and Security. In addition to distribution of fire extinguishers and first aid kit in the targeted camps.
Thus, Establishing participatory committees is a part of the exit strategy. Moreover, exit stratigy related activities will take place and vocational and/or business management training will be conducted with support around (400) beneficiaries by micro and small businesses.
In total, this project will directly benefit about (19,680) IDPs who will all benefit from the project activities that are planned to be established in these camps (Azaz RC, Bab al Nour,Tal Jubeen Camp and Ekdeh Camp).
- Azaz RC (360 M, 600 W, 400 B, 640 G) 2000 IDPs/ 400 HHs.
- Bab AlNour Camp (1482 M,1740 W, 2265 B, 2058 G) 7545 IDPs/ 1556 HHs.
- Tal Jubeen Camp (2007 M, 2715 W, 1523 B, 2205 G) 8450 IDPs/ 1063 HHs.
- Ekdeh Camp (514 M,469 W, 337 B, 365 G) 1685 IDPs/ 390 HHs.
As a result, (40) IDPs living in these targeted sites will participate with participatory management committees (including active participation by women), also (400) IDPs will be benefiting from resilience projects, In-kind grants (800$ per person), In addition that (19.680) IDPs will get the self-run emergency response capabilities (including first aid and fire response)
The project is tailored to meet priorities of CCCM CLUSTER 2019 HRP RESULTS FRAMEWORK also contributes to HRP 2019 Strategical objective of increasing resilience and access to services and livelihood opportunities to IDPs living in camps.

Needs

By referring to CCCM SECTOR INPUTS 2019 HNO, Of the estimated 6.2 million internally displaced persons in Syria, an estimated 871,150, or 14 per cent of all IDPs, are living in IDP sites where they receive humanitarian assistance and services. During this time period, an estimated 18% of IDPs resorted to, or transited through, IDP sites. As some of key messages by CCCM SECTOR INPUTS 2019 HNO, and against the backdrop of overcrowding, there is limited absorption capacity in IDP sites. Also the absence of accountable camp management and maintenance of facilities in IDP sites, particularly for informal/self-established IDP sites, are deeply concerning and contribute to alarming reports of uncovered needs in these sites. More than 13.5 million people continue to require humanitarian assistance, all reports from different sides, HNO, INGOs, and NGOs mentioned that over 400,000 people have been displacement to the north of Syria. Aleppo governorates have been received large numbers of these waves of displacement, that a huge part of this displacement has been moved to Euphrates shield, settlement in Aleppo and Azaz city. Protection Risk Analysis

Maram uses Gender mainstreaming through programming. This includes having both male and female staff to make all participants comfortable to contribute where this project will strive to ensure that the safety and dignity of beneficiaries are prioritized. The equality and impartiality of access to the assistance will be ensured through the vulnerability assessment, with priority given to most vulnerable groups and people with special needs requiring urgent assistance. As GBV mainly affects girls and women, the project will focus on these groups and create a space where women and girl feel safe and can develop both professionally and personally by providing some trainings, and strengthen safety and security for those at risk of GBV through the implementation of infrastructure improvements and the development of GBV-related policies, also ensure adequate monitoring and evaluation of GBV-related programming. The project program will seek to: • Involve women and other at-risk groups as staff and leaders in site governance mechanisms and community decision-making structures throughout the entire lifecycle of the camp (with due caution in situations where this poses a potential security risk or increases the risk of GBV). • Strive for 50 % representation of females within the project. Provide them with training as well as targeted support. • Ensure women (and where appropriate, adolescent girls) are actively involved in the committees and management groups, as necessary, engage in dialogue with males to ensure their support. • Support women, adolescent girls and other at-risk groups in identifying and speaking out about factors that may increase the risk of GBV in sites (e.g. factors related to site management; security; shelter; availability of and access to resources such as food, fuel, water, and sanitation; referral services; etc.). Link with GBV specialists to ensure that this is done in a safe and ethical manner. To ensure meaningful access, all activities will promote and help protect and the rights of all IDPs, such as women and girls. Moreover, all beneficiaries will be sensitized on their right to equitable and safe assistance, and where and how to obtain it to avoid risks of sexual exploitation and abuse.Accountability measures will be established with a view to improving programming, assisting in understanding beneficiary and community perceptions, promoting beneficiaries empowerment and assisting in the early detection of problems such as targeting, misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse. Information provision and feedback mechanisms are designed to be accessible, effective, and confidential and respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can immediately be put in place.As for risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls, there will be,1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism2. Train staff on code of conduct3. Code of conduct in all staff contracts.Also, Address any SEA complaints through,1. Post monitoring/ client feedback forms are used2. Include PSEA topic in regular supervision sessions.In addition to analyze data and Monitoring staff. The project will ensure that work will be in line with humanitarian principles and follow the principles of Do No Harm.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1- Organizations Acceptance by beneficiaries, communities and other stakeholders. 2- Deterioration of safety and security context. 3- Exchange rates change significantly and therefore the budget is insufficient to reach the targeted number of beneficiaries 4- Local Markets unable to deliver goods and services as contracted. 5- MF unable to maintain access to the most vulnerable populations despite changes in conflict dynamics inside Syria.Risk 1: Organizations Acceptance by beneficiaries, communities and other stakeholders: CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: Maram Foundation will prepare a community outreach plan, will attending and conduct coordination meetings with target community members and the local authorities to explain project activities, targets, and purposes. LIKELIHOOD: Medium IMPACT: Medium Risk 2- Deterioration of safety and security context: CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: In general, the security situation will be acceptable because of the project located in the demilitarized zone, but any way Maram Foundation, in addition to the procedures that are always taken into consideration in all projects:  MF maintains a security risk management system for its areas of operation in order to adapt and respond to changes in context.  MF maintains a full stakeholder and power analysis for its areas of operation in order to better understand the context. MF on a daily basis reviews security information related to its areas of operation and monitors staff movement as per the current security conditions.  MF supports its staff with training and capacity building on security and risk management. LIKELIHOOD: Low IMPACT: High Risk 3: Exchange rates change significantly and therefore the budget is insufficient to reach the targeted number of beneficiaries CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE:  MF will maintain a regular dialogue with its donor in the event of a significant change.  MF conducts regular price monitoring, utilizing existing information where available.  Procuring and storing project materials at an early stage in the project to avoid the possibility of rising of items and supplies prices, in addition, Maram will contract with the contractor in the dollar to avoid the risk of changing currency exchange rates LIKELIHOOD: Medium IMPACT: Medium Risk 4: MF unable to maintain access to the most vulnerable populations despite changes in conflict dynamics inside Syria CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: MF has adopted a Whole-of-Syria structure in order to provide multiple entry-points to vulnerable affected populations and to enable a more flexible response. Risk 6: The camp committee’s structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities.
