Project Summary

The project aims to ensure access for all children and women including of the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities to lifesaving and preventive nutrition services, the comprehensive quality CMAM services, disseminate and support optimal IYCF-E practices and promote health and nutrition seeking and basic hygienic behaviors within the population in need in northwest Syria, Besides, the project is going to provide harmonized emergency community-based services for all children and women of the population in need by applying integrated health, nutrition, child protection, and WaSH approaches.
This project will target five subdistricts Afrin, Atareb, Jandairis, Salqin, and Maaret Tamsrin, the total number of targeted population is 219000 (IDPs 143000, Host communities 75000, Returnees 1000), the number of targeted children under five years is 42500 and PLWGs is 22800.
the scope of services include:
3 fixed CMAM center within the health facilities:
1. Al-Hakeem PHC center level 3 in Afrin includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions)
2. Al-Hakeem PHC center level 2 in Abin Saman in Atareb subdistrict includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions)
3. Save the SoulHospital for child and maternity care in Salqin includes (SC inpatient, OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion, and micronutrient interventions) ,
And through 5 Rapid Response teams that will be dispatched to the areas of high IDP flow in Atareb, Salqin, Afrin, Jandiris, and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts to provide field nutrition, child protection, health, and hygiene promoting services to children under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women with including the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities, The RRTs will screen, provide emergency treatment, if needed, registers and refers all identified cases (CMAM and IYCF-E, IMNCI-E and BEmONC) to the respective fixed healthcare and PSS facilities with the special referral slip for tracking and immediate service in the final place of IDP destination.
This project will provide the appropriate nutrition services to all age and sex groups; boys and girls, women, men, with attention to people with special needs, without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and host communities, Whilst ensuring that inclusion is mainstreamed throughout these activities to avoid exclusion of the most vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities.

4 - Needs

Continued hostilities in the north-west with large scale displacement have resulted in populations experiencing poor nutrition conditions in 2019. Further, people with disabilities are disproportionately impacted by the ongoing conflict, the escalation of hostilities from 1 December 2019 to 31 January 2020 resulted in the displacement of nearly 520, 000 people mainly from southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates, 80% of displaced are women and children. Many of the people displaced have been displaced several times, intensifying existing vulnerabilities. CCCM 2/2020.Approximately 1,179,869 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and 3,443,752 children under 59 months of age need life-saving nutrition services. Chronic malnutrition is at its highest in Syria, with almost 500,000 children suffering from stunting of physical and cognitive development.Almost 137,000 children under the age of 5 years in Syria are acutely malnourished. These children face life-threatening risks and require immediate treatment. Malnourished children with complications have increased twofold in some sub-districts and more stabilization centers are needed.Almost one in three pregnant women is anemic while maternal malnutrition has increased five-fold compared to last year, leading to poor intrauterine growth, high risks during pregnancy, and childbirth complications.This project will target five subdistricts, the severity ranking of these subdistricts between 4 to 5, the total number of population in the targeted location 742008 including 178338 people in need (48735 PLWs and 129603 CU5), the disabled children 6 - 59 Months are 19440, and the number of new IDPs arrived at these subdistricts as following: Afrin 49,211, Atareb 27,290, Jandairis 14,698, Salqin 22,349, and Maaret Tamsrin 59,496, according to the last update of CCCM 02/02/2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

SDI will implement the project as following SDI will operate 3 fixed CMAM center within the health facilities:1. Al-Hakeem PHC center level 3 in Afrin includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions) 2. Al-Hakeem PHC center level 2 in Abin Saman in Atareb subdistrict includes (OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion and micronutrient interventions) 3. Save the SoulHospital for child and maternity care in Salqin includes (SC inpatient, OTP, TSFP, IYCF-E promotion, and micronutrient interventions),At the fixed HFs, SDI will screen all children under 5 years and PLW who visit the clinics for nutrition services or other services (RH, or IMCI clinics, etc), select beneficiaries through MUAC screening and/ or Weight for Height/ Weight for Length (Z-score), provide SAM treatment for children with uncomplicated SAM using Plumpy nut and MAM treatment for children and PLW with MAM using Plumpy sup, treating complicated SAM according to inpatient management protocol in (SC), Provide essential WASH services in CMAM sites, Provide micronutrient supplementation to pregnant women and children, Provide one-on-one counseling on WHO-recommended (IYCF-E) and maternal nutrition practices, disseminate IYCF- E messages.In the fixed center, the nutrition staff is one doctor, one nutritionist, and one nurse, also 4 CHWs for outreach activities. Also, 5 RRTs that will be dispatched to Atareb, Salqin, Afrin, Jandiris, and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts to provide field nutrition, child protection, health, and hygiene promoting services, The RRTs will screen, provide emergency treatment, if needed, registers and refers all identified cases (CMAM and IYCF-E, IMNCI-E and BEmONC) to the respective fixed healthcare and PSS facilities with the special referral slip for tracking and immediate service in the final place of IDP destination.each RRTs include 1 Nutritionist, 2 CHWs,1 Nurse, 1 Midwife, and 1 data Entry.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Some women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as divorced women and widows, may not be able to access basic services. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation measure: more female CHWs will be allocated to provide services at the community level and RRTs will access all beneficiaries especially new IDPs to services and refer cases to receive appropriate and specialized care, in this project female staff is about 85%, that increases access of women and girls to health services. Risk related to GBV on CHWs, and RRTs Staff when visiting the homes, and IDPs sites, also risks of GBV on beneficiaries and health and nutrition staff in health facilities Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High, Mitigation measure: each two CHWs will work together, RRTs have female and male staff, training of health staff on providing first-line support, identification of GBV cases, safe referrals, and ensuring access of the affected population to the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health, Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers of women and girls, Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High, Mitigation measure: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, including victim assistance, train staff on the code of conduct, including the code of conduct of all staff contracts and address any SEA complaints, SDI is a member of PSEA network. Risk of discrimination or if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Likelihood: Low, Impact: High Mitigation measure: the project design encompasses accessibility of services by vulnerable groups (physical accessibility, disaggregated areas for women and girls, availability of female staff, Signboards) with effective community-based complaints mechanisms.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: Regular and timely provision of specialized nutritious foods and available to targeted beneficiaries. Risk1: Access to targeted areas becomes hindered or insecure. Mitigation measure1: Stock prepositioning in multiple, safe, and close locations to delivery sites and secure enough supplies to avoid stop the treatment of patients Assumption2: Technical skills are available. Risk2: shortage in the specialized technical staff in the targeted areas. Mitigation measure2: More non-health and health workers will be trained to provide appropriate services.Assumption3: No further deterioration of the security situation. Risk3: Attacks on health facilities and health workers during moving between camps, communities, and houses. Mitigation measure3: SDI will set up CMAM centers in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries. Moving the center to another safe area to avoid the risk of bombing.Assumption 4:All children and women especially those who may have physical limitations are able to reach services.Risk:Traditional habits prevent women from leave homes, physical disabilities prevent some people to access services Mitigation:SDI will bring the services close to communities and camps, to including all people in need with focusing on children, women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as women who have experienced divorce, early/forced married and widowhood, who may not be able to access basic services including nutrition.
