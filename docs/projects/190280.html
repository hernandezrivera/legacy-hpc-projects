Project Summary

Exisitng Funded Proejct-
Inadequate access to essential medicines and basic services. With limited food, clean water and proper shelter, incidences of malaria, cholera, measles, and COVID-19 are expected to rise
Health care services in Amhara are require support to resume basic health services to the affected communities, Currently, most health facilities are functional, and minimal services are provided. However some health facilities requires, restocking and support with MHNT In addition, health extension programs have no adequate supplies to resume their community-based health services.
The project will provide technical support to the woreda health office to establish a regular active/outreach acute malnutrition screening, establish well-functioning treatment center, provide MHNT support – OPD consultation, messaging on MHPSS, COVID-19 and communicable diseases, the team will also support outbreak investigation and response.
The project will screen children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and support IYCF teaching aids and on job training to the health workers on management of acute malnutrition and IYCF activities. The project will ensure equitable promotive, preventive, and curative health and nutrition services are provided to the community and IDPs, through providing a clear guidance /direction to the zonal and woreda health offices. 84,635 affected communities will receive health services out of which 6,624 are IDPs.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

• CRS and it's implementing partner are both part of the Caritas Internationalis network and share common values and approaches as well as a long history of working together to support local communities in times of crisis across Ethiopia. • CRS works with local partners to identify and assess needs and collaboratively work on project design. CRS and its local partners will be responsible for the direct implementation of the intervention. CRS Ethiopia’s Emergency Project Officer and Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) Project Officers will lead the monitoring and evaluation of the project. CRS' robust MEAL Unit will spearheads all monitoring activities using the emergency contextualized Simple Measurement of Indicators for Learning and Evidence-based Reporting (SMILER) M&E system. The project will develop brief tools for data collection, implement participatory data planning, monitoring systems, and organize meetings with community members that involve both women and the youth (male and female), elders, people with disabilities, people with special needs, and other relevant social community groups to solicit issues and comments that will be considered in the ongoing project implementation. • CRS accompanies each partner for an agreed number of days per month aligned with critical programming activities to provide technical support and to monitor activities. CRS provides support and oversight during partner-led registration/validation and distribution events. CRS staff lead the following activities in close collaboration with partners: gender and protection assessment, market assessment, capacity building on GBV/PSEA, accountability and staff technical skill, setup of compliant and feedback mechanism, beneficiary targeting, all post distribution monitoring activities, engaging with the national shelter clusters and working groups as appropriate, reporting financial and narrative report to donors are key activities. The MEAL Project Officers assess the progress and implementation of each activity, in-line to meet set indicators and Sphere standards. CRS has robust financial, management, and logistics processes in place to ensure the highest quality programming from our partners through routine partner assessments.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

• CRS and its partner are committed to saving lives, protecting livelihoods, and enhancing resilience to shocks of the most vulnerable households in conflict-affected communities. Target communities are primarily valued stakeholders at every stage of the project cycle. Accountability to project participants and communities is one of the core values in CRS. CRS and its implementing partner will highly involve target project participants in conducting the feasibility study, implementation, and in monitoring and evaluation activities. Regular meetings with community members that involve men, women, boys, girls, and people with disabilities will be organized in coordination local Government at the woreda level. Depending on the preference of project participants, CRS will design and apply Community Feedback and Response system to facilitate two-way communication. • Emphasis on Women and Children. Project activity designs and project plans will be discussed in focus group discussions and results will be incorporated into project implementation plans and activity design. FGDs will be conducted with women and men in separate sessions. • To further reinforce accountability, promote social cohesion and mitigate conflict sensitivities, CRS and partners will also ensure that targeting of project participants to be in consultation with the local community representatives in which the most vulnerable segments of the society are fairly included in all activities. Project activity/distribution areas will be selected based on the feedback/comments from the local communities to ensure no negative effects to people accessing sites. Verification of beneficiary lists with the local communities will also be done. This will be assessed by conducting post distribution monitoring after the distributions are made. In order to avoid/minimize the possible exploitation (abuse of power) of the affected communities with the power imbalance dynamics there will also be posters developed with respect to individual and/or group entitlements along with expected staff conduct. • There will also be community sensitization on staff code of conduct and program participants protection aspects to prevent happening of possible violations and improve community awareness on how to interact with CRS and partners staff during project implementation. In addition to these, community level feedback, complaints, and response mechanisms to be established after basic trainings are provided to selected members of the committees on how to handle feedbacks/complaints in a safe, dignified, and confidential manner and on how to report those feedbacks/complaints to respective bodies. This community level feedback system will include help/feedback desk in distribution sites
