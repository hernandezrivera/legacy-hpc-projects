Project Summary

The project is to concentrate on building coherence between host and refugee communities in camps 24, 25 and 27 and one Union i.e., Hnila of Teknaf upazila of Cox’s Bazar district. Social cohesion will be strengthened through the introduction of dialogue platforms for conflict resolution, through improved access to protection services and increased knowledge about protection problems, as well as through increased safety in the immediate surroundings of the community members.
The project is designed to serve 4453 direct beneficiaries (3278 RRs and 1175 HCs) consisting of: (Community Protection Facilitators (CPFs) and supervisors (63), Community Protection Group (CPG) members (3360), Dialogue Forum members (600), Adolescence club members (150) and Risk and Vulnerability (280). Apart from this, 20000 indirect beneficiaries will be benefitted as a spillover effect. Among the project participants 50% will be women and in Disability and Inclusion sector standard will be followed.
Sustainable outcomes will be achieved through the participatory nature of the project, especially facilitated through the Community-based Monitoring Committees. Community members and local authorities are involved in the design of the new structures (dialog fora, CPGs, Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteer - RVMV), which will increase their ownership to maintain these after the project end and they will at the same time be capacitated to do so. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), environmental and Covid-19 aspects and protection concerns are considered because volunteers will be specifically trained to identify such risks together with their communities and to develop plans in a participatory manner to overcome the identified risks and vulnerabilities. Furthermore, the RVMVs are trained to become first responders in case of an emergency and are equipped with tools accordingly.
All core activities (around the CPGs, Dialog fora, and Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteers) will be implemented in a participatory manner where group members can decide about the contents discussed and the actions to be taken. In addition, two so-called Community-based Monitoring Committees one for RRs for respective authorities; one for Host Communities (HCs) and respective authorities which are formed they will not monitor in a technical sense, but will meet four times per year to assess the project process and to propose adaptations where needed. Representatives from all beneficiary groups within the project will be chosen in a participatory manner to sit in the Monitoring Committees.
Project will form Community Protection Groups (CPGs) with 50% women and girls’ members and other forum and platforms will also ensure women participation. At all level project will rightly inform through sessions about women and girls rights, pessimistic effect of early marriage, GBV and SEA. It is expected that CPGs with direct support of CPFs and other project team members will develop stronghold on protecting GBV and SEA. Moreover, they will rightly educate to use Complain Response Mechanism (CRM) system either of this project or any other organization.
The project will build communities capacities and strengthen community engagement to ensure accessibility to life-saving information and knowledge on rights and available resources to communities. The project will develop need-based actionable two way culturally appropriate communication resources and will strengthen dissemination and access to information. The project further strengthens participation of affected population through effective community engagement and functional feedback and complaint response mechanism.
The project has been quite comprehensively engaged with the Sector. On monthly basis the project will keep continue submitting 5W report, Sitrep and effectively attending in meetings. The guidance and suggestions from the Sector will be incorporated in project plan and will ensure its implementation

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to the J-MSNA 2021, 56% of household informed that they needed Protection services and 34% of households reported that needs of children in their community were not adequately met. 14% of households mentioned that members in their community wanted to access protection services but were not able to. Furthermore, according to the ISCG Social Cohesion Bulletin (June 2019), the attitude of Host Community members towards Rohingya is characterized by 59% of respondents reporting no interaction with them at all and 45% stating they were unhappy or very unhappy with their presence. Only 15% of Host Community members were stating they were happy with the Rohingya presence. The bulletin furthermore indicates that only 25% of the Rohingya Refugees and 20% of the Host community feel that Rohingya are welcome by the Host Community. Only 18% of Rohingya and 15% of the Host Community population feel there is harmony between Bangladeshi and Rohingya communities living in that area, whereas 73% of Rohingya and 34% Host Community members would like the opportunity to meet each other. According to J-MSNA 2020, 16% of households reported having witnessed tensions between Rohingya and host communities. As per J-MSNA-2020, 68% conflicts occurred over waste in the common places and 48% due to increasing crimes in those communities. The tensions caused by security concern accounts for 44%, awhile environmental degradation is a cause for 39% conflicts. Prices hike of regular commodities and declining moral standard have been reported to be causes of tension for respectively 16% and 14% of cases. Furthermore, other reasons are contributing to conflicts between the two communities, like access to government services, higher transport costs and access to grazing land.Increased social cohesion and strengthened conflict resolution are essential to assure the safety and well-being of both Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities. In order to achieve this, both communities have to enter into dialogue with each other. However, conflict and tensions within the two groups must also be resolved before a dialogue with the other group becomes possible. Furthermore, the RRs and HCs need to have equal access to protection services and need support in reducing the (environmental) risks surrounding their homes. Next to providing live-saving assistance, the Joint Response Plan 2020 also calls for promoting peaceful co-existence between the Host and Rohingya Communities, facilitating equal access to quality services for the two communities, as well as to strengthen public service infrastructure and delivery through system and capacity strengthening, which is where this project will be rooted. Furthermore, it is problematic that RRs and HCs don’t have a common platform where conflicts between the groups can be addressed. HC can turn towards the Union Parishads and RRs can address the CICs, but qualitative structured dialogue between the two groups is not given any space. Moreover, local government representatives find it difficult to resolve those conflicts because of lack of resources and lack of awareness among the communities. Protection is also an issue among Host Community Households and according to J-MSNA 2021, 52% of households having reported community members needing or accessing protection services reported barriers. Furthermore, according to innovations for Poverty Action, 46% of Host Community Members are aware of gender-based violence in their community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project has been implementing since early 2021 till the period of November 2023. The project has adopted approach to strengthen working relations with administration, camp management and other actors etc. working in the camp and host community areas. Moreover, the activities have been designed contributing to each other activities in terms of capacity development, sustainability and achieving targets. To attain the project long term goal i.e., strengthening “Peaceful Coexistence” or Social Cohesion among the host and Rohingyas communities the design of activities has been done such a way that they are integrated with communities as such towards end of the project Forums, Clubs and community groups which are the main interventions will function altogether i.e., host and Rohingya communities will function jointly and will move towards Peaceful Coexistence. The design of the project is to strengthen community capacities of different types of community groups and sustain its functions for attaining Peaceful Coexistence and Protection Services. The groups are capacitated as such they themselves under this project will coordinate and keep continue functioning and sustain the approach. Moreover, they also will coordinate with other service providers to attain protection services and continue. In Spite of this, project will search and approach to other donors to attain funds for this concept and also will work closely with other actors functioning in the same localities to support the community groups for their capacity strengthening as such they can functions independently.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Project has appointed Community Protection Facilitators (CPFs) who resides in the localities where the target households/beneficiaries reside. These CPFs have been capacitated through training, coaching and mentoring to know the protection related needs of the beneficiaries. They are also being interlinked with the protection services providing institutions as such they are well acquainted with the institutions and well known about the process of referrals. There are numbers of CPF Supervisors to properly monitor the referral systems and provide necessary support and skills to better support the beneficiaries and respond to protection needs. However, always put focus that no way there should be any adverse effect of the interventions. Moreover, ensures participations in all stages of the beneficiaries while designing the mainstreaming concept in project interventions.

3.2 - Gender

Mainstreaming Gender in all activities and at all stages of project interventions is designed in detail project proposal as this is a continuous project of three years for implementation. Community Institutions building are implementation methodology of this project. These institutions are Community Protection Groups (CPGs), Forums, Youth Clubs and Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteer Groups (RVMV). At Least 50% adult female and youth girls must be the members of these institutions. Through the process women leadership development is undertaken and have ensured the participation of women in decision making process and take active part in project implementation. Women and girls have been prioritizing while recruited local level and voluntary level staffs.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

While selecting project beneficiaries there is open to include all beneficiaries within the age of Girls under 18, Boys under18, Women 18-59yrs, Male 18-59yrs, elderly women 60+yrs and elderly men 60+yrs. So, all ages of beneficiaries are targeted to support with protection services. The supports have been planned and given according to age needs. Appealing and implementing both partner organizations have clear-cut Child Safeguarding policy and it is mandatory to follow the child safeguarding policy at all stages of the activities implementation. While implementation of the project it is clearly said that there is no adverse effect on children’s health, choices, their proper grooming, no way to abuse either physically, mentally, economically etc. All possible steps have been taken to support the proper grooming of children

3.4 - Diversity

The project has selected beneficiaries across from the communities and of all ages, gender including disabilities. More focus has been given to address the need of disable people and provide support and services according to their access and availability. They are being linked with relevant organizations working in same communities as such they attain proper focus for their needs.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project has formed two Community Based Monitoring Committees (CBMCs) one with host communities and another with Rohingya Refugees to monitor the progress and accordingly to report. The members of the committees consist of direct beneficiary groups, teachers, religious leaders, representatives of local government, of camp management (site management of CiC offices). They monitor activities and share their findings on regular basis to the field team. The committees are independent for monitoring the activities which have been implanting in their communities. Project has detail activities and implementation strategy with the CBMCs for their capacity building in terms of independent monitoring. Moreover, the committees also meet quarterly with project team including senior management of the project and share their findings and accordingly next course of actions are being set. 3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project has Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) elements. Project has formed a significant number of Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteers groups. These groups consist of members from all sections of the communities as such they can properly identify climatic risk and vulnerabilities of the populations and the areas. Accordingly prepare action plan. To address the identified Risk and Vulnerabilities the project will coordinate with other service providers, government’s relevant departments for capacity building of the target groups, resources mobilization towards reducing the identified risk and vulnerabilities. The activities will also closely monitor the progress and will further integrate required resources mobilizing from different internal and external sources.
