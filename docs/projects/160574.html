Project Summary

The aim of this humanitarian response plan will be to increased access to quality integrated lifesaving healthcare services and strengthen the capacities of primary health care system to reduce excess morbidity/mortality of common diseases and also improved the health status of 25,000 vulnerable IDPs/host populations in Pibor and Pochalla in Jonglei; Yirol East, Yirol West and Wulu in Lakes; Kapoeta East in Eastern Equatoria, Jur River in Western Bahr-al-Ghazal and Terekeka in Central Equatoria States.
LiveWell’s overall Health HRP plan budget for 2020 plan is at US$ 1,500,000, aligned to the health cluster’s strategies and the cost per beneficiary. This budget will aim at supporting the following 20 health facilities based on the following level of 4 clinical packages.
• 10 Mobile Clinics in Pibor, Kapoeta East, Yirol West, Wulu, Terekeka and Jur River
• 7 PHCUs in Jur River, Terekeka, Pibor, Yirol East, Yirol West and Wulu
• 2 PHCCs in Pibor and Pochalla
• 1 CH in Pochalla

1 - Needs

• Access to food security is a major challenge in several parts of Central, Western and Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei & Western Bhar el Ghazal States and it has caused humanitarian needs to worsen over the second quarter of 2019. This is largely attributed to ongoing conflict and impassibility of roads network by humanitarian actors (REACH, April-June 2019), hence increased cases of malnutrition in USR children. • Inadequate provision of essential healthcare services due to inadequate funding led to inadequate access to improved services such as RH and MPSS services. Development partners like World Bank support 27% of the existing health facilities in Jonglei State, 63% of Health Facilities are non-functional due to lack of funding (IRNA report, 2016/2017), resulting into increased morbidity/mortality of common diseases. • IPC report (July, 2019), 10,000 people in Cueibet in Lakes State are likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), Jonglei State populations estimated to be food insecure and 73% of its population facing crisis (IPC 3). All the proposed counties in Lakes are classified in Emergency phase (IPC 4); Awerial, Cueibet, Rumbek North, Yirol East, Yirol West etc. This is caused by a lean season, delayed rainfall, persistence inflation, displacement, assets depletion and destruction of livelihood as a result of ongoing conflicts in WES, CES, EES, Jonglei, WBS, and Lakes. • Of the 22 SMART surveys conducted between September 2018 and January 2019, eight of them showed GAM rates above the 15% WHO emergency threshold. However, five counties in Jonglei (Ayod, Pibor, Pochalla, and Twic East), Kapoeta East in Eastern Equatoria and Jur River in Western Bahr-al-Ghazal State show Critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM 15.0%-29.9%) (IPC Reports 2019). • LiveWell’s PHSA assessment (March, 2019) reported that inadequate WASH services, GAM rate of 19.9%, EPI coverage, inadequate provision of health, nutrition and WASH services are major causes of measles outbreaks in greater Pibor (IDSR, Week 8 of 2019), therefore, HC supported IOM/LiveWell, and MEDAIR/LiveWell to conduct measles vaccination campaign in 5 payams in March and September, 2019. Inadequate provision of primary health care and mental health services to be specific (9%) as a result of inaccessibility, insecurity, attack on health etc. Flooding Assessment in Pibor (Oct, 2019), 13,000 host populations are displaced as a result of flooding, and health services are severely disrupted. • Increased risks of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) in borders of Yei & Magwi, and measles outbreaks since the beginning of the year have been reported in 15 counties including Pibor, Yirol East, Jur River and including Juba and Wau. 308 suspected measles cases in Jur River have been reported of which 4 tested positive for measles. It was recommended that WHO and humanitarian partners should carry out a reactive campaign in the areas (WHO EVD bulletin & IDSR report of weeks 36 & 37, September, 2019). • Low EPI coverage in Pibor, Jur River, Yirol East, Yirol West (EWARS Week 34, 2019), coupled with flooding in major counties in Jonglei State is anticipated to likely precipitate the outbreaks, malaria outbreaks, acute watery diarrhea (AWDs) and resultant severe acute malnutrition (flooding effect on health report in Pibor, Oct 2019) in under 5 year children and other vulnerable group like disabled, and pregnant women. • LiveWell is therefore requesting your approved in this proposed HRP plan for the year 2020 to set-up the following clinical packages to provide the quality integrated essential health care services:a) Ten (10) Mobile Team (MTs) packages in Pibor, Kapoeta East, Awerial, Yirol West, Wulu, Terekeka and Jur River counties.b) Seven (7) PHCUs in Yirol East, Yirol West, and Piborc) Two (2) PHCCs in Pochalla and Pibor counties.d) One (1) County Hospital in Pochalla.

2 - Activities

SO1: REDUCE EXCESS MORBIDITY AND MORTALITY OF EPIDEMIC PRONE DISEASES AND HEALTH INSECURITIES 1. Implement integrated response for epidemic prone communicable diseases including diarrheal, vector, RVF with WASH/Nut/FSL in 3 Mobiles and 2 PHCUs in Yirol West and Jur River.2. Implement outbreak investigation and response for epidemic prone diseases in 2 Mobile, 1 PHCC and 5 PHCUs in Yirol East and Pibor.3. Implement essential primary healthcare in 2 Mobile, and 5 PHCU to provide lifesaving services targeting the most vulnerable displaced populations in Pibor and Yirol East. SO2: IMPROVE ACCESS AND SCALE-UP RESPONSE TO INTEGRATED QUALITY ESSENTIAL HEALTH CARE SERVICES TO VULNERABLE POPULATIONS4. Scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications in 5 Mobiles in Terekeka, Kapoeta East and Awerial.5. Scale up all Integrated priority activities IDSR/EWARS, PHSA, and disability tools to inform and determine the feasibility of responses 2 Mobile in Wulu, and 1 CH & 1 PHCC in Pochalla. SO3: INCREASE ACCESS TO SERVICES FOR SURVIVORS OF SGBV, DISABLED AND MENTAL HEALTH DISORDERS6. Expand essential primary healthcare emergency services to include reproductive health services (including CMR/SGBV) and MHPSS in 3 Mobiles and 2 PHCUs in Yirol West and Jur River.7. Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial support in 5 Mobiles in Terekeka, Kapoeta East and Awerial.
