Project Summary

This project is divided into 2 parts : the first where the project will respond to health care gaps in rural Dar'a by rehabilitating and equipping primary healthcare facilities, as well as providing basic healthcare services and training professional healthcare staff. This is a two-pronged approach making healthcare more accessible as well as of a higher standard.
Dorcas will repair and equip 3 PHCs in Daraa (2 PHCs level B/ 1 PHC level C). The Ministry of Health were consulted in this process and have provided an indicative list of needs and works to make the facilities safe, sanitary, and functioning once again. As part of the re-equipping process, clinic staff will be trained by suppliers on how to use and maintain the equipment. Dorcas will also engage in training of PHC staff. Based on MoH requirements, training programs will be standardized as much as possible while also being tailored to each HF’s needs. Training will focus on relevant topics linked to key service gaps in the current health system such as RH/GBV and integrated management of childhood illness (IMNCI) and any other health problem that is relevant. Once the facility is up and running, and staff are fully trained, Dorcas will ensure that patients are seen, treated and referred, and receive proper follow-up. Dorcas will also engage its MHPSS team to work specifically on the issue of mental health, providing mental healthcare services to the population in need within the catchment area of the facility.
This project falls in line with Dorcas’ ongoing work in South Syria, where a current projects endeavors to attain the same objectives within underserved areas. The project will draw on Dorcas’ vast experience in the sector, region, and lessons learned from previous projects. This project will also keeping in line with Dorcas’ approach of using social work methodology through much of its programming, and ensuring that gender and protection are not only mainstreamed, but also centralized in project planning design, implementation, and evaluation.
The 2d part that will provide support to PHCs in Aleppo Governorate Dorcas aims to give continuity and expand the health services to support the affected population and to respond to the essential and basic needs of the population by the provision of health services in 5 health centers; Support exists out of medicine and equipment supplies.
In addition to Dorcas and it is partner experience in operating a mobile clinic , Dorcas jointly with it is partner will operate a fully equipped mobile clinic in Idleb Governorate that provides consultations, medicines. Protection will also be mainstreamed through early detections and referrals pathways in all interventions.

3 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, 14.6 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarianassistance - an increase of 1.2 million compared to 2021 - with needs increasingly beingexacerbated by economic decline. The decade-long crisis has inflicted immense sufferingon the civilian population who have experienced massive violations of human rights law.For the past 3 years , this has been coupled with a global pandemic the COVID-19that has amplified the needs, in addition recently a cholera outbreak has been declared on 10 September 2022. Vital infrastructure has been destroyed, resulting in a lackprimary and secondary health facilities. The quality of health care has been furtheraffected by the deterioration in the functionality of medical equipment due to the lack ofspare parts and maintenance shortages of drugs and medical supplies. One of the most pressing concerns is the lack of technical staff, the healthcare workers and trainings. In October & December 2021 , Dorcas conducted MSNA in Aleppo and a rapid assessment in both Aleppo and Idleb and found that healthcare is one of their top 3 needs along with the food. In Aleppo, it is apparent that there is a main gap in availability of medicine, regular availability of painkillers (18%) and antibiotics 18%, 15% of respondents have expressed the lack of availability of drugs to treat chronic diseases. For Idleb, 76% of respondents tothis rapid assessment revealed that they faced difficulties when trying to access health

4 - Implementation of activities

In Daraa, Dorcas will repair 3 PHCs. The MoH has provided an indicative list ofrehabilitation requirements and works to make the facilities safe and sanitary. Repairsinclude: rehabilitation of doors/windows in facilities; roof waterproofing; repair of walls,floors, roofs, repairing fencing; painting and re-wiring electrical connections; removingand demolishing works for damaged materials and existing debris inside and outside theHF. Other possible works include WASH improvements and protective measures such aspartitioning rooms or establish privacy for services such as breastfeeding counseling andensuring secure comprehensive access for people living with disability. Dorcas will workclosely with the Health Sector and will provide oversight to engineering firm to ensure thatrehabilitation standards comply with WASH and protection standards. Before any workcan take place, Dorcas will first need to get permission to work within the area andconduct the activities. In order to repair the centers, Dorcas will follow a multi-stepprocess: signing contracts with engineering firm, signing contract supervising engineer,assessment of PHC, completion of BoQ, selection of contractor.. Provision of healthequipment and supplies and personal protective equipment. As for Aleppo, Dorcas willprovide medicine and medical equipment to PHCs as well for the mobile clinic in Idleb inaddition to capacity building, trainings and monitoring of the health staff in all areas.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

While planning and carrying out activities, special attention will be paid to ensure thatgender, age and diversity are mainstreamed throughout the project. Dorcas maintainsa gender- and conflict-sensitive perspective in designing activities, and will identifybeneficiaries in a participatory approach through: consultations with beneficiaries,internal discussions and capacity building of staff. Cases referred will also be checked. Dorcas will also ensure that healthcare services provided at PHCs, including mentalhealth services, are gender and age appropriate. This means that specific health careservices will be provided for mothers and children. All Dorcas staff will be trained onthe internal Code of Conduct and asked to sign it and be compliant to it in addition tothe CP and PSEA policies. Beneficiary data will be collected and segregated by age,sex and gender whenever possible. Male and female protection staff will be involvedthroughout the project cycle to ensure that urgent protection issues are addressed andto provide beneficiaries the opportunity to raise any concerns. Dorcas has a strongexperience in delivering protection programs and its team is well trained on CaseManagement SOPs and minimum standards. Dorcas' interventions will be guided bythe non-discrimination principle to ensure direct access to services for the mostvulnerable groups that will be identified through rigorous assessment. Internalmechanisms to report misconduct will be established
