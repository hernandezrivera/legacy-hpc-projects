Project Summary

LEAP is informed by proven, evidence-based approaches, programmatic lessons and consultations with stakeholders, including girls, boys, women, men, community leaders, partners and government actors. Though the project duration is 30-month and projected budget is $6.8 million for whole working areas but under the provision of JRP, the project will cover 6 locations including two camps in partnership with PHD and the projected budget is $1.62 million for proposed JRP period (an estimated 10% in camps). The project will employ a rights-based health services , gender-transformative (GT) and three-pronged strategy to: i) improve the individual and collective agency and resilience of adolescent girls and young women to exercise their SRH and protection rights (1100); ii) strengthen health systems to provide sex- and age-responsive quality health and protection services (1200); and iii) enhance the capacity of local women's rights organizations (WROs), including girl-led organizations (GLOs), and youth-led organizations (YLOs) on evidence-based advocacy for GARI health and protection services while advocating for long-term change for evidence-based GARI SRH, SGBV and MHPSS services and programming (1300). Improved, integrated SRHR outcomes for girls have shown to contribute to better lifelong outcomes in education, protection, livelihoods and overall wellbeing (Brookings, 2016). LEAP’s multi-sectoral and feminist approach works across health and protection sectors to strengthen horizontal linkages while integrating women and girl's agency throughout and engaging WROs/GLOs and YLOs as influencers to improve accountability for GARI SRH and SGBV services and policies.
Aligned with national and local humanitarian, health, protection and GE policies and strategies, as well as the SDGs and Canada’s Feminist International Assistance Policy (FIAP), LEAP will contribute to poverty reduction by targeting marginalized groups in Rohingya camps and host communities facing multifaceted dimensions of exclusion. In partnership with three local implementing partners and one key technical partner, LEAP will directly reach more than 79,000 people, including 49,873 females, in four Unions in Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts, as well as two Rohingya refugee camps (7,000).

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is one of 20 districts in Bangladesh lagging the national average for development indicators, with approximately 33% of the population of 2.29 million (2011) living below the poverty line compared to the national average of 31.5% (Care, 2018; World Bank, 2016). The influx of Rohingya refugees since 2017 has brought new and compounding challenges to the already weak local health and protection systems (UNHCR, Policy Brief, June 2020.) Approximately 949,000 people live in host communities (49.6% women and girls) and 860,000 Rohingya refugees live in camps (51.7% women and girls), most of whom face deteriorating situations in SRHR and protection (UNOCHA, 2020 COVID-19 Response Plan – Addendum to the JRP: Rohingya Humanitarian Crisis, Apr-Dec 2020). Families, especially women and girls, are exposed to threats from criminal groups, trafficking, violence, child abuse and neglect and child, early and forced marriage (CEFM). The incidence of CEFM has increased during the pandemic due to the disruption of education and economic activity and rising food prices. There are still gaps in the outreach of safe and quality GBV services and referral mechanisms (SAFERWORLD, June 2021). Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services are non-existent. Poor mental health can be associated with risky sexual behaviour and substance abuse, resulting in negative impacts on the SRHR of adolescent girls and young women, including heightened vulnerability to unintended pregnancy, unsafe abortion, sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and SGBV.Harmful social gender and patriarchal norms, attitudes and behaviours present in both host community and camp settings disempower women and girls. Unequal power relations, compounded by the impacts of COVID-19, confine women and girls with the burden of unpaid care work, increase their risk of intimate partner violence (IPV), hinder their access to and control over resources, exclude their participation in family and community decision-making and limit their mobility, which makes them vulnerable to GBV and restricts their SRHR (SAFERWORLD, June 2021). The contraceptive prevalence rate is only 34% compared to 62% at the national level (Family Planning 2020, BDHS 2014) due to widespread, deep-rooted stigma, misconception and societal expectations of women and girls. This is especially true for adolescent girls who face unique barriers in accessing GARI SRH and SGBV services due to lack of awareness of their rights and unresponsiveness of services. In Teknaf, 82% of adolescents felt that health providers did not address their issues (Cox’s Bazar CPSS, REACH, Child Protection (CP) Assessment, May 2020). To respond to these interrelated barriers, Plan International's proposed LEAP (Lifting Healthy, Empowered and Protected Girls and Women) project will advance the realization of SRHR of the most vulnerable adolescent girls and young women in all their diversity in refugee and host communities. LEAP will be implemented in partnership with four local partner NGOs for whole working areas but there will be two partner NGOs : 1) Samaj Kallyan O Unnayan Sangstha (SKUS) and 2) HOPE Foundation within the scope of JRP. This ultimate outcome is aligned with the 2020 Joint Response Plan (JRP), emphasizing building the capacity of both Rohingya refugee and host communities in Cox’s Bazar District. Specifically, pillar 3 of the protection framework for the humanitarian response, guiding the 2020 JRP, focuses on fostering the wellbeing of host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas and refugee camps to promote alternatives to negative coping mechanisms and promote peaceful co-existence.
