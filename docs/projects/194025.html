Project Summary

This project is in line with the Food Security and Livelihoods, Education, and Gender Base Violence Sectors’ Strategic Objectives (SO1 & SO 2), part of the revised HRP 2023, which was done mainly to respond to the current conflict in Sudan(15 April 2023) Targeting the most vulnerable Internal Displace Persons and hosts communities in Kassala state mainly in Reifi Kassala, Rei¬ Shamal Ad Delta, and Rei¬ Khashm Elgirba. 1 - Needs

In less than 40 days over 1 million people have been displaced due to clashes between the Rapid Support Forces (RSF)—a paramilitary force—and the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) continue to result in civilian casualties and generate humanitarian needs, particularly in and around Sudan’s capital city of Khartoum and in Darfur and Kordofan regions. The ongoing clashes have caused significant displacement and humanitarian needs for IDPs, who reportedly were displaced to different regions across Sudan, including the Bule Nile and Kassala states. according to the last update Displacement Tracking Matrix /DTM Sudan /IOM 23 May 2023, All IDPs within Kassala have reportedly arrived from Khartoum state and totaled 4,220. While Blue Nile totaled 1,245 The latest HRP estimates that more than 1,4 million people in the Kassala states are in need of humanitarian assistance. And the HRP targeted 1.1M for Blue Nile PIN is 732 and the targeted is 653. This project is designed to be implemented in BN & Kassala states, in 5 localities; Three in Kassala and Two in BN States. The main interventions are under; FSL, Education, and GBV sectors. Before the current crisis, 16.2 million people were food insecure across Sudan according to the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Assessment, which represented 34 percent of the population. The ongoing clashes in several parts of the country are further exacerbating food security needs. The Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster plan to reach 15 million people including those affected by the conflict, the newly displaced, and the most food insecure vulnerable population including IDPs, and residents with life-saving food and emergency agricultural assistance. The latest acute food insecurity data indicates that around 19.9 million people across Sudan were highly food insecure. GBV: In Sudan, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) is a key concern within the different communities affected by different emergencies i.e., conflicts, tribal clashes, floods, drought as well as disease outbreaks. Women and girls among the IDP communities and refugees are especially at risk of GBV. Survivors of GBV lack adequate access to quality specialized lifesaving services, the majority of Sudan’s localities lack specialized GBV services. According to the VOICES from Sudan assessment, the primary results showed the huge need for quality GBV services and more engagement of communities including men and boys in GBV risk mitigation and response. In Kassala state, the types of GBV are; sexual violence (21%), forced marriage (19%), FGM (18%), domestic violence (15%), economic violence including denial of education (15%), and psychological violence (7%). In BN state, the GBV forms are; marriage (23%), domestic violence (20%), sexual violence (20%), economic violence including denial of education (19%), psychological violence(13%), and FGM (5%). In each targeted locality, GBV-specialized services are not available. To address all the above issues this project will contribute to the provision of GBV services at the community level along with awareness-raising activities. Education: The current conflict has negatively impacted the education of affected girls, boys, and adolescents, including children with disabilities who face challenges in accessing inclusive quality education in a safe and protective learning environment. And according to revised HRP and education sector response priorities there is a need to support the most vulnerable and marginalized girls and boys at risk of exclusion from education is a priority for the displaced children and vulnerable host communities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, JASMAR will abide by Do No Harm principles. JASMAR will also strive for gender balance in recruiting staff at all levels and will train officers responsible for delivering services and inputs to participants on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches. JASMARs’ in-house gender focal point will provide a solid foundation for the program. Recognizing the critical role of women in agriculture and household nutrition, a minimum of 40 percent of participants will be women, specifically under FSL interventions. Limited access to education services forces school-age children to walk long distances to reach schools. Lack of GBV services often exposes women & girls to additional protection risks; GBV interventions will help to reduce these risks through provision of the essential infrastructure, which will offer community-based GBV services at the community level. In addition, JASMAR will ensure that women and girls are represented in the community-level committees – a minimum of 50 percent of committee members.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

JASMAR places the issue of accountability at the core of this project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation processes. Ensuring relevant, quality, and timely programming and interventions reflect this commitment. JASMAR considers the target communities as leading the solutions for the humanitarian issues they face. Thus, they are regarded as key stakeholders in the project's design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. JASMAR will work through existing community structures comprising of a representative of women, youth and community leaders to represent communities during project implementation. The project will use participatory, gender- and age-sensitive monitoring approaches to ensure girls, boys, women, and men, as well as CBOs and services providers, are involved centrally in the process of project follow-up, provide their feedback and concerns on the project performance and are informed about decisions made in community meetings and discussions. JASMAR will provide timely, relevant, and precise information about the organization, including feedback and complaints mechanisms. Communities and beneficiaries will be kept up to date on changes in feedback mechanisms and will be provided with information on how they can feedback remotely, such as via hotline, WhatsApp etc. Information sharing will be via different formats to ensure information is available promptly while taking into account language barriers and literacy status – such as flyers and posters left in communities, health facilities, hospitals etc., with simple and clear messages/standards and accountability assigned a number to all project locations. JASMAR’s functional Complaints and Response Mechanism will collect feedback where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, and a call-in through staff phone numbers – any meetings held (where still possible) will be in the locations. Complaints/feedback will be captured, categorized, and shared with relevant staff for resolution and logged into the action plan trackers. Depending on the type of feedback and complaint, some will immediately be closed through sharing the required information (different methods depending on the scale of the response – face-to-face, telephone etc.). At the same time, for some, an investigation will be undertaken to verify the facts before a concrete decision is made.
