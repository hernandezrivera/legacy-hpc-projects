Project Summary

In general humanitarian situation in Somalia remains grave, due to the country having endured decades of conflicts, insecurity, drought, seasonal floods, famine and various disease outbreaks coupled with recent Corona Virus Pandemic and desert locust infestation.
According to HNO 2022 a total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. With 2.9 million people estimated to be internally displaced throughout the country, Somalia has one of the highest numbers of IDPs in the world. Of these, 2.2 million require urgent humanitarian assistance while a total of 5.5 million vulnerable non-displaced people are projected to need humanitarian assistance in 2022.
The population of Somalia consistently experience some of the worst health outcome indicators in the world, Somalia is among the countries with the highest rankings in infant and child mortality rates of, respectively, 76.6 and 121.5 per 1,000 live births while the maternal mortality rate is 692 deaths per 100,000 live births, 79 per cent of all births are home delivered without skilled assistance.
Excess mortality in Somalia is driven primary by malnutrition, disease outbreaks (e.g., COVID-19, AWD/cholera, measles, malaria) and violence; as well as non-communicable disease including complications of pregnancy and chronic disease.
To avoid situation from worsening and improve current trends CISP seeks to build on its previous experience in Health specific and sensitive programming to step up provision of lifesaving health care services. CISP will ensure continuity in delivery of essential health care services focusing on maternal neonatal and child health services integrated with COVID-19 emergency response initiatives such as minimizing morbidity and mortality secondary to COVID-19 through capacity Building of health care workers and preventing and addressing the impact of human consequences of the outbreak by ensuring continuation of essential life-saving preventive and curative services at Health facilities where CISP operates.
CISP will also put in place measures to limit human-to-human transmission and protect individuals from exposure to COVID-19 In line with current COVID-19 pandemic response,
CISP will promote application of standard precautions such as respiratory and hand hygiene measures in in the health facilities in target locations as well as implementing additional precautions including contact and droplet precautions and airborne precautions for aerosol generating procedures for any cases where COVID-19 infection is suspected with special focus on congested areas such IDP camps. CISP will ensure there is adequate ventilation in all areas in the healthcare facility and that cleaning and disinfection procedures are followed consistently and correctly.
To promote inclusion of persons living with disabilities (PLWD) in accessing and utilization of health care services the project will ensure that medical clinics have wheel chairs, ramps and other assisted devices so that people with disabilities do not face unfortunate experiences of being left for hours without being attended to because of lack of a chair or access to a washroom because washrooms do not cater for their specific needs.
The project will also promote understanding of health care workers on special needs of persons living with disabilities so that people with disabilities can access essential health services without any discrimination.
The project targets a total of 55,490 under 5 children (28,300 girls, 27,190 boys), 63,814 WCBA and 11,099 men to address health needs by providing outpatient consultation in 6 MCHs, in patient care services in 2 referral Health Centres, providing integrated management of childhood illnesses, immunization, maternal health services (ANC, PNC, FP and skilled birth attendance), screening of children 6-59 months and PLW for acute malnutrition, referral and health education.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The target population faces continuous displacements due to armed conflict between SNA/AMISOM on one side and the Militant group on the other. The target population also faces forceful recruitment of young boys to the militia of the militant groups, although some local communities fought back and resisted they could not sustain and were ultimately defeated like what happened in some areas in Mudug region. As per the current situation the only way to mitigate is for families to send the youth to government controlled districts and for long term solutions is to liberate those areas from the iron grip of the militants. The risk involved lack of gender equity and social inclusion: CISP will mitigate exclusion and inequality by ensuring equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion and ensure they reach their full potential, both in terms of its economy and its level of governance.Social Inclusion will be promoted through: •When hiring incentive staff equal opportunities will be given to all irrespective of their social background, gender and disability. • Equal representation in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees (CDC). • Training Health staff on do no harm. The proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health and drought whose vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening drought, water scarcity and displacement into IDP settlements where children live in extremely precarious conditions with only limited community support. As well as assigning equal opportunities, tasks and responsibilities to both men and women. Community Based workers selection and trainings will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity is addressed.CISP will prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing any unintended negative effects of its intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and Psycho-social risks. CISP will ensure people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services like people with disabilities and the elderly.
