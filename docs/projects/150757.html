Project Summary

The project intends to ensure access to basic primary and life-saving health care services for 43,200 Vulnerable IDPs and Host Communities including 12,500 Girls, 12,500 Boys, 14,200 Women and 4000
affected in Daynile, Bondhere, Dharkenley District IDPs/drought affected In Mogadishu and Afgooye Corridor for Health centers of Lower Shabelle region. SOYDA through this project will provide life-saving medical consultations and drugs to the health facility, establish referral mechanisms between SOYDA facilities and secondary healthcare providers in Banadir region, improve the coverage of measles vaccinations and Vitamin A; and support safe motherhood and reproductive health while also ensuring readiness to prevent and respond to outbreaks such as malaria, measles or Acute Watery Diarrhea AWD/Cholera and promote health update through health education and beneficiary sensitization as well as linking this with the nutrition program in order to enable provide a holistic services within the organization target site.

Needs

Despite improvements in the first half of 2018, major humanitarian needs in Somalia persist, particularly among IDP populations, due to residual impacts of drought, ongoing displacement, conflict and marginalization. 5.4 million people are still in need of humanitarian assistance throughout Somalia, including 2.8 million children; although this number may decrease in the second half of the year following the impact of above average Gu rains and sustained humanitarian interventions. Malnutrition rates across Somalia remain among the worst in the world. In total, about 1.2 million children under-5 are projected to be acutely malnourished in 2018 including, 232,000 expected to be suffering from severe acute malnutrition. According to FAO-FSNAU, an estimated 294,000 children are acutely malnourished, of which nearly 55,000 are severely malnourished. The surveys show that the overall level of acute malnutrition is serious (median GAM of 14 per cent), which is an improvement from critical compared to the 2017 post-Gu assessment (median GAM of 17.4 per cent). Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence is critical (more than 4 per cent) only among Mogadishu IDPs. Of major concern is the increasing crude death rate (CDR) of over one per cent in three out of 33 population groups surveyed, as well as the under-five mortality rate (U5MR). The situation is predicted to worsen between August and October due to the limited availability of public health and nutrition services. Disease outbreaks such as AWD/cholera and measles continue to represent a major threat to children with 6,979 suspected measles cases (72 per cent being children under-5) and 5,704 suspected cases of AWD/cholera with 39 deaths reported in 2018. The project will address all above issues and contribute the reduction of morbidity and mortality of communicable diseases in our target areas.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project beneficiary may face different level of risk based on the contentious displacement in most target areas such risk include, cultural and religious barriers that might led discouragement from using health services such as immunization,vtamin A supplementation among other health product that is necessary for immediate treatment, confidential or privacy problem, poor health facility design that would not give confidence for the male and female as well as disable person separation coupled with lack of information documentation among the most vulnerable population specific needs in the target location.SOYDA organization shall however intends to mitigate such risk through improving the community information sharing on the health needs and services as well as improving the design, confidentiality/privacy and coordination mechanism in working with all actors as well improving the distance and time needed for immediate access to basic health service by creating mobile teams that will provide services closer to these disable and marginalized communities an both rural and urban IDPs set up in Mogadishu and Afgooye.SOYDA shall ensure to provide effective community mobilization and sensitization to avoid any child or mother to missed most important vaccination/immunization ,it shall also improve community engagement to review the challenges and barriers of health services on both local leaders religious leaders, women group and youth for better health services uptake.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In implementing the health service provision in highly risk and unpredictable areas there might be different factors that might led the risk of exclusion based on age and gender in circumstance such exclusion may include sexual exploitation among the female health employee, exclusion on job opportunities duty discrimination against minorities, physically challenge person, poor representation among the affected people, poor feedback mechanism as well as lack of code of conduct, monitoring and reporting channel, such risk factors might hinder the actual program implementation purpose thus led it to ineffectiveness in reaching the affected people.To mitigate this SOYDA organization intends to established clear feedback mechanism for complaints during such instance on sexual exploitation as well as poor health staff conduct that calls for unprofessional behaviors in doing so SOYDA we provide the project beneficiaries with hotline/call center that shall be responsible by a compliant officers who is going to directly report to executive director for immediate action, it shall also create reporting and monitoring system that shall ensure properly documented project information as well as ensure all heath staff signed code of conduct that will be used against any such eventuality.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Due to the porous security situation in most part of the country Somalia, there might be many conflict related risk towards the beneficiary such are military occupation of the health facilities, untargeted stray bullet to beneficiaries, cross fire violence that might erupt between the militia group and the government forces as well as sexual abuse among the vulnerable girls and women in the IDPs set up hence this might directly inflict more harm to the beneficiaries in the target location To mitigate this SOYDA shall ensure the risk of beneficiaries are minimized during the displacement by putting health facilities far from military bases to enable avoid any stray bullet and cross fire that could have otherwise danger the already displaced beneficiaries, it shall also ensure to provide psychosocial support to its project beneficiaries to cater for the need of the sexual gender based violence (SGBV) as well as establish coherence reporting and coordination pathways that will enable the beneficiary to seek immediate life-saving and life-sustaining intervention.
