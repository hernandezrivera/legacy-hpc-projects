Project Summary

This project targeting South Sudan refugees in South Kordofan state and Chadian refugees in Central Darfur state. The proposed Activities targeting children under 5 and pregnant lactating women and communities Health intervention will include PHC services (treatment of childhood illness, antenatal care, assisted deliveries, vaccination, provision of essential drugs and supplies, emergency preparedness and response in addition to building capacity of health staff in service delivery and monitoring implementations. Nutrition includes treatment of Acute Malnutrition and preventive services of IYCF, provision of nutrition supplies, training of volunteers, case identification and follow up, strengthen the community awareness of causes, danger signs and treatment of acute malnutrition, promoting positive health and nutrition behavior change. improve hygiene promotion and provide hand washing facilities at health facilities in addition to improving medical waste management and infection prevention. SC integrated services delivery based on need and is inclusive and non-discriminatory. SC will ensure assistance is available to those who are most. The services provided will be respectful of cultural and religious backgrounds and will consider environment aspects. Whenever possible, health staff composition will reflect the gender, ethnic, and linguistic balance of the beneficiary population. Enforced policies prevent and respond to any breach of SC's Child Safeguarding Policy. under protection the project will provide appropriate psychosocial support for children and protection awareness sessions and will distribute NFIs for affected victims.

Needs

The protracted conflict and economic crisis in Upper Nile and parts of Unity states in South Sudan has disabled people from growing food, leading to the disruption of their livelihoods and increasing the occurrence of displacement. This has caused an unprecedented and continued influx of South Sudanese refugees into South and West Kordofan. According to UNHCR (March 2019) population update New refugee arrivals from South Sudan continue to cross into Sudan. Over 6,200 refugees have arrived in Sudan in 2019 so far, arriving from Aweil area in Northern Bahr Ghazal State and Upper Nile State, citing violence, food insecurity and a lack of livelihoods as their reasons for leaving South Sudan. In total, 37,644 refugees were reported in South kordofan (Feb 2019). The S3M survey results for 2013 showed that the Global Wasting based on MUAC was 10.2% for Ellirri locality and the National MUAC screening campaign, carried out by the MoH in August 2016, indicated 8.5% of children were found to be with MAM. As a result of civil wars along boarders between Chad and Sudan there was exchange of refugees since 2004. These wars have been politicalized by war investors, the thing which has upgraded from simple conflict over natural resources, water, grazing land, farming land migration routes to ethnical and through to politics. Ultimately this violence caused the influx of more than 250,000 Sudanese refugees and the further displacement of 180,000 Chadians into the eastern region. However, the Chadian refugees who has been relocated from western Sudan to a bit deeper, they faced similar challenges in surrounding their camp in Um-Shalaya, insecurity, poverty, lack of farming lands, tensions among Sudanese ethnic groups from time to another and lack of basic services, health, education, protection, water in addition to suffering the consequences of 12 years’ war.

Activities

The project will respond to the basic needs of refugees and host communities while increasing their self-reliance and improving their health and nutrition status and resilience. Activities: •Primary health care services (consultation, essential medicine, vaccination, growth monitoring, health education, antenatal care, postnatal care, assisted delivery, family planning and referral for emergency obstetric to secondary level services in addition to strengthening surveillance system and building capacity of health care workers).• Provision of lifesaving nutrition service for acute malnourished children (boys and girls 6-59 months of age) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) among highly vulnerable communities. Focusing on CMAM approach and promote improved infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E). •Increase community access and knowledge to use safe water sanitation and hygiene promotion services throughout the Health facilities and at community level. •Improve national capacity of the partners to ensure effective, efficient and coherent multi-sectoral response in emergency. •Will address the gender and age specific concerns as a cross-cutting approach throughout the implementation, capacity building and resilience mechanisms SC will provide training for staff and partners on scope of gender analysis, gender aspects and how women, girls, boys, and men are affected differently in different situations. Awareness sessions for the targeted communities to improve their understanding on gender aspects and increase the capacity to prevent abuse. SC will support partner in advocating for collecting Gender disaggregated data. - Children have access to appropriate psychosocial support through Child friendly spaces activities and providing training in children’s clubs, Specialized services for severely affected children, increase awareness on sensitization for prevention of recruitment and facilitation for reintegration.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project , and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities , prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy frame work. The plan mitigation will through effectiveness of existing disposal mechanism as preventive measure for disease caused be insufficient or unsafe water supplies, poor drainage and uncontrolled diseases , Plan the location of emergency waste disposal sites with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odors. Healthcare and other forms of hazardous waste will be disposed of using appropriate methods including Sachet and cartons of RUTF/RUSF, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent spread of communicable diseases. Water source will be project focused to determine sustainable levels of water use, an early assessment of the presence, quality, and quantity and recharge rate of groundwater sources will be done. Take care to locate latrines downstream of wells, at least 30m from groundwater sources and at least 1.5m above the water table as sanitation measures. Carefully will consider location of shelter and new building to minimize pollution and damage to sensitive natural environment. Appropriate storage of food supplies to Protect health of workers and food consumer (e.g. tablets used for fumigation) and food provision will have appropriate soilid waste management plan. Availability of Standards tools and guidelines, Standards, tools and guidance documents will be available to assist humanitarian responders in managing environmental impacts and risks.
