Project Summary

This project aims to provide access to quality education to affected children and youth in the most affected states in South Sudan. The crisis has forced many children out of school for lengthy periods resulting in significant learning loss and classrooms of varied capacities and ages. The rate of children out of school in South Sudan is estimated around 70% (UNESCO, May 2018). 26% of functional schools in South Sudan have been affected by attacks on education during the year with the most commonly reported incidences being theft and looting by armed forces/groups (SSEC Report, 2017). INTERSOS currently implements an Education sector across 5 states, supporting over 80 schools in diverse form. In view of optimising this presence, integrating additional components within the school setting is a vital strategy for 2019. Schools are community aggregators, bringing unity across populations through the shared common interest and value given to education. Bolstering the system and the project can allow for enhanced protective environments for children and communities alike. The proposed project will complement the ongoing INTERSOS education responses by providing capacity building for teachers, PTAs, and education personal to improve their capacities in carrying out duties; establishing learning structures (Temporary Learning Space) with gender-segregated WASH facilities (where required); integration of nutrition screenings; to provide protective and safe learning environment through strengthening the protection capacity of teachers, provision of school materials to enhance and promote teaching and learning instructions, and support youth to promote their social well-being, co-existence, and education. The project aims to train and incentivize 200 teachers on education in emergency pedagogy, 400 school PTA on school management and education engagements, 50 education personnel on education administrative and managerial aspects to improve their technical support, support 4000 youth on psycho social support, and provide quality education to 15000 school aged children, 50% girls in affected states including Central Eqautoria, Western Eqautoria, Upper Nile, Jonglie, and Unity. The supported schools will in addition have school hygiene clubs where they receive training and inkind support. Further, teachers, school PTAs, and school officers will receive school WASH training. While the project response will be based on the cluster strategy and priorities, INTERSOS will conduct rapid education assessment to further understand education situations and will plan and response accordingly. Strengthening the school environment will also be cited and touched upon by INTERSOS’ protection proposals.

Needs

South Sudan still has one of the highest rates of illiteracy in the world and the number of illiterate people above 15 years is more than 70% of the population (UNESCO; Sept 2017). The conflict has disrupted the education of thousands of school aged children. According to the 2017 Education Cluster Assessment, 26% of functional schools have been affected by attacks on education during the year with the most commonly reported incidences being theft and looting by armed forces/groups. The crisis has forced many children out of school for lengthy periods resulting in significant learning loss and classrooms of varied capacities and ages. At the same time, children, particularly boys, have been subjected to forceful recruitment by armed actors, while girls have been subjected to early and forced marriage. While the peace process is underway at a central level, and the road to recovery is still quite long, affected states in which INTERSOS operates (particularly Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei) continue to experience high thresholds of violence against the civilian population, whose lives perish in deprivation and danger, displacement due to aggravating factors such as food insecurity and predicted famine. The repeated and ongoing armed clashes, with killings especially among youth, displacement, brought to starvation, dropped out of school, forcibly recruited into armed groups, sexually abused and labor-exploited. Civilian infrastructures mirror the level of destruction and human abjection reached in the current conflict, with education facilities razed to the ground, or occupied by military, or used as shelter by civilians fleeing war and persecution. According to the most recent assessments Unity, Bor, and Upper Nile States alone account for over 60% of IDPs in South Sudan. Further, the continuous violence in South Sudan, have emotionally and psychologically affected generations of children and young people for the rest of their lives. A study has estimated that one out of three children who live in war zones could be vulnerable to develop some form of PTSD (Post-traumatic stress disorder), psycho-pathological symptoms and lower psychosocial functioning levels during their life time, which points to the volatile and violent environment they are living in. Among those children exposed to war-related stressors for a longer period, it is generally estimated that the prevalence of post-traumatic stress symptomatology varies from 10% to 90%, manifested by anxiety disorders such as posttraumatic stress disorder and other psychiatric morbidities including depression, disruptive behaviors, and somatic symptoms Exposure to war trauma and violence has clearly been found to cause high levels of stress among children which has been associated with the development of a wide range of psychological problems. However, it is impossible for children to go through upheavals of this kind without showing their effect in difficult behavior and in variations from normality. Infantile nature has certain means at its disposal to deal with shocks, deprivations and upsets in life. Studies found that, in some children, abnormal withdrawal from the world has been noted. Some become emotionless like an automaton. Some emotional outbreaks of hysterical type have also been reported. Adolescents with significant gaps in their education are less likely to return to primary school, leaving them vulnerable to falling in with the wrong crowd as they search for purpose and acceptance. Young females in South Sudan remain severely marginalized with lower education levels and fewer opportunities for work as compared to their male peers. Educational attainment continues to be low, economic participation weak and inconsistent due to a multiplicity of factors. Enhancing the educational system extends the protection space afforded to youth who may be drawn to violence, and thus acting preventively within a dynamic of life-saving.

Activities

The project aims to carry out the following activities to address the identified needs:1. Rapid Education Assessment: in order to understand better and validate needs and priorities, a rapid assessment will be conducted. The proposed project will build existing INTERSOS education responses in these affected areas. 2. Establish additional classroom spaces within existing TLS (Temporary Learning Spaces)/Rehabilitation: The TLS allocation will be based on the dire needs and number of beneficiaries. The TLS will be provided with gender segregated WASH facilities and benches. The TLS will have the standard cluster measurement. 2 TLS (1 TLS is 2 classrooms fixed with blackboard, and 3 blocks of emergency latrines) will be established. Further, this project will rehabilitate 2 existing TLS that will require immediate maintenance and rehabilitation. The TLS set up will discussed with education stakeholders including school PTA, local authorities, etc.3. Capacity building for teachers, PTA, and educational personnel. The teachers will receive training on pedagogy in Education in Emergency, life skills, peace building, and psychosocial support. The school PTA will receive training on school management and engagements (taking education in emergency awareness campaign, resource mobilization, and child rights and protection) of education. The education personal will receive a training on education administration, management, field monitoring techniques, etc.4. Provision of school supplies: The school to be supported will need school supplies to promote their teaching and learning activities. These supplies include exercises books, chalk, and other needful school stationaries.5. Youth support: The youth will receive some youth services such as indoor games, case management, sport activities, peer education, English, secondary, and ALP classes, some skills training. 6. Provision of teachers’ incentives: The project will support 200 teachers with monthly incentive to boost their moral of teaching and voluntary work. The teachers’ incentive will be aligned with cluster rate.7. Protection education integrated activities such as; integrated Child Protection and GBV awareness activities in schools – CP/GBV clubs etc, training of school teachers (25 schools x 5 teachers per school) on CP/GBV, identification of cases and referral directly out of schools within existing referral pathways, awareness raising campaigns – GBV clubs, and provision of Dignity kits will be included.8. Conduct Nutrition screening and messaging using school governing bodies (PTAs/SMCs) in areas where GAM is above the emergency threshold in supported schools. The 2 PTA will be trained on general nutrition knowledge, screening techniques, and messaging through awareness creation per supported county. The PTA will be incentivized to enable them support schools in the whole county.9. Supporting School WASH activities: active school WASH clubs will be established in the 25 supported schools. The WASH will receive training promotion of school hygiene and sanitation training. The school clubs will receive in-kind support and running cost to facilitate their stationary and needful basic needs to support the well-functioning of established school clubs. Further, teachers, PTA, and school officers will receive training on WASH promotion.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The school PTAs are representative from community and the schools will be community schools through the support of INTERSOS. So the project implementation will be done through the community engagement and full participation so that they understand the project belongs to them. The education awareness campaigns will be done through engaging the community members including local leaders, etc.Community engagement is one of the pillars on which INTERSOS stands and thus programming is firmly rooted in ensuring direct input from communities is injected into all the activities we implement. Within the education implementation at the outset each school is supported to establish a PTA which involves representatives from the communities i.e. children’s parents, these may also be attended by other community focal points. The full participation to enhance the understanding of the functioning of schools and to actively contribute to their improvement and betterment is directly written into each project. Community engagement is furthermore sought through all awareness raising activities carried out from the school, enhancing the aggregatory power of schools and their consequential mobilising capacity to multiply the impact of messaging focused on nutrition, WASH, hygiene, and protection including GBV

INTERSOS is a Protection organisation that rolls out activities integrating GBV and CP capacity. Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through an embryonic collaboration with Humanity and Inclusion (former Handicap International )INTERSOS will be supporting the roll out of access assessments in the schools supported by our programming. The objective of assessing access allows us to put a spotlight on the diverse forms of access challenge faced by the beneficiaries and the providers of education. In so doing INTERSOS with the support of HI will highlight the needs and challenges presented by those requiring diverse support and our teaching and educational staff in highlighting the challenges they face in catering for the various spectrum of differences. Analysing access barriers will also give space to the marginalised populations that are yet not accessing INTERSOS schools and allow for targeted programming that enhances enrolment through the consideration of the various needs and populations at hand.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Thanks to a general objective of the organisation to enhance integration of protection activities and thus protection mainstreaming efforts, focus and attention will be placed on ensuring that GBV and CP themes are raised and dealt with in all programming. Enhancing the protection training of staff allows for improved identification of protection threats, providing protection staff or training to the community can also strengthen the tackling of the manifestation of the latter threats. Ensuring protection risk assessments mitigates thanks to enhanced preparation and awareness and involving children and parents in awareness activities with a focus on GBV is also aimed at addressing inequalities and threats specific to GBV matters. With the attention placed on access INTERSOS will also begin to look at addressing inequalities experienced by beneficiaries with different learning needs, and those currently facing what are yet unidentified challenges to accessing school in their specific communities due to existing inequalities or manifestations of protection threats.
