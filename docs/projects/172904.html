Project Summary

The overall objective of the proposed project is to enhance the food security and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees and enhances the livelihoods and resilience of host communities. Total number of participants are 12,000 individuals (8,000 Rohingya and 4,000 host) & (2,052 child female, 948 child male, 6,002 adult female, 2,998 adult male) from 6,000 households. Both man and women from 4,000 households will be the participants in 2 different events, 2000 households participate in one event only. Intended outputs are At least 6,000 people representing households are receiving livelihood support (75% of the participants are female); Intended outputs are at least 67% of 2,280 participants are with acceptable FCS; 2,280 participants have received food support for refugees at isolation, quarantine, SARI facilities as well as through home- delivery to older people (About 41% is female); At least 4,000 people have received portable skill support (75% female, 50% 18+); Twenty four rehabilitation initiatives are undertaken in 24 sites. Activities are A. Selection of Beneficiaries considering inclusion of the most vulnerable section of people e.g. women headed families, people with special needs , socially excluded and minorities / B. Site Selection for Mitigation schemes following standard procedures / C. Implementation of Mitigation Schemes engaging communities and ensuring safety and security of the participants And Payments in kind or e- vouchers for consumable items/ D. Livelihood Support sack & vertical gardening portable skill training on selected trade with tailored modules and supporting the beneficiaries to start new IGAs / E. Food support for refugees at isolation, quarantine, SARI facilities as well as through home- delivery to older people. Sector data and information collected by the program would be used for beneficiary selection. The process of beneficiary selection would be led by Faith In Action MEAL unit and done in consultation with local authorities. A thorough implementation plan and monitoring framework would be prepared, which will be adjusted frequently on the basis of participants feedback and learning from implementation. There will be quarterly post activity monitoring and a final evaluation / E. Increase awareness of the local institutions and communities for improving social cohesion between the Host Community and refugees.

1 - Identified Needs

Needs of Refugees:- The needs analysis for the project that started in 2020 is based mainly on REVA 3 , 94% of the Rohingya population is highly vulnerable. Since April 2020 responding to COVID-19 outbreak. Refugee Population remains 100% reliant on food assistance to sustain the minimum daily required kilocalories per individual and for dietary diversification. Moreover, refugees do not have access job market officially. The trends of food security indicators suggest that overall food security amongst the refugee population has improved, which is likely due to the regular and effective general food assistance program through in-kind and voucher programs. Under existing condition, there are so many gaps in food security in Rohingya community like: lack of opportunity for their own food production, limited financial and physical access to food, movement restrictions, protection issues and limited sources of income. So sustaining regular and timely food assistance to population is paramount and any breakage in the food supply pipeline could deepen the existing emergency situation and threaten the survival of the vulnerable Rohingya Population. For mitigating these gaps (No Suggestions) community needs food support for refugees at isolation, quarantine, SARI facilities as well as through home- delivery to older people, people in home-care and vulnerable HHs, in coordination with Health Sector and RRRC and as per needs. Providing self-reliance opportunities to refugees outside of support the health response (handicrafts, mask making , snack making, seed distributions, trainings) to prevent spreading of Covid-19, negatively impacting refugees’ economic situation. Needs of host Communities:- Prior to the start of COVID-19 response, more than 370,000 people were reportedly living under the poverty line with an 400,000 poor or vulnerable anticipated to have been impacted as a result of COVID-19 economic repercussions. The pandemic and its subsequent containment measures impacted food security and livelihoods, exacerbating existing poverty in the host communities (ISCG 04/2020; WFP 04/2020). As well as the rapid and massive increase of the refugee population, concentrated in the south of Cox’s Bazar district, has had an enormous impact on Bangladeshi host communities’ food security, economic vulnerability, market access, labors opportunities and environment. In addition, natural disaster and deforestation may prolong and worsen the food insecurity for both Rohingya and host communities and the Rohingya, exacerbating the likelihood and social tensions and the negative impact on both communities. 93% of households reported diminished income as an impact of COVID-19. Faith In Action is implementing an Complementary Food Voucher program at the moment in Camp-23, but finds many farmers and fisherman uninterested in agriculture & fisheries, due to lack of irrigation water and productivity of the land is not to the expected level due to absence of micro nutrients in the soil. For mitigating these host community needs support to agriculture, livestock and fisheries through financial literacy program. To implement this program, it needs training center, enough trained staff and other procurement and logistics support.

2 - Activities or outputs

Outputs: Total number of participants are 12,000 individuals (8,000 Rohingya and 4,000 host) & (2,052 child female, 948 child male, 6,002 adult female, 2,998 adult male) from 6,000 households. Both man and women from 4,000 households will be the participants in 2 different events, 2000 households participate in one event only. Intended outputs are At least 6,000 people representing households are receiving livelihood support (75% of the participants are female); Intended outputs are at least 67% of 2,280 participants are with acceptable FCS; 2,280 participants have received food support for refugees at isolation, quarantine, SARI facilities as well as through home- delivery to older people (About 41% is female); At least 4,000 people have received portable skill support (75% female, 50% 18+); Twenty four rehabilitation initiatives are undertaken in 24 sites.Activities 1: Selection of Beneficiaries: Faith In Action has a database for camp-23 comprising of the detailed information at household level. Database for camp 21 and camp 22 (Host Community Whykong and Baharchhara Union) will be created through collection of information door to door. The information comprises household size, literacy, consumption, disability, and age and sex data. Information on services available to the household will also be collected. Beneficiaries for different activities will be primarily identified through this process. Then a draft participants list will be prepared. on the basis of interest of the individuals and program priorities for beneficiaries ( that intends to include the most vulnerable section of beneficiaries). The beneficiary list will be finalized with the approval of the CiCs. Activities 2: Site Selection : Mitigation schemes sites will be selected following a process of community consultation and authorities advise. The database would be used for preparing and implementing the monitoring frame work.Activities 3: Livelihood Support: The project are of 4 different kinds . First, 4,000 selected women would be provided 2 day orientation on sack & vertical gardening. They would be provided with seeds, manures and other materials for sack and vertical gardening and have fortnightly visit from the extension workers. These beneficiaries are supposed to come from the same household that the mitigation scheme beneficiaries are coming from. Second 2,000 selected man would be provided 2 day orientation on sack & vertical gardening. They would be provided with seeds, manures and other materials for sack and vertical gardening and have fortnightly visit from the extension workers. Third , 2,000 selected adolescent girls and boys would be provided portable skill training on trades they are interested , the beneficiaries would be also provided participation allowances in kind to enable them to participate in the course and ensuring their household level subsistence during the training. Forth, there would be training on business planning and selected trade for 3,000 host community beneficiaries and 1,000 adolescent girls and boys would be provided portable skill training on trades they are interested. The beneficiaries will come from the ultra-poor section of people. There would be provided subsistence allowance and startup capital for the business. Subsistence allowance would be according to the sector standards.Activities 4: Increase awareness of the local institutions and communities for improving social cohesion between the Host Community and refugees: Promote the development of the right sets of vocational/technical skills for Rohingya in camp set up to tap into the opportunities for their social and e Like food processing, solar panels repair, Beads products, Embroideries, sewing machine repairs, production concrete flaps for WASH facilities, and market-oriented skills training have promoted self-help. The multi-purpose centers provide exposition grounds for their products and potential markets from camps and host populations.
