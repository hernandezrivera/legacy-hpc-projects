Project Summary

Project Goal: Women, men, boys and girls have knowledge on prevention and response to child protection issues and referral through functional referral system, advocacy sessions and meeting with key stakeholders, capacity building, psychosocial support services and other child protection services in Yola North, Michika, Madagali and Girei LGAs, Adamawa state.
Nkafamiya Rescue Mission (NRM) will implement this project to achieve the project outcome by providing support to 1500 children through the provision of psychosocial support services and case management in child friendly spaces to be established in the project locations. NRM will also establish and strengthen the existing community based structures through trainings, advocacy sessions to relevant key stakeholders such as traditional and religious leaders, women and youth groups, community based child protection committees, government relevant line ministries, security personnel, members of civilian joint task forces to support livelihood and vocational skills for out of school children which will target 500 children. Also, NRM will conduct capacity building sessions for the stakeholders so as to prevent and response to family separation through family tracing.
100 vulnerable caregivers will be targeted with material and livelihood support so as to provide livelihood for their children and enable them to be enrolled in school. IEC materials will also be developed and produce in local languages with collaboration with Translators without border so as to promote awareness on child protection and carryout sensitization activities across all project locations.

1 - Needs

NRM’s proposed protection activities for 2023, will build on the existing response in Borno and Adamawa states established since 2019. NRM aims to respond to and mitigate perilous protection needs of vulnerable populations through community based protection approach, by building on its gains of its established community protection structures to response to needs of these people living in formal/informal camps, host and returnee communities. NRM’s strong presence across 7 LGAs in NE Nigeria strengthened by its diverse programming and ability to aptly respond to emergencies over the years will provide an adequate backing for this proposed protection response. Protection programming will target extremely vulnerable persons and persons with protection concerns through a combination of protection related programming responses including; provision of immediate psychosocial support (PSS)services, psychological first aid (PFA) and community based counselling, individual and household level case management to address protection concerns, individualized protection assistance (IPA) to alleviate vulnerabilities, address environmental and remedial risks which expose individuals/ communities to protection risks through protection assessments, advocacy and coordination. NRM will continue to provide, life-saving information to communities on services offered within their communities, give communities access to services through establishment or strengthening of referral pathways, provide protection related services through IPA, increase duty bearer’s capacity for a protection capacity for all. NRM coordination and collaboration efforts with key stakeholders will aim to strengthen service provision to beneficiaries whilst ensuring persons of concerns have access to their entitlements and rights while engaging institutions and actors to enhance their duty bearer commitments. Protection mobile desks will ensure communities feel safe by embracing protection by presence which will ensure protection services are available to communities throughout the year. The mobile desk will provide access to crucial protection related services for communities situated within accessible distances. Which include referral services for gender-based violence (GBV), child protection (CP), housing, land and property (HLP), and general protection services (documentation, other human rights violations). NRM will carry out protection monitoring assessments which will identify protection risks and feed this information back into programming throughout the implementation period to strengthen the response. However crucial advocacy initiatives will be derived from this information to influence duty bearers so that they improve the protective environment of IDPs, returnees and host communities as NRM addresses protection concerns and mitigation of risks. NRM intends to reach an estimated 1235 HH (138,450 individuals) (55% female and 45%male beneficiaries will be targeted) in 4 LGAs across Borno & Adamawa states with general conflict affected communities to improve their psychological and social wellbeing. NRM’s age and gender diversity policy to programming enhances gender effective programming informed by analyzing, application and accommodation of the different needs of diverse categories age, gender, vulnerability, ethnicity, status of health, economic status. protection programming in the next year. Main protection activities will include; IPA and case management for protection cases, awareness on specific protection related themes guided by the findings of protection monitoring activities, creation or strengthening of referral pathways, providing information on services provided and important human rights awareness, collecting beneficiary feedback through protection desks and clinics, promotion of human rights through advocacy, strengthening the protection environment of persons of concern through coordination and stakeholder engagement, capacity building.

2 - Activities

Through capacity building targeting protection and other sector actors to reinforce the protective environment of beneficiaries. Targeted stakeholders may include; ministry of women and social affairs, community leaders, traditional/ religious leaders and community volunteers (known community role models who continually represent community concerns and act as conflict mediators) and local authorities. An estimated 150 stakeholders will be targeted. They will be trained on protection principles, mainstreaming and survivor/ victim centered approaches, human rights approach, community-based counselling, Psychosocial first aid, PSEA and Core humanitarian standards and referral pathways and protection concerns identification and response. NRM will continue to build on capacity building of community structures, 5 community-based protection group members will be trained on protection topics including basic case management and counselling including PFA, PSEA, mediation and conflict resolution strategies, problem solving, COVID-19 guidelines, complaint and feedback mechanisms and, small business management. The trainings will strengthen their ability to provide community-based protection services to community members using a community-based model. Members of the community will be nominated as community representatives and act as important protection services decentralization points as they are accessible to community members throughout the year every day of the week as they live within the community. They will champion awareness activities as they are well versed in understanding the context and know the trends of protection risks. NRM will familiarize the groups on referrals pathways and basic case identification in order to provide support to persons with severe/ urgent protection concerns in the community. The groups/ committees will develop community advocacy initiatives aimed at initiating behavioral change. NRM will identify vulnerable persons with protection concerns and provide IPA, PSS, mediation and referrals to services. The protection desks will act as information dissemination points and PSS safe spaces for counselling activities. NRM’s PSS interventions will fall within the bottom three categories of the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (2007). NRM will target, adolescent girls, women, youth and vulnerable persons for PSS services and mentorship programmes including direct counselling services and group PSS such as sports, art therapies, games, community level skills building on life skills and literacy for adults and adolescents girls and boys who cannot access formal education. NRM’s Protection monitoring strategy will focus on gathering crucial information on the protection risks faced by individuals through the cases reported to NRM on the protection desks and through various community engagement activities including focus group discussions, key informant interview, household surveys and community meetings. The data gathered from this daily and monthly activities will be compiled into reports which will then inform awareness activities, advocacy strategies and response strategies. Coupled with NRM awareness activities 30,200 individuals (60% female, 40% male) will be targeted.
