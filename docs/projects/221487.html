Project Summary

The ongoing Anglophone armed conflict in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon continues to worsen the precarious living conditions of children, increasing their vulnerability and affecting their development. This context exposes them to various protection risks such as family separation, physical and sexual violence, hazardous child labor, and psychosocial distress. The risk of statelessness among these children remains high due to the non-functioning of civil registration offices.
This situation necessitates the continuation of child protection responses focused on the following interventions:
• Providing individualized services to children and their guardians based on protection needs.
• Offering alternative care and reunification for unaccompanied and separated children.
• Providing psychosocial support and mental health care.
• Strengthening the technical skills of child protection actors in emergency child protection.
• Coordinating the child protection response.
However, this response remains limited given the number of children in need of protection across the 13 departments of the Northwest and Southwest. It is also limited in terms of geographical coverage, the service package, and the competencies of child protection actors. An analysis of the monthly operational presence from January to October 2024 by the CPAOR of NW/SW reveals that service delivery efforts are concentrated only in the regional capitals of the affected regions. Divisions such as Lebialem, Ndian, Donga-Mantung, and Kupe Manenguba continue to lack a significant operational presence to address child protection issues.
Regarding the service package offered, it is insufficient to assist, particularly in terms of supporting child survivors of sexual violence and providing more opportunities for alternative care for unaccompanied and separated children beyond foster care.
The current project aims to enhance children's access to quality and consistent protection services through a community-based approach. Specifically, it intends to:
• Facilitate access to care services by expanding the geographical coverage of the response to neglected divisions and subdivisions.
• Strengthen the service package by integrating case management for child survivors of sexual violence and other alternatives for interim care of unaccompanied and separated children, remote case management etc
• Improve the quality of these services by enhancing the technical skills of child protection partners.
• Strengthen coordination in the provision of rapid child protection responses.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

To ensure that vulnerable girls and boys in the conflict-affected areas of the Northwest and Southwest regions receive inclusive, integrated prevention and response services against abuse, violence, and exploitation, the following interventions will be rolled out in priority divisions. These interventions aim to provide comprehensive protection and support to children in the conflict-affected regions, ensuring their safety, well-being, and development1. Alternative Care, Family Tracing, and Reunification for Unaccompanied and Separated Childreno Identification: Unaccompanied and separated children will be identified through mass awareness campaigns in places of worship, schools, radio broadcasts, food distributions, focus group discussions, and child-friendly spaces.o Transitional Care: Once identified and registered, children will be placed in transitional care with foster families accredited by social affairs or meeting new selection criteria. These families will receive non-food items (hygiene kits, mats, blankets) and clothing kits for the children.o Case Management and Family Tracing: Individual case management will address specific needs, and family tracing will be conducted with the local Red Cross and regional social affairs. Successful tracing will include transport support and reintegration kits, with follow-up sessions. For unsuccessful cases, sustainable alternatives will be proposed.o Training: Foster families and CPAoR members will receive training on their roles in transitional care and reunification processes.2. Provision of Mental and Psychosocial Supporto Psychosocial Interventions: These will address the distress of children and their caregivers, strengthening resilience and contributing to development. Interventions will follow IASC guidelines and focus on community-based non-specialized support.o Community Spaces and Training: Establishing and strengthening friends-of-friends spaces, life skills training for adolescents, and positive parenting education will be facilitated by trained community facilitators.o Specialist Support: Referrals to health services or specialized organizations will be made for those requiring advanced support.3. Case Management Services for High-Risk Childreno Deployment of CPMIs+: Trained case management personnel will be deployed to targeted areas. CPAoR members will be trained in harmonized child case management SOPs.o Referral mechanism to identify and refers vulnerables children will be establish alon with service providers in location targeted 4. Strengthening Community-Based Child Protection Mechanismso Community Leadership: Establishing and strengthening community child protection mechanisms based on good practices and lessons learned. Existing community structures will be mapped and utilized, with a focus on inclusive participation.o Training and Support: Community structures will be trained in participatory child protection diagnostics, tailored action development, and response mechanisms. Partnerships with religious and traditional leaders will be strengthened.5. Systematic Child Registrationo Interoperability with Health System: Health services will be connected to birth registration services. Training will be provided for mayors, civil register officers, and health actors on the birth registration process.o Catch-Up Birth Registration: Priority will be given to the most vulnerable children and those in final exam classes. Coordination with education, social services, BUNEC, justice, and mayors will ensure rapid provision of birth certificates.6. Reinforcement of GBV Risk Mitigation and Prevention, Including PSEAo Community Engagement: Promoting and supporting complaints and feedback mechanisms, and conducting outreach activities in communities.
