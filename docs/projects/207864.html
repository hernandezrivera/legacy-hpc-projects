Project Summary

In 2024, the ACF’s WASH intervention will continue for Rohingya refugees in Camp 11 and the adjacent host community in Ukhiya, who have been directly or indirectly impacted by hosting Rohingya refugees. The WASH response in the camp will be focused on the operation and maintenance of existing WASH services through cost-efficient solutions. Quality provision of water and sanitation facilities will be targeted, as well as Solid Waste Management provided through infrastructure support and community engagement (especially on solid waste). Adequate hygiene behavior will be promoted, mostly focusing the community engagement and ACF will continue its integrated approach (a multi-sectoral approach) to scale up it in the camp and host community.
ACF will also work in the Host Communities, to scale up WASH support to ensure equitable access to quality WASH services aligned with national development plans. Take the proposed area selected considering the WASH vulnerability criteria (e.g., lack of improved water and sanitation) and the beneficiaries into consideration who are directly or indirectly impacted because of hosting Rohingya refugees/ FDMNs.
Supply of WASH items e.g. soap, and menstrual kits will be considered by ensuring the prior discussion with the area focal agency and WASH sector and host community focal to avoid any duplication in the response.
The intervention will continue the internal monitoring system and will allow external monitoring to make sure of equitable and quality WASH services in the intervention area.
ACF WASH response will consider environmental issues, gender, age, disability inclusion, and accountability, with emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks. This project will also ensure community ownership over the facilities and the project’s sustainability.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to the J-MSNA 2023, 86% of Rohingya refugees/ FDMNs indicate having sufficient water for their household needs, a result attributed to ongoing collaborative efforts by the Government and WASH Partners. In camp 11, the figure rises to 89%, yet 11% of individuals still require attention in water intervention. In terms of safe water access, 69% of households use the water network, while 31% depend on deep tube wells. Challenges persist, particularly in water network operations and coverage expansion to bridge the gap. Regular operation and maintenance are crucial for ensuring access to safe drinking water in the camp.Regarding safe sanitation, 97% of facilities are reported as functional, but ongoing operation and maintenance are required. In solid waste management, 73% of households practice source segregation, yet challenges persist in covering the remaining gaps. The J-MNSA highlights gaps in MHM kits and soap, necessitating collaboration with area focal agencies and the WASH sector. As per J-MSNA 2023 report, 28% of female family members feel unsafe using communal latrines at night, requiring ongoing advocacy efforts and improved lighting near latrines and access roads. According to gender analysis report by UNHCR, CARE, and ActionAid 2020, the Rohingya refugee camps in Bangladesh face a deficiency in proper WASH infrastructure, primarily due to space limitations. This inadequacy manifests in poorly situated water points and latrines, a shortage of gender-segregated and disabled-friendly facilities, and a lack of privacy and security. These circumstances heighten the risk of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), particularly affecting women and girls, and impede their access to essential WASH facilities. While there is an ongoing improvement in the situation, addressing these challenges remains important for effective intervention.In host community, according to J-MSNA, only 14% of host community households received WASH assistance, despite total humanitarian assistance reaching just 18%. 9% percent of host community households frequently worry about insufficient water. The use of shallow tube wells by 13% of households is a matter of concern due to potential water contamination. Additionally, the reported use of unimproved latrine facilities (single pit latrines without a slab) by 5% of host community households poses a serious public health risk. The REVA assessment by WFP in June 2023 revealed 40% dissatisfaction with drinking water access and 41% dissatisfaction with sanitation and hygiene in the host community. Additionally, 5% use unimproved latrines, and 23% lack functioning handwashing facilities.As per J-MSNA 2023, it is reported that, 8% host and 15% of the refugee household with at least one member of disability. ACF will address these challenges at the time of implementation based on the needs in the ground level in where emphasizing assistive devices will be consider as priority.Concerning menstrual hygiene, a significant portion in both the Rohingya camp and host community lack prior information (41% and 46%, respectively). 11% believed they discharged contaminated blood. In the host community, 18% face challenges changing clothes during menstruation, and many women and girls experience restrictions on daily activities and separate sleeping arrangements. Project interventions are crucial to address these knowledge and hygiene gaps.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Action Against Hunger (ACF) has played a crucial role in providing WASH support to Rohingya refugees and the host community in Cox's Bazar District since 2009. The organization's efforts have been particularly critical in the 2017 after refugee influx and natural disasters, which exacerbated WASH challenges and highlighted the importance of ACF's work.The proposed WASH intervention in the camp and host community aligns precisely with rationalization principles, emphasizing an integrated approach, community engagement, and sustainability. The project recognizes the evolving needs of both Rohingya refugees and the host community, as detailed in the J-MSNA 2023 report. While acknowledging achievements in water and sanitation, the report identifies ongoing challenges, stressing the importance of an integrated approach. This involves considering factors such as water point/water network operations, sanitation facility functionality, solid waste management, and the unique needs of vulnerable groups, including women special needs, and persons with disabilities.The intervention addresses the significant gap in WASH assistance to the host community, as revealed by the J-MSNA and REVA assessments. ACF WASH assistance highlights the imperative of addressing disparities in alignment with the rationalization principle of equitable service provision.In terms of continuity and predictability, the project outlines a clear plan for ongoing WASH interventions in 2024. Emphasis on the operation and maintenance of existing services ensures sustainability. The focus on cost-efficient solutions and community engagement further solidifies the commitment to long-term impact.By considering diverse needs, promoting community ownership, and incorporating feedback mechanisms, the project demonstrates a holistic and sustainable response to the WASH challenges faced by Rohingya refugees and the host community in Cox's Bazar District.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

To ensure protection in WASH, ACF will make all facilities functional with safety measures for secure and dignified use by women and girls. The improved accessibility by adding assistive features like ramps and chairs in latrines and distributing assistive devices to households with persons with disabilities will be considered based on needs.Gender-segregated signs will be placed on WASH facilities, designating areas for females. Women's participation in the WASH user group and the model mother initiative aims to empower them and reduce gender-based violence (GBV) risks.Door-to-door Hygiene kit distribution will reduce GBV risks by avoiding large gatherings. Awareness sessions will cover personal safety, disease prevention, and GBV, integrating expertise from ACF's mental health, protection, and gender teams. ACF will also train volunteers and staff on preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), GBV, and child protection to sensitize them. Advocacy for lighting near WASH facilities will continue for the safe use of latrines at nighttime. During location selection, ACF will consider privacy, safety, accessibility, and user comfort.

3.3 - Age & Disability

ACF will also consistently work to support all people regardless of age, sex, and disability by ensuring the functionality of all WASH facilities that will be accessible, inclusive and climate resilience. ACF will identify the specific needs of older adults and persons with disabilities through a needs assessment. Based on needs, ACF will provide support to the person with disabilities by making WASH facilities accessible for them and distributing assistive items like disabled-friendly chairs for defecating, buckets etc, to persons with disabilities who have mobility limitations. Engage older adults and persons with disabilities in the planning and design of WASH interventions and ensure training to WASH staff on the needs of older adults and persons with disabilities

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure that the community is involved in the program, ACF will consult with community members, throughout the implementation process. ACF will also meet with beneficiaries to discuss their needs and involve them in the program as needed.Community members can also participate in the program through the established complaint feedback mechanism. This system allows them to complain about WASH services and needs directly to ACF, or through a complaint box, hotline, or other established channels. ACF will address all complaints promptly and share the response with the person who made the complaint.In terms of sharing information about the project, ACF will participate in camp and host community meetings, as well as sector-level meetings, to share information about the project, and WASH services.ACF will also conduct regular meetings with community leaders and user committees to discuss and prioritize needs, and to plan interventions. Finally, ACF will empower affected populations to participate in decision-making by activating user groups and WASH facilities management communities and will do joint visits regularly.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

• ACF upholds the belief that everyone, irrespective of age, gender identity, disability, sexual orientation, or ethnic background, deserves protection from any form of harm, abuse, neglect, or exploitation. It enforces a zero-tolerance policy against bullying, harassment, abuse, and exploitation by its staff and associated individuals.• ACF consistently adhered to its approach of involving women in every aspect of program implementation to reduce their vulnerabilities and the project is implemented in a way that minimizes their risk of exploitation and abuse.• Making WASH facilities safe and accessible for everyone including located in safe areas. This project will consider the needs of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups. • ACF will also remain vigilant in monitoring the working environment of its staff to prevent any instances of sexual exploitation and abuse. ACF will implement its PSEA policy to report and respond properly.• To ensure gender sensitivity, ACF will provide PSEA training for both volunteers and staff. This will help to increase people's understanding of PSEA, and their ability to identify and report incidents.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

ACF will ensure Preparedness and response in line with the WASH sector strategy in case of any natural disaster and outbreak and activate the formed RRT shortly. Respecting the NEAT+, ACF will conduct the environmental and vulnerability assessment considering the landslide, flood, and cyclone risk, in which controlling high WASH disease prevalence as a preventive barrier is considered a high priority. ACF will also work to prevent AWD, scabies, and dengue in the propped area. ACF will continue to maintain its contingency plan and team capacitated to respond to the needs immediately. ACF will continue coordination and communication with relevant stakeholders and teams for the emergency response. To prevent the consequences of natural disasters, ACF WASH will work for resilient infrastructures and will ensure tie down in the facilities considering the vulnerability conditions.
