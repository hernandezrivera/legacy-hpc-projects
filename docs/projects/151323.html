Project Summary

In this project, QRCS will improve the access and utilization of Emergency and sustainable WASH related services for women and children living in the most affected areas in Somalia. The services to be provided include: Distribution of standard IEC materials for social mobilization and good personal and environmental sanitation; Support distribution of hygiene kits, provision of sanitation infrastructures and related hygiene promotion; Construction of twin latrines; Construction of drilling of deeper boreholes for affected communities (180,000 persons of beneficiaries); Rehabilitation of communal ground water tanks (Berkets) for draught affected communities (3000 persons); Capacity building of Community Water management committees including operators (40 persons); 1,100 HHs will benefit from hygiene promotion campaigns and other cholera prevention related activities(distribution of hygiene kits); Organize community sensitization and mobilization sessions at facility and outreach level in facilities managed by QRCS in the states of the federal states of Somalia. QRC has a Disaster Risk Recovery unit that will work with government and other stakeholders to find sustainable solutions to disaster impacts. The specific focus of the unit is to: support the government in developing a legal, policy and institutional framework that guides the regions in Disaster Risk Reduction; support mainstreaming it to long term development plans; implement projects that strengthen livelihoods, the recovery and resiliency of communities impacted by disasters, addressing the needs of vulnerable groups such as IDPs and communities hosting returnees. QRCS will demonstrate their commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into organizations’ strategies, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions and training and this will be highlighted in reporting. The feedback mechanism will be provided through Village Committees with the project team collating the information to be used for interventions delivery improvement.

Needs

Across Somalia there are multiple threats to the health and resilience of the population due to inadequate WASH facilities. The threats are multifactorial and multi-impact. For water supply, the population is dependent on a mix of unprotected surface water sources and protected groundwater sources. Surface water sources are seasonal and usually contaminated, while protected groundwater supplies are either difficult to access to due to distance and/or not affordable due the high costs of extraction and the dominance of private sector entrepreneurs in their operation. High water collection distances further limit access and water consumption for many people. Unpredictable rainfall patterns, conflict, displacement and poor operation and maintenance of WASH facilities has resulted in only 45 per cent of the population having access to improved/protected water sources. There are limited or no funds for maintaining WASH facilities, including the de-silting of berkads, traditional rainwater harvesting reservoirs, which collect water during the wet season for use in the dry season. At, Somalia lacks policy and institutional frameworks for financing, managing and monitoring WASH services and infrastructure which impacts on durable solutions for the displaced. There is lack of reliable and consistent data collection and analysis which limits decision-making. This proposal is aimed at addressing critical needs and provide life saving support to drought affected populations in the states of the Somalia Federal Republic. Areas of focus will be sanitation coverage, mortality rates of children under the age of 5years, at high risk of preventable waterborne diseases. The program will employ a holistic and integrated approach with others clusters such as health, food security and nutrition to maximize impacts and ensure that everyone in the affected community women, girls, boys and men have a voice in the development and implementation of the project.

Activities

• Provision of temporary safe water services through construction and rehabilitation of shallow wells to the targeted settlements and communities• Provision of emergency sanitation facilities through construction and rehabilitation of VIP latrines to targeted settlements and communities• Provision of hand washing facilities at critical moments to the people living in the target settlements and communities• Distribution of hygiene kits to the most vulnerable communities in the target regions• Development of the Staff and community on best hygiene and sanitation practices• Social mobilization and community sensitization on hygiene practices

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

QRCS will work the Government in order to build a resilient and responsive health system infrastructure. QRCS will focus both on the everyday routine challenges that front line staff face on a regular basis as well as focus on sudden shocks such as disease outbreaks. QRCS will work to build the leadership capacity of district and regional state health systems and in this regard will target Government key health managers, health facility staff, community members as well as patients accessing services so as to empower them to solve problems at their level and build relationships and social networks. QRCS will focus on strengthening existing social networks to provide access to multiple resources available in the community and forms of value such as community knowledge and reputation that can build and restore resilience to health systems. QRCS will use the model of everyday resilience building in the health system of Government through a combination of absorptive, adaptive and transformative strategies that health authorities, staff and community members can use to adopt the health system in responding to strains and shocks. This will help in producing a strengthened and more resourceful resilient health system infrastructure. QRCS will train the Health Authorities, health facility staffs, patients and community members on how to deal with sudden shocks to the health system such as outbreak of diseases like Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), Cholera, and Measles and so on. All the different strata of the health system will be taught how to manage such disease outbreaks at different levels starting from the community level to the primary health unit to the health center to the referral health facility level so as not to overwhelm only one level of the health system but to build a resilient and responsive health infrastructure into every level. QRCS will use the funding from donors as well as its own funds to ensure adequate resources and strong health system infrastructure is built to contribute to generating a resilient and responsive health system in all the states of Somalia.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

QRCS is an independent, neutral organization ensuring humanitarian protection and assistance for victims of armed conflict and other situations of violence, natural disasters such as drought and famine. It takes action in response to emergencies and at the same time promotes respect for international humanitarian law and its implementation in national law. QRCS as part of the movement of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Society it is guided by a code of conduct to ensure that displaced people are treated with the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. As such the highest regard for the protection of human life and health will be provided to all people needing and seeking help. QRCS staff and partners will ensure that not protection risk is incurred. In the event of any exposure to protection risk. QRCS has risk mitigation plan that includes: Identification of the risk, assessing it, prioritization, tracking it and finally implementation and monitoring of the progress of the mitigation measures. QRCS will follow its laid down procedures to mitigate those risks to ensure protection is provided at all times to all in need. Displaced populations, where by natural or human-made disasters are at a high risk to be excluded from accessing the humanitarian support they need the most due to multifactorial issues some of which include segregation of minority groups, stigma, exclusion due to lack of knowledge or information sharing. Taking cognizance of these risks of exclusion that displaced populations face, QRCS will actively seek an all-inclusive policy in order to reach all those in need especially in the areas QRCS is working. QRCS will recruit some of its project staff from IDPs especially for positions such as CHWs in order to ensure IDPs get inclusion in accessing and benefiting from all our projects and will also ask for their participation in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

QRCS is an equal opportunity employer and does not discriminate against anyone on the basis of sex, religion, race, or any other factor. QRCS as a member of the International Convention of the Red Cross and Red Crescent Society follow its code of conduct at all levels of its operation whether at HQ, regional office, or facility staffs, to ensure that displaced people are treated with the humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. In this regard, QRCS will ensure no discrimination of whatever form is allowed on any basis including on gender and age. QRCS believes all members of the society of all gender and age have a role to play in alleviating human need and suffering. In that vein, QRCS will engage men, women, boys and girls, young and old alike, in the delivery of services either as staff or as beneficiaries.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Populations displaced by conflict and violence face a lot of problems especially humanitarian need and lack of life-saving health services. QRCS will work to ensure the provision of access to basic life-saving health to all populations in need in our areas of interventions without discrimination. In this regard, QRCS will also work within the humanitarian context and coordinate with other actors to ensure the severe impacts of armed conflict and violence is reduced. QRCS works very closely with communities and local authorities, ensuring that local laws, customs and culture are adhered to, and avoiding action and advocacy that may be perceived as offensive.
