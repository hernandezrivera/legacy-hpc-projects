Project Summary

Food security remains a serious challenge due to continued localized hostilities, new and protracted displacements, increased numbers of recent returnees and the sustained erosion of communities' resilience after almost ten years of conflict, in addition to, loss the value of local currency with rising the food prices continuously.
The intervention will contribute to cover the gaps and respond to food needs of crisis-affected people in Aleppo rural province, through improving access to food. The project will target the most vulnerable families from IDPs & returnees (men, women, boys, and girls) who affected by the Syrian crisis.
The project will include one main activity of humanitarian intervention which is supporting the IDPs & returnees in Atareb district through distributing food kits for 1570 families for 8 months in Kafr Naseh Elatareb village (1450 from IDPs families and 120 from returnees' families), where there were 1450 families (7640 persons) in the random camps in the village (10 camps) according to the latest needs assessment and survey that conducted by MASRRAT team during April 2021.
The food kits will provide the families with 1470 Calories which is equal to 70 % per person per day according to Sphere standards.
This intervention will target the families who meet the selection criteria and at the same time we are going to focus on the families that have not received any food assistant during the last three months. The food kits' weight is approximately 80 Kg and contained the most basic materials such as (Rice, bulgur, lentils, sugar, oil, ghee, flour and other materials such as dates, jam, canned fish, olives, luncheon)
The food kits will be distributed to the IDPs & returnees to their living locations and will be procured inside Syria in order to support the local markets.
The project has not been secured yet.

3 - Needs

Syria continues to suffer from multiple and complex socioeconomic difficulties, the protracted crisis, now in its 12th year, is exacerbating food security and livelihood needs for many families across the country, further eroding the resilience and recovery prospects. The According to HNO2023, People in Need (PIN) has increased from 14.6 million in 2022 to 15.3 million in 2023, including 5.3 million IDPs, 9.9 million residents, and 52,700 returnees. According to the intersectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning and are classified as catastrophic needs in three sub-districts in two governorates. Things could get even worse if it was not for donors’ generosity which allowed humanitarian partners to deliver assistance to 7.7 million people on average per month in 2022. FSA sector foresees an increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023. Food prices in Syria have increased dramatically in 2020 and WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in November 2020 was 251 percent higher when compared to November 2019. Based on these data and through the wide coverage of MASRRAT Organization in the western countryside of Aleppo: MASRRAT team in April 2021 conducted many visits and needs assessments in Atareb district in Aleppo rural. Wherein, the results showed that the IDPs(1450 families) & returnees (120 families )suffering from a general shortage of basic food items due to the high price of foodstuffs.

4 - Implementation of activities

The Implementation plan of activities will start on 01 Jan 2023, the project duration will be 10 months wherein, the first month will include preparations, procurement, and contracting with eight months will include the distributions. The staff will be recruited directly to carry out project implementation. The project will provide assistance for 1450 vulnerable families monthly for eight months (we will check the last gap analysis from the FSL). The basket will provide the families with 1561 Kcal which is equal to 74% per day as per Sphere standards. The food basket weight is approximately 35 Kg. The targeted locations will focus on the crowdy IDPs in the random camps and the returnees' families in Kafr Naseh Elatareb village who did not receive any food support during the previous three months. At the beginning of the implementation of the project, we will sign (MOUs) with the local council in the area. The process of engaging the Local Council will start with advertising the project in the communities of intervention, explaining the mechanism of selection, and the number of potential beneficiaries under this project. T. Beneficiaries will sign on the distribution lists, and they will be verified through their IDs. The basket will include:Jam 2 kg, Halawa 1 kg, Thyme 1 kg, Olive oil 3 L, Short-Grain Rice 3 Kg, Wheat Flour 5 Kg, Soap (5 pieces) 1 piece, Red Lentils 1 Kg, Green Lentils 1 Kg, Dry White beans 1 kg, Sunflower Oil 4 L, Bulgur Wheat 3 Kg and Sugar 5 Kilo

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks will be analyzed throughout the project life cycle. The following protection risks and mitigating measures are presented: 1- Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Mitigating measures: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for the code of conduct and PSEA. 2- Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality. Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure training packages are in line with child protection minimum standards and consider the remote-managed context 2. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. 3)-Tension in the community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff Mitigating measures.Gender equality:The project will target all groups of families, where men, women, boys, and girls will be benefited from the intervention. 9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk management process designed to include all of project life. MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions according to the following Potential risks: Assumptions: • There will be permanent access to the project implementation area and the targeted beneficiaries. • There will be Cooperation with the local councils and other humanitarian agencies in the implementation of the project through sharing the data and selection of beneficiaries and others.• collaboration of the targeted families and stakeholders with the team and their participation in the implementation of the project. Risks and mitigations: • Increase in the needs: a new in influx of IDPs into the targeted locations (mitigation: collaborate with donors/INGOs to cover any additional needs) • Lack of food materials in the region (Mitigation: purchase the materials from other nearby areas) • Increase in food prices during the implementation the project (Mitigation: fix the prices with suppliers during the project period) • Deterioration of the security situation or cutting the access to the region (Mitigation: Postponing the distributions or changing distribution locations) • The transition of Coronavirus infection to a community member, if any. Mitigation Measure(s): MASRRAT will follow up the recommendation of WHO and clusters about the epidemic and the implementation of the project activities.
