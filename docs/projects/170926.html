Project Summary

Through two main components, the project aims at:
1. Support vulnerable families of GBV/SGBV survivors/victims to access their immediate needs of food.
2. Preventing and responding to Gender-Based Violence in conflict-affected areas of the Gaza Strip.
The project responses to detected GBV/SGBV concerns in the conflict-affected areas of Gaza and North Gaza. The project targets the most vulnerable communities of the Gaza Strip focusing on the families of GBV/SGBV cases who were identified to receive case management services. The objective of this action is consistent with the assigned objectives of the Food Cluster 2021 HRP, objective 2: “Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs"; and objective 2 of the protection cluster: “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence”. The main activities include:
Food Aid Component:
- Providing direct access to food for 600 HHs, GVB/SGVB survivors through food vouchers.
GBV Component:
- Providing case management support utilizing remote modality and face to face according to the needs of women to 300 GBV cases including individual counselling to 200 women,50 girls and 50 boys, distribution of 300 dignity kits, and conduct 5 remote group sessions following Hawwa Zahra manual for GBV during crisis time targeting GBV/SGBV cases. - Provide spatialized mental health (psychotherapy and medication) to 200 GBV cases referred by case managers to AISHA community centre for mental health and substance abuse. - Family therapy to 30 GBV cases including family sessions, individual therapy sessions and group sessions. - Providing emergency health care to 100 women and 50 girls including SRHR services (Sexual, reproductive health); women to be referred through case managers. - Providing legal services to the 150 women including legal consultation, mediation, court representations and legal awareness through workshops. - Provide cash assistant to 70 women survivors of GBV and their families. - Conducting awareness-raising workshops on Gender, GBV and women’s rights for 200 women, 100 girls (20 workshops; 15 women each). - Conducting 20 awareness-raising workshops for men from the families of the targeted women to involve 300 men. In addition to providing capacity building training on SGBV/GBV/ PSEA for NPA staff.

1 - Needs

In October 2020, and in collaboration between NPA, MA'AN Development Center and AISHA Association for Woman and Child Protection (AISHA), an assessment had been conducted targeting GBV/SGBV cases who received case management services as part of AISHA protection programs. The assessment aimed at addressing the socio-economic vulnerabilities of the target group. The assessment responds to the review results of AISHA protection program, conducted in mid-2020, which addressed families' urgent needs to food aid and livelihoods support interventions. Vulnerable socioeconomic profile of women families was recognized which entailed proceeding with detailed socioeconomic assessment. 52.3% of the families live in lockdown zones, 95.8% of whom are from North Gaza and Gaza governorates with an average family size of 5.5. The assessment included 86 of the most underserved vulnerable households. Teams of social workers,9 women, were responsible for the data collection. GBV specialist was consulted throughout the planning, design, analysis and interpretation of the assessment. Among the assessed population, 93% are abjectly poor and other 3.5 % found poor. The assessed population included 24.7 % females,51.5%children, 2.3 % elderly, 4.6 % people with disabilities, and 2.8 % pregnant/lactating women.According to the assessment results, 100% of the families reported food insecurity concerns while experiencing negative coping mechanisms, including: - Reducing the number of meals by all household members,- Skipping the entire day (no meal),- Asking children to work for cash/do petty trading,- Sent females (women or girls) to serve at homes,- Doing dangerous/undesirable/illegal jobs or activities (such as begging or rubble collection in the buffer zone), and- Sending children elsewhere to eat/regrouped family members for meals.Before Covid-19 pandemic, 33.7% of households reported no access to any source of income and are totally dependent on aid and the percentage increased to be 59.3% as a result of the lockdown caused by Covid-19 pandemic. 36 % of the main breadwinner is female, 4.7 % are elderlies, and 5.8 % of the main breadwinners are people with disabilities. According to the assessment results, 73.3% reported their inability to access their immediate food needs, and 87.3% of them depended on debts to obtain access to food needs.Another assessment was conducted by AISHA in Gaza and North GAZA addressing the prevalence of GBV concerns, and women access to protection services. The assessment involved 100 women through a phone survey. Several key informants were interviewed. The GBV assessment results revealed high risks of GBV. Women get exposed to various types of violence including physical, sexual, economic and denied inheritance. Poor access to GBV related services was recognized and reported by consulted women.

2 - Activities

The elaboration of the project activities had considered the conducted needs assessment, the coordination with the clusters, a market assessment, and further consultations with the target beneficiaries regarding their priorities and the proper response mechanisms with particular focus on women and children. The project activities focus on GBV/SGBV survivors and prioritized female-headed households, individuals with physical disabilities, the elderly, and other highly vulnerable individuals and groups who are more likely to resort to negative coping strategies. The project design had been informed by a market assessment for the main food items of the vouchers. The assessment revealed the availability of the identified food items. No inflation was recorded. The project activities include Food Aid Component: 1. Distribution of e-vouchers (600 family x 5 months vouchers). The project will provide food assistance to the families in reference to the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) per individual which was developed by food cluster. The value of MEB in Gaza City is 148 ILS, equal to 43 USD. % 41 of the minimum expenditure baskets is Food, which is equal to 17.51 USD. The project will provide food assistance to 600 women survivors/victims of GBV/SGBV “nominated by Aisha, the average number of their family members is 5.5 members (600 HHs*5.5 members/family = 3,300 individuals). They will be provided with a monthly top-up of the monthly minimum food expenditure per individual (17.51 USD) for a 5-month duration. Each family will receive a rechargeable credit card that MAAN will recharge on a monthly bases according to the number of family members, using the Red-Rose System. The beneficiaries will use the credit card to purchase their Vegetables, protein, and grocery needs from the pre-identified suppliers. Two rounds of food distribution will be implemented, each round will target 300 families for five months period.GBV Component:1. AISHA will select (200 women (50 are women with a disability) and 100 children (50 girls 9-12 years, 50 boys)) survivors of GBV in Gaza and North Gaza. The selection will be based on a minimum standard of GBV sub-cluster group. As Aisha will detect 300 cases through Aisha hotline, Aisha FB, Zahr online counselling and case management system. 2. Provide Case management support utilizing remote modality and face to face according to the needs of women and children to the detected 300 GBV cases (200 women, 50 girls and 50 boys); including Psycho-social support to the selected 300 GBV survivors using HAWWA Zahara manual, case management modules, remote counselling through remote modality following Aisha latest developed manual of COVID-19 and SRHR, individual counselling to (200 women (50 are women with a disability),50girls and 50 boys ), dignity kits and remote group sessions following Hawwa Zahra manual for GBV during crisis time each group of 10 persons will attend 5 sessions utilizing ICT tools like ZOOM, WhatsApp, MS Teams, etc. 3. Provide spatialized mental health (psychotherapy and medication) to 200 GBV cases (100 women ( 30 are women with a disability) and 100 children) referred by case managers to AISHA community centre for mental health and substance abuse. 4. Family therapy to 30 GBV cases including family sessions, individual therapy sessions and group sessions. 5. Provide emergency health care to (100 women (30 are women with a disability) and 50 girls) women including SRHR services, the women will be referred through case managers. 6. Provide legal services to the 150 women including legal consultation, mediation, court representations and legal awareness through workshops. 7. provide cash vouchers to 70 women survivors of GBV and their families in order to rent homes 8. Conduct awareness-raising workshops (for (200 women, 100 girls) (20 workshops; 15 persons each) on Gender, GBV, women’s rights and on SRHR using the three modules 1,2,3 Aisha has developed with SC, the three modules of SRHR include packages for sexual and reproductive rights for children. 9. Conduct awareness-raising workshops for men's families of targeted women to involve 300 men (20 workshops), the workshop will raise awareness about PSEA. 10. Provide capacity building training on SGBV/GBV/ PSEA for NPA staff.In the context of C-19, NPA, MA’AN and AISHA have completed the procurement process for PPEs, trained their staff on Occupational health and safety for workers in the context of COVID-19 and with full attention to the guidance and instructions received from MOH and WHO, the activities will be implemented considering not to expose the health and safety of staff and beneficiaries at risk in case of the outbreak of C-19 continues. If another lockdown is imposed, the partners will undertake the remote modalities, and this includes using digital platform for beneficiaries who have access to the internet, other beneficiaries can be reached by phone to ensure their access to services or they can use the hotline system. The implementation plan will be updated and adapted following all protection measures and with full attention to COVID-19 related risk mitigation measures.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

In coordination with the two implementing partners; AISHA and MAAN, NPA will ensure that affected populations’ input and feedback are considered during the project life. NPA, MAAN and Aisha have several channels through (Landlines and mobile numbers, complaint boxes or emails) for any feedback or complaint from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. All complaints' channel will be available for all people where everyone can communicate their complaints via remotely channel in case of the lockdown related to C-19 is imposed. Beneficiaries also have the chance to voice their concerns or inquiries when they receive the assistants as the project staff will be present during the implementation of activities.Cooperating partners have M&E units to measure the satisfaction of beneficiaries along with the project through proactively soliciting feedback from targeted groups on how will the interventions meet their needs and expectations using FGDs, satisfaction forms and interviews. Beneficiary satisfaction measurement enables beneficiaries to make their voices heard and allows the project team to introduce adjustments as required to improve the satisfaction and effectiveness of the actions. . In addition, MA’AN as the main partner has developed accessible complaints and feedback handling system which includes clear procedures to guarantee the accuracy of receiving, recording and documenting the complaints. The system includes restricted procedures to ensure privacy, confidentiality where the complaint submission ways are safe and easily accessible for both women and men. the system includes very specific procedures to deal with the gender-sensitive complaints in order to maintain the privacy and confidentiality.All these elements feed into routine quarterly assessments that are drawn up to track the effectiveness of impact and provide opportunities for adaptation of approach. A final evaluation will be done at the end of the project cycle, as a basis for future programming.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The cooperating partner MAAN in coordination with AISHA will assist the needs of GBV/SGBV survivors, this will be in addition to a detailed risk register that captures all possible risks to our beneficiaries with a details mitigation plan for each and every risk. Accordingly, we will select the most vulnerable food insecure households based on needs alone, without discrimination by contributing to their safety, dignity, and integrity through ensuring that they receive assistance free of charge. NPA will take all necessary measures to prevent conflicts within target groups and other stockholders and will make efforts to prevent corruption or any type of exclusion or violence during project implementation. NPA is committed to the humanitarian principle of ‘do no harm’ and has designed this project’s activities to empower beneficiaries without jeopardizing their wellbeing. Special considerations regarding age, gender, disability, and displacement, among others, will be considered at all stages of the project. The project is taking a comprehensive protection plan in the process. This includes: The selection of GBV survivors will be ensuring the confidentiality of beneficiaries, and will be taking place through a very well-established process. Any emerging need that is not addressed through project activities will be going through the established referral mechanisms of NPA, MAAN, and AISHA, and in close coordination with other stakeholders. In addition, full coordination will continue with the protection cluster throughout the project activities, to ensure that no overlapping of work or exposing beneficiaries to unnecessary duplication. The project will also address any needs that are taking place at the children in the GBV survivors’ families.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Protection concerns are integrated into all activities to ensure that people most in need can access assistance in a safe, dignified and equitable manner. The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need with all its interventions. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted. The project will ensure vulnerable people have access to project assistance including women, people with disabilities and elderly through tailored activities to their needs and alternative at the distribution points to ensure their easy and safe access to assistance. The project will also ensure to share information on the project details with beneficiaries and stakeholders, using appropriate communication tools. The project will seek cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be countered by the vulnerable groups during project implementation. All data collected for the project is disaggregated by sex, age and disabilities, which will help to improve the equal and impartial access to assistance. The implementing partners will follow the guidelines in their "Risk management plan including risk analysis and risk mitigation" and "Security and Safety Manual" to protect the lives of their staff during emergencies and in the context of C-19. The case management will be provided by AISHA as multi-sectorial services at an adequate time when all women and girls and their families can realistically access them. in addition to facilitating access to the services for persons with reduced mobility (persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) through making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. – also facilitates access to MHPSS services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g. the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled) through providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations. Also, the two implementing partners will ensure access of beneficiaries in ARAs to the project services and AISHA will use its helplines to facilitate access of women and girls in ARAs to GBV services to leave no one behind.
