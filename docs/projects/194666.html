Project Summary

South Sudan continues to be affected by conflict in some areas and the threat of conflict in others. This situation, coupled with a longstanding environment of poverty, lack of infrastructure and food insecurity, has caused mass displacement. This has occurred in an atmosphere where WASH coverage is also still very low at 41% coverage with basic water supply, 11 % coverage with basic sanitation and with 63% of the population still practicing open defecation according to the UNICEF-WHO 2019 Joint Monitoring Programme report. The situation is further compounded by the lack of sustainable operational and maintenance systems for existing WASH facilities and infrastructure because of governance challenges.
This protracted conflict has continued to affect the already poor rate of access to basic facilities, including safe water, in turn, increased the community's vulnerability to waterborne diseases, mainly diarrhea. Furthermore, the exceptionally heavy rains since July 2019 attributed to heavy flooding in the country that has washed away roads and bridges, destroyed homes, schools, and health facilities; and submerged boreholes and latrines thereby contaminating water sources and risking outbreaks of waterborne diseases. Together with poor hygiene awareness, this has greatly put the community’s health at risk.
Citing from the Flood List published on 12 October 2022, As of 10 October, as many as 118,000 people were impacted by the floods in Unity State. This is a slight fall since 30 September when 155,000 people were affected. However, flood waters began increasing once again in early October in some counties of Unity State. Panyijiar is one of the flood-affected counties in the state, Welthungerhilfe (WHH) conducted a rapid assessment in July 2022 highlighting challenges in the provision of WASH facilities in the county. The findings indicate that 63% of the boreholes require minor repairs while 35% of the inventory requires major rehabilitation. The assessment also found that 74% of people rely on boreholes for their water while the rest rely on unprotected sources thereby posing the danger that they consume potentially contaminated water. The most common use of the water is for bathing, general cleaning, cooking, drinking and laundry. In terms of sanitation, 79% of the households do not have latrines and of the 21% that have them, 94% of these do hot have handwashing facilities hence the two issues need to be addressed together. 90% indicated they practice open defecation, whereby the problem of open defecation is compounded by poverty-induced practices that have normalized open defecation to the point where some who have access to toilets will shun them based on cultural beliefs hence the need for intensified education in this project. In the 18 schools assessed, latrines stances for persons living with disabilities are only 1.9% of the available stances. There is a deficit in terms of protected water sources and sanitation facilities, however, it has been observed that there needs to be considerable investment in the construction of climate-proof sanitation facilities as the flood situation is likely to continue for some time to come. Mayom Payam has the lowest percentage (9%) of people sourcing water from a borehole but there is a sign of people still using untreated surface water across all assessed Payam.
In this regard, WHH continues to prioritize the provision of accessible, improved, and sustainable WASH services which are essential for all humanitarian, recovery, and development interventions. The proposed project is designed to enhance access to WASH services and facilities and help to contain and mitigate the potential outbreaks of waterborne diseases as current access to sanitation and hygiene facilities remains below SPHERE standards.

1 - Needs

The continuous heavy flooding situation in Panyijiar county has severely affected WASH facilities. Open defecation is still widely practiced in the area and flood waters become a breeding place for WASH related diseases such as cholera and typhoid, among others. Constructed latrines have also been flooded, rendering them unusable and becoming agents of diseases through overflowing their fecal matter into the environment. During the floods, waterpoints became contaminated with polluted flood waters. As a result, a number of previous functioning and safe waterpoints are no longer accessible as they are no longer a safe source of water for household consumption. The lack of access to improved water and sanitation and poor hygiene behaviors is the principal cause of water-related diseases. In Panyijiar, the risk of contracting waterborne diseases like diarrhea, cholera, hepatitis, typhoid, or Guinea worms is attributed to limited awareness of key hygiene behaviors amongst the majority, such as hand washing with soap, as well as poor WASH infrastructures. Furthermore, the 2021 IRNA flood assessment report also found that women and girls face challenges in ensuring safe and dignified menstrual hygiene practices. Most affected households were found to lack access to basic hygiene and sanitation kits exposing them to diseases and even up to now, many in the community still practice open defecation. To restore functionality of the water points and increase access to safe water in the affected communities water quality testing will be required. Based on the results of the testing disinfection of the waterpoints will be needed. However, access to safe water supply is still a challenge in a number of communities and the need to rehabilitate non-functioning waterpoints and drill new boreholes is necessary to prevent people from consuming contaminated water. In response to the flooding crisis, WHH continues to prioritize the provision of accessible, improved, and sustainable WASH services which are essential for all humanitarian, recovery, and development interventions. The proposed project is designed to directly address critical and unmet WASH needs, delivering life-saving water, sanitation and hygiene solutions to vulnerable communities living in Panyijiar County. It will respond to the WASH needs of the most vulnerable segment of the community which comprises women, children and people living with disabilities, as well as build their resistance capacity to deal with various shocks. The provision of WASH services will not only ensure improved access to sufficient and safe water but will in parallel tackle risks related to disease outbreaks – including waterborne diseases such as cholera. WASH actions will address good hygiene practices through hygiene promotion and awareness campaigns, contributing towards community first-line defense against illness. The increasing concern about the potential outbreak of cholera requires more focus to improve community awareness of prevention and increased support to the local health system for a better-coordinated response. The project will benefit from the existing WHH presence in Panyijiar, and the technical capacities and skillsets possessed by WHH staff to ensure the delivery of a well-targeted, evidence-based, and high-impact intervention. The project will also link WASH with Nutrition as it will be implemented in collaboration with ongoing WHH activities in food security. Moreover, it will contribute to the core strategic objectives of the South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2022, particularly HRP Strategic Objectives 2: Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5 and Strategic Objective 3: Enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement in the respect of their rights, and build resilience to shocks and stresses.

2 - Project activities

In consultation with the district administration within the county, the proposed project will construct 4 boreholes in areas identified to have unsafe water sources. The boreholes will be drilled by locally trained technicians with hand-drilling machines owned by WHH and will be equipped with India Mark II handpumps. WHH will assess the water coverage in villages in identified Payam to identify the most critical and underserved communities, and the 4 locations will be selected based on this assessment in consultation with the community representatives (chiefs, women representatives, representative PLWD and youth representatives) and the local authorities. The project will further rehabilitate 14 boreholes within the county, this will come after a technical assessment of the 14 trained handpump mechanics who will repair 14 boreholes in selected communities. The locations for rehabilitation will be determined by assessment of the water coverage and most vulnerable communities. Towards ensuring water quality in target areas, WHH will procure a water testing kit and engage a lab technician from the Ministry of water to train water technicians in the 2 locations (Ganyiel and Nyal) within the county. They will be responsible for overseeing the sample taking and conducting the water testing, and where needed water points will be chlorinated to ensure access to safe water. 20 water management committees will be formed and trained on operation and maintenance. The proposed project will enhance community and institutional hygiene promotion via the distribution of WASH kits that will enable them to implement key hygiene messages after the trainings received by the community hygiene promoters. The distributed hygiene kits will help households for 3 months to improve hygiene behaviors, and consist of a jerrycan, cup, soap, aqua tab, handwashing containers and a bucket. A leaflet with the safe water chain (pictograms and local language) will be included. Households will be selected based on vulnerability criteria (including female-headed, elderly, PLWD and economic status). This activity will benefit a total of 6300 people. The trained community hygiene promotors on Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) will distribute 400 dignity kits consisting of a leaflet (pictograms and local language), 3 panties, soap, and laundry soap, and 1 packet of reusable sanitary pads (lasts 1 year). On monthly basis, the community hygiene promotors will visit the households and advice women and girls on the correct use of reusable pads and questions related to menstrual hygiene management. Furthermore, 14 community hygiene promotors will be identified and trained on key hygiene behaviors in the household and cascade the learnings to the community via conducting hygiene awareness sessions in their communities and conducting weekly monitoring visits of the selected and trained households (20 households per hygiene promotor over a period of 3 months). Moreover, community leaders will be selected and trained on early warning, preparedness and response to flooding and the potential outbreak of diseases. The training will be held in 2 locations of the county, preferably, in Nyal and Ganyiel. To reinforce the integration with nutrition as per cluster targeting guidance, female nutrition volunteers will be identified and trained on hygiene promotion in communities, in return, they will conduct hygiene trainings in the selected communities of the county. Also, the project will select interested and committed households and support them in modifying or constructing flood-proof sanitary latrines as a durable solution for surmounting flood-led challenges. Two toilet blocks (female & male) will be constructed with 2 stances. The project anticipates that approximately 200 individuals will benefit from these toilets. Complementary to the emergency latrines, WHH SSD will provide handwashing facilities to ensure that hygiene measures are maintained.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Multiple channels of communication including hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint desks at all project activity sites will be availed to communities. The project will additionally organize regular feedback meetings for special interest groups at the community level. Project staff will be trained on how to handle special interest groups’ complaints with complaints desk and hotlines being made gender and age sensitive.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project inception meetings to be attended by communities and stakeholders will be held at all locations. Issues to be covered in the meetings will include an explanation of project objectives, methodology, activities and beneficiary selection procedures, responsibilities, and the rights thereof. Participants will additionally be sensitized on the project complaints and feedback mechanism and its application. Contact details and reporting mechanism tools will be shared, and their use explained. Regular reminders on the importance of the complaints and feedback mechanism will be communicated during activity implementation and monitoring. Project communication including adverts and visibility materials will have messaging on CFRM as well as the reporting hotline contacts.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

At the point of engagement, all WHH staff are required to undertake the PSEA course as part of their induction. WHH staff are contractually bound to appreciate and disseminate the organization’s policy on PSEA. Refresher trainings for staff will be held at project inception and staff prepared to disseminate to communities and report on violations. Communities will be informed during project inception meetings and all contact sessions with project staff. Moreover, reporting on PSEA will be an integral part of community trainings on the feedback and complaint mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries will be incorporated into the project management structures to ensure there are full participants in project implementation oversight and decision-making. Beneficiary feedback will be collected through feedback mechanisms and directly during deliberately organized feedback meetings at the community level. Welthungerhilfe has a practice of ensuring that all projects and locations have a feedback analysis committee chaired by respective project managers. The committee analyses feedback immediately after it is received and decides on responses. Responses are then directed to the responsible units for implementation which includes adjustment of approaches and communication to affected parties. The project manager follows up to ensure implementation and refers issues to higher levels and third parties as may be deemed necessary and appropriate. The response mechanism has clear guidelines for protecting information sources.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will construct context- and climate-sensitive facilities in case of future disasters that cause displacement. The project beneficiaries will also be linked to other ongoing WHH projects that are working on creating dikes that prevent flood water causing massive population displacement.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The IRNA assessment report of August 2021, and other relevant assessments found that Women and girls are exposed to risks of GBV as they walk long distances collecting water and firewood from relatively non-flooded villages. Through the rehabilitation, of old water facilities and installation of new infrastructure, the project will contribute towards reducing cases of GBV as the provision of water in nearby locations ensures their safety and security through reduced exposure to potential abuse.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) released on February 2022 identified the lack of access to WASH services as a contributor to an increased likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition, and school dropout. In that regard, the proposed project will provide WASH services in the form of water facilities, sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion thereby contributing to a reduction in the overall humanitarian needs of people in the targeted area.
