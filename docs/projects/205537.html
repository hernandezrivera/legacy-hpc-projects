Project Summary

Grassroots Relief and Development Agency (GREDA) intends to improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 5,222 emergency - affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls/boys) disaggregated by sex and age in four targeted Counties in Western Equatoria State in South Sudan as follows:-
(1) IDPs ( 500 Male and 520 female) aged between 3-17years (2) Returnees ( 500 Male and 500 female) aged between 3-17 years (3) Spontaneous refugees ( 450 Male) aged between 3-17 years (4) Host community children including children with disabilities (400 Male 1,300 female) aged between 3-17years) and (5) Youth (532 boys and 520 girls) aged between 5-17years in Maridi County, Mundri West,Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
The prioritized objective is:-
1: To Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 5,222 emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys) in Maridi, Mundri West,Mundri East and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State.
The main activities to be carried out to realize this objective and achieve the overall goal will include:1.1.
Conduct sensitization to raise community awareness on the systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.2. Identify and select children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) to access and attend schools in affected areas in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.3. Identify temporary learning spaces established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2023 in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.4. Rehabilitate temporary learning spaces or schools in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.5. Organize back to school/learning campaigns in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.6. Train teachers in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties and update them on Psycho social Support, Child Protection and referral mechanisms for a better service delivery.
1. 7. Provide children age (5-17) and teachers with teaching and learning materials in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties.
1.8. Involve the communities in design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation on the planned or an on-going education project in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties

1. Needs

Eleven years after independence and four years after the signing of the revitalized agreement for the resolution of conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS 2018) the people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions.Their situation is further worsened by endemic violent conflicts, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks exacerbated by flooding resulting in the phenomena which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education. Humanitarian access to essential services such as education, remains a challenge in an already complex humanitarian deteriorating context. Narrowing it to Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties in Western Equatoria State the counties badly affected by the multiple conflicts in South Sudan. The multiple conflicts were (1) The conflict between the host Community farmers and cattle herders from the neighboring States (2) The conflict between the Government and Sudan People Liberation Army in-Opposition (SPLA-IO) (3)The conflict between the government and armed groups in Equatoria such National Salvation Army impacted negatively and forced the majority of the populations not to access basic services due to break down of service provision mechanisms. These incursions; within or around aforementioned Counties forced many schools to be nonfunctional hence affecting enrollment and continuity of education for early learners. In a recent assessment by GREDA in Maridi,Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties between August/September 2022 an excess of 10,000 school age children are out of school due to hostilities, poverty and erratic weather patterns that have immensely contributed to drop outs, due to lack of learning structures,and issues surrounding access and safety for adolescent girls. This situation is compounded by the increase in the demand for education by IDPs/returnees children.Though all children who have been displaced are vulnerable, adolescent girls and boys remain particularly vulnerable to participation in dangerous activities and recruitment by armed actors. Adolescent girls are at risk of early and forced marriage, as well as sexual abuse and exploitation due to the low value placed on the girl child. The closure of learning spaces has left separated and unaccompanied minors without opportunities for case management. Education indicators remain amongst the worst in the Country, Far fewer girls make it to the last grade of primary education. For every 100 boys completing primary school, only 63 girls completed the cycle (UIS, 2018). Cultural and social factors play a role in keeping girls from transitioning from primary to secondary education,particularly following the onset of puberty. South Sudan is ranked among the top twenty countries in the world with the highest prevalence rates for child marriage, and, according to the 2010 NBS survey, 45% of women, ages 20-49, were married before they were 18-years-old and 7% of women, ages 15-49, were married before turning 15-years-old. Women ages 20-24, over half (52%) were married before they were 18-years-old. There is 20% of women with at least a secondary education were married, compared to 59% of those with no education (NBS, 2010). Globally, girls who have completed secondary education are six times less likely to become child brides and have better health and economic outcomes throughout their lives; under-five mortality rates also tend to be higher in child marriages (UNFPA, 2018). The challenge of unqualified teachers within the Counties has continually been a detrimental factor to realizing education goals as many are not conversant with appropriate child-centered pedagogy. This project aims at increasing basic accessible, equitable, protective, quality education in emergency to young children in Maridi, Mundri West, Mundri East and Mvolo Counties hard-to-reach areas in South Sudan.
