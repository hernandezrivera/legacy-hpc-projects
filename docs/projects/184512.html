Project Summary

GOAL will support the provision of comprehensive health & nutrition services in 21 Primary health facilities across South Kordofan (Dillinge, Habila, Abukershola, Rashad, and Talodi localities including support for stabilization Center in Talodi and Habila) and 18 clinics in North Darfur (Kutum, Al Waha, and Umbaro localities), in areas with high numbers of conflict-affected households (including long-term IDPs, vulnerable host communities, and some returnees. GOAL will continue support to rural and hard-to-reach facilities supported during 2021 and is ready to expand in South Kordofan to previously inaccessible areas as access continues to open there. GOAL also provides community health through Care Groups, school health clubs, community health promotion, defaulter tracing for RH and OTP services, and the Nutrition Impact and Positive Practice (NIPP) approach to prevent and treat Moderate Acute Malnutrition. GOAL will provide basic comprehensive care in supported clinics, and GOAL will continue to closely coordinate through the Ministry of Health and the Health and Nutrition Sectors in El Fashir, Kadugli and Khartoum, and will also engage in ongoing coordination with other partners in each locality to ensure no overlap. GOAL will continue to support the SMOH to adequately respond to outbreaks, training for local health staff, provision of drugs and medical equipment. GOAL will support the Local health department to strengthen the health system GOAL also integrates WASH into its health & nutrition programme - COVID-19 prevention measures and in clinics and communities, and ongoing support to some rural isolation centers, referral of cases, transportation of samples, surveillance and RRT logistical support.

1 - Needs

Kutum & Al waha localities remains a fragile context with recent history of conflict, remaining estimated 48,000 IDPs living in the locality, and according to the HeRAMS in 2018, 74% of the functional health facilities are those supported by GOAL. GOAL is providing health care services to 56% of the population of Kutum and Al Waha, and ensuring over 80% of the EWARN sites. The 1st semester of 2021 HIS data from Kutum & Al Waha show that communicable diseases (malaria, diarrhea, and ARI) make up 74% of totalmorbidities consulted. The persistence of poor nutrition, sanitation and lack of hygiene contributes to these morbidities and to malnutrition. During the first semester of 2021 for the EPI , the coverage for Penta 3 is 73.61% which is an improvement from 62% in 2017 while that of measles was 75.773% up from 65% in 2017 in Kutum and Al Waha localities; despite this improvement the rates still fall short of the 90% target that is set by the MoH in Sudan, thus continued support is needed. A 2019 MICS survey found only 34%of respondents washing hands at critical times, and only 27.6% of children aged 6-24 months ate 4+ food groups (WHO's minimum standard for this age). Findings from a Barrier Analysis that was conducted among both Doers (people washing hands at appropriate times) and non-Doers showed that lack of water and soap as an obstacle for 54% non-Doers and `washing hands before preparing food' is the most difficult key moment to remember or do. Perception concerning the severity or risk of family members becoming ill with diarrhoea if the respondent does not wash hands with soap or ash and water at all of the key times defined as before eating, after defecation, before cooking, after handling child faeces, before child feeding came up as a determining factor for practicing the behaviour. GOAL continues to support response to various outbreaks, including COVID, measles, Hepatitus E, and dengue fever and other diseases that have been identified in 2021 and/or are seasonal outbreaks. The health status of the 30,600 population in Rashad remains critical according to data collected by GOAL in March 2020. The HIS at the locality health department indicates high levels of morbidity with ARI at 40%, Malaria 20% and diarrheal diseases at an equal high of 20% hence leading to very low health outcomes. These three categories constitute thehighest burden of morbidity in Rashad. Immunization coverage and RH indicators remain low. At 63.8% for example, measles vaccinations remain significantly below the 95% level required to confer herd immunity on the population and set as the SPHERE minimum standard, leaving communities vulnerable to outbreaks. Talodi hosts 13,968 IDPs among a population of 48,862. An additional 30,000 gold miners travel and live there for work, and mistrust between the population, mining companies, and government remains highas evidenced by strikes there in 2019. IDPs and vulnerable populations are concentrated in often unsanitary conditions near Talodi town. The catchment population where GOAL supports 3 health facilities and a Stabilization Center is 21,327 people. Data from the locality health department indicated that just like Rashad, Malaria, diarrhea and ARI were the main morbidities, and children under 5 are particularly affected. Among all diarrhea cases, under-5 children represented 63.1%; for malaria cases 48.8%, and for ARI cases52.3%. The targeted areas for nutrition interventions not only have a high need but also poor access to nutrition service provision accessible by vulnerable households, largely due to health facilities not providing OTP services. The needs assessment identified alarmingly high rates of malnutrition in the target areas and indicated the likelihood of further deterioration in the food security and nutrition situation across the two states. The acute malnutrition PIN for under-five is about 3million and PLW is 905,004.
