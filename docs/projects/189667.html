Project Summary

The Project aims at improving equitable access to and utilization of health services through three fixed primary health care facilities including Turu PHCU, Nyatok PHCU and Pinythor PHCU in Maiwut County. To scale-up health care services in Maiwut , NIDO will establish five MMUs and ICCM to serve the hard-to-reach population with lifesaving health care services targeting a total population of 15000 people of which 4500 are women, 2700 men, 3600 girls, 3600 boys and 600 people with disabilities. NIDO will recruit and deploy qualified health workers including clinical officers, nurses, midwives, community health workers, and vaccinators to all supported fixed facilities and mobile medical units. Boma health workers will be selected at the community level to implement Boma health initiative and ICCM activities across the four counties. NIDO will build the capacity of health workers to ensure the quality of health services provision. Topics to be trained include, integrated disease surveillance and case management, immunization in practice (IIP) and effective Vaccines monitoring (EVM), BEmoNC, Mental health Gap action program (mhGAP), SGBV/CMR, IMNCI, and use of malaria rapid diagnostic test (mRDT). At the community level, the Boma health workers will also be trained on case Management using Boma health initiative training materials and guidelines for effective ICCM management. The intervention will further focus on continuous screening and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases through patient consultations. The intervention will also provide the management of injuries, antenatal care, skilled deliveries, postnatal care, and clinical management of rape (SGBV services) in the health facilities and mobile medical units. The intervention will also Integrate and strengthen mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and services for people with disabilities. The project will continue streamlining and strengthening the referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), sexual and gender-based violence survivors and, maternal and child health emergencies. NIDO’s health team, through this response, will conduct routine and outreach immunization services to children under 59 months of age. Throughout the provision of health services, NIDO will ensure Covid-19 prevention and appropriate response activities are integrated while continue providing essential lifesaving health services. All fixed health facilities; MMUs and ICCM will be equipped with IPC/WASH materials, PPEs, and IEC materials on Covid-19 to further strengthen the prevention. EVD prevention, surveillance, and response readiness to mitigate the impact of an Ebola outbreak will be continued as part of disease outbreak, prevention and response. The project will integrate WASH and Nutrition into health programming to increase community mobilization and sensitization on health-related messaging including COVID-19 prevention, availability of immunization services, ANC, skilled deliveries, CMR services, and MHPSS. This project will engage all communities in the targeted counties throughout the implementation stages to enable the sustainability of the program. Patient Exit Interviews will be conducted at fixed facilities and MMUs. NIDO will also implement community feedback mechanisms such as regular community consultative meetings, feedback boxes and AAP groups to continue providing feedback and complaints. For the successful implementation of this project, NIDO will engage the communities during the selection of Mobile medical (MMUs) and community support for storage spaces for the essential drugs and MMU equipment. The selected MMUs and health facilities (PHCCs and PHCUs) will be equipped with essential medical equipment, drugs and test kits. All healthcare staff will be trained in the core protection principles, including 'Do No Harm'.”

1 - Needs

The current scale and coverage of the humanitarian response across Upper Nile remains inadequate to meet humanitarian health needs. Insecurity remains unpredictable; sporadic clashes between the opposition group and government, inter-communal fighting including cattle raiding and revenge attack have caused spikes in tensions, displacement, and loss of lives. The protracted conflict and its aftermath in eastern Upper Nile has reduced the availability of and access to health services, safe water, reduced food production, disrupted livelihoods and markets, consequently exacerbating morbidity and mortality amongst the most vulnerable and remotely located communities, especially affecting women, children and people with disabilities. According to FSNMS quantitative component report 2021, the main barriers in accessing health care in Maiwut County include flooding, insecurity, Closure of facilities due to violence and long distance. Whilst, HNO 2022 affirmed that Maiwut County is in extreme food insecure. This further impacted the health situation of the populationDespite health intervention in eastern Upper Nile, Malaria and typhoid remained the main perceived health problems (REACH Jan-Mar 2021 Situation overview). A Joint Rapid Needs Assessment in Maiwut where NIDO participated, highlighted Malaria, ARI, Diarrhea, and Skin infections the most common morbidities. Children under 5years (CU5) and PLW are particularly vulnerable to diseases. The majority of communities in Maiwut, County is cut off by swamps limiting their access to health services. During dry season, the community keep on migrating to the river site in search of water as such community access to health care become challenging. Hence, Lack of immunization, antenatal care, skilled deliveries, and postnatal care services to these communities. The porous border and limited capacity to curtail COVID-19 spread will likely increase the risk of transmission in eastern upper Nile, exacerbated by the low Covid-19 vaccination coverage. There remain significant protection concerns in Maiwut County as a result of inter-communal conflicts and proximity to cantonment sites, with an extremely limited number of actors providing any form of protection services. Women, girls, people with disabilities and children extremely suffer the most as they are exposed to multiple protection risks including mental health problems, psychological distress, sexual gender-based violence(SGBV), and other abuses. Lack of CMR services due to limited access to health care services. To address the above needs, NIDO proposes to provide lifesaving emergency essential health care services to the communities of Mawut County to reduce excess morbidity and mortality. NIDO will scale-up the existing primary health care services in Maiwut through three fix health facilities and five Mobile medical units (MMUs). NIDO will increase its MMUs to ensure increase access to health care services in hard to reach Vulnerable communities. Disease outbreaks will be detected early, responded to promptly and adequate surveillance systems will be in place through Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS). Immunization, ANC, skilled deliveries and postnatal care services will be provided. NIDO will ensure COVID-19 control and prevention activities are integrated while continue providing essential lifesaving health services. EVD prevention, surveillance, and response readiness to mitigate the impact of an Ebola outbreak will be continued as part of disease outbreak, prevention and response. Referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors, and maternal and child health emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and services for persons with disabilities will be integrated and strengthened. The project will continue providing curative health services to the population.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

NIDO will use the beneficiaries’ feedbacks to shape and adapt into project design and implementation. NIDO will undertake routine Patient Exit Satisfaction Interviews to get beneficiaries feedback on beneficiaries’ satisfaction on the services they received. NIDO will then address feedback received and will adapt its programme to address any concerns or gaps highlighted. During monthly consultation meetings, NIDO will then provide feedback to the beneficiaries on any changes made to the project based on feedback received. Additionally, NIDO's MEAL team will conduct quarterly FGDs with community members to gain feedback on NIDO's health project activities. NIDO's response to feedback will be shared back with community leader. Action plans will be developed with NIDO's project team and where appropriate actions will be followed up to improve the implementation of the project

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our integrated program (Health, Nutrition, WASH and protection) programs. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. Exit audit is conducted at the facilities and MMU to every beneficiaries and all these will capture feedback from individuals with disabilities and others. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1) Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2) Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3) Individual interview form, 4) Suggestion boxes installed at all supported facilities and communities. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. By using a variety of methods to receive feedback, NIDO will ensure that feedback from vulnerable and marginalized groups is captured. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites. IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the project leads through appropriate channels.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All NIDO staff and volunteers who are based in the four locations are trained on NIDO's PSEA and safeguarding policies, and ways to report incidents. Each NIDO field office has safeguarding champions who are given more intensive training on NIDO's safeguarding and PSEA approach. During project kick-off meetings with communities NIDO will inform communities of the PSEA policy, who the safeguarding champions are, what constitutes PSEA, emphasize that all project activities are delivered free of charge and that beneficiaries should not be asked to accept anything in exchange for any goods or services. NIDO will place posters and visuals outlining NIDO's policy and where community members can report at distribution sites. Additionally, NIDO will have a help desk manned by NIDO staff at each distribution site, to collect complaints and feedback and to oversee distributions. Finally, NIDO's independent MEAL team and project management staff from Juba will conduct ad-hoc monitoring visits to project sites, meeting with community members and will reiterate the training and NIDO's position on PSEA

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NIDO will conduct sensitization and mobilization prior to start of project, through consultations with community leaders, both formal and informal. Chiefs, project management committees, Boma health Committees, women and youth groups will be provided with full details of assistance including health and hygiene promotion activities to be provided. The project introductory meetings in all project areas will include explanation and orientation and how to make feedback and complaints. Moreover; NIDO staff and local authority will be trained on how to orient, collect, record beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received, as well as how and when to respond. All staff will be trained on core data protection and confidentiality principals also. NIDO has already conducted an AAP study in each location proposed which asked a statistically significant sample size what the preferred means of both receiving information and giving feedback were. Overall, individuals felt more comfortable giving feedback verbally through NIDO staff or community leaders. Interestingly, females preferred female community leaders or direct to NIDO staff. Further, all participants stated they preferred to have information shared verbally from NIDO staff, and then community leaders. A significantly fewer cohort stated they preferred giving feedback to local authorities. The verbal nature of feedback and information was emphasized throughout and is unsurprising given the literacy levels of the wider population in all counties, and the limited/no access to electricity and mobile phone connection for more advanced feedback mechanisms e.g SMS, hotlines or email. NIDO will ensure to have the following community engagement throughout the project lifecycle• Project kick-off and close meetings with Community Leaders, women leaders, and local authorities, • Monthly FGDs with disaggregated cohorts of the target population • In-depth interviews with sample beneficiaries • Patient exit interviews to measure satisfaction • Case studies. • Complaints hot-desk and AAP champions at trainings and mass health education campaigns

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, NIDO will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All our project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Group Discussion facilitation for all group, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on our mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Executive Director, Program Manager, and Human Resource Manager. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project will continue to provide lifesaving essential health care services through fix health facilities, MMUs and BHI/ICCM, health education and promotion activities, disease surveillance, timely detection of outbreak and responded with 48hours, streamlining and strengthening the referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), sexual and gender-based violence survivors and, maternal and child health emergencies. NIDO will build the capacity of health providers to ensure quality health services provision to affected population. This improve the health situation of the affected population. NIDO has existing protection programs in 2/4 locations proposed under this intervention. These actions have established referral pathway mappings already in place - following extensive safety audits and service mapping. All project staff, regardless of sector, are trained in these referral pathways covering where possible all aspects of the referral pathway. in the other two locations, NIDO has already conducted service and referral mapping, as well as safety audits. all staff have bene trained on PFA and GBV basic concepts and are aware of the referral pathways available in each location. NIDO already has links with key protection actor in all location to refer any cases for structured case management and follow up. As NIDO is the main health actor in all locations, all SGBV referrals receive CMR from NIDO in each location. NIDO is able to conduct PSS and case management in 2/4 locations. In the remaining 2 - NIDO health staff are able to refer cases for this support. The team will provide screening; psychosocial support and referral of GBV cases to appropriate care facility. As this might require emergency intervention within 72hours for SGBV survivors, the MMU Midwife will be equipped with PEP kits and trained on its use before conducting the referral. The team will raise awareness to community about timely referral of SGBV case and where to seek care for the survivor. NIDO Protection's Team will train all healthcare staff on GBV basic concepts and GBV referral pathways, to ensure that treatment and referrals are conducted in safe, survivor centred approach.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will continue to aware the community on the early mitigation of flooding by building dike and ensuring setting in highland. NIDO will be holding community meeting on climatic mitigation with special focus on mitigating population displacement during flooding. Continuous awareness will be undertaken to various targeted communities on how to prevent flooding to affect them by ensuring they settle in high ground and building of dikes.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

One of the key drivers of malnutrition in South Sudan is poor health status, and, limited/lack of access to any form of primary or secondary healthcare for adults and children alike. By improving the health status of the population as a whole, through increasing access to curative and preventative health services - free of charge - NIDO is reducing morbidities and mortalities and enabling populations to continue working (in livelihood opportunities), and reduce likelihood of becoming malnourished as a result of disease and poor health status
