Project Summary

PUI has been implementing projects in South Sudan, in Aweil North since April 2015, through an integrated approach, combining Health, Nutrition, FSL and basic WASH interventions. In 2018, PUI has provided Community Management of Acute Malnutrition services in 15 nutrition sites, in Aweil North – including two in the disputed 14-mile are a – with funding from WFP, UNICEF, GIZ and ECHO. With the expansion of its programs since 2015, PUI has reached more beneficiaries and ensures early identification of malnutrition through outreach activities. The ratio SAM:MAM has indeed changed, with a fewer SAM cases. In 2016, the percentage of new SAM cases was 50%, while in 2018 the percentage of SAM cases decreased to 34%. Moreover, the number of children admitted in the stabilization Center has dropped dramatically. In 2016, the average number of admissions in the Stabilization Center was 26 per month, even reaching 39 admissions per month in the peak of the lean season. In 2018, the average number of admissions is 12 per month, with no peak in the lean season. The lower number of admissions in the Stabilization Center and the lower percentage of children with SAM in OTP can be associated with an increased access to OTP and TSFP and early identification of malnutrition through outreach activities. According to the latest reports, there are 5,576 SAM children, 17,004 MAM children and 23,813 MAM PLW in Aweil North expected in 2019.
Although Aweil North -and Northern Bahr el Ghazal in general- is a stable area, malnutrition remains a major public health issue, which requires continuing activities there. In 2018, PUI aims to implement a complete CMAM program, targeting and treating children under 5 years old and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) for Severe and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (SAM and MAM respectively), through OTP, TSFP and BSFP services – the latter in all of Aweil North while the two others will be in PUI’s 15 nutrition sites. Vulnerable populations will be screened (active and passive screenings) for malnutrition by PUI’s staff, including the Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) who will be trained to screen, trace, refer and sensitize community members. Children under 5, will also be screened and treated for malaria. In the meantime, CNVs, Nutrition Health Workers and members of the Mother to Mother Support Groups will be trained to disseminate individual key messages for mothers and caretakers on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Feeding (MIYCN). To complete these activities, PUI hopes to start Conditional General Food Distribution in PUI’s catchment area for its FSL activities and foster community’s participation and engagement, to lower dependence on the nutrition program and encourage resilience and communities’ future self-reliance. With the same goal in mind, PUI will also establish Project Monitoring Committees and train community members on project monitoring. Finally, to counteract the paucity of reliable secondary health and nutrition data in several parts of South Sudan, PUI plans to conduct SMART surveys, in coordination with the Nutrition Information Working Group. In line with the cluster’s strategic objective for the HRP 2019, PUI wishes “to enhance the nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and nutrition information” and thus contribute to the generation of consistent and reliable public health data.
The purpose of the project is thus to provide quality Community Management of Acute Malnutrition and prevent as much as possible malnutrition through health promotion and early identification of populations at risk. PUI will foster community participation and will aim to reinforce the community’s engagement and ownership of the services, to ensure a healthy lifestyle and productive community.

Needs

GAM rates in South Sudan and specifically in Northern Bahr el Ghazal continue to be above the emergency threshold of 15%. FSNMS report Round 20 for Northern Bahr el Ghazal showed a GAM rate in December 2017 of 15.9% (13.2 - 19.0) for children aged 6 to 59 months, despite being a time known for reflecting a typical post-harvest seasonal improvements, with expectedly lower GAM rates attributed to humanitarian assistance, food stocks from harvests, improved access to nutrition and health services, reduction in morbidity and disease outbreaks during the dry season. Moreover, the Key IPC findings released in January 2018 showed Critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM 15.0% - 29.9%) in all counties of Northern Bahr el Ghazal (except Aweil Centre). For May to July 2018 in the peak of the lean season, the whole of Northern Bahr el Ghazal was projected to experience extremely critical levels of acute malnutrition. 74% of the population in the State was also projected to be in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis – 705,000 people) and 4 (Emergency – 335,000 people) between May and July 2018. Focus Group Discussions with mother to mother support groups in early September 2018 also revealed that many men leave for Khartoum to find work and to be able to provide their families with food; however, the working and living conditions are deemed so harsh there that many hesitate to go, if it is only for a few sacks of seeds and supplies. The mothers in the Focus Groups reported to “having to fight for their food.” Acute malnutrition is indeed caused by inadequate food intake as a result of food insecurity (due to heavy rains, pests’ infections, agricultural and social practices, conflicts and displacements of populations), poor MIYCN practices and outbreaks of diarrhoea and other illnesses. Although key IPC findings for September 2018 show signs of improvement for food security and nutrition rates, 53.9% of Northern Bahr el Ghazal is still considered to be in crisis (IPC Phase 3) and emergency (IPC Phase 4). Aweil North is experiencing a food security “crisis” (IPC Phase 3!) and food insecurity would be even higher if not for the presence of humanitarian actors. The situation is also “serious” for nutrition in Aweil North for September 2018 and is expected to remain “serious” from October to December 2018. The latest analysis (GAM and FSNMS) indeed show that there are 5,576 SAM children, 17,004 MAM children and 23,813 MAM PLW in Aweil North. Food insecurity mainly affects children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), leading to increased morbidity and mortality. As undernutrition in children below 2 years old can have long lasting impacts on a child’s growth and learning abilities, food assistance to the most vulnerable populations is paramount to decreasing malnutrition rates and saving lives. By conducting early identification of malnourished populations and treating them for Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), PUI hopes to decrease their vulnerability levels and overall, admit a lower number of patients for SAM with complications.

Activities

Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) currently implements in nutrition programs and provides complete treatment for SAM and MAM in Aweil North, Northern Bahr el Ghazal in 15 nutrition sites, including 2 in the disputed 14-mile area. PUI hopes to continue its OTP and TSFP services in its 15 sites and aims to add BSFP for Aweil North, targeting children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). In 2019, PUI also aims to start conditional general food distribution (CGFD) in its 15 nutrition sites. To counter malnutrition in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, PUI develops Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) activities directly in line with all the nutrition cluster HRP strategic objectives for 2019, including:- Organize active and passive screening of all children under 5 years old and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) through outreach activities led by the Community Volunteers - objective 1 (life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk);- Provide complete treatment of SAM and MAM in PUI’s 15 nutrition sites for children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women through OTP (Outpatient Therapeutic Program) and TSFP (Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program), including referral to PUI’s Stabilization Center (Majak Kaar) - objective 1; - Provide Blanket Supplementary Feed (BSFP) services for children under 5 and PLW in Aweil North - objective 1; - Implement Conditional General Food Distribution and foster community’s participation and engagement, in PUI’s catchment area - objective 4 (increased access to integrated response for nutrition/health/WASH/FSL/protection);- Train Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) to screen, trace, refer and sensitize children under 5 and PLW for malnutrition - objective 2 (increased access to MIYCN nutrition program to prevent under-nutrition); - Train CNVs, Nutrition Health Workers and members of the Mother to Mother Support Groups to disseminate individual key messages for mothers and caretakers on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Feeding (MIYCF) - objective 2; - Carry out 3 Nutrition SMART surveys in coordination with the Nutrition Information Working Group - objective 3 (enhanced nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information for early warning and coordinated response); - All children entering the program are tested for malaria and treated when positive - objective 4; - Establish a focal point for GBV issues - objective 4; - Establish Project Monitoring Committees and train community members on project monitoring - objective 4.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

To ensure quality programming and good services, PUI prioritizes needs of the communities. They have the opportunity to influence decisions in the different phases of the program cycle management, especially during assessments and needs identification, as well as strategy planning and monitoring and evaluation. All views of the different targeted groups are taken into account. Première Urgence Internationale has the support of the County Health Department (CHD) in Aweil North for its 15 nutrition sites and concretely involves local communities through the Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs), community meetings, Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and exchanges regularly with the Boma Health Committee. In September 2018, PUI organized its strategic workshop in Aweil North and invited all relevant stakeholders, including the CHD, the Boma Health Committee (30 people) and organized focus group discussions directly at community level (170 people). The groups varied from one village to the other, as some involved women part of the Mother to Mother Support Groups, mainly aged from 15 years old to 40 years old; others included the Boma Health Committee of Majak Kaar (more men involved) and farmer’s groups set up for PUI’s food security and livelihood activities (a majority of women attended the discussion). These discussions are instrumental to assess local perspectives of community capacities and vulnerabilities as well as to outline local priorities in addressing their vulnerabilities. Such consultations will not only help PUI to fine-tune and improve its programs, it will also reinforce the community’s acceptance and ownership of future activities.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The community members have the opportunity to influence the whole project cycle management. They are indeed consulted for feedback on the current services and give some recommendations to PUI for new activities in 2019. Then, once a project is starting or about to start, PUI shall organize a kick-off day to celebrate the project and inform the community members on the forthcoming activities. During the project launch phase, appropriate sessions will be organised with all stakeholders to provide information on the project's objectives, the implementation methodology, the planned activities, the selection criteria of the beneficiaries, etc. The health and nutrition workers, the Community Nutrition Volunteers and the Mother to Mother Support Groups are all groups on which PUI can rely on to provide the beneficiaries with information. While implementing the project, a number of feedback mechanisms will be put in place to acquire the relevant data which will enable PUI to tailor its activities to the needs of the populations. Some of these mechanisms include monitoring visits, community leaders meetings and key informant interviews.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A key aspect of PUI’s program is its integrated approach, which underscores the importance of awareness-raising on primary health care, nutrition and hygiene. In each nutrition site, Première Urgence Internationale has a minimum of 8 Community Nutrition Volunteers, equipped with a bike and a backpack containing Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) sensitization materials. During their home visits or at the nutrition sites, the CNVs explain basic hygiene practices, sensitize caretakers on the importance of using mosquito nets to prevent malaria and emphasize why pregnant women should come for ANC visits or practice safe MIYCN. Discussions are also facilitated with the Mother-to-Mother Support Groups –PUI has established 56 groups-, who raise those issues with their communities and spread basic health and nutrition messages, aiming to improve the general nutrition status of the households. These mother to mother support groups have already showed great results, as mothers are increasingly open to information, eager to share experience and show the positive impact of MIYCN practices; the linkages with the health facilities have also improved. Moreover, PUI has painted sensitization messages on the walls of its nutrition sites in the villages to reach as many community members as possible, showing safe child feeding practices, mothers using mosquito nets and washing hands with their children before a meal. Furthermore, if messages are shared on paper, they are written both in English and in Thuangjang, a Dinka dialect. Throughout the years, these actions have helped foster a greater acceptance of the activities and greater proximity between PUI and community members – which helps adapt messages accordingly.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PUI employees are all bound by PUI’s policies, including the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy (PSEA). Staff are trained and informed on the protection policies and sign a certificate to agree with the protection policies. Beneficiaries in the communities are informed of PUI’s protection policies and are asked to report any violation. PUI has also endeavored to recruit more female staff but the challenges of low literacy, especially for women, is a hindrance, especially when qualified staff is needed to provide quality life services. Moreover, awareness activities related to basic human rights and Gender based violence will be integrated into PUI programs and target both men and women adjusted to their specific roles in the community. PUI integrates basic (S)GBV awareness activities in its nutrition and health programs, during health promotion sessions through story-telling, which has shown very positive results and fostered engaging discussions. While it is true that women in general are more vulnerable to (S)GBV, PUI engages both men and women in the promotion of gender equality, streamlining inclusivity in all its activities. In order to break the cycle of silence and stigma, different group sessions are held to allow more space for women and men to freely express themselves. During these focus group discussions, PUI will discuss a focal point for GBV issues in the community and will ensure that there are referral pathways, established with partners in Aweil North. Given PUI has been working for 3 years with the communities and is well respected for the given services and the NGO’s reliability, PUI also plans to do an initial assessment to have greater visibility on needs, practices, attitudes and knowledge of the communities on (S)GBV matters. Depending on the outcome of the assessment, (S)GBV awareness activities will be readjusted to the needs of the community.

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

PUI aims to include all relevant stakeholders in its consultations: traditional community leaders, local authorities (ie, the CHD, Commissioners, etc.), the Boma Health Committee and community members. Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In 2019, PUI plans to establish Project Monitoring Committees in all its sites of intervention, aiming to enhance ownership of the program in the community. PUI will start with an initial discussion and explanation of the role of the project committees and discuss with the community and local authorities the selection process of its members. The selection will consider specific criteria to provide equal opportunities for all and ensure equal participation of women and men. The community members involved in the Project Monitoring Committees will then ensure that activities run smoothly and that the services are provided as planned, all while providing feedback to PUI’s staff. This system will be paramount to improving capacity building and increasing ownership of the activities.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

PUI is aware that some areas in Aweil North are hard-to-reach and often necessitate several hours of trekking by foot for the beneficiaries. To ensure there are fewer barriers to access services, PUI has steadily increased the number of nutrition sites since starting its activities in 2015, to reach 15 in November 2018, including two sites in the disputed 14-mile area – Kiir Kang Bek and Malek Miir. For instance, the latter are facing specific challenges due to access constraints and the fact that it is, by definition, an internationally disputed area. The number of IDPs is higher in both Bomas than elsewhere in PUI’s catchment area, with 37% IDPs in Kiir Kang Bek and 16% in Malek Miir, as Arab nomadic tribes migrate depending on the seasons. Given the access constraints, there are no other NGOs or public services. By opening more sites, including in this area, PUI thus tries to reach as many vulnerable populations as possible to deliver quality management of acute malnutrition.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

PUI always informs its beneficiaries on the services provided, what supplies they will receive (mosquito net, RUTF, RUSF, etc.) and about the timing of the services. For instance, CNVs provide services regularly and screen children once in 3 months, during home visits or mass screening campaignsPUI's work and the behavior of the staff are always guided by the organization's Protection Policies, to ensure protection for its staff members and the beneficiaries: staff is indeed not allowed to ask for money or is expected to provide services equally, for all those in need (no favors), in order to prevent coercion or exploitation. There are complaint and feedback mechanisms in place and community members know to whom they can address their concerns, as PUI has been involved in the community since 2015 and is well accepted. Feedback can be provided through staff or CNVs, as well as PUI's supervisors and managers, individually or during group discussions.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring shall take into account the access of the beneficiaries to the services. This includes assessing the distance to and fro the distribution sites, the modes of transportation to reach distribution areas and bring the distributed items to the beneficiaries’ homes, the time to access the services as well as the time each beneficiary can spare in their daily routines to access the services. As there is little knowledge of safe Infant and Young Child Feeding Practices, PUI endeavours to not only raise awareness on the availability of the nutrition services (right to information) but also on IYCF practices, through group discussions, the support of Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and education messages and paintings for all to see.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

For its new projects, PUI shall endeavour to learn about the perceptions of safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. The aim is to know whether they feel safe when coming to the distribution sites or during the distributions; PUI shall also try to learn how the beneficiaries feel regarding their dignity, whether the nutrition sites are clean and welcoming for instance. Feedback will be gathered during community meetings (during assessments, focus group discussions, Mother to Mother Support Groups) and through the Project Monitoring Committees.
