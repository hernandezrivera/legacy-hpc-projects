Objective 3: Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes protection, safety and dignity of targeted men, women, boys and girls with focus on sustainable resilience-building approaches.

Needs

UNHCR has carried out a comprehensive and detailed infrastructure sweep in planning its 2019 WASH activities and will also complete a Knowledge, Attitude, and Practices (KAP) survey by the end of 2018. These assessments were carried out to better understand the WASH related needs of refugees in order to inform programme planning. UNHCR is the area focal agency for 15 camps (1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, Kutupalong RC, 3, 4, 4 Extension, 5, 17, Chakmarkul, Choukali, Nayapara RC, 26 and 27) and Kutupalong Transit Center covering 40.4% of refugees in Ukhia and Teknaf Districts. UNHCR will also address the needs of the host communities where they have shared interests with the refugee community, in order to meet immediate needs and ease social cohesion. The main needs identified in the sanitation sector are: • The demand for new integrated family toilets and bathing areas in new-build housing in new decongestion sites.• The current low quality of infrastructure with improved semi-permanent latrines with larger pit volumes and superstructures that provide improved safety and dignity.• Better managed and safer desludging and treatment of faecal sludge from existing latrines and septic tanks.• Continued demand for dedicated, safe and dignified sanitation facilities for women and children.• Reduction in amounts of grey and black water channelled into open drains and stream courses.• To reduce pollution of surface water sources and the surface aquifer. The main needs in the sanitation sector are addressing: • Poor water quality results at source and household levels.• Lack of access to sufficient potable water in some of the camps. • Lack of community ownership of water points and community. The main needs in the hygiene sector are: • Weak community engagement activities, understanding of practices, ownership and developing feedback and accountability mechanisms are as yet weak.• Resources and education for menstrual hygiene management are severely lacking in the camps.• Personal hygiene practices are less than optimal with the danger of a major outbreak of AWD or similar highly likely. The main needs in regards to solid waste management are: • The ad-hoc disposal of plastics and food waste are becoming a progressively serious problem in the camps. If no action is taken to develop solid waste collection and disposal systems now, the problem will become progressively more serious for its impact on health over the coming monsoon seasons. The main needs in regards to coordination: • The needs identified are also complimented by UNHCR’s experience in its coordination activities in various forums in close collaboration with the WASH Sector. UNHCR has acted in a field coordination capacity since October 2017 in Kutupalong and Nayapara, co-leading weekly/bi-weekly meetings. UNHCR acts as a WASH Area Focal Agency alongside UNICEF and IOM. UNHCR leads the Water Technical Working Group (TWG) and Sanitation TWG. All these structures need to be strengthened and expanded to meet the growing needs and expectations of the refugee community. The main needs in regards to capacity building are: • A lack of available and specifically trained national staff, there has been a reliance on the international community, particularly the UN and the INGOs to fill the technical requirements in areas of need. The main needs in regards to community engagement: • The response has focused on immediate life-saving activities. The refugee communities themselves have also naturally been in a state of flux and their involvement in the planning decisions that affect their lives has been lacking in many cases. All of these activities are needed to reduce the transmission of diseases and provide dignified access to WASH services. This next phase of response will take place while also continuing to work to mitigate the expected impacts of events such as acute water shortages, acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) outbreaks, floods, landslides or cyclones.

Activities or outputs

The needs found in the UNHCR infrastructure sweep and the KAP survey, along with UNHCR’s global WASH knowledge base form the foundation for its planned activities described below.The 2019 phase of the emergency response will focus on the transition from the emergency phase to the medium phase. It will focus on the replacement and/or improvement of the emergency WASH facilities with a mid-term horizon in mind.UNHCR and its partners will concentrate on the following activities to cover the needs of roughly 363,746 refugees in camps where it acts as WASH Area Focal Agency as well as 100,000 beneficiaries in host communities.Key interventions include: Water Services (specifically in Nayapara camp)SANITATION SERVICES such as care and maintenance of existing communal latrines, and engaging with and educating the camp communities in the design, care and basic maintenance of the facilities; Provision of sanitation items for specific needs (e.g: Potties for children, bedpans for elder people); Provision of latrine and septic tank de-sludging services, with improved faecal sludge disposal; and provision of improved sanitation services in neighbouring host communities.IMPROVEMENT OF SANITATION INFRASTRUCTURE such as Construction of gender segregated public latrines, and household latrines in existing housing where space permits; Construction of small bore sewerage systems with significant on-site treatment facilities; Construction and development of safe, cost effective, financially and socially sustainable Faecal Sludge Management units wherever determined to be necessary; Design, construction, operation and maintenance of treated faecal sludge final disposal sites; Development of a masterplan for sanitation systems including the most relevant, feasible system(s) (centralised and decentralised) for faecal sludge treatment/management.CAPACITY DEVELOPMENT OF LOCAL ACTORS including DPHE, private sector and local actors in toilet design, sludge treatment and solid waste disposal.HYGIENE services will include Training and support to outreach workers to mobilize different sections of the targeted population and encourage positive health and hygiene behaviours through participatory methodologies; Providing access to preferred products, information and supportive facilities for menstrual hygiene management for women and girls through different distribution modalities including cash, voucher and in-kind; Capacity building activities for WASH sector partners to be able to implement efficient hygiene promotion programs; and Communication campaigns based on priority public health risks and in collaboration with Communicating with Communities (CWC) working group.IMPROVEMENT OF SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT through the development of collection and disposal systems; and the construction, operation and maintenance of intermediary solid waste disposal sites in every UNHCR camp. A PROTECTION LENSE will be mainstreamed across activities through the establishment of feedback mechanism with and for beneficiaries to address safety, dignity and access issues raised by users and/or to redefine their assistance; Further development of local water governance in the camps, with the establishment of further water gender balanced committees, or when not possible, specific women’s committees, and capacity building of partners and local authorities.
