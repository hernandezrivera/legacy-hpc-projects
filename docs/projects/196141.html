Project Summary

The malnutrition situation continues to deteriorate in NWS, the main drivers include poverty, low socioeconomic status, unsafe living conditions and diseases, suboptimal maternal, infant, and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices, and high rates of maternal malnutrition. The economic downturn and depreciation of the Syrian Pound in addition to the impact of COVID-19 in NWS had an intensifying effect on the staggering results. In response to the identified need, NGO X will implement the following package of activities which will benefit 37,800 individuals
1. Provide community-based one-to-one counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding and maternal nutrition practices.
2. Provision of specialized nutritious food (LNS) to children 6-59 months
3. Early identification and referral of Acute Malnutrition cases in PLW and children 6-59 months
4. Provision of supplementary assistance via e-vouchers for households with PLW and/or children under 2 years to access nutritious foods
MIYCF messaging and counselling is a component in varying delivery methods including posters, group sessions and household visits. Family MUAC Approach (FMA) is also included on group and individual levels, providing the caregivers MUAC tapes and the nearest functioning nutrition services for self-referral where needed. Screening and referral by the nutrition staff will be provided at household visits.
Ongoing activities blanket distribution of LNS-SQ for children 6-59 months for 6 rounds (months), coupled with MIYCF and FMA activities, to address micronutrient deficiencies and malnutrition. This is implemented in Maaret Tmasrin in Idleb and Azaz, Aghtrin, Suran, and Jandairis in Aleppo. MIYCF messaging and counselling in addition to FMA and screening, through household visits and group sessions will be implemented in Armanaz, Qourqeena, Darkoush in Idleb and Azaz in Aleppo.
A planned intervention will provide 5 rounds of e-vouchers for households with PLW and/or children under 2 years to access nutritious foods as they are the most vulnerable, have the highest malnutrition rates, and are within the 1,000 days window of opportunity. These HHs will also receive counselling on MIYCF practices to support healthier decisions for nutrition outcomes. This will be implemented in Azaz, Aleppo and Maaret Tmasrin, Idleb.
The project targets the most vulnerable populations living in camps, with a focus on women and children within the following categories: caregivers of children 6-59 months of reproductive age, and pregnant and lactating women. Adolescent and youth (10-24 years) are not targeted directly but included if they fall within the previously mentioned categories.
All projects will follow SPHERE standards, cluster SOPs, and internal SOPs and regulations. A set of indicators are in place to track progress according to standards and to report on 4W indicators to the cluster on monthly basis. Monitoring is conducted by the M&E team and by third party monitoring, at point of activity/distribution and 3-4 weeks after to assess the quality, usefulness and retention of assistance and information shared. Evaluations includes a base and end line assessment TPM will share reports upon completion of the project.
NGO X will be implementing this project directly, and 50% of funding has already been secured from multiple different donor sources.

3 - Needs

HNO indicated that 5.5 million people, mostly women, children, and IDPs, require nutrition help. If they don't receive dietary support, a third of these kids will lose their future. GAM rates have increased 4 times since 2019. According to HNO, the project areas of NGO X are at a severity level of 5, with malnutrition in PLW at 12%, anemia common in children aged 6 to 59 months and in 46% of women of reproductive age, and both at 54%. GAM rates are on average 1% in Azaz and Idleb subdistricts and 3% in Jisr Al Shugoor subdistrict, according to a surveillance report from November 2022. For PLW-MAM, the rates reached 21% in Idleb, 10.1% in Jisr Al Shugoor, and 8.2% in Azaz. According to the SENS research, only 19.1% of mothers start breastfeeding in the first hour of giving birth, and only 53.5% of mothers continue breastfeeding after a year, which is low compared to other countries where rates can reach 80%. Reduced access to healthcare, markets, and basic necessities as well as the displacement devaluation of the SYP in combination with an unstable economy are factors contributing to these negative outcomes. Stunting rates will rise from 19.3% in 2019 to 24.5% in 2021, 1 in 11 children will be eating diets that are below minimal standards, and almost half of mothers and children will have anemia. These findings highlight persistently low rates of micronutrient and food intake, as well as frequent infections in younger children and poor eating habits.

4 - Implementation of activities

Nutrition activities via the nutrition community workers target highly vulnerable IDP families living in ITSs and IDP camps in Idleb and North Aleppo, who are simultaneously receiving food security assistance from NGO X as a multi-sectoral intervention. All targeted caregivers of children under 5, and women of reproductive age, of which 4,800 pregnant and lactating women will be trained on the family MUAC approach and provided with a MUAC tape to keep alongside information on the nutrition treatment and prevention services available nearby, aiming to increase early self-identification of acute malnutrition within households and health-seeking behaviours. Nutrition counselling will be provided to the same population on preventing and treating malnutrition and MIYCN. 3,500 HHs with PLW and/or children under 2 years will also receive a top-up using E-Vouchers to access nutritious foods and LNS products specific to children and PLW to address micronutrient deficiencies and malnutrition. Coordination and referrals will be carried out between health and nutrition actors in the area and with NGO X’s food security assistance program to ensure the most vulnerable to malnutrition receive both food assistance and nutrition services. Monitoring and evaluation for each project is conducted by NGO X and a TPM, NGO X provides treatment through implementing partners which have their own HRP application. NGO X will collect data that monthly to contribute to the 4Ws report to the cluster.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: lack of privacy for women, physical risk of traveling to site of activity. Impact: Women may face harassment/safety issues while traveling. Likelihood: Possible. Mitigation: Gender balanced team as NGO X will hire a higher proportion of female staff given the target population is PLW and children under 5; Increase home visits to BNS; Locations for nutrition activities are coordinated with CM/LC to ensure safety, privacy and proximity. Regular monitoring will include questions about safety and privacy. Risk: tensions between project BNS, non-BNS and HHs due to variations in voucher value due to eligibility criteria. Impact: community tensions with NGO’s staff. Likelihood: Possible. Mitigation: E-voucher modality avoids in person distributions and thus reduced community tensions of varying values; Inclusive eligibility criteria cover whole AoOs; Information sharing on selection criteria and programme objectives. Monitoring of tensions between HH. Risk: limited access to NGO X’s reporting mechanisms for women. Impact: women’s voice and opinion would not be communicated on issues faced with assistances. Likelihood: Likely. Mitigations: Diversify CFM and ensure accessibility, especially for those who can’t read/access mobile phones; Consult communities, including women on preferable reporting and communication channels; Conduct regular awareness sessions to increase CFM awareness . Monitoring: track usage and access to CFM in regular monitoring activities and evaluations


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of sexual exploitation, abuse (SEA) and violence by staff or local stakeholders affecting female and child nutrition beneficiaries.Mitigation: Ensure all involved actors are informed about and sign NGO X’s Safeguarding Policies and Code of Conduct; Consult beneficiaries (including women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities) to design activities and support affected populations to understand their rights; Provide PSEA trainings for staff, with information on survivor rights and reinforce the obligation to report SEA/misconduct; Inform beneficiaries of CFM channels and share information with staff and targeted communities about NGO X’s procedures to report, investigate and take disciplinary action in cases of sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment; Communicate targeting criteria and provide clear information to affected populations explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for assistance; Ensure a high ratio of female staff in the nutrition team and a comprehensive recruitment process with reference checking; Ensure parents/caregivers are present during nutrition activities that involve children and when staff from NGO X are interacting with children. Monitoring: Ensure women are consulted in activity monitoring and PDMs.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, security conditions remain sufficiently stable to allow NGO X to operate. Risk (less likely): Continued military offensives presents the risk of death or injury to NGO X staff damage to or destruction of NGO X premises or assets; reduced access to programme areas and of border closure. Mitigation: Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and Standard Operating Procedures are in place, and an experienced security team. Assumption: It is assumed that large scale vaccinations will not be possible in NWS in 2023 and therefore assumed that COVID19 will continue to be a risk for all of 2023. Risk (likely): Spread of COVID19 in NWS. Mitigation: NGO X has identified food security as an 'essential service' in light of COVID-19, which means it will aim to continue food security delivery as possible. As the nutrition delivery is highly integrated within the FS programme, NGO X foresees continuation of this activity. Focus on family MUAC approach, safe screening, HH level visits, reduced number of beneficiaries in group sessions are all other mitigations in light of the pandemic and in line with the cluster recommendations.Risk (likely): Spread of cholera in NWS. Mitigation: NGO X will refer any suspect cases to focal cholera response teams in respective area of operation. NGO X is sharing frequent messaging on cholera to ensure prevention, detection and timely referrals at HH level.
