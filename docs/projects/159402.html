Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable and affected population staying in ECA, Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, GCA. UNFPA has already built the key elements of a response mechanism as part of its humanitarian interventions and is keen to continue strengthening this mechanism. It will place a greater emphasis on assistance, strengthening community resilience, and improving access of humanitarian actors to the affected population. Special attention will be given to supporting the most vulnerable population: people with disabilities, people living in isolated settlements (in particular 0-5 km) with limited access to government service providers, social services, and single women-headed households along the contact line.
Differentiated approaches to programming will continue to be applied in 2020 considering the prevailing humanitarian situation:
The primary focus for humanitarian response will remain to be on the communities 0-5 km and 5-20 km the contact line (Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts).
Critical security/safety needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and comers from ECA communities will be prioritized in 20+ area, where durable solutions for GBV response are implemented in a safe and survivors-oriented manner.
Due to restricted humanitarian access to NGCA for protection activities, the project will seek alternative partnership opportunities with local actors; will reach out to those in need through remote solutions; will use existing access points such as healthcare facilities for delivering aid.
Four main project implementation areas that are in line with SOs will include:
Assistance – developing a network of accessible, affordable and accountable shelters, safe spaces, day-care centres for DV / GBV survivors based on needs assessment of the vulnerable population. These protection solutions according to needs assessment are the most relevant for 5-20 km and 20+ km zones due to security issues and the presence of capable service providers.
Protection – capacity building and sensitization of service providers; awareness-raising within the general population, training and developing distant learning solutions for actors responsible for GBV response; strengthening accountability to affected population (AAP) mechanism, providing updates on Minimal Standards on GBViE.
Resilience – supporting the development of community-based early warning system (for 0-5 km, 5-20 km zones as a priority), involving humanitarian actors, community activists; SGBV coordination to strengthen the response and prevention systems, to improve referral pathway and to ensure AAP.
Access – supporting targeted PSS MTs interventions, awareness-raising/sensitizing sessions and events in selected settlements (with emphasis on isolated settlements and those in 0-5 km zone).
UNFPA supported models of service delivery will eventually build trust in those services among survivors and trigger their willingness to report their cases (as a part of AAP) and seek help. Engagement of non-state actors (local NGO’s, FBO’s, CBO’s) to act as service providers at the local community level will strengthen the prevention pillar of the overall GBV response strategy.
A monitoring and evaluation plan, defined through previous interventions to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets, is used to ensure the quality of project results. A feedback mechanism from project beneficiaries will be operating, including through regular GBV WGs and GBV SC meetings, National Toll-Free Hotline.
In order to achieve maximum complementarity, the project will seek to coordinate, and to the extent possible work jointly, with other complementary projects and initiatives in the area of gender equality and GBV or those working to rehabilitate and develop local social infrastructure and institutional capacity.

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a significant protection concern and it imposes life-threatening risks to the most vulnerable, including IDPs and host communities members in the conflict-affected areas of Ukraine, particularly in the areas close to the contact line. The conflict in Eastern Ukraine increases risks of GBV, in particular, domestic violence because of increased tolerance to violent behavioural patterns in society and psychological disorders resulting in negative stress coping mechanisms by partners involved in the hostilities. The OSCE found that amongst conflict-affected women in Ukraine, the prevalence of physical and/or sexual violence by current partners who fought in the armed conflict in Ukraine is of 31 % compared to 15% for partners who were not combatants (Well-being and Safety of Women OSCE led survey; 2018).In GCA, for example, 20% of conflict-affected older people, including 75% of women reported experiencing at least one type of violence and abuse. (Humanitarian needs of older women and men in government-controlled areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, Ukraine; Baseline report, 2018; HelpAge ).Therefore, GBV prevention and support to potential survivors should also target conflict-affected people with specific needs, in particular people with disabilities, older people and female-headed households. There is also a risk of negative coping strategies by the affected population, which further increases the risks of mental, physical and reproductive health issues.A lack of reliable and comprehensive data on conflict-related GBV in Donbas remains a challenge, including data on domestic violence by former combatants compared to the rest of the population (What’s next for veterans in Ukraine? Promoting inclusion to improve the reintegration architecture for former combatants: Policy Brief endnote 10; 2019; International Alert) and data on GBV affecting people with specific needs, particularly older women and women with disabilities. It is partly explained by GBV cases underreporting due to survivors` fear of stigma, a lack of support services and referral pathways. However, local institutional partners report that compared to 2017 and before, they are increasingly being approached by GBV survivors and people at risk of GBV, particularly domestic violence. Ukrainian legislative and institutional frameworks have been improved over the past years, setting the ground for the development of specialized services for GBV survivors, prevention of violence and promoting a multi-sectoral approach in GBV response (healthcare, education, and justice sectors engagement). Nevertheless, GBV survivors and people at risk of GBV still do not have adequate access to life-saving information and services, including psychosocial support, health, and legal assistance, as well as shelters and safe spaces. For example, GBV survivors have no option but to remain in unsafe environments due to a crucial lack of shelter decisions in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts. Also, such support is hardly accessible to GBV survivors and those at risk in isolated settlements along the contact line, due to the lack of humanitarian access and the scarce presence of healthcare, psychosocial services, and law enforcement authorities.Coordinated psychosocial support, legal assistance, including legal redress, medical care and the provision of shelters and safe spaces should be scaled-up in order to bridge unmet needs. Community-based support mechanisms should also be fostered. There is finally a need to strengthen the capacity of state and non-state service providers, including specialized actors, to prevent and respond to GBV in a coordinated and inter-sectoral way. As of today, over 26 000 referrals (over 5000 IDPs) were considered by primary PSS trough PSS MTs, over 25 000 referrals concerned GBV cases. And over 1000 GBV survivors (over 100 IDPs) received safe spaces in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts.

2 - Activities & outcomes

1. Essential GBV services (PSS Mobile Teams and referral to health, “safe space”, protection, livelihood) are available and accessible for the most vulnerable women and girls living in the areas close to the contact line (also along the contact line). 2. Regional costed Actions Plans (programmes) on multi-sectoral GBV response and prevention developed. 3. Essential GBV service packages adopted in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. 4. General and specialized PSS service providers have the capacity to effectively deliver integrated services for the protection of GBV survivors. 5. Regional healthcare system has the capacity to identify survivors of physical and sexual violence and provide them with services according to international standards. 6. Police and justice services, including legal support to GBV survivors, are integrated as part of multi-sectoral GBV response and prevention systems at the regional level. 7. National-level government-led information and awareness-raising campaign on GBV response and prevention launched in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. 8. Local service providers (including providers operating along the contact line) equipped with information materials on GBV and incapacitated to implement community-level GBV prevention, advocacy and communication actions.
