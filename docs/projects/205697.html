Project Summary

With the current funding from UNICEF and other projected funding opportunities Hold the Child will continue providing life-saving and support services to the most vulnerable, conflict, returnee, flood-affected girls, boys, adolescents, youths, women, and men in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection risks in targeted areas (targeting Host Communities, IDPs, Returnees and persons with disability).
For the effective delivery of the services Hold the Child will continue working closely with other partners under the Protection cluster and be supported by the CP AoR and other technical working groups like CMTF, UASC, PSS Working group, and CAAFAG working group. Hold the Child will continue working closely with other sectors not limited to food security, livelihoods, education, health, nutrition, WASH, shelter and settlement and camp management in reference to CPMS 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 respectively. The project will focus on other key activities of the CP AoR that are enhancing the localization agenda since Hold the Child has the knowledge and strength to continue in CP coordination in WBeG CPWG, participation in various capacity-building opportunities, ensuring the quality of CP services and contributing in resource mobilization.
The services will be delivered through different modalities namely: static, outreach, and mobile.
With the ongoing project and other projected funding opportunities delivery of services will be through the following key sub-thematic areas to at-risk and vulnerable children, adolescents, youths, people with disabilities (PWDs), and community members:
1. Provision of comprehensive case management including through cash-based assistance (CPMS standards 18, 11, 13, 20);
2. Provision of family tracing and reunification for UASC (CPMS standards 13, 18);
3. Provision of quality age and gender-appropriate individual, group, and specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and parenting skills through community-based interventions including mobile teams to extremely vulnerable girls, boys, and their caregivers (CPMS standards 10, 15);
4. Conduct child protection and EORE awareness-raising & prevention messaging activities for women, men, girls, and boys including children with disabilities (CPMS standard 6).
5. Establishing and strengthening referral mechanisms (CPMS standards 14, 16, 17);
6. Capacity building to frontline service providers and volunteers on Child Protection including PSS, case management, and general CPiE (CPMS standards 2, 18);
7. Establishing and enhancing community-based Protection Structures
8. Conducting community-based protection activities including resilience and PSS activities, PSEA, peacebuilding and pieces of training through AGD approaches.
9. Conducting KI interviews at the Payam level, enhancing the Protection Monitoring System.

1. Needs

Eleven years since independence and four years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, South Sudan continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict all adversely affect the food security of South Sudanese families. Their situation is worsened by access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as measles and cholera outbreaks and climatic shocks resulting in extraordinary flooding and localized drought. These, in turn, have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hamper access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, especially for women and girls. In reference to HNO 2023: The humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that 9.4 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance, a staggering 76 per cent of South Sudan's population, increased by 500,000 people from 2022. At least 148,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. Child protection and general protection needs have significantly increased since 2022. It is estimated that 56 counties are at catastrophic severity 5. Safety and security, lack of freedom of movement, HLP issues, discontinuation of child protection services due to access constraints and lack of funding are the main drivers of increased protection needs. Forced displacement, killings, injury, sexual violence, GBV, child recruitment and abduction, forced marriage, family separation, loss and destruction of civil documentation, HLP, forced eviction, and ERW contamination are some of the ongoing human rights violations. Livelihoods are disrupted or destroyed. Education and health care remain at risk, with schools and hospitals occupied, looted, or damaged by conflict and floods. Food insecurity exacerbates protection and exploitation risks, resulting in the adoption of negative coping mechanisms, including school dropouts, child labour, child marriage, and human trafficking.As per Sudan Crisis Situation Report No. 16: 317,993 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 88.5 per cent of all arrivals. New arrivals continue to report harassment, intimidation, torture and extortion of money during their journey to South Sudan, including by security personnel along the route. More than 9,643 refugees and asylum-seekers are in Aweil.In the Abyei Administrative Area, inter-communal violence and tensions continue to affect the ability of humanitarians to provide services. As per Humanitarian Access Snapshot (September 2023): 31 incidents related to humanitarian access constraints were reported across South Sudan, compared to 28 incidents in August 2023. Violence and threats against humanitarian personnel and assets remained the highest with 12 recorded incidents, followed by operational interference in the implementation of humanitarian activities and active hostilities military operations impeding humanitarian operations with six and five incidents respectively. As a result, 25 humanitarian staff in Pochalla, were relocated; and humanitarian activities were suspended. Over 43,848 cases (20,633 girls and 23,215 boys) as unaccompanied, separated or missing and at-risk most vulnerable children have been registered as per to UASC report for caseload summary of 31st October 2023, while 17,291 children (8,088 girls and 9,203 boys) as active caseload. Due to the above needs and protracted protection issues, there is a need for continued Humanitarian Response in the targeted counties within South Sudan

2. Project activities

Based on the current funding from UNICEF Hold the Child will continue implementing Child Protection activities in Western Bahr El Ghazal and expand to other targeted locations with the following activities namely: 1. Provide comprehensive case management, including cash-based assistance. Target 4,500 children (2,250 girls and 2,250 boys). 2. Provide family tracing and reunification for UASC, including identification, registration, documentation, interim care, family tracing, follow-up visits and reunification, reintegration follow-ups and case closures. Target 600 children (300 girls and 300 boys) 3. Provide quality age and gender-appropriate individual, group and specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) through community-based interventions including mobile teams to extremely vulnerable girls and boys. Target 60,000 children (30,000 girls and 30,000 boys); 4. Provide quality age and gender-appropriate individual, group and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and parenting skills through community-based interventions including mobile teams to parents and caregivers. Target 20,000 adults (13,000 females and 7,000 males);5. Conduct child protection awareness-raising & prevention messaging activities for women, men, girls and boys including children with disabilities. Target 65,100 children (32,550 girls and 32,550 boys); 20,850 adults (13,425 females and 7,425 males);6. Conduct EORE in the affected communities. Target 45,000 individuals (15,000 girls, 15,000 boys, 7,500 females and 7,500 males);7. Updating and strengthening the referral pathways to enable the implementation of case management services across the targeted counties. Target 7 referral pathways;8. Capacity building to frontline service providers, and volunteers including government line ministry social workers, Community Leaders on Child Protection, including PSS, case management, and general CPiE. Target 850 adults (425 females and 425 males);9. Establish and enhance community-based Protection Structures. Target 10 structures;10. Conduct community-based protection activities including resilience and PSS activities, PSEA, peacebuilding and training through AGD approaches. Target 45;11. Conduct KI interviews at the Payam level, to enhance Protection Monitoring System. Target 400 KIs; Hold the Child will continue adapting specific remote modalities and methodologies in the delivery of the outlined activities. The modalities are door-to-door, use of phones & tablets, especially for case management, use of the available hotlines and helplines, for instance, the GBV hotlines, use of help desks, individual support, especially on MHPSS, through different channels of communications including through existing community-based child protection groups and focal points, radio programs, social media, bulk SMS and outreach volunteers, special consideration to children with disabilities among others modalities. Hold the Child with geographical presence and more than 10 years of technical expertise will continue providing child protection critical services to support the most vulnerable children and their caregivers across South Sudan with support from the Child Protection Area of Responsibility, the line ministries, related technical groups and other stakeholders in the smooth running of the project from inception to exit.
