Project Summary

The overall goal is to provide tailored assistance and protection to communities living in isolated settlements located along the contact line and in NGCA in Eastern Ukraine. PUI considers populations of such areas to be the most vulnerable within the ongoing conflict due to their restricted access to basic services to cover their fundamental needs. Indeed, in certain cases the settlements are entirely blocked due to bad road conditions or closure of neighboring checkpoints by the militaries. This situation impacts their physical and mental well-being, and raises protection concerns on daily basis. The proposed project will be a pilot intervention that will be implemented in 2 settlements in GCA and 1 in NGCA in order to assess the feasibility and challenges of such an intervention in the Ukrainian context given proven PUI institutional experience. PUI approach will aim at establishing a community mobilization mechanism through the recruitment, capacity building and support of community mobilizers (CMs) functioning at a local formal or informal social center recognized by the community members. CMs will be responsible for organizing all the activities foreseen by the given project with the support and after a range of training sessions conducted by PUI technical specialists. All the planned activities will aim at strengthening the community capacities to organize an emergency response to the ongoing conflict effects by their own resources and newly acquired skills.
Adapted assistance to isolated is one of the three pillars of PUI’s current programming in Ukraine. The concept of intervention proposed in this concept note is based on the programming reflections led by the mission at the end of 2018, which clearly highlighted the relevance to increase the focus on these locations and their populations, given the severity of their needs. The reflection was fed by the conclusions of PUI’s former needs’ assessments, especially the one conducted at the end of 2017. The fact that recent assessments, such as the one conducted by REACH at the beginning of 2019, highlight very similar findings to past assessments conducted by PUI in 2017 shows that the populations of these settlements remain very vulnerable and need adapted humanitarian assistance. PUI decided to include isolated communities assistance at the core of its current programming due to the complementarity with its current geographical targeting and activities in Ukraine (MHPSS, support to the primary healthcare system in vulnerable areas, EECPs interventions).
PUI deems necessary to consider NGCA for this intervention, as it is expected that vulnerabilities of isolated settlements are very high in these areas due to the poor quality of services, including in locations beyond 20km from LoC. Due to the many constraints of access in NGCA, PUI acknowledges that all the components proposed below might not be feasible. Within the framework of this intervention, PUI will aim to at least collect some basic information with its implementing partner, to determine target locations and most adequate modus operandi. PUI is currently not accredited in NGCA-Donetsk, but the organization benefits from a good access to this area, maintain good relationships with the de facto authorities, and has 1 project manager based in Donetsk. Its current projects are implemented through partnership with DDC. PUI senior management (incl. expatriate staffs) managed to visit NGCA-Donetsk several times, and met with high-level representatives such as the de facto Minister of Foreign Affairs, de facto Minister of Health, and the Committee for Accreditation of Humanitarian Missions, to discuss needs and advocate for more humanitarian access. PUI has applied for mission accreditation and if granted, PUI will consider possibilities for direct monitoring of the proposed activities. Local partnership through DDC is still the only possible modality as long as the accreditation has not been granted.

1 - Needs

Serious concerns exist towards restriction of movement of the locals residing in close proximity to the Line of Contact, such as specific threats to their security due to ongoing military actions and high military presence in the area and impeded access to basic services required to meet their fundamental needs. Isolated settlements are typically characterized with lack or low presence of local civil authorities, being located beyond or in close proximity to zero checkpoints of government-controlled areas, missing transportation system, pharmacies, shops, educational or health facilities. In October 2017, PUI conducted an assessment of 15 isolated settlements. Among 514 interviewed 56% were female and 44% male. Based on PUI observations, elderly people experience complexities with taking care of themselves due to low pensions they receive (or do not receive, if they could not afford travelling to the neighboring bigger settlements), availability of special needs’ care and infrastructures, disabilities or critical effects of chronic and disrupted relationships with other family members or friends, who could support them. In February 2019, REACH partnered with the Protection Cluster to conduct an assessment focusing on the protection needs of people living in isolated settlements. The report’s findings are similar to the conclusions of PUI’s assessment conducted in 2017. - Access to basic services was highlighted as a significant protection concern (especially due to long distances to other settlements, and lack of transport options)- 40% of households interviewed reported experiencing challenges when accessing healthcare services.Based on MHPSS assessment conducted by PUI/IMC in early 2018, mental health conditions were mentioned as the ones of the most important concerns. In particular, children going to their schools by foot or bus (located directly at the Line of Contact) were reported to experience outstanding stress, which their parents did not know how to deal with. When it comes to the reasons for psychological distress among communities, separation from family/property/work and disruption of access to basic services were cited as the two main reasons. More than 70% of the interviewees living in the 1-5km zone experienced physiological signs of distress. Common signs of distress and coping skills were differently expressed by men and women. When asked their current coping strategies compared to pre-conflict (which was ‘outdoor activities’), men answered “nothing” as the most common response, suggesting that men’s prior engagement in stress reducing activities is largely not seen as available to them now. Women identified most common pre-conflict coping strategies as related to recreational activities like “singing” and community/village activities and events.The REACH assessment conducted in February 2019 echoes with these findings. According to the report, the two main concerns for populations living in isolated settlements, as highlighted by health practitioners interviewed within the framework of this assessment, were: 1/psychological stress due to proximity to security risks and 2/injuries and wounds caused by conflict activity. The draft report also highlighted the specific vulnerability of older people in terms of psychological concerns (isolation, loneliness, immobility more important). In PUI’s hard-to-reach settlement assessment, first aid kits availability at the community level was spelled out as an important gap by a significant number of interviewees due to the fact that they have no immediate access to pharmacy while having to cope with minor injuries from the ongoing shootings or shelling.Another important lesson learned from PUI’s hard-to-reach settlement assessment was that 100% of respondents pointed out inadequacy of the “traditional” humanitarian assistance distribution process, pointing out towards the necessity to develop mechanisms more inclusive of community feedbacks.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1. Protection and resilience mechanisms are strengthened at community level;. A1-Identification and capacity-building of community mobilizers 8 CMs (community mobilizers) will be identified jointly with community members and local government. These volunteers will be selected based on several criteria including motivation, capacity to interact with community members and stakeholders, acceptability by community members. CMs will receive capacity-building with a strong emphasis on protection principles and protection concerns, referrals (including on confidentiality and informed consent principles) first aid provision, psychological first aid, general principles of care, communication skills, community-based health education, GBV, gender and age needs. A2- Social cohesion activities and special curriculaTogether with community members, CMs will identify social cohesion activities aiming at strengthening links between community members, avoid isolation of vulnerable categories and improve their stress-resistance under conditions of the ongoing conflict. Special curricula and programmes will be developed for different vulnerable categories (where applicable):- Psychosocial activities designed for 9 to 12 years old children - Curriculum for adolescents in the final years of secondary school - Psychoeducational program me for adults as well, on how to organize supportive and safe environment for children- Recreational activities will be carried out for children and elderly people.- First aid training sessions conducted to enable local residents to provide basic first aid to their family members in emergency cases A3-psychological and protection support to persons living in close proximity to the contact lineCMs will also act as interlocutors of individuals in need of protection and/or psychosocial support and individuals with special needs seeking professional assistance. The CMs will be able to identify such individuals, provide them with basic advice and/or refer them to other stakeholders or mechanisms (like UNHCR cash voucher mechanisms) with the support of PUI based on the identified needs. Specific sessions will be organized with PUI program staff for individual persons of concerns, in confidentiality. This activity will enter in synergy with other PUI programmes including referrals of severely distressed people to professional health workers trained by PUI on mhGAP intervention, 2) non-emergency health referrals to secondary healthcare facilities, 3) enrollment for distribution of vouchers for free medicines and kits for emergency trauma/surgery for the most vulnerable population. Outcome 2. Preparedness capacities are developed at community level to respond to emergency needs in health and shelter. A1- Prepositionning of emergency kitsThe activities planned will include pre-positioning of emergency kits at community level such as: shelter kits for minor emergency repairs and insulation, first aid kits, energy/hibernation kits, security equipment (anti-blast films, protective boards to improve the safety of the social center selected for the activities implementation). The kits will be under the responsibility of the CMs, who will be sensitized in taking into consideration gender and age needs in using them. This will develop internal emergency response capacities case of prolonged restriction of access to these areas, to support small repairs, provision of basic NFIs or first aid on a case-by-case basis. Regarding winterization needs, PUI will liaise with relevant actors. A2- Access to safe health premises This activity will also provide remote health workers with clean and well-arranged place, where their patients could be reached out. The premises chosen are located in the center of each location in order to be as reachable as possible for the elderly. When needed, PUI will provide the elderly with tools such as walking frames in order to facilitate their mobility.
