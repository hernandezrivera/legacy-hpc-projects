Project Summary

This project aims at providing emergency food security and livelihood support to vulnerable households engaged in farming, and/or animal breeding with limited financial capacity to acquire the needed production inputs, as well as acutely vulnerable households with limited capacity to cover basic food needs in Gaza Strip, with a specific focus on the Access Restricted Area (ARA). Beneficiaries, engaged in farming, or animal breeding, will receive livelihood support (i.e. cash assistance, in-kind support for rehabilitation). They are vulnerable households, affected by the recent 11-day hostilities, IHL/IHRL violations, deteriorating economic situation, COVID-19 pandemic, and/or other types of shocks. It will help them to maintain their production capacity and protect their livelihoods against potential shocks, while the most acutely vulnerable households suffering from limited access to food and income opportunities will be supported through the provision of multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) and the creation of temporary job opportunities (CFW) to cover their immediate basic needs. PUI also focuses on the infrastructural challenges for protecting agricultural livelihoods in the ARA, such as shortages of electricity and water for agricultural use, through the provision of solar PV systems for agricultural wells and water ponds. The Action will enable vulnerable households (HH) engaged in farming, or animal breeding, including vulnerable headed women HH, to maintain their agricultural activities and enhance their coping capacity to future shocks. It is worth mentioning that the planned intervention was built on the priority focus matrix prepared by MoA (Ministry of Agriculture), FSS (Food Security Sector) and in coordination with them, as well as the main actors working in the same intervention areas. The project activities will contribute to the strategic objective SO3 “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued” through meeting the basic needs and protecting the fragile livelihoods so that they have better capacity to cope with the protracted crisis. Specifically, it will contribute to the following food security sector objectives: The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced (CO1) and Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs (CO2). PUI will ensure the mainstreaming of PSEA as an effort to respect and promote the principle of do-no-harm that guides humanitarian work. The planned action was closely coordinated with the FSS, MoA, Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), INGOs, NGOs, local partner CBOs, as well as with other relevant local actors. Also, coordination with key actors in the area of intervention is already in place with ACTED, Care, Action Against Hunger, Al Nakheel association for development, ACAD, PARC, and UAWC. Extensive consultations were held with all the main stakeholders to discuss the proposed action, avoid duplications, and ensure prioritization of the most urgent needs. PUI will implement the proposed action with its two strategic partners, namely PARC and UAWC, to benefit from and build on the added value and specific experience of each organization. The partnerships with PARC and UAWC have proven to be successful and effective in the joint projects implemented during the last two years. PUI will distribute the roles and responsibilities among the partners based on the specialization and the experience in the intervention area. The intervention areas for the
different activities were determined according to the priority/focus matrix prepared by FSS in consultation with the MoA. Consultations were held with the MoSD regarding the target group and intervention areas for MPCA/CfW.

1 - Needs

Food security in Palestine continues to be affected by the protracted nature of the crisis, repeated shocks, and continued restrictions on freedom of movement. In the Gaza Strip, the last hostilities have exacerbated the already dire living conditions, undermined by years of blockade. Also, the pandemic is placing significant stress on all dimensions of the food system threatening the food security and livelihoods of thousands of vulnerable people in both West Bank and Gaza Strip (HNO2022/FSS). Impact on already vulnerable groups has been dramatic: e.g. FHH, PwD or older people facing difficulties to be participating in the workforce. People whose livelihoods are dependent on the ARA at land and sea are exposed to another layer of vulnerabilities due to the enforcement of these areas. According to World Bank (June 2021), the agricultural sector suffered a direct damage of USD 45 million, while the MoA estimated it was accounting for USD 55 million. More than 26,000 dunums of orchards, vegetable crops and greenhouses, and more than 450 livestock and poultry farms, were fully or partially destroyed (HNO2022/FSS). The bi-weekly survey, conducted by FAO in collaboration with PACU, PARC, and PUI in September 2021 on the impact of stressors on the Palestinian food system highlighted the limited availability and higher prices of agricultural inputs. It confirmed the availability of animal fodder but at prices higher than pre-May escalation levels; liquidity shortages and/or limited access to credit by small producers; stable consumer demand on poultry but limited production capacity due to high cost of fodder; continuous, sea access restrictions. Farmers are facing restrictions on the export of vegetable crops and reduced earnings from agriculture. Open-field farms and GH rehabilitation In June and October 2021, PUI, through its local partner CBOs and UAWC, carried out focus group discussions with 92 (15F, 77 M) small-scale vegetable OL/GH farmers (Open Land / Green House). In addition, another assessment was conducted by PARC’s team in June 2021 with 96 GH farmers affected by the last 11-day hostilities. Challenges and gaps identified: lack of access to the lands; limited access to water due to electricity outages; reduced income; inability to meet family basic needs; debts accumulation; at risk of being detained due to debts; low prices in the local market. Priority needs: cash assistance for basic needs; conditional cash assistance for livelihoods; rehabilitation of water ponds; and solar energy for water wells and ponds. FGDs were conducted with 74 poultry breeders (20 F/54 M) and 27 livestock breeders (15 F/12 M) from Rafah and Khan Younis in October 2021. It confirmed that breeders are suffering due to the closure of Gaza; monopolization of big traders; COVID-19 pandemic; high prices of inputs (i.e. poultry, fodder, etc.); very high cost of fodder; and extreme weather conditions. Priority needs: new varieties of livestock; rehabilitation of barns; PV solar energy and MPCA. Preferred assistance modality: cash assistance (57%); in-kind (34%); and vouchers (9%). Agricultural water wells and ponds PARC’s assessment conducted in 2017 and updated in 2021 identified 8,779 greenhouses (GH) and 2,805 water ponds in Gaza. Accordingly, PARC established ponds for 300 farmers but there is still a significant gap as the water and electricity issues are still there. Meaning that solar systems for water ponds are really needed. In addition, 4 water wells in eastern Khan Younis (covering 2,080 dunums) were assessed and found in an urgent need of solar energy systems. MPCA According to FAO’s survey on the impact of stressors on food system (September 2021), food insecurity levels remained high in Gaza with 7 out of 10 respondents continued to experience poor or border line food consumption levels throughout the month. The poverty rate in Gaza was estimated at 53% in 2017 (PCBS), and is expected to have increased to 56.4% as a result of the May hostilities according to World Bank (HNO2022). Cash for Work (CFW) for agricultural laborers: High unemployment rates among agricultural laborers and the low wages were highlighted in some recent reports as well as during the discussions with farmers and local partners. According to PCBS and HNO2022, the unemployment rate for the second quarter of 2021 was 45%. 19,000 workers were affected by direct damages to facilities, 37% of them working in the agriculture sector (Ministry of Labor; HNO2022). SEA, as a form of GBV, is a risk that is always present in humanitarian interventions. Thus the need to ensure beneficiaries are protected from SEA should always be highlighted.it is important to note that the risk and protection from sea are associated with the unique age, gender and diversity characteristics and how they are perceived in the social sphere.

2 - Activities

PARC and UAWC were effectively involved in the design of the intervention, and will have a direct role in the implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of the planned activities. A1(PUI+UAWC): Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for farmers and breeders.Provision of conditional cash assistance (CCT), vouchers, and/or inputs to 130 (20 F, 110 M) vulnerable households engaged in farming and affected by protection threats and/or other types of shock (economic and environmental) in the ARA. Farmers (50 OL farmer, 80 GH farmers ) will receive cash and/or voucher assistance amounting to 3,000 ILS (938 USD) in 2 installments. It represents the average cost of agricultural inputs to cultivate 2 dunums with vegetable crops or to rehabilitate small-scale greenhouses (1 dunum max) (North Gaza, Gaza).Rehabilitation livestock farms for 80 breeders (15 F, 65 M). They will benefit from the rehabilitation works implemented and supervised by the partners. The assistance amount (1,500 USD/BNF) will cover the essential rehabilitation works need to protect the animals against harsh weather conditions and other shocks. Priority will be given to the livestock farms affected by the last 11-day hostilities (North Gaza, Gaza, Khan Younis, Rafah)The different installments will be conditional. This is to say that the BNFs have to fulfill certain agreed-upon requirements/pre-conditions (i.e. purchasing inputs and implementing works in advance) in order to receive any of the payments.According to CalP’s standard definitions, cash cannot be restricted except through vouchers. However, under this modality, the restrictions are imposed on the utilization of the cash assistance through the pre-conditions that BNFs have to achieve in order to receive the payments.A2 (PUI + PARC): Ensuring safe and continuous access to water resources by farmers a. Provision of solar systems for 2 large-scale agricultural wells and 20 water ponds (200m3) serving 140 (10 F, 130 M) farmers (100 farmers for wells, 40 farmers for ponds) in average (30 kw/well, 3kw/pond). The cost of rehabilitation works and providing solar energy for water wells (40,000 USD/well) and ponds (4,000 USD) was estimated based on the previous experience of partners in this field where they provided PV (photo-voltaic) systems for a number of water wells and ponds during the last years. It will be coordinated with MoA, FAO, and other active actors implementing similar interventions. Priority will be given to the wells and ponds which were affected by May 2021 hostilities and serve the highest number of small-scale farmers with a specific focus on ARA farmers (Khan Younis, Rafah) A3 (PUI): Cash-based support to HH with limited access to income and/or food due to recurrent and emerging crises .Provision of MPCA to 200 HHs (100 FHH, 100 MHH) acutely vulnerable, affectedby the last 11-day hostilities (especially HHs who are still displaced), chronic economic crisis, COVID-19 pandemic, IHL/IHRL violations, escalation of violence, and/or any other shocks to cover their immediate basic needs. The hh will receive MPCA to cover their immediate basic needs for a period of three months. PUI will rely on the monthly transfer value (1,046 ILS/327 USD) determined by MEB TF. The transfer value is equivalent to 60% of the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) value for an average HH of 6 members (1,743 ILS/HH). PUI will coordinate with the MoSD for the identification of the HH who will benefit from the MPCA using the unified vulnerability assessment tool (UVAT) (Khan Younis, Rafah). .Creation of temporary job opportunities (CFW) for 100 unskilled agricultural laborers (30 F, 70 M). They will benefit from the creation of temporary job opportunities through CFW (60 working days) to support vulnerable small-scale farmers in the different agricultural activities (i.e. planting, weeding, and harvest) knowing that the activity will not target the farmers who received agricultural support under the same intervention. The daily wage will be (60 ILS/19 USD) according to the standard wages used by the cash actors but close coordination will be made with the CWG and MoL. All targeted hh will be selected according to the shocks incurred and socio-economic vulnerability criteria. Criteria: hh who are affected by the ongoing deterioration of the economic situation, and/or unforeseen protection incidents (Khan Younis, Rafah) The partners will be mainly in charge of data collection and implementing the activities on the ground under the direct supervision of PUI, while PUI will lead the coordination with the relevant ministries, beneficiary selection processes, feedback/complaints mechanism, MEAL (i.e. spot-check visits, PDMs, and impact survey), reporting to the donor, and coordination with the donor and FSS.It is important to note that due to prevailing cultural norms and limitations, women are unequally represented within the agricultural and herding professions. Women who are active in these livelihood areas will be prioritized for the support. PUI will work to dismantle the barriers women, PwD and other diverse groups have to face when accessing the workforce by promoting equal decision making within the household and providing timely awareness-raising within the community in terms of AGD. PUI will mainstream the IASC's PSEA principles throughout the whole action. PUI has also set internal SOPs on how to receive and handle SEA related complaints, based on the PUI PSEA policy. PUI is also currently co-chairing the oPt PSEA network. The beneficiaries, project staff, members of local partner CBOs, contractors, subcontractors, and all persons involved in the implementation of the activities will be consulted and sensitized on the protection risks related with the project activities, including SEA through awareness-raising sessions and distributing leaflets (inclusive, based on peoples’ characteristics, e.g. child-friendly, disability friendly, etc

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The response modalities for this response will be mostly considering cash assistance. MSNA indicates that cash transfers were the most preferred form of assistance (77%), followed by food in-kind (36%). Similarly, PUI evaluations (2020 and 2021) among supported farmers has shown the same request from the supported workers. Cash will allow them to better adjust to their professional needs, and involve them in the decision making process. There is no evidence that cash or in-kind modalities are associated with higher or lower risk of SEA. Thus PUI will ensure to promote PSEA regardless of the service modality, as it is understood that SEA is a prevalent issue regardless of the sector, modality or location. It is important to note nonetheless that different service modalities may be associated with different types of SEA. PUI will be vigilant in ensuring SEA risks are monitored and appropriate prevention and mitigation plans are in place.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI has a long established presence in the ARA, a deep knowledge of its context, and close collaboration with its diverse population, local CBOs, community leaders and municipalities. The Action’s primary goal is to respond to the affected population’s needs, expressed and identified during the needs assessment phase, through FGDs and phone surveys. The community-based protection approach involves a continuous process of self-assessment and analysis of their vulnerabilities and needs, used as an entry point for the elaboration of local protection strategies. As soon as the action starts, PUI will meet with community representatives and all selected beneficiaries to inform them about the accepted project and explain how beneficiaries are expected to participate and contribute for the success of the action. This meeting will allow participants to inform PUI about their needs and involve them in a participatory approach. All participants will be informed about PUI Feedback & Response Mechanism (FRM) as well as PUI’s commitment to ensuring PSEA. During those meetings, PUI staff members will ensure to hand out information cards on the mechanism and explain the FRM. Meanwhile, feedbacks may be provided verbally in the field, through text message, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards). Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries impacted by PUI / partners interventions as well. PUI MEAL team will pay attention to highlight that feedback, whether positive or negative, will all be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. Monthly internal reporting will include analysis of the received feedbacks, as well as during the yearly strategy exercises.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

By adopting a gender, age, and diversity (AGD) approach, PUI will ensure that its FRM is inclusive to all different groups of people. More specifically, information and awareness about the existence of the FRM and how it is operated will be disseminated to different groups of people and not only to the heads of the household. PUI has developed a child-friendly FRM information material to ensure children know how to report effectively. For people with visual disabilities or those not able to read and write, PUI will use verbal communication at household level. The reporting channels will also be diverse to ensure access. People who can use a phone will be able to submit their inputs through the PUI hotline. Those who are unable to use a phone (older people, children, PwD, etc.) will be able to make verbal complaints to PUI staff during their field visits. Soliciting feedback is also of utmost importance, considering the prevailing social norms around who can complain. Thus, during the regular beneficiary satisfaction surveys, people will be asked if they are aware of the FRM, and if they are not, they will be informed on how to use it and will be asked if they want to make a complaint. To keep communication effective, PUI has a specific budget to guarantee the inclusion of different groups. So, if a person who is deaf wants to raise a complaint, PUI will be able to consult a sign language interpreter. PUI makes sure to have dedicated staff included in the FRM process that are representative of the targeted communities, to make sure that the person feels comfortable in raising an issue. Lastly, the FRM can be a channel to receive Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) complaints. To ensure the safety of the survivors reporting, PUI has established separate procedures in receiving and processing SEA-related complaints, and staff members involved in the FRM will be trained in safely receiving disclosures from survivors and PSEA minimum standards.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is crucial in humanitarian aid delivery. PUI is dedicated in ensuring do no harm and dignity to all people accessing services regardless of their personal characteristics. PUI is currently developing a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on this strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed Action onset. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project will be drafted Action onset, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEA is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects for protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEA, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEA reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEA training will be provided to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is specifically designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI and its partners will analyze possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with the community, CBOs, PDOs and beneficiaries with a specific focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore, the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneficiaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities as income providers, to ensure equal access to productive resources, facilitate access to market while promoting joint decision-making in income use. PUI and partners are working to ensure that programs and services are available and accessible to persons with and without disabilities. All partners will work on a non-discriminatory approach, where a person with a disability is able to exercise their protected rights on an equal basis with other members of the community. PUI is committed to protect Rights of Persons with Disabilities and inclusion principles and practices of the most vulnerable. PUI ensures that personswith disabilities and their families are involved and consulted at all stages of the project cycle. For instance, during the needs assessment phase, PUI includes persons who identifies with one or several of the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on disabilities to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities. As a result, PUI and partners will identify and work to remove any barriers that may prevent persons with disabilities from effectively and safely using services and participating in projects as well as build on identified enablers. During the planning phase, PUI ensured that the disability-related findings and recommendations from the assessment stage have been carried through into the project design. PUI then, make adaptations when necessary to ensure accessibility and inclusiveness of project services. COVID-19 specific issues in terms of access will also be taken into consideration. Specifically, hygiene, prevention, and control measures and protocols will be in place in all planned project activities. The protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reflect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These enforced changes in modality with also be part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people. Accessible information in different modalities for different groups of people regarding COVID-19 will be provided. Lastly, the continuation of the FRM, regardless of COVID-19 related changes in programming will be ensured.
