Project Summary

The project intends to improve the living conditions of women, children and men among IDPs and host community who are at risk of AWD/cholera in Somalia through an integrated WASH response by increasing sustained access to safe water, appropriate sanitation and promotion of good hygiene practices.

Needs

Findings from a rapid WASH assessment conducted by COOPI in Gedo & Bay regions in September 2018 indicate serious challenges in access to sustainable safe water supply largely for IDPs, urban and rural poor communities. More than 60% of Dollow and Luuq IDPs access water from river and the rest buy from vendors at high price of $0.05/20lts, In Baidoa 37% of IDPs reported to buy water from vendors and majority draw water from open HDW hence are at risk of AWD. FGD with target communities show that functioning water sources, specifically boreholes, have weak management structures leading to periodic breakdown or suspension of services. Most HHs average distance to water sources in rural locations is 500 to 1000 meters. Long distance to water sources exposes women &girls to risk of GBV in addition to the exhausting burden of carrying water on their backs. In Dollow 6 Villages are faced with acute water needs due to continuing drought and are relying on water trucking. It is worthwhile to note that COOPI is providing water vouchers to these affected communities for 1 month now. Access to latrines in the target IDPs &rural population is below SPHERE and WASH cluster standards. In the IDP settlements, access to latrines stands at 20% across all districts and is shared by nearly 10 HHs thus compromising cleanliness, privacy and convenience especially for women & girls. Latrines in IDP settlements are in deplorable condition and expose users to the risk of AWD. The lack of latrines especially in rural areas has continued to promote open defecation, which has had severe impacts on the health of the populations’ affected. All schools & OTPs surveyed were found to have inadequate WASH facilities. Poor hygiene and sanitation practices and especially the lack of hand washing practice with soap during critical moments has increased the vulnerability of affected populations.

Activities

Provide 1700HH (IDPs and drought affected host communities in IPC 3&4) with vouchers (90Liters/HH/day) to access safe water for one month. Construct 30 hand dug wells and rehabilitation of 20 hand dug wells to increase availability/ access to safe water through deepening and protection of hand dug wells located in strategic locations currently with severe water shortages. Chlorinate 500 HDWs and test water quality to ensure +ve Chlorine residue in flood/AWD prone areas Identify the most vulnerable IDP households and Support to promote HHWT, safe water collection and storage to AWD risk men, women, boys and girls through distribution of 1700 Hygiene kits to 1700 HHs. Pre position supplies in strategic AWD high risk districts. Conduct KAP at inception & at the end of project, select & train 20 CHPs ensuring gender parity (40% women) from 10 locations targeting all the project beneficiaries. HP through dissemination of key hygiene messages such as hand washing with soap, safe disposal of excreta & HHWT to IDPs, community, schools, MCH/ OTPs to respond/prevent AWD. Provide training to WASH committees on well chlorination, bucket chlorination as well as household water treatment to ensure that target groups access safe water as well as community based operations and maintenance. To create linkages with schools, nutrition/stabilization centres and health posts to ensure that WASH services in these locations meet the needs of these specific groups. Construct/disludge 100 latrines with hand washing facilities for IDPs in emergency. Safety of children, security & privacy of women& girls &prevention of flooding are considered in design & silting. Institutional latrines are separated by sex& marked. Desludge existing latrines where feasible. Identification of sites shall involve women & girls. Water Environmental Sanitation (WES) Committees and HDW caretakers Training.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project centres on increasing communities’ resilience to short-term and chronic emergencies by strengthening WASH system and improving the overall health of the beneficiaries. The program will address the immediate need to avail water facilities and sanitation outcomes while working towards the longer-term goal of sustainably intergrating WASH into the health system framework, thereby attracting longterm strategic responses and commitments that will enable continuous provision of quality WASH services. The project will implement activities alongside or directly through MOH staff to ensure that the capacity and responsibility for the provision of WASH services remains within the ministry. Targeted trainings and supportive follow-up will strengthen local partners’ (e.g. CHVs, health workers, community leaders) capacity to deliver quality services in the long-term. The project will specifically invest in Water Services Management Commities, and the Community Health Volunteers as these individuals will remain in their communities to further WASH and health education and link communities to services.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project will ensure protection issues especially for women; girls and children are considered and integrated in the project implementation process. As much as possible the project will take deliberate action to involve women, girls (and children where possible) in the implementation process through consultation, respect of their decisions and wishes and actual participation. To guarantee protection the planned WASH facilities will be age and gender sensitive. The project adapts Do-No-Harm and protection principles in all community interactions to avoid causing more suffering for the target groups. Regular monitoring and PDM will be carried out to monitor potential harmful impact of the activities. Furthermore, all project distribution sites will consider access, safety and security of all categories of beneficiaries. A central location will be identified for water distribution as women and children are expected to collect water from trucks. Water kiosk will be constructed in selected locations/ settlement in consultation with beneficiaries to identify central location for the exercise. Designs will consider usage by children. Women and girls will be involved in locating safe sites for the construction of the kiosk guided by COOPI engineer to ensure safety (hence reduce risk to GBV) and convenience by ensuring distance to nearest water point is within the SPHERE standards.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

This project keen on gender issues. During collection of data on needs at the field and in the needs analysis. Based on the gender needs, the project's objectives, outcomes and activities have been designed to address the different needs identified. Women and girls are most affected by limited access to safe water sources as they are the main water collectors in the Household. They walk long distance in search for water, are affected health wise as they carry water on their backs even when they are pregnant or are also carrying young babies at the same time. As they walk the long distance to water sources, they are at risk of GBV. In cases where contaminated water is used, the young children are affected most, they get diarrhoeal diseases and fall sick and women that have to care for them and at the same time perform other household chores. Girls end up having less time to study as they spend much time fetching water. Women also loose time collecting water at the expense of other productive activities including breastfeeding the young. All trainings incorporate of women, men and youth. In WES committee’s and women are empowered in to participate and hold position of decision-making. CHPs include 50% women and men representation.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

COOPI will apply a conflict sensitive lens throughout project and implementation. As part of the baseline assessment, COOPI will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that project stakeholders understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. COOPI will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the project are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social groups. All implementing staff will be trained in the Do-No-Harm approach that will enable them to perceive dividers and connectors within communities that can serve to harm or help in situations of conflict. Understanding the unique challenges and opportunities in each target area will allow staff to avoid negative impacts and emphasize peace-building initiatives.
