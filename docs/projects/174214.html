Project Summary

Through this project, Sirri Care Foundation aims to support at Survivors and at risk women, men, boys and girls with livelihoods opportunities, access to dignity kits, safe spaces and psychosocial support services in selected Camp and host communities in Jere LGA of Borno State Nigeria. This will be done through the integration of community members in the delivery of project activities to ensure their inclusiveness and participation for sustainability .

1 - Needs

The prolonged violence in the Northeast Nigeria has brought untold, suffering to the already vulnerable population. Many have lost their lives and others properties and a lot are still undergoing trauma from the effect of the insurgency on them. An estimated 7.7. million people are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance and protection in the most affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. Of this number, 1.6 million are internally displaced (IDPs), with 48% being women. Displacement has limited access to services and protection and has exposed more women, men, girls and boys to GBV including intimate partner violence. The Insecurity situation has made it near impossible for populace to have access to their farm lands this heightened disruption of livelihoods has exacerbated the risks of GBV. This has Women, girls and boys to resort to negative coping mechanisms such as survival sex and are more likely to Sexual abuse and abuse. The multi-year Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRS) (2019-2021), stated that the violent conﬂict has resulted in deaths, displacement of about 1.8 million people (440,000 women, 364,000 men, 614,000 girls, and 516,000 boys) loss, and destruction of property, deterioration of living conditions, accumulated stress, weakened resilience, and heightened vulnerabilities. The conﬂict has had disproportionate impacts along gender lines as casualties in NE Nigeria are predominantly young men. The separation of families has to a signiﬁcant increase in female-headed households particularly adolescent girls as main breadwinners. According to the UNHCR Vulnerability Screening (2017), 63% of vulnerable households within the most conflict- affected areas in the North-East are female-headed. Female-headed households are forced into new roles as breadwinners with limited skills and less likely to access services and assistance. Also, the culture of silence has made many to not speak up against GBV and SEA leading to suppressed anger and trauma. In addition, the lack/limited information on GBV, SEA and safe space to discuss issues of menstrual health management has increased risks of teenage pregnancy and sexual harassment. There is need to support at Survivors and at risk women, men, boys and girls including persons with disabilities especially female-headed households with livelihoods opportunities, access to dignity kits, safe spaces and psychosocial support services in camps and host communities.

2 - Activities

Act1. Carry out assessments to determine preferred and innovative, age and gender context-demand-driven vocational and income-generating activities for survivors and at-risk women, men and adolescent boys and girls, including persons with disabilities. Act2. Establish and equip 2 Women and Girls Friendly Spaces with vocational and recreational activities. Act3. Provision of skills and vocational training with start-up kit/capital for 100 women, and Adolescent girls of especially female headed households to boost their socio-economic capital and self-reliance. Act4. support 100 women, men youths especially female headed households with start-up kits to establish small-scale businesses. Act5. Organize and support 200 women, men and youths to form 4 groups of Village loans and saving associations (VLSA). (25 members per group) made up of those provided with start-up kits/funds. Act6. Distribute Standardized dignity kits to 300 women and girls especially Female headed households including those leaving with disabilities. Act7. Provide 100 women, men youths with 2 cycles of 3 days training course in basic business skills, including bookkeeping, saving, and basic business management skills. Act8. Train community members and protection staff on PSS, human rights, PSEA to be able to identify and link up at-risk women, men, boys and girls to receive appropriate services. Act9. Carry out awareness creation on gender equality, gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and mitigation including PSEA and principles that address negative social norms and principles within project communities. Act10. Provide PSS for GBV survivors and at-risk women and girls including individual and group counseling.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The proposed project is designed such that gender is mainstreamed at all stages of the project (design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation). It will enhance access to services for women, men, boys and girls including persons living with disabilities thus, promotes gender equality. Also, project will ensure compliance with COVID-19 principles and also carry out sensitization on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, ensure the strict implementation of Do No Harm Principle and child safeguarding. Sirri Care will ensures that all program participants receive assistance in a safe, dignified, accessible and non-discriminating manner.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Sirri Care will work with community members including women, men, boys, girls and people with disabilities to establish gender-sensitive and age appropriate compliant mechanisms in project target communities. The established complaint channels will provide for community members and people with disabilities that are directly affected by the project a means to raise complaints about the quality of program including staff behavior. Also, there will be complaint and feedback desks and boxes at strategic areas in the project communities to ensure that community members are able to provide feedback and complaints about the project. Sirri Care also carry out field consultations in project locations to establish the level and quality of project delivery
