Project Summary

The over eight year’s Syrian conflict has affected health service delivery more so in the NWS. Between December and January 2020 there has been escalated systematic airstrike and ground shelling targeted on health institutions and other public installation. Increased insecurity in NWS has reduce access to health facilities by the sick. HeRAM Qrt 4 report show that there are only 56% functional health facilities Out of the functioning facilities 84% health facilities are in settlements/communities while 16% are in IDP camps. It is worth to mention that 78% of HFs located in settlements/communities and 10% of IDP the facilities are serving both IDPs and residents. In the January 2020 4W report, 41% outpatient consultation reordered from severity >3 with over 1 M consultation, increase in trauma, mental health (high cases of distress and anxiety) with NCD contributing to 45% of morbidity. Health, protection & nutrition RNA conducted by SRD –Nov 2019 in Aleppo & Idleb – highlighted beneficiary barriers to health services as insecurity, high cost of treatment, transportation and emergency medical referral, lack of qualified health workers and specialized doctors while lack of medicine and medical equipment are big challenge. Cases of GBV especially among young girls and women, lack of shelter, NFI and WASH services are serious humanitarian gaps affecting IDPs in NWS. Over 350,000 IDPs moving to safer places towards the north border with Turkey and this may worsen should shelling and airstrike continue CCCM Jan.2020.The most affected are IDPs, returnees, and elderly with chronic diseases, women and children in hard to reach areas or areas experiencing change of administration between GoS and Opposition control. This project will contribute to the major HRP 2020 to improve access to essential health services through provision of comprehensive PHC, secondary and tertiary health care to the most vulnerable people with highest health needs in NWS.

4 - Needs

The nine year Syrian protracted crisis have subjected over 12 M people to critical health needs of which 44% are at severity of scale >3. 11.4M people have high health needs the most vulnerable groups; <5 yrs, WRA, >65yrs including people living in restricted areas, people with chronic ailments and women and young girls. HeRAM Qrt 4 (Turkey hub) identified the following health challenges and need, intensified airstrike and ground shelling (around Maarat al-Numan and Saraqib) where 950,000 displaced between 1 December & 28 Feb 2020. Almost 300 boys, girls, women and men are reported to have died as a direct result of hostilities in Idleb and Aleppo since 1 January and 18 February 2020 alone. Number of health workers (medical doctors + nurse + midwife) per 10,000 population, by administrative unit (benchmark >22 HWs for 10,000 population). Health functionality resources shows that 4 of functioning facilities reported difficulties in accessibility mainly due to security and distance. 12% of functioning facilities are in temporary structure building. 11% of the facilities are without/with an interrupted water supply. Health facilities delivery pointsThe January 2020 the 4W report show increase in caseloads with over 1M outpatient consultation, 23,433 trauma consultation and C-section 4004. Over 41% treatment courses was offered to severity >3.RNA; Health, protection & nutrition conducted by SRD (Nov-2019) in Aleppo & Idleb Govern. (Dana, Daret, Azza, Afrin, Jandairis, Aghtarin, Seraqeb Sub Districts) identified beneficiaries varied health needs which included access to health facilities, lack of qualified HWs, specialized doctors, medical equipment and supplies (drugs). The need to capacity build the HW on specialized care i.e. cancer, & NCDs. Barrier to health services included high cost of treatment, high cost of transport to clinic, emergency referral services not affordable to the most vulnerable people. Those with chronic disease 16%, PwD 11% & PLW 41%.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project directly contributes to health cluster strategic objective in HPR 2020, to increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need by implementing 2 strategic objectives. 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas of high severity of needs, and 3: Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context. 1.1 Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referral. 850,000 consultations, 3000 trauma, 188 C-sections1.2 Provide routine immunization services to all children <1 years of age- 1250 <1 children followed and receive DPT 31.3 Provide health facilities with essential medicines and medical supplies- 950000 treatment courses including dressing, drugs, dialysis services3.1 Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services. (132 HCWs and 500 CHWs trained and provided with relevant tools/equipment)3.2 Increase access to health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical units and supporting referral (10MMUs, 10 PHs & 1 Hospital refurbished).The activities delivered including consultations, treatments and deliveries will be reported monthly on 4Ws and health facility monthly report collected and reported in the DHIS 2. HeRAM report provided quarterly and compiled by HC. Disease outbreak monitored through monthly EWARS report from health. EHPS & WHO standards will be followed in hiring medical staff; Doctors, nurses, MW, lab tech, pharm tech, & CHW.Staff will be trained on specific treatment guidelines/SOPs including GBV &protection. All vulnerable group’s youths, PwD, elderly, youths, PLW needs included.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Community Engagement and Participation: WV will use the exist of a feedback mechanism for this project utilizing available communication means, social media, in addition to the face-to-face approach and guide the teams to establish local partnerships, building the capacity of partners to effectively engage with communities. Gender Based Violence- World Vision will advocate for the prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence be considered as life-saving intervention. Health staff will be trained on trauma-sensitive, survivor-centered first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: WVI will provide preparedness trainings for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents mitigation measures and apply the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities as mitigation measures.WVI will enhance awareness Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy including accountability measures to new & old staff including partners Duty of Care: WV will make sure to use “duty of care” policies detailing clear implementation procedures in a manner ensuring the resilience and well-being of contracted health staff who often have been subject to physical harm and mental stress exacerbated by the protracted nature of the crisis. Conflict Sensitivity- WV will Conduct context analysis and regularly update it, the views of partners and other stakeholders considered to ensure sustainability of assistance and access to services. Equitable access for all vulnerable groups: including children <5, WRA, 3) PwD, elderly <50 & IDPs, returnees. Project data analyzed and disaggregate by sex, age and disability- needs of vulnerable and minority groups including risk & rights violation factored in project cycle (assessment, analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring).

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Coordination and duplication: Actively participate in HC, UN, Health Directorates & local council coordination meetings (planning/mapping & reviews). Provide monthly HeRAM and 4 Ws report and share assessment conduct to avoid resource wastage and encourage complementarity. Link with other sectors like WAS, Nutrition, NFI, shelter and collaborate on addressing issues affecting IDPs, which impact on their health. Safe referral pathways for GBV cases and awareness created at community and facility levels.2. Accountability to Affected Populations: All community members will be involved in the full project cycle; identifying needs, planning, implementation and review. Public participation/sensitization by partners on the objectives and expected outcome of the project shared with community. Safe community feedback mechanism like tall free calls, anonymous reports, feedback boxes etc. Exit interviews and start, mid, end project surveys conducted, and findings shared with larger community. M&E reports collected monthly analyzed and shared with relevant people while findings used to improve services.3. Implementation context: Escalating insecurity and exchange of control restricting access to HF, movement of supplies etc. WV will design contingency plan with partners- follow CCCM IPD movement plans and relocate the services appropriately. Adapt MMU services to reach isolated and remote areas. Collaborate with HC/WHO for increased pipeline supplies. Conduct joint advocacy on NWS situation. 4. Fraud and Aid Deviation: All staff (WV/Partners/vendors/contractors etc) trained and sign ant-fraud and good conduct policy. Procurement and supply chain policy in place and staff abide to. HR ensure all staff have back ground check done before hiring, reported cases are taken seriously and investigated quickly by independent team without biasness. Disciplinary measures taken to those found guilty up to including dismissal and prosecution if necessary.
