Project Summary

The project will be implementing in the locality of Azum (central Darfur), it has an estimated total population of 350,000, those classified as settled population are estimated to be around 92,000, IDPs in the locality are around135,000 and returnees’ number approximately 86,000. The locality also hosts 9,000 refugees in Um-Shalaya administrative unit. Many tribes are found in the Azum. They are classified into settled tribes and nomadic tribes.
SAHARI will use the fund of SHF to fit the gape in priority area to covering through implementing policy of SHF and follow HRP objectives which complementing with sector priorities.
The project aims to assist the vulnerable communities ( men , women , boys and girl) in Central Darfur (IDPs, host , returnees and nomadic community) by providing means for households to secure food, protect their livelihoods and wellbeing particularly for women and support to reestablish their livelihoods base, reduce risks on women seeking livelihoods means and contribute to empowering social coherence, and raising community awareness on peace building and inter-tribal reconciliation mechanisms, through a set of activities such as :
*Improve agricultural production of 50,000 vulnerable households provided with agriculture inputs and services. This will be done through training of farmers in crop husbandry practices, procurement and distribution crop seeds , vegetable seeds and hand tools .
*Improve protection of livestock through vaccination /treatment, targeting 30000 pastoralism and nomadic community and demarcation of migratory routes (80km) a long Auzum locality, Training of 10 community animal health workers (CAHWs) ,Provision of kits for 4 CAHWs
* Increase income level of 3000 households, especially women headed households provided with income generation packages including cheese making, food processing, and poultry restocking.
The project will be targeted 22.940 (11000 men, 8000 women and 3940 youth halves of them female) .

In central Darfur it clear that, the food security and livelihood crisis in general was exacerbated by the disruption of the seasonal migration of livestock, due to the insecurity at the borders. Combined with increased vulnerability to disease outbreaks due to the sheer density of animals, means higher rates of animal disease and death, posing a real threat to people. Livelihoods and Food Security.Also, our target groups reflect that there are have inadequate veterinary services and animal production services, no vaccination of animals, beside that their livestock affected by blockage of livestock migratory routes which has led to congestion of livestock, overgrazing and spread of livestock disease. SAHARI support the talk of target groups through assessment in 2021, The results from the HHQ show that anthrax (35.2%) was the most disease-causing death in the respondents’ herds, followed by plague (30.5%), small pox (16.8%), rinderpest (9.5%) and pneumonia (5.5%). almost 90% of respond had not vaccinated their herds.Recent SAHARI 2022 assessments in Azum highlight the negative environmental impact because of ongoing and expected large scale returns to Azum locality with limited livelihood opportunities not reliant on natural resource exploitation and increased demand for fuel wood and construction material the search for which can expose women and girls to abuse and assault.Conclusion of the deterioration in food security and nutrition conditions compared to prior years is caused by three main factors: (a) the economic crisis (b) conflict-induced displacements (c) poor harvest due to droughtWomen in Azum area were recognized as users and collectors of fuel wood, and as victims of environmental deterioration that caused energy scarcity. Time use surveys have shown that women spend long hours in fuel collection so for that reasons woman needs training on production and use of Fuel-Efficient Stoves.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The long terms conflict and continuation displacement of SSR in Darfur has made children, girls, and women more vulnerable to various forms of abuse and exploitation. In their daily life, women and girls face a significant risk of abuse. Women and girls traveling to far places for various reasons, such as to collect water and fire wood, and even to distant market places, face different kinds of abuse. Domestic violence is also common. For SAHARI understanding how men and women are affected differently by conflict and displaced of SSR and hosting in the affected existing communities, anddesigning the right programs and projects that address gender issues is a priority, with the aim of preventing and responding to GBV risks. As a result of this, SAHARI has rolled out the DO NO HARM APPROACH in numerous contexts in addition to CLTs approach amid to let community-to lead total sanitation methodology using specific participatory processes and diagram tools which aim to give women as well as men more control over their lives as the basis for individual, household, community and organizational development. As part of this project, SAHARI trained women to production and use of fuel efficient stove, this activity provides women with protection from the risks that they may face while collecting firewood .community (especially women) will be given awareness on HIV/AIDS and early or self testing on breast cancer to increase their own concerns related moreover, the COVID-19 . Awareness rising sessions on gender-based violence will be conducted for women, men, boys and girls. While implementing the training of income generation activities and also during the campaigns, the community (especially women) will be given awareness on HIV/AIDS and early or self-testing on breast cancer to increase their own concerns related moreover, the COVID-19
