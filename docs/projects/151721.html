Needs

Despite improvements of humanitarian situation of the country, there are still pressing humanitarian needs in Somalia. According to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted by FSANU across Somalia in June and July 2018, 1.5 million people live in emergency and crises (FSNAU-FEWS NET 2018 Post Gu Technical Release - 02 Sep 2018). This highlight the magnitude of humanitarian assistance needed which might be increased though 2019 due to flooding, droughts in some parts and conflict. The long civil war eliminated community preparedness and resilience to withstand drought effects. According to humanitarian Dashboard released by OCHA 2nd September 2018, recent assessments have concluded that acute malnutrition amongst some IDPs is at critical levels of 15 per cent or above, which is above the emergency threshold. The slight improvement in the overall outlook since 2017 can be attributed to increased resilience and a multi-sectoral humanitarian response. However, concerted efforts involving sanitation, protection, livelihood, and health and food security services are still required to improve the situation further. As revealed by FSNAU, Results from 30 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners between in June and July 2018 indicate that the overall nutrition situation in Somalia has continued to improve due to a combination of improved food security conditions, reduced outbreak of diseases and sustained humanitarian interventions. However, high level of acute malnutrition tends to persist in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes.

Activities

1. Work on the provision of integrated baskets of food to include each package basket: Sugar 20 kg - Rice 20 kg - Flour 20 kg - Oil 6 litres – Dates 4 kg - Local beans 10 kg - Sorghum 7 kg. 2. Distribution of baskets to 100,000 families affected by the recurrent drought and floods the equivalent of 600,000 people of men, women, boys and girls and elders especially the neediest and poor groups in Somalia in South Central, Puntland and Somaliland. At least 50% of the targeted households will be female headed families and their identification will have done in cooperation with local authorities in each location.3. Distribution of 800 tones of Dates shipped from Saudi Arabia as part of nutrition program for affected people in Somaliland, Puntland and South Central Somalia. Dates will given maximum cares during shipment and will be stored in a cold chain. However, each family will get 16 kg of fresh Dates that means 50,000 household will benefit from this nutrition program. 4. Organize and facilitate training for local implementing partners on project implementation, guidance and on contracts. 5. Supervision and follow-up on the planning, preparation, distribution and delivery of nutritious food to the beneficiaries concerned. OIC coalition organizations will carry out the distribution with leadership and guidance from OIC in collaboration with relevant authorities of federal and regional states in the country. Comprehensive media coverage will be done for all sites of distribution.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

To avoid exclusion of certain people from benefiting the project, strong cooperation with community elders and religious leaders and village councils and local authorities and with beneficiary committee, in place that detects any deviation in the process. In addition, OIC maintains lists of target beneficiaries verified externally to make sure that people are selected fairly.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risk being faced by beneficiaries based on gender and age might happen at 2 points; one at the registration of beneficiaries and while distribution of food. people might tempt to rob food being distributed to elder women and a lonely women or girls. To prevent from occurring that we cooperate with security institutions to tighten security of the area and prevent robber to approach from the site .

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

To mitigate the impact of armed conflict and violence :1. Cooperation with security institutions to guarding the site.2. Setup a committee from beneficiaries, elders and religious leaders to endure secutiy of the site using local means and tactics where there is no security institutions.3. Distribution of food and nutritious dates through commercial stores and big shops whereby grouped beneficiaries do get food with vouchers.
