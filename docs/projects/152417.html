Project Summary

As Shelter and NFI (S-NFI) Cluster Lead Agency (CLA), IOM is requesting USD 10,945,779 to respond to the lifesaving humanitarian needs faced by an estimated 7.1 million displaced persons across South Sudan. With the requested funding, IOM will reach up to 220,816 individuals considered extremely vulnerable (defined as those in acute and moderate need according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) methodology) and whose capacity to anticipate, cope with and recover from conflict stresses have been eroded. These most vulnerable individuals in the targeted communities include newly displaced persons, IDPs in protracted displacement in POCs and collective centres, IDPs living with host communities, returnees, and members of host communities who have lost their income, shelter, assets and food sources. Proposed activities are in line with HRP strategic objectives, as well as the S-NFI Cluster objectives and key activities. The outputs of the proposed IOM S-NFI project are as follows:
Output 1: Provide effective and quality Cluster coordination to better respond to Shelter and essential NFI needs and enhance resilience of most vulnerable displaced population in South Sudan. (HRP SO 1, SO2 & SO3; S- NFI Cluster Objectives 1, 2, & 3.
Output 2: Improve access to life saving shelter and life sustaining non-food items and enhance resilience of most vulnerable population through area-based and settlement programming. (HRP SO1, SO2 & SO3; S- NFI Cluster Objectives 1, 2, & 3.
The S-NFI Cluster will further enhance national and sub-national level coordination between government and partners to improve access to IDPs and returnees, as well as continue to advocate at all forums and levels for adequate funding to meet the S-NFI needs of affected people.
In its efforts to effectively mainstream protection sensitive responses, address gender based violence, and ensure accountability to affected populations (AAP) in 2018, the Cluster has successfully rolled out new tools to mainstream Protection, GBV, AAP and GBV, which will be fully employed to enhance the overall quality of S-NFI response. The S-NFI Cluster will continue to encourage partners to build on the success of CBI programming implemented in the last two years and maintain the target of at least 30% of the target caseload being assisted through Cash Based Interventions (CBI). The Shelter-NFI Cluster will continue to invest and explore effective avenues to strengthen the capacity of both established and new partners in 2019.
As the S-NFI leading agency, IOM will respond to 22% of the Cluster target caseload for 2019 and provide shelter, NFI and settlement support to a total of 220,816 individuals in 2019, with the aim of increasing the resilience of the target population when addressing their S-NFI needs. In line with the Cluster objective to improve integrated responses, IOM has piloted a Settlement project in Wau, and intends to replicate the model to other areas, including Rubkona, Yambio and Renk. IOM will reach a total of 80,000 people (61% of the Cluster’s total settlement target) based on evidence generated from the project, and ensure alignment with the Global Shelter Cluster strategy. For settlement programming, IOM aims to reach 8,000 HHs in Wau and the rest of the beneficiaries in Yambio, Renk, Rubkona. IOM will continue to expand its CBI and plans to reach 40% of its target caseload through CBI approach. IOM will ensure that the needs of men, women, boys and girls are taken into account throughout the project cycle.
IOM will further improve its program quality by strengthening its MEAL capacities, data analysis and evidence to inform planning, coordination, and decision-making.

Needs

According to the South Sudan HNO, the country’s protracted crisis, spanning over half a decade, has left 7 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance. Almost 4.5 million people remain displaced, including nearly 2 million internally and approximately 2.5 million that have fled to neighbouring countries. Eighty-two percent of South Sudanese live below the poverty line of $1.99 per day. The S-NFI Cluster estimates that approximately 1.7 million people will have limited or no access to adequate shelter and NFI in 2019. This represents a 7% increase from 2018 and is an indication of deteriorating socio-economic conditions in the country. Lack of shelter continues to expose affected communities to health and protection risks, particularly women, children and persons with disabilities. S-NFI supplies are inaccessible due to insecurity, low purchasing power and non-functional markets. These factors increase the vulnerability of IDPs to GBV, especially when women and children are required to travel long distances in search of resources. The revitalization of the South Sudanese peace process and recently signed agreements offer new opportunities in 2019 for the country and its conflict-affected population. The prospects for peace and development are encouraging and may begin to generate some confidence for durable solutions, including returns, relocation or local integration. However, the scope, scale and flows of returns remain difficult to project, given the dwindling state of the national economy and limited basic infrastructure in the country, which will impede returns, integration and recovery in the near future. UNITY: Central and Southern Unity counties are still contested areas between SPLA and SPLA-iO (Leer, Rubkuay, Thaker, Mayendit). A lot of potential remains for the conflict to re-emerge, which would lead to new needs. Population movement still likely in Leer, Toch and Mayendit counties, with residual high needs for assistance as the presence of humanitarian partners in central-south Unity is not guaranteed due to insecurity. JONGLEI: Clashes during April 2018 provoked new waves of displacements in Uror, Nyrol, Akobo counties (Walgak, Lankien, Waat, Chuil, Akobo), with up to 25,000 IDPs. Returnees (former refugees) to Akobo, Pochalla and Boma reported from Ethiopia, and returns possible in the Fangak areas. Jonglei remains an area in need of humanitarian assistance, as periodic clashes continue and the environment is not conducive to large scale of cultivation and livelihood activities. UPPER NILE: Areas of tension between SPLA and iO remain in Pagak and Nasir. Access to Tonga is opened by the river from Malakal, but the situation is still volatile. IDPs and (refugee) returnees reported from Ethiopia are often going back and forth, in Maiwut and Longochuk counties (areas with limited humanitarian presence), with returnees moving to Fashoda and Renk too. WESTERN BAHR EL GHAZAL: In Raja there are risks of clashes, with opposition presence around the area. Phase 4 in last IPC projects is likely to remain. The situation might worsen due to floods and food insecurity. Access to southern Wau is still not certain, with clashes resuming and contested areas provoking displacement of people in bush areas. Conflict also expanded in the roads between Deim Zubeir to Raja town and Raja town to Boro Medina, coupled with food insecurity, displacing people to Wau and Western Equatoria.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

In line with its standard operating procedures, IOM conducts a need assessment prior to any intervention, through which community needs are identified. During the current year, the S-NFI team contacted more than 628 people (459 women, 149 men and 20 girls) through household interviews, focus group discussions, and key informant interviews. In addition, the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix unit conduct Village Assessment and multi-sectoral surveys to assess the overall situation on-the ground, particularly gaps in services and the needs of communities.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

All relevant information concerning the project are disseminated to beneficiaries throughout the project cycle in the form of community committees, and information sessions with community leaderships, youth and women’s groups, and other groups. Information is also disseminated through megaphones, radio, posters, and other visual and audio communication means appropriate for the context.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context-specific and tailored for the targeted audience, respecting age, gender, and diversity. Response teams develop messages based on the information collected during assessments – household interviews, focus group discussion and key informant interviews – to understand community needs and the best way to communicate. Message are translated into the local language, taking into account the cultural context and target beneficiaries. The IOM communication team has significant experience in developing context specific messages and coordinating with other clusters in the design of sectoral specific messages, such as malaria, Ebola, HIV/AIDs, Protection, and GBV.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All members of the IOM response team have been trained on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, which makes them aware of all important and relevant concerns related to project design and implementation prior to rolling out of any intervention. The S/NFI team also works closely with protection officer to ensure protection is mainstreamed in all activities and that the safety and security of targeted population is prioritized and safeguarded.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

IOM's Shelter and NFI team has developed and rolled out the concept of community committees, whereby a representative of every group within the community is included, respecting age, gender and diversity. Examples, amongst others, are representatives from people with special needs (PSN), elderly women, adolescent girls, and people with no community links. This community committee ensures that all groups in the community are considered and that information is not filtered through community leadership.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

A complaints desk is made available for beneficiaries during every intervention. The response team has a complaints-feedback template used for every intervention, which was developed by the team in coordination with the IOM Protection Officer. Channels to increase the inclusivity and reach of complaint-feedback mechanisms are regularly discussed and new ideas and amendments are always tested on-the-ground and in consultation with the IOM Protection Officer prior to operationalization as an SOP. IOM conducts monitoring of all interventions with a built in check for community feedback. The information is analysed and shared with the response team to inform the intervention on-the-ground.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IOM ensures participatory monitoring of project activities. The community is engaged in its monitoring and evaluation through community committees, composed of representatives from different groups from the community. The purpose and objective of this committee is two-fold, namely (1) to ensure that the IOM response team is conducting operations properly and respecting the community all throughout the project cycle, and (2) to ensure that, during targeting, beneficiaries that are identified for that intervention are those that meet the selection criteria and without bias.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All S-NFI interventions are based on a needs-assessment, baseline, context and conflict-analysis, stakeholder analysis, and desk reviews, through which risk analysis that considers the specific needs of targeted community respecting age, gender and diversity is prioritized during the triangulation of data. Further, all activities are coordinated with Cluster and State Focal Points that provide crucial background information and also analysis on protection risks and the specific needs of different population groups. The S-NFI team also works closely with the Protection Officer within all interventions. This serves to ensure that the team has a comprehensive understanding of protection risks, including the specific needs of different groups, and that this is utilized to inform the delivery of services moving forward.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The S-NFI Cluster assessment tools are designed to collect data on beneficiary access to response provided. There are mandatory questions in data collection tools concerning access of beneficiaries desegregated by gender, age, and vulnerabilities. Questions related to security and access enable that access to interventions by beneficiaries in safe locations and in an accountable manner. The collected data is further analysed and lessons learnt are documented.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All S-NFI staff are trained on protection, psychological first aid, protection against sexual abuse and exploitation, and gender. This means that staff is equipped throughout the project cycle to identify protection threats and inequalities, and to mitigate or handle identified situations during the response cycle. Furthermore, prior to any intervention, needs-assessment, baseline, context and conflict-analysis, stakeholder analysis, and desk reviews enable the team to identify any protection threats or inequalities. This information will further inform the design of projects that address inequalities or protection threats.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

This is measured through both assessment or baselines and PDMs or end line assessment for all S-NFI interventions. All interventions are prepared in consultation with the Cluster, State Focal Points and the Safety and Security Unit of IOM, which provide contextual updates on safety and security, and dynamics of both the area and of the targeted affected population. During assessments, beneficiaries and their representatives are consulted to ensure the safety of beneficiaries. IOM ensures the provision of mitigation measures to enable beneficiaries to feel safe while accessing program interventions.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

PDM and end line tools are designed to measure whether the response was conducted in ways that respect dignity of beneficiaries. As a standard practice, assessments are conducted prior to interventions, through which community needs are identified in a participatory way. IOM is increasing its focus on cash based intervention, through which beneficiaries will have more liberty and empowerment in improve their living condition and takes decision about their well-being.
