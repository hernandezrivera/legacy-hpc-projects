Project Summary

UNIDOR is presently, in collaboration with national, state line ministries/counterparts, local government and other humanitarian NGOs, complimenting humanitarian response to critical WASH needs of conflict affected IDPs, vulnerable host communities, returnees in 6 counties: Pariang, Rubkona, Koch, Mayendit, Leer and Panyijar of Unity State. Training of WASH management committees, Community hygiene/health promoters etc. is ongoing in those counties and to be followed by major rehabilitation and solarizing of deep-boreholes, targeting concentrated returnees’ locations that are experiencing water stress, to commence in early November 2019. And through its combined 7 multi-sectoral programmes, Health, Nutrition, WASH, Protection, Food Security and livelihood (FSL), Education and Peacebuilding and Conflict Mitigation, UNIDOR at present operates through integration approach in 10 counties: Pariang, Rubkona, Koch, Mayendit, Leer and Panyijar of Unity State; Cueibet and Yerol East of Lakes State; Jur River of Western Bahr el Ghazal State and Gogrial East of Warrap State, South Sudan. As a long time HRP partner, UNIDOR is compelled by widespread severe WASH situation in 6 counties of 2020 HRP WASH Cluster priority, namely Guit, Pariang and Panyijar in Unity State; Yirol East in Lakes State and Fashoda and Panyikang in Upper Nile State. UNIDOR intends to address the WASH needs through an integrated response in health, nutrition and GBV in those 6 counties where WASH needs are extremely demanding. In Guit County, UNIDOR will, in collaboration with nutrition partners on ground address WASH needs in an integrated approach in nutrition and will easily access the selected project locations/Payams from Bentiu/Rubkona where UNIDOR has a strong presence/field office and with WASH activities being implemented in neighboring Rubkona county. UNIDOR is already on ground in both Pariang and Panyjar counties, scaling up water, sanitation and hygiene needs through an integrated approach in nutrition and GBV targeting IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities. UNIDOR will continue addressing the WASH needs in both counties of Pariang and Panyijar if considered for funding in the next round. In Fashoda and Panyikang Counties, UNIDOR will compliment in the mitigation of WASH-related gender-based violence in an integrated approach with GBV partners on ground, targeting women and girls at risk/in need of core GBV services. In Yirol East County, UNIDOR will integrate WASH in health to address the acute WASH needs and to control water related disease outbreaks, in collaboration with state line ministries and government counterparts as well as other implementing partners on ground. Yirol East will be accessed from Yirol West where UNIDOR has presence on ground addressing nutrition needs. This project will address the vital WASH needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses. It will provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for conflicted affected IDPs, vulnerable host communities and returnees in Guit, Fashod, Panyikang and Yerol East Counties, where UNIDOR is expanding, through rehabilitation of existing handpumps and institutional gender disaggregated latrines and knowledge on key hygiene promotion messages that promote good hygiene practices. The project will also complement the integrated approach of scaling up water, sanitation and hygiene needs in nutrition and GBV targeting IDPs, returnees and vulnerable hosts in Pariang and Panyijar counties where UNIDOR is currently scaling up water, sanitation and hygiene needs in nutrition and GBV targeting IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities through an integrated approach.

1 - Needs

Guit, Pariang and Panyijar Counties in Unity State and Fashoda and Panyikang Counties in Upper Nile State and Yirol East County in Lakes State, are all among 2020 HRP WASH Cluster priority. Yirol East, Panyijar and Fashoda are Cholera hotspot counties, with respective cases load of 80%, 80% and 60% people living in Cholera hotspot locations. Guit and Pariang are the hardest to reach and underserved counties in Unity State, with malnutrition cases for children between 9-59 months with MAM/SAM 1,727/4,232 and 5,183/8,133, respectively due to persistent food insecurity, inadequate WASH basic services among others, causing affected communities, especially the children under 5 more vulnerable to water-related diseases such Cholera, Acute Water Diarrhea and other diseases In Guit, most of IDPs live in overstretched villages in remote forests and across seasonal or permanent tributaries of Naam and Nile Rivers and have limited or without basic WASH services. The WASH needs in Guit, Pariang and Panyijar counties are extremely demanding due to the fact that most of the existing WASH infrastructures were either abandoned without regular or no maintenance at all, neglected or deliberately sabotaged during the better part of the South Sudan conflict, which started in 2013. In Pariang and Guit, for example, most of WASH basic services are concerted in the refugee camp in Yida and IDPs centres, like PoCs in Bentiu/Rubkona respectively, leaving the remote vulnerable host communities who are at present hosting large number of IDPs and returnees in appalling need of WASH services. Panyijar on the other hand is one of Cholera hotspot locations due to constant floods, high aquifers coupled with bad design, more especially of protected hand dug-wells and boreholes drilled along swampy areas without elevated platforms nor water quality surveillance. Open defecation in those areas is widely practiced, increasing the risks of water-related disease outbreaks, including Cholera, acute watery diarrhoea and water born-diseases. Inadequate sanitation remains a major cause of disease in Panyijar, Guit and Pariang Counties. Improving sanitation has been acknowledged to have significantly created positive impact on health at both households and community levels. This project will provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, hosts and returnees through an integrated response in health, nutrition and GBV through rehabilitation of existing handpumps and institutional gender disaggregated latrines. Men, women, boys, girls and individuals living with disabilities will be able to re-establish access to safe and sufficient water for drinking and domestic purposes as well as access to improved sanitation facilities and key hygiene promotion messages that promote good hygiene practices. Most of safe drinking water sources/boreholes/protected wells in Panyinjar, Guit and Pariang Counties, are seasonally inundated by rain water/floods, leading to perennial contamination of drinking water sources. UNIDOR will address the alarming concerns by elevating some of the identified water points at the islands and along the swampy areas to reduce contamination risks during the flooding seasons. As per the WASH strategy for GBV mitigation, all rehabilitation/repairs of community water points and institutional latrines will be carried out after a GBV risk analysis has been conducted at focus groups discussion with women and girls, conducted by female staff on the safety and best location to access safe equitable and sustainable water source and thus reducing cases of GBV and risk of sexual exploitation of women and girls since they are the primary beneficiary. The WASH integrated response in Guit will target the TP/TSFP nutrition sites and PHCC/PHCU/health facilities so that children with SAM/MAM cases will directly benefit from this project as part of the integration.

2 - Activities

Rehabilitate 25 boreholes at Nutrition OTP/TSFP sites/Centres to provide safe and sufficient drinking water at appetite test for SAM/MAM • Distribute 10,000 WASH/Hygiene kits to children admitted for SAM treatment and discharged from functional OTP/SC • Rehabilitate/repair 25 boreholes at functional health facilities to provide access to WASH-related diseases vulnerable population • Rehabilitate/repair 50 boreholes affected IDPs, vulnerable host communities and returnees in 6 focus counties • Rehabilitate/repair 50 improved, secured (with metallic doors/locks) and gender appropriate sanitation facilities in safe locations agreed after GBV risk analysis at FGDs with beneficiaries and GBV partners active on ground in 6 counties. • Provide handwashing facilities in the rehabilitated gender disaggregated latrines for WASH-related diseases vulnerable population • Train 90 Community Hygiene/Health Promoters (CHPs), 15 per county and over 70% women; • Conduct hygiene promotion messaging, targeting 90,000 conflict affected men, women, boys and girls; • Conduct hygiene and sanitation campaigns through household to household visits targeting 9,000 HHs in 6 counties • Form and train 60 water management committees of 7-member each (5 women and 2 men) from user affected communities; • Elevate 20 WASH infrastructures in the flood-prone locations to reduce water contamination risk in flood locations; • Conduct 60 focus group discussions with women and girls co-chaired by GBV partner on ground on the selection of safe sites for rehabilitation of community water points and latrines to mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The affected community will directly access/benefit from this project. The project has a deigned monitoring tool to ascertain the project's benefits to the affected communities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project is linked to government/community system since the local capacity building of user or beneficiary communities will be one of the key components and the roles and actions of the trained community members will always be regulated and monitored by the local government within the given administrative unit or division. The government/community system will use the locally trained community members for long term interventions within and outside the community.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national and state objectives. The project objectives are in line with national objectives, rehabilitation of WASH improved facilities, including deep-boreholes and institutional latrines rehabilitation and the maintenance services that will be offered with low or no cost are parts of the national/state government framework. The whole humanitarian intervention countrywide is within the national Refugee, Return, Social Protection/IDPs Framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficient/stable due to the ongoing peace efforts between the government of South Sudan and the Opposition forces. Since the revitalized peace agreement was inked in 2018, there has been relative peace being felt countrywide, it's believed that the signatories of the 2018 Peace Accord will implement it in letter and spirit to create a conducive environment. This project would easily be linked to durable solution because of its local capacity building components. The training of WASH management committees, pump mechanics and community hygiene promoters are the absolute durable solutions that don't create only the sense of ownership but the gained knowledge will remain and replicated within the host communities and other affected beneficiaries such as IDPs, refugees and returnees. The involvement of women in WASH, for example, is a positive move which creates an opportunity for static knowledge base within the community. The domination and leadership of women in WASH: women constituting 80 percentage in WASH management committees and community hygiene promotion membership and leadership and a certain percentage in handpump mechanic, will absolutely retain the knowledge compared to their men counterparts who are always mobile.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, The project does include sustainability/handover strategy. All the project personnel selected for capacity building are officially nominated/selected and approved through government/community system. In the same manner the project is a community owned since they are fully involved right from project design phase and through project cycle.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

There are many ways in place for ensuring feedback from the most affected vulnerable community members: WASH management committees (WMCs), Community Health/Hygiene Promoters (CHPs), Handpump Mechanics who are routinely engaged with affected community members and their leaders, will be oriented and given specific context information on how to identify and reach out to the most vulnerable household heads/members, including individuals living with disabilities as they will be the direct target of the project. The CHPs, who will carry out routine hygiene promotion awareness campaigns through HH to HH visits will play greater role receiving feedback or complaint from the most vulnerable affected community members. The project staff will consult women, men, boys, girls and individuals living with disabilities together and separately during joint supervision/monitoring site visits and FGDs, based on gender dis-aggregated to express their expectations as well as their capacities to ensure their feedback is captured, documented/shared and addressed. UNIDOR has designed monitoring and evaluation tools used by M&E and project staff for conducting interviews, and discussions with various affected beneficiaries who have been directly or indirectly involved in the project. The findings and lessons learned from these interviews, discussions and information from other sources will be documented in form of reports and human stories of interest, to be used by UNIDOR, funding partners and other stakeholders including local authorities and other partners. These interviews, FGDs and separate discussions with gender and age disaggregated groups including random one-on-one interviews with selected beneficiary household leaders/members and persons living with disabilities, are centered on four major areas, for example: relevance, impact / outcome, value for money and sustainability aspects of the project. Classified interviews are conducted to measure the overall impact of the project.
