Project Summary

In the face of increasing food insecurity and the need to meet other basic urgent needs, initiatives to address immediate food needs of the most vulnerable and food insecure population are paramount in saving lives and protecting assets of the most vulnerable. The proposed project is in-line with Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022, Food Security Cluster (FSC) priorities and objectives. The project aims at improving the ability of 66,610 vulnerable people including 41,280 IDPs and 9,991 people with disability to meet their food and basic needs and reduce consumption gaps through life-saving cash assistance and agriculture inputs. Of these 52,965 people under FSC CO1 and 13,645 people under FSC CO2.
These households are affected by multiple shocks – including conflicts and facing wide consumption gaps, due to successive below-average agricultural and pastoral yields, and a lack of casual labour income opportunities in urban and IDP communities. Unconditional cash transfer (UCT) and agriculture input assistance will improve beneficiary household’s access to food and other basic needs, while preventing households from resorting to negative coping mechanisms which erode long-term resilience. Community and stakeholder engagement, gender sensitive vulnerability and eligibility criteria for beneficiary selection and emphasis on participation of men, women, boys and girls of different ages and other at-risk groups will improve gender equality and women’s empowerment and inclusion of marginalized population. Target population will be provided with six months of UCT totaling 3,675,965$ - based on the monthly transfer value recommended by Cash Working Group (CWG). In addition, 2,047 farmers households will be supported with agricultural inputs including seeds and tractor services through in kind and voucher and consumption support of 30$/month for 4 months. Cash for work will generate employment of 22,742 person days to create community assets.
Settlement and beneficiary targeting will follow a participatory and community-based process. To target the settlements, Concern will conduct stakeholder consultations and key informants interview at regional and district level. Village Relief Committees (VRC) with average 8 to 10 members in each comprising both men and women, including representatives from marginalized population groups will be established (or existing ones mobilized) and receive information on the project and the beneficiary selection process. VRCs will lead community based participatory beneficiary selection process, this will also help project to empower participating communities – including women in decision making. A key component in the beneficiary selection criteria will be gender, age and disability considerations.
In line with Concern’s approach and quality standard for Cash and Voucher based assistance and Accountability to Affected Populations, Concern will disseminate information on its Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) channels during sensitization to ensure that beneficiaries, target communities and project stakeholders are fully aware of and are able to raise any feedback or issues of concern for timely action. Concern’s independent Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) unit will regularly monitor the project and will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM).
Concern with its local NGO partners is implementing both emergency response and longer term programmes in proposed districts in this project. The proposed project will further leverage upon Concern’s efforts and strengthen envisaged resilience outcomes in the target districts, while providing the project with opportunity to ensure convergence and integration while addressing the underlying causes of vulnerability – including gender inequalities and poverty.

1. - Projects activities

1) Beneficiary household targeting: location targeting will be done by stakeholder consultation. In identified locations project team, will engage VRCs and communities to identify the most vulnerable households in-line with set criteria. Priority will be given to female-headed households and special needs groups, e.g. household headed by older people, household with members with disabilities or chronic illness, household with many children - all acknowledged to be vulnerable with difficulties in accessing food and other basic needs.The list of beneficiary households will be announced to the community, giving them opportunity to provide feedback and recommendations on the list, or lodge objections through the CRM. The list is revised where needed based on community feedback. After this, project staff with the help of trained enumerators will conduct verification survey with a sample of households to ensure they meet vulnerability criteria, and the lists are revised as necessary. The final list is announced to the community and approved by the VRC. To minimize the risk of COVID-19 virus spread, Concern adopted to conduct small group meetings instead of large public gatherings and display the list of beneficiaries. 2) Beneficiary household registration and validation: project staff with the help of enumerator will undertake beneficiary registration and validation using Digital Data Gathering tool of Concern. Subsequently information will be updated regularly and based on need. 3) UCT: Concern will transfer cash via mobile money transfer to registered 8,827 beneficiary households for 6 months during project. The transfer value is adopted form CWG recommendation. Current monthly value per household is 80$ in Awdal, 90$ in Togdheer, 70$ in Banadir, 60$ in Lower Shabelle, 85$ in Gedo and 65$ in Bay region. 4) Provision of agricultural support package to 2,047 farmers: The maize, sorghum, vegetable and cow pea seeds and tractor services to farmers at a cost of $105/farmer both in kind and through voucher system. The support will be extended to farmers growing crops under both the rainfed and irrigation production systems. The provision of agricultural inputs will be guided by the following factors: timing of the season in case of rainfed farming, familiarity of the beneficiaries with the seed varieties and their suitability to the local climatic conditions, seed rate per household. 5) Provision of cash transfer to 2,047 farmers as consumption support: Concern will provide cash to farmers at the rate of $30/farmer for 4 months to protect their agricultural produce. Without this cash transfer, the farmers will be vulnerable to exploitation by middle men who provide loans to farmers and the farmers pay in-kind with their produce. The consumption support will enable farmers meet their basic household needs during the agricultural season, while they are engaged in farming activities. 6) Cash-for-work activities to benefit 227 persons: Concern will select and engage 227 persons in cash-for-work activities aimed at creating selected community assets. The community assets include opening up access roads, tree planting and repair of school infrastructure. It is estimated that 227 persons will work for 22,742 person days spread over a period of 5 months. The cost of each person working per day is $7 including the cost of tools and supervision.7) Baseline and PDM: two main activities to assess the impact of the relief assistance activities on beneficiary households will be Baseline and PDM surveys. The baseline will be conducted with a representative sample of households prior to cash receipt, randomly selected from beneficiary lists (sex/age/disability disaggregated). To assess disability status, the short set of Washington group questions will be included in the baseline survey tool. Approximately 2 weeks after the end of every quarter transfers, PDM surveys will be conducted with sampled beneficiaries.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher based assistance has been in use since 2003 in Somalia. Latest CWG report indicated that in the month of August 2021 alone, total 36 member organizations have reached 1,781,699 people and transferred 18,720,952$. Concern participated in CWG conducted Financial Service Provider Mapping in June 2021, it confirms availability of range of service providers and service products in proposed districts.Concern leads the Somali Cash Consortium. Concern, co-chairs the national and Mogadishu sub-national CWG. In addition, Concern and its partner NGOs are active member of FSC at national and sub-national level - including in proposed areas in this project.Since 2012, Concern has been implementing various humanitarian response programme using cash and voucher modality. In the year 2020, Concern assisted 19,698 households (118,188 individuals) amount totalling to 3,640, 460$. While as the lead of the Somali Cash Consortium in the year 2020, reached 51,318 households corresponding to 307,908 vulnerable individuals in 13 regions and 25 districts with total amount of 9,680,478$Concern has established framework agreement with financial service providers. E.g. Hormuud in Bay, Lower Shabelle, Gedo and Banadir region and Telesom in Awdal and Togdheer region proposed under this project. Both EVC and ZAAD are one of the most popular, widely used and with significant market share are easy to use in many places – including in remote and rural area. Concern's agreement with service providers also enables beneficiaries to receive a mobile SIM card with mobile money transfer service activated. Mobile Money cash transfers has proven to be efficient, cost-effective, transparent and scalable to meet rapid surges in needed support. It also aligns with beneficiary preferences and is flexible to meet the unique needs of beneficiary household. Cash-based assistance allows affected people to choose and buy their own food and non-food items from local shops across the country, helping to sustain the local economy in this critical time.This modality of assistance is possible in Somalia because markets are functioning, despite the challenges associated with violence and inter-clan conflicts. Prices of local staples have increased since late 2016, but market-based responses continue to be feasible and appropriate across all monitored markets.The voucher system in the provision of farm inputs will be used in areas where markets are functional, and the inputs are readily available in the market. In addition, the voucher system also helps to create market linkages between the farmers and the vendors of agricultural inputs. This is particularly critical in the tractor hire services where farmers will be issued with vouchers to access the services after, which the tractor owners will present the vouchers to Concern for redemption based on the number of tractor hours used. Concern's experience in providing agricultural support to smallholder farmers will be crucial to ensure that agriculture inputs are provided in a timely manner with quality. The provision of agricultural inputs will be guided by the following factors: timing of the season in case of rainfed farming, familiarity of the beneficiaries with the seed varieties and their suitability to the local climatic conditions, seed rate per household. To ensure that seeds delivered are of the right quality, seed testing will be done to establish viability (at least 80%) and purity among other factors.Effective coordination and guidance provided by FSC and CWG at national and sub-national level also creates enabling environment for adopting cash and voucher based assistance in the proposed districts under this project. Concern adopts monthly transfer value recommended by CWG. Thus it helps project to ensure that provided assistance is calculated based on reliable methodology and data and is sufficient to achieve intended results.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Concern regularly refers to joint market monitoring and CWG dashboard shared by FSC and CWG. In addition, Concern and local NGO partners continuously monitor the market at the district and below district level to respond to any changes. Concern under BRCiS Consortium also monitors changes in market price and functioning on monthly basis under Early Warning Early Action work stream. Markets in proposed districts in this project have remained functional and there is adequate infrastructure to enable this modality to work effectively.Markets in the proposed districts that are targeted in the project are functioning. Joint Market and Supply Chain Update of week 10 to 17 September 2021 indicated little variation in price and availability of basic items like food, fuel etc. in the markets in the proposed district. Similarly, currency exchange rate fluctuation is not reported significantly to affect purchasing power. Markets are accessible – both in rural and urban areas as well as IDP and non-IDP population. Recent market assessments have not reported any major issues of cross-border trades and availability of basic items. Experience so far has not indicated that prices are inflated due to cash transfer programming in communities.Food prices, supply, and market conditions will be monitored monthly to determine if price and supply changes could impact beneficiaries’ ability to use provided assistance to meet their food and other basic needs. Monitoring includes FSNAU/FEWSNET initiatives (e.g. monthly market data update), CWG market updates and WFP and REACH price/market monitoring. The main assumption for the project is that there will be price stability over the project period and inflation will remain reasonable not to significantly erode the value and affect the household’s ability to meet their food needs. The project will continuously monitor market conditions and adapt appropriate mitigation measures to significant commodities, particularly food price fluctuations. In addition, the security continues to prevail and project teams have access to the proposed project areas and beneficiaries. Concern will closely coordinate with CWG and FSC - if assistance transfer value needs to be adjusted to achieve the intended results.The markets for farm inputs exist in major towns and either through the in-kind or voucher system, the seeds will be accessible to farmers. The strategy would be to supply inputs, especially that farmers are familiar with and the quantities that will be supplied are not high as to cause market prices to rise. Concern will procure the inputs and issue vouchers to farmers well ahead of the season to avoid prices increases that usually occur closet to the planting season.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, Minimum Inter-agency Standards for Protection Mainstreaming, Concerns’ protection mainstreaming strategy and Do No Harm principles, the project will ensure accountability and minimize harm to all beneficiaries at all stages of the programme cycle. Further, Concern has a Programme Participant Protection Policy (P4), Concern Code of Conduct and Safeguarding policies - which all Concern employees, partners, contractors and consultants must sign up to during their contract of employment and engagement. The policies sets out and articulates clearly the standards of behavior expected including to ensure Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) when interacting with programme participants in the locations where Concern implements programmes. Staff and other contractors are regularly sensitized on these safeguarding policies. Concern commits to deliver relief food assistance with a consistently high level of safety, accountability, accessibility, and participation, right from its first interactions with the community through to the end of the intervention. Concern adopts community based participatory targeting for beneficiary selection led by VRCs. VRC include representation by female members and marginalized groups, ensuring good clan and age balance. E.g. Concern’s cash and voucher based assistance, actively targets women within households to be registered to receive assistance. For example in 2021, in one of the project in Baidoa, 58 percent of total targeted IDP households were headed by a woman, and 14 percent of beneficiary households had at least one family member with disability.The mobile money transfer modality will also help to avoid causing unintended harm, protection risks and allows beneficiary household to receive assistance in a safe and dignified manner. At the onset of engagement with communities and stakeholders, Concern will conduct sensitization sessions on its fully functional CRM while also re-confirming that they can access the available feedback channels used. Posters with the channels and information on how to lodge complaints will be mounted in strategic locations in the community. In line with the handling procedures, complaints categorized as sensitive are channeled to the Country Director per practice, to ensure they are addressed appropriately. Protection concerns from beneficiaries will also be monitored regularly as part of the PDM processes as well as the regular tracking and updating of the protection risk assessment/analysis in the project locations.Concern uses available mobile phone technology to send short messages and voice messages to target beneficiary households about entitlement before the transfer of assistance and after transfer informing about transfer, its purpose, source of assistance, CRM information and zero tolerance to corruption and fraud. In light of the prevailing COVID-19 situation in country, the project will monitor and adapt its programming informed by the evolving status to minimize and prevent its continued spread. Government and organization protocols will be adhered to throughout program implementation and monitoring processes to not do harm to beneficiaries. These may include social/physical distance during sensitization and other activities, use of masks by project teams, telephonic data collection and information dissemination. In the target areas, Concern has adopted conflict sensitive programming. Concern continuously assesses the potential impact of its programme in creating or resolving conflicts. Concern takes consideration of conflicts linked to selection of project location, resource transfers, operational activities like selection of vendors, hiring of staff or enumerators, visibility material, lack of interaction with local authorities etc.
