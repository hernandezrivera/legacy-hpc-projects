Project Summary

The project aims to provide timely equitable access to WASH services to people affected by the conflict in Yobe states. The project targets 40.000 IDPs, Host Communities, and returnees in Geidam, and Yunusari LGAs in Yobe state. The affected population will be supported in the construction and rehabilitation of boreholes and latrines, distribution of essentials WASH NFIs. The action will also implement a Behavior Change Campaign such as general hygiene promotion and community sensitization, specifically targeting the most vulnerable and newly displaced people. The response is aligned with the 2021 HRP Strategic Objectives and WASH Sector Objectives as well as with the WASH Sector response to COVID-19 guidance and priorities.
The present intervention will include the following components:
Water supply, which will include new installation and rehabilitation/repair of boreholes/ hand pumps, mechanics training, and establishment of Water Sources management committees for the rehabilitated/ repaired water sources.
Sanitation, through latrines construction and rehabilitation as well as the provision of emergency latrines.
Hygiene Promotion activities will comprise both hygiene education and promotional activities to sensitize beneficiaries on the importance of good hygiene practices, such as hand washing and prevention of diarrheal diseases with a special focus on the prevention of WASH-born disease. Hygiene promotion activities will continue with more focus on promotional and demonstration activities like latrine use maintenance, safe water chain, and households’ visits. Hygiene kits will be distributed during the awareness sessions.
School WASH activities, in consultation with the education department there will be the establishment of school hygiene clubs and awareness sessions using a child to child approach for a selected number of schools.

1 - Needs

Now in its eleventh year, the violent conflict in northeast Nigeria continues to affect the lives of millions of Nigerians, in particular women and children. The situation leads to serious existing threats, the presence of insurgents throughout the region and regularly attack against vulnerable communities, uprooting civilians from their homes. This has the consequence to constrain access to basic services, including access to safe drinking water, latrines, and garbage disposal facilities. With 3.9 million people in need in Borno State and 2.6 million in Yobe state, the ongoing displacement continues to put an additional strain on limited WASH facilities in crowded displacement sites.Despite the presence of actors in these areas, water supply remains complicated. According to WASH assessments conducted by COOPI in October 2021 in the target areas, a large number of households reported that they did not have enough water to cover their basic. In fact, in northern Yobe, COOPI has identified only few free-flowing boreholes while most of the residents use water from unsafe sources. The consumption of this water puts the residents at risk of water-borne diseases, especially in the case of epidemics. The urge for improved access to safe water was indeed further underlined by the recent peak of acute water diarrheal cases.The lack of access to improved sanitation facilities were shared by fewer than 10 households. In Yobe, 39% of households reported either using unimproved sanitation facilities or sharing improved sanitation facilities with 10 or more households (COOPI Assessment 2021). In Northeast Nigeria, 42% of people are not using latrines for cultural or availability constraints. In Borno, 50% and 39% in Yobe of households reported either using unimproved sanitation facilities or sharing improved sanitation facilities with 10 or more households while only 2% of people have access to ventilated improved pit (VIP) toilet (REACH, 2020)The hygiene awareness and practices of the population in targeted LGAs is low, particularly in areas outside the main towns, and among the IDP and returnee Populations. The most common driver of WASH needs across target LGAs was lack of access to an improved handwashing facility, hygiene kits, and soap, and lack of awareness. One of the reasons that this composite indicator was developed was due to the change in needs during the COVID-19 pandemic, with handwashing needed to prevent transmission and infection. A key driver of the WASH LSGs is the lack of access to soap where a large proportion of households respectively reported not having access to soap and hygiene kits. This situation is leading to the rapid spread of diseases and faecally-transmitted infections such as Cholera and Diarrhea, which further exacerbate General Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in children. In fact, the Cholera outbreak has resulted in a larger number of cases and is currently affecting 35 LGAs in Nigeria. As of 1st October 2021, there were 2,800 Cholera cases in Yobe State across 14 LGAs and 1,680 in Borno State across 12 LGAs.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The proposed project offers a context-appropriate holistic and blended package of WASH interventions complementing other EU-funded Yobe and Bornopackage early recovery efforts which meets early recovery needs and enhances social protection in the immediate term while strengthening individual, household, and community capacities, improving nutrition, and supporting the long-term. Furthermore, COOPI is committed to applying the humanitarian-development nexus approach to support Yobe State and ensure coherence between political, security, development, and humanitarian actors. The proposed action will focus on meeting the immediate needs of conflict-affected and vulnerable groups in Yobe and Borno States while addressing some of the underlying system failures and drivers of conflict and building the capacity of government agencies to build trust and better respond to the WASH needs of communities through the provision of WASH infrastructures, capacity building and supplementing other FSL and nutrition programs being carried out in same locations. The proposed project activities also align with the Yobe Stategovernment’s identified intervention and targets for development.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The action will adopt a participatory approach where affected communities & community leadership will be the center of the action. Special attention will be given to the involvement of vulnerable groups and people that may be discriminated and stigmatized based on gender, ethnicity, disability, experienced violence, and abuse, ensuring they are not left behind. The project also fits with Protection and GBV mainstreaming purpose. Protection is mainstreamed in the key components of the design and the implementation of each step of the project. COOPI will closely monitor the respect of its application. A strong sensitization will be carried out among the beneficiaries. They will be informed of their right, and how to report any breach. COOPI, following its protection mainstreaming guidelines, will continue to work with the community leaders/stakeholders to strengthen the understanding of the project by members of the community, while clearly communicating the program design to them for feedbacks to inform implementation processes. COOPI has a wealth of community-based/local expertise from Yobe and the implementation LGAs, who speak the local languages (Hausa, Kanuri etc.), thus are familiar with the terrain and understand cultural dynamics of the various communities. This will enhance accessibility to the target population and smooth implementation. Regular Risk Assessments through internal security analysis and communication with external forums for security updates, such as sectors, local and governmental authorities, UNOCHA, INSO and other agencies will also be conducted.COOPI implements a Child Protection project (including PSS) inside Yobe state. Project Managers of WASH and Protection will coordinate each other, so that in the programming level, protection mainstreaming is enhanced. COOPI will develop approaches for people with special needs and multiple disabilities which included both physical and learning difficulties. Prioritiy interventions for people who require a high level of assistance from others with their water and hygiene needs will be ensured. Together with community leaders, COOPI will carry out assessments to identify and analyse the barriers that keep children, youths and adult with disabilities away from accessing WASH services. Do this in coordination with other sectors (such as child protection, MHPSS and education) in order to promote coordinated programming and avoid assessment fatigue.COOPI will support families of people with disability and help family members to understand the importance for everyone in the household to have equal access to water and good hygiene practices. Furthermore, COOPI will commit not to engage children in the management of the distribution and other intervention sites. The staff on site will be recognizable by wearing visibility and reflective jackets. COOPI staff involved will be trained and sensitized on the COOPI Code of Ethics and COOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to sign COOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after the completion of the training sessions. Beneficiaries and community leaders will be sensitized on PSEA and the COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition to reporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistle blower policy via email or helpline phone number. COOPI Protection Officers, PSEA Focal Person and PM coordinate each other, so that at the programming level, protection is mainstreamed.Protection and the prevention/mitigation of Gender-Based Violence is a cross-cutting activity throughout COOPI’s all projects.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI developed a strong Accountability to Affected Population and Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) across the target LGAs through previous and ongoing interventions covering child protection, food security, livelihoods, WASH and nutrition activities. COOPI’s Community Feedback Assistants and Project Management Committees support CRM mechanism in alignment with guidance provided by WASH Sector, donors, Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC)’, the Accountability to Affected Populations Framework and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership’s (HAP).COOPI has a structured identification and training of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM), focal persons, in target communities with Periodic Monitoring of feedback on a quarterly basis. To ensure a people-centered approach, established community-level WASH Committees are trained on receiving feedback and setting up feedback channels including phone lines, logbooks, complaint boxes as well as CRM helpdesks. Multiple feedback mechanisms are put in place after consultation in the LGAs. COOPI use a standardized CRM guide a community Accountability assessment will be carried to identify preferable communication channels in target communities. A context-focused primary feedback mechanism will be designed and integrated into a broader country-wide CRM system. This integrates into a global digital complaint management system – COMPAS, which facilitates the input of beneficiary data and disaggregates according to sex, age, and disability already. CRM Team is equipped with computers/tablets to reduce the risk of data mismanagement/breaches in data protection. The CRM team also makes sure that beneficiaries are aware of how to report any gender-related issues through the complaint desk, and the complaint boxes. COOPI ensures regular and representative consultation of affected populations before, during and after the implementation of activities to capture their preferences and their respective priority needs.
