Project Summary

Syria International NGO Regional Forum (SIRF): In light of the increasingly regional nature of the Syrian Crisis, and the humanitarian and political effects on neighboring countries, a regionally focused forum was assembled in April 2013 to give voice to INGOs conducting humanitarian responses in the region and to facilitate their collective engagement. SIRF exists to serve the needs of its members and advocate on behalf of populations affected by the Syrian Crisis, articulated around 4 core functions: coordination, representation, advocacy and information management.
NES INGO Forum: The NES INGO Forum is the lead coordination body for NGOs working cross border from Iraq in Syria, as endorsed by the WoS Strategic Steering Group. The NES Forum objective is to ensure a principled, appropriate, accountable, and effective NGO humanitarian response in NES. The forum has made significant strides in ensuring a cohesive coordination structure and that NES response actors are well-represented. In the absence of formal UN leadership structures for cross border NGOs, the NES Forum will continue to take an expanded role in coordinating with the UN hub in Qamishli, the WoS Inter-Sector Group and OCHA Regional Office.
The NES INGO Forum continues to lead needs identification, strategic planning and response monitoring around the humanitarian response in NES. The NES Forum also enables humanitarian space for NGOs through leading on negotiations with local authorities around NGO access and protection concerns, and direct engagement with the UN in Qamishli. Additionally, the Forum seeks to enhance communication and information-sharing across hubs through management of inter-sector data flow through 4Ws and response matrices.
Humanitarian Access Team: The HAT plays a critical role in the transmission of granular reporting and analysis to the wider humanitarian community, taking a WoS learning approach. The HAT maintains an online map-based web platform with critical humanitarian and conflict-related data that is published on www.humanitarianaccessteam.org:
- Humanitarian Analysis: market locations, service provision, community assessments with stakeholder analysis, and access impediments.
- Conflict Analysis: key conflict incidents and trends, zones of control, military infrastructure and proximity to relevant programmatic areas, control of and access to road networks, local armed actor developments, and risk by sub-district.
- Thematic reports and situation reports improve strategic understanding of the conflict.
The HAT currently shares reports and access to the HAT web platform with approximately 1,000 users from 300 humanitarian actors, donors and policy makers. With local partners, the HAT shares various reports and are exploring ways to give access to the platform while maintaining security. As such, the HAT fills a critical gap in the coordination architecture by contributing to a better-understanding of social, political, economic, and conflict dynamics as they pertain to humanitarian needs and access.

Needs

SIRFThe humanitarian response to the crisis in Syria remains one of the most complex in the world today. Compounded by ever increasing humanitarian needs, acute access restriction and the segregation in aid modalities have resulted in a historically fragmented response. Despite evident progress made since the creation of the Whole-of-Syria approach in 2014, the July 2015 Operational Peer Review and the 2016 WoS Review have highlighted the need to strengthen coordination and sharpen information sharing and analysis in order to deliver a more predictable, efficient and principled response. As a key founding architect of the WoS approach, and with its entire WoS scope, focus and mandate, the Syria INGO Regional Forum (SIRF) is central and essential to the strengthening of collective leadership, to the representation of all different coordination platforms of the WoS Response and to more streamlined and aggregated information-sharing and analysis as co-Chair of the SIMAWG.With this project SIRF will further its strategic priority of improving coordination, information sharing and analysis across all WoS hubs and lead the representation of the NGO voice in WoS technical and strategic bodies. SIRF’s vision is therefore to engage both with host agencies and donors who fund coordination in the region to develop a much more strategic and concerted approach to NGO coordination, representation and advocacy in order to maximise synergies, opportunities, and value for money. Coordination in NES:Inter-agency coordination remains a challenge to an effective scale up of the humanitarian response in NES due to the unique operational setup. In NES there are UN Agencies and partners working from Damascus and a sub-office in Qamishli, NGOs, both international and local, working cross border from Iraq or from within Syria but with the approval only of the Self Administration, and an increasing number of actors, both private contractors and NGOs, working on stabilization initiatives. Within this complex landscape, and in the absence of formal UN leadership structures for cross border NGOs, the NES Forum continues to play a key role as the lead coordination body for NES NGOs and will continue to take an expanded role in coordinating with the UN hub in Qamishli, the WoS Inter-Sector Group and OCHA Regional Office. Humanitarian and Conflict AnalysisMercy Corps strongly believes that if sound and timely humanitarian, context and conflict sensitivity analysis is available and disseminated, organisations will be more coordinated and responsive to needs and programme interventions will be able to adapt and address localised conflict and power dynamics, re-enforcing socially cohesive and conflict sensitive outcomes. The HAT provides unique, granular analyses of the context for the different coordination mechanisms and engages in coordination at all levels, to provide services, which keep the humanitarian community informed in order to adapt and design appropriate responses.

Protection Risk Analysis

The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with the inter-agency coordination mechanisms supported by Mercy Corps in Syria, and the mitigation measures that will be taken. The principle of risk analysis and conflict sensitive programming is a corner stone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff are trained on key protection principles and tools. Mercy Corps support to the coordination system in Syria aims to allow partners and participates make safe, informed and protection oriented decisions. As the work of the of the coordination mechanisms supported by MC is to inform the wider humanitarian community, Mercy Corps endeavors to ensure that:1. Information and coordination provided supports relevant, protection sensitive and community sensitive programming. There are significant protection risks associated with poor humanitarian planning and response, from exclusion of particular groups, exacerbating tensions between communities and with authorities. The SIRF, NES INGO Forum and the HAT support informed decision making and coordination with relevant groups, based in liaison with actors, staff with experience in the Syria context and analysis.2. Information provided by coordination mechanisms and the HAT is accurate and allows for informed analysis. Mercy Corps coordination support aims to mitigate against protection risks associated with programming in unsafe locations for beneficiaries and staff. Our support is adjusted to the context, in providing daily, weekly and monthly updates where relevant to our partner organizations. Dedicated on-ground field staff support the accuracy and relevance of analysis and guidance provided. 3. Advocacy by NGO’s is relevant and informed. Coordination forums and the HAT support development of appropriate analysis for informed advocacy in the Syria context. The SIRF, NES INGO forum and the HAT are engaged with the wider Whole of Syria response to ensure the operational concerns of partners are adequately reflected in inter-agency coordination and represented to the appropriate actors.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and cluster analysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2019 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria.The humanitarian situation in NWS deteriorated in 2018. The establishment of the demilitarised zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. According to the HAT’s Dec 2018 Report, “The DMZ Agreement”, there is likely to be a partial or complete breakdown in the political process if key provisions are not fully implemented, leading to a surge in IDP movement. Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signalling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. This has meant that there is questions of operational viability across northern Syria in 2019. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enable and adaptable programming.
