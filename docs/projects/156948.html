Project Summary

The project aims to support the livelihoods and development of vulnerable crises- affected communities, while focusing on female and male youth by conducting early recovery and livelihoods activities under the following four components , first, supporting Local Spatial Planning, Implementation and Livelihoods through forming community committees in three locations in Aleppo, where these committees will be provided training and technical support on mapping of needs, project and spatial planning, resource mobilization and implementation of projects to maintain and develop and rehabilitate livelihoods related sites and infrastructure. Moreover, community prioritised sites for rehabilitation and support (two community initiatives per location) will be funded by POINT to be conducted. , second, livelihoods and economy related Infrastructure Rehabilitation, where POINT will conduct further assessment to identify sites that needs rehabilitation and have a positive impact on the economy and livelihoods and will conduct a total of eight rehabilitation projects, these two components will reach directly the population of Mare’, Jarablus and Daret Azza of 420 thousand people who were affected by the ongoing war.
Third, Vocational and skills training to youth to cover skill gaps in targeted communities, after conducting needed labour market assessments, POINT will identify skill gaps to conduct vocational and skills training to 1200 female and male youth. and lastly Small-micro enterprise financial support - Livelihood Grant Support, where POINT will provide technical and financial support (though in-kind and cash grants to 400 small-micro enterprises) to support local markets. By conducting this holistic and inclusive support and including female, male and people with disabilities and injures, POINT aims to generate livelihoods opportunities and revive local markets and develop targeted communities in Northern and western rural Aleppo areas and in As-Sweida city.

Needs

Daret Izza community in Jabal samaan District in Western Rural Aleppo, is scored 4 on the inter sector severity scale (HNO 2019 data), and is home to over 31 housed people in need. Because POINT is implementing ERL activities in Daret Izza, POINT conducted a needs assessment in Daret izzaa and Based the conducted assessment in Nov. 2018 POINT has identified key needs in relation to the recovery and development of Daret Izza and livelihoods of people living in the area, as a result of the key informants interviews and focus group discussion, POINT identified the following needs.Lack of community engagement in spatial planning of the village, as the only body responsible for this is the local council, did not enable prople of daret Azza to have any voice to community needs and most needed basic services. 35%of the total infrastructure is either partially or completely damaged due to the war, over the course of the past 7 years, a large skill gap has formed especially with the youth, which led to decreasing livelihoods and a low average income (80-140) USD per HH. Moreover, most basic services are lacking, the cleaning of the village public areas and extending to clean drinking water and electricity as the people are depending on their own scarce financial resources to access these services. POINT has conducted an assessment in Jarablus and Mare’ communities during January of 2019, in Jarablus and A’zaz districts in Northern rural Aleppo, as it also scored 4 and 3 respectively on the inter sector severity scale, and collectively has over 37 thousand people in need, POINT identified the following needs:Damaged infrastructure (45%), the unemployment rate reached 45% among male youth and over 80% among females and lack of skills in different vocations. Additionally, the village is suffering from accumulated waste and debris, as local authorities are not able to handle solid waste due to lack of funding sources the number of populations in Mare’ are estimated about 38,000 with a number of IDPs around 8,000, whereas in Jarablus the number of populations are about 140,000 with a around 120,000 IDPs.Last, POINT has conducted an assessment in As-Sweida city in As-Sweida governate in December 2018. As-Sweida Governorate has a population of around 94 thousand individuals, and 70% of them are in acute need of assistance (HNO 2019), it has a score of 4 on the inter sectorial severity scale). Most of the population in As-Sweida is aged 40 to 45; with third of the total population is IDPs from other locations, because the area is considered to be relatively safe and the security situation in stable. The very high average of age indicates that the city suffers from the lack of youth and the immigration outside the governorate or out of Syria, moreover, People in the city depends a lot on remittances from family and friends living outside Syria in addition to other negative coping strategies like working in dangerous professions, cutting down on meals, not purchasing medicines or even child labor.Most common profession is trading, mostly mid and small-scale, like trading of food products, electricity related devices and electronics and farming products. The infrastructure of the city is not sufficient to serve the local population, the population has bad access to wate. A small number of craftsmen are still working in the area less than 5% of total functioning businesses, however, their businesses are relatively small due to the lack of governmental support to traditional crafts. As- Sweida is highly dependent on agriculture. However, because of the lack of water resources, governmental subsidies and distorted crops-related value chains, the agriculture production is not profitable enough to support sustainable agriculture activities, this caused a shortage of production due to lack of proper agriculture inputs and very poor marketing of products. The local Market is functional and accessible, and most products and services are imported

Implementation of activities

Component 1 “ supporting Local Spatial Planning, Implementation and Livelihoods through forming community committees .Point will set up the best environment to encourage joint community spatial planning and implementation of actions that will contribute to the overall rehabilitation and protection of physical infrastructure and natural resources of targeted areas in Western and northern Rural Aleppo, by forming a community management group to prepare plans that will have positive livelihoods impact though its activities, then Point will follow up on the implementation of the first priority activities (two community initiatives per location) in the proposed and agreed plan moreover, Point will provide technical assistance in the management of the activities implementation,last, Point will monitor the impact of each activity, both in terms of community strengthening (participation; inclusion; cohesion and societal impact) and in terms of livelihood creation. Taking into consideration the different accessibility needs of people with disabilities/injuries and other vulnerable groups. Component 2 “livelihoods and economy related Infrastructure Rehabilitation”Point will identify eight livelihoods or economic infrastructure for rehabilitation, POINT will engage with the communities, local authorities and other stakeholders to identify the most critical livelihoods related sites that needs to be rehabilitated, then POINT will prepare BOQs and contract contractors to conduct the rehabilitation or design a Cash for work scheme to conduct the rehabilitation works.This activity will directly benefit the an estimated population in targeted population (Daret Izza 20,000 beneficiaries, Jarablus 20,000 beneficiaries and Mare' 20,000). Component 3 “Vocational and skills training to youth to cover skill gaps in targeted communities” in Jarablus, Mare’ and Daret Izza.Under this component, POINT will conduct the following activities to cover skills gaps and increase the livelihoods opportunities in targeted communities, 1- Vocational/Academic professional diploma in Business and ICT BTEC level 52 Skills development training for youth (English language, Olive Production, life skills training, ...etc)3 Start your business training (Small business training – one week per training)4 Careers guidance and employability skills for youth including business ethics, developing CVs, Job interview skills etc.) Total number of beneficiaries for this activity will be a total of 1200 beneficiaries in three locations, 400 females and Component 4 “Small-micro enterprise financial support - Livelihood Grant Support” in in Jarablus, Mare’, Daret Izza, And As-Sweida cityThe project provides start-up grants for the groups who want to start (or re-start) their own business, livelihood training graduates or those who are skilled and experienced and only need resources to re-start/start a business. Recipients of business grants are selected based on selection criteria developed during the inception phase. The selection process takes into account a (simple) business plan/idea to be submitted by the applicant, and the selection of grant recipients is done in a collaborative process that involves both the Point team and local stakeholdersGrants will be distributed as cash, or in-kind support, depending on what is feasible and most appropriate in the local context. To prevent unequal access to resources and the risk of jealousy or intra-community tensions, a maximum value for each area is established, taking into account, where relevant, ceilings set by stakeholders. Cash grants, or in-kind support are distributed directly by Point field team. Recipients will be monitored and followed up with as their business develops. This activity will directly support beneficiaries through cash and in-kind livelihood grants to 400 beneficiaries. (200 women, and 200 men) to support establish or expand 400 Small-micro enterprises.
