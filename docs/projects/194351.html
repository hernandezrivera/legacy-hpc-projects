Project Summary

This project will contribute to the overall strategic objectives of the 2023 HRP and the three sectoral FSL cluster sector objectives through support provided by CAO’s donors and Food security and livelihood partners in order to improve food security and livelihoods situations during an emergency and resilience building of 71,400 (11,900HH) vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls by providing improved access to livelihood inputs for food production, consumption, and dietary diversity.
The outcome of this project is anticipated to increase agricultural food production, protect and build productive assets and restore livelihoods for the vulnerable individuals that are affected by floods, inter communal conflicts, and physical access among vulnerable populations in high food insecure counties of Ayod, Fangak and Canal/Pigi counties whose populations are currently in IPC phase 5 4, and Phase 3 and has high disease comorbidity due to preventable diseases, high GAM rates of Malnutrition
Community Action Organization (CAO) is proposing a 12-month FSL project targeting 11,900 HH (71,400 vulnerable individuals; 10,400 IDPs, 17,600 returnees, 43,900 host community members; of whom 28,859 Women, 24,887 Men, 7,810 boys and 10,344 girls). CAO is an active FSL national partners having implemented emergency food security and livelihoods under 2021 SSHF SA1 in Ayod county and Fangak.
In line with the FSL cluster and 2023 HRP strategic objectives, CAO will scale up response in the target locations by distributing main season and dry season response kits (crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), train and develop the capacity of child protection workers, WASH staff, CNVs, and health workers on the establishment of Kitchen gardens and modern agronomic practices, livestock vaccination, training of CAHWs, assets creation, and business (VSLA) creation at the community level.
The overall objectives of CAO’s planned FSL project are to; 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in line with the HRP SOs and FSL cluster objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. This will help to reduce vulnerabilities and risks associated with the current severe food insecurity, poor economic conditions, climatic shocks or floods, and reduced market access due to the poor road conditions among the affected population in Ayod, Fangak, and Canal/Pigi counties.
In this proposed project, CAO has targeted the following counties; Ayod, Fangak, and Canal/Pigi (Jonglei state) currently in IPC phase 4 & 5 (IPC, Feb 2022). The project will contribute to protection and recovery of livelihoods of the vulnerable population in Jonglei state.
In line with strategic cluster objectives 2: CLO2 Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support and
objective 3, SO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks, CAO intends to reach out to the target population with the following activities; 1) Identification and validating the vulnerable households for their eligibility using a standard criteria, 2) Distribution of on-farm inputs crops and vegetable seeds and to enhance access to food production technologies, 3) Distribution of off-farm inputs/kits (fisheries’ hooks and fishing nets) to enhance access to off-farm food production inputs and 4) Link the beneficiaries to already available health facilities and referral pathway for effective case management to ensure effective integration.
CAO will target vulnerable households in consideration of gender and people living with disabilities, and ensure protection and Accountability to the affected population. CAO has a technical FSL specialist, is implementing FSL and protection with Oxfam GB in Ayod.

1 - Needs

The current South Sudan IPC report informs that, 7.74M (63% of the population) People were facing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between February to July 2022 7.74M (63% of the population) People facing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) Phase with 87,000 People likely in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) acute food insecurity in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Ayod counties in Jonglei State; Pibor County in Greater Pibor Administrative Area; Cueibet and Rumbek North counties in Lakes State; and Leer and Mayendit counties in Unity State (South Sudan IPC, Feb 2022). The current security situation is characterized by Sub-national and localized violence in many parts of south sudan and this continue to drive internal population displacements (WFP report, July 2022). The combined effects of these multiple factors continue to result in a loss of human lives and assets, displacement, disruption, and destruction of livelihoods coupled with consecutive years of high floods that submerge human settlements, farmlands, and pastureland as well as disrupted markets and delivery of humanitarian assistance to the flood-affected populations (HNO, 2022); the continuing and worsening macro-economic crisis that is resulting in food price hikes due to the devaluation of the South Sudanese Pound have disrupted livelihoods and slowed the flow and delivery of both commercial and humanitarian supplies and services. This is attributed to persistent conflict causing loss of assets and life, disruption of livelihoods and agricultural activities, immense floods destroying farmlands and crop fields, poor economic conditions due to the continuous depreciation and devaluation of SSP currency, indirect effects of COVID-19 and Russia-Ukraine conflict impacting delivery of humanitarian assistance during the peak of the lean season (June – August 2022) when food shortages were typically at worst levels. Most populations in Ayod, Fangak, and Canal/Pigi counties are in IPC phase 4 and 5 with critical food insecurity and thus are in urgent need of food and livelihood assistance. The likelihood of improving food security in the entire county remains uncertain owing to the severe effects of the recent floods, ethnic conflicts, and violence, rampant insecurity that left thousands of people displaced. Therefore, there is urgent need for emergency and humanitarian food assistance to avert the worsening food insecurity in Ayod, Fangak, and Canal/Pigi counties. As the lean season peaks, the food security situation in Fangak, Canal pigi and Ayod county is projected to worsen in the first quarter of 2023 (Jan – April) with more populations moving back to their settlements with virtually nothing to begin a new life in the face of a poor economy. Additionally, new Fangak is currently experiencing the effects of 3rd and 4th wave of immense floods which resulted into mass displacement of about 25,000 people as of September 2022. The situation has been exacerbated by new arrival of 1,000 displaced people from Tonga in Upper Nile as a result of conflicts (Inter-cluster conflict spot-check assessment mission report, September 2022). Overall, Jonglei is likely to face extreme food insecurity with scores in catastrophe (IPC phase 5) (SS IPC Dec 2022-July 2023 projections). Many women and children are being highlighted as the vulnerable populations bearing the extreme effects of the multiple factors that drive food insecurity in Fangak. Currently, there are ongoing peace dialogues in Ayod and the situation seems favorable to rebuild resilience owing to relative stability and reduced fighting. This could provide an enabling environment for Community Action Organization to provide food assistance, re-build and enhance households’ capacity to produce their own food and adoption to safe and positive livelihood coping strategies. This, calls for increased support to households to cultivate by provision of of quality emergency livelihoods inputs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CAO will ensure that, during implementation of the project, project committees and monitoring groups will be established and largely comprising of women, girls and people with disabilities. These different categories will be charged to participate actively and p]urged to provide feedback on how the project is being implemented especially whether they are all benefiting from the project, or if there are challenges hampering delivery of the services to the target population.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At CAO, the project beneficiaries and community members form key aspect of the project implementation strategy. CAO will hold stakeholders meeting at community level to include boma chiefs, payam sultans and administrators and women and youth representative groups. these groups will be oriented on the project, and educated about their roles and rights during the project life. Further, these stakeholders meetings will foster mobilization of the affected population and beneficiaries to take up interventions and own the project at large to ensure that project activities are implemented at full scale without sabotage. All relevant stakeholders will be engage to raise awareness and participate in monitoring of the project to ensure that community is fully engage d during implementation of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAO has a functional policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse that govern its programs. In this project, CAO will ensure that FSL staff recruited to implement this project are provided with orientation training on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. All staff will sign the policy code to ensure that they adhere to the PSEA policy guideline. Further, CAO will bomilize community beneficiaries and create awareness about PSEA, empower them to report any form of PSEA cases within and around the distribution sites and project sites in the community where CAO will implement this project. CRSF will fit suggestion boxes at distribution sites to collect confidential information about any case of PSEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To use beneficiaries feedback to adapt project assistance, CAO will organize stakeholders’ mobilization and sensitization workshops and focus group discussions forums where information on the project implementation will be provided to the beneficiaries and entire community. It is thus anticipated that, through these workshops and discussion forums, CAO will expect to get complaints and feedback from the community members and beneficiaries on how the project is being implemented to benefit the community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CAO believes in giving accountability to all parties during and after implementation of the project as a key strategy. To ensure complaints and feedback mechanisms, CAO will ensure routine sensitization of the community to equip beneficiaries with information on how to fully participate and engage in project implementation. CAO will empower beneficiaries to conduct whistle blow at all project sites so that the risk of complaints and feed back is lessened and mitigated. To mitigate the risk of impartial selection and registration of beneficiaries, CAO will ensure that, beneficiary targeting, registration and verification is done in a transparent way by engaging women representatives, community elders and chiefs and entire community trough mobilizations of the community.The project consultation meeting with beneficiaries will consider the attendance to have 50% female, 50% youth, and 50% persons with a disability during consultation and decision-making processes. Beneficiaries will be informed about this criterion to ensure voices of female, youth, and persons with disability are heard in relation to project design and implementation. The beneficiaries will be provided with information about program activities before the commencement of the project to involve them in project management, create awareness and sense of ownership, and provide accountability mechanism. CAO will ensure staff are trained on protection of persons of all age including child protection and GBV risks. CAO recognizes the community engagement and therefore will ensure participation of community members inactivities at community level hence championing community protection mechanisms.CRSF will train community existing protection structures such as church leaders,school administrations, and local authorities on protection principles including GBV risk and referral pathway existing in the community. Host community and returnees’ members will be engaged during the identification of project beneficiaries in order to build a strong relationship between returnees and resident community members. The confidential complain mechanism will be establish and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination. CAO will position a complain desk and box in a visible area and place accountability to the affected population officer to receive complains. Complaints received will go through the case management process to generate appropriate solutions and feedback. All decisions will be generated through inclusive participation, harmonized agreements and decentralized problem-solving approach to concerns and needs; while ensuring accountability to the affected population through holding stakeholders’ meetings and focus group discussions with community members. CAO will conduct planned focus group discussions to engage women, adolescents and people living with disabilities during implementation phase to review the success or failures of strategies, devise mechanisms to change if need arise in order to improve the program performance. People living with disabilities will be prioritized, given special consideration and supported to benefit from livelihood kits. These will be targeted and provided with a specialized training so that they can maximize gains from CAO's FSL response. CAO will fit suggestion boxes to receive complaints from the community members at every nutrition site, and formation of mother support group swill empower them and adolescent girls to share the challenges, successes, what is not working well with the project and devise change of strategies to ensure achieve its objectives. CAO will coordination with protection partners like mines action partners and ministry of gender to ensure that GBV is integrated into this project through conducting training of FSL staff on the GBV related risks to identify, report and refer cases for management. CAO will ensure to mainstream protection awareness messages at distribution sites of livelihood kits

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

CAO aims at building community resilience to natural shock like climatic changes. To enforce this, CAO will provide relevant information on how to mitigate climate shocks through afforestation and ensuring that affected communities devise local means or protecting the environment so as to protect their environment. Thus, CAO will provide information on climate change and its effects as a means of providing early warning information so that affected communities can devise means to mitigate and reduce the floods by less than 30% by the year 2025 in Ayod, Fangak and Canal/Pigi

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

CaO will ensure that FSL staff are trained on GBV, reporting and referral mechanism of GBV cases in Ayod, Fangak, and Canal/Pigi counties. CAO is a specialized protection partner with specialized staff in GBV and Child protection. Thus, CAO will ensure that, its technical protection staff train FSL staff on protection measures as well as support to provide GBV awareness to the FSL beneficiaries. This will contribute to GBV reduction by more than 80% in the project location through provision of timely information on GBV referral pathways, access to information and case management in Ayod, Fangak, and Canal/Pigi counties

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Since the project aims to contribute to the recovery of livelihoods, CAO will ensure that, beneficiaries are trained on agribusiness skills like value addition, vegetable production for sale and food and training women and farmers groups on village savings and loans associations (VSLAs) in the project target locations. This will ensure that, farmers are trained on sustainable copying strategies that reduce their vulnerability from IPC 4 to IPC zero. Further, CAO will distribute emergency livelihood kits like fishing kits, crop and vegetable kits to combat any form of catastrophe that may emanate.
