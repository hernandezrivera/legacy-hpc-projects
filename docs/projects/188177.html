Project Summary

INSAN aims through The proposed project to continue providing preventive and curative nutrition services to vulnerable displaced children under 5 (boys and girls) and PLWs in 143 camps in 9 communities (Kelly , Hazano, Kafr – Kafrehmul , Maaret Elekhwan , Kaftin , Haranbush , Sheikh Baher , Kafr Nabi Kafr Jales) during COVID-19 pandemic through sustaining the operations of two mobile clinics ( Caravans) .
These clinics have established to reach to underserved communities who face challenges to access to health & nutrition services due to distance and transportation difficulties especially children and women.
Two mobile rapid response teams will mitigate the risk of infection and ensure continuity of life-saving nutrition services as the followings:
1- Continuity of life-saving nutrition services particularly CMAM, micronutrient supplementation, and IYCF-E under guidance for adaptations in the context of COVID-19.
2- Facility and community-based nutritional assessment for PLWs and children under 5 by MUAC and Z-scores.
3- Deployment of two mobile rapid response teams: MUAC screening and referral complicated SAM cases and provision of therapeutic for SAM and MAM cased among children 6-59 months and malnourished PLWs .
4- Provision of Maternal nutrition and appropriate IYCF-E counseling and messages for pregnant women and mothers.
5- 12 CHWs and 2 team leaders will conduct community outreach activities promoting on IYCF-E, Maternal nutrition, health, and WASH-related topics and massages based on NWS nutrition cluster and TWG recommendations in the context of COVID-19 to promote and support IYCF-E.
6- Distribution of micronutrient supplementary to PLWs and children under 5 based on SoPs and nutrition cluster recommendations.
7- Conduct training on IYCF-E and CMAM programming in the context of COVID-19 for mobile clinics and community staff.
8- Conduct training on MUAC screening and referral and roll-out of a family MUAC approach in which caregivers, PLW, and family members.
14400 beneficiaries ( CU5 & PLW ) expected to be reached during 2022 .
INSAN will submit monthly 4W report to nutrition cluster.

4 - Needs

The main groups affected by malnutrition are infants, young children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The different forms of malnutrition include wasting, that is, severe acute malnutrition (SAM) moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) as well as chronic malnutrition or stunting (low height for age) and micronutrient deficiency especially iron-deficiency anemia which occurs in young children, PLW but in girls of reproductive age. According to the Child Nutrition Report (WOS, 2020) the percentage wasting in children 6-59 months was 12%. Modelling estimates indicated that the prevalence of stunting in children 6-59 months increased from 23% in 2012 to 30% in 2020 while iron-deficiency anemia affected one in three women (2019). The rate of exclusive breastfeeding in infants 0-5 months was already low but declined further from 43% to 29% (2014-2020). In 2021, 85 sub districts across Syria had 25% of children suffering from stunting (severity 3 classification). (HNO 2022 )on other hand , In February 2021, INSAN CHARITY team conducted need assessment to a Random Sample of IDPS in the camps in five communities (Haranbush , Kaftin , Kafr-kafrehmul , Kelly , Maaret Alekhwan)The results were as following: According to the IDPS information 81% of families have children under five years and 47% of families have children with malnutrition .

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of GBV :mitigation measures through the following :INSANS will apply its gender policy on all the project teams to align with the GBV and protection SOPs.INSANS will also monitor the activities to ensure adherence to protection principles and will report to the HR and MEAL unit in the Turkey office on any violations of the code of conduct. Thus, feedback will be provided to program management. The trained project team inside Syria is already present and will work to ensure that all project activities and services will be provided to beneficiaries in dignity and respect and the intervention will not cause further damage to the emotional safety of the beneficiaries by training and raising awareness of all staff. Through its MEAL department, INSAN will conduct full complaints and monitoring system to guarantee the effective participation of the beneficiaries in identifying and preventing protection risks. These measures will help in protection mainstreaming through “Do No Harm” analysis and programmingRisk of securing data : INSAN will ensure the confidentiality of beneficiaries, to protect information safely gathered about women and children according to data protection policies should not reveal women and children’s names or any identifying information to anyone not directly involved in child and women care.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

risk of SEAMitigation measures :- Female staff will be hired to decrease the risks of SEA.- Female SEA focal point will be assigned with assigned phone number and email to report any case of SEA.- INSAN CHARITY is member in PSEA network where it attends regular meeting and report to the PSEA network.- Posters and banners of PSEA networks which includes awareness information on PSEA and PSEA phone number and email , will behanged in all the clinics and other locations of the project .- INSAN has PSEA policy describes standard of behavior for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. This policy will be described to staff .- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms will be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk#1: COVID-19 Pandemic Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigate Measures:INSAN will take the necessary measures, procedures, and precautions to protect health care workers and beneficiaries and will mitigate the pandemic effect if happened based on WHO, NWS health and nutrition cluster & CDC recommendations and guidelines as followings:A. At facility level:1. Activating Online Consultations for PLWs and children.2. Providing staff with Personal Protection Equipment PPE (Gown, Gogglers, FFP Masks, caps, and gloves) with multiple access to Alcohol for rubbing.3. Limiting the accessible areas for patients in facilities to eliminate virus spread. B. Community Level:1. Providing CHWs with personal protection materials (masks, gloves, alcohol hand sanitizer).2. Activating Online Community Health outreach activities such as nutrition awareness sessions / IYCF-E and CMAM consultations for vulnerable.3. Developing remotely/online raising awareness methods especially on COVID-19 prevention and proper handling. 4. Train CHWs on WHO-COVID-19 protection procedures and precautions.Risk#2 Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:Mitigation measures:- Providing preparedness trainings for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents .- Preparing contingency plan to move mobile clinics to safer places .
