Project Summary

The GBV Prevention, Mitigation, and Response for Resilient Communities in Pibor, Pariang, Koch, Malakal, and Akobo project provides an integrated response to GBV within displaced and host communities in South Sudan. This project seeks to reduce GBV incidence and impact through prevention, mitigation, and response activities, while enhancing community resilience and local capacity to prevent GBV. The project adopts a multi-sectoral approach, integrating GBV programming with Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), Health, and Child Protection (CP), ensuring a holistic response to the needs of vulnerable populations.
Additionally, the project incorporates Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), disability-inclusive planning, Gender Age Marker (GAM), GBV safety audits, and the Centrality of Protection strategy. These principles are embedded throughout the project’s design and implementation to ensure effectiveness and sustainability.
Key Activities:
Prevention Activities:
• Community Awareness and Training: Awareness campaigns will engage men, women, boys, and girls to promote gender equality, educate communities about GBV, and shift harmful norms. Evidence-based approaches like Engaging Men and Boys (EMB), Social Analysis and Action (SAA), and intergenerational dialogue will address GBV’s root causes and foster safer, more cohesive communities. These activities complement efforts to create safer environments and contribute to Child Protection (CP) by empowering families to safeguard children.
• Capacity Building: Training will enhance the skills of local leaders, service providers, and law enforcement to prevent, detect, and respond to GBV effectively. Training sessions will integrate GAM principles, ensuring the diverse needs of all genders, age groups, and individuals with disabilities are addressed.
Mitigation Activities:
• Community-Based Protection Networks: Protection groups will be established or strengthened to ensure effective, local-level GBV prevention and response mechanisms. These networks will work with Health, WASH, and FSL sectors to meet survivors’ health, hygiene, and economic needs.
• Safe Spaces and Services: Safe spaces will provide survivors with medical care, psycho-social support, and legal assistance. These spaces will emphasize disability inclusion, ensuring accessibility for all survivors in a safe, supportive environment.
• Engagement with Local Leaders: Local leaders will advocate against harmful practices like early and forced marriage, fostering cultural transformation and promoting Child Protection priorities.
Response Activities:
• Referral Pathways: Strengthened referral systems will ensure survivors can access appropriate, timely services across Health, CP, FSL, and WASH sectors, contributing to Centrality of Protection principles.
• Case Management: Survivor-centered GBV case management will provide holistic and coordinated services, supporting recovery and reintegration.
Reporting Commitment: CARE and its implementing partners will regularly report GBV intervention implementation through the 5Ws and resources mobilized via Financial Tracking Services (FTS). This ensures accountability, transparency, and data-driven decision-making to maximize the project’s impact.

1. Needs

GBV is a pervasive protection concern in South Sudan, fueled by entrenched gender inequality, harmful social norms, and the compounding impacts of conflict, displacement, and climate-related shocks. According to the 2024 Protection Risk Monitoring System (PRMS), GBV was identified as a significant risk in more than one-third of the communities surveyed, underscoring its widespread impact. Findings from the Inter-Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA) revealed that a substantial proportion of women and girls avoid public spaces due to safety concerns, with many households reporting barriers to accessing essential services, including healthcare, legal support, and psychosocial care. These challenges are particularly pronounced in the priority counties of Pariang, Pibor, Koch, Malakal, and Akobo, which CARE International has identified for targeted intervention due to acute needs and significant service gaps.In Malakal, GBV risks are alarmingly high. According to the 2023 report by the UN Commission on Human Rights in South Sudan, titled "Conflict-Related Sexual Violence in South Sudan", widespread sexual violence has been used as a tactic of war, with state actors failing to prevent or punish perpetrators. A December 2023 report by VOA highlighted a surge in GBV cases, driven by cultural factors and ongoing conflict in Upper Nile State. UNFPA reports a critical demand for clinical management of rape and reproductive health services for survivors, but service availability remains insufficient. Displaced women and girls in overcrowded camps are particularly vulnerable, with over 70% of survivors unable to access lifesaving services, leaving thousands without critical support.In Pariang, findings from the July 2024 CARE Rapid Gender Assessment (RGA) revealed that 45% of women and girls lack access to safe spaces, and 63% of households reported an increase in intimate partner violence since displacement in 2023. Economic hardship further exacerbates vulnerabilities, with 60% of households indicating that income loss heightens GBV risks. The lack of referral pathways and minimal presence of service providers severely limits survivors’ ability to access critical support services.In Pibor, a combination of conflict and natural disasters has exacerbated GBV risks, particularly for adolescent girls. Reports from the South Sudanese Ministry of Gender indicate that nearly 60% of girls in the area are married before the age of 18, with many forced into early unions as a negative coping mechanism. Limited community engagement on GBV prevention and harmful practices perpetuate violence, leaving survivors without access to psychosocial or case management support.In Koch, entrenched harmful norms, including child marriage and intimate partner violence, are key drivers of GBV. According to the 2024 PRMS, over 50% of girls are married before the age of 18, while women’s mobility is severely restricted due to threats of violence. This environment perpetuates GBV and creates barriers to accessing vital services, including psychosocial support, legal aid, and case management. Existing service delivery infrastructure is insufficient, necessitating mobile outreach and community-based programming to address these gaps.Akobo remains one of the most underserved areas for GBV prevention and response services. Findings from the 2024 ISNA indicate that 70% of women avoid public spaces due to safety concerns, and adolescent girls frequently report sexual harassment in displacement camps. The lack of safe spaces and limited provision of dignity kits further exacerbate vulnerabilities, leaving women and girls without the resources to safeguard their well-being.The selection of these counties reflects the severity of GBV risks and CARE International’s strategic focus on addressing these challenges. Displaced populations, including returnees, face heightened risks, with the 2024 PRMS reporting that 98% of returnees in South Sudan are exposed

2. Project activities

The GBV Prevention, Mitigation, and Response for Resilient Communities project in Pibor, Pariang, Koch, Malakal, and Akobo addresses gender-based violence (GBV) affecting displaced and host communities in South Sudan. This initiative aims to reduce GBV incidence and impact through prevention, mitigation, and response activities, while enhancing community resilience and capacity. By adopting a multi-sectoral approach, the project integrates GBV programming with Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), Health, and Child Protection (CP), ensuring a holistic response that meets the diverse needs of vulnerable populations.Furthermore, the project aligns with global best practices, incorporating principles such as Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), disability-inclusive planning, the Gender Age Marker (GAM), GBV safety audits, and the Centrality of Protection strategy. These elements are embedded throughout the project's design and implementation to ensure inclusivity, effectiveness, and sustainability.Key Activities:Prevention Activities:• Community Awareness and Social Behavior Change: The project implements campaigns targeting all community members to promote gender equality and foster positive social behavior change. Evidence-based approaches like Engaging Men and Boys (EMB), Social Analysis and Action (SAA), and intergenerational dialogue are employed to address the root causes of GBV, contributing to safer and more cohesive communities. These activities also support Child Protection (CP) by empowering families with the knowledge to safeguard children effectively.• Capacity Building: Training programs are conducted for local leaders, service providers, and law enforcement personnel to enhance their ability to prevent, detect, and respond to GBV. These sessions incorporate GAM principles, ensuring that the diverse needs of all genders, age groups, and individuals with disabilities are addressed across all sectors of the response.Mitigation Activities:• Community-Based Protection Networks: The project establishes or strengthens community-based protection groups to ensure effective local-level GBV prevention and response mechanisms. These networks collaborate with Health, WASH, and FSL sectors to provide a coordinated response to survivors, addressing their health, hygiene, and economic needs as part of an integrated intervention strategy.• Safe Spaces and Services: Safe spaces are set up for GBV survivors, offering a range of services, including medical care, psycho-social support, and legal assistance. These spaces emphasize disability inclusion, ensuring that survivors with disabilities can access the services and support they need in a safe and accessible environment.• Gender Audits: Regular gender audits are conducted to assess and address gender disparities within the community and the project's implementation. These audits help identify potential risks and areas for improvement, ensuring that interventions are gender-responsive and effectively mitigate GBV risks.• Engagement with Local Leaders: The project engages community leaders to challenge harmful practices like early and forced marriage, while also ensuring these leaders advocate for Child Protection (CP) and Training and Awareness priorities to foster long-term cultural and behavioral change.Response Activities:• Referral Pathways: The project strengthens referral systems for survivors, ensuring they can access services in Health, CP, FSL, and WASH sectors, addressing the diverse needs of GBV survivors in a comprehensive manner. • Case Management: Survivor-centered GBV case management is implemented to ensure survivors receive coordinated services, including in Health, Education, and FSL sectors. This integrated support system provides a holistic approach to their recovery and reintegration.
