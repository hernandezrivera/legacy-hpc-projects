Project Summary

Grow Strong Foundation (GSF) shall provide GBV tailored services for 5,569 (70% Women and girls and 30 percent Men and Boys) conflict affected beneficiaries including Internally displaced persons, Host community and returnee populations, in camps and host communities especially for women and girls’ in Bama, Gwoza and Damaturu LGAs in Borno and Yobe states respectively. The project shall respond to the needs of access to services especially linkage to legal services for survivors of Gender based violence for a 12-month duration.
GSF shall provide comprehensive GBV case management to 500 women and girl survivors of Gender based violence using the UN Victim assistance framework and other relevant guiding documents including the Gender based violence minimum standards in emergency setting. Same persons who shall benefit from the case management process shall have access to dignity kit support to protect and improve their dignity, respect, rights and protection across the proposed implementation areas across Borno and Yobe, 1655 women and girls shall benefit from dignity kits support across the two states. 250 survivors will receive socio-economic assistance to empower them to protect themselves and be self-reliant, this would be in form of skills development and support with tools for startup or cash transfer modalities for persons who already have business ideas and were previously engaged in a business. In turn the supported 250 survivors shall better support their families.
To ensure the safety and protection of case managers involved in case management efforts and activities for survivors of GBV, GSF shall ensure that it abides by the principles of confidentiality and anonymity of frontline workers and beneficiary. GSF shall work with also stakeholders from Nigeria Bar Association, Female lawyers’ association for the protection of GBV survivors alongside the security personnel in the communities of intervention. GSF shall emphasize on working with the Human Rights Commission where we would provide trainings to ensure that GBV survivors receive the needed protection required as well as to build on awareness to break the barriers of stigma.
Through establishment of women and girls’ safe spaces, GSF would facilitate life-skill sessions and psychosocial support through women and girls safe space programming and sessions. The space shall promote and ensure counselling for women and girls who have been distressed as a result of their exposure to abuse and risks.
GSF shall through sensitization, community engagement and capacity building on GBV prevention and principles would address, reduce and mitigate negative social and cultural norms that causes violence against women and girls. A total of 2624 beneficiaries shall be reached, 60 percent Men and Boys and 40 percent Men and Boys. Men and boys shall be targeted because they are supposed to be seen as protectors of women and girls rather than perpetrators of the acts of GBV. Awareness messaging shall also focus on PSEA for humanitarian workers as well as for beneficiaries of different genders so as to reduce the risks exploitations by humanitarian and aid workers.
GSF shall leverage on existing community based mechanisms in the implementing LGA to promote and support GBV assistance and activities who are from the community. Where the committees are non-existent GSF shall take the liberty to create new committee members across locations. Through the community based mechanism, GSF shall promote peer to peer learning and support for beneficiaries and community based staff and frontline workers on GBV.
GSF shall support the coordination efforts of the GBV sub sector at the local coordination efforts for where GSF has presence, at the state level, GSF shall support the state level quarterly review workshops as well as end of year review and planning meetings. GSF shall be very instrumental also in ensuring that every three months, the GBV service mapping is conducted with partners.

1 - Needs

The crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states, hereafter referred to as the BAY states is now in its 13th year and the protection as well as humanitarian needs linger. The crisis continues undiminished, and the affected population continues to face increasing hardship. Lack of protection and humanitarian intervention for the population in inaccessible areas remains a serious protection concern, while the closure of camps in late 2021 has also exposed the IDPs in camps around Maiduguri to further protection risks. The Protection needs are arduous, especially for women and girls, who still lack adequate protection and access to basic services, and are at risk of violence, abduction, rape, gender-based violence, forced and child marriage, and other violations of their rights, (2022-2023 Protection strategy)According to the HPC 2022 GBV guidance on needs, there is continues to be glaring gap in the provision of GBV response services in a number of LGAs across the BAY states especially in the hard-to reach areas. In Yobe state for example, there are access challenges in the provision of health and legal services for GBV survivors with only one functional Sexual Assault and Referral Centre (SARC center) located in Damaturu. There is also a lack of safe spaces for women and girls across a number of LGAs. MHPSS and livelihood opportunities have also been highlighted as being inadequate in a number of hard-to-reach areas. The absence of civil authorities, especially court sessions, in several LGAs outside Maiduguri, continues to hinder access to justice and other legal services for affected people including survivors of GBV. The GBV Sub sector will seek to address the highlighted gaps/needs by scaling up GBV response services including MHPSS in these locations.According to a GSF assessment conducted, Gwoza LGA, specifically the GSS camp implementation area is highly vulnerable to conflict and incursion of crisis due to the fragility of the location and their proximity to neighboring communities that are under the influence of VEO group of Boko haram while Bama has some wards under the partial control of violent extremism and many communities are lacking security presence within the locations and still experiencing flashes of attacks and are subjected to conflict prone situation of phone theft, gang fights, drug abuse, burglary, rape, GBV, hatred between IDPs and host communities and thuggery as reveal by the FGDs and the KIIs conducted to stake holders. With a high number of unemployed youth population serving as political thugs, making them most vulnerable to conflict and influence of actors. Furthermore, Bama is known to have an influx of immigrants from a variety of localities, most of whom come with new cultures and behaviors into the communities as a result of displacement by Boko haram attacks. Poor enrollment of school-age children in schools, drug abuse, and gang fights and hatred between IDPs and host communities are the most common issues faced by communities in Bama. Unemployment, drug abuse, and water scarcity are issues are also common and sometimes cause conflict., the Bama community members across have a fair level of trust in the security forces but prefer reporting security related issues to community heads because of the respect they have for the traditional institution. All of the issues faced in these LGAs, expose women and girls to hostile and non-friendly environments thereby the risks of perpetrating GBV in form of rape, intimate partner violence, denial of resources, child marriage due to poverty and survival sex is high and cannot wait.

2 - Activities

1. Provide comprehensive multispectral and lifesaving GBV services aligned to IASC GBV guidelines and UN Victim Assistance framework 2. Support survivors of GBV with dignity kits for their protection and rights.3. Create awareness and risk communication to Boys, Girls and women on GBV risks, PSEA, Child rights and Birth certification.4. Establish and equip safe spaces for women and girls5. Referrals to multi-sectoral services for survivors of GBV.6. Support income generating activities for survivors of GBV7. Conduct GBV risk assessment at each LGA and implement the outcome of the assessment8. Training of institutions, Female lawyers’ association, Nigeria Bar Association and Security personnel on survivor’s safety and confidentiality.9. Support the GBV sub-sector to facilitate quarterly review meetings and monthly LGA level GBV coordination meetings10. Report using the GBV IMS and be part of the Data Gathering organizations.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to affected people is at the heart of humanitarian action. Accountability cannot exist unless organizations create the space and opportunity for people to provide feedback about programmes and services, or to complain about its quality, relevance, and timeliness, or the respect and dignity shown in its delivery. Direct beneficiaries such as young adolescents, community gatekeepers and traditional/religious leaders and government partners will benefit from PSEA Standards model of training. They shall be introduced to referral pathways for reporting suspected cases. Through capacity building on PSEA, community level actors would be able to identify, support and refer cases of PSEA and GBV. The project would leverage on GSF established toll free lines for effective feedback mechanisms.Through the safe space for women and girls established at each project locations, beneficiaries are allowed to have confidential reporting and receive immediate counselling from the case workers and officers that are trained and are drawn from the community.
