Project Summary

Since the beginning of the year, the number of people displaced has continued to rise in northern Mozambique. According to IOM/DTM Baseline Assessment Round 19 August 2023, there were an estimated 946,508 IDPs, an additional 17% compared to 850,599 with an increment of returnees population for a total of 571,468. The conflict has also spread to new and previously calm locations in southern districts of the province causing additional movements and challenges in the response.
The displacements are also putting a strain on poorly developed social services and host communities/families—where more than 70 per cent of the displaced reside.
UNICEF plans to implement education in crises responsive activities across Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa. The main objective is to address the needs of IDP, Host Communities and Returnees affected by the conflict. A total of of 180,315 beneficiaries (179,005 children and 1,310 adults) will benefit from education activities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The Northern Province of Mozambique (Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa) have been experiencing severe consequences of the actions of non-state armed groups/insurgents particularly in the North, since 2017. This protracted crisis reached in August 2023 850,599 IDPs with an increment of returnees population for a total of 571,468 with continuing attacks during the year. The conflict, displacement of communities and school closure not only interrupted educational progresses, but also affected livelihood and hindered children’s access to essential basic services including school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection, health, access to clean water and sanitation services. For instance, in the province of CD the net enrolment is 70% is far lower than the national enrolment rate, which is 93%. As per last year, the district considered safe or safer (in thesouthern area), are not anymore safe and the school have been targeted as shelter relief for IDPs escaping with consequence of not been used for learning porpure and creating interruption on the pupils studies. UNICEF has very actively supportedthe education localauthorities and implementing partners to respond the current EiE needs in its cluster coordination role. Within this current proposal UNICEF will be adapting the response to ensure medium to longer term impact on the education sector through an integrated approach, for example: Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) through a resilient approach, including WASH in schools, distribution of supplies – educational and hygiene materials, teacher training on Psychosocial Support (PSS), including referrals mechanism in collaboration with Child Protection colleagues and on Teachers in Crisis Context (TiCC)but also on ViolenceAgainst Children (VAC), Gender Based Violence (GBV) and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) supporting prevention of violence. At the same time, families, not being able to provide the basics for their children to attend schools. Providing appropriate education and hygiene supplies cannot only ensure physical and emotional protection to children, but also attract parents, caregivers and children to school, ensure their enrollment and prevent dropout. The project will target children, teachers and communities affected by conflict in the provinces in Cabo Delgado,Nampula and Niassa including host communities, displaced, returnees and children with disabilities. Monitoring- the monitoring process will be jointly done with DPE and partners to ensure quality implementation, participation and accountability on the outcomesplanned.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The geographical areas will be the provinces of Cabo Delgado, Niassa and Nampula with a set of comprehensive education activities: 1. Setting up temporary learning spaces (TLS) and latrines to enable to respond to the influx of IDP children and reinforce the capacity of existing school. Equally, it aims to to support the set-up satellite schools located closer o affected community locations (mixed materials TLS and tents); 2. Conducting emergency repairs on education facilities and latrines to meet INEE and Sphere Minimum Standards and ensuring the reopening of schools and/or classrooms that were not being used due to misuse, damage or partial destruction; 3. Providingteaching and learning materials for students and teachers. The distribution of materials includes Learner´s and School-in-a -box kits, assistance devices for children with disabilities and Menstrual Hygiene Management Kitts (MHM); 4. Providing Teachers Training on Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC) international Package (addressing topics like MHPSS and GBV) particularly for teachers teaching in overcrowded classrooms; 5. Providing learning opportunities to develop life-saving skills particularly adolescents and youth, including self-care – hygiene, nutrition, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, violence risk prevention and mitigation, strongly linked with prevention of exploitation and recruitment by armed forces; 6. Conducting back to school campaigns for children, parents and communities; 7. Strengthening the capacity of local Authorities at central, provincial and district level to response on conflict emergency; 8. Providing accelerated learning opportunities for children; 9. Creating alternative learning spaces.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Consultations and Complain mechanism will be in place so that beneficiaries can influence the project design and implementation. Necessarily the proposal will engage teachers, parents and caregivers, throughout Parents and Teacher Associations and School Management Committee engagement, holding UNICEF accountable for theoutcomes committed with. In the monitoring of the process, UNICEF ensures that children are comfortable and engaged listened to, so the activities firstly aim to address their stress and traumas rather than learning and studying. In this monitoring, the communities, parents or caretakers of these children will be as much as involved as possible through creation of reference groups, discussion groups of different sex and age and also using the complaint boxes that will help us improve our implementation. From an organizational point of view, UNICEF is deeply committed to PSEA across all programs and initiatives. The some commitment is demanded from all governmental counterparts and all implementing partners supported by UNICEF.There is a PSEA dedicated team in the country office, supporting PSEAoperationalization, monitoring and evaluation and a focal point is each field office, such as Cabo Delgado and Nampula. From a proposal point of view, all teachers will benefit from training in PSEA and following that they will be able to support identifying and referring and preventing SEA in and around schools.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

A risk assessment process will be carried out respecting the origin of all communities. UNICEF aims to reduce the risks caused by hazards and anything else like social or cultural aspects that can harm children. UNICEF dedicates a significant part of its activities directed at women and children. Because many children and families are hosted by their families in the targeted districts, in our activities we will also involve these members of their families. In the places for children that are identified and permitted by the authorities to play and to do other activities, UNICEF will adhere to and promote all regulations set by the government and MINEDH.Additionally, as a co-lead of the WASH and Education clusters and CP Area of Responsibility, and as active member of the ICCG, UNICEF is gathers the intersectoral know-how to ensure a coordinated response to all protection risks working togetherwith other agencies and organizations, mobilizing stakeholders, resources, avoiding overlaps and leveraging the impact of the intervention.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNICEF will work with NGOs and staff experienced in delivery specific services and support for children with disabilities (CWD) so that they can fully participate in the activities. Therefore as explained above, these activities are organized in good collaboration with the protection, WASH and health cluster and their selected NGOs in order to provide an intersectoral service for all these children. Parents, caregivers andlocal authorities and community will be fully involved in the promotion of inclusive activities and education. In the long run, facing and overcoming challenges and reducing the prejudices from the general public and increasing their chances to actively participate in society will help them to grow stronger and more resilient.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The selected location of education facilities will be preceded by an evaluation of the location in collaboration with the local authorities, so that the location can be considered safe with materials that are safe for children to use, free of natural hazards (floods, storms) and clean with basic WASH conditions, and with confirmation on responsibilities for maintaining security, WASH and protection. At the same time the activities will aim to raise environmental awareness and protection ofnatural resources and avoiding erosion for instance by planting fruit and shade trees, creating living fences, grass planting and directing and harvesting rainwater.
