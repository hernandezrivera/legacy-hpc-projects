Project Summary

The priority provinces for 2024 are targeted to strengthen the national capacity to improve access to quality treatment for 382,015 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition in the existing nutrition treatment units, both the outpatient (OTPs) and inpatient units (IPFs), while strengthening preventive nutrition services at community and health facility levels. This will include i) the provision of nutrition therapeutic supplies including RUTF as well as medicine for the timely and quality treatment of children suffering from SAM and medical complications deriving from SAM and ii) Strengthening the capacity of SAM treatment of health structures and an effective nutritional surveillance system and iii) strengthening of nutrition preventive interventions through awareness raising activities using the promotion of optimal infant and young child feeding practices as an entry point. This action will also contribute to the measles response through Vitamin A supplementation for 1,120,000 children.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Needs Chad is one of the world’s least developed countries. It is currently ranked 190th out of 191 countries on the Human Development Index (HDI). All indicators confirm the high level of poverty affecting large segments of the population. In 2011, 66.3% of the population was living below the poverty line of 3.20 dollars a day. The value of 44.7 world hunger index in 2020 reflects a steady decline in poverty since 2000 (50.9). Nevertheless, the country is ranked last in the index, with index and has "alarming" levels of hunger. The poverty rate is 42.4 for male-headed households and 42.1% for female-headed households. Around 2.4 million Chadians (or just over 15% of the population) live in extreme poverty (ECOSIT4 ). Chad is facing persistent humanitarian problems linked to the growing insecurity in some parts of the country and in neighboring countries, as well as socio-economic health and climatic challenges, all within a context of political transition. Exposure to the effects of climate change, the scale of violence perpetrated by non-state armed groups in the Lake Chad Basin, the fragile security situation in neighboring countries and inter inter-community conflicts in a context of underdevelopment all contribute to perpetuating the humanitarian situation in Chad.Malnutrition takes the lives and futures of children. It stunts their growth and puts them at higher risk for diseases, diminishing their capacity to fully function, leaving them vulnerable to developmental delays, and death. Wasting and other forms of malnutrition are the result of maternal malnutrition, low birthweight, poor feeding and care practices, and infection exacerbated by food insecurity, limited access to safe drinking water, and poverty. The results of the SMART survey conducted in September 2022 shows that the nutritional situation remains critical in most Sahel Belt provinces, even though overall acute malnutrition has decreased to 8.6% as compared to 10.9% in 2021. This decrease could be attributed to the survey being conducted at the end of the harvest season, when food is more available and accessible, as well as to the actions carried out by different humanitarian actors in 2022. Nevertheless, 11 provinces are still recording a prevalence above the WHO threshold of 10% (alert). At national level, severe acute malnutrition (SAM) is estimated at 1.5% according to the survey, as compared to a 2% prevalence in 2021. This worrying situation could be exacerbated in 2024 by low agricultural production. Indeed, according to the September 2023 report from the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS Net), a significant delay in the cropping cycle has been reported in agricultural and agro-pastoral areas due to the poor season (long dry spells, low rainfall). Crop losses have been reported at Lake Chad, with a drop of around 10% in the area sown. In addition to the rainfall disruption, the occupation of farmland by refugees has led to losses of 300 and 200 hectares respectively in Sila and Ouaddai provinces Low levels of rainfall, coupled with repeated long dry spells during the rainy season, have led to a deterioration in the pastoral situation in agro-pastoral and transhumance areas. The condition of the animals has been severely affected and is below that of a normal year. Also, per the September 2023 FEWSNet report, markets in the Sahelian zone are showing a drop in the supply of imported food products, particularly in localities bordering Sudan, due to the cessation of cross-border flows. Low agricultural production will lead to low food availability in vulnerable households, with an increase in cases of acute malnutrition. Activities 1.)
