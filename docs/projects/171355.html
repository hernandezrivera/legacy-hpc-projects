Project Summary

The project will contribute to the Food Security Sector Strategic Objective #3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The objective of the project is to increase food security resilience of vulnerable Palestinian through the provision of essential services and promotion of agricultural activities in Qalqiliya governorate. The project will focus on vulnerable farmers and their communities by providing rehabilitation of their agricultural land, including improved irrigation systems, enhancing access through rehabilitation of agricultural roads, along with emergency technical support; in order to increase agricultural yields. The project also aims to address violations and other threats faced by Palestinian farmers, by increasing knowledge of referral pathway regarding any legal issues and confiscation of agricultural lands along with advocacy and localized awareness campaigns. In order to do so, SIF will cooperate with NRC, through its partners ESDC, especially for awareness training on rights violations. The choice of Qalqiliya governorate has been informed by the launching in September 2019 of the Strategic plan by the local authorities for this governorate identifying critical needs for intervention in agriculture and spelling out the main challenges.

1 - Needs

Ministry of Agriculture’s strategy on agricultural development highlights the fertile qualityof Qalqiliya’s terrain, the water availability and the temperate climate that allows for theproduction of tropical produce like guava, avocado, mango, that are accompanied withwillingness from the agricultural sector to incorporate new and improved agriculturaltechniques. Upon the launch of the "Three Years Development Clusters Plan" in WB in 2019 and as the first work plan to be launched was in Qalqiliya cluster, SIF has been in contact with the MoA and conducted a meeting in September 2019 to discuss the potential interventions to respond to the needs in Qalqiliya Governorate. MoA has put SIF in direct contact with the Agriculture Directorate of Qalqiliya; meetings were conducted at the Directorate in the presence of the different departments including: the planning, technical, women development and services departments to discuss the needs in further details. These needs were validated and updated later on in October 2020 for the sake of designing this project.Agriculture in Qalqiliya governorate amounts in total to 86% rain fedagriculture and 14% irrigated agriculture (Summary documenthttp://www.palestinecabinet.gov.ps/WebSite/Upload/Documents/GOVPLAN/3naqeed3.pdf.Pdf with strategy الخطة-التنموية-للقطاع-الزراعي-محافظة-قلقيلية ). Although it is consideredas one of the most important governorates in the West Bank in terms of agriculture, thegovernorate faces challenges that are political, relating to natural resources (land, water),technical facilities and expertise. Political challenges due to the fact that 20% of its area isIsraeli settlements and 74% is area C, where the majority of agricultural land and availablearea for future urban development are located. Enhancing agricultural activities canstrengthen farmers' resilience in their land as a safe way to meet the challenges of thewall and the nearby settlements. Total land area of the Qalqiliya governorate is 173 km.The arable area is about 125,000 dunums of which only 70,000 dunums are undercultivation, and 25,000 dunums are in need for agricultural reclamation. This can beattributed to: the high costs of reclamation and rehabilitation of agricultural land;difficulties to work in lands next to the wall and near settlements, with threat ofconfiscation; lack of adequate agricultural roads, problem of wild boars in the area.Regarding irrigation, although Qalqiliya is located on the western groundwater basin,which accounts for 52% of the total water resources in the West Bank, Israel exploitsabout 80 percent of Palestinian groundwater in the West Bank. Qalqiliyagovernorate faces many challenges related to the water available for agriculture: lack of water resource in the Eastern part of the governorate; where most of the agriculturalactivities are rain fed; worn-out main water lines; use of diesel for most of the agriculturalgroundwater wells with its result on the water prices; limited water harvesting projects.Qalqiliya suffers with the rest of the West Bank of the phenomenon of an increase ofwomen headed households. (www.pcbs.gov.ps/post.aspx?lang=en&ItemID=3503).According to the update at the end of 2018 of the PCBS, 12% of the households in the West Bankare women-headed households. Due to the restrictive Israeli occupation, some womenhave become widows or have lost their husbands to prison, while other men are strugglingto find employment. Due to discrimination in formal workforces and society’s assumptionthat women should not be responsible for household finances, a large majority of womenin the West Bank are technically unemployed; although almost all of them are working insome way. Most women are responsible for unpaid work within the home, and 85 percentof unpaid women in Gaza and West Bank work in agriculture. Therefore, while manywomen contribute to the economy through the agriculture value chain, they are still unpaidor underpaid for their work. Despite this major contribution, an estimated 40 percent ofrural Palestinian women at working age carry out unpaid work, which means that theirproduction is not comprehensively covered in the Gross Domestic Product (GDP). Thismarginalizes their role in the production process. SIF is very much aware of this challengeas will work with the communities to increase acceptance of the work of women inagriculture and support their empowerment.

2 - Activities

In October 2020, SIF has conducted another field visit to Qalqiliya and met with the Director of Directorate and representatives from the different departments to validate the needs and interventions were discussed in details in terms of locations, design of activities, quantities … etc. The project mainly aims to increase food security resilience and access to agricultural lands of vulnerable population in the governorate of Qalqiliya. In order to do so, the activities will be based on 3 main results: 1. 540 dunum of land for 150 farmers (range of 3.6 dunum each) is rehabilitated in a comprehensive way. 2. 10km of agricultural roads are rehabilitated to benefit 400 farmers. 3. 200 individuals (98 females – 102 males) are taught how to provide protection to their communities in regards to legal rights specifically regarding land rights and referral procedures. Main activities per result: Result 1: Comprehensive Rehabilitation of 540 dunum of land for 150 farmers’ households (743 individuals [365 females, 378 males]) aiming to also improve the quality of the agriculture through provision of emergency technical support to targeted farmers. a) Comprehensive Rehabilitation of 100 dunums including 20 agricultural cisterns, building of terraces, and levelling for 20 farmers’ households (98 individuals [48 females, 50 males]. b) Rehabilitation of irrigation carrying lines (4 KM of pipes and other spare parts); will benefit an estimate of 80 farmers’ households (for 240 dunums) – (392 individuals [192 females, 200 males]. c) Emergency technical support for men and women farmers. d) Collective fencing of agricultural lands for 200 dunums benefiting 50 farmers’ households - (245 individuals [120 females, 125 males]). SIF and ESDC will be identifying potential farmers’ households via household visits and interviews applying a socio-economic scoring selection. The head of the woman-headed household will be given a care time survey to ensure the performance of the agricultural-related activities will not be a hindrance to their regular child caring and chores. Result 2: 10km of Agricultural roads will be rehabilitated, benefitting 400 farmers’ households (1,960 individuals [960 females, 1,000 males]). Selection of the roads will be done through advertisement in the village council; pplication by farmers; identification of priority roads with technical visit and assessment of costs. Result 3: Awareness raising of the targeted communities in regards to the Israeli land’s regulation in area C. ESDC will coordinate with NRC who will start with provision of Legal awareness rising in targeted villages: a) ESDC will follow up with the constitution of local technical committees (one per each target community with around 5 members) to be able to follow up activities on the ground. This will take place during the startup stakeholders’ meeting. The members will sign a MoU together with the ToR of the committees, to ensure transparency in the implementation. b) 10 awareness sessions, on lands rights violation and other legal right that will target 170 from community and 30 local committee members (98 females – 102 males). c) Production related video or photo exhibit to raise awareness on the land issues For all aspect of the project SIF will be providing a complaint number with targeted communities for suggestions or complaints through the project life. The participation of vulnerable groups in program planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation will help to ensure that these programs are responsive to protection concerns; Coordinate with relevant actors can substantially improve a program’s impact in terms of protection. In that regards SIF and ESDC will cooperate with NRC, already established intervention. And will also be providing a complaint hotline number in municipalities for suggestions or complaints about all beneficiaries through the project life. We will be indicating a DO NO HARM principle, (https://emergency.unhcr.org › entry ›humanitarian-principles). SIF interventions in the Palestinian Territory have a principal approach of individual and group physical and psychological integrity, self-respect and empowerment. The project mission is to enhance the quality of life of Palestinian communities and it is in-line with SIF values, especially alleviating the suffering of the poorest, respecting human dignity, and the beneficiaries’ diversity (cultural, religious or social). Human dignity is inherent to every human being, inalienable and independent of the state and occupation which is highly respected by SIF. The necessary realism of proper humanitarian assistance cannot merely be reduced to technical know-how bereft of ideals; thus solidarity and respect to human dignity are paramount. Development is not solely in economic terms it has a combination of social, political, financial, natural, physical and spiritual expression. SIF core values are impartial, independent and neutral the interventions are grounded in the idea of solidarity with the people it helps, the focus of the projects are in building the local capacity and resilience of communities by providing support to vulnerable populations enabling communities to better prepare for and respond to the situation on the ground. This project brings unity in taking pride, being honourable, noble and worthy bringing a sense of pride to the beneficiaries to till their lands. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

SIF beneficiaries’ selection methodology is a fair, transparent and accountable system with blueprinted procedures incorporated in the mission’s implementation of projects. A multi-faceted approach is applied considering the various levels of involvement for all stakeholders. The approach consists of · Kick-off meeting/discussion with local councils (3 phase discussion approach: we introduce SIF, we introduce the project and the guidelines for steering committee selection) · Steering committee selection and verification. · Public information sessions in the selected villages about SIF (4’Ws) open to the whole community, an announcement for submission of applications. · NRC risk assessment community information sessions. · Application submission process. · All applications are entered into a scientific and mathematical database system providing scoring for each beneficiary. · Field visits with technical and social aspects are conducted to validate the beneficiary selection. · Scoring system application list verification and review with the steering committee. · Publication of the list in the selected villages. · Response to feedback mechanism. · Final list for selected beneficiaries. Moreover, information dissemination through announcements; for transparency and credibility with communities; we announce through village councils' and others CBO’s Facebook pages and through mosques calls in addition to keeping the old method of the announcement (written announcement to be published in allpublic places). After finalizing the selection of beneficiaries the list will be announced and feedback from the community will be taken into consideration. SIF West Bank applies a beneficiary participatory approach to its projects in many phases of humanitarian activities such as assessment, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. There are community discussions, presentations and awareness sessions, feedback mechanisms in addition to pre and post-assessments. Additionally, SIF West Bank’s community-based interventions are based on potential, promise, and performance. SIF will provide all the beneficiaries with the opportunity to be advised about the intervention, take feedback, have access to information concerning the project and organization and integrate them with key decisions made during the project implementation. Furthermore, committees help SIF to understand deeply the community needs by gathering information relevant to the design of the project's intervention in terms of geographical locations, and needs. As providing information and gathering feedback on the design is one of the main components of accountability to beneficiaries, public announcements will be revised with the presence of the project committee and published in key locations around the village to give equal chance to the community to apply.
