Project Summary

All indicators show that Gedaref state suffers from many critical challenges in providing health services to both the local community and the refugees. The intervention aims to fill the gaps in curative health care and preventive aspects. Preventive and curative actions will include supporting the health system to provide quality health services, mobilizing the community to raise awareness of preventable health problems, and promoting healthy behavior.
Improving environmental conditions, such as water, to minimize the spread of certain environment-related diseases is another key action that will be implemented. The fact that this particular geographic area is at high risk for natural and man-made disasters makes preparedness planning a priority action in order to respond in a timely manner when such a situation occurs. This action must be supported by appropriate data collection and analysis in order to make evidence-based decisions without delay. This could go a long way toward minimizing the impact of delayed actions, for example, in responding to epidemics.
Availability of some specialized heath services on in few centers make it almost impossible for majority of the people to pay for transportation to go to those centers for such services. Some of these cases are life threatening emergencies such as obstructed labor or bleedings associated with pregnancy and child birth. This intervention will ensure the timely transportation of such patients to the centers providing such services through the support of referral system.
Protection is another issue which needs to be considered as one of the key interventions for vulnerable groups. These services are intended for survivors of GBV cases, children and some groups among the general population with some forms of disabilities.
The area being one of those areas identified as having high burden of malnutrition makes it critical to support the nutrition interventions in different forms. This includes proper screening of children aged under five years and PLW using MUAC and referring them on time to treatment centers. The treatment centers for malnutrition cases will be supported to ensure quality services and proper follow up of children admitted to the programs. Logistic support for transportation of supplies such as RUTF and RUSF will be given to each treatment center to avoid stock rapture of these supplies. Nutrition team will also be trained on different topics to build their capacity in handling of cases, reporting and follow up of children admitted to the center.
Community volunteers will be identified among the communities under the catchment areas of the health facilities and receive trainings on communication skills, different topics related to health promotion and on how to do MUAC screening properly for children and PLW.

1 - Needs

According to the health cluster forecast, 9.1 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance for health in 2022 of which 2.7 million are in severe condition and 4.6 million are categorized as extreme condition as projected by the sector. Of this population, 3 million are IDPs and the vast majority (65%) are women. The major driving factors for the deteriorating humanitarian situation in Sudan are the annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, border conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis. The key health indicators show the worsening situation in the country. Availability of emergency medicines has reached 43% and the Pentavalent vaccine drop out this year is 8%. The available health work force is enough to cover only 17% of Sudan population and the price of medicine has increased by 1000% . According to the multi-sectoral need assessment (MSNA) conducted towards the end of 2020, 85% of the surveyed households across different states reported different barriers to access health services including lack of medicines in health facilities and cost of services (REACH multi-sectoral assessment report 2020). 98% of the assessed households in MSNA in 2020 reported that they pay for health services, mainly medicines. Among the reported barriers to access healthcare, 70% reported lack of medicines in the health facilities, 66% reported that the costs for the services were too high and 26% reported transportation means to reach health facilities was too high. All the health facilities assessed in the area are facing structural weaknesses in terms of access to water, storage capacity of water, hygiene protocols and practices, vector control, waste management and electricity. These weaknesses prevent the good functioning of the health centers.Gedaref State has 12 localities/districts and Basonda is one of them. Basonda locality has been categorized with 4 out of 5 in terms of severity according to nutrition sector HNO 2022 with stunting rank of 5 severity. Based on the 2022 Nutrition cluster estimate, the locality has 6,243 children categorized as GAM of which 1,150 are severely malnourished and 172 of them need SC treatment due to additional complications. The GAM estimate for children is 14.3% and SAM estimates is 2.6% for this locality based on the HNO 2022. Al Fashega, the other locality of Gedaref state categorized as level 4 severity has total under-five population of 3,885 of which 1,251 are GAM and 273 are severely malnourished. 41 of the SAM children are expected to be with additional complications which need to be treated at SC. The GAM rate for children is 11.3% and SAM rate is 2.5% for this locality. Malaria has been a leading cause of OPD consultation according to the monthly epidemiologic bulletin of the Eastern Sudan emergency response coordination for the Ethiopian refugees during March with about 6% of the total OPD consultations followed by dysentery, typhoid and leishmaniosis (Monthly epidemiologic Bulletin, March 2021). In one or the other, these morbidities are related with poor water supply or poor environmental factors which favour the transmission of the diseases. In terms of epidemic prone diseases, Gedaref State reported 3 positive polio cases between 2020/21 (out of 58 nationally reported) until April 2021. The highest number of cases per state (11) was reported from Kassala which shares a border with Gedaref, and 5 cases from Gezira state, another neighbouring state, making Gedaref a high risk to further outbreak of polio virus.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

PUI is strongly committed to monitor the potential environmental impact of their interventions. The organization will implement a waste management plan that aims at mitigating the risks associated with hazardous waste. These risks could be significantly reduced through simple measures (reduction and sorting of waste, reduction of storage and transportation, training and sensitization). The reduction of waste is also encouraged by reducing the amount at source (choosing products that generate less waste, equipment that can be reused).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI strive to implement the principles of cross-cutting protection in the implementation of their activities, namely : - The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries - Ensure effective, ethical and meaningful access to the assistance delivered - Accountability, participation and empowerment of people During the construction and rehabilitation of the various targeted services (health centers, latrines, schools, etc.), special attention will be paid to the accessibility of these sites to people with reduced mobility. Specific adjustments will be made to the facilities in order to guarantee identical access to all persons from the community. When delivering information messages (selection of beneficiaries, presentation of intervention modalities, complaint mechanism, etc.), organizations will ensure that the information is heard and understood by all, without retention or bias. Their representativeness and consideration will therefore be ensured at all stages of the project cycle. Prevention of negative effects of assistance provided PUI will take particular care to avoid any negative effects or tensions that could result from the process of selecting beneficiaries and/or localities to be targeted ("do no harm" principle). The inclusion of communities in the decisions helps prevent unintended negative impacts while guaranteeing the dignity and supporting the legitimacy of local leaders. Access to assistance and services is tailored to all individuals and groups in order to prevent potential restrictions in accessibility caused by physical or social vulnerabilities that beneficiaries may suffer. PUI will ensure that the most vulnerable people are not left out of the selections and have equal access to the services targeted by this project by, for example, conducting individual household visits and collecting potential complaints and claims regarding vulnerability criteria and the selection process.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The complaint mechanism is an essential component of the accountability of programs at PUI. The mechanism covers a wide range of stakeholders, starting with affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors. Complaints are a formal expression of dissatisfaction or dissatisfaction with something and / or resentment of someone who calls for an answer. They may to carry on: i) The quality of the work carried out on the program. This may include: timeliness, safety and relevance activities. This includes complaints about targeting, the quality of items or services, and the failure to keep promises. (ii) The behaviour of staff or volunteers. This may include: a lack of respect for the members of the community; allegations of corruption, nepotism, favouritism or discrimination; or allegations of allegations of sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation. The process is carried out in real time to allow external stakeholders to express their concerns, complain or provide feedback on PUI activities in a given field at any time. Three different "entry points" are used for this purpose: the establishment of a telephone number, a "complaints box" in health centers, and a dedicated e-mail address for collecting comments. The PUI complaint mechanism is characterized by four criteria, it is: 1) Clearly defined: since the term "comments" is a broad concept, the complaints mechanism is framed in such a way that stakeholders are fully aware of "what they can comment on". 2) Inclusive: the mechanism allows all key program stakeholders to access and use it. Special attention is given to " give voice" to the most vulnerable people in the communities and those who may have difficulty for some reason in expressing their reactions/views. 3) Safe and confidential: Individuals, groups or entities must feel safe in their interactions with the mechanism set up by PUI. The potential fear of retaliation is clearly addressed and mitigated. 4) Responsive: PUI has a responsibility to provide stakeholders who provide feedback with an effective response to the comment. Received. Trainings related to the MEAL Pack are regularly carried out by the HQ or the field. In fact, these tools are already understood and appropriated by the teams. However, discussions are regularly organized to discuss ways to improve the mechanisms in place and to adapt them to the context and needs.
