Project Summary

Help a Child (HAC) is an International Christian humanitarian and development organization based in the Netherlands. Help a Child International works has country offices in India, South Sudan, Rwanda, Burundi, Malawi, DRC, and Kenya, and works in Uganda and Somalia through partners.
HAC South Sudan currently implements an EU-funded UNICEF Education in Emergencies (EiE) project in Wau County of Western Bahr-el-Ghazal State, UNICEF UNICEF-funded Early Childhood Development (ECD) project in Pibor and a privately funded child protection and ECD project Kids@Risk in Pibor. Additionally, we are the lead of the South Sudan Joint Response (2024-2026) implemented in Tonj North and Pibor.
In Pibor, Help a Child is the State Education Cluster Lead, and at the national level, it is a member of the Technical Working Group (TWG) of the education cluster on EiE, the board chair for the National Education Coalition (NEC), and active member of the Western Bahr-el-Ghazal state education cluster. HAC has experience constructing Semi-Permanent Learning Spaces (SPLSs) from its GPE-funded UNICEF project in Central Equatoria where it constructed 8 SPLSs and is currently constructing 4 Climate Resilience Classrooms (CRCs) in Wau. The constructions are in line with INEE Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies.
This project will target IDPs, Returnees, and Host Communities in Aweil East and Aweil South Counties of Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal States, Pibor County in Jonglei State, and Tonj North County of Warrap State. Climate change has played a very negative role in the current crisis in these counties where flooding has not only affected farming causing hunger and displacement but also contributed to the closing of schools. Moreover, the lack of qualified teachers is a common challenge, and the few existing schools are incapacitated to provide the relevant teaching and learning needs of the affected children. Through this intervention, HAC South Sudan will deliver education supplies to both learners and teachers and construct accessible pit latrines with changing rooms. The program will also ensure that volunteer teachers are trained on basic pedagogical, psychosocial support (PSS), classroom management skills, and the South Sudan new curriculum. They will be supported with monthly incentives for the project duration regulated by the National Education Cluster. We will also include advocacy through Boruboru innovative approach to pass strong messages to the stakeholders.

1. Needs

Education is a basic human right, hence all children regardless of their race, social status, age or disability are entitled to access it. According to the Humanitarian Need Response Plan (HNRP) the assessed education needs in 2024 include the increasing obstacles with access to education for up to 2 million children and adolescents between 6 and 17 years of age (53% being girls). Obstacles include early marriage and GBV, especially for girls. With increasing instances of conflict and violence - both because of the Sudanese conflict and internal ethnic or clan-based conflict - there is a growing number of IDPs and returnees, which calls for immediate response. As of July 2024, there were over 311,000 IDPs, 1,535,000 resident communities, and 189,000 returnees in the country, especially in towns at extreme border points (HNRP 2024). The education disparities in the selected locations are stipulated, with Pibor boasting the following disaggregated data of 500 (300 male, 200 female) children aged 3-5 years, 888 (508 males, 380 female) children aged 3-17 years, and 2,500 (1,500 male, 1000 female) children aged 6-17 years out of school, and 50 (30 males, 20 female) adult officials lacking the basic skills to monitor and support the normal running and monitoring of education activitiesMeanwhile, the country’s education system is weakened by inadequate policy dissemination, insufficient teacher training, and a lack of essential resources. Teachers struggle with pedagogical skills, classroom management, and implementing the new curriculum. The few qualified teachers in South Sudan have moved to work for development organizations in search of better pay as the government has failed to pay their wages. Severe shortages of teaching materials, including chalk and textbooks, disrupt instruction, with up to eight students sharing one book. Efforts to distribute textbooks have fallen short. Additionally, poor infrastructure, such as the absence of gender-sensitive pit latrines and sanitation facilities, further hinders learning, particularly in areas such as Tonj North County, where open defecation is common. These issues collectively undermine effective teaching and student engagement.

2. Project activities

Our plan is to enhance equitable access to secure, protective, and high-quality education to empower individuals for a much more prosperous life for 29, 388 (18,208 males, 11,180 females) children aged between 3-17 in Pibor, Aweil East, Aweil South, and Tonj North Counties of Jonglei, Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal, and Warrap States respectively. Equitable access will cover the provision of teaching and learning materials to the teachers and learners, building WASH facilities with a particular focus on girls and children with disabilities. Help a Child with the State Ministry of General Education and Instructions (SMOGEI) in the prioritized states will work closely with the local education stakeholders, the parents, PTAs/SMCs, church leaders, and other bodies to create awareness of the importance of education for all children. Help a Child will also target government stakeholders, CSOs, and the public on the key themes of early and forced marriages, girls' education, and gender-based violence (GBV) through the Girl Child Education Day celebration to challenge the existing gender disparities affecting girls' enrollment and retention as 53% of the total 2.8 million Out of school children are girls (UNICEF 2023). The event will be championed by the girls and female role models in passing strong messages on the importance of the education of all children regardless of their gender and the potential that education girls and women have. The project, in line with the education cluster strategic objectives, will strengthen the capacities of teachers, PTAs/SMCs, Counties, and Payam education supervisors to enhance their monitoring skills throughout the project. Teachers will be trained on basic pedagogical and classroom management skills, life skills, South Sudan's new curriculum, and PSS. The teachers will also get trained on the South Sudan Code of Conduct for teachers. The project will also ensure the formation of peace clubs which will champion the school-led back-to-school campaigns as part of the strategies to advocate for the enrolment of the out-of-school childrenThe project will also ensure the purchase, transportation, and distribution of education supplies to school children in the targeted schools in the 4 counties. The construction of With the multi-sectoral approach, schools will receive multiple services from different partners working in Child Protection, GBV, Nutrition, and other essential forms of intervention to form a complete set of responses for the sustainability of the project.
