Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to deliver and sustain the existing low cost, high impact essential emergency health services to the remaining catchment population of 4,784 female and 4,416 in Duk while in Twic East 3,883 female and 3,584 male (HRP 2020) respectively who are not reached by SSHF SA1/2 with a focus to women, children & returnees.
The goal of the project is to contribute to the reduction in health related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high quality Lifesaving emergency health interventions for the most vulnerable populations notably U5 children and pregnant and lactating women.
The project target group is composed of U5 (at least 80% of the beneficiaries, boys and girls equally targeted) and P&LW women (at least 40% of the beneficiaries) from host and returnees communities with curative and preventive services.
With funding from UNICEF/WB/UKAID, JDF in partnership with CHD is supporting only 10 health facilities out of 16 health facilities in Duk County while in Twic East 7 health facilities out of 10 health facilities are supported. This project will allow JDF to expand services in both Duk and Twic East counties filling health services gaps not supported by WB/UNICEF funding.
The project will implement 2 County Hospital, 2 PHCU, 2 PHCC with two mobile unit in Twic East and Duk County supporting all the preventive and curative packages to the most vulnerable group of the rural communities and islands.
The package of services to be supported includes Child health services, Maternal and neonatal health services, Basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care, Sexual and gender-based violence services, Distribution of essential medicines and supplies , Emergency preparedness and response, Disease surveillance and outbreak response and quality improvement and supervision. This programme is relevant in addressing problems identified by strengthening Provision of Essential Health Services with particular focus on maternal and child health care both preventive and curative services to the most vulnerable group of the rural communities supported by pre-positioning of essential drugs, medical equipment & supplies to all the targeted health facilities as well as strengthening health facilities and capacity of health staffs to respond to quality health services in the two counties.
These frontline interventions will be supported in specific areas with the roll-out of community-based health services (BHI) to bolster community resilience and basic services provision. This combined with emergency preparedness and response will ensure service continuity.

This project intends to contribute to the reduction of excess morbidity and mortality by scaling up the provision of high quality essential and lifesaving emergency primary health services with fully integrated WASH, Nutrition, FSL, Protection, Education, psychosocial support services for the most vulnerable populations notably U5 children and pregnant and lactating women.

1 - Needs

After decades of conflict, massive displacement of the population, widespread insecurity, and lack of public funding, the health care system as well as the health status of the people of South Sudan has suffered enormously. Health status of the population in South Sudan, especially children, is dire as evidenced by some of the health-related indicators. The under-five mortality rate and maternal mortality ratio are among the highest in the world at 91 per 1000 live births (CME 2016) and 789 per 100,000 live births(WHO 2015) respectively. These targeted counties are inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people, persons with disabilities who are physically and psychologically exposed to multitude of stressors. Access to basic services in the area is literally nonexistent with population who are subjected to protracted conflict, poor economic conditions and disruption of livelihoods.In addition, demand for basic health services is high and 60% of population does not have access to health care with only a fraction of the population having access to health care services. The disease outbreaks such as cholera and measles continue to be a significant burden.These counties are in the cluster hot spot map in which the risk of waterborne diseases remains high with many households having no access to improved water sources contributing to sporadic measles and AWD outbreaks despite an integrated vaccination campaign outreaches in the Host communities (EPI March 2019).The counties primary health care system has many challenges, such as insufficient qualified staff, poor infrastructure, inadequate health facilities, lack of drugs and medical supplies despite of significant efforts made by the MoH and NGOs.JDF did not conduct a detail assessment. However, from the available information in registers in 17 health facilities, the analysis of RRHP Report 2019 for the period Jan – June 2019 evidenced that the coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely lowest with ANC 1 at 20% and ANC 4 at 8% while Skilled delivery is less than 6%, since few health units are permanently staffed with skilled birth attendants. The unmet need for family planning remains high with only 4% utilizing services (RRHP reports 2019). Child Health indicators are alarming with low immunization coverage which is extremely very poor with Penta3 coverage of only 14% (MOH EPI 2019), this situation was further complicated by looting of cold chain equipment and limited access to and difficulty in vaccine supply to remote areas; therefore, children under the age of 5 is at risk of measles outbreaks (RRHP reports 2019). There is also high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (80%), diarrhoea (15%) and pneumonia (5%) in U 5 years which contributes to 70% of outpatient consultations and calls for high sensitization campaigns for timely health seeking behaviour, good hygiene practices and public health initiatives (RRHP Report 2019). Malnutrition is high, GAM at 27.4 %, MAM at 21.5% and SAM at 5.9%which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention (HDC SS report 2019). These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency health care for the next 12 month to ensure adequate availability and accessibility of health services to the communities in the two counties. JDF activities will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL clusters including provision of services pertaining to clinical management of rape and mental illnesses for people with disabilities and mental disorders who are included as vulnerable groups owing to their high risk during emergencies. JDF response modalities will be static, fixed and mobile response mechanism in an integrated approach.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Prevention strategies to avoid protection issues will be duly taken into consideration by ensuring that the mobile response are close to the population so that they are not exposed to protection incidents. Gender balance will be ensured during recruitment of health personnel. Health workers will be trained on the clinical management of rape and protection issues concerning women and child abuse. JDF will ensure that services are targeting beneficiaries including men, women, boys and girls, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority group. Referral pathways will be identified between communities, health facilities and safe houses for the clients facing gender based violence.appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need to make informed decisions, provide feedback on the humanitarian interventions, share concerns and submit complaints. The project will ensure that information dissemination includes, as minimum, accessible and timely information on cluster strategies, targeting criteria, geographical focus, services provided and entitlements, programmatic changes, and community feedback and complaints mechanisms; The project will translate messages into local languages and deliver them through context appropriate, protection sensitive methods and channels such as, face-to-face meetings, notice boards, radio, mobile phones, and public fora, community outreach workers, religious leaders, training and awareness-raising sessions, social groups and community centres; Ensure that visible and accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, tailored to the operational context and the preferences of communities, which can appropriately handle complaints like violations of the law (such as sexual exploitation and abuse),institutional policies / codes of conduct; and quality andappropriateness of humanitarian programmes

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The accountability to the affected people is essential ingredients for protection analysis for responsible use of power, combined with effective and quality programming that recognizes the community’s dignity, capacity, and ability for self-determination as well as strengthening accountability, investments in direct and sustained engagement with communities play a key role in securing protection outcomes. This in turn requires will a transparent and continuous two-way communication that captures records and relays the expressed risks, needs, capacities and aspirations of communities. JDF is accountable to the women, men, boys and girls, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority groups, and persons of diverse sexual orientation or gender identity whose lives it aims to improve, and places this responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. The project will take into the account of a broad spectrum of the community that must be able to participate in decision-making in a way that is inclusive, nondiscriminatory and has real influence in the humanitarian response; for communities to engage meaningfully and to make informed decisions, the project will establish two-way communication. Dialogue, furthermore, cannot be limited to the simple exchange of information but needs to contribute to programme design, management and delivery, pursued regularly and aligned with the different phases of the programme cycle.Meetings with communities and county Health Department will be more frequent and periodic and will be monitored through indicators and targets for AAP. Data collected will be dis aggregated by gender, so as to identify any unique needs to the gender and this information will be shared to beneficiaries through community meetings.
