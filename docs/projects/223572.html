Project Summary

The project is aiming at addressing the basic health challenges (PHC services, Reproductive Health Services and routine immunization services and campaign) faced by internally displaced persons and host communities, especially women and children of the conflict affected persons as well as host communities in hard to reach areas of Giedam, Nguru, Bade and Nguru LGAs of Yobe state. In the target location, CBI shall be building the capacity of primary healthcare workers on effective delivery of integrated primary health care and sexual reproductive health services and facilitating referrals to secondary healthcare facilities as well as follow-up on services for which referrals were made. Support the provision of and making available essential drugs and medicine to ten (10) health facilities within the LGAs. CBI will train 50 primary health care workers which includes CHEWs (25), Nurses (10), mid-wives (15) on the services aforementioned. CBI mode of response or operation will also include mobile hard-to-reach team approach with regular visit to communities within the selected LGAs to render PHC services to the affected population. CBI will also be providing capacity building on sexual reproductive health services to 50 Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) on practical observational skills around health service delivery in RH as well as responding to outbreaks within the serving communities. Immunization against childhood diseases such as diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, polio and measles is one of the most important means of preventing childhood morbidity and mortality. Achieving and maintaining high levels of immunization coverage must therefore be a priority for all health systems. Immunization and vaccination are two of the most important public health interventions and constitute a cost effective strategy to reduce both the morbidity and mortality associated with infectious diseases.
All CHVs would be selected by and from their local communities to serve as volunteers. They will be trained and supervised. CHVs in each settlement of the project implementation areas will regularly record child immunization, track pregnant women, and register births in their settlements to ensure that as many newborns as possible receive their birth immunization against polio (OPV0) as well as other routine immunization in a timely manner. They will establish personal relationships with the families they serve and build trust and become valued sources of information. During immunization campaigns, CHVs will mobilize the caregivers in their communities to immunize their children. The CHVs will go house to house to support families, resolve issues of non-compliance when they can.
CBI, through a strategic partnership with WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA as well as Health Sector and Reproductive Health Sub-sector shall train the CHEWs on minimal initial service package on Reproductive Health. For the hard-to-reach and underserved communities within the selected LGAs, CBI shall deploy five (5) mobile health outreach teams – Giedam:1, Jakusko:1, Bade: 2 and Nguru: 1 to provide appropriate integrated health services and support to the people of concern.

1 - Needs

The conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in northeast Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. Some 8.4 million people in the northeast states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) are in need of humanitarian aid in 2022. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential health services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. The conflict and insecurity make humanitarian operations difficult and dangerous. Most main supply routes and secondary roads in Borno State are highly risky for humanitarian staff or materials to traverse. Access to health services is hindered by the high cost of medical care/services – as reported by 75% of IDPs, 61% of returnees, and 80% of non-displaced households. Internally displaced persons' camps are overburdened and devoid of adequate infrastructures for basic survival. The number of people in need of health care keeps increasing as security issues soar. In northeast Nigeria, 5.0 million people including high percentage of women (13%) and children (70%) are in need of life-saving and essential health services, amongst which 2 million are women and girls of reproductive age. The BAY states have the highest maternal mortality rate in Nigeria, estimated at 1,549/100,000 live births. Approximately 18% of IDPs (396,000 people), 17% of people in host communities (663,000) and 23% of returnees (345,000) do not have sufficient access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene. Affected people remain at significant risk of epidemic-prone diseases, like polio, measles malaria, AWD, cholera and viral hemorrhagic fevers due to low access to essential healthcare, seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and vulnerabilities because of continuous displacement. Women and children are left increasingly susceptible to disease outbreaks. The current public health systems in the BAY states are not able to meet the needs of the affected populations. Health systems face a variety of challenges, including dilapidated infrastructure, lack of qualified staff, gaps in the supply of medicines and medical supplies, and under-funding.Ninety-three (93) new-suspected cases and seven (7) deaths were reported in week 33 of the year 2022. The cumulative number of suspected cholera cases is 320 with 16 cholera-associated deaths (CFR- 5.0%). The Honorable commissioner for health in Yobe state has acknowledged a cholera outbreak in the state after receiving confirmation from the laboratory and has communicated to all relevant partners and agencies to support the state in tackling the situation. Ongoing relocation of IDPs in Borno state IDP camp is increasing the influx from the state to Yobe state neighbouring LGAs which will exacerbates the risk of disease outbreaks including cholera, hepatitis, meningitis and measles.It is also clear from assessment reporting and information obtained from sector team, CBI is the only partners supporting health facilities in Bade LGA and looking at the caseload, this will be an upgrade to the services in the LGA facing another set of health facilities and communities. For Nguru LGA, there is no partner presence except the mobile hard to reach team supported by WHO which have lesser coverage when we look at the current lean and rainy season epidemic prone diseases.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

As a core value in planning and implementation of all programs and activities, CBI, advocate, employ “do no harm” approach, which implies taking cognizance of protection risks or potential violations of rights of members of target communities or simply put, DOING HARM PRINCIPLE to intended solace to community members or target beneficiaries. CBI staff have been trained on protection issues and those who will be newly recruited will also be trained by Protection actors and can identify and refer all cases of GBV, child abuse etc. Through the proposed projects in the areas, and so we will ensure that adequate Do No Harm analyses are being conducted and that participatory methods in all aspects of the project cycle are implemented. All services to be provided will be designed to ensure safe and easy access for all taking into account vulnerable person with specific needs (women, children, elderly, etc). Our monitoring tools will also include protection mainstreaming indicators as well as questions related to protection mainstreaming in all potential data to be collected so that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. CBI, also ensure that marginalized groups Women, Children and people living with disabilities) in the communities are carried along in process and outcomes of all interventions. Thus, in the course of executing this project, due care will be taken towards ensuring that fairness is being done to gender equity. This means due consideration is given to the inclusion of issues pertaining to the life and the life chances of men and women. On the side of disability, CBI has always allowed for representation of persons living with disability in the course of planning and implementation of its programs and activities. The design and delivery of activities should also factor in the special needs of people living with disability. Regarding conflict sensitivity, CBI is always conscious of the local norms and conflicts dynamics. We will therefore work with all the relevant stakeholders to identify any key concerns and factors that could lead to resistance/spoilage.In summary, CBI will employ the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) which does not only look the community as beneficiary but as partner: it recognizes the need to ensure people affected by crisis are not only informed and consulted about the core humanitarian but at the center of decision-making process.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

With the ongoing NHF funded project in Bade, Jakusko and Giedam LGAs, we have already set up Accountability and Quality Improvement Committee (AQIC) which comprises of Government health workers, community (IDP and host community) leaders and religious leaders who we directly and will continue to liaise and related with on the issues of accountability and feedback mechanism. This committee will be having monthly meetings and discuss several issues affecting the community. During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to health will be raised and aired and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners and sectors implementing in the location. In addition, CBI shall use the existing compliant mechanisms to receive feedback from the beneficiaries of the project and community members that might have any concern on the implementation of the project, all the received complaints, concerns and other forms of feedback will be address accordingly and immediately.The religious and community leaders will take part in project monitoring, ensuring that the project benefits the target population. Monthly meeting will be done with the religious and community leaders and the team of the health facilities to discuss the service utilization, attitude of health worker toward the patients and any other relevant aspect that needs to be addressed. The performance of the community volunteer work and the messages sent will be discussed as well. At all stages, any complaints from the patients will be reported to the in-charge of the health facilities or the head of the community leader who will have daily contact with the health team. Beneficiaries to the intervention will be provided with feedback using Community-based approach/mechanism, which is accessible at all, time.
