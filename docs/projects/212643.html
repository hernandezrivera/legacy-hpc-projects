Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the protection and resilience of Syrian IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host communities through advocacy, coordination, and provision of community-based protection services and legal services. UNHCR will continue to lead the Protection Sector to ensure coordinated protection interventions and programming in collaboration with the Government. UNHCR’s protection interventions include provision of integrated protection services through a network of 117 centers in all 14 Governorates. The Community Centres, paired with 120 mobile units and around 2551 outreach volunteers of multifunctional thematic capacities, are fundamental to the implementation of UNHCR’s Protection and Solutions Strategy for Syria while applying a participatory age, gender, and diversity approach. The protection services at UNHCR Community Centres include, but are not limited to, psycho-social support, GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response, child protection case management, legal awareness and legal aid, non-formal education programmes, services for persons with disabilities and older persons, provision of general and medical in-kind assistance, social and recreational activities.
Needs
The plight of Syrians has further deteriorated over the course of 2023. The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) highlights Gender-Based Violence (GBV) as a primary concern; women and girls are facing increased denial of resources and education, restrictions on movement, exploitation and family violence, and technology-facilitated violence, The protracted crisis combined with patriarchal structures and economic hardship have normalized GBV,, the situation has forced many families to resort to negative coping mechanisms. Changing roles within family structures have resulted in increased domestic violence. Children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and are in need of critical child protection services. Deepening poverty and lack of access to services also have a profoundly negative impact on children’s well-being. Housing, land, and property (HLP), and civil documentation continue to be key protection concerns, and this limits people’s access to basic services and humanitarian assistance. Key challenges include illegal or undocumented HLP transactions, disputes, access to land for livelihoods, land contamination with landmines, and lack of documentation.
Implementation
The project will be implemented through integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including 1)targeted support to persons with specific protection needs, persons with disabilities, and the elderly; 2) legal counselling and legal representation before courts and administrative bodies to the most vulnerable Syrians on civil and HLP documentation, documenting civil events and enhancing the protection of women and children; 3) legal awareness raising across the country 4) advocacy and support to government on civil documentation, HLP and other legal matters, 5) provision of response services in respect to GBV and child protection, which will include individual case management and referrals to other services; 6)psychosocial support; 7) community- based committees; 8) PSEA awareness raising and response 9) support community centres, community-led initiatives and enhance community engagement and resilience. UNHCR will strengthen its outreach to all people in need. Curriculum-based prevention awareness sessions will be provided to reduce harmful behaviors and promote gender equality. In 2024 UNHCR will continue mainstreaming its global GBV policy in all sectors, ensuring survivors are considered when planning for legal or livelihood activities, men and boys are engaged in GBV prevention and response, and women committees are supported. Surveys at community centers will be utilized as feedback tools to inform and enhance the quality of protection services provided.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection risk Risk1: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when delivering assistance & service. Mitigating measures: Raise awareness among UNHCR & partners' staff, volunteers, and communities about SEA and about all humanitarian aid and services being free. Assess, monitor & enhance the capacity of partners to prevent and respond to SEA allegations. Apply mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff members. Enhance the complaint mechanisms & ensure follow up while adhering to the standards of data protection and confidentiality. Risk2: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines all sector partners follow. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria are shared with all partners. Regular monitoring, consultation, client satisfaction surveys, PDM and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk3: Lack of involvement and technical capacity of national stakeholders in areas where the response to needs require their involvement e.g. access to civil documentation and HLP documents. Mitigating measures: Proactive coordination with relevant authorities. Strengthen national institutions in service delivery. Risk4: Lack of/low quality of services and referrals expose survivors to further harm, discrimination, & stigmatization. Mitigation measures: Ensure all services are provided in a confidential, dignified, and safe manner. PSEA UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Inter-Country Network (ICN) in Syria, as well as the secretariate of the national ICN. UNHCR is co-funding the PSEA Coordinators' position in 2024 and will work closely with different Protection Sub-working Groups and the PSEA network to ensure prevention and response mechanisms are in place regarding the “do no harm” principle. PSEA mandatory training and annual refresher sessions are compulsory to UNHCR own and partners’ staff. Since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners.Risk Risk1: Fluid context, shifting frontlines and unstable political situation in North Syria. Mitigating measures: Proactively coordinate with UNDSS and sister agencies in terms of security & access issues. Advocate at senior levels with all stakeholders to identify political solutions. Risk2: Limited ability to monitor the delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk-based monitoring tools and establish remote/third-party monitoring arrangements through supporting partners. Risk3: Delayed/lack of approvals. Mitigation: advocacy with relevant authorities for uninterrupted access. Risk4: Resistance and non-acceptance from the community for CP & GBV activities. Mitigation: Engagement of all community members in all phases of the project cycle
