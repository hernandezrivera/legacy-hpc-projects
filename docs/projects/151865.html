Project Summary

The proposed nine-month project aims to provide an integrated response to urgent needs faced by IDPs in Borno State through the provision of emergency CCCM, Shelter and NFI assistance in formal and informal camp settlements. In order to contribute to a comprehensive humanitarian response, ACTED proposes to build on its experience and current presence on the ground in priority areas, to provide CCCM assistance in Mafa and Dikwa IDP sites, through dedicated static CCCM Teams, in order to facilitate site improvements, strengthen partner coordination, and improve information sharing and management structures. To strengthen the rapid response capacity of the Shelter and NFI sector to address onsets of IDP influxes, ACTED proposes to establish a pipeline of emergency shelter and non-food item assistance kits, available to Shelter and NFI Sector Partners for distribution.
In light of the volume of IDPs living in formal camps and informal settlements in Borno State, and the continuous increase in new arrivals, the CCCM set-up in the State is insufficient to meet the large-scale needs, with limited funding and too few active sector partners – ACTED is one of only two active INGO partners in the CCCM sector. As a result, living standards in camps do not meet SPHERE standards, and there is a lack of coordination between implementing partners at the camp level, resulting in unaddressed critical assistance gaps. To address this, the proposed project aims to support and strengthen existing CCCM activities in Dikwa and Mafa, set up by IOM, by establishing static CCCM Teams, in order to strengthen reception systems, camp coordination, information sharing, and camp management mechanisms to facilitate a comprehensive humanitarian response. CCCM Teams will work with existing camp management structures to strengthen coordination of intervention systems, and will promote sustainability by supporting camp committees to gain relevant skills and tools required for community-led intervention activities. The static CCCM Teams and associated assistance is expected to benefit a total of 27,626 beneficiaries in Dikwa, and 25,243 in Mafa.
Secondly, the project aims to establish a pipeline of Bama type emergency shelters, ESKs and NFIs in strategically located warehouses in Monguno, in order to facilitate rapid response time for SNFI Sector partners operating within these LGAs and the surrounding areas. Stockpiled NFIs and emergency shelters will be made available to sector partners for distribution in order to facilitate significant reduction in sector response time, and enabling immediate emergency response to new arrivals. Thus, the proposed activity will act in conjunction with the wider CCCM and SNFI Sector. The goods procured under this project would enable direct emergency shelter and NFI assistance to 10,000 households, projected to benefit 50,000 beneficiaries.

Needs

Displacement trends have continued to increase in 2018, with northeastern Nigeria recording a weekly average of over 4,500 new arrivals according to IOM DTM ETT data. The ongoing insurgency continues to evolve, with the Nigerian Security Forces increasing operations to retake LGA territories, resulting in a constant flow of displacement which shows no sign of abating: a total of 2,396 movements were recorded between 26 September and 02 October 2018(IOM ETT 86). Thus, IDP sites continue to face large numbers of new arrivals, while simultaneously suffering from over-congestion and associated degenerative trends in camp conditions and quality of life. Land acquisition for the extension of campsites has been steadily pursued by the CCCM, Shelter and NFI Sector, requiring response from the wider partner community to facilitate emergency shelters to support the relocation of IDPs. This requires direct access of partners to prepositioned emergency shelters and NFIs to respond to the needs of over congested camps. In Dikwa, according to REACH Initiative’s LGA Profiling Assessment, most new arrivals indicated the reason for relocation to Dikwa was the ability to escape AOG controlled villages, or to be closer their location of origin. Due to the strategic location of Dikwa it has increasingly become a point of first arrival for IDPs fleeing rural villages where Nigerian Security Forces and AOGs are in conflict. As it remains a front-line location, the number of IDPs and new arrivals is expected to increase, placing greater stress on already over-extended local resources and over-congested sites. In Mafa, ACTED has been a primary CCCM Agency since 2017, providing critical reception management assistance to new arrivals and supporting the coordination of implementing partners. According to the LGA Profiling Assessment conducted by REACH Initiative in Mafa LGA, over 3,200 IDPs arrived in Mafa during the first 6 months of 2018, with most IDPs arriving from neighboring LGAs of Jere and Konduga due to the continued Nigerian Security Forces operations. Humanitarian partners operating in Mafa cited specific challenges in regards to the capacity to provide adequate shelter in response to the continuous influx of IDPs. Mafa and Dikwa are among the 40 over-congested IDP sites in Borno State, with both currently hosting over 1,000 IDPs beyond camp capacity, while also having recorded among the highest number of new arrivals in 2018. Both sites urgently require CCCM assistance for coordinated humanitarian response, to aid in the delivery of de-congestion strategies, and to ensure reception management systems capable of providing first-line response to new arrivals including shelter and NFI assistance. According to a recent rapid assessment conducted in Dikwa by ACTED, new arrivals are facing long wait times in the temporary shelter units provisioned by reception management before shelter assistance can be secured. Rapid response capacity through access to stockpiled emergency shelter and NFI kits would enable the strengthening of comprehensive reception systems management. The continued influx of new arrivals in Mafa and Dikwa has largely over-stretched available resources for interventions, resulting in prevalent and critical gaps in first-line response to new arrivals. Over crowding, insecurity, flooding, and exposure to the elements remain of serious concern for IDPs that are unable to access adequate emergency assistance. Serious protection concerns arise for women and girls who are often living in squalid conditions in sites characterized by overcrowding and limited access to safe, sanitary and dignified accommodation; further increasing risks of gender-based violence and harassment.

Activities

A1.1.1 Manage reception services to ensure comprehensive first-line response to new arrivals: ACTED will register new arrivals using IOM’s biometric registration system to verify identity, collect household demographic information, and to enable accurate camp demographics tracking. ACTED will perform vulnerability assessments on new arrivals and conduct referrals to appropriate humanitarian partners.A1.1.2 Site assessments and gap identification: ACTED’s CCCM Team will conduct site assessments in order to determine the most critical gaps. The assessment will be done in coordination between ACTED’s CCCM and ACTED’s AME field team, and will consult with IOM, implementing partners, local leaders, and IDPs. Results will be shared with the relevant sectors, and identified gaps will be categorized according to urgency. A1.2.1 Site Improvements: ACTED will conduct assessments in the sites of operation, and will hold consultations with local stakeholders, including NEMA/SEMA, IOM, local leaders, and the IDP community, in order to prioritize critical matters accordingly, and to develop an agenda for site improvement plans. Site improvements will aim to provide rapid impact site upgrades, and will focus on mitigating environmental risks, such as flooding during the rainy season. A1.2.2 Establish effective coordination and information sharing mechanisms:ACTED will set up or strengthen the following coordination mechanisms: The CCCM Sector formal camp monitoring tool will be utilized by ACTED, and will be reported to the Sector monthly; 2 camp coordination meetings and 2 camp committee meetings per month. ACTED will establish camp committees to promote community-led interventions.A1.2.3 Establish and manage Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (CRM) and strengthen communication with beneficiary communities: ACTED’s CCCM Team will establish a CRM in implementation sites where it does not already exist, in order to establish a safe, confidential, and accessible direct communication pathway between beneficiaries and implementing partners, consisting of complaints/suggestions boxes, a CRM hotline, and a CRM Desk. A2.1.1 Procure and preposition 6,000 Bama Type emergency shelters: ACTED will preposition a total of 6,000 Bama emergency shelters over 9 months in the Monguno Logistics Sector Warehouse. ACTED will release shelters to sector partners via request, and in close collaboration with NEMA/SEMA, IOM, Sector partners, and humanitarian partners operating on the ground. Partner led distributions will enable core life-saving shelter provisions to families that are newly arrived, or who are already living in sites with no access to markets and that have not already received shelter assistance from other partners. A2.2.2 Procurement and prepositioning of 8,000 non-food item kits: ACTED will preposition a total of 8,000 NFI kits over 9 months, which will be stockpiled in Monguno. ACTED will release kits to sector partners via request, and in close collaboration with NEMA/SEMA, IOM, Sector partners, and humanitarian partners operating on the ground. Partner led distributions will enable core life-saving NFI provisions to families that are newly arrived, or who are already living in sites with no access to markets and that have not already received NFI assistance from other partners.A2.2.3 Procurement and prepositioning of 2,000 emergency shelter kits: ACTED will preposition a total of 2,000 ESKs over 9 months in Monguno. ACTED will release kits to sector partners via request, and in close collaboration with NEMA/SEMA, IOM, Sector partners, and humanitarian partners operating on the ground. Partner led distributions will enable core life-saving ESK provisions to families that are newly arrived until more durable shelter solutions can be provided by partners.
