Additionally, peer mentoring and structured peer-led child-protection programes will be implemented. Each hub will also have a community outreach component which is linked with the services being provided at the centre. WC uses a cascade group approach to transmit DRR, CP, PSEA and GBV messaging at the household level. The hubs will also act as a centre to refer boys and girls to other specialized services such as primary health, nutrition, and case management for CP and GBV.

Needs

While there is significant focus on children, adolescents (13-18) have few organized activities or opportunities. Protection risks (SEA, illegal labor, early marriage, trafficking) abound. Teens have an opportunity to not only protect themselves, but with a community-based approach, increase protection of other vulnerable populations in the camp as well. Psychological distress continues to be a pressing issue, and global health sector estimates are that 17% of children are suffering severe mental health impacts. For girls’ issues are related to memories of violence before movement and exposure to sexual violence before the flight, and for boys, memories of violence before movement and missing family members. WC has been implementing adolescent empowerment (pre-vocational training, protection messaging) and structured PSS services ( sports and games, self-protection, safe spaces) in ten camps, and will strengthen these activities while narrowing the scope to four camps where WC has been the only CP implementing partner (4 ext, 20, 20 ext., 16). WC also has implemented a self-help group program in these camps supported by PFA-trained volunteers. These volunteers are identifying vulnerable children to prioritize in these activities (distressed, child-headed households, disabled), witha goal or reaching 300 children in each center.There is a need for improved social cohesion, and youth engagement is an excellent space to build peer bridges between host and refugee communities.

Activities or outputs

WC will continue to operate four adolescent development hubs across the four identified camps. The Hubs (already in existence and operated by WC) offer a comprehensive approach to service provision covering alternative education in English and Burmese language, gender specific skills building programmes (sewing and bicycle repair) and psychosocial support, as well ongoing sports, games and arts programmes. Additionally, peer mentoring and structured peer-led child-protection programes will be implemented. Each hub will also have a community outreach component which is linked with the services being provided at the centre. WC uses a cascade group approach to transmit DRR, CP, PSEA and GBV messaging at the household level. The hubs will also act as a centre to refer boys and girls to other specialized services such as primary health, nutrition, and case management for CP and GBV. Similar activities will be introduced in Ukhia Secondary School as after-school programs. WC has already worked on environmental protection activities with these children. Parallel activities will empower adolescents for self-protection and increased resilience in the host community. In addition,to improve social cohesion, adolescent groups int the host community will be invited to visit the refugee adolescent hubs for joint activities, and to foster peer-to-peer interaction. Improving from last year, WC will ensure active coordination in the Protection Sector and CP Sub-sector meetings, and report through the 5W.
