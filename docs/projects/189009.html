Project Summary


The GBV prevention and response program of International Medical Corps (IMC) in South Sudan aims to empower communities and build resilience through promoting social and behavioral change to address the root causes of GBV in the country. The project targets key population to create a sustainable change in attitude and practices through male engagement, working with community leaders and building capacity of local stakeholders. Leveraging on the vast experiences, gains, and successes of IMC in implementing GBV prevention and response interventions through the FCDO-HARISS program, this project will be continued to implement in the states of Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau IDP Camp and County), Upper Nile and Malakal PoC and County), Unity (Nyal County), and in Canal/Pigi County. It will directly benefit about 27495 vulnerable women and girls across the target locations. One of the project’s key priorities is to further strengthen core services to survivors of GBV and other vulnerable women and girls in the aforesaid intervention sites. These services include quality case management using a survivor-centered approach, along with psychosocial support to enhance healing and recovery. IMC UK works to ensure that survivors’ holistic needs are met by providing multi-sectoral services, referring to health centers for clinical management of rape, to legal justice services and to other kinds of support based on the needs of survivors. In addition to individual PSS for survivors, IMC UK will continue with PSS group activities for all vulnerable women and girls in the women girls’ safe spaces (WGSS). To ensure sustainability of the project and ownership by the community, especially of the WGSSs, in Wau, Nyal and Malakal, IMC already established, leadership committee comprise of 8 members from the community selected by their fellow women with the responsibility to give support, information, feedback, and suggestions that will foster empowerment and ownership of the space and the overall wellbeing of their members. The women committee members will be further empowered with knowledge and skills through hands-on management of the spaces and co-leading with them on the daily functioning of the WGSS. The women committee will be continuously mentored and trained on GBV basic concepts, safe referral, and referral pathways, and on leadership and conflict management and facilitation techniques and on various psychosocial support activities using IMC’s and IRC’s global WGSS toolkit. IMC UK will implement holistic community engagement strategies on the promotion of long-term positive change in the targeted communities. IMC will use the GBV community mobilization tool called SASA! - Phase-based strategy to help communities rethink about “power” and the positive use of power. “Preventing Violence against Women and Girls: Engaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP)” is also another tool that will be used to ensure men engagement with accountability to prevent GBV in the targeted community in Nyal, Wau and Malakal. In addition, IMC trains and collaborates with GBV Taskforce members in Wau, Malakal and Nyal. These will be supported by staff IMC UK staff to do awareness raising, and safe confidential referral of any survivors who disclose to them. IMC UK will provide emergency cash assistance for case management to the vulnerable women and girls. Cash assistance is one of the interagency minimum standards in GBV programming in emergency (Standard 11: Dignity Kits, Cash Assistance). In addition to this, IMC UK opts to implement cash assistance based on lesson learnt and feedback from women and girls on importance of cash assistance as lifesaving; for example, it can help a survivor meet the costs associated with fleeing an abusive relationship (e.g., rent, temporary shelter, transportation, food, clothing, etc).

1 - Needs

Gender-Based Violence is a persistent and serious problem in South Sudan. GBV affects men, women, boys and girls, but it disproportionally affects women and girls. According to data collected by the Gender-based Violence – Information Management System (GBV-IMS) in South Sudan during 2021 approximately 98% of reported GBV incidents affected women and girls. Similarly, Data from GBVIMS by IMC collected in 2021 shows that 98% of the GBV cases were reported by women and girls with denial of resources being the highest cases reported followed by physical assault. Gender inequality, exacerbated by decades of armed conflict, severe drought, massive flooding, have contributed to the increase in GBV risks. According to HNO 2022, an estimated 2.6 million people risk facing gender-based violence in 2022, which represents a 25 per cent increase compared to 2021 estimates. The risk of rape, physical assault, sexual abuse, forced and early marriage remain a GBV concerns to many women and girls. South Sudan’s HNO 2022 emphasized that structural gender inequality and unequal power relations between men and women are the root causes of GBV in the country. The GBV Sub-Cluster’s Strategy for 2019-2021 underscores pervasive rape and other forms of GBV that undergoes unreported due to high level of stigma and shame, lack of awareness among women about their rights and opportunities for redress and access to services for survivors. Report from the safety audit conducted jointly by IMC and GBV subcluster members in 2021 in Western Barh el Ghazal, Unity state and upper Nile Wau, Nyal and Malakal indicated greater concerns about GBV, specifically domestic violence, rape and forced marriage where Women and girls are exposed to this forms of GBV in their routine activities in their communities such as water points, latrines, distribution areas (food and non-food items), markets and/or firewood collection sites, among others.The situation is similar in Canal Pigi county where sexual assault against women and girls is reported to be prevalent. Poor economic conditions made girls especially vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse. Women and girls have limited access to opportunities such as education due to limiting social norms. An assessment done by REACH in Canal Pigi in 2021, showed that domestic violence is the main protection concern among assessed women (25%); while early marriage was the main protection concern for surveyed girls (26%). In addition, reports from IMC GBVIMS shows that Physical assault has accounted for most cases throughout the year, while psychological and emotional abuse accounted for the second highest number of GBV cases perpetrated and reported. That is, 31.34% physical assault, 30.21% emotional/psychological denial of resources, and 15% of cases were forced marriage. Currently IMC is the only partner implementing GBV services in Canal/Pigi. Overall, across all the target project sites, GBV cases continue to be prevalent while high barriers to reporting and services still exist due to the sensitivity of the topic as well as structural and socio-economic reasons. In addition, IMC is the only partner providing GBV services in the locations proposed ( Wau, Nyal, Malakal and Canal Pigi. Therefore, continuation of GBV prevention and response, including GBV case management services is essential to ensure quality of care and comprehensive support to GBV survivors. To improve the holistic quality of services and support offered by IMC in the recent years, structured awareness raising for women and girls, including men and boys will be further scaled-up through use of community structures who are comprised of IMC trained GBV taskforce and SASA community activists.

2 - Project activities

IMC will implement the following activities using different approaches including community engagement and sensitization, specialized facility-based case management and PSS services provision within WGSSs, provision of dignity kits and cash assistance and capacity building of local communities and other service providers. Below are specific activities to be implemented: Access of GBV survivors to timely and quality response and referral services, through Provision of survivor-centered case management and psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery and psychosocial support services within women and girl safes paces (WGSS) to promote healing, well-being, and empowerment. Internal case review sessions will be conducted on a weekly basis with all caseworkers for the purpose of supporting each other in self-care and in addressing special cases. To address the needs of survivors in a timely manner, individual case review sessions will also be conducted with each caseworker to facilitate proper follow-up and documentation. Provision of Cash assistance as part of case management and referralsMC UK will provide cash assistance for case management to the vulnerable to GBV survivors. Cash assistance is one of the interagency minimum standards in GBV programming in emergency (Standard 11: Dignity Kits, Cash and Voucher Assistance). In addition to this, IMC UK opts to implement cash assistance based on lessons learned and feedback from women and girls on importance of cash assistance as a lifesaving intervention; for example, it can help a survivor meet the costs associated with fleeing an abusive relationship (e.g., rent, temporary shelter, transportation, food, clothing, etc). Implement GBV prevention and risk mitigation activities, through holistic community engagement, such as Engaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP) and Start, Awareness, Support, and Action (SASA!) activities. Awareness raising activities will be conducted through IMC trained community structures who are comprised of GBV taskforce as well as SASA community activists Awareness will be done using pictorial BCC and IEC materials; ‘payam’ and school awareness campaigns; community dialogue sessions; radio show guesting; sports-for-development campaigns (i.e. integrating GBV messages during football tournaments), as well as theatrical presentations, such as street role plays/dramas and song and dances depicting situations of violence against women. IMC will also organize activities for the International Women’s Day, 16-day of Activism. Provision of Dignity Kits to the Most Marginalized and Vulnerable Women and Girls of Reproductive Age. IMC UK will ensure that most-at-risk women and girls to GBV will have access to dignity kits to ensure that their dignity is restored. The MEAL Officers and Women Support Officers or Prevention Officers will conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDMs), with women and girls after they receive the kits to get their feedback on its quality and usefulness. Enhance capacities of frontline workers, key community leaders and community-based organizations on GBV prevention, mitigation and response. As a way of ensuring sustainability of this project, IMC will continue to provide various targeted trainings to key GBV actors – the frontline responders such as the GBV case workers; community leaders, social workers from the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare trained women’s committee members, among others on GBV basic concepts, and on quality GBV prevention, mitigation and response.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

IMC UK will provide cash assistance for case management to the vulnerable to GBV survivors. The Cash Assistance will support the survivors of GBV procure items available in the market based on their needs. This will allow them better independence to choose the items that are locally available in the market based on their needs

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In all the locations where the project will be implemented, the markets have locally available options of the items that are regularly needed by the survivors. The items include undergarments, sanitary pads, food items and clothes.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IMC will further strengthen and/or put in place various CBFRM mechanisms that will ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are catered for. IMC ensures accountability to affected population is incorporated from the design phase by consulting affected communities, with equal representation from women, girls, PWDs, elderly, etc., on their specific needs and vulnerabilities, that should be adapted by the project. During project implementation, the project will use different channels, such as AAP monthly meetings, beneficiary feedback from members of WGSSs, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, etc., to inform affected communities on their rights and entitlements, and mechanisms available to them to share feedback and complaints. IMC will work closely with community structures to identify the heightened risks faced by PWD and addresses them. This includes older persons. When doing so, IMC will put in considerations the intersectionality of age gender, socio-economic factors and other diversities.In addition, also will ensure that WGSS are disability friendly in that they have ramp accessible to the PWD. In addition, IMC will ensure that the PWD are prioritized during the dignity kits distribution and that the kits are taken to the location close to them. IMC will continue to support activities meant to create awareness on the human rights of the PWD including elderly person on human right, GBV and services available.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crisis. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employee has to undergo a training on PSEA, and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs and CHWs. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other service points will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through trainings to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC GBV t teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is responsible to log in the complaints into IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to Ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provides guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2) major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalised people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will ultimately promote community participation on management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community Help desks, Post distribution Monitoring, AAP meetings and field registers as a safe and user-friendly channels to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community’ preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project. During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country local procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. Communities will be sensitized on existing AAP structures as a means of sharing complaints and feedback. During these sensitizations, communities will also be consulted on the most context-appropriate and safe avenue to share complaints and feedback. The information will be used to strengthen the CBFRM in the implementation sites. Further, the existing AAP committees will be trained on receiving, handling and responding to complaints and feedback on the project interventions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

• IMC regularly maintains contact with the government of South Sudan, UNDSS and MLO security staff, UNOCHA and partner NGOs to receive regular security updates. In addition, IMC works in coordination with local authorities and other stakeholders to coordinate activities with an emphasis placed on the safety and security of the staff on the ground. IMC also relies on a strong relationship with the beneficiaries to share information regarding upcoming fighting and potential threats. • IMC has also Security Risks Analysis (SRAs), Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) and Contingency Plans in all its project locations, which are being updated bi-annually, or as need arise.• IMC ensures that multi-sectoral referral pathways are in place in all its project locations, especially for survivors of protection incidents. Monthly safety audits are being conducted, which results are shared to and discussed with relevant national and local authorities and other NGOs to draw timely, actionable solutions, and provide targeted support to the survivors.• IMC will continue to implement all planned activities in full equity, without any discrimination based on race, color, origin, religion, age, gender nor disability. Particular attention will be given to ensure that children, PLW, vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors have access to quality health and protection services. Full access and preventive activities will also integrate persons with disabilities, elderly and youth. Special adjustments are being done by IMC to accommodate the special needs of PWDs and elderly, such as doing house-to-house visits to them; ensuring that the WGGSs are accessible to women and girls with movement constraints and physical disabilities, supporting rehabilitation where necessary such as ramps and grab bars, among others.• IMC will further strengthen existing and/or establish Quality Accountability of the affected population (QAAP) committees and other CBFRM modalities, which were selected by the community, to ensure that everyone has equitable and easy access to context-specific complaint and feedback mechanisms.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

IMC incorporates protection principles in all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare workers will be trained with a focus on GBV core concept, PFA and safe referrals considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. Proper consent-based referral for survivors of GBV to medical facility, MHPSS and nutrition facilities will be done. frontline staff will be trained on PFA , GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Furthermore, IMC will continue to provide PSS interventions, including psychoeducation and handicraft activities within WGSS. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and with the aim of empowering women and improving their psychosocial wellbeing.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition, FSL, WASH and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure to have early warning system in place to reduce the flood related impact. With the support of the stakeholders/partners IMC will carry out a more in-depth needs assessment on flood risks in the selected operational areas, establish and strengthen early warning system and train community members to apply Community Based Disaster Risk Reduction (CBDRR) and Climate Change adaptation (CCA) initiatives in affected communities. Further, through these initiatives in the target communities, each Boma will have an operational Community Disaster Response Team. The trained local facilitators will accompany the communities develop the risk maps, setting up of the early warning system, contingency plan, and awareness campaign. IMC will ensure improved awareness to contribute safe waste disposal and mitigate blockage of drainage system
