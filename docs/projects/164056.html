Project Summary

DRC will deliver Early Recovery assistance to persons of concern (PoCs) targeting conflict-affected youth and adults, men and women, including people with disabilities, to develop skills and increase income-generating opportunities with an aim to strengthen their access to livelihoods. PoCs with protection related concerns and vulnerabilities will be particularly targeted, and internal and external referrals will be made to ensure that protection services are provided in parallel based on identified needs. Initial needs assessments will be conducted jointly with MoE, MoSAL, and SARC field staff, when applicable. These assessments will allow DRC to select specific locations for its interventions where multi-sectoral assistance will respond to specific priority needs identified on the ground. In addition at the beginning of 2020, a labor market assessment will be conducted to be informed on the type of training/internship services to provide, as well as to the type of businesses to priorities within the business-start up support.
The eligibility of applicants will be based on a combination of their vulnerability and suitability for the Economic Recovery activity of their interest.
Delivery of skills development trainings (vocational trainings (VTs), internship placements, employability skills) will be implemented by DRC in collaboration with the MoE, the MoSAL, and SARC. Awareness-raising on the rights and responsibilities of employers and employees will be provided in collaboration with the DRC Protection department.
Market analysis will be conducted to understand the market needs, gaps, and opportunities. Business Skills Development Trainings will be delivered targeting PoC who lost their productive assets due to the conflict. Upon successful completion of the training, each business plan will be assessed by a panel, and those selected will be provided with MicroGrants (MGs) (Assets restoration). Micro Business owners group formulation will be assisted to facilitate its linkage with existing MSMEs, investors, and Chamber of Commerce for business development opportunities.
MGs, VTs, and internship activities will be provided to PWDs and women in rural and urban areas. Community-based peer groups of Micro Business owners will be formulated to facilitate experience sharing and to provide Life skills and PSS sessions. (In coordination with Protection).
To promote equal and open participation of the interested population, DRC will advertise the Economic Recovery activities in each area, with clear beneficiary selection criteria, details of the activities and deadline for the submission of applications. The outreach will be conducted through displaying banners at the prominent places in the targeted area, radio messages (if allowed), and flyers distribution. Additionally, local authorities will be contacted to inform them about the program and seek their support for information dissemination.

4 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (2019) it is estimated that half of Syrians are unemployed and that more than 83% live below the poverty line. The severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills, destruction and lack of productive assets, and destruction of economic infrastructure has been exacerbating the situation, resulting in extremely restricted households’ ability to purchase most items and meet their basic needs. This situation has led to individuals and households adopting harmful coping strategies. Adult women and youth, both male and female are groups who experienced a change in their traditional roles and therefore have carried an extra burden comparing to since the pre-crisis time according to research (Syria Resilience Consortium (SRC), 2019). Women often have been forced to take on the responsibilities of the head of the household due to the absence of males, their inability to work, and/or insufficient income, however, many of these women have limited experiences or skills in income-generating activities, and those who attempt to find work outside the home are often hindered both by cultural norms and a hostile working environment for women. Similarly, youth in conflict-affected areas have suffered from a lack of skills development and training opportunities, compounded by high rates of unemployment. Furthermore, at least 15% of the population in Syria lives with disabilities with restricted access to income-generating activities, however, they are expected from the society to be the main providers of their households. Without humanitarian interventions or an overall improvement in the socio-economic situation, people may continue to resort to increasingly exploitative and hazardous activities as their financial and material resources are exhausted. Consultations with communities have indicated that access to livelihood opportunities and basic services are among their primary concerns as affected people seek to rebuild their lives.

(In coordination with Protection) MEAL plan will be put for each project and Economic Recovery field-based staff, together with MEAL staff will be conducting the monitoring and evaluation according to the set plan.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Resources and response capacities being limited and far below known needs, there will always be a perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation measures: Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis and service mapping inform the response formulation, and that beneficiary selection results based on a set criterion is documented and made transparent to the community members. Inform community members on a feedback mechanism and ensure that the received feedback is addressed in a timely manner. Prepare a response strategy for in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension, such as add-on of other activities or change in activities, in consultation with concerned communities. Risk: Exposure of beneficiaries to gender-based violence through their placement in vocational trainings, internship, or support to access the market through income-generating activitiesLikelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation measures: Conduct a detailed assessment of the vocational training providers, intern hosts, and conduct frequent monitoring of activities. Ensure that the service providers are aware of the code of conduct and zero-tolerance policy towards gender-based violence. Raise awareness of beneficiaries on their rights and inform them on feedback mechanism, and contacts of focal persons of relevant activities. Ensure that the received feedback and/or concern is addressed immediately. Consult with beneficiaries, their family members, and community members on appropriate timing and location of activities and design the activity in line with consultation results.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Liquidity and inflation risk to be added by grantsRisk: Loose access due to security destabilizationMitigation measures: Assess the security situation of areas of implementation prior to activity commencement and identify potential security destabilization risks and scenarios. Prepare contingency plan, including remote management when applicable, as well as plan B which includes the relocation of activity implementation to enable DRC continuous delivery of services. Ensure all staff is aware of the plans. Monitor the security situation closely on a daily bases. Risk: Increasing inflation and high depreciation of Syrian Pound with consequent increase of prices in the market. This can affect the Micro Grants activities and having an impact on the quality of start-up kits that can be purchased with a limited amount of money.Mitigation measure: Monitor prices in the market and have a supplier database updated.
