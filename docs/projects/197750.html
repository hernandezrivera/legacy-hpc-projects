Project Summary

Binaa seeks to sustain the existing Protection help desks in 3 camps in Harnboush, Sheikh Bahr, and Bhorta community in Idlib and Aleppo, in addition to establishing one Protection Help desk in a new camp that will be established in the Bazagha, Aleppo. the aim is to support vulnerable children, women & girls at-risk groups, and affected population to easily access available services within the targeted areas.
the project is designed to integrated protection services (GP,GBV,CP) in 4 targeted sites for 2 years through providing quality protection services, with a focus on community-based approaches.
through help desks and outreach mechanisms for 1850 BNFs( 964 W,906 M) .
The project is designed to improve access to 40 quality and life-saving GBV response
services for GBV survivors and W&G at risk and put measures in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV through providing referral services to GBV related, medical, and specialized PSS services according to the referral pathway mechanism provided by GBV SC, plus BINAA's Protection officers will develop the GBV service mapping referral pathways, and coordinate with partners who provide these services on ground. Training of 24 GBV workers, in line with GBV SC (GBV SOPs, PSS, Women empowerment, and CM) and participation in the training organized by the GBV SC when available to ensure alignment with SC GBV standards.
And enhance strategies to empower women and girls, with a particular focus on most at
risk groups (adolescent girls, divorced and widowed W&Gs, W&Gs with disabilities and
older women) to prevent GBV and to change negative attitudes and believes that foster
harmful social norms, and COVID-19 awareness-raising interventions (including on
associated GBV risks and GBV key messages)for 2909 BNFs(826 M,1199W, 224 B,660
G) at 4 sites.
As well as promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response
through training 45 non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV risk mitigation according to
(IASC GBV guidelines, GBV SOPs).Development GBV safety audits in the camps and
establishment focal points (IASC GBV guidelines).
the project is designed to increase and more equitable access 4233 BNFs in 2022,2347 adults(1407W,940 M), 1886 children( 855 Boys,1031 girls) to quality child protection
interventions in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action,
through Provide community-based child protection by engaging 1700 children in non-specialized MHPSS services through structured child protection programs, and 223 (133 W,90 M) in parenting programs, awareness-raising, and community events to prevent and respond to child
protection issues for 2400, providing specialized child protection services, case
management for 86 (G, B) and Referral of child protection cases to specialized services
(child protection needs) according to the referral pathway mechanism provides by
CP SC, plus BINAA's Protection officers will develop the CP service mapping referral path
ways, and coordinate with partners who provide these services on the ground. Integrating of
PwDs with the community by a specialist for PwDs and integrate them into activities where
the centers are on flat ground, surrounded by a fence, the entrance is paved, and all the
camps are paved, and with special latrines' with hand railing and ramp. Training of 24 CP
workers in line with child protection minimum standards.
The implementing activities will be according to the Operational guidance note for GP, CP, and
GBV Activities in COVID 19.
Through continuing services in 3 protection help desks and outreach mechanisms (mobile team) including awareness-raising sessions.
the project will target the CwDs by increasing the access of CwDs to services, inclusion them with the activities, and providing transportation to facilitate access to services, in addition to adapting protection help desks to facilitate access for CwDs

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023 It is estimated that 15.1 million people will need protection assistance in 2023. 70 percent of the population live in communities where it is reported that lack/loss of civil documentation limits access to basic services and humanitarian assistance. Child protection risks are equally likely to worsen in 2023, with rights violations continuously on the rise.Currently, 1,234 of 1,420 IDP sites are unplanned, self-settled locations with high population density. In north-east Syria, where 278,200 IDPs already reside in over 260 sites.Approximately 8.5 million people are in need of GBV assistance , of which 55.7 percent are women and 37.5 percent are girls. Women and girls in Syria are increasingly at risk of GBV. This is exacerbated by the economic and food crises, and reduced opportunities for women and girls to live in a place they consider safe. 84 percent of children live in locations where child marriage is an issue for girls aged between 15 and 17 years. More than six million children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse and exploitation, and need critical child protection services. BINAA FGD’s key findings in the assessed camps:63%KIs highlighted that there are cases of unaccompanied orseparated children and the high rate of children engaging in work that prevents them fromenrollment in schools,and caregivers suffering from severe PS problems that may lead tothe child’s exposure neglect,violence.
