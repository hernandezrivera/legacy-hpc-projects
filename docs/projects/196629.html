Project Summary

The project aims to target the most vulnerable IDPs in the camps and informal
settlements with life-saving WASH services within Afrin and Azaz district. The project will focus on
the following activities:
- Emergency water trucking: By supporting the IDPs with potable water with FRC between 0.2-0.5 mg/l with ratio between 25-35 l/p/d
- Solid waste management: Through collecting the solid waste from the targeted IDPs sites on daily basis and send it away to the proper assigned dumpsites/landfills
- Emergency sanitation (desludging): Proper desludging and disposing for the waste water from the pits and septic tanks in the targeted locations.
- HKs distribution: Providing HKs for the IDPs through voucher modality, which will be complementarity items.
- Hygiene promotion for all the targeted sites. This activity will provide general hygiene messages for the IDPs spreading the key hygiene messages such as public health, personal hygiene, etc.., in addition to the messages about the precautionary measures for COVID 19.
The project is targeting all age and gender groups, taking into consideration the specific needs of each group and focusing on people with disabilities.
The selected locations were targeted according to the identified needs, where the IDPs sites needs to be supported with emergency WASH services since the IDPs living in last resort sites. This project is continuation to the support of the current IDPs where there is high need to continue water supplying to control the WBD, cholera outbreak and COVID 19 with high need to sanitation management and improvement where the sanitation status is really bad in most of the location (lack of proper latrines). Also the solid waste management will affect the environmental health if it stopes.
In addition to that, according to Bahar presence and regular assessment, there is high need for hygiene items since the IDPs didn't receive hygiene items for more than six months.

3 - Needs

According to HNO, Critically bad sanitation conditions, poor hygiene practices and deterioration of water quality have a direct impact on increasing public health risks, including the new rise of AWD/Cholera, also malnutrition and COVID-19, and further impact on the livelihood and protection of HHs.At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional with implication for both public and environmental health. The Governorates with lower sewer connection rate (Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo) are currently the most affected by the cholera/AWD outbreak, reporting 96 % of all suspected cases by 22nd October 2022. Observations on handwashing practices indicate that 16% HHs (32% in IDP sites) did not have soap and/or water at handwashing facility or did not have handwashing stations at all (vis-a-vis 13% and 23% for IDP in2021) and 59 % of households was not able to access some of needed hygiene items. A general deterioration in water quality has been registered with 21 % of HHs not drinking chlorinated water. Only 61% of water from water trucking was chlorinated compared to 81% last year and only 86% of water from water networks, compared to 90% last yearBahar is supporting around 132 sites within Afrin district, which are in high need to be covered with emergency WASH services. This project will benefit 13,318 HHs targeting 69,337 IDPs (all age groups) taking into consideration people with disabilities.

4 - Implementation of activities

This project will benefit around 69,337 IDPs through improving living conditions and providing life-saving assistance and sustainable solutions for IDP sites where possible. The activities will be implemented as follows:- Water trucking: Bahar provides water trucking 30 l/p/d with FRC 0.2-0.5 mg/l. Water trucking is a short-term solution, in order to meet the needs, and is normally applicable to IDP sites. The water trucking is used as a lifesaving intervention covering all IDP sites. The amount of water may be increased to 35 l/p/d to respond to cholera outbreak with minimum FRC 0.5 mg/l.- Solid Waste Management: Bahar implements SWM activities on a regular basis in IDPs sites. It is very important to maintain hygiene, thus preventing diseases and disasters that can increase when neglecting waste removal activities. The implementation will be through renting solid waste trucks with workers to collect the garbage and send it to the landfill. - Desludging: removing the scum and the sludge layers from the septic tanks. It is important to point out that desludging is a short-term solution, in order to meet the needs. - Hygiene Kits: Procurement and distribution of 26,636 consumables HKs in 2 rounds. -Hygiene promotion activities will provide general hygiene messages related to general hygiene with the focus on COVID-19 and cholera outbreak.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Emergency water supply: Risk: Movements of water trucks in congested camp-like settings. Mitigation: Consultation of the community (women and girls separately) on locations of distribution points, delivery routes, and schedules. Usage of wider access routes and avoidance of school areas. Safety training for truck drivers/operators. -Emergency sanitation: Risk: Movements of desludging trucks in congested camp-like settings; Reliability of services. Mitigation: Consultation of the community (women and girls separately) on desludging services modality/ frequency, truck routes, and schedules. Usage of wider access routes and avoidance of school areas. Safety training for truck drivers/operators.-SWM: Risk: Movements of garbage trucks in congested camp-like settings; Unreliability of services. Mitigation: Consultation of the community (women and girls separately) on SWM services modality/ frequency, truck routes, and schedules. Provide HHs with solid waste containers/plastic bags to ease disposal of waste from HH to the public SW collection points. - HKs distribution: Risk: Sale or exchange of NFI kits at lesser value. Mitigation: Consultation of community throughout program cycle to determine the reasoning behind it asking for solution.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying the related procedures. Bahar ensures that the field team attends PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal points responsibility is to ensure training is delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complains pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor and report if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: The security situation is stable in the project area. Risk: Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of the project area. Mitigation: ● In case of emergency situations, the activity plan will be re-adjusted based on an assessment of the situation. ● Relevant authorities/groups are aware of the project and technical authorities are committed to ensuring basic safety/access for activity implementation. Assumption: The fuel prices remain stable on the local market. Risk: Insufficient quantity and increase of fuel prices. Mitigation: Regular assessment of the increase of fuel price in the market in the target areas and will accommodate the increase if it's more than 15% than agreed with the contractors. Assumption: Staff is abiding by the PSEA policy and reporting any violations/ monitored violations. Risk: Sexual exploitation and abuse. Mitigation: ● PSEA principles developed and shared with all staff ● Staff and personnel having direct contact with affected population are aware of PSEA requirement in all interventions, consideration will be made to prevent PSEA. Assumption: Policies on fraud and supply chain are in place and followed. Risk: Fraud, aid diversion, and mishandling of supplies. Mitigation: ●Beneficiaries complaint mechanism in place (hotline, complaint boxes, staff members, field office, etc.) ● MEAL team acts as focal point ● Beneficiaries are provided with communication methods through brochures, community sessions, etc.
