Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring access to the hard to reach, inaccessible and vulnerable community in selected three counties of former Jonglei state.
To achieve this Nile Hope anticipates to increase access to emergency essential primary health care services through an integrated service delivery package using both the developmental and emergency service especially in PEHSP supported counties Akobo and Uror counties, while working closely with the developmental partners in Pigi/Canal which is IMC for Health and Nile Hope for integrated Nutrition services county.
with a focus on the major causes of Morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable community (Mothers and under-five children). So as to contribute to an improved resilience of crisis affected populations, Nile Hope believes that an enhanced coordination between humanitarian and development approaches is essential. As a result, the development support has increasingly been forced to move away from the ambitions of building sustainable health structures and come to focus more on service delivery and resilience oriented activities. In that way, the gap between humanitarian and development interventions has shrunk. Hence, Nile Hope will work on enhancing synergy the emergency humanitarian response to focus on lifesaving activities while striving at building more durable solutions from the start. With the urgent life-saving needs that exist in South Sudan at present this is a considerable challenge which requires a joint effort of intertwining and create synergy both the development and humanitarian responses together.
Develop a County level Emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic diseases

Provision of MHPSS services, Treatment /Management of SAM cases with medical complications in all the three supported PHCCs, Equip fifteen facilities with WASH in health facilities, Provision of CMR services in all the three PHCCs and One supported county Hospital, Integrate patients and care takers to received food from IPs and the WFP to selectively target beneficiaries and in areas where FSL intervention exists Nile Hope will integrate beneficiaries with FSL support. four facilities, Provision of BeMONC / CeMONC services. 1 - Needs

Repeated inter-clan, inter-tribal conflict had resulted in difficulty to ensure access to health care provision in both East and West side of Akobo County.In early two quarters of 2019 it had resulted in suspension of most humanitarian activities as a result of difficult to access to payams within the county which is mainly attributed to internal inter-clan conflict and tension. As a result of recent flooding in Akobo West and Central Akobo an estimated 783 House Holds are displaced, estimated 5,215 mothers and 923 children are directly affected by the flooding. Most of the displaced persons are living in an island placing increasing pressure on household food security. For the last five years under the world bank funding Health infrastructure and medical equipment was not a priority of concern which resulted that health infrastructure to be in bad share and with almost very limited supplies. Walgak-PHCC was vandalized where by the cold chain Corrugated Iron sheet of most of the PHCC were removed and looted leading temporary relocation and suspension of services in the area. The recent closure of the four health facilities has affected access to health services in the locality posing an increased burden in cases in the existing poorly equipped health facilities. Lack of access to safe drinking water due to contaminated water sources also poses an immediate threat to people’s lives. In Akobo if the immediate needs are not urgently addressed, there is likely to be high risk of epidemic outbreaks of water-borne diseases. Child health cold chain facility remain to be a challenge; Cold chain infrastructure is in Akobo West will be rejuvenated.Similarly, recent IPC grading June 2019 to January 2020 showed that together with Cueibet and Panyikang Pigi/Canal is one of the three counties where 21,000 people are in IPC Phase 4. In addition, a SMART survey conducted by IMC in early April showed the GAM Rate of 24.3 % (19.5-29.8) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) of 6.7 percent (4.6-9.7), indicative of Emergency (IPC Phase 4) with some households in catastrophe. The health systems in Pigi remains to be dire where EPI service to the sobat corridor has never been covered after the total closure of PHC facilities in 2014.IMC is running five Primary Health care facilities in Kurwai, Peri, Kolanyang and Khorflus areas such as Atar and Alam doesn't have health care services. Community in Pigi use swamps and river water. The high burden in Kala azar, and season spikes of malaria as a result of the swamps eaves the county with a high disease burden in the county.In Uror there are only PHCUs and ill equipped and staffed PHCCs with no referral capacity to provide major and emergency surgery, Nile Hope had taken the initiative to support the PHCC with BEMoNC supplies and minor surge capacity that enabled it to provide feeble service to patients that can provide BEmONC, while CEmONC and complicated medical and surgical case remain to be a challenge. The only referral area is Lanken Hospital, this is nine hours walking distance or 3-4 hours driving, which is much worst during rainy season. Like all the other World Bank supported counties the it has a poor health infrastructure and is poorly equipped since the project never allow to do major rehabilitation and purchase of medical equipment.Economic incapability limited access to food and basic needs, loss of crop as a result of flooding had left most of the mothers and adolescent girls vulnerable

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

As citizens all the rights of beneficiaries will be recognized and protected, Nile Hope works with the CHD, BHT and the local administration to ensure that IDPs and returnees deserve dignity and full right to access and be beneficiaries of the project

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes this project mainly aims at reaching to the IDPs and the returnees who were displaced as a result of chronic conflict hence almost 35-40% of the beneficiaries are IDPs and returnees and the project will include the perspective of these beneficiaries

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project focuses at providing safe site to stay, get integrated to the host community and create a common integral service for both the IDP and the host community for those who are now willing and or who are not able to go back to their home we support them to be able to settle in the areas they had temporarily settled. In doing so the local authority, community are part of the integration to create a peaceful coexistence of the community

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project fits well with the national Objective of reaching to the 35% of IDPs and returnees to be able to have access to health care services.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The primary duty and responsibility of Nile Hope as implementing partner is to also provide protection and humanitarian assistance to IDPs and Returnees. Nile Hope shall engage other IPs, the local authority and the community/Host/ and the IDPs and returnees themselves to ensure a joint effort to ensure a safe protection to IDPs and returnees in the supported three counties

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, It will hand over the project to the local County Health department and the payam Health team to take over and continue providing the services that were under this project. Nile Hope also anticipates to continue with expansion through either other sources and the community level BHI will also be one of the integral element to ensure enhance referal linkage and minimize associated disease burden attributed to access.
