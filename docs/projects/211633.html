Project Summary

The intervention will involve implementing both One-Off and Multi-Round (8 Rounds) MPCA for the crises affected population in NW Syria. One-Off MPCA will be utilized for any Emergency that may occur during the year while the Multi-Round MPCA will cover the needs of the most vulnerable HHs selected based on the harmonized beneficiary selection criteria developed by the Cash Working Group targeting up to 2,000 HHs with One-Off MPCA valued at150$ as per the CWG recommendations.
MPCA Value: Concern will comply with the CWG recommendations for Multi-Round MPCA (100 USD x8) targeting a total of 1,000 HHs, and the transfer currency will be primarily USD to preserve the value of the assistance and protect the purchasing power of recipient HHs, as long as 100 USD bills remain available. An alternative would be TRY if local/current context justifies; and provided decision is consulted with the CWG, Donor, and other NGOs in the same area.
Baseline Survey: As soon as the beneficiary verification is complete, when undertaking beneficiary registration, the baseline survey will be carried out to measure Coping Strategies Index (CSI) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) using sample size calculator software with confidence level at 95% and 5% confidence interval.
Distribution point selection: All distribution sites will be agreed upon with the local authorities for security and coordination purposes. The selected distribution point should be meeting the minimum requirement of safety and security including:
• Safe for both staff and Beneficiaries, with a minimum of one entrance and one exit
• Located in a central location from all beneficiaries’ residence
• Accessible for all beneficiaries including PWDs, and should have enough space for two separate lines
• Shaded from weather conditions, equipped with a water resource for drink, and separate latrines for males and females
Beneficiary Selection Criteria: Concern and Implementing Partners will rely on the harmonized beneficiary selection criteria which is being developed by the CWG, if the fund was secured prior to receiving the beneficiary selection criteria, the below criteria serve as a guideline to identify and prioritize households in need of assistance during the winter period:
1. Disability.
2. Chronic Diseases.
3. Pregnant/Lactating Women.
4. Children Attending School.
5. Household hosting orphaned, unaccompanied children or homeless relatives due to the conflict.
6. Living in a temporary settlement.
7. Household with no source of income.
8. Household headed by a person with a disability.
Beneficiary Invitation: each of the selected beneficiaries will receive a coupon from the local council agent specifying the date, time and location of the distribution.
Voucher Distribution: The beneficiaries will collect their vouchers from the distribution table in the distribution point after verifying the beneficiary ID against the approved beneficiary list. The program staff responsible for voucher distribution will stamp the voucher “verified” once the verification is completed and collect beneficiary’s signature on the Voucher Distribution Form, then guide the beneficiary to the redemption table.
Voucher Redemption: when arrived to the redemption table, the programme staff responsible for verification will compare the beneficiary ID with the list and stamped voucher, and ask the beneficiary to sign the Cash Redemption Form. Once the signature is completed, the designated staff will take a photo of the voucher and beneficiary ID to be archived as a Mean of Verification. Then stamp the voucher “Paid” before handing it over to the Financial Services Provider who will exchange the voucher for the determined value ($100 per voucher) and keep the voucher for Financial Reconciliation.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Distribution/Redemption Monitoring: The MEAL Field Team will be present in the distribution point to fill the cash distribution/redemption monitoring form. They should focus on the process of the distribution. They provide an opportunity to gather feedback on how distribution proceeded; whether the information provided before a distribution matched what beneficiaries experienced during the process; whether beneficiaries received what was expected; and whether there were any protection concerns.All cash transfers will be conducted through the Turkish Post Office (PTT). A separate FSP will be selected by Concern and Implementing Partners to distribute the cash. The process for selection will follow a procurement process based on the value of the funds being transferred and the transfer fees related to this process. The procurement process will follow standard Concern Worldwide tools and other criteria will also be considered in the selection. The FSP will be subject to legal vetting to ensure they are not a prohibited party.Post Distribution Monitoring: PDMs are crucial to ensuring that beneficiaries receive the full value of cash transfer and are treated fairly. A ten percent sample of beneficiaries will be selected after each distribution round to gather information on the MPCA intervention, and the PDM visit will take place within two weeks of the last distribution day each round and using the CWG Harmonized PDM Tool. Key topics for the questionnaire include whether there were any problems in the receipt of cash, including in the transfer value, at the distribution site, or in spending the money. Staff will also ask about security or safety issues happening as a result of the program at the community or household level; and any general feedback on program implementation.Endline Survey: About 1 week after the final distribution, but no more than 2 weeks after, the endline survey inclusive of measurement of all project indicators will be conducted with a sample of the project beneficiaries. These measurements are compared against the baseline data, and reported through the Final Report.During all project phases, Concern and Implementing Partners will consider the AAP measures, mainstreaming of protection, and PSEA. The project has a risk analysis and mitigation matrix. Along with all activities, monitoring, and evaluation processes will be carried out. At the end of the project, a meeting will be made for the project staff and key stakeholders to evaluate the project results and register the lessons learned and the best practices that have been followed to achieve project results.
