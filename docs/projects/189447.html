Project Summary

South Sudan remains to be among the countries with a high prevalence of gender-based violence where half of the girls marry below the age of 18. The rigidly patriarchal system and historic marginalization of women are the root causes of the GBV in the country. This is further exacerbated by recurrent natural disasters mainly flooding and the ongoing conflicts in the different places. Therefore, is imperative that humanitarian interventions are sensitive to this reality, given that they may both cause and exacerbate the incidence of GBV cases. Action Against Hunger proposes to: a) continue its efforts to mainstream GBV and gender considerations in its Nutrition, Health, WASH, and FSL integrated programs and b) Support the generation of evidence and learning for a better understanding of GBV prevention and r risk mitigation humanitarian community in South Sudan providing an evidence base for GBV prevention and risk mitigation.
The purpose of this project is to prevent gender-based violence and mitigate the protection risks for women for affected women, men, boys, and girls in affected communities by monitoring and addressing the gender dynamics on the ground. It will also put in place measures that will enhance safe and dignified access to available services for survivors and vulnerable women and girls in those communities. The project will be implemented from Jan. 1, 2022, to Dec. 31, 2022, and shall benefit the target groups in IDPs, host communities and returnees in Pibor, Fangak, Tonj East, Tonj North and Gogrial East Counties of South Sudan.
In 2022, Action against hunger will conduct gender analysis, its program implementation areas by using the Rapid Gender Analysis tools to generate evidence to support a gender-sensitive and gender transformative program implementation. It will also strengthen its partnership with gender-focused and like-minded organizations for sharing lessons and best practices.
This project will mainly focus on awareness-raising and information dissemination on GBV prevention and risk mitigation aspects and available services, through sensitization of men and boys and engagement of men caregivers on a dialogue on protection in Men Group Discussions, the project to tackle the root causes of gender inequality and stereotypes. The MtMSG group and the CNVs will also be the gears in dissemination of the information to men, women, boys and girls in the communities as well as on the Nutrition Sites using tailored IEC materials. ACF will conduct mapping of available services in those locations, train the staff on PFA, and safe and confidential referral of survivors. ACF will pilot innovative and environment-friendly IGA options engaging vulnerable women and girls, in particular.

1 - Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) threatens the overall well-being of South Sudan’s population, particularly women and girls. Studies indicate that some 65 percent of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime. According to UNICEF (2021), 50% of women have suffered from intimate partner violence (IPV). Child marriage, sexual abuse, rape/harassment, and/or harassment of women and girls, including by armed force, were key protection issues raised across the country. The elongated flooding and frequent inter-communal conflicts have increased the women, men, boys, and girls’ risk of exposure to different types of violence, including rape, murder, and torture. GBV can be detrimental to the nutritional and health status of women as well as their children if present, given that she is denied decision-making and purchasing power: this causes deterioration of physical health and mental wellbeing. According to a study by UNICEF, there is a clear and positive relation between IPV child malnutrition. Women who experienced any form of intimate partner violence are more likely to give birth to stunted children. Having little control over one’s sexuality and pressure on women to bear additional children increases the stress on household food and nutrition security and limits the ability to breastfeed. The gender analysis conducted by Action against hunger in Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazel in 2018, revealed that those who experience SGBV are often ostracized, excluded from valuable income-generating opportunities. Girls with disabilities were reported to be at risk of coercion into sex in exchange for support, and as a result, vulnerable to unwanted pregnancy and sexually transmitted disease. Moreover, men’s lack of knowledge and engagement in nutrition and health programs often lead to their restricting women’s movement to services in favor of attending to household needs, a reality that inhibits the access of women to critical basic services. Further, the stigma around GBV, especially sexual exploitation and abuse, has precluded many from seeking treatment, exacerbating the physical health and nutrition effects of the attack. The above facts call for coordinated and standardized efforts for the delivery of gender-responsive and do no harm approaches.

2 - Project activities

Gender Analysis: We will conduct a rapid gender analysis in each of Action Against Hunger’s areas of interventions to assess gender and GBV dynamics that affect or undermine current humanitarian intervention strategies. This will inform any recommendations and changes in approach for our organization as well as for cluster partners. Safety Audit: Action Against Hunger will conduct Safety Audits in its health, nutrition, WASH and FSL intervention sites using validated tools to assess protection risks in and around nutrition facilities and suggest measures for risk prevention and mitigation. The findings will enable us to mitigate the potential risks posed through different sectors for example ensuring availability of gender segregated WASH facilities at nutritional sites, reducing waiting time for beneficiaries coming to site to avail services ensuring FSL cash distribution centers are close to beneficiary homes to reduce long distance movement. Recommendations from the Safety audits will be used for improving programming approaches. Provision of Psychosocial support: Action Against Hunger will capacity building activities for its staff and the frontline workers in areas GBV basic concepts and psychological first aid (PFA) to improve their skills so as to provide group and individualized psychosocial support for vulnerable women, men, boys and girls in affected areas. Selected recreational activities and skill-building activities will be identified and facilitated by PSS workers and trained community volunteers. Facilitate Safe and confidential referral for specialized services: Action Against Hunger will train all site and community level staff in our areas on identifying available referral mechanisms for cases of GBV, as well as raising awareness among program participants on such pathways. As part of this effort, we will work with the GBV sub-cluster to gather information on available services and the latest referral pathways.Awareness-raising on gender equality and GBV topics: Action against Hunger will conduct awareness-raising and information dissemination sessions using the different platforms to disseminate information on GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and available services for women, men, boys, and girls. The Mother-to-Mother Group discussion forums and the Men group discussion forums will be established at nutrition sites as one way of reaching out to community members with key messages. Existing tools and guidelines will be developed to facilitate contextualized sessions in the intervention areas. Support and train vulnerable women and girls, including survivors and ability-challenged persons on fuel-efficient stove production: With the current flooding and constantly changing climate, women, girls, boys, and men are exposed to risks of GBV while collecting fuelwood for the household. Action against Hunger will identify vulnerable groups with a particular focus on women and girls of women and girls, and people with disabilities to support and train them on fuel-efficient cooking stove productionCapacity Building of staff and Gender Focal points: Action Against Hunger will continue to train its entire staff on gender and GBV concepts to establish a universal commitment to the values of gender equality and GBV awareness-raising. Feedback and Complaints Mechanism: Supporting the implementation of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policies within the organization and within the community, and promoting awareness of complaint mechanisms.Dignity Kit Distribution: Dignity kits will be distributed for GBV survivors, highly vulnerable women, and girls at riskSupport and train women and girls on Fuel-Efficient Stove production: Action against Hunger will identify and select vulnerable and most at-risk women and girls from its project sites and train them on the production of Fuel-Efficient stoves as means of risk mitigation and also income generation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries, as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries will be analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our nutrition and WaSH programs. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure the provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specially asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our sites, we avail FCM modalities as “minimum standards” (1). Monthly staff meeting with first-line implementers 2. Focus Group Discussion with direct beneficiaries. 3. Individual interview Form, 4. Comment Box at Field office gates and nutrition sites. 5. Anonymous Hotline Numbers

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Action Against Hunger trains all of its staff and volunteers on the organization’s PSEA Policy. During our mobilization meetings, Action Against Hunger shares information about PSEA from the organization is PSEA Policy. During project implementation, the organization regularly shares PSEA information with beneficiaries as a reminder/refresher to emphasize the organization’s commitment to Zero tolerance on PSEA

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Regular meetings will be held with beneficiaries to tell them about the project and the feedback/compliant mechanisms. We have standard, context-appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers,) to be displayed at all project sites and on project structures (e.g. equipment used by the beneficiaries. During all community meetings, training, and sensitization sessions a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implemented during the monthly meetings. Feedback will be provided to beneficiaries on how their concerns have been addressed.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To mitigate the risks identified above, Action against Hunger will employ the following mechanisms.To prevent the instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV, ACF will ensure gender-sensitive targeting and intervention at all levels. We will do sensitization to staff and beneficiaries, ensure reporting mechanisms are in place and are known by staff and beneficiaries. Action against Hunger sensitizes all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. Action Against Hunger will do sensitization to staff and beneficiaries, ensure reporting mechanisms are in place and are known by staff and beneficiaries Staff and beneficiaries will also be sensitized about services available, and strong referral linkage will be established with service providers. All staff and volunteers will be given orientation on the existing policies and on safe and confidential service provision. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Action Against Hunger provides integrated services to beneficiaries at community level. Key protection services are incorporated within our programs, which include identifying and assigning GBV focal persons at all facilities, training staff on GBV prevention, risk mitigation, Psychological First Aid (PFA), and referral pathways, increasing the number of female staff in the project team and committees at community/village level, raising awareness in the community on GBV issues, conducting safety audits at our project sites etc.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Action against Hunger promotes sustainable and climate smart interventions in its project areas. In 2022, it aims to plant 5000 trees in Warrap state only, engaging men and women in the communities. Through its FSL activities, it also prioritizes and supports women and girls to engage in the production of kitchen gardening to improve their access to food and self-sufficiency. Through this project, it will also continue piloting and support of climate aware interventions such as the production efficient cooking stove as means for protection risk mitigation

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The production of more food, promotion of resilient activities will go a long way in supporting populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. Enhancing the productivity of people in need of humanitarian aid will reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 as they become self-reliant and continue to fend for themselves. Action Against hunger strongly invests on women empowerment, prevention, and mitigation of the risks for women and girls that are affecting their health and productivity. This will also include sensitization of the community on potential GBV threats and available referral pathways and services.
