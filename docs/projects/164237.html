Project Summary

This project aims to improve education service provision in NW Syria; a geographic location that has absorbed a lot of IDPs as a result of the recent displacement wave movements started in Dec 2019 targeting 15 schools in Jandairis, Sheikh El-Hadid and Ma’btali Sub-Districts with a focus on mainstreaming child protection.
The overall objective of the project is to increase access and quality of education to conflict-affected children. The specific objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to formal & non-formal education for 15 schools with 3867 school-aged girls and boys between ages 5 -17 “boys: 2058, & girls: 1809”. Plus, 152 schools’ personnel “80 men, & 72 women”.
By designing this project, Sadad puts in mind to address the most critical needs of boys and girls children to ensure safe and equitable access to the protective, clean and quality education environment. This will be achieved through integrating education and wash interventions in addition to mainstreaming child protection in education as follow:
- Provision of school personnel incentives for the 178 education personnel in the learning facilities.
- Covering the running costs for the 15 learning facilities.
- Distribute teaching and learning materials for 15 learning facilities.
- Procure and distribute stationery school bags for students the 15 learning facilities.
- Conducting awareness sessions targeting 15 schools and parents of those children.
- Updating the schools’ safety plans, and install/renew first aid equipment for all learning facilities.
- The project will also promote Back-to- Learning campaigns.
- Conduct hygiene promotion and distribution of hygiene kits to boys and girls students in both formal and non-formal settings.
- Conducting ASER tests in the 15 learning facilities for 1200 (600 boys – 600 girls) school-aged children (5-17) enrolled in non-formal education.
- Fuel distribution during the winter season in the 15 learning facilities.
- Providing/printing textbooks for students in the 15 learning facilities.
- Providing 152 teachers working in the targeted schools with capacity development (teachers in a crisis context, PSS safety, working with children with disabilities)
- Provision of formal education for 15 schools and non-formal education for the students targeted in the same location and in TECs.
- Establish sensitive WASH facilities within all the schools.
- Conduct minor rehabilitation for 15 school buildings targeting 116 classrooms.
MEAL officers will focus on planning and regular review of activities against indicators, assessment of whether activities are carried out as planned, and identification and referral of issues during implementation; MEAL staff support with building and maintaining data management systems, providing tools, training, and guidelines, and program-level monitoring and analysis, data is collected on a weekly basis and consolidated monthly.
M&E Tools:
1-Data collection forms to collect the numbers of students in formal education monthly from facilitators;
2-Form to track the attendance in non-formal education;
3-ASER tool to measure education outcomes at the start of the project and at the end of the academic school year;
4-Complaint forms to register complaint or feedback from students, teachers or caregivers;
5-Basic information registration forms for (teachers and students);
6- Satisfaction surveys.
7- KAP survey that provides access to quantitative and qualitative information and monitors the baseline and end-line of distribution of hygiene kits in the targeted schools.

4 - Needs

The situation in Syria was exacerbated especially as the conflict enters its ninth year, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. Some 13.1 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 5.6 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services, while 6.3 million people are internally displaced. The humanitarian situation across northwest Syria continue to deteriorate at an alarming rate due to ongoing violence, which has a devastating effect on the four million people in northwest Syria. Lack of safety and security constitutes the greatest barrier to accessing education, with schools and education facilities seemingly under attack and occupied by armed actors. HNO Syria 2019.The current population in the proposed location of intervention “which is Jandairis, Ma’btali, and Sheikh El-Hadid Sub-districts is around 68478 according to HNO 2019. The estimated numbers of IDPs arrival in the assessed communities ranged from 24000 to 50000 individuals a 52% increase compared to the number of arrivals reported in December. According to the Afrin Education Assembly records of the current academic school year, findings indicated that nearly 24929 school-aged boys and girls have been registered in the current academic school year, (11796 girls – 13133 boys aged 3 – 17) In addition 831 teachers and educational personnel (393 females – 483 males).Findings show that stationery kits, school’s bags, learning materials, recreational and PSS activities are in the top needs of students, while sufficient incentives, provision of teaching materials, and capacity building comes at the top needs of teachers. In addition, the assessment showed needs to light repairs and improvement of classrooms, gender-sensitive WASH facilities especially enabling access of children with disabilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will start with focus on the formal education based on the adopted Syrian curriculum 2667 (1209 girls – 1458 boys) school-aged children (5-17) including 260 Cwd and 123 (60 women – 63 men) educational personnel are in need to continuity of the educational process through covering the incentives with permission to work and support education in the targeted location from the Turkish Authorities, covering monthly running expenses, conduct minor rehabilitation of the 15 schools ensuring accessibility of CwD, provision of students and teachers’ kits, provision of textbooks & recreational materials, conduct training to education personnel; teachers in a crisis context, Basic psychosocial support in education: A training manual for teachers and other stakeholders, and training on working with children with disabilities, conduct Thematic Group Discussions (TGDs) targeting parents of children in the targeted community on subjects like; The Right to Education, Child Labor, Child Early Marriage in crisis contexts and establish Parent Teachers Associations (PTAs), provision pf school-based psychosocial support and life skills programs' activities in formal settings during the school academic year and in the summer-time. Finally, conduct academic examinations to the 2667 school-aged children in formal settings. For the non-formal education 1200 (600 boys, 600 girls) school-aged children (5-17) including 116 CwD and 29 (12 women, 17 men) educational personnel are in need to equip the schools with what is needed to ease the access of CwD, covering the incentives for educational personnel for 6 months, covering monthly running expenses, provision of students’ and teachers’ kits, provision of textbooks and recreational materials, transition of the non-formal education students to formal education after using; catch-up classes, literacy, and numeracy classes in non-formal settings and after school hours based on the results of ASER baseline survey.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

#Risk_1: Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, a direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff/students or military use of the school. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: All attacks will be reported to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism. If possible, learning facilities would be structurally strengthened to better withstand the type of attack they are most likely to be exposed to. Learning facilities will be provided with safety protocols in place besides safety resources like; walkie-talkies, firefighting equipment, and exit signs. Sadad will trace population and re-program the project within the new locations where targeted beneficiaries were displaced to.#Risk_2: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Students participate in the bullying of students. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: Teachers and school personnel will be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. Students, teachers and other education personnel should be provided regular psychosocial support services. Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct and policy on bullying.#Risk_3: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: Learning facilities should have Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place and these should be regularly monitored and corrective action is taken when needed.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

#Assumption1: The security situation within the targeted villages in Olive Branch is estimated to remain safe to ensure the best implementation and provision of services.#Risk 1: Likelihood medium, Impact is high. The risk of Sadad has no more access to the targeted locations, an intensification of hostilities, change in areas’ control or any other reason. Mitigation: Sadad will trace population and re-program the project within the new locations where targeted beneficiaries were displaced to, or if this is not applicable, Sadad will try to transfer the project to Azaz Sub-district according to the needs identified in assessments and after coordination with the Education cluster.#Assumption2: The security situation within the targeted villages in Olive Branch is estimated to remain safe to ensure the best implementation and provision of services.Risk1: Likelihood medium: Changes in the security situation within the project target areas and here the main risk may come from airstrikes, clashes between the armed groups causing the suspension of project’s activities and restricting access to the formal education in schools. Impact: high. Mitigation: Schools to be provided with safety and security equipment and there will be a continuous risk assessments and a safety and security plan in addition to putting in place an emergency response in case any of the targeted schools were targeted or bombed as well as ensuring that staff members, teachers, and education personnel are trained in safety and security measures including evacuation. When the security situation turns bad and insecure, there will be a suspension of project activities. Also, during the rehabilitation phase, ensure that windows are being replaced with plastic sheets instead of glass, and all safety tools are in place and rehabilitate the basement “if any” to be used as a temporary shelter.
