4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrian people, thereby affecting their ability to sustain adequate standards of living, and access essential infrastructure and services. The World Bank reclassified Syria as a Low-Income Country (LIC) in 2018 implying that the Gross National Income per capita has fallen below US$ 996. Against this backdrop, there are 9.9 million people inside Syria requiring Early Recovery and Livelihoods support given the cumulative impact of the crisis on the population and broader macro-economic developments. Early Recovery and Livelihoods needs are on the rise and the response needs to be scaled up to reduce dependency on humanitarian life-saving assistance and to prevent the prevalence of harmful coping mechanisms. Displacements from areas still facing hostilities to relatively stable areas are likely to increase demand on essential services and employment opportunities. Likewise, returns will require rehabilitation of services and infrastructure and resumption of economic activity.3 Moreover, the first Key Message mentioned in ERL HNO report was: More than half of the working-age population is without access to sustained employment leading to poverty and contributing poor living standards. Youth without reliable employment may be exposed to a variety of risks including involvement in crime and trafficking. In conjunction with the above MIDMAR implemented a need assessment with 107 respondents, 41 of them were females and 66 were males, the questionnaire covered 21 sub-districts.The mean required training was in the following order:• Designing and implementing solar systems• Graphic designing and montage• Nursing• ProgrammingAfter this need assessment we compare these result with a market study we have implemented in the same area we decide to focus on two training topics which are:• Designing and implementing solar systems• Graphic design with a video montage

5 - Implementation of activities

The overall objective is to contribute to the socio-economic inclusion of the Syrian population affected by the conflict through technical and practical skills development.Outcome 1.1 Promoting suitable competencies for the labor market.Output 1.1.1 A need assessment and market study have been conducted. Output 1.1.2 Vocational training modules to respond to the Syrian Society needs have been provided to 120 Participants.Outcome 1.2 Enhanced social cohesion through enhancing participant's capacity for mutual diverse communicationOutput 1.2.1 Negotiation skills training with practical methods that simulate real cases of the Syrian context had been providedOutput 1.2.2. Soft skills training has been provided in order to increase the job opportunities chances in the future Output 1.2.3. Graduators will be referred to our partners (HAYAT and UOSSM with a recommendation from our side that shows that the participant meets the common selection criteria. This value chain approach will be achieved by referring vocational training Graduators to the Microfinance fund program provided by HAYAT and UOSSM to participate in "alternative energy to operate health facilities" projectOutput 1.2.4. Graduates will be linked with an online platform to follow scientific subjects and share members' experiences and job opportunitiesThe rationale for the Implementation: Split for Seven calendar months, with activities scheduled in Sarmada and Afrin, the Action is divided into two main components working in parallel: 1. The vocational training component:1.1. Designing and implementing Solar Systems (400 h)/ Sarmada+Afrin1.2.Graphic Design and Video Montage (400 h)/Afrin2- Soft skills component to promote social cohesion as a core component of the project which includes the following topics:• Entrepreneurship• Small project management• Negotiation skills• Community leadership• Job interviews• Feasibility study• Financial management• Self-employment skills

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks:Women during the project could face a risk of GBV (harassment, physical violence, verbal violence) during traveling to training centers by themselves, and cultural issues with regard to attending the training by themselves not accompanied by their (brothers, husbands or any men relatives) and the joint activities with men.Risk mitigation:We will provide transportation for women to bring and send women back to their homes.We will provide separate training venues for women.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Associated Risks for providing the training 1- Large displacement waves due to military actions, Medium ProbabilityWe will mitigate this risk by working in two locations in parallel (Sarmada, Afrin) in this case, if one of the locations has been attacked then we can refer the displaced participants to the other location.2- Community refused of mixed courses between women and men, high probability.You will reduce this risk by splitting the training into two training rooms one for women and one for men
