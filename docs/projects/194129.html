Project Summary

As a result of the clashes that erupted throughout the country between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) on 15 April 2023, the already fragile protection environment in Sudan has significantly deteriorated, with a multitude of protection risks that have further worsened access to services such as water, health care, electricity, and basic items and commodities such as food, cash, and fuel for millions of people. Several cities have faced heavy bombardments and clashes, and associated unexploded ordnance, causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical water, health care and power infrastructure, increasing of criminal activities, with indiscriminate attacks on civilians and widespread looting. Furthermore, high insecurity and looting of humanitarian assets and offices have compromised humanitarian access to key locations. As of 21 May 2023 a total of 1,042,114 individuals (209,136 households) have been displaced across the country mostly from urban areas in affected states. Vulnerable groups are increasingly exposed to protection risks with significant harm to their physical and mental wellbeing.
This project intends to contribute to promoting a favorable protection environment in communities particularly affected by the current crisis, while providing humanitarian assistance to individuals and groups. The proposed intervention intends to provide General, Child, and GBV related protection services in the areas of interventions with the cluster estimating 7.3 million people in need of these protection services. The project is in line with the Cluster and Sub-Cluster Objectives and will target localities across the target areas identified as “hotspots” by the Protection Cluster and partners.
IOM will improve access of vulnerable crisis-affected people, especially girls and women, to primary healthcare and protection services, including GBV response services. Also, IOM will assist conflict-affected population with the distribution of non-food items (NFIs), in coordination with the Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items(ES/NFI) Cluster. IOM will also work towards strengthening the capacity and availability of GBV services through the training and deployment of social workers and psychologists, in addition to enhancing existing referral mechanism to address GBV and child protection issues and cases. To improve the crisis-affected people’s access to community-based dispute resolution mechanisms, IOM also will build the capacity of informal mediators and dispute resolvers. To achieve this aim, IOM will work with the existing community committees, community leaders, and representatives from other community-based organisations, including women and youth groups as well as those belonging to both farmers and herder groups. IOM staff will also contribute to “protection by presence” by collecting data on protection needs, gaps and trends for advocacy, IOM will coordinate with its other existing programming in the targeted states, such as the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and humanitarian response and transition interventions including Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) and health activities, to leverage effective synergies between programme activities in view of delivering the best protection outcomes for affected populations.
In coordination with the Civil Registry and UNHCR, IOM will support the local government to strengthen their capacity and resources to improve the birth registration of IDPs and service provision to persons with disabilities through the provision of office equipment, trainings, and facilitating regular visits of government staff to hard-to-reach areas to provide the services to rural communities. Finally, IOM will support the establishment and/or reactivation of women’s groups and centres in secure areas to ensure access to safe engagement platforms as well as to information on protection services.

Implementing Partners

In coordination with international and national partners, including State authorities, IOM intends to target the most conflict affected areas in Khartoum, Northern, and Eastern States, Darfur region. Nevertheless, the volatile security situation on the ground and the significant challenges in accessing and providing humanitarian assistance in target areas will require IOM to adopt a certain of flexibility in the provision of humanitarian assistance as well as to adapt its interventions to changes of an already very volatile environment. Moreover, such flexibility will allow IOM to prioritize those locations where protection and humanitarian needs are more urgent, to tackling the above mentioned challenges and address humanitarian needs accordingly.

1 - Needs

The protection environment remains extremely fragile in Sudan, where a multitude of already existing protection risks and issues have been further exacerbated by the current conflict, including protection of civilians (due to the fighting frequently occurring in urban areas), difficult or no access to humanitarian services, weak/lack of rule of law, inter-communal violence, presence of non-state armed groups, increasing criminal activities. Gaps in the protection environment have significantly widened since the beginning of the conflict, with civilians in many parts of the country that remain exposed to ongoing hostilities, resulting in civilian casualties and forced displacements as people seek safety. The current people in need is 7.3 million (including 3.6 million people for General Protection, 2 million people for Child Protection and 1.3 million people GBV Protection categories).In line with the National Plan for the Protection of Civilians, the proposed action aims to promote a more favorable protection environment, by improving access to basic and protection services to conflict affected populations. In particular, the proposed action intends to strengthen protection monitoring and protection by presence in target locations and to ensure the rights of men, women, boys, and girls are upheld. Moreover, many people in remote areas, especially IDPs and returnees but also persons with disabilities.The fragile protection environment in the affected states is hindering vulnerable, crisis-affected populations’ access to protection services. Vulnerable groups are increasingly exposed to protection risks with significant harm to their physical and mental wellbeing. This includes children and an increased number of women, girls who are exposed to risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) including domestic violence and increased instances of assaults by armed assailants. Assistance such as psychosocial support (PSS) to child and adolescent and GBV survivors is limited in the current crisis. IOM recommends additional capacity building of GBV service providers, the deployment of GBV social workers and psychologists, and create safe spaces for survivors especially women and girls who are disproportionately affected receive necessary support including access to information on available GBV and protection services. The crises also hampers people’s access to basic services. Services that cater to the needs of persons with disabilities are limited, and given their specific vulnerabilities, there is a need to build the capacity of health and social welfare staff on addressing their needs and overcoming the legal barriers they face when trying to access basic services. Many people in remote areas, especially protracted and newly displaced IDPs and returnees, may lack access to birth registration or have lost their identification during the crisis, and therefore also often legal identity. This is creating a situation where people cannot access basic services that require birth certificates or other proof of identification to receive critical services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1 - contributing to Protection and GBV Objectives 1: Vulnerable crisis-affected people in the target areas, especially girls and women, have access to protection services including GBV response services. To achieve this output, IOM will: 1.1. Strengthen access to primary healthcare services among the target population in coordination with IOM’s Health team. 1.2. Strengthen access to NFI support including dignity kits in displacement contexts, in coordination with IOM’s NFI team. 1.3. Build the capacity of GBV service providers on life-saving responses including case management and referrals to external specialists, caring for child and adolescent survivors, psychosocial support (PSS), and GBV case management (including non-therapeutic interventions to help cope with stressors). All PSS activities will be in line with the IOM Manual on Community-Based Mental Health and PSS in Emergencies and Displacement.1.4. Deploy experienced GBV social workers and psychologists for providing case management support and PSS (e.g., psychological counselling, peer support groups, organisation of socio-relational activities). The psychologists will also provide PSS to other crisis-affected population, including the youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities. 1.5. In coordination with relevant partners, enhance existing referral mechanisms to address GBV and child protection issues and cases. Output 2 - contributing to Protection Objective 2: People in the target areas have access to community-based dispute resolution mechanisms. To achieve this output, IOM will: 2.1. Build capacity of informal mediators and dispute resolvers, including the representatives from existing various committees in the target areas and other community leaders such as village chiefs, imams, women group leaders and youth group leaders. 2.2. Raise awareness of available informal dispute resolution avenues among the target population. 2.3. Sensitize school youth to the importance of peacebuilding and coexistence. Output 3 - contributing to Protection Objective 2: “Protection by presence” and protection monitoring mechanisms are enhanced, in coordination with local communities and partner organisations. To achieve this output, IOM will: 3.1. Monitor the protection situation in concerned locations and collect relevant information on protection needs, gaps, and trends for advocacy, programmatic, and ad hoc assistance purposes. IOM will enhance protection monitoring mechanisms, tracking population movements in synergy with its DTM programme, and identifying vulnerable persons in need of assistance. Relevant findings will be shared regularly with the Protection Cluster, Protection Working Group (PWGs), GBV Sub PWGs of the targets states. Output 4 - contributing to Protection Objective 3: The local government has the capacity and resources to improve birth registration of IDPs and service provision to persons with disabilities. To achieve this output, IOM will: 4.1. Increase the local government’s capacity to improve access to birth registration services among IDPs (provision of computers, printers, internet access, a generator and solar power). 4.2 Support the local government in organizing a mobile registration team to hard-to-reach localities (provision of transportation). 4.3 Build the capacity of health and social welfare staff on addressing the needs of persons with disabilities. Output 5 - contributing to GBV Objective 2: Safe platforms for engagement are made available for women and girls to access information related to women’s empowerment and available protection services including GBV services. To achieve this output, IOM will: 5.1 Support the establishment and/or reactivation of women’s groups and centres in the target areas. 5.2. Raise awareness of GBV and available protection services among women and girls.5.3. Link the women's groups with GBV service providers at state and national level.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will solely focus on protection issues, including GBV response, thus when designing and implementing all project activities, protection of the crisis-affected population, particularly girls, women, and persons with disabilities, will be mainstreamed. IOM will ensure mainstream survivor-centred and trauma-informed approaches when creating and implementing activities directly impacting GBV survivors. The proposed action will target the returnee and displaced population and the most vulnerable individuals and households from the host communities. Furthermore, IOM will keep local authorities/communities and other relevant partners involved and/or informed on the implementation of field activities as well as on the selection criteria to avoid risks of tensions with local communities. Moreover, vulnerable individuals from the receiving communities will also be targeted. Key actions will therefore include identifying local authorities and community leaders and strengthening and supporting their role where possible; displaying and/or explaining criteria in identifying individuals to be selected for the activities (for example, for the distribution of NFIs); and, including individuals from any group (IDPs, returnees, vulnerable individuals from the local communities). Other relevant actions will include organising distributions at the safest time of the day; providing information on protection from exploitation and abuse and additional protection-related concerns; involving in the project’s implementation male and female staff; and keeping a list of individuals involved and related data confidential.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IOM is part of the UNCT led in country coordination structure in PSEA. Sudan has also developed Sudan’s PSEA Country Level Action Plan 2022, in which five key areas of engagement are covered: prevention activities; safe and accessible reporting; victims’ rights to assistance; and accountability and investigation; and in country PSEA coordination structure. In particular, the in country coordination structure avails opportunities for networking, data gathering and sharing, as well as developing feedback loops related to support needs at every level of programme implementation. IOM is part of these inter-agency coordination approaches, including the PSEA Working Group, and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected populations, to provide feedback and complaint mechanisms to all beneficiaries, and to ensure programming is aligned with Sudan’s PSEA Country Level Action Plan 2022. For example. IOM has an internal PSEA focal point structure for the Migrant Resource and Response Centres in Khartoum, Gedaref and Kassala states that is also linked to IOM's sub-offices across Sudan, including the Darfur states. All IOM staff and contractors have completed mandatory PSEA trainings. IOM will take measures to have a similar structure in each project location. As for the prevention of SEA, IOM has a zero tolerance policy towards SEA and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanisms for alleged/suspected SEA cases. IOM will inform all beneficiaries of the available feedback and complaint mechanisms, including PSEA reporting. Similarly, IOM PSEA focal staff will take measures to train Implementing Partners on PSEA and establish an accountability structure to report incidents of SEA for support provisions, prevention, and mitigation of incidents, through the internal network of PSEA sub-focal and focal staff at the field, programmatic, and mission levels.
