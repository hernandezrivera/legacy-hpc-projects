Project Summary

The project aims at supporting war affected households by rehabilitating partially damaged houses and distributing NFI kits to displaced and returnees in Maret Annuman in Idleb and in Al Bab in Aleppo. In total, 1,900 HHs will benefit from the activities of this project (4,892 females – 4,608 males). The rehabilitation will target 400 houses that are partially damaged houses that people are living in or have returned to. NFI kits will be distributed to 800 HHs that have been displaced from different areas into Idleb and Aleppo (400 kits to the same beneficiaries of the rehabilitation). In winter, the project targets 1100 HHs with winter NFI kits to protect people from harsh weather conditions.

Needs

As the war enters its eighth year in Syria, the humanitarian situation is becoming worse. 2018 witnessed several forced displacements from Eastern Ghouta, Daraa, and Northern Homs into Idleb and Aleppo. According to HNO 2019, 4.7 million people need shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters, while 4.4 million people are in need of NFI assistance. Though there is a decrease in the need of NFIs in WoS, the number of people in need of NFI assistance in Idleb has increased by over 600,000. In the period between September 2018 and January 2019, the CCCM Cluster tracked 125,095 displacements in North-West Syria. Of those, 81,753 displacements were to Idleb, 42,575 to Aleppo and 767 to Hama governorate. The waves of displacement have limited the capacity to host all these people specially in Idleb. The need for durable shelter solutions is increasing as most people have been displaced for more than a year in Idleb and Aleppo and even the host communities whose houses have been damaged are trying to return and repair their houses if possible. MaretAnnuman in Idleb and in Al Bab in Aleppo have witnessed several airstrikes in the period between 2013 to 2017 that resulted in destruction of public buildings, general infrastructure, as well as private houses. In 2018, these two districts hosted a lot of IDPs coming from surrounding areas of Maret Annuman or from other cities in Al Bab in addition to the returning movement to Al Bab after the withdrawal of ISIS from the area. Around 1,300 houses have been partially damaged in Maret Annuman (HIHFAD Needs assessment, December 2018) during heavy fighting and airstrikes. These houses are damaged and do not provide the basic living conditions. Some have damaged water tanks which may result in water borne diseases as they are open or leak a lot. Some do not have doors or windows for the rooms or even for the house which may result in some protection issues and leave residents vulnerable to theft and exposed to harsh weather conditions. Given the poor economic conditions and lack or non-existence of livelihood opportunities for majority of the youths who are able and willing to work, they cannot rehabilitate their houses and live in them. Some of these people continue to live in the damaged houses as they have no other alternatives. Continued emergency needs exist for populations displaced in/from areas such as northeast Syria and areas where local agreements are reached. On the other hand, a significant number of IDPs were able or would like to return to their communities of origin, increasing the need to repair and rehabilitate damaged shelters to move toward solutions for those able to return. As a result of war, female headed households increased and this highlights the need for appropriate shelters especially among female-headed households as they face higher risks of sexual harassment or exploitation in absence of male family members. In addition, the number of people with disabilities also increased and these people are more vulnerable to weather-borne risks such as pneumonia and other respiratory diseases.

Implementation of activities

This project will compromise Rehabilitation of 400 partially damaged houses and distributing 800 core NFI kits in addition to distributing 1100 winter NFI kits. The activities of the project will benefit 9,500 beneficiaries (2,138 boys under 18 , 2,127 girls under 18, 2470 men, 2765 women)Rehabilitation of 400 partially damaged houses: 400 HHS will benefit from this activity (2000 beneficiaries: 467 boys under 18, 457 girls under 18, 496 men, 580 women) HIHFAD will receive applications from beneficiaries who are in need of house rehabilitation, the application will include the ID of the applicants in addition to property ownership documents that will be verified by the community. In case of rent, there must be a contract for at least six months from the completion of the repair. Two members of the staff (one male and one female) who are trained on HLP will be focal points responsible for any HLP issues confronted during implementation of the project. A technical needs assessment of every house will be conducted before starting the implementation of the rehabilitation works. The house should be impartially damaged, and the cost of all rehabilitation works will not exceed $900 for each house. The selection of beneficiaries for the Shelter rehabilitation activities will be based on the following criteria:- Families who have returned to their damaged houses.- Female headed households (both from host communities and IDPs);- Families with chronically ill members or with disabilities (both from host communities and IDPs)- People from host community who still live in their damaged houses; Affected people will be consulted about what they need to be rehabilitated as a priority in case the damage is more than average. After identifying the needed works, HIHFAD will announce a tender to contract with a competent local contractor to provide some job opportunities for the youths in the targeted area. A field engineer from the organization will supervise the work to make sure that all works are according to the standard. People who live in the house will be part of supervising the contractors work and they will be involved in the rehabilitation if they are capable of.Distributing 800 core NFI kits:After rehabilitating the houses, the families will receive NFI kits which will be according to the Shleter&NFI standards. In this case families will have their houses rehabilitated in addition to core NFI kits. While other 400 NFI kits will be distributed to newly displaced people or to returnees in these areas.Distributing 1100 winter NFI kits:Distribution of these kits will take place between 15th October 2019 and 30th November 2019. The criteria and final locations will be shared with the Shelter&NFI cluster to avoid any duplication. Distribution will take place in a fixed distribution point with taking consideration to give priorities to the elderly. There will be separation between men and women while there will be door to door distribution to people with disabilities.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment of women and girls)Mitigation: 1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 - Train staff on code of conduct – 3 - Include code of conduct in all staff contracts that address any SEA complaints. Risk: Some beneficiaries (most vulnerable) might be at risks such as harassment during the distribution of NFI kits if the distribution points are not organized:Mitigation: 1 - allocate one day for distributing to female headed households when possible.2 - Separate between men and women during the distribution3 - Give priority to vulnerable people (elderly and pregnant women) in addition to door to door distribution to people with disabilities. Risk: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated.Mitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP DD. 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Access restriction due to local fighting between different arm source groups in the targeted location which may restrict the access. The local fighting might delay the implementation of the activities. Mitigation: HIHFAD Security team will monitor the situation on the ground and send reports when necessary. The works will be suspended during the fighting and resumed after assessment of the situation. Assumption: the security situation remains relatively stable which allow people to continue living in the area and the activities of the project to take place. Risk: Lack of skilled workers and competent contractors to implement the rehabilitation works.Mitigation: HIHFAD Announce for tender and communicating with other organizations to recommend some good competent contractors in addition to supervising the work of the contractor by HIHFAD field engineers.Risk: Closure of the border for commercial shipments or changing some regulation related to allowed items to be exported to Syria may have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities.Mitigation:1. Explore alternative supply lines through local procurement2. Communicate regularly with logistic cluster to have valid information about new or changing regulations.Assumption: The crossing borders are open for humanitarian and commercial shipments.
