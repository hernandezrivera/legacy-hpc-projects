Project Summary

Magwi and Kajo Keji are high risk counties for communicable diseases outbreaks such as viral hemorrhagic fevers, Cholera and Malaria. Seasonal Outbreaks of Cholera and Malaria are frequently reported during last few years. Frequent cross border movement through Nimule and displacements in Kajokeji poses threat for communicable disease outbreaks.
ARC is currently operating 10 health facilities in Kajo keji and 30 health facilities in Magwi including PHCCs and PHCUs. The project will help prepare the health facilities for possible communicable disease outbreaks through trainings of front-line health workers on case definitions, case management and infection prevention and control including (prepositioning of PPEs). The project will also provide life saving essential health care services through mobile clinics to distant and remote populations, which are not able to access the health services. The package of health services consists of OPD consultations for communicable and non communicable diseases, Immunization for Children Under 5 years of age and provision of antenatal care and referrals of to health facilities for deliveries and treatment of complications, and ICCM. Health workers will also be trained on clinical management of rape survivors.

Needs

Magwi and Kajo keji counties are classified as high risk areas for epidemics such as Viral Hemorrhagic Fever, Cholera and Malaria. Voilent conflicts in Kajo Keji resulted in mass migration of population which displaced the IDPs from the camps. Many health facilities were forced to close down in Kajo keji due to damages and insecurity during this conflict. Currently, ARC is operating the ten PHCUs, which are still functioning in the county including Korijo IDP Settlement, which is hosting over 40,000 IDPs. Limited BEmONC services are available in Korijo settlement but these services need to be strengthened. After recent peace agreement, repatriation is expected next year subject to improvements in security situation. Magwi county is another high risk area for epidemic outbreak, with Nimule being the largest entry point into South Sudan, which recieves over 45,000 travellers a week. The county has faces seasonal communicable diseases outbreaks including AWD and Malaria. With an Ebola Virus Disease outbreak across border in Democratic Republic of Congo, both these counties are included in high risk areas. A recent EVD alert in Yei during October 2018 created an alarming situation. National EVD Taskforce and WHO has frequently communicated the needs for improving the screening and preparedness across the high risk areas. The existing health structures have limited capacity and human resources to respond to any epidemic outbreaks. Low coverage of immunizations, high rates of malnutrition, lack of WASH facilities and fragile health systems further increases the likelihood of morbidity and mortality in an event of outbreaks.

Activities

ARC is currently providing health services in 30 health facilities across Magwi and 10 health facilities in Kajo Keji Counties. The activities under proposed project will complement and strengthen the existing health services through addressing gaps in essential services and improving outreach services to beneficiaries. The major activities include:1. Provision of Out patient health services through mobile clinics to treat common illnesses2. Immunization campaigns for children under 5 years of age3. Improving ICCM services in community to treat Malaria, Diarrhea, and Pneumonias 4. Conducting health education and promotion campaigns to sensitize population on communicable diseases 5. Strengthening BEmONC services through addressing gaps in existing services6. Improving referral system from the community to health facilities7. Ensuring preparedness at health facilities to respond to communicable disease outbreaks and prevent excess mortality and morbidity8. Training of health workers on case definition, IPC and BEmONC services to improve quality of services9. Ensure monthly reporting from the health facilities on DHIS and IDSR 10. Training of health workers on clinical management of rape survivors and referral to cases to appropriate protection mechanisms 11. Sensitization and engagement of respective communities and BHTs on principles gender equality The activities intend to deliver an integrated and holistic health response, which range from provision of curative health services to prevention of epidemics and health education services.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The target beneficiaries will be informed and involved in the process of project monitoring and evaluation. Also, beneficiaries will be made aware about the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) system which ARC will analyse from time to time to measure beneficiaries’ access barriers to services. Through period program activity reports, ARC will documents the bureaucratic and physical access impediments, its magnitude, scope and impact on programming. All the information will be documented in the Complaints and Response Mechanism database held by the MEAL Coordinator at a central location in Juba. Appropriate action will be taken accordingly. However, sensitive issues will be channeled through the channels provided by ARC.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

All the information collected that contains details of beneficiaries will be kept confidential to the organization and utilized for project purposes only. This information will be kept within ARC office in lockable cupboards under the custody of the MEAL department. The MEAL Coordinator will develop tools for MEAL staff to periodically conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PMD)/Post Service Surveys with beneficiaries to collect and analyze information on various issues including beneficiary-safety. This information will be analyzed and reported accordingly to the project management team

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

ARC will measure the dignity of beneficiaries through organized focused group discussions and interviews with beneficiaries to evaluate the nature and quality of services provided including if the services are provided in a dignified manner and identify areas for improvement. The M&E team will assist to collect and analyze information shared through the feedback mechanisms and Post Service Surveys and to develop a report which will be shared with the program teams for action. Issues that will require the attention of the senior management will be escalated accordingly.
