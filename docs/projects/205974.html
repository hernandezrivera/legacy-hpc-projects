Project Summary

Through this project WV will provide emergency life-saving primary healthcare interventions through a combination of static and mobile health facilities at the IDP camps and affected communities in the target locations. WV will work in collaboration with the State MoH, Health Facility and other health actors' staff and community health volunteers to provide quality health services. This project will strengthen technical capacity of health workers in disease surveillance, control, management, and community sensitization. World Vision will operate 4 static clinics (1 Static clinic in Renk Transit Camp for Sudan crisis returnees, 1 in Melut for IDPs/returnees at Dengthoma 1&2 and returnees transiting from Paloich, 1 in Fashoda and 1 in Twic to serve the IDPs in Panyok and Akoc camps). WV will also operate 3 mobile clinics (1 in Tonj North county for Majok Lou, Rorkor, Majak tit, Makuok, Titbel, Kuanyiel, Manthiang, Awul and 1 in Tambura for affected populations settled in Yukusufu, Bavuru, Basanabaru, Kpotonoyo, 1 in Twic county for 10 (Panamath, Panther, Majook Noon, Aweng, Majak-Aher, Abyie-Dau, Gomgoi, Nyindeng-Ayuel, Tiit-Noon and Mayomchol-Luk) IDP settlements.
This project will respond to the humanitarian needs and health gaps that have continued to emerge and not served by HPF and other health projects implemented by WV in Warrap, Upper Nile and WES. WV will collaborate with WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA to ensure that essential medicines and reproductive health commodities are secured and delivered to the health sites and cold chain is efficiently maintained.
WV will target 107,211 individuals (60,778 host community 26,641 IDPs, 19,792 returnees) triggered by the Sudan Crisis. The project aims to provide critical lifesaving health, nutrition and WASH interventions to the most vulnerable particularly women and children among the IDPs, returnees and host communities in the target locations.
Through provision of static primary healthcare and mobile outreach with a package of lifesaving health interventions including diagnosis and management of common illnesses, normal deliveries, family planning, routine immunizations, and health awareness campaigns. In line with the 2024 Health CO1, this project will build the capacity of staff in disease surveillance, CMR, BeMONC/CeMONC, cholera case management, infection prevention and control. The project will strengthen and use already existing structures such as the Boma Health Workers, community nutrition volunteers, mother to mother support groups (MTMSG), men and women champion groups, to deliver health education, messaging on health promotion and diseases prevention among IDPs and the host communities. This project will ensure that the health response is integrated with Protection, FSL, WASH and nutrition interventions. This will be done by ensuring that the beneficiaries have access to sanitation and water supplies at the selected primary healthcare units, mobile clinics supported under this project. All cases identified with SAM and MAM at the health facilities and mobile clinic units are treated or referred to the OTP/TSFP sites and those with complicated SAM will be referred to the stabilization centers currently being supported by WV in the target locations. WV will further mitigate the gaps in service delivery by procuring medicines and essential equipment to support the health response. In line with 2024 Health CO2: WV will conduct reactive measles campaign; put in place community feedback mechanisms; In line with 2024 Health CO3, WV will facilitate quarterly review meetings with other humanitarian organizations and the community to discuss humanitarian issues affecting the target locations. In this project WV seeks to complement ongoing funding from Health Pooled Fund (HPF), Global Affairs Canada, UNICEF health projects, BHA, WVUS, WV Ireland funding focused on health system strengthening with emphasis on maternal child health and nutrition implemented in the
target locations.

1. Needs

According to the revised Basic Package for Health and Nutrition services for South Sudan, 2023, the country’s health system and infrastructure are among the poorest in the world. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. There is a severe shortage oftrained health professionals in all categories. Thus, people rely on inadequately trained or low skilled health workers to provide their healthcare. The Sudan Crisis, intercommunal and political violence in country have dragged back the progress made in strengthening.of the health system in the 6 Counties. Some of these facilities were vandalized but catchment populations displaced across more than the IDP camps and within the communities in Tambura in WEQ, Renk, Melut, Fashoda, in Upper Nile, Gorgrial East, and Twic, counties in Warrap. The various interagency assessments revealed severe needs of the population under target. The include but are not limited to i) Poor infrastructure for quality health service delivery following the vandalization by the conflict ii) shortage of medical equipment/commodities despite efforts to replace the required commodities through other funding sources mainly HPF. Some Cold Chain Equipment were left useless after robbing the solar panels which affects storage of vaccines and EPI coverage places like Tambura. ii) poor immunization coverage leading to rampant preventable vaccine diseases such as measles iv) lack of psychosocial support v) poor clinical management of rape survivors vi) long distance between the health facilities and the camps or settlement sites hinders access as the population struggle to reach the service sites. vi) Staffing gaps due to the presence of the IDP without additional support to the resources earmarked for the host populations, vi) then lack of budget for integrated outreach services for hard-to-reach populations in the far Payams. WVI aim to bridge the above barriers to access to health care through the aforementioned health cluster Humanitarian Response Plan objectives.

2. Project activities

WV will work in collaboration with the State MoH staff, Health Facility staff and other health actors and community health volunteers to provide quality health services. This project seeks to strengthen the technical capacity of health workers in disease surveillance, control, management, and community sensitization. WV will provide emergency primary healthcare through a combination of static & mobile health facilities at the IDP camps & affected communities in the target locations. 4 clinics- Upper Nile (1 Static clinic in Renk Transit Camp for Sudan crisis returnees, 1 in Melut for IDPs/returnees at Dengthoma 1&2 & returnees transiting from Paloch, 1 in Fashoda Kodok for IDPs and 1 in Twic for IDPs in Panyok and Akoc camps). WV will also operate 4 mobile clinics (2 in Tonj North County for Majok Lou, Rorkor, Majak tit, Makuok, Titbel, Kuanyiel, Manthiang, Awul and 1 in Tambura for affected populations settled in Yukusufu, Bavuru, Basanabaru, Kpotonoyo, 1 in Twic county for 10 (Panamath, Panther, Majook Noon, Aweng, Majak-Aher, Abyie-Dau, Gomgoi, Nyindeng-Ayuel, Tiit-Noon and Mayomchol-Luk) IDP settlements. WV will target 107,211 individuals (60,778 host communities; 26,641 IDPs, 19,792 returnees) triggered by the Sudan crisis. The project aims to provide critical health, nutrition and WASH interventions to vulnerable groups (women & children) among the IDPs, returnees and host communities in the target counties of Renk, Melut, Fashoda, Twic, Tonj North, Gogrial East and Tambura. Through provision of static primary healthcare and mobile outreach services. In line with CO1 (To improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child & adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations), this project will build the capacity of staff in disease surveillance, CMR, BeMONC/CeMONC, and strengthen and use existing structures such as the Boma Health Workers, CNV, MTMSG, men & women champion groups to deliver educational health messages on health promotion and diseases prevention in IDPs & host communities. Project will be integrated with Protection, FSL, WASH and nutrition interventions. Beneficiaries will have access to sanitation & water supplies in selected PHCUs, mobile clinics. CO2 (Specialized protection and multi-sectoral services that are sensitive to gender, age and disability are provided to vulnerable groups, including survivors of gender-based violence, neglect, and sexual exploitation and abuse) in line with this objective WV will support the static and mobile clinics with IHK kits, conduct reactive measles campaigns, produce and distribute IEC materials and establish community feedback mechanisms. C03 (To enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms, WV will facilitate quarterly review meetings with other humanitarians and the community to discuss humanitarian matters affecting the target locations.) in alignment to this objective WV seeks to complement/leverage the existing funding from HPF, Global Affairs Canada, UNICEF health, BHA, WVUS, WV Ireland projects in Renk, Melut, Fashoda, Tonj North, Gogrial East, Twic, Warrap and Tambura. These projects have a focus on health system strengthening with emphasis on maternal child health and nutrition.
