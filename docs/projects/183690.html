Project Summary

UNFPA and WFP jointly will respond to the humanitarian need for vulnerable GBV survivors and women at risk for GBV through contributing to improving access of vulnerable women and girls including women and girls with disabilities to essential, safe, and high-quality multi-sectoral services to eliminate GBV in the West Bank and Gaza Strip and improve their socio-economic status. It is a complementary approach to combining GBV service with food assistance. It supports the strengthening of the social protection system by complementing the Government's social benefits thereby recognizing people's multi-sectoral needs, maximizing collective efforts to address chronic vulnerabilities, and responding to shocks through integrated multi-sectoral services to protect vulnerable women from falling into the negative coping mechanisms and improve their socio-economic condition and their well- being.
The project will respond to the need for GBV multi-sectorial services including the socioeconomic needs by implementing a comprehensive package of protection interventions in the 2 Gaza governorates (Khan Younis, and Rafah) and 3 West Bank governorates (Jenin, Hebron, and Nablus).
The intervention will target 1,000 of the most vulnerable women and girls especially those who are left behind and registered in the national social safety network database, as the COVID-19 situation continues, the need to scale up GBV services continues to increase, as well the urgency to ensure a robust system of government and non-government actors to provide quality survivor-centered GBV services that meet minimum standards. The project is addressing the need of vulnerable women who are exposed to GBV that are left behind, coordination with MoSD to support the waiting list under the social safety net. More focus will be given to pregnant and lactating women that received services from the mobile clinic in the most affected and marginalized areas. UNFPA and WFP jointly will integrate CVA as an urgent protection tool, helping women and girls affected by COVID loss of income and additional GBV risks to access urgent items. This is will be complemented with nutrition awareness sessions for beneficiaries and service providers. As well as to increase the knowledge and capacity of the GBV organizations on PSEA. Moreover, the list of beneficiaries with UNWOMEN, MoSD, Food Security Cluster and UNRWA to avoid overlapping of CVA.
Special attention will be paid to women with disabilities and elderly women.

1 - Needs

The protracted emergency and conflict-related violence combined with the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, have exacerbated pre-existing risks facing women and girls in Palestine such as discrimination and gender-based violence (GBV), and increased food insecurity among them. The pandemic crisis has affected women and contributed to a behavioral dynamic of men being more frustrated, unable to fulfill their expected role in a society that is very patriarchal in nature where men’s main role is mainly their responsibility to provide for and protect their families. Some women who had a somewhat stable situation before the recent crisis; have become GBV survivors due to the economic deterioration resulting from the loss of livelihoods caused by the recurrent lockdowns.Many of them do not have the savings and capabilities to cope with the change and are now at high risk of violence. Based on the Pin analysis in HNO (2022) process, the total number of People in Need for the GBV sector is 453,660, with 288,557 women in the Gaza Strip and 165,103 in the West Bank. Pursuant to the Rapid Needs Assessment conducted in May 2021 by the GBV SC, women and girls were subjected to different forms of violence, such as verbal, physical, economic, psychosocial, and sexual violence during escalation. The escalation and its consequences caused anger, fear, anxiety, overcrowdedness, lack of security and privacy, and the inability to provide basic needs, which founded a fertile ground for continued and increased violence against women and girls. There are currently 87,000 pregnant women in Gaza and in vulnerable areas of the West Bank. Around 29,000 women will give birth over the next 3 months, and require access to essential healthcare services. Prior to the increase in escalations 30.5% of pregnant and lactating women were suffering from anemia in Palestine (West Bank and Gaza). It is expected that this may increase, particularly in Gaza. Meanwhile, the risk of gender-based violence, including harmful coping mechanisms such as child marriage, often increase during crises, further endangering the lives and well-being of women and girls. Accordingly, this joint project aims to address the critical gap in nutrition, food security, MHPSS, SRH and GBV services of the most vulnerable women groups in the West Bank and Gaza. It will address life-threatening micronutrient malnutrition and SRH counseling among the most vulnerable women of reproductive age through the provision of appropriate counseling. It will fill the gaps in GBV services and MHPSS through the provision of food and nonfood (protection) items, GBV and MHPSS counseling jointly delivered by UNFPA and WFP. According to MoSD statistical report (2020), 358 GBV survivors/cases were dealt with in the West Bank. Most of them were in a severe condition where 47% of GBV survivors got married between 20-29 years old, these women were exposed to physical violence, psychosocial and economic violence, 18% of them have been referred to the shelter, and 30% of these women have received cash assistance. Moreover, according to SAWA organization, after the crisis, a 20% increase in calls regarding mental health and psychosocial support and abuse/violence cases was recorded, and the majority came from young men and adolescent boys that had experienced abuse from their families at home. Calls from women seeking support increased from 40% to 58% of the total calls regarding abuse and domestic violence from partners increased by 38%. As indicated in the research paper conducted by Juzoor in June (2020) in the West Bank, a considerable increase in the demand for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS) was recorded from women, girls, and boys due to an apparent increase in the level of violence. The study showed that 25- 30% of the reported cases experienced physical violence by their spouses, and around 70% of Palestinians believe that domestic violence will likely increase amid the recurrent lockdowns. A policy brief issued by A report published by CARE in April 2020 showed that COVID-19 is likely to have a major impact on the health and nutritional status of children and women in the West Bank and Gaza. Lockdown, movement restrictions, and the re-prioritization of resources and health services toward COVID-19 response have significantly impeded women and girls’ ability to access the already limited GBV support, including safe spaces, shelters, and medical, psychosocial, and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services.The World Bank Report “Gaza Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment (RDNA), June 2021 “ stated that Poverty in Gaza is estimated to increase over the COVID-19 induced peak of 2020 with the poverty headcount rate reaching 59.3 percent in 2021. According to this report, it expects that poverty levels will further deteriorate reaching around 64% post-COVID-19 pandemic. Unemployment in the Gaza strip is three times higher than that in the West Bank and is expected to increase drastically due to the COVID-19 effects since the declaration of the state of emergency in Palestine on March 5th, 2020. More than 120,000 Palestinian lost their jobs in 2020. An additional 81,000 HHs need assistance with food distribution due to COVID 19. Furthermore, the humanitarian consequences of the recent hostilities in Gaza have been devastating, exacerbating the impact of almost 14 years of an Israeli blockade, internal Palestinian political divisions, recurrent escalations and COVID-19 pandemic. Seven out of ten people were already food insecure and in need of food assistance to have a meal on their table. WFP household monitoring during the hostilities and in the aftermath in Gaza in May 2021 shows a significant increase of food and non- food needs for affected people and host families. Almost all families reported applying two main coping strategies: purchasing food on credit and reducing the daily number of meals. Moreover, COVID-19 and the May 2021 escalation

2 - Activities

The project will respond to the humanitarian needs of vulnerable GBV survivors, at risk of GBV, including GBV with disabilities and vulnerable pregnant & lactating women, in the most vulnerable areas in the West Bank and Gaza. The project will contribute to improving access of vulnerable women and girls to essential, safe, and high-quality multi-sectoral services to eliminate GBV and improve food security in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. It is a complementary approach of combining GBV services with food assistance, to strengthen the social protection system by complementing the Government's social benefits. This joint project will maximize the collective efforts to address chronic vulnerabilities and respond to shocks, through protecting vulnerable women from falling into the negative coping mechanisms, improving their socio-economic condition and their well-being. The interventions will target 1,000 of the most vulnerable GBV women and girls who are left behind on the waiting list of the National Social Safety Net database, while the PLW that referred to the mobile clinic to receive services. Special attention will be paid to women with disabilities and elderly women will be provided with health, including nutrition, and protection services. This in addition consultation of needs for these groups will through focus groups with girls and women with disabilities and elderly to better understand their needs and respond properly to these needs, Accordingly, the different needs based on the age factor will also be taken into consideration and the services and kind of items that can be redeemed by the food and protection vouchers will be identified. . Moreover, beneficiaries will be oriented on PESA through the information campaign to ensure that beneficiaries know how to report such cases. During the voucher cards, distribution beneficiaries will be received a GBV service directory and a leaflet that include SAWA toll-free number and email to clarify clearly that humanitarian support is free of charge.The project will respond to the need of GBV multi-sectoral services, nutrition, and food, by implementing a comprehensive package of protection and nutrition in emergencies interventions in the 2 Gaza governorates (Khan Younis, and Rafah) and 3 West Bank governorates (Jenin, Hebron, and Nablus). PROTECTION CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence Activity 1.1. UNFPA will provide multi-sectoral GBV services and SRH services and counseling, including health, psychosocial and legal services for 1000 women (600 women in WB and 400 women in Gaza), including 400 pregnant and lactating women, 40 with disabilities). Services will be offered through the face-to-face and virtual modalities in response to COVID-19 crisis. Activity 1.2 UNFPA partners will refer 400 GBV survivors (300 Gaza and 100 WB women and girls) for advanced and specialized MHPSS services through the authorized and specialized service providers in Gaza and West Bank. Activity 1.3. WFP will provide Cash-Based Transfers in the value of U$ 50 for 4 times a year for purchasing protection items for 1,000 women that will be jointly selected through a pre-set criteria between two agencies and in cooperation with MoSD and mobile clinics (400 in West Bank and 600 in Gaza) including 400 PLW. Activity 1.4 UNFPA will conduct 50 awareness sessions and advocacy campaigns for 900 GBV survivors and community members through social media and protection networks in the Gaza Strip and West Bank about GBV issues, COVID-19 effects on vulnerable people, cybercrimes, and PSEA. Food Security Sector Objective 2: Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs [SO3]. Under this objective, the sector will undertake interventions that aim to provide direct access to food; and support through unrestricted cash and voucher modalities. Activity2.1. WFP will provide Cash-Based Transfers in the value of U$ 60 on a monthly basis for purchasing food items for the same 1,000 women that will be jointly selected through a pre-set criteria between two agencies and in cooperation with MoSD and mobile clinics (400 in West Bank and 600 in Gaza) including 400 PLW. Activity 2.2 WFP will provide 50 nutrition awareness-raising including on Nutrition issues to the same 900 beneficiaries including 400 pregnant and lactating women. Activity 2.3 UNPPA and WFP will train 60 community health workers and midwives (50 female, and 10 male) mainly working at the mobile clinics and safe motherhood centers on pregnant and lactating women's healthy nutrition.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNFPA minimum standards and all partner GBV service providers know how to apply these principles in their work with GBV survivors. 2) UNFPA and WFP staff, as well as partners, have staff been oriented toward PESA and inclusion of Code of Conduct of humanitarian staff and partners. SoP regarding reporting incidents is in place. PESA clauses are embedded in the agreements with all partners; Beneficiaries will be oriented on PESA through the communication materials to be developed to ensure that beneficiaries know how to report such cases. During the voucher cards distribution beneficiaries will be received a GBV service directory and a leaflet that include SAWA toll-free helpline “121” and email address for any complain and a clear message that humanitarian support is free of charge. UNFPA assessed its partners on PSEA, developed an action plan to support its capacity and conducted a number of awareness and training workshops for all its partners and the GBV SC members in Gaza and West Bank.The service providers have been trained to protect the identity and safety of GBV survivors by not disclosing any personal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. All partners are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centred Approach to provide meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors.
