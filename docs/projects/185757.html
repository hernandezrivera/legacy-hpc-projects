Project Summary

NGO Forum in partnership with DCA, in close coordination with the DPHE, site management, CiCs, and UNO's, will respond to the WASH needs of both refugees and host community focusing in Teknaf area. The security context in the target area has recently deteriorated, with increasing tensions between host communities and refugees, exacerbated by major loss of livelihood and income due to the COVID-19 pandemic, as well as ongoing environmental deterioration and pressure on local resources, such as water and agricultural land.
To enhance social cohesion and minimize conflict, NGO Forum/DCA will consult the refugees and host communities in order to priorities activities that will integrate the WASH needs of the most vulnerable affected population. The Teknaf area suffers each year from water scarcity during the dry season and since the Master Water Network implementation has been hampered due to COVID-19 in 2020, the proposed project will include contingency capacity for water trucking and mini water treatment to cover drinking-water needs or minimize drought impact. NGO Forum/DCA will maintain and improve water and sanitation infrastructures emphasizing durable solutions to the solid waste issue, dislodging Latrines as part of regular sanitation service to support BNFs in coping with natural disasters.
The project will continue with strengthening hygiene promotion activities building on the good work done during COVID response, as well as addressing Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) gaps and explore the use of the voucher system in conducive market environment. As an integrated programming approach, NGO Forum/DCA will continue to train and sensitize the WASH team, especially the community volunteers, on SGBV topics enabling them to identify cases and do referrals.

1 - Identified Needs

According to the J-MSNA conducted in 2021, presence of the refugee communities has raised concerns over local environmental degradation, falling wages and rising prices, exerting additional pressures on localities where public services and infrastructure were already lagging behind the national average. Furthermore, the COVID-19 pandemic and resulting Govt. control measures have exacerbated the access to essential services. Access to improved water source remains a challenge in Teknaf with about 26% of households reporting not having enough water to meet their domestic needs. Sanitation infrastructure maintenance has also seen some challenges with 14% of households reporting open defecation compared to last year. Even though 88% of the host communities indicate tube wells and hand pumps as their main source of water, the quality of those sources can very often be questionable due to both operation and maintenance and lack of resources to ensure water quality. Solid waste disposal remains an area requiring improvement, with more reduction and reuse at the household and community level, and more recycling infrastructure needed. Despite the good work done by WASH/Health/CWC agencies during the COVID-19 response, with 98% of households reporting increased hand washing practices, there remains room for improving behavior change communication with COVID specific hygiene messaging, especially considering the near-total lack of COVID-19 conscious behavior now commonly observed among both host and refugee communities. Gender and all-inclusive WASH concerns are not always mainstreamed in WASH programs and MHM issues have seen gaps develop especially during the COVID-19 response period.
