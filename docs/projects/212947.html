Project Summary

According to HNO 2023 , Overall, 13.56 million people need of WASH assistance, of whom 51 percent are women and girls, 44 percent are children and 17 percent have a disability. Almost 1.5 million more people are in acute need to WASH services. Moreover, according to the 2023 MSNA and experts’ consultation in HCT response area "only 54 percent of the population in Syria has access to an improved water source. Furthermore, only half of the households in northern Syria reported having sufficient water during the 30 days prior to the assessment'
In fact, several factors have adversely impacted access to WASH services in Syria, including the February 2023 earthquake, conflict hostilities, economic decline, power shortages, and the impact of climate change. As a result of the conflict and earthquake, damage to WASH systems has restricted the population’s access to water and sanitation services in affected communities. Additionally, economic decline has impeded service providers' technical and financial capacities to operate and maintain water and sanitation systems adequately. This economic downturn has also affected households, limiting their ability to adopt coping mechanisms to maintain basic access to WASH services.
Under the 2024-2025 HRP strategic objectives, (1)Saves lives: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable crisis-affected women, girls, boys, and men through life-saving multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance, in particular in areas with high severity of needs and (3) Increase resilience and access to services: Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities in areas with high severity of needs. The project aims to provide a coordinated, inclusive, adapted response to the complex crisis affecting the targeted IDPs in three villages in Idleb, Kafr Jalis ( Farah, Shamuna,& Saffa) which holds 7500 IDPs of the most vulnerable people, mainly, widows, orphans, elderly people, injured people and low-income families and children It will focus on delivering immediate life-saving assistance to the most vulnerable population affected by displacements while working on increasing the resilience of the communities by supporting and restoring the WASH systems. The project will emphasize achieving the following sectoral objectives which are:
• Save lives and reduce Protection risks among displaced populations by providing essential WASH supplies and services and enhancing hygiene practices;
• Save lives, reduce WASH-related diseases and malnutrition by providing essential WASH supplies and services, and enhance hygiene practices among the most vulnerable populations;
• Save lives by providing essential WASH supplies and services, and enhancing hygiene practices among the most vulnerable populations affected by the crisis
• Provide humanitarian WASH responses in a coordinated, inclusive, adapted, and dignified manner to the most vulnerable populations in the three villages.
Under those objectives, the project will implement the following activities:
Activity 1.1 Improve access to safe water through humanitarian emergency response for IDPs by installing and constructing a water station that works with hybrid energy (solar and fuel).
Activity 1.2 Improve access to sanitation through humanitarian emergency for the community by constructing sewage systems in the three villages.
Activity 1.3 Improve public health and environmental protection through emergency solid-waste management response for IDPs.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project design is based on HNO data to cover the need to consider vulnerabilities in North of Syria which is a priority area for the displacements and conflict-related response as well as vector-borne activities (leishmaniosis). On the other hand, the project will provide services to the 1250 HHS of IDPs live in dignified shelter in the three villages in Kafr Jalis , Idleb , Nw of Syria.The project will enhance preparedness to keep an emergency response capacity to deal with crises and will continue stressing improvements in risky hygiene behaviors and in accessing hygiene and menstrual items. Actions will be taken to support a sustainable management of service provision by reinforcing WASH entities. The project will also adapt the sustainability of the project by establishing a solar System station to provide clean water which secures sustainable resources of clean water for IDPs with the focus on the right of access of all IDPs to WASH services despite of their political or ethical opinionThe project will ensure gender, environmental, age, and disability inclusion and protection responsive programming are considered in all the intervention phases and make sure of including the most vulnerable in the project planning and monitoring and mitigate the GBV cases by providing and securing access to clean water, especially for pregnant women and babies and make sure that the sewage system will cover the needs of IDPs and provide more safe spaces for women and girls. The project activities will help to mitigate GBV risks and will also promote the presence of women in WASH entities.The project will focus on activating continuous monitoring of activities and sharing them with partners by providing monthly reports on the level of outcomes. The WASH Sector will monitor response activities through regular monthly reports on WASH Activities using a unified tool, Info Activity, on the (5Ws) platform to ensure the delivery of activities in the best possible manner and prevent duplication of services. It will also focus on continuous field visits and engaging community groups in monitoring processes.Finally , The project will ensure that the ’do no harm’ principle is systematically applied. Potential protection risks related to the implementation of WASH-related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring those risks, have been identified and will be updated during the year. Nima Relief will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups, including women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female-headed households, to greater protection risks, particularly GBV and MHM. Nima Relief will also prioritize the awareness of menstrual hygiene practices during the project period by providing raising awareness sessions in the three camps for the IDPs.
