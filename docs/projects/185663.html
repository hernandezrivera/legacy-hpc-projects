Project Summary

In 2022 DRC will have been streamlining both its global strategy and joint response plan to increase coordination with food security sector and presence in the field. With this aim DRC will strengthen the resilience, self-reliance and wellbeing of Rohingya refugees and host community towards the effects of climatic change, environmental degradation, and natural disasters through overall protection services which specifically includes livelihood and skill development interventions under economic recovery. Under skill development initiatives, DRC will focus on improving the skills of Rohingya and influx affected host communities to adapt climate change and impacts through waste management, tree plantation, soil stabilization, construction and reconstruction of community livelihood assets through mobilizing cash for workers. Improving shelter structures through lattice production and supporting income generation of vulnerable Rohingya women through providing skills training and in kind and cash support to start and continue their small-scale business will be another major intervention under livelihood support. Waste management and use of natural resources in targeted camps and host communities will be carried out through implementation of regenerative circular economy solutions using the 4R concept (Redesign, Reuse, Reduce and Recycle). Through this intervention, solutions will be provided to water scarcity to promote dry-season homestead gardening where some of these will be cash based. DRC will focus on partnership development with local NGOs and onboarding them as implementing partner to align with nationalization policy. Last but not the least, establish strong linkage and coordination with government and other actors through increasing presence in camps and host and capacity development of local volunteers and community-based organizations will also be prioritized. By this piece of the project, all segments of the vulnerable Rohingya and host households - most vulnerable men and women, people with disabilities of all aged groups, households having with elder people, under two children, pregnant and lactating mothers -under targeted areas will be benefited.
DRC has a zero-tolerance policy to sexual abuse and exploitation and an active member of the PSEA Network and has a PSEA policy and an internal mechanism to report SEA concerns. Through DRC protection programming PSEA trainings to all of its staff and volunteers will be arranged where LLH sector will be integrated. DRC LLH sector will also participate in its protection mainstreaming training jointly with protection programme. DRC is also committed to ensuring an AGD approach and protection principle (best interest of children, confidentiality, do no harm) across its programmatic cycle. For this purpose, data collection tools and sampling approaches are being revised to ensure data collection can be disaggregated by AGD profiles. DRC is committed to the principles of AAP and mechanism has been put in place to ensure these principles are respected during implementation and while interacting with partners. In its efforts to ensure AAP, DRC will continue to strengthen its engagement with community groups to increase outreach and refer GBV cases to DRC’s protection teams.

1 - Identified Needs

In 2020 during COVID-19 pandemic DRC started working in food security sector focusing on livelihood programming. There are several challenges in context are continuously which included the sheer size of the affected population, extreme congestion in camps, the lack of infrastructure, the risk of large-scale epidemics and a possible second disaster in the form of floods or cyclones during the annual monsoon season. Based on available data from the camps through DRC’s protection monitoring, the protection and livelihood situation has deteriorated since April 2020, negatively impacting refugees’ sense of safety, security and stability. Added to movement restrictions, this has affected the humanitarian community’s efforts to deliver a robust multi-sectoral response during COVID-19. This pandemic situation also negatively affected the income source and livelihood of both households in camps and host community. This triggered the vulnerability of the overall safety of Rohingya and host people, especially of women and children. In 2021, the overarching priorities of DRC in protection and livelihood sectors will include upholding needs of the Rohingya and vulnerable host community as well as improving the quality of and access to its services, with specific efforts on mitigating the impact of the refugee influx on the local environment. Ensuring secured livelihoods of the selected women refugee households through building technical skills followed by employment generation and livelihood promotion will be another intervention. Improved shelter structures, land stabilization and environment protection, waste management with livelihoods income generation will be the strategies of DRC to aim to reduce the suffering of the vulnerable and help access better livelihoods. DRC’s intervention will support food security with knowledge and skills for self-reliance services to target longer-term sustainability of the project.
