Project Summary

CPPLI is proposing an 11-month project that focused to contribute in positively impacting crisis affected children and adolescents’ well-being, enhancing their resilience and reducing their stress through quality child protection services in line with the inter-agency CPMS. The project theory of change (ToC) aims to strengthen prevention and response to child protection risks through community-led approach. The project fundamentally will achieve 3 main outcomes and will be implemented in 3 locations: Michika and Madagali in Adamawa and Askira Uba in Borno. The first outcome focus to provide comprehensive case management services through an individualized direct support, linkages and referrals of children and their families affected by crisis who face child protection concerns. CPPLI will identify UASC and other children at risks including CAAFAGs and children with disabilities and register them for case management services. In coordination with the MWASD, CPPLI will place children deprived of parental care to alternative care arrangements and supervise the arrangements to ensure is in the best interest of the children and in line with the do-no harm principles. In collaboration with ICRC, MWASD and the community-based structures, CPPLI will facilitate FTR services to UASC and will ensure timely return of the children back to their primary caregivers in line with their best interest. In total, CPPLI is targeting to reach 400 children with specialized case management services. The second outcome is to support children, adolescents and caregivers affected by crisis to access quality mental health and psychosocial support services and information on childcare and protection through age and gendered appropriate approaches: CPPLI will coordinate with the existing community-based mechanisms to establish CFS centers within the 3 project locations to provide structured age-specific, creative and recreational activities including life skill education interventions for children and adolescents. In total, CPPLI is targeting 3000 children under CFS activities where 240 are adolescents benefitted from life skill education. In addition, positive parenting sessions for caregivers of vulnerable children and foster parents will be established targeting 180 caregivers. CPPLI will further provide information to 5000 girls, boys, men and women on child protection and childcare through awareness raising activities focusing on prevailing child protection issues within project locations. The third outcome aim at strengthening and supporting community-based child protection and referral mechanisms to support prevention and response interventions on issues of child protection concerns including capacity building for social welfare workforce from the ministry of women affairs and social development and the child protection staff. CPPLI will identify existing community-based committees within the 3 project locations who support prevention and response intervention on CP issues. The committees will be strengthened and supported to effectively address CP concerns. CPPLI will conduct functionality survey for the existing committees to determine their capacities that promote children’s right, safety, development, well-being and participation. CPPLI will build a capacity building plan for the committees in line with the capacity gaps identified from the functionality survey to support and strengthen positive strategies and social norms and constructively address negative social norms thereby enhance community-designed and led processes that support ownership and positive outcomes for children. CPPLI will coordinate with the CPSS to conduct service mapping of CP actors within the project locations and develop/update LGA level referral pathway on quarterly basis.

1 - Needs

In the 11th year of the crisis and human rights violations by the Boko Haram insurgents in the Northeast Nigeria, the crisis has shown no any sign of abating, subsiding at one point, resurge and escalate at another. More than 20,000 people have been killed and over 2 million people displaced from their communities relying solely on humanitarian assistance and adding to the existing stressors of the host communities. Between 2020 and 2021, over 946 people have been killed in the BAY states due to the cruel attacks by the insurgents. Specifically, children and adolescents have been victims of abduction, killing, sexual violence, recruitment into armed groups and exposure to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence. Adamawa and Borno have been specifically impacted by the crisis exposing children to human rights violations, child labor, sexual violence, abduction, targeted killings, separation from families, and other child protection risks. The COVID-19 pandemic had also contributed in exacerbating the existing risks faced by children in the states. This had severely aggravated on the ability of the affected population particularly children and adolescent to enhance their resilience, build positive coping mechanisms and have access to services in line with their specific and diverse needs. From the humanitarian needs overview of the child protection sub sector for the 2021, 484,356 and 932,107 people are respectively in need of child protection assistance in Adamawa and Borno with a total of 328,828 people targeted in Adamawa and 431,681 targeted in Borno. Of the total number of people targeted for the child protection services, children constituted 70% of the total population. In Adamawa, Michika and Madagali are among the most critical LGAs targeted by the CPSS in the HNO. A total of 88,498 people are targeted for the CPSS service while 41,299 people are targeted in Madagali. Askira Uba is also among the critical LGA in Borno and have a priority of 32,453 people targeted for child protection services. In addition, the sector have indicated that 16,733 children and adolescents are in needs of comprehensive case management services and 134,969 children in need of mental health and psychosocial support services in the year 2021. At the moment, there are limited child protection partners on ground addressing needs of children and their caregivers in this LGAs of Michika and Madagali in Adamawa and Askira Uba Borno which indicates the needs to scale up child protection response in the LGAs.

2 - Activities

The 2021 CPSS HNO highlighted that an estimated 1.7 million children and adolescents in northeast Nigeria are in need of child protection and specialized support services and at least 434,000 boys and 492,000 girls have been affected by protection risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence. In line with the CPSS response monitoring dashboard from January to March 2021, there are total of 1,000,268 people targeted for child protection and so far only 137,763 persons making a total of 14% of the targeted people have been reached comprising of 134,025 (31%) from Borno and 1,641 (0.5%) from Adamawa. From the data reported by the CPSS, an estimated 862,505 persons are still in need of urgent child protection services. Children have been exposed to protection risks exploitation, neglect, abuse, violence, separation from families, distress, trauma and association with armed groups or armed forces, parents and caregiver’s ability to care for and protect the children has been negatively impacted due to the consequences of the conflicts. So far, from the 16,733 children and adolescents targeted for comprehensive case management services, only 6% have been reached and out of the 134,969 children in need of mental health and psychosocial support services, only 14% have accessed services thereby exposing the children to further risk of developing negative coping mechanism due to lack of services to address there diverse and specific needs. This has indicated severity of child protection needs on ground and the need to rapidly scale up child protection responses in the targeted communities: To address the gaps identified, CPPLI is proposing to provide comprehensive case management services through an individualized direct support, linkages and referrals of children and their families affected by crisis who face child protection concerns. CPPLI will identify UASC and other children at risks including CAAFAGs and children with disabilities and register them for case management services in the targeted locations of Michika and Madagali in Adamawa and Askira Uba in Borno. In coordination with the MWASD, CPPLI will facilitate alternative care arrangements for children deprived of parental care or whose care arrangement is accessed as inadequate in line with the best interest of the children. n collaboration with ICRC, MWASD and the community-based structures, CPPLI will facilitate FTR services to UASC and will ensure timely return of the children back to their primary caregivers in line with their best interest. CPPLI will also support children, adolescents and caregivers affected by crisis to access quality mental health and psychosocial support services and information on childcare and protection through age and gendered appropriate approaches: CPPLI will coordinate with the existing community-based mechanisms to establish CFS centers within the 3 project locations to provide structured age-specific, creative and recreational activities including life skill education interventions for children and adolescents. In addition, positive parenting sessions for caregivers of vulnerable children and foster parents will be established, CPPLI will further provide information to caregivers and children on child protection and childcare in line with the prevailing child protection concerns within the project locations. CPPLI will strengthening and support existing community systems and referral mechanisms including capacity building for social workers from the ministry of women affairs and social development and other child protection staff to support prevention and response interventions on issues of child protection concerns. CPPLI will identify existing community-based committees within the 3 project locations who support prevention and response intervention on CP issues. The committees will be strengthened and supported to effectively address CP concerns.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CPPLI have policies on safeguarding and prevention of sexual exploitation and Abuse which are in line with the 6 core principles of PSEA for the Interagency Standing Committee. Project staff, caseworkers and community structures will be trained on safeguarding and PSEA and the community structures together with the beneficiaries will have access to the contact details of the safeguarding focal point for the organization to report any violation of the safeguarding code of conduct and SEA issues during the course of the implementation. All staff and caseworkers under the project will be inducted on the safeguarding and PSEA policies of CPPLI and sign the policies to ensure compliance. This is to support and ensure that children and beneficiaries are not expose to harm due to their interaction with us, our associates or our programmes. CPPLI will further train staff, volunteers and other community structures on gender inclusion and influencing, gender-specific violence and violence against women and girls. Caseworkers will be oriented on sexual and gender-based violence against children and how this cross cut in child protection programming to effectively respond to sexual and gender-based violence incidents particularly in caring for child and adolescent survivors of sexual violence. Children and adolescents’ girls and boys will also be informed on protection from sexual gender-based violence and in collaboration with the GBV actors on ground they will have access to survivor-centered services appropriate to their age, gender and disability. CPPLI will also conduct quarterly safety audits for the project locations to support in identifying and mitigating risk areas within the project locations. Monthly safeguarding risk assessments with children, caregivers, adolescents and other beneficiaries will be conducted to support in identifying risks before, during and after activities and mitigate the probability of the risks occurrence accordingly. CPPLI will ensure that programme activities are not imposed against the will of the community members and activities will be conducted across the clusters of the project locations to ensure that no part of the community is discriminated or marginalized in receiving assistance under the project and any deliberate act to discriminate or marginalize any part of the community will be challenged accordingly.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

This project is developed on the basis of needs identified and best available evidence including the draft CPSS guidance of the 2022 HRP building upon the huge gaps identified in achieving the 2021 HRP for the sub sector as indicated in the 2021 monitoring dashboard. The project has activities that were surrounded across the 3 main strategic objectives of the sub sector which will be providing comprehensive case management services to unaccompanied and separated children and other children at risk of abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence including children who are formerly associated with armed forces and/or armed groups as well as children with disabilities. Further, vulnerable children and caregivers will also have access to quality mental mental health and psychosocial support services including information on child protection and care. Existing Community-Based Child Protection Committees will also be strengthened to be functional and active to provide adequate preventive and responsive actions to most vulnerable children in their communities. The project had also built upon the key lessons and gaps of the 2020 standard allocation for the Nigerian Humanitarian Fund and geared towards enhancing wellbeing and building positive coping mechanism of children and adolescents affected by child protection concerns within the selected locations in Adamawa and Borno.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In line with Translators without Borders (TWB) Accountability to Affected population framework, CPPLI understand that AAP offers children and other beneficiary population receiving humanitarian assistance a rights-based framework that promote safety and participation in project activities. CPPLI will ensure that children and other beneficiaries have participate in all decisions that affect their lives, receive the information they need to make informed decisions and to complain if they feel the help they receive is not adequate or has unwelcomed consequences. CPPLI will ensure a bottom-up approach in the programming where community led initiatives are strengthened and communities are allow to shape the project activities. CPPLI will ensure that best interest principles for children guides the implementation, monitoring, and adjustments of programme activities under the project. Children and other people receiving assistance will ensured not to be discriminated on the basis of gender, sexual orientation, age, disabilities or any other reason for participating in the project activities. Leveraging on the “Feedback Context Analysis” for Adamawa and Borno states conducted by Plan International Nigeria under the FCDO-ProSPINE+ project, CPPLI will establish context-based feedback mechanism that promotes gender and age-appropriate, inclusive, safe and confidential ways for children and young people to receive information, provide feedback and meaningfully participate in influencing the project programming. CPPLI will conduct monthly feedback sessions via focus group discussion with boys, girls, men and women including persons with disabilities to receive and respond to feedback/complain on the project implementation. Quarterly feedback sessions will also be conducted where CPPLI staff visits to project communities and interact with the community structures, children, caregivers and other beneficiaries will be done to inform the needs for programme adjustments.
