Project Summary

Medair will be implementing a multisectoral health and nutrition programming to meet the acute and recovery needs in Sudan in the states of Khartoum and Blue Nile States. Objectives of this multisectoral approach are:
1) Bolstering the provision and access to essential services for the communities in Blue Nile State which includes the host community and the potential influx of returnees into the state from South Sudan;
2) Supporting the Khartoum State MOH’s efforts in its response to COVID-19 which the state currently has the most cases and deaths.

1 - Needs

Medair's assessments of PHCCs in Damazine and Al Roseires localities found gaps in IMCI, IPC, outbreak prone disease surveillance and reporting and community health education. This is critical since PHCCs have reported outbreaks of communicable diseases such as cholera and malaria. Although PHCCs reported no COVID-19 cases, the lack of IPC measures leaves health facilities and staff vulnerable to transmission between and among staff and patients. PPEs were absent as well as standard measures on screening and isolation. The lack of water inherent in most facilities continue to negatively affect the implementation of IPC. Handwashing stations and latrines in many of the facilities seen were non-functional. Stockouts of essential medications, particularly for children under 5, often occur in the middle of each month. Although ANC services are provided, deliveries and PNC services are conducted at home by the facilities’ midwives. Despite the MOH mandate on the provision of nutrition services at primary health facilities, significant gaps were found in all facilities assessed. Many provided no nutrition services. In health facilities that provided limited services, children are turned away due to lack of therapeutic food supplies. This is particularly in Al Gari which records 15 SAM admissions per month and 100 MAM cases per month, especially during the rainy season. In facilities that have no nutrition services, patients are referred to another PHCC located 7 kms away. Additionally, nutrition practices at the household is also a contributor to malnutrition. Although breastfeeding is practiced, there are mothers who hold wrong perceptions about their ability to breastfeed leading to the early introduction of water and other foods. Khartoum State has been prioritized for Medair’s COVID-19 response where 75% of all confirmed cases are found. After 2 months of a lull in cases, a 5.5 fold increase in cases was seen during the month of November indicating a surge in incidence. Additionally, out of the 660 health staff tested positive, over 87% were based in Khartoum.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Integrated Health & Nutrition ProgrammingIn two Blue Nile locations, Medair will support two MOH supported primary health facilities through indirect support via staff capacity building on topics including IMCI, disease reporting and surveillance, supply chain management, ANC, PNC and COVID-19. In addition to staff trainings, Medair will also support MOH staff through coaching and mentoring to ensure they are using their newly enhanced skills. Medair will also rehabilitate facilities where needed. PPEs will also be provided. Medair will facilitate the movement of essential drugs using the WHO pipeline. If needed, Medair will procure drugs internationally. In the same two primary health facilities, Medair will also support the integration and provision of nutrition services through OTP and TSFP. If emergency rates of malnutrition are found, Medair will expand programming to CMAM. Medair will support direct training and/or recruitment of appropriate health facility staff to provide nutrition services at the supported primary healthcare facilities. Treatment will be provided for children under 5 and PLWs with severe and moderate acute malnutrition. Medair will support multisectoral BCC on the practice of critical health, nutrition and hygiene behaviours including exclusive breastfeeding to pregnant women and mothers of children under 2 years of age in the clinics' catchment areas. Specific messaging to caregivers of children with acute malnutrition and PLWs with acute malnutrition will be carried out within the nutrition programmes. The use of MUAC will be implemented with the assumption that COVID-19 levels fall enough to enable community MUAC screenings and / or supplies are available to carry out individual screenings at the facility. COVID-19 ResponseMedair also has COVID-19 response activities in Khartoum state in the IDP settlement area of Jebel Awliya. Medair will expand into the Mandela area supporting an additional primary healthcare facility where staff will be trained on COVID-19 protocols including IPC and PPE usage. Health staff will later be mentored by Medair staff to resolve issues. Refresher trainings will be provided periodically. Medair will support health facilities with PPEs as well. Community health activities will also expand with a network of 16 volunteers who will conduct house-to-house messaging on COVID-19 prevention. The community health activities will be conducted using COVID-19 risk reduction guidelines to protect both volunteers and beneficiaries.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

There is some consideration of the environment as it relates to biomedical waste of primary health facilities. Some health facilities have adopted negative practices in disposing waste in the open with potential harm to themselves and the community-at-large. The project is supporting safer methods of disposal through the establishment of proper waste pit disposal. Staff training on proper waste disposal will also be carried out.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Medair will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. Medair will engage and coordinate with partners that are well-versed in protection mainstreaming. Although the project's aim is to reach all groups, Medair recognizes that some groups are more vulnerable to protection risks than others. Therefore, Medair will ensure that the needs of vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities and special needs, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly, are prioritized and addressed. Their feedback will be shared with other partners to ensure protection issues continue to be prioritized. Furthermore, when needed, Medair will refer beneficiaries who are in need of protection services to partners with the capacity to serve them.
