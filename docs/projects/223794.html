Project Summary

This project aims to address the critical WASH needs of vulnerable populations in prioritized LGAs across the BAY states, including IDPs, host communities, and returnees. The target locations include high-need LGAs such as Maiduguri, Bama, and Ngala in Borno; Michika, Madagali, and Gombi in Adamawa; and Damaturu, Fika, and Geidam in Yobe. The project will directly benefit 15,000 individuals, including 6,000 IDPs, 6,000 host community members, and 3,000 returnees.
To enhance access to safe water, the project will rehabilitate 25 water points (11 in Borno, 9 in Adamawa, and 5 in Yobe) and construct 8 new water points (4 in Borno and 2 each in Adamawa and Yobe). Sanitation infrastructure will be improved through the construction of 30 gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, with equal distribution across the three states. Hygiene promotion efforts will include 30 awareness sessions to educate communities on best practices, reaching 15,000 individuals, alongside the distribution of 900 hygiene kits, prioritizing women, children, and persons with disabilities. Additionally, the project will establish 15 waste management systems in cholera-prone LGAs to ensure the safe disposal of waste and reduce the risk of disease outbreaks.
By addressing these critical WASH needs, the project will improve access to clean water, enhance sanitation facilities, and promote hygiene practices, ultimately reducing the prevalence of waterborne diseases and fostering healthier and more resilient communities.

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria is facing a WASH crisis of immense scale, requiring urgent and sustained intervention to address the needs of 5.2 million vulnerable individuals in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. Conflict-driven displacement, recurrent natural disasters, and deteriorating infrastructure have left displaced populations, returnees, and host communities struggling with inadequate access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services. The 2024 flooding alone affected over 650,000 people, far surpassing earlier projections of 450,000, and significantly exacerbated existing vulnerabilities. Borno, hosting 85% of displaced populations, faces immense pressure on its already limited resources, while Adamawa and Yobe contend with significant WASH gaps amidst sporadic attacks and economic challenges. Access to safe water is critically low, with 22% of households in Adamawa and 21% in Borno relying on unimproved sources. Sanitation is equally dire, with 36% of households in Borno, 30% in Adamawa, and 43% in Yobe using unimproved facilities, exposing communities to heightened risks of waterborne diseases such as cholera. Cholera remains a persistent threat, with 2024 outbreaks resulting in a 6.1% case fatality rate in Yobe, 2.2% in Adamawa, and 0.7% in Borno. Over 1,875 cases were reported across the BAY states by October 2025. IDP camps are particularly affected, with latrine-to-person ratios exceeding Sphere standards in camps like Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, and Jere, where some latrines serve over 150 people. Desludging services and new sanitation facilities are urgently needed to mitigate health risks. Further compounding the crisis, 48% of water facilities and 73% of sanitation facilities in camps classified as extreme (level 4) or severe (level 3) are either partially or non-functional. These breakdowns, coupled with persistent climate shocks, security risks, and economic constraints, have left communities without the resilience to withstand these challenges. Your funding support will be instrumental in addressing these urgent needs. Together, we can provide life-saving interventions, rehabilitate water and sanitation facilities, and implement sustainable solutions to restore dignity, health, and hope to millions of people in Northeast Nigeria.

2 - Activities

The project will address critical WASH needs by implementing a range of targeted activities in the prioritized LGAs of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states. To enhance access to safe water, the project will rehabilitate 25 existing water points, including 11 in Borno, 9 in Adamawa, and 5 in Yobe, while also constructing 8 new water points, with 4 in Borno and two each in Adamawa and Yobe. Sanitation infrastructure will be significantly improved through the construction of 30 gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, strategically distributed across the three states, with 10 in Borno, 10 in Adamawa, and 10 in Yobe. To promote better hygiene practices and reduce the prevalence of waterborne diseases, the project will conduct 200 hygiene awareness sessions, targeting 15,000 individuals, and distribute 900 hygiene kits, prioritizing women, children, and persons with disabilities. Additionally, to mitigate the risks of cholera and other diseases, 15 waste management systems will be established in cholera-prone LGAs, ensuring safe and effective disposal of waste.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project adopts a robust protection framework integrating measures to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Through a comprehensive Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) communication strategy, the initiative prioritizes staff training and community engagement. It ensures rigorous awareness campaigns, clearly outlining reporting mechanisms and a zero-tolerance policy for exploitation and abuse. Establishing accessible and confidential reporting channels and swift, sensitive responses to reported cases form integral components. This approach aims to create a secure environment, emphasizing dignity and respect for all individuals involved, particularly vulnerable groups, by enforcing strict codes of conduct and empowering communities to actively participate in safeguarding efforts.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project is committed to inclusivity, ensuring feedback collection from the most vulnerable community members through tailored approaches. By directly engaging with these groups in dedicated sessions and leveraging trusted intermediaries and community leaders, the project will facilitate open dialogue and gather insights in safe, accessible spaces. Adaptive feedback mechanisms, including simplified forms and alternative communication channels, will cater to diverse needs. Empowerment initiatives will equip vulnerable individuals with the resources and knowledge to participate actively in feedback processes. Continuous engagement and follow-ups will foster a transparent and accountable feedback loop, ensuring that the expressed concerns, needs, and suggestions of the most vulnerable community members guide and shape the project's decisions and actions.
