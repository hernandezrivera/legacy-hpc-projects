Project Summary

The project targets 8,000 critically vulnerable households IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities, which is 2,000 for Mayendit and 2,000 Leer Counties of Unity State and 4,000 Canal/Pigi of Jonglei State. MHA will cover Food-insecure households, Women, men, elderly and children in affected areas have access to safe and nutritious food, healthy livestock, increased markets, People at risk of malnutrition in affected areas able to meet their basic nutrition requirements for multi-functional roles and development. According to MHA data/tracking of the IDPs and the refugee's returnees, big numbers/influx of the returnees are continuously coming back to their original homes in which 19,000 has been recorded in Leer and 15,000 in Mayendit Counties putting too much pressure on small services in the area. According to REACH January 2019 in Jonglei State, the proportion of assessed settlements reporting the presence of returnees in their settlement increased from 42% in December 2018 to 61% in March 2019.
The purpose of the project is to empower local farmers with transitional and recovery activities by increasing local agriculture production, markets, nutrition and livestock interventions through better farming practices utilizing community based farming experiential learning techniques and instilling the culture of saving in Leer, Mayendit Counties of Unity State and Canary/Pigi of Jonglei State by end of 2020. Providing livelihoods and strengthening local markets is paramount while supporting individuals and communities in the return and recovery stage because it will enable communities to become more resilient to shocks and regain destroyed things. MHA community-based programming interventions will promote the revitalization of livelihoods and markets by grouping local farmers into groups using farmer Field School (FFS), local farmers access to microfinance by instilling the culture of saving using Group Saving and Loans Associations (GSLAs). GSLA/FFS groups will be trained on financial literacy, enterprise selection, planning, and management, community skilling to mostly youth and women, apprenticeship skills for youth, value chain development, farming as a business and market research so that beneficiaries fully maximize profits from their small scale individual and group businesses. Prioritization will be based on findings from market research while considering environmental sustainability and risks such as currency inflation and accessibility. Backyard/kitchen gardening with practical nutritional cooking demonstrations using locally available food. Junior Farmer Field School (JFFS) will be introduced in schools for setting up demonstration sites for learning and supplementing food at school. And it will work as a Psycho-social (PSS) activity in which protection will be mainstreamed.
Livestock treatment and vaccinations will be done in cases of disease outbreaks and for preventative measures for high-risk diseases. Vaccinations will target 1,000 Poultry, 40,000 Cattle and 200 dogs. They will be treated against worm infestation, ectoparasite, pneumonia in calves, etc. 10 Community animal health workers in each County will be trained on livestock disease management and vaccination and FFS community groups will be trained on sustainable livestock disease controls and management practices utilizing locally available materials.
Restricted Grants to GSLA/FFS groups. This will boost critically vulnerable groups with small starting capital at the group level and because of too much poverty that grant will work as ‘’switch’’ to economic empowerment and food security. GSLA will promote saving and increase member’s access to finance within their groups in which they will easily access loans thus members adding more capital in their small scale businesses, starting up new IGAs and increased production leading to improved living standards of communities. MHA will recruit, interview, train and mentor Community-Based Facilitators (CBF)

1 - Needs

In August 2019, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity persist in the country. During this period, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food. In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Since January 2019, Canal/Pigi County has been classified by IPC to be experiencing level 4 ‘acute’ food insecurity. In total, 92,000 people were estimated to need food assistance with 31,000 people experiencing IPC 3 ‘crisis’, 46,000 in IPC 4 ‘emergency’, and 10,000 in IPC 5 ‘catastrophe’ conditions. According to MHA assessment in Canal/Pigi, there are high levels of malnutrition and this indicates that the population is experiencing ‘serious’ conditions according to IPC acute malnutrition classification nearly one-third of PLW were also acutely malnourished (31.7%) and high levels of perceived hunger. The food security indicators suggest that 5000HH in Canal/Pigi County may be facing famine and the situation could get worse into the lean season as families deplete the harvest that can last only 2.9 months. This is also attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets, and poor sanitation and hygiene. Canal/Pigi County has been classified as “critical” based on SMART Surveys and FSNMS respectively. Since January, big numbers of returnees throughout Kurwai payam, and IDPs who have come to Kurwai, reportedly did not have access to the last food distribution in January 2019, again populations in Koliet (the main town in Alam payam) and Alam payam in general, have no access to humanitarian services. Over 24,000 big influx of returnees coming back to Leer and Mayendit Counties are putting too much pressure on small services in the area.Unity State currently is in IPC 3 in which Mayendit and Leer Counties are seriously affected, the conflict continues to severely limit food availability and access, and significantly restrict market functioning in Leer and Mayendit Counties of Unity State, prices of staple foods and essential commodities continued and remained high in the middle of the harvest period when prices typically decline due to increasing supply. As conflict restricted cultivation the harvests were below average and typical price increases were observed in May 2019 compared to previous months. The increasing depreciation of the currency has worsened the situation. Providing livelihoods and strengthening local markets is paramount while supporting individuals and communities in the return and recovery stage. MHA community-based programming interventions will promote the revitalization of livelihoods and markets using FFS Methodology, instilling the culture of saving using GSLAs, training on financial literacy, enterprise selection, planning, and management, livestock vaccination and treatment and improved market spaces. Value chain development, farming as a business and market research so that project beneficiaries fully maximize the profits from their businesses. MHA is on ground Canal/Pigi implementing a general protection project funded by SSHF and is active in Leer and Mayendit with field offices implementing the GBV project funded by UNESCO which will be utilized to effectively implement the project.

2 - Activities

Formation and establishment of Farmer Field School (FFS) community-based farmer groups and this will be integrated with Junior Farmer Field School (JFFS). Target 20, 7 in Mayendit, 6 in Leer and 7 in Canal/Pigi FFS groups will be formed, 10 Community Based Facilitators (CBF) will be trained on FFS methodology supporting JFFS as well, demonstration learning sites will be set both in communities and schools to supplement food, reduce malnutrition and for learning purposes in schools and each class will have a separate demo site. The selected school's pupils will learn through JFFS which will work as PPS activity as well thus protection mainstreaming. FFS and JFFS groups will be provided with farming inputs and seeds. Will be training on better agricultural practices like land preparation, vegetable production, water for production & irrigation, value chain, pest & disease control. MHA trained CBF and community volunteers utilizing government field-based agricultural officers will provide continuous support to farmers throughout the project. Distribution of farm inputs and seeds: FFS and JFFS will receive vegetable seeds specifically 40g onion seeds, 40g okra seeds, 40g eggplants seeds, maize crops, and sorghum, 40g watermelon seeds, 40g kale (sukumawiki) seeds, and 40g tomato seeds. These vegetable seeds will be utilized in backyard/kitchen gardens of communities and school demo sites. All FFS groups will receive farming tools each FFS group receive 30 hoes, 1 wheelbarrow, 3 spades, 2 strings, 4 rakes, 2 axes, 3 pangas, and 3wateringcans. Distribution of fishing gear to specific FFS groups and households and training will include but not limited to fishing methods, use of fishing equipment, fish handling, and preservation techniques. It should be noted that communities will be consulted and oriented before choosing the tools and seeds they want based on market opportunities and security in the area. Key sustainable farming practices training, value chain development, market research, FFS learning exchange visits, trade fair expeditions, and graduations will be conducted.Formation and establishment of 20 GSLA groups integrated with restricted small grants given out to well-formed and empowered GSLA groups typically for group business investments. Through trainings on enterprise selection, planning & management, financial literacy and market research groups will be empowered to come up with small scale Income Generating Activities (IGAs) and group members individual IGA in which we MHA plan to support them with 50% grant but restricted to only GSLA/FFS group member’s collective investment. For individual group members, they get loans from their GSLA and paid back. The possibility is that most GSLA groups will be FFS groups and most of the training will be shared. Each GSLA group will be supported with startup kits which will be given out once. 10 FFS CBF will be also trained on GSLA methodology to support saving groups daily as well. Establishment of Backyard gardening/kitchen gardens which will be utilized in both JFFS and FFS. All project beneficiaries will be trained on different types of kitchen gardens, how to set up and manage nursery beds, organically manage vegetables and they will transplant into their home gardens. Some FFS/GSLA groups will be trained on vegetable growing as a business which will help them get GSLA weekly savings mostly women. Behavioral change communication on nutrition and referral for malnourished children will be integrated not limited to practical cooking demonstrations using the locally available food.Livestock vaccination and treatment services will be provided to vulnerable agro pastoral communities targeting 10,000 livestock heads and 40 CAHWS will be retrained. Provision of vouchers and livestock treatment and special attention will be given to project direct beneficiaries. Monitoring and Evaluation, different FFS, JFFS and GSLA tracking tools will be developed and utilized.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, all key project stakeholders will be actively involved starting from planning up to final stage of project evaluation. MHA will use its Community Based Approach like the participatory monitoring and evaluation as a strategy for community involvement. MHA believes that participatory M & E is not just a matter of using participatory techniques within a conventional monitoring and evaluation setting but it is about radically rethinking who initiates and undertakes the process, and who learns or benefits from the project interventions. Project communities and local authorities will be engaged in all stages of the implementation cycle for example in planning meetings, registration of beneficiaries, implementation, M&E and in identifying the criteria for inclusion and exclusion. They all participate during the process of conducting Vulnerability Assessment Tool (VAT) for beneficiaries mapping, verification and validation. Community Based specific committees comprising of communities and local authorities will be selected, empowered and some of them will work as project volunteers as well. MHA will also use local community volunteers as community trainers, post distribution monitoring; baseline, midterm and end of project evaluations. Project local authorities and communities will be also engaged in community feedback mechanisms. Community Based Protection Networks, cultural leaders, line ministry community based technical staff, community leadership, other implementing partners will be actively involved in the project implementation as MHA exit strategy.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, Currently the environment is safe both in Leer and Mayendit Counties of Unity State and Canal/Pigi of Jonglei State. MHA will empower targeted project beneficiaries with increased livelihoods and markets to IDPs, Returnees, host communities and the demobilized youths in the area. This will be of great value for stabilization of communities and peace building. Project interventions like FFS, JFFS, GSLA, livestock vaccination and treatment integrated with financial trainings is crucial to enable households to diversify their asset base, improve feeding patterns and spread risks. Therefore MHA will aim at increasing access to productive resources, improve value chains and diversify income sources to build household and community resilience. This will automatically make the area more peaceful thus social economic improvement of the households. The MHA will build on recent investments to strengthen its organizational security and contingency measures with an eye towards business continuity. Additional efforts will be made to continue to improve the safety of the MHA staff and assets. The MHA monitors security indicators in the area and in case of any unexpected conflict, MHA has a unique plan to evacuate staff in layers based on the threat level. Non-essential staff will be evacuated first, followed by experts and national relocatable staff. Program activities will be scaled down according to the level of threat. In the event of an evacuation, activities will be reduced to only life-saving activities and ongoing communication with local authorities. The programs will rely on the capacity of the local community which has been central to the programs ,to take ownership of program activities in the event of a significant deterioration of the security situation. But MHA is strongly planning to empower local communities and almost all project staff are going to be community based in which of any threat they can still revise means of implementing the project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, MHA will focus on supporting improvement of traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at local level and also work with local authorities and community leaders. MHA will closely work with Ministries operating in the location and community-based cooperatives thereby directly building their capacity to respond to humanitarian and development needs in the future. Community trainings will be focused on increasing knowledge and skills on sustainable farming practices utilizing community based experiential farming from model farmers, instilling the culture of saving and starting up community based saving groups, value chain development, marketing, backyard gardening, practical cooking demonstrations among others which they will continue using after the end of the action. Particular focus is put on women, youth and school going pupils to be the young leaders of change and participate in spreading food production and marketing sustainable practices, proper hygiene and nutrition messages in their houses and communities. During the course of the project, a beneficiary feedback and complaints response mechanism will be implemented to ensure equal participation of all groups. Transparency within the community with all the ongoing activities and clear timeframes will help in preparing for contingency planning when MHA will end the project. MHA will share, as necessary, given information with key partners and stakeholders in the location (e.g. INGOs/NNGOs/CBOs working in the area, community leaders, religious leaders, local authorities, and institutions schools and health centers). Clear handover with local communities, authorities will be done to ensure smooth transition
