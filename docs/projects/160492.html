Project Summary

The child protection intervention will target 31,010 beneficiaries in the two counties of Mayom and Abienhom for a duration of twelfth months period. The project will address the existing child protection gaps with the overall objective to provide immediate live-saving child protection services for of host, IDPs and returnees vulnerable boys girls, women and men who have been severely affected and traumatized by the protracted and recurrent crisis of December 2013 and July 2016 respectively as well as the devastating floods in Mayom and Abienhom counties. The flagship CP activities include provision of comprehensive case management, case referral pathways, Psycho-social support services and enhanced capacity of case workers and community based child protection networks on preparedness, protection risk mitigation, awareness raising and prevention messages to the most vulnerable children with their care givers and other community members in the two counties.

1 - Needs

The 2019 HNO stated that about 5,725,000 million South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys face protection risks and violations due to widespread conflict causing multiple shocks and stresses. These include food insecurity, destitution, disease, natural disaster and the absence of essential services. The reports articulated that civilian population continues to be subject to deliberate attacks, conflict-related sexual violence, abductions, forced recruitment including boys and girls, cruel and unusual treatment, destruction of housing and property, forced displacement and family separation. The HRP 2020 projections show that there are 2.5 million people in need for child protection services throughout the country in 2020. This necessitates a robust child protection intervention through provision of live-saving services that will include comprehensive case management, structured and unstructed PSS services, cash transfer for vulnerable caregivers with their foster children and capacity building of case workers and caregivers to meet the basic needs of these children. In Mayom county, CHIDDO has been implementing UNICEF funded Child Protection activities that ended last August 2019, the end of the PCA left a total active caseload of 390 children (205 boys and 185 girls) with active caseload of 153 children (98 boys 55 girls) and majority of them are vulnerable children at high risks awaiting immediate reunification with their families. This intervention will be a continuation part of CHIDDO-UNICEF last Aug 2019 PCA to address the urgent conditions of these vulnerable boys and girls by reunifying them with their families without delay. The ongoing floods also submerged 3 CFS,1 center school and recreational kits in Wankey, Bieh, Ruadh and Kuerbuone Payams and left a total number 339 boys and girls without playing space. The floods also caused a huge devastation of harvested crops, submergence of cattle grazing pasture, houses, schools and all other sources of livelihood in Mayom county, which will increase women, men, boys’ and girls’ vulnerability and risks to exploitation, abuse and neglect, which requires immediate support in form of cash transfer to these groups. In Abienhom county, there is need for urgent intervention on child protection issues like comprehensive case management in addition to awareness program on GBV, sexual abuse, exploitation, provision of psycho social support services among boys and girls, women and men as recommended by the recent IRNA September 2019 floods assessment reports conducted in Abienhom county by CARE International, RCDI, SP and SPEDP. The second CP burning issue is the high risk of vulnerable boys and girls that were left unattended to in the county by Christian Mission Initiative (CMI) with unknown total caseloads. CHIDDO will continue liaise with CMI in getting the exact caseload to support these high risk children since their PCA with UNICEF last year. Abiemnhom is just a two hours’ drive from Mayom County, CHIDDO will address these child protection gaps due to its well conversant nature with the local context, visible presence and established good relationships with the local community beside the required technical capacity in child protection.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, CHIDDO is obliged to comply with the South Sudan National Social Protection Policy Framework whose overall goal is to ‘respond to and address the multiple vulnerabilities faced by South Sudanese children and citizens, with a particular focus on the poorest and most excluded sectors. All beneficiaries rights will be legally recognized according to this Social Protection Framework to equally access the necessary services under this proposed child protection project regardless of ethnic backgrounds, regions and religious affiliations.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, CHIDDO will ensure that all the conflict affected IDPs, returnees, host community children and other vulnerable beneficiaries access the needed child protection services in proportion to their needs without any barriers or discrimination. CHIDDO sticks to humanitarian principles of Do No Harm, participation and involvement of beneficiaries, neutrality, strong governance and leadership throughout the Programme cycle.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, this child project is linked to the development long term goals and short term objectives of the National Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare as stated in the MoGCSW strategic plan (2013-2018). CHIDDO has been working and communicating its main humanitarian activities with the county departments and state governments since 2016. This project is strongly linked with the objective 1 of Child Protection Directorate in the Ministry that supports the development and implementation of standards for child welfare, child protection and policies for the protection of children and their well-being such as the policy on children without parental care and objective 2 that ensures children’s rights are promoted and protected in South Sudan. CHIDDO' strong presence in the two counties (Magwi and Mayom) established good relations in Abiemnhom and engagement with the local child protection networks/structures in as well as th County Gender, Child Welfare at county and state levels.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this child protect intervention is linked to South Sudan National Social Protection Policy Framework RSR (P158867)) under the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare that has been adopted and whose overall goal is to ‘respond to and address the multiple vulnerabilities faced by South Sudanese citizens, with a particular focus on the poorest and most excluded sectors. It is also connected to the goal of the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare Strategic Plan (2013-2018) stated under the Directorate of Child Welfare that states “All children protected from all forms of violence, exploitation, discrimination, neglect and abuse. This is also pinned to the specific objective 1 of Child Protection Directorate in the Ministry that supports the development and implementation of standards for child welfare, child protection and policies for the protection of children and their well-being such as the policy on children without parental care and Objective 2 that ensure children’s rights are promoted and protected in South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project locations (Mayom and Abiemnhom counties) are environmentally safe and stable. This child protection intervention will be implemented in a secure, friendly and neutral environmental areas that will not pose harm to children and other beneficiaries since most of the activities have no hardware activities that could be a source of danger to children or the surrounding environment.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, CHIDDO will first hold community based participatory planning and consultative meetings (from the project outset to evaluation level) with the key beneficiaries and stakeholders including (CBCPCs/Ns, County Gender and Child Welfare Department Officials, community leaders, etc.…) who will be involved actively to ensure collective participation and decision making are unanimous to define the project exit strategy (goal, objectives, outcomes and activities) in preparation for exit strategy and brainstorm their minds to think and suggest ways that will make Child Protection project sustainable and community ownership after the funding stops. CHIDDO also will conduct capacity building training workshops and mentorship for women and men, girls and boys and other key community structures with the aim of increasing their capacity and help the CBCPCs members to develop a clear project process monitoring and detailed project sustainability plan/exit strategy schedule. The project will consult the views of the stakeholders on CBCPCs program in all the targeted Counties and Payams to ensure smooth exit strategy that will lead sustainability and ownership.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The vulnerable people with disabilities will be provided with the necessary information like any other person at the start of the project to make sure that they report their complaints and satisfaction with the project deliverables. Vulnerable young girls, women and boys will all be prioritized in every activity or services given using vulnerability selection criteria for balanced inclusion purposes.
