Project Summary

The situation in the North West and South West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon escalated in October 2016, when the Anglophone population started making political demands leading to protests and strikes. This unrest emerged from historically perceived marginalisation of Anglophone Cameroonians. A year later, on October 1st, 2017, secessionists declared independence from the Republic of Cameroon. Since then violence has escalated and Internally Displaced People (IDP) targeted are living in the forest or host communities in villages, urban or peri urban areas in the NWSW regions. These IDPs lack basic social services. The most vulnerable population are women, pregnant women, newborn babies, children under five, young children both boys and girls. This security situation in the NWSW has had a direct impact on access to health services for vulnerable women and children. Those functional are not sufficiently equipped. Due to mass displacement with many people seeking shelter in remote forested areas access to even basic health services is almost impossible. Furthermore, more than 40 per cent of Community Health Workers (CHW) are no longer working in insecure health districts.
UNICEF alongside partners will deliver essential health services aimed at increasing access to basic health maternal, newborn and child-care and services where health facility is functional and mainly in communities and hard to reach areas.
OVERALL GOAL/OBJECTIVE
Increase coverages of basic maternal, newborn and child health preventive and curative and promotional package amidst the COVID-19 pandemic
SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES
• Increase vaccine coverage in routine immunization in the 6-59 months (pentavalent 3, measles)
• Increase vaccine coverage for routine immunization in pregnant women
• Provide treatment the three main childhood killers for children under 5 years old (Malaria, diarrhoea, and Respiratory tract infections)
• Provide prevention care to pregnant women and Children: anti anaemics, Vit A supplementation, deworming
• Provide malaria prevention tools: LLINs and Intermittent preventive treatment to families
• Improve quality of maternal and newborn care with the provision of basic kits for mothers and newborns, training and coaching of health care providers
• Build capacities of health care providers, health facilities and communities on COVIC-19 prevention and infection prevention Control;
Direct beneficiaries:
- 3,000 newborns
- 10,000 Pregnant women
- 50,000 children under five of age
- 20,000 families/households corresponding to about 100,000 people

1 - Besoins / Needs

OCHA estimated in February 2021 at 705,800 the number of Internally Displaced Persons, 360,500 the number of returnees and 3 million the number of affected populations in the North West and South West (NWSW) regions (OCHA February 2021) . Since the onset of the crisis in the NWSW, basic maternal, newborn and child health interventions have gradually decreased as from 2018 coinciding with the worsened socio-political situation especially and now with the occurrence of the COVID-19 pandemic. The intensified routine immunization conducted in 2020 and 2021 in collaboration with the Regional Delegations for Health in the NWSW contributed to increase despite the prevailing insecurity and the COVID-19 pandemic. In October 2021, Measles and Penta3 coverages are 57.8% and 63.6% respectively in the NW and to 65.1% and 68.9% respectively in the SW against the national target of 95% (MoH, August 2020). However, the progress on life-saving services delivery in the NWSW was impacted by COVID-19 restrictions on activities. 53,036 children 0–11 months (24,082 in SW and 28,954 in NW) are zero-doses as of October 2021 in both regions. Immunization coverage below 60% exposing under-five children to epidemics of vaccine preventable diseases. The closure of over 19% of 742 (SW 26% and NW 11%) health facilities (HF) in these regions and partially functional of most facilities limit access to HFs for the conflict-populations especially and IDPs (Health Cluster, Aug. 2021). Malaria, diarrhoea, and pneumonia the three main killers’ diseases in children under five are prevailing with high risks of dying due to inaccessibility to HFs. Maternal, newborn and child health and services are compromised with huge gaps. Fewer pregnant women go for ANC visits. In the SW for example only 38% of expected pregnant women (PW) were received in HFs. Deliveries in HFs have decreased putting newborn survival at stake. Maternal and child deaths are also in rising. UNICEF trained 100 health care providers working in 17 referral health facilities in neonatal care and kangaroo mother care and supplied materials and equipment for quality newborn care in 2021. This action must be strengthened and scale-up. Planned Output, activities and expected Results (including # of beneficiaries) Output 1: By December 2022, children under five have and PW access to basic preventive, curative, and promotional health care and services. Activity 1.1: Support outreach vaccination in every month in targeted health districts. At least 40,000 under five are vaccinated with routine vaccines (Penta3 and measles and others). At least 5,000 PW received tetanus vaccine, ferrous folic, intermittent preventive treatment (IPT), and deworming tablets Activity 1.2: Organize out-reach, hit and run in special populations, hard to reach areas - At least 10,000 under five are vaccinated with routine vaccines (Penta3 and measles and others) - At least 2,000 PW receive tetanus vaccine, IPT, and deworming tablets, Activity 1.3: Treatment of children for childhood illnesses (ARI, diarrhea and malaria). At least 15,000 children receive treatment for ARI and diarrhoea. At least 20,000 children receive anti-malaria drugs against malaria Activity 1.4: Distribution resuscitation kits - At least 100 health facilities received at least one resuscitation kit. Training of 100 services providers on newborn care (KMC) in 4 HFs in the NWSW. Activity 1.5: Distribution of delivery kits to PW and newborn kits to care givers. At least 2,000 PW receive delivery kits. At least 1000 mothers receive newborn kits.Output 2: By December 2021, communities have access to information on preventive and promotional health care to adopt healthy behaviors. Activity 2.1: Protect families against Malaria. 20,000 household receive Treated Mosquito Nets (LLINs). Training of 250 Community Health Workers on Integrated Community Case Management in the NWSW. Activity 2.2: Mobilize communities, families and individual on Essential family practice
