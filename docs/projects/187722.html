Project Summary

The intervention is part of a project aiming at securing the basic needs and providing integrated community-based protection activities in Karm Jazmati, Seken Shebebi, Karm al Tarrab, and Dahret Awwad. The project is held by Caritas Germany (CG), back-funded by the German Federal Foreign Ministry (GFO) and implemented from 01.04.2019 -31.12.2022 by Caritas Syria (CS).
The protection activities carried out under this project have been regularly aligned to the protection sector strategy. In the 2019 established community center in Karm al Jazmati, integrated community-based protection services are offered to residents in Jazmati, Seken Shebebi, Karm al Tarrab, and Dahret Awwad.
The center follows an open-door policy, ensuring the active engagement of the community, allowing real-time evaluations and timely adjustments of activities as per beneficiaries’ feedback. In response to the feedback collected from beneficiaries, i.e. via PDMs, satisfaction surveys, and FGDs, and following the strategy of the protection sector, the activities are regularly adjusted to the actual needs, or new activities are included.
In 2022, women are provided with courses aiming to prevent GBV, such as literacy. Boys and girls in school age are provided PSS and interactive learning courses accompanying them throughout the school year. Women, men, and children receive a consecutive and tailored package of protection-related awareness sessions. Boys and girls 14-17 years old will participate in community initiatives. Further, socially isolated elderly are visited in their homes by the trained CS elderly team at least once a month, following up on their psychosocial needs as well as aiming to re(establish) relationships in the community. Women are invited to participate in PSS group sessions. In case mothers of young children are participating, the CS team has established a CFS in the center to take care of the children while the mothers are attending courses. In addition, capacity strengthening activities for the CS team will be offered to further increase the responding capacity of CS.
The target groups are selected interconnectedly, aiming for greater impact within the families and, in turns, the entire community: For example, the husbands or adult sons of women participating in the awareness, PSS or GBV prevention sessions, are invited to FDGs to assess their interest and needs in men’s awareness sessions. The parents of children coming to the center are actively addressed and invited either to participate in one of the protection services or for regular follow-up regarding their children. With a newly established safe referral policy, CS aims to enhance their assistance to cases that need other or more specialized support.
Following a participatory approach, the needs have been regularly assessed, i.e., via PDMs applying a proportional piling method, and FDGs. Furthermore, a robust FCM has contributed to ensure the engagement and thus self-identification of the needs in the community. Hence, throughout the project lifetime, the engagement of all community members has been promoted, establishing a two-way communication with the community. This has ensured a timely incorporation of community feedback.
The regular coordination with the Aleppo-based sub-sectors Protection, CP, GBV, and FSL, CS ensures alignment of its protection activities while avoiding duplication of efforts. The community center in Jazmati has been well received by the residents of Jazmati since its opening in 2019 and continues to be the central point for the provision of integrated community-based protection services, to women, adolescent girls, children, men and elderly.

4 - Needs

The MSNA 2021 KI Survey Analysis shows high CP risks in the project area Jebel Saman: 59% of respondents deemed child marriage as very common, girls aged from 12-17 being the most affected (43%), boys aged 12-17 the second most affected (14%). Child labor has been reported very common by 14% of respondents in Jebel Saman (63% yes but not very common). Most affected are boys aged 15-17 (87% very common), but also younger boys are affected (12-14: 29% very common, below 12: 14% very common). The age group 15-17 is also the most affected when looking at girls: 43% very common, and age group 12-14 29% very common. Generally, risks for children in Jebel Saman have been rated as very common for girls and boys less than 12 (57%). To reduce protection risks for school-aged boys and girls, PSS and interactive learning sessions will be offered in the CC. Boys and girls 14-17 are invited to youth initiatives. According to the MSNA HH Survey Analysis, 6% of women and girls in Jebel Saman have been feeling unsafe, further demonstrating the need for GBV prevention activities such as literacy courses in the CC.Further, 73% of respondents in Jebel Saman have reported that at least one HH member has been showing signs of psychological distress 30 days before the survey. 39% boys<18, 45% girls<18, 55% men, 64% women have self-reported psychological distress. Caritas will provide awareness and PSS sessions to women, men, children, youth, and elderly to prevent or reduce psychological distress

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with the sector strategy for 2022-23,an integrated approach, targeting all family members, is applied. All activities respect C19 prevention measures. They are conducted by trained Caritas case workers and volunteers in the CC (except for elderly activity - outreach) according to 3months cycles (i.e. each week one awareness session for 8 weeks). Elderly and CP activities run the entire year for the same participants. Sessions’ topics are identified via participatory methods such as FDGs. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Target groups of the project such as children, women, and elderly, are at particular risk of being affected by PSEA. The COVID19 pandemic as well as the economic hardship have increased the risks for these groups. During the design phase of the “new phase” of the project, these risks have been identified. Caritas follows a zero-tolerance strategy of sexual abuse and exploitation. Supported by an external consultant, CS has reviewed and updated its Safeguarding policies in the first quarter of 2021. On an organisational level, all staff members in Caritas have been trained on the safeguarding policy as well as the Code of Conduct which is binding for all staff and volunteers working in this project. In each CS regional office – as in Aleppo - there is a Safeguarding Focal Point appointed to ensure that the policies are followed by each staff member and FCM for beneficiaries and staff are understood and followed-up on. Verbally as well as with sensation material visible in the center or during distributions, beneficiaries are informed about their rights and about safe feedback channels. (reference: https://www.caritas.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Caritas-Internationalis-Children-and-Vulnerable-Adults-Safeguarding-Policy.pdf)


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures
