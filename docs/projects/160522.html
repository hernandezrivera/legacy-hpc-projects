Project Summary

Armed conflict since December 2013 in South Sudan, have destroyed livelihoods and water infrastructures, increased sexual and gender-based violence, given rise to alarming rates of food insecurity and malnutrition as well as frequent cholera outbreaks, have left about: 7 million people (about two thirds of the population) in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection; 4.5 million people had to flee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them internally displaced within South Sudan (SOF 2019).
According to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System in 2018 (July/August) within WASH core indicators integrated in the survey, in the entire country: 29% of the population only has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30 minute distance without facing protection concerns; 90% of the population at least practices open defecation, either because they do not have access to or are not accustomed to using a basic sanitation facility; 13% of the population only has access to WASH NFIs, such as Jerry cans, soap and mosquito nets. 75% of the population reported households with members that had been self-diagnosed with a water-borne illness in the previous two-week period; 5.7 million most vulnerable are in urgent need of WASH services (HNO 2019).
Panyijiar and Yirol East counties are located in Unity and Lakes State in South Sudan respectively. Although there is abundant surface and ground water resources in these locations, thousands of people in these localities do not have access to improved water supply and sanitation services largely precipitated by the protracted civil war since December 2013; the available Sanitation, hygiene and water services have been destroyed or malfunctioned to depreciation.
Humane Aid for Community Organization (HACO) has been implementing Peace building, Livelihoods, WASH and GBV Projects in Lakes and Unity States since 2015 and in view of the aforementioned crisis proposes a WASH Project integrating GBV, Nutrition and Health.
HACO intends to provide timely and equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees through sustainable solutions. HACO response aims at mitigating WASH related GBV by providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities and tackling issues surrounding gender, safety in WASH including provision of dignity kits and NFIs for Hygiene, Sanitation and rehabilitation of Water points. Since the targeted areas are facing Severe food shortage with likely consequence of malnutrition (IPC 3, 4 and 5), HACO will integrate WASH in Nutrition to provide caretakers of SAM/MAM children at OTP/SCs with famine prevention kits. Due to the area being disease prone, a WASH in health response is planned for outbreak prevention and response as well as risk reduction. The project will also train nutrition and Health workers on WASH minimum package for SAM and MAM Admissions.
This project proposes to reach 30,000 Community Members: 30% IDPs, 15% Returnees and 55% Vulnerable host Community, 60% Female and 40% Male.

1 - Needs

The cumulative effects of years of armed conflict in South Sudan since December 2013, have destroyed livelihoods and water infrastructures, increased sexual and gender-based violence, given rise to alarming rates of food insecurity and malnutrition as well as frequent cholera outbreaks, have left about: 7 million people (about two thirds of the population) in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection; 4.5 million people had to flee their homes in search of safety, nearly 2 million of them internally displaced within South Sudan (SOF 2019). According to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System in 2018 (July/August) within WASH core indicators integrated in the survey, in the entire country: 29% of the population only has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30 minute distance without facing protection concerns; 90% of the population at least practices open defecation, either because they do not have access to or are not accustomed to using a basic sanitation facility; 13% of the population only has access to WASH NFIs, such as Jerry cans, soap and mosquito nets. 75% of the population reported households with members that had been self-diagnosed with a water-borne illness in the previous two week period; 5.7 million most vulnerable are in urgent need of WASH services (2019 HNO/HRP).The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report published in September 2019 indicates that more than 4.5 million people (39 percent of the population) need food assistance. Acute food insecurity is driven by localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, lack of economic opportunities, eroded livelihoods and low coping capacity. The IPC report finds, acute malnutrition levels among children under five years of age have increased significantly, from 13% in 2018 to 16% in 2019, which is above the emergency threshold of 15%. It is estimated that 1.3 million children will be affected by acute malnutrition in 2020. The high rates of malnutrition are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country. In order to forestall the aforementioned crisis, HACO in concert with other Humanitarian actors intends to provide timely and equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees through sustainable solutions. HACO response aims at mitigating WASH related GBV risks by providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities and tackling issues surrounding gender, safety and WASH including provision of dignity kits and NFIs for Hygiene, Sanitation and rehabilitation of Water points. Due to high GAM rates in the Project areas, HACO will integrate WASH in Nutrition to provide caretakers of SAM/MAM children at OTP/SC with famine prevention kits. Since the locations targeted are disease prone, a WASH in health response is planned for outbreak prevention and response as well as risk reduction. This project will also train nutrition and Health workers on WASH minimum package for SAM and MAM Admissions.

2 - Activities

HACO proposes the following activities to mitigate WASH related Humanitarian Crisis in the targeted locations: 1. Provide timely, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees (Rehabilitate 56 Water Points): WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses2. Mitigate WASH related gender based violence: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation in targeted communities based on Protection assessment conducted by female staff with women, girls and children for WASH activities adequately planned with the community input. (PSEA sessions, FGDS and construction/rehabilitation of 60 Latrines and Hand Washing facilities)3. Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package: WASH integrated interventions of SAM/MAM admissions at OTP/SC targeting caretakers of the malnourished children up-to the household level aiming to break the vicious circle of diarrhea diseases/nematodes with malnutrition, addressing also small stock open defecation affecting young children (2000 SAM/MAM will be provided with Famine prevention kits).4. Integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level: WASH in Health lifesaving engineering for outbreak control such as (cholera/AWD, typhoid, EVD) by transmission contexts (waterborne, intra domiciliary, school, market, funerals, and health facilities). WASH activities will be conducted through an integrated and multidisciplinary approach involving WASH user committees coordination with Hygiene Promoters (Hygiene Promotion sessions at household and public gatherings).

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiary rights will be legally recognized. This will be done by human rights awareness raising sessions with the project beneficiaries and open dialogues with community Leaders. Their views, opinions and decisions about the project will be respected. They will be informed about availability of services and referral pathways for reporting cases of abuse. The reporting system will offer a safety net for the protection of beneficiaries against abuse by project staff.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project design and implementation is aligned to the perspectives, rights and needs ofthe different target population groups such as IDPs, returnees and the vulnerablehost community. Consultations with the community to gather information on theirviews, needs and specific vulnerabilities of the target beneficiaries has been done and will continue to be sought during project implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The consultative process will involve the IDPs, returnees and host communities to ensure that decisions taken by HACO will be aligned to the interests of the IDPs, returnees and Vulnerable host Community.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The Proposed WASH Project is linked to government/community systems the fact that it involves the State Ministry of Physical Infrastructure and Urban development in the project from inception through implementation especially in assessing broken down water points, the rehabilitation of broken hand pumps as well as actively engaging in training hygiene promoters and WASH user committees. The rational for full engagement of the State ministry of Water and Urban development in the project implementation is for project ownership and sustainability purposes-long term interventions. The WASH user committees and hygiene promoters will act as community volunteers when the project ends and will be the ones managing the WASH facilities within their locations.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

This project is aligned to National Objectives such as Social protection of IDPs, National Refugee Return Framework which provide for durable solutions to internally displaced persons who will no longer have specific assistance and protection needs that are linked to their displacement and can enjoy their humanrights without discrimination on account of their displacement. The proposed WASH project willenable the sustainable reintegration of IDPs, returnees at their place of origin by offering WASH services in their residence locations. It will also provide for IDPs sustainable local integration by providing WASH services in areas where internally displaced persons are residing.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The Protection Environment in South Sudan has generally improved but is still fragile due to pockets ofinsecurity, drought and Economic Crisis. The proposed WASH Support Project will facilitate IDPs and returnees to enjoy without discrimination Long-term WASH services within their own communities. The WASH project will help anchor them within the affairs of their own community. Through this Project returnees and IDPs will get a foundation for adequate standard of living, especially access to adequate WASH services and for those that will be employed as Hygiene promoters or pump mechanics, it will provide them with access to employment and livelihoods which will build their confidence; propelling them to participate in public affairs at all levels of the community engagement on an equal basis with the resident population.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The Proposed WASH Project is linked to Community and Government Systems for Sustainability Purposes. Project Activities will be carried out in collaboration with the State Ministry of Physical Infrastructure and Local Community leaders. The Proposed WASH Project provides for Sustainability and handover of project activities in the sense that the State Ministry of Physical Infrastructure and Urban development will be involved in project implementation; in training hygiene promoters, Water Pump Mechanics and WASH user committees. The rational for full engagement of the State ministry of Water and Urban development in the project implementation is for project ownership and for sustainability purposes-long term interventions. The State Ministry of Physical Infrastructure officials, WASH user committees and hygiene promoters will Manage WASH facilities when the project ends.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The above mentioned risks will be mitigated in the following ways: There will be the training of HACO staff on PSEA. There will also be human rights awareness raising sessions, availability of services, access and referral pathways for project beneficiaries who survive sexual violence and abuse. Community leaders and leaders of associations such as women group leaders, youth associations who will be involved in beneficiary prioritization to reduce cases of disfavoring some potential beneficiaries based on their vulnerabilities such as gender, age or tribe. The beneficiaries will be fully protected from sexual exploitation and abuse from HACO staff who will have signed the PSEA as part of their Contract obligation. HACO staff will also follow the humanitarian principle of DO NO HARM to the project beneficiaries.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed project that intends to provide timely, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees will employ Complaints Feedback Mechanism as a means of communicating the project aims and aiding project correction during project implementation. The AAP approach will target acute vulnerabilities; namely of Disabled Persons; by consulting the persons with Disabilities about their concerns and ensuring at least 10% of the WASH facilities especially Latrines are user friendly to persons with disabilities. The Illiterate will be consulted about their concerns in relation to the project activities, Women and Girls will be consulted in Mitigating WASH related gender based violence: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation in targeted communities based on Protection assessment conducted by female staff with women, girls and children for WASH project activities adequately planned with the community inputs. PSEA sessions, FGDS and proposed construction/rehabilitation of Water points and Latrines will be done after Consulting Women and Girls about the appropriate locations for them to access WASH Services. The Proposed WASH Project will have a direct complaints and feedback mechanism; Community Communication and Engagement involving Community dialogues. The project implementors will have an open door policy for beneficiaries to give their feedback through FGDs. WASH user committees will be set up to raise issues of concern during project implementation. WASH user Committees will be an inclusive body composed of Men and women in the Project site taking care of age and gender. The role of the WASH User committees will be to receive and register complaints raised by beneficiaries, coordinate with HACO and give feedback whenever possible. HACO WASH Field teams shall set aside a desk for complaints and feedback.
