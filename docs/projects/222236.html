Project Summary

CARE International is a 78-year-old global confederation working to fight poverty and social injustice in the world, with a specific focus on the empowerment of women and girls. We work side-by-side with communities to understand the root causes of poverty and find innovative, locally led solutions. CARE seeks to demonstrate the results and methodologies used in our projects to ensure transparency and accountability and advance the quality of our work. In Mozambique, CARE International began its operations in 1984, initially focusing on providing emergency assistance and food distributions for those affected by the protracted war between government and rebel forces. From 1990 to 1994, its project portfolio included recovery to disaster and development activities. Currently, CARE has offices in Maputo, Maxixe, Nampula and Pemba and works across the country in partnership with civil society organizations.
Armed-conflict, insecurity, and violence have affected the Province of Cabo Delgado since late 2017. Violence against civilians and civilian infrastructure, arson, and looting by non-state armed groups (NSAG) as well as criminal activity have led to the destruction of homes, livelihoods, markets, and basic services and exacerbated structural and developmental challenges, resulting in extreme levels of humanitarian needs and protection risks for girls, boys, women, and men.
As mentioned above, the complex crisis affecting Cabo Delgado is resulting in multiple protection risks directly and indirectly related to conflict and displaced with many women, men, girls and boys having directly experiences violence against themselves or their own families. With reported accounts of sexual violence perpetuated by insurgents, a surge in intimate partner violence (IPV) and other forms of gender-based violence (GBV) (CARE, RGA for CDG, April 2022). Girls are particularly vulnerable to forced marriages sometimes as young as the age of 12 and early/unwanted pregnancies often resulting from survival sex. Girls and women with disabilities are systematically and persistently stigmatized based on their gender and disability and have an elevated risk of experiencing GBV (LFTW: DIRGA, 1/2022). As a result, women, and girls but also many men and boys have lost hope for the future and lack strategies to emotionally cope with their situation.
Protection concerns are exacerbated by scarcity of materials, supplies, and services in both districts. Lack of basic safety and assistance, including the discontinuation of social protection cash grants for vulnerable populations, leave women and girls exposed to ongoing GBV risks. CARE’s RGA for CDG (April 2022) identified instances of transactional sex for food occurring in resettlement centres. RNA participants from Ancuabe also reported that some men trade humanitarian assistance for sex or alcohol.
Linked to the conflict in Cabo Delgado, sexual exploitation is a significant concern where allegations of aid-for-sex have been brought forward in recent years (Human Rights Watch 2021, Centre of Public Integrity 2023.
Building on its current interventions, CARE is proposing an intervention in key affected areas focusing on direct GBV interventions (i.e. case management and referrals, provision of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) to survivors of GBV, awareness raising and capacity building (refreshers) of local authorities) as well as general protection interventions (i.e. protection monitoring, provision of MHPSS to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees impacted by the conflict, awareness raising on protection risks including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and provision of information on available protection services, referrals to protection services and capacity building local authorities and humanitarian actors). CARE is proposing a project that will be implemented in the districts of Ancuabe, Chiure, Macomia, Meluco and Nangade.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

CARE’s provision of protection support to crisis-affected communities in Cabo Delgado targeting internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees with prioritization of women, children and marginalized/vulnerable groups i.e. persons with disability focusing on implementation of GBV interventions as well as general protection interventions. GBV interventions include 1) support with case management and referral of available services to survivors of GBV, 2) provision of MHPSS for GBV survivors, 3) provision of dignity kits to GBV survivors, 4) capacity building of community members, local actors, and women and girls’ networks, 5) GBV prevention and risk mitigation through awareness raising, developed/improved access to GBV IEC materials and 6) development of women and girls’ empowerment/life skill activities. As for general protection interventions, CARE will focus on the following – 1) protection monitoring, 2) provision of MHPSS to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees impacted by the conflict, 3) awareness raising on protection risks including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and provision of information on available protection services, referrals to protection services and 4) capacity building local authorities and humanitarian actors.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

GBV specific activities: • Specialized GBV Case management services & referral – building on its experience, CARE will through the establishment / continuation of existing women and girls’ safe spaces (WGSS) provide case management and referral support to GBV survivors. • Psychosocial support, including Psychological first aid – CARE has been providing MHPSS to GBV survivors in community affected by the current crisis, leveraging on this CARE proposes continuation of this to 1) reduce risk of mental health problems, 2) ensure effectiveness of aid provided, 3) encourage/support recovery and resilience. This will be done through individual and group consultations as well as creative arts activities. It will also • Capacity enhancement for community, local actors, and women and girls’ networks – currently, CARE is implementing the Women Lead in Emergencies (WLiE) programme which supports women directly affected by crisis to participate in humanitarian action. It enables women’s groups and collectives to take the lead in responding to and recovering from natural disasters, conflict and other emergencies affecting their communities. It provides accessible and practical guidance and tools for CARE staff and partners on ways of working with groups of poor and marginalized women to facilitate their participation and leadership in humanitarian action. • GBV Prevention and risk mitigation activities (Awareness raising, campaign, and access to GBV IEC materials) – CARE will conduct awareness raising activities to support in the prevention/elimination of violence through community dialogues, theatre, radio hotspots etc.• Women and girl’s Empowerment and Life Skill Activities – building on the WLiE approach CARE will identify income generating activities (IGAs) for women to encourage economic participation. General protection activities: • Protection monitoring - Protection monitoring refers to the systematic process of collecting, verifying and analyzing available information with the objective of identifying protection risks and human rights violations. It also helps to determine the needs and priorities for affected populations. In the context of Cabo Delgado and the conflict, things are ever changing and with these changes come changes in protection risks hence the importance of the constant monitoring of protection concerns. The results of protection monitoring can be used for advocacy and to achieve a community-focused response that is evidence-based and coherent.• Psychosocial support, including Psychological first aid – CARE has been providing MHPSS to communities affected by the current crisis, leveraging on this CARE proposes continuation of this to 1) reduce risk of mental health problems, 2) ensure effectiveness of aid provided, 3) encourage/support recovery and resilience• Awareness raising on protection risks, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and provision of information and referrals to protection services – this includes continued trainings of staff and related personnel (e.g. partners, vendors), engaging community members and direct beneficiaries on their rights and available reporting mechanisms).• Capacity building local authorities and humanitarian actors – this is key to ensure improvement in service delivery.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CARE will use a community-based protection risk analysis tool - this tool will engage with community members to identify the potential risks. The community-based protection risk analysis serves as a basis for community protection actionplans, identifying the most relevant risks for each group within a community. It ensures that community members have the same understanding of the protection risks they are going to address throughtheir work and take time to think about the causes and impacts of the threats the community faces,in order to better decide on activities to tackle them.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Taking into consideration that many communities are patriarchal, which in turn limits women's decision making, autonomy and participation in community activities - awareness raising through community dialogues with all (especially men as well as community leaders, religious leaders who are often "gatekeepers") to ensure the equitable participation and access to assistance to women and girls.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

conduct inclusive assessments to 1) identify people with disabilities and 2) understand their barriers to participation and access to assistance. The findings of these assessments would in turn help develop strategies respond to these challenges
