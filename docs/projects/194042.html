Project Summary

Darfur faces major challenges in her quest for sustainable peace and development. The armed conflict in Darfur erupted in early 2003. Over the past 19 years, civilians have been subjected to systematic violations of human rights, humanitarian law, causing massive displacement and violence and an IDP community held captive by fear. The lack of confidence in governance and rule of law institutions are further compounded by the destruction of infrastructure and livelihoods, the absence of basic social services and a negative impact on the environment and the ecosystem. Despite some progresses have been made by national and international efforts, recently partly signed peace deal followed the removal of post regime and as strategic goal for transitional government; still there are considerable number of internally displaced persons sheltering in IDPs camps with very little humanitarian assistance, in addition to poor or absent of GBV support activities which exacerbating the living conditions particularly women and children protection. The protracted conflict led to prolonged stay at IDPs camps, moreover the current economic conditions with its daily inflation and there are no adequate employment opportunities for IDPs family to generate sufficient income for their households, these lack of income create massive violence at household level and community level, as tensions have been raising among household members due to the unmet of multiple daily needs as well as criminal acts are also increasing targeting more the vulnerable community members (women and children).
Trust Organization will contribute to the three GBV Sub -Sector objectives:
1- Strengthen the provision of life- saving specialized quality GBV services, for survivors of GBV and vulnerable women and girls. (with a focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 1), through the provision of different services to vulnerable women & girls including GBV survivors (Case management, PSS, referrals), assessments, community awareness-raising, and programming, provision of dignity kits, and capacity building of GBV frontline actors. this project will contribute to tackle those matters by raising up the community member’s level of understanding to their issues, and develop their capacities to be able to solve their problems by themselves.
2-Enhance strategies for community resilience through the empowerment of women and girls and strengthened community-based interventions (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 2), by supporting women centers, community protection networks, and training of women & girls on vocational skills, the project will work on IGA to provide resources and skills for women and girls to grasp an activities that will help them to increase their income and develop their economic situation.
3- Promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 3), by training on GBV for Non-GBV actors. this Objective will be addressed through capacity building which accompanied by meetings with sector coordinators, presentations/interactions at Cluster meetings, products developed/updated guidelines, sensitization briefings, awareness raising messages, assessment and safety audits tools and reports.

1 - Needs

Violence against women and girls is one of human rights violation, also any of gender based violence that result in or likely to result in physical, sexual or psychological or suffering to women, include threats of such acts, coercion or arbitrary derivations of liberty, whether occurring in public or private life on 24 May 2022, an interagency assessment mission, has been conduct by a joint team comprising HAC, OCHA, UNICEF, UNDP, International Medical Corps and MC/E in Sharq Jabal Marra (leiba, Area), and the assessment come up with some recommendations concerns protection issues which need intervention to be solved.• On 4 September 2022, HAC reported the arrival of newly displaced persons from Sharq Jabal Marra to Mershing IDPs camps, following fighting between SLA-AW factions. As a result of the above, an interagency team was set up to conduct a combined rapid needs assessment to the newly displaced persons, and the protracted IDPs/ host communities affected by floods in August 2022 as reported by HAC, this assessment also come up with some GBV issues, there for this project will address directly those issues, and find the adorable solution for it. On 18 April, a team of humanitarian organizations comprising HAC, OCHA, UNFPA, UNHCR, UNICEF, WFP, IOM, and OHCHR conducted a rapid needs assessment to determine the needs of the newly displaced persons in OTASH IDPs camp and they found that The situation of GBV is not stable due to the lack of services, and the assessment team came up with some Major recommendations: - Raise awareness on GBV and referral pathways among the center especially the protection committee and community leaders. - Establish a Women committee to be concerned about the women and girls' situation in center 15 and link it with the women committee in the WGSS in center 13. - Involve women and girls in the PSS activities in the WGSS. therefore the project will be designed to address all those issues and put a durable salutation for it by the activities which will involve in the project.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1: Support women & girls with startup capital for Income Generating Activities and vocational skills• Activity includes training women and girls on vocational skills and supporting them with start-up capital to put the skills into productive use, this targeted 300 (100 women, 100 girls, 100 youth and men) • Skills building/vocational activities should attempt to avoid reinforcing existing gender stereotypes by offering activities on a variety of skills. • Skills building/vocational activities should be undertaken within the framework of psychosocial support interventions for women and girls (and within a protection framework) 2: Provide 4500 dignity kitsDistribution of comprehensive dignity kits that contain menstrual hygiene materials, soap, underwear, and information on available GBV services, including where and how to access those services as well as messages on PSEA. Dignity kits also include items that may help mitigate GBV risks such as lights and culturally appropriate clothing items, for (4500 women and girls under protective age)3: Provide specialized GBV response services for 1500 people (women, men, girls, boys)Specialized GBV services include:• Case management for GBV survivors• CMR and other health services for survivors• MPHSS services for survivors including Psychological First Aid (PFA), Focused individual and group PSS counselling, specialized mental health services for GBV survivors• Provision of other material and cash support for GBV survivors or at-risk women and girls in critical emergency situations (Case by case basis including referral related costs)• Referral of GBV survivors to GBV related services (legal services, specialized PSS, health services, or if necessary, to non-GBV services (WASH, NFI, FSL etc.).4: Conduct 6 GBV assessmentsThis is GBV specific assessments at locality level. Activities include GBV situation analysis, KAP surveys, safety audits etc.5: conduct training on GBV for specialized GBV service providers for 300 people (men, women, girls).6: Conduct large scale public community awareness raising campaigns on GBV.Awareness activities (involving large number of people), on GBV include awareness-raising campaigns, strategies, events, sessions, TV/radio, theatre, engaging men and boys in preventing GBV as well as structural behavioral change programs. The topics of such sessions may include different topics on GBV. Sessions on the risk and use of technology/ social media are also encouraged 7: Conduct community-based awareness raising sessions for 4500 people (3000 women and girl, 1500 men and boy).8: conduct protection monitoring to identify protection concern (physical safety, material safety, legal safety issues, freedom of movement and residence) and most vulnerable individuals for assistance through this project, referrals and advocacy activities, the monitoring report also will be shared with protection sector at the state and national level.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

• The project activities and implementation design considered the impact on the environment with a mitigation and enhancement process, the planned women centers – if supported - will be rehabilitated using to the extent possible there no environmentally harmful materials. The promotion of fuel-efficient stoves - if supported - will contribute to the improved livelihood of vulnerable households while reducing environmental impact as fewer trees are cut for cooking fuel. In addition, during the dignity kits distribution, women and girls will be sensitized to environmentally friendly ways of disposing of the used sanitary wear. Through the women and girls’ empowerment program, the GBV AoR partners will explore the production of reusable sanitary pads as a replacement for disposable ones.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this project, TPDO is focusing on promoting effective participation of targeted communities across gender, age, and vulnerability throughout the project cycle to ensure GBV survivors and those at risk are not exposed to additional harm. In addition, the project will take into the account mainstreaming of meaningful access, safety, and dignity when implementing activities. also GBV needs will continue to be monitored throughout 2023 and will continuously inform the responses. Periodic monitoring will be conducted through the GBV Sub-cluster (GBVSC) and GBV working groups at the state level, CBPNs, and multi-cluster rapid assessments. It will also be achieved through available data from other clusters and proxy indicators as well as GBV stand-alone assessments. Advocacy for stand-alone GBV assessments is ongoing, and if successful, it will contribute to a better understanding of GBV needs. in addition to that TPDO will ensure the reporting of all activities monthly and quarterly using the 4Ws, to be shared at national level, in coordination with GBV, and protection Sub- cluster lead at state level, and flowing SHF's reporting policies.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is core in the TPDO response and will be strengthened through continuous consultation with the affected population on the response modalities as well as their satisfaction with services provided. In addition, TPDO is an active member of the national PSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team and together is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanisms are accessible to survivors of SEA. TPDO will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the SEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. TPDO’s partners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regards to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities.This is done by conducting focus group discussions with women and girls in women centers. The project will support the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improving beneficiaries’ daily lives through improved formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequality as the root cause of GBV.
