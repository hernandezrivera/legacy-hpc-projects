Project Summary

UNICEF’s water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) humanitarian response in northeast Nigeria, plays two roles: as service provider; and as the provider of last resort (POLR). Beginning in 2019, the WASH sector in Northeast Nigeria began evaluating the geo-location assigning of partners to ensure effective accountability in the delivery of humanitarian WASH services. Under this mechanism, the humanitarian response from different WASH partners is coordinated by UNICEF and the State Ministry of Water Resources so partners provide specific WASH services to IDP camps and host communities allocated to them. Additionally as provider of last resort, UNICEF may have to cover LGAs where critical needs/gaps arise due to new displacement or WASH related diseases or gaps left by diminished capacity/departure of other WASH partners. This may include interventions in two other states (Adamawa and Yobe) as well.
Through this project, UNICEF will have specific target IDP camps in Maiduguri Metropolitan City and 10 LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kara Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala). UNICEF will adhere to the SPHERE standards in the humanitarian WASH services it provides, and scale up the sustenance of the services as needed.
To meet the demands of the POLR responsibility, UNICEF may need to mobilize additional resources. Through this project UNICEF will provide the initial life saving response to fill the gaps, prioritizing areas receiving newly displaced people or under an outbreak of cholera, hepatitis or other WASH-related diseases. UNICEF will strive to sustain the basic initial WASH services until further response becomes available from other sector partners.
The project objectives and targets are:
1) Affected people have safe and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water for domestic needs, as per SPHERE standards. This objective aims at scaling up sustainable water supply coverage in IDP camps to reach 15 litres per person per day. Target: 200,000 people.
2) Affected people have safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities, as per SPHERE standards. This objective aims at scaling up sustainable sanitation coverage in IDP camps to reach latrine coverage standard of 20 users per latrine. Target: 500,000 people.
3) Affected people benefit from community tailored gender- and age-sensitive hygiene messages aiming at hygienic behavior and practices as per sector’s standard. This objective aims at reaching 1,126,800 people in IDP camps and host communities with risk informed behaviour change communication messages in 2020.
4) Affected people benefit from basic gender- and age-sensitive hygiene items including top-ups as per sector standards. This objective aims to provide new arrivals in IDP camps with initial response hygiene kits and long term IDPs replenishment kits in IDP camps in target LGAs, including cash for hygiene kits in MMC and Jere LGAs. Target: 1,126,800 people.

1 - Needs

According to the needs assessment for the WASH Sector Humanitarian Response Plan in 2020 an estimated 3,577,800 people need humanitarian intervention related to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in Northeast Nigeria. Overall, the WASH sector will target 2,394,020 people, this project targets 1,126,800 people with a variable number of beneficiaries for different activities, according to assessed needs and vulnerabilities. UNICEF addresses approximately 50 percent of the sector targets. The needs of the beneficiaries are variable by age group and by gender. For instance, children’s pottys are part of the hygiene kits to address the needs of young children who are not able to use the standard latrines. Further, 20 percent of the latrines provided under the project will have ramps to facilitate easier access for beneficiaries with physical disabilities. All latrines for females will have security and privacy enhancements to ensure safety and dignity is safeguarded from issues such as voyeurism which is a common problem. The design of latrines and sanitary pads has been guided by feedback from users provided in previous projects. The design of latrines and showers to ensure fitness for purpose is guided by feedback from project personnel, peers from the Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) sector and beneficiaries. In response to the feedback, gender- segregated combined blocks of showers and latrines have been adopted and the different genders will access their facilities from different directions. Similarly, girls and women of child-bearing age will receive reusable sanitary pads, which are more preferred to the single use disposable pads. The project integrates life-saving and standard of living for the beneficiaries. The life-saving interventions target newly displaced people and population affected by disease outbreaks. Long term internally displaced people living in formal and informal camps are targeted for improved and sustainable access to basic water supply, sanitation and hygiene services. The interface of the nexus between humanitarian assistance and development is to be explored at all times. The first instance is to link the service provision to support regular service providers from the sector government agencies in water, sanitation and hygiene, including building definite links with institutional structures in CCCM and the State Emergency Management Agency. There are on-going discussions with the Commissioner for Water Resources at State level to increase distributive benefits from the humanitarian assistance to the host communities in Municipalities and Local Government Area capitals depending on the production capacity of water systems developed under the humanitarian assistance. This approach will limit excessive abstraction of groundwater while increasing the return on investment in terms of cost per beneficiary.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement nine key activities (outlined below) through various partnerships, Long Term Agreement (LTA) contracts, Small Scale Funding Agreements (SSFA) or Project Cooperation Agreements (PCA) with selected partners and State Govt Agencies under the Basic Cooperation Agreement with Federal Government of Nigeria and Annual Work Plans with Borno, Adamawa and Yobe State Govts. Since 2019, the WASH sector in North east Nigeria has been allocating specific WASH response to various partners in different LGAs or camps to increase transparency in use of resources collectively available to the sector and promote efficiency of use of the resources and facilitate transparent accountability of the partners to beneficiaries and the sector. This project will consolidate UNICEF capacity as a sector partner implementing in allocated locations/camps in Maiduguri Municipality and 10 LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kala Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala), where UNICEF will reach SPHERE standards. Additional resources will be needed for UNICEF to play the second role of provider of last resort (POLR) where there is no other capacity from the sector. The key activities to be implemented under this project are: 1) Water trucking for limited periods to provide temporary access to safe water to newly displaced people or people affected by water, sanitation and hygiene related disease outbreaks. UNICEF will deploy LTA contractors on water trucking to the areas in need. Target: 50,000 people. 2) Optimizing water production, storage and distribution to provide 15 litres per person per day, increase storage and expand distribution to reduce water collection time to within 30 minutes round trip including queuing time. This is based on assessed needs and tested optimal water production of existing water resources. Higher capacity pumps matching the tested safe water pumping will be installed. Storage tank capacities will be increased to cover two days' water demand in case of breakdowns. Priority are large population camps with high capacity boreholes. This activity will optimize benefit for the affected population and enable possible development of water supply for host communities and other population. Target: 200,000 people. 3) Daily chlorination and management of the operation and maintenance of water supply services to sustain access to safe water in IDP camps. Implementing partners will support the management of existing water systems in camps allocated to UNICEF: daily chlorination of water using automatic chlorine dossers; monitoring of the free residual chlorine levels and faecal contamination of the water at production point and at the shelters using the water; and organizing orderly queuing at the water collection points and support repairs in case of breakdown. The target is to restore service within 48 hours. Target: 500,000 people. 4) Increasing access to safe sanitation. UNICEF will construct additional latrines to reach the SPHERE standard. Target 200,000 people. 5) Desludging latrines in allocated camps and per demand from CCCM for other camps. Target: 300,000 people. 6) Daily cleaning and disinfection of latrines including garbage collection and general camp cleaning. Target: 500,000 people 7) Distribution of hygiene kits including cash for kits in Maiduguri. Target: 1,126,800 people in-kind; and 5,000 households in cash for hygiene kits 8) Risk informed hygiene promotion in camps and host communities, and cholera hotspots, using a range of communication channels. Target: 1,126,800 people. 9) Cholera prevention and urban chlorination. Distribution of water treatment chemicals to Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States for municipal water treatment and distribution in cholera affected LGAs. Target: 3 States and all Cholera hot spots.
