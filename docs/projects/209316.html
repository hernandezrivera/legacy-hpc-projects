Project Summary

As a result of conflict, massive displacement and economic decline, 20.3 million people in Sudan, accounting for more than 42% of the country's population, experienced high levels of acute food insecurity (classified as Integrated Phase Classification – IPC - Phase 3 or above – Crisis, Emergency or Famine) between July and September 2023, during the peak lean season. Out of this number, 14 million people (29% of the total population) were classified in IPC Phase 3 Crisis, while nearly 6.3 million people (13% of the total population) were classified in IPC Phase 4 Emergency. The projections for October 2023 to February 2024 indicate 15 million people, accounting for more than 31% of the country's population, experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity - the highest ever recorded figure coinciding with Sudan’s harvest season. Out of this number, 11.1 million people (23% of the population) are classified in IPC Phase 3 Crisis, while 3.8 million people (8% of the population) are classified in IPC Phase 4 Emergency.
The coping mechanisms used by the population (starting with those most frequently employed) were consuming less expensive food, reducing the size of meals, borrowing food, reducing the number of meals per day and feeding children first. Sources of food (starting with those most frequently cited) were borrowing food from others, agricultural/livestock production, the household’s food reserves and food gifts from others. Of the consulted population, approximately 20% reported consuming one meal or less per day and approximately 65% reported consuming two meals per day. A rapid nutritional assessment carried out in Ag Geneina and Ardamata in September 2023 screened 281 children under five years of age using Middle Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC), identifying 34 with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and 18 with severe acute malnutrition (SAM). The assessment found that the key challenges to feeding babies and young children included lack of support for non-breastfed infants and the quantity and quality of food available for complementary feeding.
In March 2023 in Central Darfur, the chronic malnutrition (moderate and severe) rate among children under five years was 47.5% and the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate among children under five years was 17.8% (a population of 108,427 children suffering from GAM). Considering that the GAM rate in Central Darfur already surpassed the 15% GAM emergency threshold, the current nutritional situation is very concerning considering that conditions have deteriorated as a result of six months of intense conflict, impacting food, water, hygiene and health services access and availability.
This project is designed to respond to the urgent needs of conflict-affected people by providing inclusive timely gender gender-sensitive lifesaving assistance through general food distribution nutrition assistance while looking at durable and sustainable livelihoods and food security actions by applying nexus programming in FSL. This project will target 14698 people in need (7496 females and 7202 males) from the IDPs, Returnees, and Host Communities and 61,131 by nutrition 70 % females including PLWs, Children under 5, and people with disabilities.
The project will focus on several critical issues, including gender, protection, nutrition-sensitive programming, and Natural Resource Management (NRM). These issues are vital for ensuring social equity and the sustainable use of valuable resources that will benefit both current and future generations. To ensure success, CRS will closely monitor the project's implementation, particularly in targeting the vulnerable Children Under 5, PLW/G and People with Disabilities in West & Central Darfur states and Red Sea. The project will involve community mobilizers, nutrition screenings alongside MoH, monitoring, and reporting.
