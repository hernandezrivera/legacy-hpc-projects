Project Summary

Widespread malnutrition still persists in Somalia and it affects disproportionately children under the age of five, as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). The main risk factors and causes related to acute food insecurity over recent years, conflict & violence, disease outbreaks, drought, locust infestation, recurrent flooding and population displacements, this coupled with low access to health services, poor access to water and sanitation, poor maternal nutrition, low immunization and high disease burden. Poor feeding practices for infants and young children are also strongly linked with undernutrition.
According to post Gu FSNAU and nutrition analysis 2021 indicate national level the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has still remained at a serious level of 11.1%. Based on this analysis and the latest population figures, the nutrition cluster estimates that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of five years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services urgently. Out of this a total of 295,515 will be severely acute malnourished children (SAM) and around 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM) children. In addition to this, around 245,909 Pregnant & Lactating Women (PLWs) will be at risk of acute malnutrition. On the same regards an estimated that around 629,567 children between 6 to 23 months and 283,305 PLWs will at a greater risk as well. Hence, the total people in need (both for curative and preventive services) for nutrition in 2022 will be 2.37 million including IDPs and Non-IDPs.
According to the 2020 Micronutrient Survey, the rates of exclusive breastfeeding in children less than six months is only at 20%. In addition, COVID 19 continues to be another major challenge as it has affected the nutrition service delivery modalities. Malnutrition both in childhood and pregnancy has adverse consequences for immediate child survival and the long-term wellbeing of children and mothers. The nutritional status of children under five years of age continues to deteriorate in most parts of Somalia thereby increasing their vulnerabilities and predisposing them to morbidity and mortality. According to the latest findings of post Gu results of FSNAU, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score).Negative coping mechanisms for malnutrition includes sharing of nutrition commodities among the siblings and may therefore hinder timely recovery. Given the nature or recurring emergencies and poverty in Somalia combined with the impact of COVID 19, many households will continue to lose their purchasing power, that will contribute to increase in malnutrition.
Therefore, immediate lifesaving humanitarian nutrition services covering both preventive and curative aspects are required and in this regards PASSO will ensure to provide integrated management of severely and moderately malnourished children boys, girls and pregnant and lactating women including the disable population in the target district of Ceel Waaq and Bardera in Gedo region, PASOS will provide comprehensive community screening services as well as treatment and referral services to both severely and moderately malnourished children including these with medical and non-medical complication of SAM cases , while cognizant of the fact that malnutrition is multifaceted in nature. PASOS will strive to coordinated with livelihood and health actors to ensure coordinated response and strengthen referral mechanism for greater outcome in managing and enhancing the target beneficiaries status.
PASOS shall also endeavor to coordinated with Local and Federal MOH as well as local communities for participation and ownership of the proposed intervention and to ensure better result for the vulnerable population in the target district

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement The returnees targeted population risk of exclusion is medium since PASOS will ensure that data is collected among the beneficiaries with the help of already community structures and effective approaches such as outreach services so that all the vulnerable populations will be reached. Lack of documentation among the returnees does not mean that they are excluded but rather their vulnerability analysis. PASOS will also ensure that all the affected population understand the process of obtaining food assistance and livelihoods opportunities at the appropriate time, date and safe location. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age The risks of exclusion based on gender and age is medium. The project’s gender focus creates new challenges for women and/or HH and community conflict by promoting women’s and age equality. PASOS will mitigate this risk by ensuring that activities targeting women include men in appropriate ways; male leaders engaged to support women’s involvement in decision making; gender impact of project (positive & negative) closely monitored; implementing agencies trained to refer GBV cases. Issues related to age exclusion will also be mitigated. There is a risk of excluding child headed household in the project. Minors will not be considered in restricted cash interventions but rather PASOS will involve the relevant local agencies to ensure that the child-headed households benefit from the unrestricted cash interventions. The risk of beneficiaries experience intimidation, extortion or theft of resources following distributions is medium and PASOS will Inform beneficiaries of potential risks, ensure distributions are conducted in secure areas and allow beneficiaries ample time to travel during daylight hours, ensure robust complaints mechanism and post distribution monitoring is in place Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence The main risks include: a) Inaccessibility of project locations due to security concerns. The risk is medium and PASOS will ensure that all the project locations are in both secure and potentially insecure areas to ensure continuous implementation of activities; and will also regularly review security status with local authorities and institutions. 2) There is also the risk of taxation by local authorities/power holders on distributed resources that the beneficiaries will face. The risk is medium and PASOS will mitigate the above mentioned risk by work with local authorities and other stakeholders to create awareness that these resources and assets are not subject to taxation, monitor and take immediate action on reports of taxation through accountability and feedback mechanisms
