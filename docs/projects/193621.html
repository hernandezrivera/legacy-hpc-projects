Project Summary

The project aims at emergency support for Gaza farmers to improve their economic situation through rehabilitation of their agricultural facilities. The project targeted groups will be vulnerable affected households whom agricultural facilities are damaged during repeated Israelis wars launched against Gaza, especially during May 2021 and August 2022. The targeted groups must be engaged in greenhouses, open land and poultry and sheep breeding. The financial capacities of these Farmers groups are limited, and they cannot cover the costs of the needed production inputs and needed materials for the rehabilitation of their agricultural facilities without any external financial support. through the implementation of the project activities they will be supported through conditional restricted cash assistance for the rehabilitation of their damaged agricultural facilities ( OL, GH , poultry farms and livestock rehabilitation), the farmers facilities will be rehabilitated through delivering by in kind modality , UAWC will provide targeted groups by material needed for the rehabilitation and installation of PV system through tendering the needed materials and devices and then contracting with the tender winner who will be responsible for supply fo materials for UAWC to deliver it to the targeted , the project team will follow up the rehabilitation according the materials technical specification of the tender .. UAWC focuses also on the infrastructural challenges for protecting agricultural livelihoods in the Gaza Strip such as; damaged agricultural land, greenhouses, poultry farms and rehabilitation of livestock's for sheep breeders In addition to the rehabilitation of the agricultural facilities, PV system will be installed for poultry farms and livestock's to alleviate the shortages of electricity. The PV system for the poultry farm will be used as power supply for the farm to light and ventilate it , as well as for livestock , the PV system will be very useful for:: operating the small milking machine, ventilation the livestock during the hot days in the summer , lighting the farm during the very long night in the winter to facilitate the feeding of the sheep, operating the water pump ( About 0.5 HP) for filling the water tank for sheep drinking and supply lights to breeders when the goats are going to bring birth. moreover, the breeder themselves can benefit from PV system for lighting. The Action will enable vulnerable households to maintain their agricultural activities and enhance their coping capacity to future shocks. The project will contribute to the following overarching country strategic objectives: SO3: The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced (CO1The planned action was closely coordinated with the Food Security Sector, ministry of Agriculture (MoA), ministry INGO and NGOs The project activities are described as follow: 1. 110 OL rehabilitation in north and Gaza governorates; 55 farmers will be supported through conditional and restricted cash assistance needed for the rehabilitation of 2 OL Dunms for each farmers, each farmer will receive 950 USD in 2 installment , the farmers will cultivate open field crops such as potatoes , onion, carrots cabbage and cauliflower, here is worth to mentioned that some farmers prefer to cultivate trees seedlings such as citrus and olives trees .
2.50 GH rehabilitation the north, Khan Yunis and Rafah; 100 GH farmers will be supported
through the rehabilitation of their greenhouses. cultivation them with different vegetables crops such as tomatoes, cucumber eggplant, pepper and sweet pepper(Paprika)
3. 20 poultry farm rehabilitation with PV systems in the North, Gaza and middle area, 20 poultry breeders will be supported through service delivery assistance needed for the rehabilitation of the poultry farm. 4. 30 livestock for breeders will be rehabilitated and PV system will be installed

1 - Needs

Food security in Palestine continues to be affected by prolonged conflict, economic stagnation, restrictions on trade and access to resources, and high unemployment and poverty rates. Lack of means to afford nutritious and healthy food and significant consumption gap due to the uneven economic recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic and widespread supply chain disruption caused by the Ukraine/Russia crisis are among the main drivers of food insecurity. The inability to overcome the consumption gap pushes the affected population to use negative coping strategies, sometimes already exhausted, thereby increasing the number of vulnerable persons. An estimated 1.8 million individuals (33.6% of the population HNO 2023) suffer from moderate to severe food insecurity. In 2022, approximately 1 out of 3 people experienced some form of food insecurity, 84% (1.54 million people) of which are expected to need humanitarian assistance. 77 per cent of those in need of humanitarian assistance are in the Gaza Strip (1.19 million people), and 23 per cent in the West Bank (353 thousand people). 81 per cent of those in West Bank (about 285 thousand people) are in Area A and B. People with a female-headed household are found to be more in need of assistance than people headed by males (29% against 26% ( The nutrition situation is characterized by relatively high malnutrition rates, driven by poverty, food insecurity, and poor dietary habits. Macro/Micronutrient deficiencies and obesity pose significant challenges to health and well-being. Extremely Moreover, all socio-economic indicators and humanitarian conditions continue to deteriorate due to the cumulative effects of a 15-year economic blockade: by land, sea, and air (since 2007). In addition, the ongoing wage crisis of PA and de facto civil servants is adding new segments of the population who need immediate assistance, including food aid. With already 53% of people reported living below the poverty line, poverty rates will increase further if this situation persists. The recurrent hostilities further reduced the profitability and sustainability of agricultural activities for many farmers, driving them to continuous losses and risking their farming practices. Hence, the already poor and limited agricultural investments are generally highly expected to decrease, leaving vulnerable farmers with no choice but to abandon their activities. According to the MoA in Gaza Strip, the last hostilities in August 2022 hit tens of agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock, and poultry farms, causing damages and losses estimated at $1m Overall in Palestine, small producers (Small-scale businesses) have been particularly hit by the reduced demand, the unprecedented challenges connected to the volatility of global commodities prices due to the Ukraine crisis, and the impediments in accessing credit and markets. The Ukraine- Russia conflict has posed numerous challenges to the already vulnerable food system, particularly the price volatility and uncertainty it has contributed to. After the price spikes of essential food items such as wheat and sunflower oil, the situation stabilized between July and August. However, the price levels are still considerably higher compared to the pre-Ukraine crisis period. Moreover, the disruption of several supply chains worldwide (e.g., fertilizers) will severely affect Palestine's agricultural production, leading to dramatic price increases and shortages of many agricultural inputs. ., the agricultural priorities for HRP 2023 includes the needs for the rehabilitation of 1550 OL, 970 GH, 1080 poultry farms and 1750 livestock farms. in addition to the rehabilitation of mentioned agricultural facilities, there is a need for the installation of PV system to reduce the negative effect of electricity shortage on farmers activities. UAWC designed the project intervention based on above mentioned needs and the coordination and consultation with MOA and FFS taking into consideration their recommendation and priorities moreover UAWC took into consideration the findings and needs for its needs assessment for the livelihoods for 8 focus groups of farmers and breeders , the focus groups were conducted during May, July ,August and October 2022 in North Gaza, Gaza , Middle area ,Khan Younis and Rafah , the average number of the attendees for each FG was 21 participants, for more details, please see the attached needs assessments reports . The main findings and needs were as :follow: • Installation of solar energy systems for their water wells/ponds to reduce the irrigation cost • Rehabilitation of water ponds and wells. • Access to inputs for cultivation, including seeds, seedlings, fertilizers, and basic equipment. • Support basic livelihood needs such as housing, food, education, and health • Rehabilitation of greenhouses and open land • Rehabilitation of livestock farms and fodder distribution • Vet services and vaccination of the sheep Please see attached needs Assessment reports Key Limitations: • Lack of cash/financial capacity to reinvest in agriculture. • Access to water for irrigation. • High cost of and lack of availability of some inputs, including water. • Loss of production capacity due to limited financial capacity to either fully rehabilitate land/greenhouses or secure needed inputs. • High cost of water extraction because of reliance on expensive diesel fuel and electricity shortages • Lack of cash/financial capacity to reinvest in agriculture. • Inability to access some of their land holdings.

During the design of the project activities, all potential target groups were involved in the design of the project, priority was given to the interest of these groups and to the relevant stakeholders, as well as the consideration of the gender equity relevant parties were considered, as for the issue of equality Gender, whether women, men, people with special needs, or age groups between 20 to 65 years. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

As most project activities will be carried out in a sensitive area, a risk analysis and security incidents will be discussed and updated in a participatory approach with the target population. Consequently, mitigation measures will be carried out to reduce potential risks adhering to the principle of “Do No Harm”. UAWC support for vulnerable farmers is based on the humanitarian principles (humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence), the ‘do no harm’ concept and other UAWC values, such as non-discrimination, UAWC support is guided by a people-centered approach, which means that the interests of beneficiaries are at the center of operations and humanitarian assistance is tailored to their differentiated needs. The most important principles that believes in is providing service to the target groups without causing harm that matters or exploiting them in exchange for providing these services, as UAWC through its follow-up team and through its Committee follows up on that, and if one of the employees breaches it, the appropriate penalty is inflicted upon him according to the existed code of conductTechnical assessments will consider the safety, dignity, and confidentiality of the served population while also protecting data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, vital to coping in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on them and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.Before implementing activities, project staff will attend a training session on the overall protection principles and how to detect GBV. This should equip them with knowledge and tools to conduct a risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood, and mitigation measures to be taken into consideration in advance. Most importantly, activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and full engagement and consultation of targeted communities in liaison with relevant authorities whenever needed and appropriate. Specifically, the following steps will be implemented to integrate do no harm throughout the project: 1- Project risk assessment will be prepared at the project start to identify clear measures to mitigate any risks with beneficiaries, contractors, and UAWC staff, community committees and the broader community by the project. 2-Active engagement with targeted communities, including their representatives, through the project community committees that will include farmer representatives (women CBOs and local committees in target localities). This approach will be the basis for all steps of the project cycle, ensuring meaningful representationof women and youth whenever possible to ensure that the project’s design is responsive to project beneficiaries' different needs and concerns. 3- Supporting women in accessing cash for work activities by providing safe and appropriate work opportunities in terms of location and time. All activities will follow a quick risk assessment which will identify any potential risks related to women’s participation in the activities and identify means to support them within the project period or beyond through a referral mechanism. 4- Cash for work activity will be aligned with the child protection policy prohibiting child labor. It will also promote women participations in agricultural typically un-accounted for, employing the equal access principle of protection and considering preserving the dignity of targeted women. This will be conducted by tailoring activities according to what is most suitable for them in terms of timing, form, location, payment method, and implications of HH and childcare burdens. 5- Activities will be adapted to COVID19 related preventive measures
