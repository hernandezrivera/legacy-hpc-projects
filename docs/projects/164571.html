Project Summary

This project aims to support the reintegration of young women and men in Syria into the labour force through the provision of vocational skills training in carpentry. The project will replicate UNIDO’s successful intervention in Lebanon whereby a training center on carpentry at the Zgharta Official Technical Institute was rehabilitated with state-of-the-art standards and cutting-edge machines from Felder and is now an example of South-South collaboration. Building on this established relationship, UNIDO will replicate this intervention in Syria. At least 200 women and men youth will be trained and UNIDO will potentially partner with UNICEF to ensure adequate outreach

4 - Needs

Since 2011, Syria’s prolonged crisis has taken an extensive toll on its population, caused massive destruction of physical infrastructure and led to high levels of emigration of qualified personnel. The industrial sector has also been severely affected by the loss in its infrastructure and human capital. While a number of industrial establishment resumed their activity, accessibility to jobs is constrained by a lack of training opportunities since about two thirds of TVET centers and complexes are damaged or out of service. Syrian youth in particular are at a disadvantage. Having been out of the education system for a number of years, it is difficult for them to get the training they need in order to access jobs and sustain their livelihoods. This might increase social tensions and exacerbate poverty and economic insecurity. This project aims to ease these challenges by supporting young women and men in accessing training and thereafter easing their reintegration into the labour force and society as whole.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will start with an inception phase whereby the location of the center will be finalized in addition to the specification and procurement of the machines from Felder. Following civil works, machines will be installed and the curriculum will be developed. An outreach strategy –in potential collaboration with UNICEF –will be implemented in order to maximize the participation of young women and men in the training. Recognizing that the carpentry sector in particular, and industry more generally is a male dominated field, UNIDO will aim to maximize the participation young women in the project. The training programmes will be designed with gender responsive safety measures in order to be attractive for women and will aim to advance gender equality through taking into consideration the difference in male and female needs, abilities, challenges and concerns. Based on security conditions and access, the project will also encourage the participation of Syrian trainers and trainees from different areas across the country. The project will contribute to meeting the ‘Access to Livelihoods and essential basic services’ needs identified in the 2019 Humanitarian Needs overview and is in line with the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan – Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sectors - Objective 1. Strengthen access to livelihoods by creating income-generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery – Output 1.3 Vocational and skills training provided.
