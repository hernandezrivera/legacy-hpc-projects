Project Summary

For Integrated Protection in Gedaref and Blue Nile, specialized Women’s Protection and Empowerment (WPE) prevention and response services will be delivered to support women and girls at risk, such as elderly women, pregnant women, breastfeeding women, women and girls with disabilities, and female headed households (HHs). The project will support the integration of reproductive health and WPE services so that gender-based violence (GBV) survivors can access safe and timely clinical care for the harmful health consequences - both physical and emotional. The project will also deliver Child Protection (CP) services to ensure that children are safe in their homes when they experience harm. The IRC will provide access to quality services and a robust CP response inclusive of case management and Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) where the IRC's Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) curriculum will be implemented. The IRC will also engage community members to further mitigate potential risks for children and adolescents. Additionally, the IRC will introduce activities to assist in reducing the risk of water-borne diseases and vector control within the catchment areas of the supported Health Facilities through risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) activities by involving IRC Integrated protection. The IRC will target the refugees in both Blue Nile and Gedaref states, affected by WASH-related diseases and outbreaks as well as flooding and malnutrition, to address their dire needs in terms of WASH support. The IRC prioritizes gender and protection as cross-cutting themes across all areas of intervention to ensure that services are equally accessible and safe for clients and that the unique needs of individuals are addressed. especially children under 5 and pregnant, lactating women (PLW), and people with disabilities. WASH activities will be bolstered by life-saving, integrated health and nutrition services to reduce excess morbidity and mortality for the refugee population. This will be done through primary health care facilities and referral of complicated cases to secondary facilities. The health package will include reproductive, maternal and child health services, treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases, mental health and psychosocial support, emergency preparedness and response including response to COVID-19 pandemic. The IRC will provide life-saving treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), referrals to stabilization centers and counseling of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) on infant and young child feeding (IYCF). The IRC will work directly in the camps to provide services, and through established Ministry of Health (MoH) facilities to scale up services to refugees in urban areas. Refugee HHs in Tuneydbah camp will also receive one round of multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to meet their basic needs. The transfer value will be distributed as per the guidelines of the Cash Working Group. The IRC will use the strategy of blanket coverage and cover around 21,000 refugees in Tuneydbah camp in Gadaref state.

1 - Needs

Protection: As of 20 September 2021, Gedaref is hosting 64,180 refugees from the Tigray conflict in Northern Ethiopia, while Blue Nile state is hosting 11,254 refugees from Ethiopia and South Sudan. Across Gedaref and Blue Nile, common child protection concerns for refugees include the separation of children from their family, child labor, and early marriage. UNFPA’s Voices from Sudan (2020) report states that specific risks for women and girls include forced/early marriage, sexual violence, and domestic violence. The Interagency GBV Assessment Report (August 2021) also highlights specific concerns in Um Rakuba and Tuneydbah camps for women and girls related to sexual and physical violence, early/forced marriage, trafficking, and sexual exploitation. In addition, the assessments show that participants have limited knowledge regarding the availability of GBV and CP services. It highlights that lack of information, stigma, shame and the potential for retaliation from community and family, contribute to significant underreporting. WASH: According to UNHCR, water supply in Gedaref and Blue Nile refugee camps is not sufficient due to low production capacity. Refugees are now receiving 14 l/p/d which is less than the standards for the minimum amount of water (20l/p/d). Excreta disposal remains a key gap in the camp, as access to sanitation facilities is limited and far below industry standards, and is anticipated to worsen due to increasing population and poor soil permeability. At maximum capacity of 35,000 refugees in Tunaydbah camp (Gedaref) and more than 8000 in Blue Nile, refugees will need 1,800 latrines and 1,200 bathing shelters. Yet, the current amount is 120 stances of emergency trench latrines and showers serving 6,000 people for a short duration, below the SPHERE standards. Hygiene promotion is a key gap in the camps with poor hygiene practices impacting the health of refugees. Women and girls are affected by the limited access to menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits and safe, private, and hygienic sanitation facilities supportive of MHM. Health and Nutrition: According to UNHCR’s 2021 Basic Needs and Vulnerability Assessment (BaNVA), refugees in camp or camp like situations reported higher health vulnerability than refugees out of camps. UNHCR further reported that availability of essential medicines declined steadily to reach 43% in 2021 compared to 57% during the 2020 assessment. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) for Sudan is projected to remain high in 2022 at 13.6%, with 64 localities classified by WHO as very high malnutrition prevalence rate of 15% and above, and 9 localities falling into catastrophic levels of GAM 30% and above. This is against the backdrop of a fragile health system severely strained by economic decline, displacements, flooding, and multiple disease outbreaks including COVID-19 and hepatitis E outbreak, including among the refugees. IRC clinic data in Tunaydbah camp indicates malaria is still a leading cause of morbidity for the refugee community with 1,398 confirmed cases recorded in 8 months. Having passed through traumatizing events during displacement and in the camps, and faced with limited coping mechanisms to the refugees, mental health conditions are quite frequent commonly manifesting as depression, anxiety, and post-traumatic stress disorders with limited services available. Food Security and Livelihoods: Refugees of the Tigray (north Ethiopia) crises are also entirely reliant on humanitarian assistance to meet their immediate food and other basic needs. However, risk assessments and IRC’s monitoring activities show that the current food and non-food item (NFI) distributions do not fully meet refugees’ basic needs. The main unmet needs include food and household items.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project will have no negative impacts on the environment. The project will not impact negatively on the environment. Although latrine construction activities are likely to have minimal on-site environmental impacts, most of the negative environmental impacts are likely to be seen off-site, where construction materials such as sand, fired bricks and timber will be sourced. Therefore, all tender/bidding documents for latrine construction activities will include clauses to ensure that contractors/suppliers obtain construction materials from authorized/certified sources. In addition, where appropriate IRC will promote the use of soil stabilized blocks (SSB) in lieu of fire bricks and contractors/suppliers will be required to adhere to the stipulated environmental mitigation measures below which will form part of the contract conditions.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, the IRC makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. In all aspects of the project, from design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. Community outreach will include messaging on prevention and mitigation of GBV and CP risks All staffing working on the project will be trained on core protection concepts, gender mainstreaming, and referral procedures. The IRC will integrate activities within its referral system, allowing the team to refer potential cases to the health team. All field-based staff will receive information about available protection and response services to make safe and ethical referrals upon beneficiary request, to ensure the relay of information on access to services if/when asked during HH visits.Under Food Security activities, a comprehensive community engagement will be conducted to understand the implementation barriers and seek improved ways to reach the most vulnerable HHs. The IRC will ensure the participation of women and encourage women leaders to take part in the working committees to advocate the needs of women at grassroot level. A specific selection criterion will be included for disabled people and unaccompanied children to be included in the MPCA. At the end of each project, the Lesson Learned discussions will be conducted to capture the views of the local community and align program strategy with learnings. The WASH activities will be inclusive of feedback and participation from target beneficiaries, particularly women, including through focus group discussions and by ensuring their inclusion within the WUCs and as hand pump mechanics. The IRC will increase the registration and identification of persons with disabilities and the elderly to ensure that unique needs are addressed, while also strengthening existing community support structures for these groups, such as surrogate water collectors. Hygiene promotion activities will also include information on available services for survivors of SEA. Beneficiaries will also be made aware of the IRC’s Code of Conduct and opportunities for reporting to prevent and reduce the risk of SEA. The IRC will engage with the targeted refugees and local authorities at the camp level to explain its principles of impartiality and provide opportunities for complaint and redress through focus group discussions, community leader meetings, and suggestion boxes within supported health facilities. The IRC will conduct a needs assessment to further identify households that have people living with disabilities and elderly people to ensure the successful delivery of interventions, particularly regarding water collection and hygiene awareness. Through house-to-house visits, CHVs will also identify people with disabilities or special needs, conduct follow-ups, and ensure that their unique needs are being met.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The IRC adopts a client-centered approach that puts the target population at the heart of the project. All sectors will conduct regular feedback sessions with caregivers, women and girls within the safe spaces to ensure activities and services are adequately responding to their needs. Women, girls and parents will be involved in the decision-making regarding the location of safe spaces, latrines, water points, distribution points etc. Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with survivors of GBV on a voluntary basis. The IRC team will engage with women, girls, children, and caregivers from crisis-affected communities in planning and implementing strategies to prevent, mitigate and address the consequences of CP risks and GBV. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). Across all sectors, the IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including focus group discussions (FGDs), KIIs, feedback boxes, and a hotline, which will inform project design and implementation. The IRC also has a neutral department called Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, who is responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
