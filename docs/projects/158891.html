Project Summary

PCDCR targets affected children by conflict related violence & violations& child protection workers, namely the community-based organizations (CBOs)working on children protection in Jordan Valley (JV) in area C of West Bank(WB). This target results from urgent needs & bad psychosocial status of children in JV because of school drop-out, working in Israeli settlements, facing sexual harassments, suffering from health diseases & others, as stated in HNO2019 & other sources. The JV in area C of WB is targeted since it’s one of most critical areas in WB & people there particularly children face many protection challenges from occupation at different levels.Also, JV is highlighted in HNO2019,HRP2020,protection cluster due to increasing needs &funding shortfalls.The project targets the most affected 6areas in JV: Ein Shibli-Nablus governorate, AlAqaba, Kardala, Bardala, & Ein Elbeida-Tubas governorate, & AlAuja-Jericho governorate. PCDCR, member in Global Partnership to End Violence against Children, is located in Nablus & access all targeted locations & populations to provide its services. PCDCR’s previous experiences in targeting all WB particularly for children projects
As children in JV suffer from conflict related violations & violence & JV is one of the vulnerable areas for Palestinians, PCDCR targets children both males & females & CBOs in order to achieve complementary work to meet children’s need
PCDCR under this project aims at (Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent & mitigate the effects of the occupation & conflict related violence on children in JV). The project goal is achieved by attaining the following strategic objectives:
1.The wellbeing of children who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations is improved through PSS services
2.Child protection workers are empowered in MHPSS for children affected by conflict related violence & violations. Hence, PCDCR contributes to & align with HRP2020 objectives as well as protection cluster objectives related to children protection especially in areaC. To that end, the following interventions will be conducted:
1-Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support & programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children & families who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations(responsive). It includes individual & group counselling for 134 children(males & females) in need
2-Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support(MHPSS)(remedial).6 CBOs (24 male & female representatives)working on child protection in JV will be trained & empowered on guidelines on MHPSS. The guideline on MHPSS will be conducted & distributed to the participants
Protection & gender are mainstreamed under the project. PCDCR also has solid experiences in this filed & good coordination & partnerships. PCDCR accesses targeted areas & communities through:
1-CBOs: PCDCR has long experience with them as Ein Elbeida Women Center, Bardala Women Center,& others
2-Municipal & village councils: PCDCR has cooperated with Ein Shibli village council in conducting plans, trainings, meetings with different beneficiaries, & other activities related to women economic empowerment. PCDCR’s recently cooperated with AlAuja municipality on accountability & municipalities’ development
3-Schools: PCDCR’s cooperated with different schools in targeted locations so PCDCR can coordinate with schools-if needed- only to get lists of dropped out children or most affected children in targeted locations.No activities'll be inside schools; but they'll be in CBOs. PCDCR contacted Education Cluster coordinator & ensured that’s no need for coordinating with EC
PCDCR contacted Child Protection AoR/MHPSS coordinator during the proposal phase to consult him on the activities and ensure PCDCR as active member, & to exchange sources as Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action

1 - Needs

Palestinian children suffer from conflict-related violations particularly children living in Jordan Valley(JV). Based on HNO2019, children’re exposed to all elements of coercive environment & particularly affected by impaired access to education in areaC. Particularly, children in JV have faced an increase in overall grave violations & settler violence. HNO ensures that funding shortfalls increase the need for child protection &MHPSS interventions since humanitarian needs have increased particularly for children.HRP2018-2020also ensures existence of IHRL violations & conflict-related violence against children in area C includingJV. Palestinian JV constitutes25% of total area of WB& includes 52,000Palestinians according to an article on agriculture in Palestinian JV2018. Thus,it’s one of highest economic resources for its climate zones & agriculture nature. However, as JV is in area C, Palestinian face restrictions in accessing most lands there causing Palestinian labor in Israeli settlements; noting that there’re 35settlements in JV. Women & children are the most affected individuals in working in settlements. Palestinian children(males & females) inJV are the most affected group than other areas of WB. Above all, children in JV face many conflict related violations resulting in negative effects on their psychosocial status. According to officers in MoE as deputy director of Directorate of Education in Tubas, children in JV face severe risks & take long time while going to their school due to occupation, study in camps as occupation destroyed their schools, drop out of schools to work in Israeli settlement,&face abuse & harassment. All these cause devastating psychosocial effect on children.For PCBS2017,the highest levels of illiteracy were inJV particularly in North JV in Tubas with4,7% of illiteracy & South JV in Jericho5,5%. Based on article on AlHadath news2016, 5.5%of settlement workers in JV are children under16. Human Rights Watch2015 indicated that33 children out of38 (total sample for their report) stated following violations in JV: lower wages than other workers in WB, exposure to poisonous chemical materials without the required safety procedures or tools, no legal contract resulting in informal labour conditions; thus, no labour insurance. The sample suffered from forced school drop-out because of poor life conditions caused by Israeli control over the land & water. PCDCR contacted Directorate of Labor office in JV-Jericho. They indicate that children living in JV are forced to leave school & work in Israeli settlements where they face violence as: school drop-out, sexual harassments, drugs, & health diseases due to unhealthy work environment with unjust conditions.Percentage of children labor in Israeli settlement increases causing negative impact on their psychological & social wellbeing due to harsh living conditionsHence, children living in JV suffer from lack of receiving protection response interventions to prevent & mitigate the effects of occupation & conflict related violence.This problem is due to2 reasons;1-lack of psychosocial support(PSS) services for the affected children which let their psychosocial status getting worse;2-lack of capabilities of children protection workers on providing MHPSS in these areas. To meet these needs, PCDCR’ll participate in providing protection response mechanisms for affected children in JV through providing them with PSS services & empowering CBOs in providing MHPSS for them. PCDCR targets male & female children as they both suffer from the same occupation violence. PCDCR focuses on children due to annual increase in percentages of school drop-out, child labor, & violations against them. Such vulnerable group should be in schools not in labor market.Protection concerns’re considered in all stages to ensure safety for children, engaged parties & staff. PCDCR is member in Global Partnership to End Violence against Children & has children safeguard policy & code of conduct

2 - Activities

As PCDCR a local NGO capable of covering all West Bank to access targeted locations &population easily, the following activities will be implemented successfully.Hence, PCDCR will achieve project goal, contribute to the strategic objectives of HRP2020 & protection cluster objectives,under which our project is proposed, & meet HNO2019 needs. The following activities consider gender & age needs, & protection for all engaged entities:1.Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support & programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children & families who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations(Responsive).To that end, the following will be done:1.1.Training 10graduates of psychology (both males & females will gain equal opportunities) to qualify them as educators run the structured psychosocial support(PSS) group sessions(group counselling) for children in need for20 hours1.2.Holding 6preparatory meetings for targeted community-based organizations(CBOs) working on child protection to initiate the project & explain its goals along with their roles. It’ll be one meeting in each targeted CBO & 15participants (males & females) per meeting.1.3.Holding 4focus group discussions to assess the needs of children affected by occupation & conflict related violence & violations in JV. Thus, based on these discussions the specialized PSS interventions (both individual & group counselling) will be arranged appropriately. The breakdown of 4focus group discussions is:2group focus discussions for children (1 for each of male &female children); & 2group focus discussions for parents (one for each of women & men). Each focus group discussion includes 12participants1.4.Providing 84 children, who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations & dropped out of school or involved in labor, with specialized PSS services (group counselling). This will be done through groups. Hence, 12 groups of children (6 groups for males & 6 for females) will be targeted as follows: 2 groups in each location & 7 children in each group will receive four sessions of PSS. The PSS services (group counselling) will be run by the trained specialized educators for both female & male children1.5.Providing 50 children (25males;25females), who have been affected by conflict related violence & violations & dropped out of school or involved in labour, with specialized PSS (individual counselling).This will be done by psychosocial specialist at PCDCR premise. 1.6.Preparing & publishing 1000 copies of an educational brochure to be distributed on children (both males & females), who are affected by conflict violence & occupation. The brochure will reinforce the knowledge & services they receive in the PSS sessions (individual & group counselling)2.Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health & psychosocial support (Remedial);through the following sub-activities:2.1.Holding 6meetings for CBOs working on child protection to prepare them for the training;1 meeting for each CBO2.2.Holding 2central trainings for 6 cooperated CBOs working on child protection on the guidelines on MHPSS. Each central training includes 3 CBOs working on child protection & lasts for 20hours. Each CBO will have 4 representatives in the training (both males & females).2.3.Preparing, conducting, & printing 500copies of a guideline to be distributed on CBOs’ representatives who work on child protection & attend the training.PCDCR maintains protection as it’s member of Global Partnership to End Violence against Children, & considers international agreements on children protection & Do No Harm principles. PCDCR’s separate program for children rights, children safeguard policy, code of conduct, & specialized staff. Thus, PCDCR commits to implement its interventions without any harm for all beneficiaries & staff

PCDCR will maintain collecting feedback from engaged entities in the project as part of community engagement by different ways. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

This project is designed to achieve strategic objective #1 of oPt HRP2020 responding to 2019 HNO which considers the violations of IHL & IHRL in the oPt as the core of severe protection threats against Palestinians. HNO confirms that 350,000 people living in 67communities in West Bank(WB), as areaC including Jordan Valley(JV), vulnerable to settler violence, particularly children. Accordingly, the proposed interventions’ll contribute in enhancing protection by respecting human rights in accordance with IHL & IHRL, demanding accountability, & mitigating violations impact.Thus, the proposed project aims at protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent & mitigate the effects of the occupation & conflict related violence on children in JV. PCDCR looks for responding to children’s needs through providing PSS services & empowering CBOs working on children issues by training them on guidelines of MHPSS. Hence, children realize their full potential & abilities to live with dignity, honor & respect. The project promotes protection through PSS that is one of self-protection mechanisms. This can be achieved by the project activities that focus on structured psychosocial support services for male & female children including (individual & group counseling). The interventions considers gender & age disaggregation of affected children. The project management’ll prioritize safety & dignity of beneficiaries by providing services to vulnerable groups (affected children in JV) confidently & privately. So, those targeted groups are encouraged to receive this kind of support without being stigmatized. Whereas, each member of project staff will be committed to PCDCR child protection policies besides signing children code of conduct. Also, each CBO in the 6 locations in JV area C of WB will sign code of conduct to promote safety, dignity, & confidentiality of beneficiaries’ data.In addition, PCDCR’ll ensure & commit to implement its proposed interventions without any harm for all beneficiaries & staff as PCDCR considers Do No Harm principles & has a separate program for children protection rights & one for democracy & human rights under which protection must be maintained . Even in some cases where there is a need to move the beneficiaries to safer place, PCDCR’ll take the required procedures to do so & maintain safe places.Furthermore, PCDCR considers the child protection standards under this humanitarian response as follows:1- Ensuring quality of child protection response through:1.1.Coordination with CBOs,NGOs, OCHA, municipal & village councils & others1.2.Human resources. PCDCR’ll hire a qualified staff for the project to invest their capacities & capabilities. They’ll sign & commit to the PCDCR’s child safeguarding policy & respect international child protection laws & agreements1.3.Project’s cycle management will be directed towards mainly protecting all targeted groups particularly children & ensures quality of service provision. It’ll also tackle any challenges or violations against child rights at national or international laws1.4.Information management for information exchange with partners & enriching PCDCR database. Thus, PCDCR’ll provide its further services depending on the needs of collected & managed information, measure quality of service provision, & develop them as needed. PCDCR’ll also maintain the privacy of children as it has internal procedures to prevent any abuse for children related information as names, photos,.etc1.5.PCDCR’ll monitor child protection through designing the project, monitoring the planning, & implementing of project activities2-PCDCR develops adequate child protection strategies through community based mechanisms. It’ll integrate CBOs’ efforts to train & empower them on guidelines on MHPSS due to their significant role for the local community as local references. The community based mechanisms are highlighted to ensure more protection & care for children in their environments

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PCDCR adopts the principles of humanitarian action including equality and impartiality. Hence, PCDCR will ensure & enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services by the targeted vulnerable groups (children) even persons with disabilities & CBOs. This target depends mainly on the needs and priority to the most urgent cases of distress. The children will be reached through coordinating with local and village councils and CBOs in order to target the most conflict-affected beneficiaries in need without any discrimination on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class or political opinions. Accordingly, PCDCR will coordinate with the local entities in the targeted locations to identify the most affected children who are in need. Additionally, PCDCR has a MoU with the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Social Development; thus, the PCDCR could cooperate with the ministries to reach the children in need referring to their documents and records. Furtherly, PCDCR will provide individual MHPSS (individual counselling) as needed for children and referring to their priorities only regardless of their backgrounds.
