Project Summary

Part 1: Nutrition surveillance system generates evidence-based information
To extending The routine nutrition surveillance program provides up-to-date regular information on GAM , SAM , MAM , MAM within PLWs, Stunting at burden communities and supports mapping of interventions considering the “need of children and PLWs ”. about 3% of our target are with disabilities.
Community and facility-based surveillance are planned to scale up to cover all Sub-Districts in the Northwest of Syria.
With the assumption that the UNSCR for cross border humanitarian support is renewed for 2021 which will provide the necessary humanitarian space to continue service delivery
Part 2: Activation of 15 Rapid Response Team provides lifesaving essential integrated health, nutrition and child protection field services. And 3 mother and Baby areas (MBAs)
Part 3: All the teams of surveillance and RRT will disseminate awareness messages on Covid-19 to the visited households.
Part 4: Ensure compliance with five key messages on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) ,Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV).
Part 5: UNICEF as they are the main pipeline managing agency in North West Syria and to ensure no break in the pipeline is happening , PAC will receive the supplies based on annual and quarterly forecast and in accordance to the estimated needs among mothers and children
Part 6: Effective and efficient coordination and information management for nutrition cluster (salaries and other cost of the cluster co-coordinator and IMO).
This is to support the nutrition cluster’s activities through allocating Information officer and cluster co-coordinator
Part 7: Conducting SMART survey to assess the nutritional status and anemia among Hosts and IDPs in NWS by targeting children and women 15 - 49 years. and to assess infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices among primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months.

4 - Needs

According to Syria HNO 2021 the poor nutrition situation in northwest Syria continues, with increases in acute and chronic malnutrition (stunting) rates among children. The high prevalence of stunting in children aged 6-59 months and acute malnutrition in pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are of primary concern. The prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased from 19 percent to 33 percent (35 percent for boys, 31 percent for girls) between May 2019 and September 2020, while the prevalence of acute malnutrition increased from 1 percent to 2 percent over the same period. For PLW, proxy prevalence of acute malnutrition across northwest Syria is at 11 percent. There are also significant differences across northwest Syria. The proxy prevalence of acute malnutrition was higher in Idleb compared to Aleppo among children aged 6-59 months (4 percent vs. 1 percent) and PLW (20 percent vs. 2 percent) and was higher among displaced persons for both children aged 6-59 months and PLW. As well Some 4.1 million people in need in Northwest Syria. 1.6 million people live in1,376 camps/sites in northwest Syria (80% women and children). Economic crisis is inhibiting individuals’ independent access to vital supplies. The project aims to fill the selected/complement identified gaps for the period of the allocation in key geographical areas ( Daret Azza/ Dana/ Idleb/ Darkosh/ Salqin / Bennsh / KafrTakharim/ Teftnaz). In order to prevent and decrease the malnutrition rates within peoplein need especially boys and girls U5 and PLW. And responding to IDPs Children andmothers in Northwest Syria by scaling up Rapid response teams and referral mechanisms.

Registering all screened cases and reporting to the nutritionists and data entry team member 6. Distribute micro-nutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women 7. Monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes 8. Capacity building for health workers. 13. supporting the coordination in Nutrition cluster . 14. supporting and managing the nutrition supplies for all cluster partners. 15. supporting the surveillance system in both “community and facility” 16. conducting SMART nutrition survey 17. Disseminating awareness messages for Covid-19.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- For this risk , the SOPs applied in all projects activities should be activated once this risk happened. 2- safety policy which prioritizes the staff and beneficiaries 3- regularly convened a wide range of stakeholders to share knowledge and build evidence on what works to address violence against women and girls and to take actions 4- ensuring that governing bodies are made fully aware of any serious incidents and are accountable for ensuring appropriate investigation, reporting and response. 5- changing the locations according the donor's requirements. 6- Accountability to affected populations (AAP) will be strengthened, as well as due diligence and remote monitoring mechanisms. 7- maximizes the responses capacity ,Resource Mobilization and advocacy for the case. 8- Incident Reports to donors and other and activating the SOPs which related to safety and evacuation During implementation of the proposed project activities, ensure community interests are respected, and project objectives and activities are transparent and understood by the local population. The beneficiaries and affected population will have direct access to share their feedback and complaints. In addition, PAC staff will conduct regular visits and monitor project activities in the target communities to ensure smooth implementation and liaise with community members. The beneficiary committees and PAC staff together will provide adequate information to the beneficiaries and affected population, and ensure that the project objectives are well explained. PAC staff will ensure compliance with five key messages on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and on Prevention of SexualExploitation and Abuse (PSEA).
