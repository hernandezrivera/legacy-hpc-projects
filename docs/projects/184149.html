Project Summary

According to the latest findings of 2021 post Gu results of FSNAU, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) at the national level has remained at a serious level of 11.1 per cent weight-for-height z-score. Based on this analysis and the latest population figures, the cluster estimates that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of 5 years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services urgently. Out of this, 295,515 children under the age of 5 are likely to be severely malnourished and around 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM). In addition to this, around 245,909 PLWs will be at risk of acute malnutrition. A total of 463,204 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance. In this regard, it is estimated that around 629,567 children between 6 to 23 months and 283,305 PLWs will be at greater risk as well. The total number of IDPs that need nutrition services will be around 0.3 million including women and children. IDPs face numerous challenges in accessing nutrition services and as per the recent estimates the GAM rates in IDPs of Mogadishu and Jowhar are 16.5 per cent and 17.2 per cent respectively. Hence, combined the total people in need for both curative and preventive services for nutrition in 2022 will be 2.37 million including IDPs and non-IDPs. In terms of inequities, there are population groups that bear the brunt of malnutrition disproportionately. These disparities could be gender and disabilities related, financial and belonging to minorities’ clans and being an IDP. According to a Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) study on malnutrition cases in Somalia, 53.9% of children under 5 suffer from anemia. One-third of children have iron deficiency anemia (IDA) and vitamin A deficit, increasing their risk of infection and mortality. IDA among women is also in excess of 30%, increasing the risk of low birth weight babies and birth complications (Somalia Micronutrient Survey 2019). Over half the women in the FSNAU study suffered from Vitamin A deficiency, while 49.1% pregnant women and 46.6% non-pregnant women of reproductive age suffered from anemia and contribute to perpetuating the cycle of malnutrition as malnourished girls as they are at higher risk of giving birth to infants who are small for gestational age and/or of low birth weight. Among the various underlying factors, food insecurity remains a major driver of malnutrition in Somalia. Based on the latest projections by FSNAU, from October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. It is therefore estimated that around 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes between October and December 2021.
In view of the above, Aid Vision proposes One-year quality and inclusively lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition care services aimed at supporting communities in Shangani district of Banadir Region, Warsheikh and Balcad Districts of Middle Shabelle Region.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Aid Vision will ensure all minority and majority as well as people living with disabilities are involved in the implementation of the project and be able to be transparent in terms of service delivery and employment of Community nutrition workers. Training will also consider gender sensitivity - equal representation of men and women. All this will ensure gender and equity issues are addressed among the community members. Aid Vision will collect gender disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to Nutrition activities to ensure they have equal access to quality nutrition care services. Aid Vision will ensure that all segments of the affected population including pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months have equal access to Nutrition assistance. The minority and marginalized communities who are at risk of being neglected/ excluded are also given quality and equal nutrition care services to prevent malnutrition. Aid Vision will also advocate community participation in the implementation of the program. This will help in getting complaints and feedback responses that enhance effective decision making and promote accountability to the affected population Aid Vision proposed project is designed in way that prioritizes the rights, safety and dignity of the beneficiaries. The target communities are fully involved and aware of the roles of each stakeholder where each sees the other as partners and equals. The proposed project will not discriminate against any beneficiary and will enable equal and impartial assistance and services for all. Targeting of vulnerable children and adults will be done on basis of their specific needs. The staff will be inducted on their duties and responsibilities and particularly the rights of the beneficiaries to ensure there are no any form of patient exploitation at the facilities and Aid Vision offices. Watertight mechanisms will be put in place to ensure served populations voices are heard and can file their complaints without being victimized.
