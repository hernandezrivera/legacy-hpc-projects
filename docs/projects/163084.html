Project Summary

Zimbabwe faces the prospect of another devastating humanitarian crisis, which is likely to have a disproportionately negative impact on school infrastructure, teaching and learning processes, as well as the physical and mental well -being of educators and learners. UNICEF has renewed its commitment to meet the urgent humanitarian needs of those affected and ensure that learners have safe and secure learning environments that promote their protection, physical, social, emotional and cognitive well-being and readiness to participate in learning. Against a total of 1,204,043 people in need, the Education cluster is targeting 853,032 pre-primary and primary school-aged beneficiaries in the 33 districts. UNICEF will support 333,841 boys and girls in 1,252 schools in the most vulnerable communities in 14 districts (10 districts with severity rankings of 5 and 3 districts: Buhera, Chimanimani and Makoni, which were affected by Cyclone Idai as well as Bulawayo, where urban poverty is prevalent). It is hoped that UNICEF-supported interventions will enable children, especially the vulnerable or those who are excluded from the education system, to access equitable and inclusive quality education opportunities in line with the Core Commitments to Children.
Recognizing that an effective humanitarian response depends on robust education systems, UNICEF will also support the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education (MoPSE) to strengthen the sector’s coordination capacity and enhance the effective delivery of quality education services. This will help contribute to a timely and well-coordinated sector response. The following is a list of the proposed interventions as part of the humanitarian response process:
1. Support trainings on Disaster Risk Reduction and Psychosocial Support for MoPSE personnel at national and sub-national level;
2. Support trainings for the education cluster to strengthen the sector’s capacity for coordination, integration and enhance efficient and effective delivery of quality education services;
3. Support to MoPSE to collect and disseminate real time data using an SMS-based survey (RapidPro) to understand the status and needs of learners and educators and schools affected and enhance response times and interventions.
4. Provide Emergency School Improvement Grants for minor repairs to damaged infrastructure or the temporary procurement of inputs for school feeding; and
5. Provide temporary housing and temporary learning spaces (tents) for affected teachers and learners, respectively;
6. Provide teaching and learning materials (textbooks; School-in-a-box kits; ECD kits and recreational learning kits) to minimize the cost burden on parents.

1 - Activities

UNICEF will conduct the following activities to strengthen the system and respond directly to the humanitarian needs of affected learners and educators: a) Provide Emergency School Improvement Grants to reduce the burden of the socio-economic crisis on schools; b) Support trainings for the education cluster to strengthen the sector’s capacity for coordination and integration and enhance efficient and effective delivery of quality education services;a) Provide temporary housing and temporary learning spaces (tents) for affected teachers and learners, respectively; b) Provide teaching and learning materials (textbooks; School-in-a-box kits; ECD kits and recreational learning kits) to minimize the cost burden on parents; c) Support national and sub-national level trainings on Disaster Risk Reduction and Psychosocial Support for MoPSE personnel, learners and community members; d) Support to MoPSE to collect and disseminate real time data using an SMS-based survey (RapidPro) to understand the status and needs of learners and educators and schools affected and enhance response times and interventions.

2 - Indicators

• Number of persons (male and female) trained;• Number of schools in humanitarian situations receiving UNICEF-supported Emergency School Improvement Grants;• Number of children (boys and girls) benefiting from UNICEF-supported school feeding;• Number of schools supported with UNICEF-supported temporary learning spaces; and• Number of schools and learners receiving UNICEF-supported supplies per year.
