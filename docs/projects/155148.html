Project Summary

The project aims to provide sustainable WASH facilities and services for affected populations in Cox Bazar, designed, implemented and managed in collaboration with communities. It's purpose is to support a transition from emergency WASH to more sustainable systems, foster community ownership and engagement with these systems and services and foster social cohesion through joint management and increased service provision for both Rohingya and host communities.

Objectives

S01 - Ensure effective, sufficient and continuous provision of life saving water and sanitation services for targeted men, women, boys and girlS02 - Ensure that all targeted women, men, girls and boys have the means and are encouraged to adopt individual and collective measures increasing health seeking behaviours to mitigate public health risksS03 - Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes protection, safety and dignity of targeted men, women, boys and girls with focus on sustainable resilience-building approaches.

Needs

S01 - Ensure effective, sufficient and continuous provision of life saving water and sanitation services for targeted men, women, boys and girls • Approximately 20% of the population still report issues with water points, noting that they are either too far away or there are queues (REACH, 2018). Oxfam’s Social Architecture Report showed women in particular face difficulties in accessing water points due to distance and harassment at the water point. • Likewise, at one time, only 70% of water points and 82% of latrines are functional, meaning that issues of access and with ensuring maintenance of minimum standards is still problematic. • Whilst water quality testing shows that ground water sources are mostly free from faecal contamination, contamination increases exponentially further down the water chain, with gross levels of contamination at household levels, and only 38% of households reported treating their water before drinking (REACH, 2018). More failsafe methods of ensuring water quality further down the water chain are required to improve health outcomes relating to water borne diseases. S02 - Ensure that all targeted women, men, girls and boys have the means and are encouraged to adopt individual and collective measures increasing health seeking behaviours to mitigate public health risks • 27% of households still report facing problems accessing basic hygiene items such as soap (REACH, 2018); Whilst Oxfam’s KAP shows that 82% of people reported handwashing with soap as a practice, our findings corroborate REACH’s in that much fewer numbers could name 3 key times for handwashing. • 30% of women face issues in accessing products to manage their menstrual periods. Combined with the issues of accessing facilities described below, this poses a significant issue for women and adolescent girls in the camp. S03 - Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes protection, safety and dignity of targeted men, women, boys and girls with focus on sustainable resilience-building approaches. • In Oxfam’s most recent Protection Monitoring report, a staggering 54% of people noted that WASH was the main source of conflict in the camps, and accessing different types of WASH facilities (water points, latrines and bathing areas) were noted as being amongst the top four risks in people’s lives (Oxfam, Nov. 2018)• Shame – including a lack of segregated facilities – was a major concern for both women and men, highlighted in the Protection Monitoring Report and the Social Architecture report. Recent studies undertaken by UNHCR, SGBV Sub-working group and Oxfam assessing preferences on bathing facilities, highlighted that household level facilities are preferred to maintain privacy. • A lack of lighting at WASH facilities was highlighted in being a significant barrier to accessing facilities at night, with women and adolescent girls fearing violence and sexual assault. Only 2% of latrines across the camps currently have a source of lighting (REACH, 2018)• These figures corroborate that there is a significant gap in adapting facilities based on community feedback to mitigate concerns and issues that cause people to feel unsafe and undignified.
