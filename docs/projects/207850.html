Project Summary

The RISE project specifically targets 1200 learners between the ages of 3-14, with a significant emphasis on promoting girls' education, aiming for a 55% participation rate. The implementation of this project will take place in Camp- 4. PRANTIC has established all 12 Learning Centers in RC and 4 Digital Learning Centers (DLC) in HC. Apprantly need not any construction.
The RISE project aims to provide access to education for 960 learners including 528 girls in Rohingya Community (RC) and 240 learners including 132 girls in Host Community (HC) at the age of 3-14 , will follow the Myanmar Curriculum approved by the Education Sector and HC respectively. Among the total number of learners, 3% CWD will be included in the learning centers. By implementing this initiative, PRANTIC aims to foster an environment that promotes learning, empowering Rohingya and Host community children with knowledge and skills that will have a profound impact on their overall well-being in future.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya response is facing a severe funding crisis, which has resulted in limited or suspended aid, further worsening an already desperate situation. Consequently, the Rohingya community has been compelled to resort to negative coping mechanisms. Instead of receiving an education, school-age children are being forced into income-generating activities to support their families. Furthermore, young girls are being subjected to early marriages, depriving them of their right to a childhood and a promising future. The lack of access to education for Rohingya children will have profound and long-lasting repercussions on the future generation of Rohingya. Their overall well-being, opportunities, and potential for a brighter future will be significantly compromised if their educational needs are not adequately addressed. In response to these pressing challenges, the RISE project has been meticulously designed to guarantee quality education for as many Rohingya children as possible while maintaining cost-effectiveness.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The Prantic Unnayan Society Education Program in Ukhiya, Cox's Bazar, aligns with the suggested actions in the 2023 MSNA Plan Guidance Note on Protection Mainstreaming. The project incorporates protection measures into various aspects of its implementation to ensure the well-being and safety.PRANTIC recognizes the importance of female representation and will make efforts to recruit female staff members both within the project and the organization as a whole. This step aims to enhance female participation and ensure diverse perspectives. To establish effective feedback mechanisms and address concerns, PRANTIC will introduce a complaint/advice box. This box will serve as a confidential channel for beneficiaries to voice their complaints, provide advice, and make suggestions. The implementation of this approach aligns with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Safeguarding policies ensuring a safe and respectful environment for all.To maintain community engagement and receive feedback on project activities, PRANTIC will continue involving parents and local leaders such as imams, Head Majhis, and sub-Majhis in the evaluation of learners. Their participation provide valuable insights and strengthen community ownership of the project. Moreover, PRANTIC will collect information from the complaint/advice box allowing beneficiaries to share their thoughts, concerns, and recommendations. Here is how the project addresses this cross-cutting issues;• Community-Based Surveillance and Monitoring by the CESG• Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)• Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR)• Volunteer Team for Education-related Awareness to Parents and students• Observation of National and International DaysIn brief, Prantic Unnayan Society Education Program demonstrates a comprehensive approach to protection mainstreaming by integrating protection measures across various components of the project.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The RISE project aims to provide access to education for 960 (80X12) students including 528 girls in Rohingya Community (RC) & 240 (60X4) students including 132 girls in Host Community (HC). Students in the 5-14 age group will follow the Myanmar curriculum approved by the Education Sector. PRANTIC is dedicated to promoting inclusivity and ensuring the participation of persons with disabilities (PWD) and children with disabilities (CWD) during the implementation of the project. As part of this commitment, PRANTIC will strive to include a minimum of 5% of PWD and CWD within the project, based on their availability within the targeted population. By actively including PWD and CWD in the project, PRANTIC aims to provide them with equal opportunities and access to the project's activities, services, and benefits. This inclusive approach recognizes the rights and unique needs of individuals with disabilities and seeks to create a supportive and empowering environment that promotes their well-being and development. PRANTIC will ensure that appropriate measures and accommodations are in place to facilitate the participation and engagement of PWD and CWD. This may include providing accessible infrastructure, adaptive learning materials, specialized support, and targeted interventions to meet their specific requirements.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Prantic Unnayan Society strongly feels that the impacted people should have the chance to raise concerns, offer input, and receive a response, so it made sure that this initiative has the appropriate mechanisms in place. The gathered comments from the impacted populations will be examined during project implementation, and necessary measures will be implemented on a regular basis. In this project, possible PSEA hazards in program delivery contexts will be evaluated and mitigating actions will be determined. PRANTIC also provides copies of the PSEA Code of Conduct to all employees, as well as other safety precautions, and reminds them of their responsibilities. Through the project outcomes framework, the project focus is tasked with keeping an eye on AAP-related outputs and indicators. In order to ensure active engagement and a successful community feedback system by the facility, PRANTIC also includes affected populations in the monitoring process to ensure community feedback through facility based CESG & Parents’ meeting. PRANTIC also collect feedback in ‘Complaint & Suggestion box’ in each of learning center.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. In order to reduce the dangers of future disasters, Prantic Unnayan Society planted a suitable number of trees on the boundary of the Learning Centers and took into account the usual flooding and landslides when building the facilities. PRANTIC took all kind of initiatives for any kind of incidents and climate disaster. All Learning centers are well equipped with kits and have DRR plan to protect & survive from disasters. The LCs are used as Safe Havens for the disaster affected victims. Before monsoon appears, PRANTIC teams tie down all the facilities taking preparation to cover up the disaster.
