Project Summary

The intervention presents a double aim. On one side, it works towards reinforcing the production capacity of vulnerable farmers and livestock owners residing in IDPs settlements and rural villages in the Badhaade district in Lower Juba to ensure the sustaining of life in a region hit hardly by recurrent droughts and political instability and violence. On the other side, it aims to provide immediate, lifesaving support to vulnerable households in hard-to-reach areas whose livelihood and access to food has been affected by the current drought situation. This support will be provided through Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance to IDPs, returnees and host community households both in Urban and Rural setting. The intervention will focus on the most vulnerable among the households whose livelihood opportunities are gravely affected, giving priority to Households from minority groups, women-led Households, and Households with members as People Living With Disabilities. The approach aims at ensuring the application of the principles of Protection Mainstreaming in every aspect of the intervention, contributing to prevention, support, and safeguarding of people at risk and vulnerable under different kinds of threats while ensuring the improvement of their life conditions, access to livelihood opportunities and better food security and stability. In such way, the three identified priority areas for centrality of protection approach in Somalia will be targeted, as it has been done by AVSI in its previous interventions in the area. AVSI will pay specific attention to the overlapping risks and situations of discrimination among the targeted communities, which are involved directly in every phase of the project, and whose contribution will also ensure the correct identification of the people most in need. Particular attention will be provided to vulnerable IDPs, refugee communities and also returnees, working towards a reduction of the risks of conflicts, favouring resettlement and longer-term, durable solutions. In this way, the project will contribute to the maintenance of a favourable protection environment in the targeted region, taking in account the multi-layered nature of the crisis, and working towards the reinforcement of the resilience capacities and internal support systems of the affected groups. 1. - Projects activities

Distribution of farming Kits to 700 farmers.- 700 farmers will receive an integrated farming kit including seeds, fertilizers, farming tools, PICS BagsSupply of pesticide and multi vitamins treatments for farm animals.- Provision of multivitamin and anti-parassitary treatment to 6000 heads of livestock with the involvement of staff from Jubaland’s Ministry of Livestock.Provision of 20 Donkeys with Carts.- Provision of 20 donkeys with carts to the targeted communities to improve their access to markets and transportation of their produce (harvest) goods and mothers, children and elderly people in need of urgent medical attention.Training of farmers on Agricultural best practices and financial literacy- Training of 700 farmers on adaptative practices of agriculture in face of climate change with inclusion of financial literacy and management.Training of livestock owners on livestock management and climate change adaptation.- Training of 600 farmers on good livestock management and climate change adaptation practicesMulti-purpose Cash Assistance to vulnerable households.- Provision of monthly MPCA to 1000 vulnerable households (average of 6 members per household) for 3 months. The limited time frame for this activity is based on the guidelines for emergency cash assistance provided by the Cash Working Group. The intervention is aiming to provide life-saving support to families in conditions of extreme vulnerability, in order to avoid excessive reliance on cash provision but focus on providing immediate relief. However, the project aims to maintain flexibility and adapt to changed conditions on the field, possibly increasing the time frame of the intervention based on assessment of the situation and contextual needs.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The distribution of cash instead of direct in-kind support is justified by the experience and knowledge of the context gathered so far in the targeted areas. AVSI has recorded improvement in beneficiary monthly income, expenditure on food, reduced debt which are both indications of improvement in the general well-being of the beneficiaries. The crisis affecting the targeted households is layered and multifaceted, with specific needs for each household. The provision of MPCA will allow each family to independently provide to their needs.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project design kept in consideration the indications provided by the National Cash Working Group, adapting the amount to also the additional strain put on households by the current crisis situation. The design was also based on the evaluation of previous projects with a cash component, which provided guidance on best practices.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

For Risk 4.4 - Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors, AVSI Foundation will apply its strict security measures, which are based on precise collection of information from the field, tight collaboration with partners, communities and local authorities in order to prevent increasing the situation of risk for the people involved. The distribution activities will be carefully planned and will ensure the proper outreach of the beneficiaries and the security of the whole operation, including in areas with heightened risks, where involvement of local contacts and partners will allow the minimum risk in reaching to those areas.For Risk 4.5 - Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area, the direct involvement of the whole community will ensure that all the activities involving issues of land ownership and tenure will be avoided in the planning phase of the implementation. The selection of beneficiaries will be based on a participatory approach that will allow ownership and accountability towards the communities. We do not foresee heightened risks connect to contamination by explosive hazards in the area, but clearance with security authorities and community will be ensured.
