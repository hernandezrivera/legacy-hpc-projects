Needs

Comprising around 40 percent of the Libyan population, children have been among the most impacted by the ongoing conflict. Violations of international human rights and humanitarian law are common and violence against children is prevalent throughout Libya. A 2017 UNICEF-NCDC study on violence against children found increased levels of gender-based violence as well as other forms of abuse and violence in conflict-affected regions. The overwhelming majority of children, 91% (93% of boys and 90% of girls) reported having been exposed to at least one type of violence at school and home. 85% of children (87% of boys, and 83% of girls) reported experiences of any type of violence at school, and almost three-quarters of children, 74% (79% of boys, and 69% of girls), reported experiences of any type of violence at home. 10% of children (16% of boys and 5%of girls) reported ever having experienced any type of violence at the hands of police or militia. Children with disabilities were more likely to report being exposed to physical and emotional violence by adults both at home and at school. Children are at-risk from mines and unexploded ordnance, the high prevalence of weapons across the country, a fragmentation of families and communities, and a critical breakdown of basic services, including health and education. Children who have been exposed to armed conflict and instability (including children on the move) are in critical need of psychosocial support, safe environment and spaces to play, learn and be supported.Limited supportive child protection and family welfare services within communities, including more focused specialized services for vulnerable children affected by armed conflict, continue to be a major gap and need. Moreover, the presence and control of militias within communities in which children live, increases risk of association with armed groups. Recruitment and use of children violates their rights and causes them physical, developmental, emotional, mental, and spiritual harm. Children who return may also face a number of challenges - to face an uncertain future and a further fight for acceptance from their family and community. There is lack of social, and in some cases economic, opportunities for adolescents/youth to engage positively among themselves, and to make a meaningful and positive contribution to their families and communities., children are at risk of death and injury and other grave violations. UNICEF foresees a niche in the release and re-integration of children associated with armed groups as an entry point for strengthening capacity to deliver child protection services and policy advocacy on child rights. A more significant investment on the Children Affected by Armed Conflict (CAAC) monitoring work is significantly needed to strengthen the monitoring, reporting and response to grave child rights violations. There is also a need to strengthening inter-agency coordination, leadership and advocacy in the area of child protection, and undertake child protection needs assessment.

Activities

1. Community-based child protection and psychosocial support services, including but not limited to safe spaces and case management for vulnerable children, including children on the move, children with disabilities, as well as rehabilitation and reintegration of children associated with armed groups and other vulnerable conflict affected and displaced children. 2. Specialized child protection and gender based violence services: Provision of specialized child protection services to survivors of all types of violence, including GBV, and close collaboration with other sectors through established referral pathways and strengthening of child protection sub-sector coordination. 3. Awareness raising in increased capacity of local actors and community members on key child protection issues including child recruitment, GBV, discrimination and violence against children, Mine Risk Education ,access to services that prevent and respond to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect. 4. Skills development and capacity strengthening of social workers and other child protection services providers, including to undertake case management and referral for extremely vulnerable children, including children associated with armed groups and other children affected by armed conflict. 5. Strengthening the monitoring, reporting and response to grave child rights violations. - The Child Protection programme will contribute with Youth and Education components to ensure the integration of the most vulnerable youth and Children (including migrant youth, including adolescents, and children associated with armed conflict) have access to life skill training and to TVET through a protective and inclusive environment. - UNICEF will need dedicated coordination capacity in Libya to ensure to widen partnerships and ensure a more strategic, well-coordinated and effective response across the country. Coordination helps to make sure that child protection responses are prioritized, efficient, predictable and effective, hence the criticality of this project. This project covers both the staff costs related to the coordination function, including information management, as well as the costs of key strategic activities that are related to the six core coordination functions, namely: i) support service delivery based on needs; ii) inform strategic decision-making through needs assessments and gaps analysis; iii) plan and implement cluster strategies; iv) monitor and evaluate performance; v) build national capacities; and vi) support advocacy efforts in coordination with existing platforms. UNICEF through the supplies department purchase all supplies needed for the intervention and not through implementing partners to ensure quality and meeting UNICEF procurement policies. Supplies includes recreational kits, Mine Risk Education materials and other needed kits.
