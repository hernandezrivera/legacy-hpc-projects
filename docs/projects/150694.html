Project Summary

The provision of equitable and cost-effective services to vulnerable communities residing in Area C, Seam Zone and East Jerusalem remains a challenge in the presence of fragmented geographical area, scattered refugee population, checkpoints, physical barrier, and restrictions on movement of staff and patients as well as the deteriorated socioeconomic situation. This is on the augmentation of the crawling of settlements, demolitions, displacements and other Israeli measures that hampered the Palestinian’s health and hindered the provision of basic services by health care providers. Palestinian communities in Area C are among the most vulnerable in the West Bank. Around 224,627 people in need for mobile health services residing in 158 communities located in Area C, and an estimated of 30,000 Palestinian Bedouin and herders where identified as acutely vulnerably. Israel maintains control over Area C and Palestinian movement is restricted by a complex system of physical and administrative obstacles. These restrictions on freedom of movement combined with Israeli settlement development severely undermines Palestinian livelihoods and access to basic services. The mobile health clinic project objective is to mitigate the impact of the crisis on health services for vulnerable people by providing access to mobile health clinics in communities facing access and movement restrictions. The project will target 7 communities in Area C in the South area of WB, Palestine. Total catchment population is around 6,370 including women, men, girls, boys and elderly. Locations are: Al-Rawa’in Bedouin community, Arab Al-Rashaydeh, Arab Al-Rashaydeh Al Barriyeh, and Wadi Fukin in Bethlehem Governorate, and Najada and um Al Daraj, Beit Mirsim and Um Al Kheir in Hebron Governorate. Whereas access to primary health care is a humanitarian need among all segments of the population, specific groups are the most impacted such as women, children, persons with disabilities and elderly
On top of the Israeli restrictions on movement, women and girls sometimes face social barriers in accessing basic services due to social restrictions on their mobility. Women often bear the main responsibility for care in the family, and the burden of additional travel and time to seek care for children and elderly family members who fall ill or require follow up for chronic conditions. In addition, women sometimes suffer from a lack of adequate ante natal and reproductive health services, which are very important to lower the risk of complications and death for both mother and baby. The project will contribute to the first main objective of the health system in Palestine that Palestinians to enjoy their rights to critical services and assistance, and to the cluster objectives: - ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building
MdM-CH decided to opt for direct implementation, in order to ensure the highest quality in the services provided. The project team will be recruited and managed by MdM-CH. Acceptance and participation of the communities at all stages of a project’s lifecycle is a cornerstone of MdM-CH’s approach. Thanks to its 25-year international experience in the implementation of projects and more specifically its experience of running operations embedded within local communities in Palestine, MdM-CH has developed methodologies and tools for CwC that will help guarantee continued acceptance for its services (https://www.coordinationsud.org/wp-content/uploads/Travailler-avec-les-communaut%C3%A9s-M%C3%A9decins-du-Monde-2013.pdf).
To ensure access to the communities in question and maximize the impact of the intervention, so far, MdM-CH conducted a preliminary assessment in each location, during which the team exchanged with village councils and UNRWA focal persons. Key members of the community with whom to build a relationship based on trust were identified. Collaboration and close coordination with community representatives and MoH regional PHCC’s, mobile clinics and hospitals will be maintained throughout the project.
MdM-CH will provide the MoH endorsed primary health care package, including screening and treatment and/or referral for communicable and non-communicable diseases. In addition, MdM-CH will provide specialized services as regards Sexual and Reproductive Health (ante natal and post-natal care, family planning, awareness sessions), emergency vaccinations and mental health following MdM medical tools and protocols for SRH interventions. (https://issuu.com/medecinsdumonde/docs/guide_sant___sexuelle_et_reproducti)
The mobile clinic will provide services to each targeted community at least 4 times per month, according to a monthly schedule.

Needs

Palestinian communities in Area C are among the most vulnerable in the West Bank out of which 114,000 people across 100 communities, are located in Area C, Hebron H2 and “Seam Zone”, deprived of essential primary healthcare services. These communities face several barriers to accessing primary healthcare and emergency services, in particular the acute vulnerable like elderly people, pregnant and lactating women, children, and people with disabilities .Besides Area C, Palestinians residing in the Seam Zone are also among the most vulnerable in the West Bank due to their isolation between the Green Line and the Barrier . Plans are being advanced by the Government of Israel to transfer and resettle Palestinian Bedouin communities living in rural locations throughout the central West Bank in Area C and to concentrate them into three urbanized townships planned and designed by the Israeli Civil Administration. These communities, many of whom are refugees, pursue a traditional lifestyle of herding and have suffered a serious decline in living conditions in recent years in addition to increased protection threats, especially the risk of forced displacement. There are increasing restrictions on their access to land, natural resources and markets for their products while they often live in very basic structures, lack community infrastructure and public transportations and have limited access to health and other essential services. In addition to that although the number of PHCC has increase the last years, it is outlined according the MoH national health strategy of 2017-2022 that in terms of geographic distribution and coverage different districts and especially marginalized areas in area C remain completely exposed to health hazard (only 4 mobile clinics of MoH covering Area C, reflecting only 0,9% of the total Primary health clinics). As a result, 100 communities, with a population of 113,590 are without access to basic primary healthcare services. To all the above we should underline the increase gap due to the closure of UNRWA’s existing six mobile clinics serving 39 communities in Area C.Moreover, the limited access to land and livelihoods affects the economic resources, which in turn also affects at risk communities and Palestinians ability to access healthcare, including medicine which carry further economic burden for the more exposed and vulnerable: pregnant women and patients suffering from NCDs. National health indicators demonstrate that NCDs accounting for the largest proportion of morbidity and mortality in Palestine. With the main NCDs (cardiovascular diseases, cancer, stroke, diabetes) is being responsible for 58% of total deaths in 2016. Additionally, breast cancer is being one of the most common cancer for women representing 32,4% of all cancers cases among females and with the highest rates being recorded in Ramallah and Bethlehem governorates, highlighting a need for early detection and prevention/awareness sessions. Moreover, in the project location of Bethlehem is recorder the lowest number of visits in PHCC in 2017 due to access restrictions. Regarding the importance of SRH services, is documented that Hebron Governorate represents 50% of the total near miss pregnancy cases in all WB and having at the same time the higher rates of live births in all oPt (25.255 in 2017 ). In addition to that Hebron holds one of the highest rates as governorate of pregnant women referred to high risk pregnancy clinics (21,1% ) and where are recorded the highest rates of anemia deficiency in both pregnant women and children at age of 12 months, leaving pregnant women and neonate extremely exposed.

Activities

An estimated population of 6370, residing in Area C, faces augmented humanitarian needs as a result of their isolation. MDM-CH mobile clinics project has set as overall objective the delivery of high quality primary health care services, to this marginalized population, focusing on treatment, as well as on prevention.Additionally, MDM-CH believe in the multi-faceted approach to increase the resilience of people in need: For the provision of holistic care to this vulnerable population, discussion and plan of co-action with MOH and UNRWA primary health clinicsis a top priority.Eligibility criteria for the inclusion of communities to the aforementioned project, are summoned up:- Communities whose citizens require one hour, or more, to get access to a primary health care facility level 2, or above, fully operational.- Communities reachable in 30min or more, for emergency response medical services, in the face of a crucial health need.- Communities situated within a radius of 2km from an IDF, or a settlement.- Communities inside Area C.This project’s personnel consist of physician, nurse, midwive, psychosocial counselor and support staff, and their mission is the provision of high quality consultations, examinations, treatments, awareness and health promotion. When treatment is required, essential drugs will be administered free of charge for the beneficiaries.The burden of Non-Communicable Disease is disproportioned, for the Palestinian population. To reduce Morbidity and Mortality derived, one should focus to effective detection and management of early symptoms: Blood sugar tests and arterial pressure control is cardinal for the treatment of diabetes and hypertension. Further referral will be provided to these patients, to ensure proper diagnosis and pejorative factors.Prevention is more important than treatment and community education is of great importance for MDM-CH aiming at promoting health behaviors in order to reduce the incidence of these disease and the associate morbidity and mortality to the possible extend. Awareness raising sessions are going to be focused on health promotion and the dangers of sedentary life, smoking, as well as other avoidable factors. Furthermore, whenever needed, additional expertise is going to be requested, through a network of other health services provision actors. It is important to highlight that MDM-CH is an active member of Protection and Health Clusters and issues of importance can be addressed throughout their meetings. The intervention design was based on the tailored needs of the acute vulnerable women, children, elderly, patients with chronic diseases, and will prioritize secure and confident access to health careAnte and postnatal care are special fields of care, of immense value for women. Through this project, adequate access to specialized support is going to be ensured, for mother and child, as well as profound consultation regarding family planning. Education is the cornerstone of prevention. Besides the access to special medication, like oral contraception pills and post contact pills, confident consultation is going to be achieved. Men will also be supported end encouraged to participate in family planning sessions, in the awareness raising meetings.Finally, mental health support is going to be provided by psychosocial counselors, and for cases that need further referral, it is going to be reassured by the network of MDM-CH collaborators. The psychosocial counsellor will provide guidance and counselling to the individuals and groups in the targeted locations (following IASC WHO standards), conduct group activities and raise the awareness in the communities on issues related to psychosocial well-being, optimum development of children and adolescents, in addition to do the referrals of severe cases which need mental health support to the specialized organizations.
