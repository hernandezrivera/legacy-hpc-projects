Project Summary

Enhance protection of the most vulnerable populations affected by the ongoing crisis in Borno State through provision of GBV response services; increase of availability and access to nutritious and diversified food via micro-gardening interventions, and provision of improved cooking solutions in order combat the high high prevalence of gender-based violence, Sexual violence and socioeconomic violence found. The project will also address, the significant threat of evolution with an upsurge in the instrumental use of women as perpetrators on the one hand and their position as survivors of GBV, abductions and detentions on the other resulting to direct and indirect consequences including kidnapping, early forced marriage, rape, sexual harassment, extra judicial killings and loss of livelihood.

Needs

In the three-worst‐affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, the insurgency has displaced 1.9 million people. Borno State, the epicentre of the crisis, in particular has faced escalating levels of insecurity which led to massive population movements. The last International Organization for Migration (IOM) August 2018 Displacement Tracking Matrix XXIV estimated that 75% of IDPs are located in Borno. The Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis of food and nutrition insecurity levels released in March 2018 indicates that the people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States in Phases 3 and 4 are considered to be affected and in need of various types of food security and livelihoods assistance.A lifetime prevalence of gender-based violence (GBV) shows the rate at about 16%, with a Sexual violence of 33.2% and socioeconomic violence found to be 28.4% in Borno State [United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) 2017)]. The vulnerability population unfortunately expands with about 43.3% of women being full time house wives and an additional 9% are unskilled, 14.9% being widowed and 20.6% having more than six children to cater for. These findings are similar in Yobe state (UNFPA 2017).At the same time, women and girls are being attacked while engaging in performing everyday basic tasks for sustenance such as fetching water, collecting firewood and going to the latrines (2018 HRP). During the 2018 Joint Safe Access to Fuel and Energy (SAFE) Assessment (FAO, WFP and UNHCR) conducted in 4 Local Government Areas in Borno State (Jere, Konduga, Ngala and Gwoza), protection risks were identified as one of the most significant challenges related to energy access which involves mostly women and children. In fact, 85% of women and girls interviewed reported facing protection risks when collecting firewood.Negative coping strategies (begging, borrowing, forced prostitution, survival sex, child marriage, and selling of woodfuel and productive assets), particularly by IDPs, especially female-headed and child-headed households, are common coping strategies for economically depressed households (2018 Humanitarian Response Plan). The issue of limited energy access is a very pressing one in North-East Nigeria and is aggravated by conflict and negative impacts of climate change. Since women and children are mainly responsible for collecting woodfuel, the provision of safe energy access options will be important to harness resilience and dignity. UNFPA and FAO will ensure that all phases of this project are implemented in close consultation with affected communities and establish a feedback mechanism, which will allow men, boys, women, and girls express any complaint. Beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanism, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. In order, not to cause further harm (Do No Harm Principle. UNFPA /FAO will also ensure that their staff and partners comply with their respective Code of Conduct. sensitization for beneficiaries on the project purpose, their entitlements, the selection criteria, water harvesting techniques, gender issues and GBV, PSEA & community feedback and complaint mechanisms, sexual reproductive health (SRH) and climate change.

Activities

Activity 1: Provide psycho-social and case management services to survivors of GBV This will be done using survivor centered approach. UNFPA This will involve Production and distribution of guidelines to service providers, Procurement and distribution of 72,000 NFIs (Dignity Kits, hygiene kits) to women and girls of reproductive age to mitigate further risks of GBV and Establish 5 community based protection systems through village committees for the purpose of identification and response to GBV, and community participatory appraisal. Establishment and management of additional 5 Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) and 1 safe shelter. UNFPA and FAO will also ensure that their staff and partners comply with their respective Code of Conduct. Sensitization for beneficiaries on the project purpose, their entitlements, the selection criteria, the risk of mines on GBV , PSEA and complaint mechanisms will be conducted. Activity 2: Economic empowerment support to vulnerable community members including GBV survivors This will be done through adult learning, skills acquisition and cooperative establishment after need assessment has been done. Activity 3: Agricultural inputs support through Micro gardeningFAO will distribute and train selected community members Vocational training in relevant areas (micro/backyard-gardening and water harvesting techniques, organizational, leadership and entrepreneurial skills, basic financial management, bookkeeping, marketing, negotiation and basic knowledge to provide technical support to customers, etc.)Distribution of nutrition-sensitive vegetable kits including seeds such as okra, roselle, sorrel, watermelon, amaranthus, cabbage, tomato, carrot, pepper and onions and water harvesting kits to selected women, men, adolescent girls and boys Activity 4: Distribution of locally produced fuel-efficient cookstoves, to vulnerable beneficiariesFAO will train 100 beneficiaries in innovative fuel-efficient technologies and distribute locally manufactured fuel efficient cook stoves to 20,000 households . FAO will further conduct Awareness raising campaign for beneficiaries on SAFE, micro/backyard-gardening, gender issues and GBV, PSEA, Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) and climate change. UNFPA and FAO are active members of both GBV Sub Sector and Protection Sector working groups and Protection Sector where they contribute to policy formulation and technical guidance to implementing partners. In addition to this, FAO co-lead with WFP the Food Security Sector and is also coordinating the Working Group on Safe Access to Fuel and Energy (SAFE) in Maiduguri, under the FSS, in order to share information, coordinate activities and advocate for the issue of energy access. UNFPA and FAO will share the lessons learned which will emanate from the Project to the above mentioned Coordination systems. Monitoring and evaluation system will be developed for the joint intervention that will include joint monitoring missions of FAO and UNFPA and development of common data collection tools to capture the joint intervention deliverables.UNFPA and FAO will ensure that rigorous monitoring mechanisms are built throughout the project cycle. Routine monitoring of project performance, and tracking of key programme indicators based on regular collection of implementation data. Reports to be submitted to OCHA will be based on the indicators and outputs of the project.
