Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will support essential health care that prevents and reduces excess mortality and morbidity in crisis-affected populations with a focus on the core areas of emergency health response: communicable diseases, reproductive health, non-communicable diseases, injury, and first aid care, mental health, nutrition and community health activities, surveillance and control of epidemic prone diseases in vulnerable communities in Kapoeta North, South and East in Eastern Equatoria State; Akobo, Nyirol and Uror counties in Jonglei State, and Tonj East, North and Twic in Warrap State. Save the Children has presence in these locations that need to be strengthened to ensure life-saving healthcare services.
This will be achieved by supporting the State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and County Health Department (CHD) and in coordination with the health cluster through static and mobile health facilities and community outreach supporting the Boma Health Initiative. SC will coordinate the response with the RRC as well as with the health clusters and other actors in the areas to consider the need for initial assessments.
Objectives:
1. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.
2. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases
3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms
SC will target beneficiaries with primary health care services through supporting existing static sites and by supporting mobile clinics in hard to reach locations including MHPSS and sexual and reproductive health care services. The project will target 338752 total beneficiaries desegregated into169, 219 male and 169,533 female and will also target persons with disability, host communities and IDPs. SC will support MOH in delivering quality and cost effective healthcare services, health system strengthening, capacity building of staff at national level, state level including the CHD as well as coordination activities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan’s health system relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services. Limited health facility functionality and capacity, and poor coverage, have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by floods, food insecurity and frequent conflicts. Women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. Government contributions to health services was just 1.9% of 2021 national budget allocations with the addition of an underperforming economy also contributing to poor health outcomes. Only 8% of the 1,869 reported health facilities are providing the full package of BPHNS and 53% of the facilities are moderately functional , implying inadequate access and unavailability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the country. 58% of the Bomas in the country are still not having Boma health teams (Community Health system). Only 59% of the population can access health facilities in one hour’s time while 80% reported barriers in accessing health services . Extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage) has made children more vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 52.6% of all morbidities and 58.2% of all mortalities in 2021. While no new cholera outbreak has been declared since the end of 2017, the number of reported AWD cases is increasing and 20 counties are now classified as cholera hotspots with a high risk of new outbreaks. Further, Hepatitis E cases significantly rose to over 1,800 cases with 11 deaths and still remained above epidemic thresholds in 2021. Vaccine Derived Poliovirus 2(cVDPV2), (59 cases reported since the outbreak began in 2020 of which 42% are female) and the threat of infectious disease outbreaks like COVID19 and EVD also drive humanitarian needs. Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births Only 39% women are availing recommended Four Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy and institutionalized delivery as well as skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3% and 14.7% respectively . Access to BEmONC is very low and contributes to pregnancy related and new-born morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6% with modern methods at 5%, with the unmet need for family planning estimated at 29.7% for 2020 (FP 2020). Gender inequality and gender-based violence are widespread and perpetuated by several factors including socio-cultural norms and armed group presence in communities. Recent mapping in 37 of the 79 counties revealed that leprosy is still endemic in all surveyed counties (indicating high burden of Neglected Tropical Diseases) . Only 20 health facilities are providing treatment in 17 counties nationwide. Access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) remains inadequate. WHO prevalence estimates of mental disorders in conflict settings to South Sudan, is approx. 2.5 million cases, emphasizing the risk of mental health burden in the affected population. Research study shows 41% of probable occurrences of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) in Six states. Attack on health care workers/facilities continues to negatively impact provision of and access to health services. Health facilities in some locations were reported to be closed due to violence and flooding, for example in Tonj East and Tonj north, Twic in Warrap State and Abyei Administrative Area, Kapoeta North Counties in Eastern Equatoria. Access of communities to health services is further hindered by insecurity and harassment on the way to facilities.

2 - Project activities

SC will continue to work with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and County Health Department (CHD), to support Primary Health Care Centres (PHCC) and mobile medical units. Community outreach will also be supported through Boma Health Initiative (BHI) volunteers to improve access to quality life-saving healthcare, with a focus on offering curative and preventative child and maternal health services. The support includes technical, managerial, material, and monetary support to enable facilities to provide quality life-saving interventions safely in the catchment areas, and includes the provision of additional clinical staff for the PHCCs, setting up and running the planned mobile clinics, and ICCM programming, in addition, to support community-based disease surveillance. SC will continue coordination with the county health team, supporting coordination activities at the county level through our field offices in Kapoeta, Bor, Lankien, Walgak and Akobo East. Basic Primary Health Care: SC will continue the support for essential health care that prevents and reduces excess mortality and morbidity in crisis-affected populations with a focus on the core areas of emergency health response: communicable diseases, reproductive health, non-communicable diseases, injury, and first aid care, mental health, nutrition and community health activities. The supported static health facilities and mobile medical units will provide the full package of primary health care service provision with one additional clinical officer and one midwife to assist in mobile clinic referrals requiring maternal and neonatal health care. Services will follow the national clinical guidelines, national IMNCI guidelines, mhGAP Community Toolkit and SC’s common approach to maternal and neonatal health care referencing the New Born Health Field Guide for Humanitarian Settings (NBHFG). To this end, SC will provide mhGAP Community Toolkit training to BHI volunteers and healthcare workers to meet the increased demand for MHPSS services in health care settings. Nutrition programming and staff will be integrated into the mobile medical units to ensure complementarity between sectors and that beneficiaries are receiving a holistic response. Save the Children will support referrals for comprehensive reproductive health services, medical and surgical emergency for comprehensive health services. However, to reduce the number of external referrals, SC will fundraise to renovate the existing static structures. SC will support the activities using the humanitarian pipeline, supporting collection from UN warehouse freight and last-mile distribution. SC will add additional supplies directly purchased through pre-approved suppliers, two of which SC has existing framework agreements. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The system has 3 feedback systems composed of 3 core elements, 1. Information sharing, 2. Participation, 3. Feedback and response with 2 main approaches; a). Hotline system, b). Face to face meeting with the beneficiaries, in addition to the suggestion boxes set up in each facility. The hotline method will be based on a centralized phone line (to be established) received and documented by the accountability focal person (also a member of the MEAL team). All the complaints and feedback received from the stakeholders through the phone line shall be categorized appropriately and forwarded for management actions. Formal feedback shall be provided to the complainant within a maximum period of 15 days for normal complaints (categories 1-3 as per SCI categorization). The Face-to-Face system of accountability shall involve the project team establishing a compliant and feedback desk during all the project activities like meetings, trainings, and distributions. The complaints and feedback received from the beneficiaries during the face-to-face meetings shall be addressed following a similar procedure for the hotline system.Save the Children will proactively through engagement of community structures seek out those who are the most marginalized including girls, adolescent wives and mothers, children out of school, children living without appropriate care, and children with disability, to ensure they can access our program. Community and beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be established through an Accountability to Affected Persons Committee (AAP committee) that will be established, trained on ways they can monitor and report complaints with scheduled engagements with SC community mobilizers.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable by ensuring that: a) The feedback and complaint mechanism design and implementation is appropriate for all members of the community including those who are not literate, the elderly, women and girls and individuals living with disabilities; b) Ensure that the most vulnerable have equal access to the feedback and complaint mechanisms; c) Ensure that there is sufficient time, resources and flexibility to implement a feedback mechanism and respond to feedback once the mechanism is in place and to actively solicit feedback from individuals representing marginalized groups; d) Conducting a thorough context analysis to ensure that all the targeted groups/ vulnerable groups are considered before deciding on a particular feedback mechanism and ensuring the mechanism has different ways to report into it, including methods which are without cost and which are suitable for persons who are not literate; e) Sensitise beneficiaries including the most vulnerable to the purpose and process of giving feedback, both at the start of the project and on an ongoing basis, and allow time to build trust in the mechanism and to be able to adapt the mechanism if all or part of it is not working well, or if feedback isn’t being received; f) Engage with external stakeholders including representatives of the most vulnerable groups about the feedback mechanism and establish referral protocols; g) Ensure that there is sufficient scope in the programme design to make changes and respond to requests to increase or reallocate resources; h) Ensure those with ‘first contact’ with beneficiaries (often project staff) understand the purpose of the feedback mechanism and the processes and timelines for responding to feedback; i) The project will careful give consideration to how feedback will be analysed and how the feedback and any required responses will be shared back with the community in a manner which is inclusive and age-appropriate. j) SCI staff members receive annual training on the response mechanism to ensure staff’s capacity to manage and oversee the system appropriately.Participatory feedback sessions with children through appropriate methods such as drawings, posters in child friendly language with visuals, group meetings will be implemented. Save the Children’s child safeguarding policy will also be explained to children and their communities and how to lodge a complaint in safe way explained and contact details of who to contact in case of a complaint shared while respecting child safeguarding principles. In all the above systems, confidentiality shall be central to handling the complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries. All action generated from the documented complaints and feedback mechanism will be tracked using an Action Plan Tracker. The system will update relevant thematic manager and technical specialist on which action plans have been developed and implemented and which need to be followed up on.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SC will ensure equitable and equal service provision to all individuals irrespective of age, physical disability, ethnicity, religion or political orientation in line with the humanitarian principles of neutrality and impartiality. SC will also ensure strict observation and practice of all its do-no-harm policies (safeguarding and child protection, PSEA, Antifraud, etc.) by all its staff to ensure children's rights and all other groups rights are not violated in the process of implementation. Lastly, the CRF mechanism will be continuously used to identify and timely respond to any grievances that may arise during the process.Information will be shared using different media; written posters will illustrations, feedback boxes at service points and child protection help desks. It will also be integrated in the community awareness campaign, meetings with beneficiaries, one on one dialogues, or counseling etc. All staff and community volunteers and front line workers will be trained on child safeguarding along with code of conduct and PSEA for prevention and given the opportunity to report, whistle blow accordingly. Any staff, volunteer would have to adhere to the policies which are already existent in the organization. And in case of an incident, a standard process would be followed to handle it in standard and responsive approach.Engagement with local populations Most of the work in protection from sexual exploitation and abuse will be done at community, payam and county level. Engaging local populations and having support locally is a crucial element in succeeding in this work. This will involve, raising awareness in local communities of what PSEA is, that there is zero tolerance for PSEA, the options for reporting suspected PSEA cases, how the reporting mechanism works and where they go access further support and referrals. Raising awareness Key to prevention and response is raising understanding among local populations about their rights, for example entitlement to aid without sexual exploitation), the prohibition against humanitarian, development and any other personnel engaging in such acts and how to report it when it occurs. Specific tasks will include the development and delivery of information sheets, posters, and having community based training sessions to properly inform local staff and local populations. Building Effective Complaints Mechanisms. A major element in engaging with the local population will be based on building effective complaints mechanisms. In order for the project to establish a safe, accessible and confidential complaints mechanism (i.e. system for receiving allegations or rumours of SEA). The local community in each geographical area will be engaged in designing it to ensure its usability.

SCI is dedicated to improving the effectiveness, quality and accountability of its programs through evidence-based decisions based on traditional and community-based participatory monitoring. SCIs accountability commitments aim to facilitate the participation of the affection population ensuring that program design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation processes and decision are informed by and reflect the views of those the interventions are designed for.To ensure beneficiaries accountability to affected populations and participation, SCI will: 1. Regularly and periodic review of projects with key stakeholders and strengthening the feedback mechanisms with the beneficiaries.The project will ensure that there is a functional feedback and accountability mechanism established within the project locations. Needs assessments will be conducted before starting new activities and the affected population will be asked how they would prefer to share feedback with the programme and receive assistance. The s targeted schools will be installed with the accessible suggestion boxes, to allow teachers and learners to share their feedback, requests & concerns about the project. The project will conduct a stakeholders workshop prior to the project launch in all the project locations, this will provided an opportunity for various stakeholders to learn in details about various components of the project, including the design, mode of implementation, targeted beneficiaries and about PSEAH and how to report this confidentially. The project team will be jointly conducting a periodic review of the program with the stakeholders to track the progress and offer any control measures, this will equally provide an opportunity to the stakeholders to feedback into the implementation process and track the progress. Community meetings which include vulnerable community members will be held termly with adaptations made to the projects where possible based on the feedback received.
