Project Summary

The proposed project aims to strengthen protection services for Rohingya refugees, focusing on vulnerable groups such as children, women, and girls, and persons with disabilities in Camps 2 East and 2 West in Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar. It will promote peace and coexistence through diverse initiatives, promoting social cohesion by implementing targeted interventions, including intensive community engagement, faith-based approaches, recreational activities, consultation and dialogues with relevant stakeholders, rigorous awareness efforts, and capacity-building initiatives.
The project will implement various activities to promote peace and social coexistence, address protection risks, and prevent harmful practices in the Rohingya community. Structured awareness sessions will be designed for men, boys, women, girls, and key community persons, including faith leaders, teachers, and community leaders. These sessions will promote peaceful coexistence using culturally sensitive, gender-responsive approaches and religious teachings to encourage community-wide behaviour change and foster greater acceptance of peace-building efforts. Women outreach volunteers (WOVs) will be trained to serve as trusted resources within the community, promoting social cohesion and enhancing local protection mechanisms.
The project will organize recreational activities, such as gender-sensitive games for youth, and also arrange artistic and cultural initiatives such as essay writing, poetry, and music performances, particularly involving children and youth. These initiatives aim to integrate peace-promoting messages and mitigate risks like substance abuse, trafficking, and violence while providing safe spaces for constructive engagement, especially for youths.
The project will also mobilize religious leaders to promote peace and coexistence using faith-based approaches. These leaders will develop and disseminate peace-promoting messages aligned with Islamic principles, emphasizing peace and nonviolence. Preparatory gatherings and interfaith dialogues will further strengthen understanding and cooperation among communities.
The proposed project will actively engage various community groups, including women’s groups, Imams, Majhis, and youth, through regular consultation meetings and awareness-raising activities.
Through strong trust building between community group leaders and the community people, and as per participatory decision from the community, the small-scale disputes within their communities will be resolved by the community leaders and the project will intensively support in this regard.
The implementation team of the proposed project will collaborate with peer protection actors in the targeted camp to strengthen peace and security and ensure a cohesive approach to protection. Regular consultation meetings, dialogue, and group discussions with the camp-level protection actors will be conducted under the project where different potential peace-building and security assurance discussions will be held. As a joint approach, the project will also engage community representatives along with actors, so that the representatives can put their insight into building camp-level social cohesion and contribute to taken initiatives under the project.
Through these initiatives, the project will reduce harmful practices, strengthen community bonds, and empower residents to collectively manage risks. By fostering social cohesion and community ownership, the project will create a safer, more peaceful environment, enhancing the dignity and well-being of camp residents. This 12-month intervention will directly benefit 12,500 individuals in Camps 2 East and 2 West, addressing critical gaps identified in the Joint Response Plan while establishing a sustainable foundation for peace and security.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Based on the identified needs and gaps highlighted by multiple sources, the proposed project aims to address significant protection risks in Camps 2 East and 2 West, Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar. The intervention will be evidence-based, aligning with data from reports like the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), Protection Sector monitoring, and protection camp focal points. These insights reveal persistent challenges, including crime, violence, and gender-based vulnerabilities, particularly affecting women, girls, and children. By addressing these critical gaps, the project seeks to build peace and social cohesion, enabling the Rohingya refugee population to live in a safer and more dignified environment.Data from the J-MSNA indicates that 48% of HHs in the camps cite crime, violence, and different protection concerns. Women and girls face heightened risks, particularly during nighttime movements near WASH facilities, exacerbated by inadequate lighting and theft of solar batteries. According to the Joint Protection Monitoring Report Q2 (2024), sexual violence, including rape (5%) and sexual assault (5%), accounted for 11% of reported GBV cases. Additionally, 64.6% of camps reported that women and girls feel unsafe or very unsafe, with heightened risks of kidnapping, abduction, and assault reported as critical issues.Child labor remains a significant issue, with 7.58% of child protection cases reported between January and September 2024 involving children aged 12-17. Of these, 7.24% were boys, and only 0.34% were girls, reflecting stark gender disparities. Adolescents face additional vulnerabilities due to peer pressure, substance abuse, and trafficking risks, often exacerbated by economic hardship. The CPIMS+ report highlights that many children are forced into work, increasing their exposure to abuse and exploitation.The security environment in the camps has deteriorated significantly, with organized groups driving violence and insecurity. In 2023, 1,830 serious protection incidents, including murder, physical assault, kidnapping, and extortion, were recorded—an increase of 181% from 2022. The situation worsened in 2024, with a 40% rise in incidents in Q2 alone, including 27 killings, 112 serious physical assaults, and 183 kidnappings or abductions. Over 2,000 cases of forced recruitment, often involving coercion and threats, were reported between March and May 2024. These incidents affect men and boys but also indirectly harm women and girls through intimidation and gender-based violence.Recreational activities, such as sports and creative initiatives, will engage youth while addressing substance abuse, trafficking, and violence. By integrating peace-promoting messages, these activities will encourage positive community dynamics. To enhance safety and promote peaceful co-existence, the project will be in collaboration with local law enforcement, humanitarian actors, and camp authorities to ensure a coordinated response to peace security concerns.By aligning its objectives with these identified needs and gaps, the project will reduce protection risks, foster social cohesion, and enable the Rohingya population to live in a safer and more harmonious environment. Through this data-driven approach, the project aims to achieve meaningful and sustainable improvements in the lives of camp residents.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will apply protection principles across all stages of the project to safeguard girls, women, children, and vulnerable groups from violence, abuse, or exploitation. The project team will ensure that all the beneficiaries can access services with safety and dignity. Every activity will be guided by a protection lens, with staff well-oriented in protection mainstreaming to recognize and mitigate protection risks. The protection mainstreaming involves under the proposed project integrating protection principles to ensure access, safety, and dignity in Rohingya refugee camps. This proposed project will prioritize the safety and dignity of the Rohingyas, minimize harm, and enhance their well-being, which will reduce their vulnerability to physical and psychosocial risks. Besides this, the project activities will ensure meaningful access for the beneficiaries especially children, women, person with disabilities by eliminating barriers and providing equitable access to assistance. The person with disabilities will have inclusive access to all project activities (e.g. disability screening in every data collection, practicing SADD data, inclusive visibility in CFM hub, regular coordination with disability expert organization, etc.) To ensure accountability, Islamic Relief has already set up complaints and Feedback mechanisms in the targeted camps for affected populations to assess interventions, voice concerns, and seek resolutions. Through this project’s intervention, IRB will promote the participation and empowerment of the Rohingya people through capacity development, including them in decision making, and empowerment. Through adherence to "Do No Harm" and "Leave No One Behind" principles, the project aims to create a safe, inclusive environment that addresses protection risks and empowers rights holders within the project framework.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will prioritize age, diversity, and disability inclusion across all activities to ensure that every rights holder has equitable access to services. During the project implementation, the team will actively include persons with disabilities in all project activities and ensure they are aware of relevant protection risks, similar to other rights holders. The registration and documentation process will identify rights holders by age and disability, allowing for tailored support. The project team will collect SADDD (Sex, Age, and Disability, Desegregated Data) information for this project’s activity implementation. To ensure the equitable access of the project activities to the person with disabilities, the project will ensure participation of person with disabilities in every awareness session, consultation and other gatherings, to prioritise their needs and opinions, continuing inclusive visibilities in CFM hub and set-up ramping to the facilities. Targeted activities will be developed for various age groups; for instance, youth-specific initiatives will address the unique needs and interests of young people within the project. Additionally, the project will aim for at least 3% of all rights holders to be individuals with disabilities, ensuring meaningful participation and representation. The project will establish a community-based protection mechanism for ensuring child protection. The project will offer inclusive sports and recreational activities for children with and without disabilities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure accountability to the affected population, the project will conduct timely refresher training to the project staff on core humanitarian standards so that the staff will respond to the community with committed manner and to grow empathy for the community to whom they are working. The MEAL team will actively gather community feedback through FGDs, and consultations and prioritise their needs and recommendations. The project will also involve community members in decision-making processes to enhance project improvements and will prioritize transparency with clear communication about services provided to the community. The project will deploy CFM boxes and provide accessible contact information in local languages at the service point following the existing CFM practise by IRB in the targeted camp. This will enable beneficiaries to voice concerns effectively. Orientation sessions will equip the selected beneficiaries with knowledge on utilizing the CFM, and community representatives will be invited to project meetings to ensure their voices are heard in the planning and evaluation processes. To empower the community, the project team will invite community representatives to every project meeting. In addition, the monitoring and evaluation process will involve relevant stakeholders to work together on improving the project. This approach will strengthen community involvement and ownership. The project team will ensure that all common messages and information education communication (IEC) materials are available in English and Rohingya languages. For age, gender, and disability inclusion, this project will ensure that all demographic groups (especially women, older persons, persons with disabilities, and youth) are included, and their specific needs will be addressed at each step of AAP planning and implementation.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will orient and ensure that 100% of project staff (frontline staff, enumerators, volunteers, senior management) and related personnel (including vendors, suppliers, and third parties) receive mandatory PSEA training (including regular refreshers). We must mainstream PSEA in all of our project activities and stages of the project cycle to ensure proper prevention. IEC materials on PSEA will be displayed at dedicated protection service points. Through targeted awareness activities, the community will receive information on PSEA. A designated PSEA focal person (in each camp) will be available for community members and rights holders to report cases and receive immediate support. A robust mechanism will be in place to address and respond to PSEA issues effectively. As a member of the PSEA network in Cox’s Bazar, IRB will adhere to all guidelines provided by the network and follow its protocols closely. IRB will ensure any SEA allegation, investigate properly, and take necessary actions in line with standard operating procedures (SOP) for SEA complaint referral in the Rohingya response. We will identify risks that must be addressed with the technical support of the PSEA network. PSEA awareness materials (such as posters) including key PSEA messages, and the available CFM will be visible to beneficiaries. The project will ensure that 100% of project beneficiaries will be aware of PSEA and reporting mechanisms. IRB will also ensure that 100% of project staff receive training on how to receive and refer SEA disclosures safely, respectfully, and confidentially. This will ensure safe, accessible, confidential, culturally appropriate, and victim-centered reporting channels for sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment, tailored to beneficiaries' needs and preferences. All contractors, vendors, and third parties engaged in this project will sign the code of conduct and abide by PSEA principles.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project aims to enhance emergency preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) efforts while addressing climate change and environmental protection in Rohingya refugee camps. Protection services play a crucial role during emergencies, especially during relocations. The project team, working closely with camp protection focal points, will proactively assess various protection risks, such as GBV, landslides, and flooding hazards in new or risk-prone areas. Identified risks will be shared with relevant stakeholders, ensuring a coordinated response through established referral mechanisms.The project team is committed to safeguarding the environment by avoiding actions that could harm local ecosystems, such as tree cutting, soil excavation, and the excessive use of plastic materials. Regular awareness sessions with the community will focus on building resilience to potential climate and environmental risks, empowering individuals to take proactive steps in protecting their surroundings during emergencies.During meetings, group discussions, and awareness sessions, the project team will promote environment-friendly practices, such as using reusable water containers and avoiding materials harmful to the environment. Through effective referral mechanisms, awareness-raising, and information-sharing activities, the project will prioritize environmental protection concerns and swiftly coordinate with relevant actors for any identified cases in the referral hub. Through the planned activities, this project will play a vital role in climate change adaptation and mitigation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, in the event of emergencies within the camps, the project team will coordinate closely with the Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) to ensure a swift and effective response and provide joint and coordinated support to the affected community. To enhance community resilience before, during, and after emergencies, the project will conduct regular awareness-raising sessions that inform community members of protection risks that may intensify in crises. These sessions will include guidance on personal safety, reporting mechanisms, and safe access to services, helping to reduce vulnerabilities associated with emergency conditions. Through these efforts, the project aims to empower the Rohingya community with knowledge and resources to better withstand and respond to protection risks that arise during crises. To ensure emergency preparedness and response (EPR), the project has kept a provision of contingency funds.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project actively engages youth aged 15 to 24 in various activities to strengthen protection in their communities. Youth will participate in awareness-raising programs focused on identifying protection risks and understanding mitigation measures. They will also join community consultation meetings alongside local representatives, promoting a shared commitment to safety and inclusion. Through capacity-building training and consistent awareness sessions, youth will be empowered to play a key role in reducing protection risks within their communities. They will also be integrated into referral mechanisms. This will enable them to easily access reporting channels and support others in addressing protection concerns. Additionally, youth will take part in recreational activities, such as indoor games, protect against substance abuse, and reduce other risks. These initiatives aim to equip young people as proactive contributors to a safer camp environment. Above all, the project has a dedicated team including community mobilizers, field facilitators, and community volunteers who will contribute and engage in joint response in any emergency that occurs in camps. This project will create participatory approaches that actively engage youth in project design, implementation, and monitoring, ensuring their voices are heard and recognized. To reflect the youth’s participation in the project cycle, this project will create referral pathways among agencies. The project will develop age-appropriate youth engagement strategies. IRB will ensure specialized training for staff and volunteers who work directly with adolescents and youth (15-24 years old) on how to effectively and appropriately communicate with this age group.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the proposed project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by focusing on community-driven initiatives that empower individuals and groups to address their own challenges. Through structured awareness sessions on protection risks and mitigation strategies and faith-based approaches, the refugees will be equipped with the knowledge to enhance their capacity to navigate risks independently.The project further encourages solidarity and peer support by establishing mediator forums, peace promotion groups, and women-led organizations, enabling refugees to take ownership of community safety and social cohesion efforts. Recreational activities and creative initiatives will foster a sense of purpose and collaboration among youth, enhancing their ability to contribute positively to their communities. These measures aim to gradually reduce dependency on external aid and promote sustainable coping mechanisms, enabling refugees to build resilience and move towards self-reliance.
