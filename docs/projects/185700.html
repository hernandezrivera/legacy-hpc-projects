Project Summary

To reach the goal of satisfactory conditions of water, sanitation and hygiene for Rohingya refugees and host community, Green Hill (GH) will improve WASH facilities and interventions in camps 1W and 17 as well as in selected host communities under Cox’s Bazar District. The activities will address key issues by enhancing community engagement resulting in increased access to WASH services for sustainability of system operations and maintenance.
According to the WASH Sector’s JRP strategy, GH will focus on strengthening participatory approaches and capacity building for Rohingya refugees aiming to improve behavioral change. Gender responsive community mobilization will be reinforced to ensure participation, especially of women and girls, in decision-making related to design, feedback mechanisms, implementation and monitoring of essential WASH services. GH will prioritize meeting community needs for critical hygiene supplies, soap, water containers, and menstrual hygiene management material. In addition, GH will integrate clean energy solutions by installing solar lights into the WASH facilities. These solar lights will also provide further protection at night to all beneficiaries accessing the WASH facilities including pregnant women, children, elderly and person with disability etc. All activities will be implemented with a focus on PSEA, accountability to affected populations, protection, age, gender and diversity, disaster risk management and environmental ecosystem with the concept of do no harm.
Since early 2020, GH has been implementing essential WASH projects in camp 1W, 17 and host communities. Building off this experience, this project seeks to ensure approximately 3,500 households, covering approximately 17,000 individuals (UNHCR Population Factsheet, October, 2021) in 2 blocks of camp 1W, 17 and 2 unions in the Host community level with quality water supply, improved sanitation facilities and hygiene practices, improved waste & fecal sludge management in respect with Sphere WASH Standards and WASH Sector collaboration. GH prioritizes quality implementation, durable solutions and building resilience through community engagement. Moreover, emergency responses like AWD, COVID-19, fire, flood, monsoon and cyclone will be prioritized as needed.

1 - Identified Needs

With the support of UN agencies and hundreds of international and national organizations, the Bangladesh government is able to manage the emergency. Initially, it was difficult to ensure proper WASH services for the vulnerable communities and maintain standards, however, coordinated and collaborative efforts are making progress. Given the large number of refugees, it is critical for WASH actors to continue strengthening efforts to ensure quality, sufficient, equitable and dignified access to WASH services for all women, men, girls and boys, inclusive of all age groups and persons with disabilities. Water: According to Joint Multi Sectoral Need Assessment(October, 2021), 56% of the population are living with gaps regarding Water, Sanitation and Hygiene services. Besides this, WASH sector baseline, 75% water sources are reported functional, only 45% households have biological risk-free drinking water. J-MSNA (October, 2021) identified 36% people using unprotected tube wells as a main source for drinking water. Day by day, the water table is moving downwards which results in tube-well functionality challenges. Hand pumps are still a major source for drinking water, bathing and domestic use. Sanitation: According to the WASH sector gap analysis 92% sanitation facilities are functional though regular repair and maintenance work is required. Solid waste management is another mentionable challenge as only 51% of the households are disposing waste in designated areas. 16 % households reported disposing waste directly in open space. Moreover, proper waste and fecal sludge management is another concerning issue in this area; especially during the monsoon season. Promotion and Community Engagement: According to J-MSNA (October, 2021), 93% of the community have access to soap for hand washing. However, the scenario in the case of practice level is not satisfactory. Access to MHM facilities remain a barrier for many females due to stigma, taboo and conservative practices. In some instances, we have identified that IEC material regarding MHM is not contextualized. Considering all of these important factors and the gaps that remain to be addressed, GH will continue its current WASH program in two camps and host communities within camp boundaries with technical support from Community Partners International (CPI). GH will contribute to operations and maintenance systems necessary to expand and ensure sustainability of WASH infrastructure. This will be done by ensuring community participation and engagement, leveraging the existing projects using a multi-level approach that seeks to identify and address problems at the individual, household, community, and environmental levels in a complementary way.
