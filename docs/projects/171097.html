Project Summary

To improve the effectiveness and efficiency of humanitarian health interventions by strengthening coordination and emergency preparedness through implementing the six core cluster functions. Through better coordination and information management, the highest number of vulnerable people across oPt can have access to life-saving and life-sustaining healthcare services that are accountable to affected population.

1 - Needs

The emergence of the COVID-19 outbreak emphasized the need for improved coordination of partners’ response, including better emergency preparedness within the Health Cluster and robust early warning system in place. The fragile healthcare system in the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) has been stretched heightening the need for stronger cluster coordination and information management to ensure more effective and accountable response and that the most vulnerable people are reached. Resources are overextended and a significant amount have been redirected to respond to the urgent needs brought on by the outbreak. In addition, as healthcare workers succumb to COVID-19, the number of functioning healthcare facilities has fallen. Direct mortality from the COVID-19 outbreak and indirect increased mortality from other conditions linked to disruption of essential service delivery is of major concern. Monitoring healthcare facilities and resources availability has become more difficult as the situation is fluid, demanding greater investment in tools such as the HeRAMS which currently only covered MoH facilities mainly in the Gaza Strip but need to be expanded to include NGO healthcare facilities and include facilities in West Bank. Thus, as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak and pre-existing humanitarian crisis, it is estimated that about 1.45 million (56% females) Palestinians across oPt need health-related humanitarian assistance. Stronger coordination and information management has been made even more pertinent with the ongoing challenges presented by the COVID-19 outbreak such as rapidly increasing caseload, demand to scale-up laboratory testing, surveillance, contact tracing, isolation and case management as well as critical shortages of life-saving equipment, pharmaceutical and disposables for COVID-19, movement restrictions linked to public health measures which are increasing barriers to accessing healthcare services. Patients and their companions in the West Bank, outside East Jerusalem, and Gaza Strip have continued to face major barriers to healthcare access due to the delay and denial of permits required by Israel to move between areas of oPt, as well as to Israel and Jordan. Through advocacy and negotiations with the relevant authorities the cluster will work with partners to ensure that these communities including those in Area C (where mobile clinics are regularly denied operation) have access to essential health services. Cross-cutting issues such as gender, age, disability, protection and mental health demand greater collaboration and information sharing between clusters to ensure the integration of these crucial issues in all aspects of health interventions. For the project to have a full picture of the health issues affecting various vulnerable groups within the community there is need for more intensive community engagement. In order to ensure that the above humanitarian health needs are addressed, there is need for a robust cluster coordination structure providing leadership and technical guidance to the over 100 partners who make up the Health Cluster. With this number of partners and 40% being national NGOs it is important that the Health Cluster fosters stronger partnerships, complementarity and capacity building. Platforms such as the Technical Working Groups and the Strategic Advisory Group offer opportunities for the Cluster and partners to benefit from technical expertise. In addition, there is massive health information generated from day to day operations, assessments that need to be done to ensure that the ever-changing needs of the population are identified and met. Information needs to be properly collected (including disaggregated by gender, age and disability) and analysed in order to highlight the health needs of the vulnerable groups and for a more evidence-based approach to cluster priority setting and identification of gaps within interventions.

2 - Activities

The Health Cluster Coordination Team will aim to enhance coordination and the response of its 100 partner agencies. Key coordination activities such as the monthly meetings and providing platforms such as the Health Cluster website and social media for partners to engage and strengthen partnerships will continue to take place. Working together with partners in monitoring of health indicators and setting of minimum standards for health and interventions with guidance from technical expertise from the WHO and latest scientific evidence. The Health Cluster will promote greater information sharing with other clusters and inter-sectorial coordination and linkages to ensure a comprehensive operational response. Across the oPt, the Health Cluster activities will include: 1. Developing the emergency preparedness capacity of health partners. This will include systematic mapping, training on emergency preparedness, table-top exercises in addition to joint emergency response interventions. 2. Establishing a multi-hazard early warning indicator dashboard to support emergency preparedness and rapid response in collaboration with OCHA.3. Publishing Situational Reports (monthly)/ Health Cluster Bulletin (quarterly). 4. Developing a Health Cluster Contingency Plan to ensure a timely response to emergencies, whether it is conflict-related or disease outbreak e.g. COVID-19.5. Expand the HeRAMS (an approach to monthly monitoring of health facilities and resources availability) to encompass not only MoH facilities but also NGO facilities. 6. Establishing a Strategic Advisory group consisting of health partners with technical expertise to guide the cluster’s strategy and policy. 7. Collaborating with the Protection Cluster, WHO and the ICRC to promote monitoring for attacks against healthcare, including barriers to accessing healthcare. This will enhance the protection of healthcare staff as well as the beneficiaries. 8. Conducting health needs assessments, by mainstreaming geographical analysis using GIS, focusing on key areas, such as trauma needs, mobile primary healthcare needs.9. Mobilizing resources through donor engagements via quarterly briefings, disseminating information the impact of funding shortages. 10. Enhancing gender, age, disability and protection mainstreaming through having a dedicated focal point to support the cluster and partners to improve reflection of these key areas in interventions and reporting as well as providing trainings to partners. 11. Enhancing community engagement in health through the active participation of beneficiaries during monthly project visits and interaction with beneficiaries on social media platforms. The Cluster will establish a tracking tool to follow on the progress of AAP implementation throughout the HPC, in Cluster HRP projects that receive funding. 12. Continuing the work of the technical working groups i.e. Trauma, Nutrition, Mobile Clinics and Emergency
