Needs

Attacks on health facilities have rendered 46 per cent of hospitals and primary health facilities in Syria as either partially functional or not functional (HNO2019). Even if access to healthcare structures is possible, many locations lack the necessary staff, lack equipment and medicines to serve the population in need (DAI assessment in Northern Rural Homs). At the same time private service providers appear but costs are often high and contribute to driving vulnerable populations further into adopting negative coping strategies to get health needs met. Conclusively one can state that healthcare is available, however to a varying degree, at cost and oftentimes also at a distance. Also major gaps in service provision in health facilities are apparent. According to the REACH Humanitarian Situation Overview in Syria (HSOS) from March 2018, in Homs Governorate only four out of 21 communities reported that no medical items were available in their community, whereas all 21 communities reported that the majority of women did not have access to formal health services (MCH) for maternal care. Which co-relates to the top three most common health problems reported concerning MCHN, such as: 78% of the communities reported severe diseases affecting children under 5, 74% reported pregnancy related conditions and 43% reported maternal health issues (REACH, 2018).Additional to that it is important to mentioned that access to medications related to chronic diseases (such as heart diseases, hypertension, diabetes) cause serious hardship for especially the elderly, as they are expensive and elderly have a very limited resource base, especially since many of their families have fled, have been sub-scripted into the army or struggle to find a well sufficiently well paying jobs to cover such constant needs. Among the different sub-districts where Dorcas plans to support health services it is evident that needs are very high - many times higher than the previous year, due to people starting to return. In 2018 1,250,956 Syrians returned, the top sub-district that welcomed returnees were Jebel Saman, Aleppo (MNM, Dec 2018). 89% of them returned to their own house, often needing to restart their lives there completely. Locations of planned, partially already financed health programming are: Jebel Saman with a severity ranking for health 3 and hosts 1,052,505PiN, Homs has a severity ranking 3 and hosts 401,432PiN, Qatana has a severity ranking 4 and hosts 226,611PiN, Zabadani has a severity ranking 3 and hosts 16,472PiN, Deir ez-Zoor has a severity ranking 5 and hosts 135,986PiN, Mansura has a severity ranking 3 and hosts 31,171PiN, Maadan has a severity ranking 4 and hosts 9,192PiN, Kherbet Ghazala has a severity ranking 4 and hosts 27,850PiN, Dara’a has a severity ranking 4 and hosts 139,984PiN, Talbiseh has a severity ranking 5 and hosts 88,184PiN, Taldo has a severity ranking 4 and hosts 42,398PiN, Harbnafsa has a severity ranking of 2 and hosts 20,459PiN.

Implementation of activities

DAI supports health programming through different approaches, 1 - through supporting private health centers operated by local civil society actors, with training, medicines or to cover needs to overcome gaps in service provision; 2 - through directly working with the ministry of health, where DAI has signed an MoU. This approach primarily targets rehabilitation of damaged health infrastructure, training of staffs and provision of missing medical equipment, supplies and medicines. 3 - Finally DAI also runs mobile health teams that provide assistance to cover urgent emergency health needs, especially in Ar-Raqqa and Deir ez-Zoor governorate.
