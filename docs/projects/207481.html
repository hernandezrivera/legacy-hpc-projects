Project Summary

This programme aims to deliver child protection services to ensure that children are protected from all forms of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect through strong child protection systems and to establish well-coordinated age, gender and disability-responsive child protection services. This will also enhance access to quality integrated child protection services to prevent and effectively respond to cases of child abuse and empower children to protect themselves.
Most vulnerable and at-risk children and adolescents will have increased access to safe and age-gender-sensitive child protection services following the child protection sectoral guideline and activity grouping including , child protection awareness raising, specialized gender-sensitive case management and referral services. The gender-sensitive approach will ensure that the best interests and specific needs of boys and girls are addressed, considering different gender and vulnerability risks, discrimination, and inequalities. This project will provide Holistic and improved case management support. Structured Psychosocial Social Support will be provided following Tdh's Global Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) framework. For the case management support, alternative care for children, and family tracing/reunification services for unaccompanied and separated children and referrals for additional support is included. The non-structured PSS sessions will be conducted at PHCs’ Child Play Areas. The focus of PSS will be on promoting positive coping mechanisms and empowering self-protection. The project will form and train Community Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPCs) to prevent and respond to Child Protection cases. Adolescents will be empowered through Participatory Action Research and arts-based projects led by them to build the resilience capacities of the children towards transformative changes.
Tdh will provide support to caregivers through the Positive Parenting Skills program. To address this issue, Tdh will be providing One-on-one PM+ counselling to caregivers (16 years and above) experiencing stress, anxiety, depression, or difficulties in coping with daily challenges. Referrals to Level-4 services will be made for high-risk cases of MHPSS. To address language challenges, IEC materials will be published in both Bangla, English and Rohingya languages to increase awareness among the beneficiaries.
Tdh will ensure Implementing partner's capacity building thorough capacity need assessment and robust capacity building initiatives. and regular partner monitoring will ensure CP activities is meeting minimum standard following the sectoral guideline and CP minimum standard as well as timely response will be followed by partner progress tracker.
By attending the CPSS monthly meetings, SAG meeting , workshops and providing technical help as needed, Tdh will continue to maintain excellent collaboration with the child protection sub-sector (CPSS), case management technical working group (CMTWG), and CPSS localization working group. Through active participation and contribution, Tdh CP will also coordinate with protection working group (PWG), the mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) working group, the age and disability working group, and the youth working group. The status report, the 5W report, and the need-based report will be produced as per plan to support sectors for overall CP activities, coordination, and planning.
Children and adolescents will directly benefit from the Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities, including individual case management services , structured PSS and non-structured PSS. Community Based CP activities and CP system strengthening and Capacity building.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Currently 965,467 Rohingya people living in Cox’s Bazar and Bhasan Char who are mostly dependent on humanitarian support. Among them 52% are children (UNHCR-Govt. Population factsheet September 2023). Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (JMSNA) 2023 by the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) highlights protection concerns as a top priority in the refugee camps (48%), along with lack of cooking/sleeping space (30%), and low access to services (17%). The 2022 Child Protection Sector Assessment found high levels of child labour (64%), neglect (59%), child marriage (57%) and trafficking (32%). From another study in 2023 among the host community, 63% of children had experienced natural disasters including cyclones (37%), floods (28%), and earthquakes (16%); resulting in psychological distress (24%), loss of education materials (21%), going hungry (19%), limited access to shelter (16%), separation from family (9%), physical injury (8%), and physical and/or sexual abuse (1%). Additionally, MSNA 2023 highlights 48% of respondents reported that the Rohingya community is at protection risk of crime and violence this revealed that parents use negative coping mechanisms e.g., child labor and child marriage because of negative coping strategies. There is also limited access to national protection systems including response (case management) and prevention services. The current level of interventions for out of school adolescents are inadequate to fully empower them with skills. Limited child participation, this is so because children are less visible, valued, and their voices not hard.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The development of self-protection capacities is the kye of Tdh’s Child Protection approach which operates on a socio-ecological level, empowering individuals (Case Management, MHPSS for the children ), support to the caregiver and families (Positive Parenting, PM+), and the wider community capacity enhancement towards child protection (CBCPC), to respond to child protection risks and increase their wellbeing. This will also be achieved through outreach activities which will equip targeted communities with the knowledge to improve hygiene practices, identification of child protection risks, and health-seeking behaviour as well as information regarding services accessibility. strengthening child protection system through capacity building will be another major intervention of this project. The project activities will be implemented following the guidelines from the RRRC and CiCs. Consultation will be continued with the authorities during different phases of implementation. Tdh will continue to build the capacity of health staff, WASH staff, and community health workers, including child protection mainstreaming, as well as volunteers’ support to the Case Management process. This will enable communities to engage meaningfully and substantially in all aspects of Tdh programs. This will also help to strengthen the community’s role as driving force of change, and their capacity to respond. The project will be implemented following the prevailing guidelines of the RRRC and respective CiC through approval of projects from NGOAB and regular consultation with the authorities for the implementation of different activities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Tdh ensures protection is mainstreamed throughout all its projects and endeavors to uphold the Sphere Protection principles and mitigate potential protection risks. This is achieved by:Prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm: Community sensitization, especially with respect to the full participation of women and girls in project activities, will be core to Tdh’s endeavors to do no harm and mitigate the impact of any unintended negative consequences. Protection desks in the PHCs will confidentially address and refer protection cases, preventing further harm. Confidentiality in case management cases will safeguard survivor information and identity, ensuring safety and dignity.Meaningful access: Special attention will be given to vulnerable individuals and groups having difficulty in accessing assistance and basic services. The barriers that prevent children, adolescents and adults, especially women and girls and people with disabilities, from accessing services will be assessed and addressed accordingly. Case management will serve as the mechanism for children to access crisis and longer-term psychosocial support. Timely and appropriate referrals to medical partners, legal practitioners, etc, will be crucial in addressing survivors' needs, given limited health and social support providers in the camps.Accountability: Appropriate CFRM mechanisms are in place for affected populations to provide their feedback on the quantity and quality of services provided, as well as receive timely and appropriate responses. These include ongoing monitoring activities (KIIs, FGDs, etc), as well as formal evaluation exercises.

3.3 - Age & Disability

All participants in camps 26 and 27 and host communities in the catchment area are considered vulnerable based on humanitarian and social criteria. Under child protection activities, Tdh will prioritize women and girls with disabilities, early married girls, and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) survivors. For case management, the selection criteria will be children at high risk of violence, abuse, exploitation and/or neglect. Community outreach sessions will also target the most vulnerable and incorporate age-gender- and disability-inclusive practices. Thd will also follow non Discrimination principles form UNHCR for Inclusion of all gender and age group. Tdh will be responsible for ensuring the inclusion of children of all ages, regardless of their gender or status, in child protection programs and services and ensure that their voices are heard. to ensure these services Tdh a conducted inclusion training for its partner in 2023, including outreach services for children with disabilities who may face barriers in accessing multi-purpose child which will continue in 2024 as well.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Following the IASC and Tdh framework, Tdh will implement activities following three main phases. (I) Taking account: the project team will involve beneficiaries at the beginning of the project to update the baseline data and share all relevant information related to the project and involve them in the decision-making process. (2) Giving account: Tdh will organize a series of consultations focused on sharing information with communities related to the progress of the project activities, especially during multisectoral community outreach sessions. (3) Being held to account: Tdh will organize formal and informal meetings at key moments of the project (beginning, midterm , and annual evaluation) to allow the communities to assess the project and to give positive and negative feedback related to the project performances. The Complaint, Feedback and Response Mechanism that is already established at the camp level will continue getting direct information from the beneficiaries and addressing them through proper channels. In addition, under child protection, Tdh will prioritize women and girls with disabilities, early married girls, and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) survivors. For case management, the selection criteria will be children at high risk of violence, abuse, exploitation and/or neglect. Community outreach sessions will also target the most vulnerable and incorporate age-gender- and disability-inclusive practices. Integrated outreach session will be disseminating key CP, Health and WASH messages following outreach guideline to raise community awareness on child protection and other protection concerns.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Tdh has a Policy on the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). and Active member of the PSEA Network . The Ethics and Compliance Officer along with the PSEA focal of the Tdh will be responsible for implementing and reinforcing the policy, coordinating its daily application, and ensuring that project participants are aware of that. All project staffs and volunteers will be trained on the PSEA policy as a preventive measure of PSEA cases. To prevent SEA cases, Tdh will ensure all staff, volunteers, guards, cleaners receive PSEA orientation and CoC. An effective reporting mechanism will be established to ensure SEA cases are reported through the proper channel. Tdh follows the zero-tolerance policy and doesn't allow any deviation from the victim's rights for SEA cases. Additionally, Tdh will ensure Child friendly PSEA Mechanism including but not limited to aware children on reporting mechanism. confidentiality, proper investigation following the guideline and mass awareness to report the incident if any . Robust prevention and response mechanism will be ensured through PSEA leadership by Tdh's senior management.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Tdh will conduct and complete a risk assessment, considering the emergency situation and operational plan. Based on the findings, a robust Emergency Response and Preparedness plan will be implemented for real-time response. The project aims to address emergency situations such as monsoons, cyclones, floods, fires, and other disasters through a dedicated emergency response team in Bhasan Char, following the IASC ERP approach and guidance from UN agencies. Tdh will actively promote protection mainstreaming across sectors in preparedness and response efforts, with a specific focus on age, gender, and diversity approaches.
