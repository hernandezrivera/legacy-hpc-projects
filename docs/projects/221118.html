Project Summary

The project targets 2,840(2,000 boys and 840 girls) children, adolescents, and youths (3-17 years old) who will have access to educational programming and attend schools in Leer County,Unity State. About 5
temporary learning spaces will beestablished in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and 5 sex-segregated WASH facilities will be established. About ((1,000 girls and 2,000 boys) will have access to new gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys,1:30 for girls). Another 2000(1200 boys and 800 girls) children will benefit from rehabilitated gender-sensitive WASH facilities inline with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60for boys, 1:30 for girls. About 15 teachers (10 males and 5 females) will be supported with teachers' incentives for 12months.
Furthermore, about 1800 (1000 girls and 800 boys) children andyouth (6-17 years) will benefi t from recreational material supplies,and children (3-5 years) will benefi t from Early Childhood andDevelopment (ECD) kits, and another 1,500(800 boys and 700 girls).The adult direct beneficiaries are teachers who will be trained on EiEs, basic pedagogy, and learner-centered methodologies,targeting 15 teachers (10 males and 5 females), and they will also be trained on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms. Other adult beneficiaries are community education committee members and local education officials who will be trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS), and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) targeting 35 people (20 males and15 Females). Active mobilization for school campaigns will be organized to launch back-to-school/learning campaigns at least once per term(Three times a year)

1. Needs

The situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal county (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4 and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees will likely experience Phase 5 (Catastrophe). In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. In the current analysis period (July to September 2024), 53 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 or above (Serious or worse). During the post-harvest period of October 2024 to March 2025, the acute malnutrition situation is expected to deteriorate in 13 counties, while improvement in acute malnutrition is expected in only four counties. The AMN classification in 76 counties is projected to remain the same in the post-harvest projection period. During the lean season (April to June 2025), the severity of acute malnutrition is expected to deteriorate in 62 counties, remain similar in 16 counties and improve in two counties. The situation in Baliet County of Upper Nile State is projected to deteriorate from Phase 4 to IPC AMN Phase 5 (Extremely Critical) because of elevated morbidity and poor access to health services.J4CO with vast experiences in education programming in Leer and other parts of Unity state plans to implement education in Emergencies to respond to the education needs of the most vulnerable children both of the host, displaced, returnees, and refugees children by establishing semi-permanent learning spaces, support teachers, training and incentivize them, establish WASH facilities to promote a protective environment for education in Leer county.J4CO with vast experiences in education programming in Leer and other parts of Unity state plans to implement education in Emergencies to respond to the education needs of the most vulnerable children both of the host, displaced, returnees, and refugees children by establishing semi-permanent learning spaces, support teachers

2. Project activities

undertaken to achieve the overall objectives project/ cluster objectives and humanitarian cluster objectives; The activities are the construction of 5 new Temporary Learning Spaces and 5 segregated latrines of 2 stances each. renovate 2 already existing TLS will be rehabilitated also with 2 sex-segregated latrines of 2 stances each. About 1500 pupils (800 boys, 700 girls) 3-5 years old will be enrolled in ECD, and 3,500 (1500 girls, 2,000) will be enrolled in basic primary school. The project will ensure that of EiE supplies including, ECD, teaching, and learning materials are provided to 3,000 pupils (2000 boys girls 1000). The project will also support the training of 15 teachers (10 males, 5 Females) of Pedagogy, ethics, and classroom management (3 each from the 5 schools) on psychosocial support, and referral mechanisms for Child Protection/nutrition/health and 35 PTA members on school governance. All the 15 teachers will be provided with incentives for motivation for 12 months. Out of the 10 teachers, 5 of them (2 teachers from each school) will be trained in ECD animations and programming. Other activities will include training teachers on PSS and establishment of referral pathways, training adolescent girls on MHM and provision of dignity kits, Conducting Back to learning Campaigns, Community Engagement Meetings.
