Project Summary

The global outbreak of COVID-19 is putting further pressure to the already deteriorating socio-economic situation in Gaza. The restrictions on daily life activities as a result of the measures enforced by the local authority to prevent the spread of the virus are having severe impacts on lives and livelihoods, leading to significant increase in need for assistance. UNRWA continues to provide in-kind food assistance to more than 1 million Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip, covering a part of daily caloric needs, yet the COVID-19 emergency has added further struggles to the people. The population has already been facing years of humanitarian crisis, and many people are relying on daily labour and casual work to secure their minimum requirements, which are heavily affected by COVID-19 restrictions. The families with high dependency ratios, including those with children, elderly and headed by females or persons with disability, are particularly at risk, as they are more likely than others to suffer from poverty or other forms of deprivation. Through this project, UNRWA will provide one-off multi-purpose cash assistance of US$40 to 180,000 vulnerable Palestine refugees identified by its multi-dimensional vulnerability criteria of identified cash-strapped groups including female-headed households, households headed by an elderly person or an adolescent, persons with disability, and persons with special requirements.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic has brought major social, economic and financial shocks, with long-term consequences. Quarantine and social distancing measures enforced by governments to mitigate and suppress the spread of the virus are having severe impacts on lives and livelihoods, leading to significant increases in food insecurity. According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 49.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2020. Palestine refugees, who account for around two-thirds of the total population in Gaza, are facing even higher unemployment rate of 49.9 per cent compared to 47.7 per cent for non-refugees. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 66.8 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2020), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 73.6 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2020). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. 68 per cent of households in Gaza are food insecure, and poverty rates increased from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent in 2017 (SEFSec Survey, 2018). Conditions have since continued to deteriorate, including most recently due to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. PCBS identified 17 per cent decrease in the already low number of employed persons in the local market between the first and second quarter of 2020 in Gaza confirming the impact of COVID-19. The World Bank forecasts that by end of 2020 the poverty rate in Gaza will increase from 53 to 64 per cent, or the equivalent to 37,300 new poor families. UNRWA has a comprehensive refugee registration system as well as information on specific vulnerabilities collected in previous poverty assessment. In July 2020, UNRWA first piloted to provide cash assistance using a multi-dimensional eligibility criteria amidst COVID-19 for likely cash-strapped groups within the population in immediate need of further assistance. This overlaid female-headed households, persons with disabilities, elderlies, children including orphans, and persons with chronic disease. The circumstance of the beneficiaries selected through this approach was further verified to confirm that identified vulnerabilities were still relevant. The same process will be used for this project with further refinement of the criteria itself.

2 - Activities

UNRWA will provide unconditional one-off cash assistance specifically to Palestine refugees whose socio-economic situation has worsened due to COVID-19 developments. The assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable families using UNRWA’s multi-dimensional vulnerability criteria of the most vulnerable groups, who are assumed to be cash-strapped. The status of the listed beneficiaries will be further verified through UNRWA’s developed verification processes, conducted by Relief and Social workers. UNRWA’s Finance Department will arrange the bank transfer based on the verified list of beneficiaries. SMS will be sent to the targeted beneficiaries when the transfer is completed. A phone number will be included in SMS for beneficiaries to contact in case of any issues or concerns. UNRWA aims to provide support to 45,000 vulnerable refugees per quarter with an annual target of 180,000 people. UNRWA has a well-established monitoring mechanism with dedicated Monitoring and Evaluation team who regularly conducts post-distribution monitoring for food distribution. In this project, the M&E team will conduct post distribution monitoring survey and assessment through home visits if the situation allows, otherwise the monitoring will be done remotely.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. The pandemic is resulting in an increase in the protection threats facing groups with multiple overlapping vulnerabilities, which includes those at risk of domestic and intimate partner violence and the elderly. The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse may have also increased, as evidenced in other crisis situations. The Agency is ensuring that protection principles are mainstreamed and integrated in the response, and that special attention is paid to the needs of the most vulnerable. Initiatives to raise awareness on and prevent gender-based violence (GBV) have been launched across all fields. Communication with communities will be supported through dedicated hotlines to provide information on available services. The Agency has also reviewed guidance from the Inter-Agency Standing Committee on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in the context of COVID-19, which will inform the response. Potential risk factor is the community acceptance of limited support provided to the targeted population, while the majority of the people in Gaza are facing financial challenges. UNRWA’s multi-dimensional eligibility criteria takes into consideration major vulnerability factors and uses a clear scoring system to prioritize the people who are at risk in fair and objective manner. The Agency’s comprehensive registration and data management systems ensures clear record-keeping to guard against duplicated efforts.
