Project Summary

The program will strengthen access to lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups in conflict-affected Northwest Syria (NWS) through community and facility-based Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E) services and activities. The NGO will provide Nutrition services in Al Karameh Camp, Atmeh Camp, Southern Atmeh Camp clusters, Kafr Yahmoul community and Idleb City in Idleb Governorate, and Bab Al Salameh camp cluster through a partner in Aleppo Governorate. Children aged 0 to 59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) will be primarily targeted. PLWs are particularly vulnerable due to their specific nutritional requirements and health risks, while young children can be more vulnerable to illness, disease, and death related to nutrition.
Services will include individual counselling lessons, group awareness raising sessions, re-lactation sessions, postnatal support sessions for PLWs. IYCF counselling will include basic and skilled support to initiate early breastfeeding within the first hour after delivery and to support exclusive breastfeeding, cup-feeding, re-lactation, clarification of myths and misconceptions about breastfeeding, the benefits of the first touch between the mother and new-born infant, the benefits of colostrum and support to resolve breast problems. Mothers and caregivers of young children will also be counselled on the importance of continued breastfeeding and appropriate complementary feeding.
Screening will be conducted using the Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) measurements and the presence of oedema to identify malnutrition cases among children and PLWs. Identified cases will be referred to NGO's supervised Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) clinics in Idleb or under partner supervised CMAM clinic in Aleppo. Confirmed cases will be admitted to either Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) or Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) for treatment, dependent upon the severity of malnutrition present. Beneficiaries will be identified through utilizing methods such as systematic identification, screening, referral and treatment of needs for acutely malnourished cases.
The NGO will also build the capacity of service providers, including IYCF facilitators and CMAM facilitators and doctors, and community members through providing routine training on IYCF-E and Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) topics.

4 - Needs

The national prevalence of chronic malnutrition of children is 12.6% in Syria, with Idleb and Aleppo almost reaching WHO’s classification of high level of malnutrition presence. A SMART Nutrition survey by UNICEF in 2019 reports at least one out of three children living in NWS is stunted; more than 130,000 children under 5 likely to not reach their full educational potential. Over half of these children are affected by displacement. Strongly linked to chronic malnutrition is poor infant feeding practices. According to a UNICEF humanitarian response plan report from mid-2019, nutrition surveillance system in NWS indicate that up to 90% of children aged below 6 months are not exclusively breastfed and most mothers are not practicing optimal complementary feeding practices. From the NGO’s needs assessment in June 2019, the primary barrier to eating more food as reported by PLWs is high food prices or no money to buy food (58% respondents), in line with findings from a REACH needs assessment which reported that the price of baby formula increased in Syria by 78% between April and June 2019. Other barriers to good nutrition identified included disease/illness, either caused by or causing malnutrition, contaminated food or water, lack of appropriate medical care, lack of willingness to eat or appetite loss due to the context/emotional state, and a lack of knowledge on how to eat, particularly by parents regarding their children. As a result of displacement, lack of income, and resource constraints, negative coping strategies were also reported; people in 64% of assessed communities reportedly used negative coping such as reducing meal size (reported in 42% of assessed communities). When mothers were asked about breastfeeding, the most common reason was ‘not enough milk’, while other reasons included the children being twins or there being other children, the mother is fearful of airstrikes, and the child is an orphan and/or separated from his/her parents.

5 - Implementation of activities

The NGO will support 3 IYCF facilities and 5 CMAM clinics, and services integrated into 1 Mobile Medical Unit (MMU), 1 maternity hospital and 2 primary healthcare facilities PHCs. The NGO’s Nutrition team will support facility and community-based IYCF activities based on WHO-recommended IYCF practices. Consultations will be provided by trained IYCF facilitators specialized in working with PLWs, mothers, and caregivers. CMAM doctors and nurses will provide women and children with micronutrient supplementation for the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies in the 5 CMAM clinics. The NGO will also support facility and community-based screening for acute malnutrition through MUAC screening for PLWs, and through MUAC, Z Score charts, and detection the intensity of oedema for children aged under 5 years. CMAM clinics will provide treatment for acutely malnourished children and PLWs through an Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP). The NGO will provide training for IYCF supervisors, facilitators, and CMAM facilitators, on optimal IYCF practices, CMAM protocols, identification and causes of acute malnutrition, guiding principles of breastmilk substitutes (BMS) use, communication, counselling and support group facilitation skills. Communities members will be educated on issues affecting the nutritional status of PLWs and children and the importance of optimal IYCF practices through awareness-raising sessions. The NGO will work as closely as possible to monitor activities periodically to maintain high quality by analysing data, weekly meetings, field visits, and reviewing the monthly reports of MEAL Team The targets have been calculated through The NGO's experience working in Al-Karameh, Atmeh and Bab Al-Salameh camp cluster. It takes into account the inclusion of Adult PLWs, adolescent women and secondary caregivers. The activities ensure access for persons with disabilities through access modification.
