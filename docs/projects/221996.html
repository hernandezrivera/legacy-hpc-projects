Project Summary

Help a Child (HAC) – also known as Red een Kind - is a Christian, international development and relief organization headquartered in the Netherlands and active in East Africa and India. Our South Sudan team leads the South Sudan Joint Response (2024-2026) and implements it in Tonj North and Pibor. In partnership with UNICEF, we implement an Education in Emergencies project in Wau County and an Early Childhood Development (ECD) project in Pibor. We also have a privately funded ECD project in Pibor. Internationally, 41.5% of our nearly 13M euros expenditure went towards child development and protection in 2023.
This response will consist of two main components: child protection and prevention of gender-based violence (GBV). With the first component, we prioritize child-centred interventions by providing psychosocial support and material assistance while enhancing local child protection systems in remote areas. This will be achieved in collaboration with communities and partners to ensure effective child protection services, updating referral pathways, and training community leaders in Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) and in prevention and response. These efforts will promote efficient mobilization and adaptability to evolving needs. The project aims to support a total of 8,500 individuals, including 1,275 internally displaced persons (IDPs), 2,550 returnees, and 4,675 members of the host community. The gender breakdown includes 1275 boys, 1700 girls, 2125 men, 2550 women, including 850 (170 boys, 255 girls, 170 men & 255 women) people living with disabilities will be reached across the four targeted locations: Aweil East, Aweil South, Pibor, and Tonj North.
The second component focuses on a strategic GBV approach to address the critical GBV situation in Pibor, Tonj North, Aweil East, and Aweil South. This will involve targeted prevention, risk mitigation, and response strategies through community engagement, integrating GBV actions across sectors, and enhanced access to support services. These efforts aim to foster a safer environment and promote a culture of respect and equality. Collaboration with local stakeholders, government agencies, and international partners will be key to the initiative’s success. The GBV program targets 2,368 individuals, including 355 IDPs, 711 returnees, and 1,302 from host communities. 1. Needs

The 2018 Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) continues to provide hope for peace. However, sub-national violence persists in some areas, leading to the displacement of people and loss of lives and livelihoods. In addition, it increases protection risks, particularly for women and girls and other vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities faced with discrimination and marginalization rooted in gendered social and cultural norms. Root-causes to child protection and gender-based violence persist. Women are seen as property and as a source of wealth that have limited agency in economic, political and cultural aspects of life and are often denied resources, education and opportunities. According to the latest GBV prevalence survey (UNFPA 2023), intimate partner violence for married women (aged 15 to 49) is on the rise (49.6%, physical and/or sexual) and substantial proportions of women (aged 15-64) in South Sudan experience GBV either in form of physical (34.0%) or sexual (13.5%) violence in their lifetime. As the crisis deepens, the need for effective and comprehensive protection strategies becomes increasingly urgent. Displaced individuals, particularly women and children, face heightened risks of violence, exploitation, and abuse, further exacerbating the already fragile humanitarian situation.To respond to these needs, HAC will deliver child-centered protection interventions in Aweil East, Aweil South, Pibor, and Tonj North.

2. Project activities

HAC will undertake community protection and cohesion approaches including providing counselling and psychosocial support for the selected target groups by use of approaches such as What’s Up People as great assessment tool and community engagement, What’s Up children as PSS support, What’s Up Parents for discussing protection concerns, BOB for the younger kids and their parents etc. and for GBV, Help a Child will use Zoom Out in combination with Community Dialogues. A total of 12,814 boys, girls, men, and women will access specialized, structured, and community-based mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services. Additionally, 178 awareness-raising sessions will be conducted with children and caregivers, focusing on child protection (CP) issues, protection risks, counselling, and positive parenting. To address Gender-Based Violence concerns, 132 sessions will be held to educate women, men, boys, and girls on GBV risks, available response services, and the importance of timely reporting.The plan aims to reach 1,600 individuals through group psychosocial support (PSS) services, including recreational and social activities. Moreover, 676 women and adolescent girls will receive dignity kits to support their personal needs. To strengthen service delivery, four GBV referral pathways will be established and made fully functional at the county level, alongside the updating of four child Protection service mapping and referral pathways.Capacity-building initiatives will include training 292 community leaders, humanitarian workers, child protection partners, and government social welfare staff in Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) and specialized training on GBV response and prevention for 92 individuals.
