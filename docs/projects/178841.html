Project Summary

TThe proposed intervention is in line with the WASH 2021 HRP Specific Objectives 1, 2 and 4, aims at addressing the needs of people affected by the conflict in Syria, coupled with the deteriorating context due to the COVID-19 pandemic and worsened by hyperinflationary economic crisis. The draft WaSH 2021 HNO reports that 12.2 million people require WaSH assistance in Syria, with 36% of the population relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water sources. In addition, at least 70% of the sewage networks are being discharged untreated and half of them are not functional.
The intervention will foster synergies with TGH planned Education and Shelter activities. This intervention will be focused on 4 areas: South Idlib, Hama, Rural Damascus and Dar’a. The targeted areas were selected based on their accessibility, the severity of their needs varying between 3 and 5 as per the draft WaSH 2021 HNO, TGH field assessments and consultations with SARC, local authorities and municipalities.
In line with Objective 1, activities 1.1 and 1.3, TGH will rehabilitate boreholes connected to water networks and ensure water supply to an estimated 66,845 vulnerable individuals in As-Saan (Hama), Khan Shaykun (South Idlib), Al’awameed (Rural Damascus) and Tassil (Dar’a). TGH is also planning to rehabilitate sewage networks in order to ensure sanitation in the targeted localities.
As part of Objective 2, activity 2.1, Objective 4, activity 4.1, and in line with Education Sector objectives, TGH will rehabilitate 7 damaged school buildings, secure the access to water for drinking and hygiene purposes and ensure excreta evacuation to increase measures against infection, prevention and control both at school and at home in Khan Shaykun (Idlib), Kafr Batna and Karnaz (Hama), Nashabiyeh and Duma (Rural Damascus), Dar’a and Sheikh Miskine (Dar’a). The intervention will be part of an integrated approach with Education HRP and should benefit to an estimated 5,005 students, teachers and other education personnel.
The proposed intervention has been designed based on TGH 3-year experience in implementing WASH projects (with a specific emphasis on WASH-in-school interventions) in Syria. In 2020, TGH contributed to improving access to basic WASH services to a total of 556,023 individuals through emergency water trucking, rehabilitation or installation of boreholes, pumping stations and Reverse Osmosis units, rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools, and the distribution of hygiene kits and soaps combined with hygiene promotion campaigns. TGH is an active member of the WASH Cluster and has always ensured to report on 4Ws on a monthly basis.
As part of its activities, TGH puts a specific emphasis on abiding by protection principles through providing gender and disabled friendly WASH facilities in schools taking into account menstrual needs, including doors lockable from the inside and lighting. TGH will also ensure to provide WASH facilities accessible for children with disabilities.

4 - Needs

The selected areas of intervention were identified based on field assessments carried out by TGH between December 2020 and March 2021 in Rural Damascus, Hama, Dar’a and Idleb governorates. In Idleb, Khan Shaykun, TGH noted that 100% of the households currently rely on private unprotected boreholes or water trucking for water supply. In Hama governorate, TGH identified high WASH needs in As-Saan where about 21,200 people rely on non-protected private wells for their daily water supply. In Rural Damascus, Haran Al’awameed, TGH identified important WASH needs as there are only 5 functional boreholes out of 13. Also, based on the severity scale of 5, the area has been identified with high needs in terms of access to water and sanitation facilities. The situation in Dar’a governorate is similar with areas of acute needs. In December 2020, TGH assessed the area of Tassil and identified big gaps in terms of sanitation, with not functional sewage networks and water networks, which have been heavily affected by the conflict. In a context of COVID-19, it is a priority to implement WaSH interventions in social institutions, such as schools, in order to reduce the virus propagation among the entire community. The identification of the WASH needs in schools has been done in collaboration with the DoE, and taking into account the severity scale of the Education Sector. The following WaSH-in-school needs have been identified: 1 school in Idleb (Khan Shaykun), 2 schools in Hama (Kafr Batna and Karnaz), 2 schools in Rural Damascus (Nashabiyeh and Duma) and 2 schools in Dar’a (Dar’a and Sheikh Miskine).

5 - Implementation of activities

TGH will coordinate with the relevant local authorities such as water boards, municipalities, SARC and WASH Sector to identify damaged facilities to be rehabilitated and avoid any overlapping or duplication with other organizations. A detailed assessment of the facilities will be conducted by qualified TGH engineers to identify the specific works to be carried out by selected local contractors. Contractor selection will be based on TGH procurement and policies procedures ensuring transparency and accountability all along the selection process. TGH WASH engineers will be in charge of monitoring the works to ensure that works are delivered as per standards. All the works will be handed to over to local authorities, i.e. water authorities or DoE. TGH will also ensure to provide training on how to maintain facilities if specific needs are identified in order to ensure the sustainability of the intervention. As part of kit distributions, TGH will rely on SARC volunteers to conduct the distributions and hygiene awareness sessions under the constant supervision of TGH staff. Monitoring and evaluation will be carried out by TGH MEAL team through baseline and end line household surveys aiming at evaluating the impact of the activities on living conditions. In addition, regular monitoring visits will be carried out in order to consult beneficiaries and operating adjustments if needs be. To support M&E activities and ensure accountability towards beneficiaries, TGH also set up a strong Complaints & Feedback mechanism through hotline, complaint boxes and regular consultations of the local community in order to allow beneficiaries of all age, gender and background to reach the organization.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Risk of theft of kits: TGH works closely with the communities of the supported schools. Not owning a warehouse reduces the risk of theft when the kits are under TGH responsibility. - Toilets safety and risk of GBV: TGH ensures that when working in toilet blocks in schools, these are separated by gender. Additionally, the works that TGH carries, will ensure that include locks for doors and lighting in the facilities.- Exclusion of beneficiaries: e.g. people with disability (exclusion): TGH ensures that all the people with disabilities have access to the facilities built. In the schools the rehabilitations always include accessibility features such as ramps and handles. - Local inequalities – tensions between IDPs, host communities and/or returnees: TGH has a transparent approach for the selection of the locations, boreholes and beneficiaries, based on needs. This and the communication and collaboration with the local leaders and authorities will reduce the risk of inequalities.- Risk of theft of WaSH rehabilitation material: TGH agrees that all the safety of the material while doing the rehabilitation is responsibility of the contractor. Once the works are finished, everything is handed over to the local authorities. We ensure that the authorities and local population takes responsibility and ownership over the material by including them in the assessment and decision making of the work to be carried out.- Exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO) during implementation of activities. TGH ensures that the area of work is safe and has no explosive devises. TGH coordinates with local population, local authorities and official mine action organisations to assure that the areas of work are safe.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

- Abuse and exploitation (sexual exploitation and abuse): All of TGH staff receives PSEA training when joining the organization. Additionally, in each of TGH’s base in Syria there is a PSEA focal point in order to follow any potential issue. For external contractors, TGH ensures that they sign an Ethics Clause along the contract.- Content of kits not gender sensitive: TGH follows the WaSH Sector guidelines to harmonize the content of the kits. The hygiene kits to be distributed are not gender oriented and all the items can be used by both genders.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Suspension of TGH activities by authorities. Maintain trustful and transparent relationships with all counterparts and authorities and seek for authorization for assessments and implementation of activities to the relevant Ministries and Directorates.- Access to project locations remains granted but access to beneficiaries is limited by local authorities. TGH will count with staff from the area of intervention and avoid travels from Damascus.- Increasing of the conflict related fighting. TGH constantly monitors the security situation in the areas of intervention. There will be preventive measures in place in order to ensure the safety of the staff.- Currency fluctuations (SYP towards USD) - Inflation rate increases. TGH has already included the inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inflation TGH will adjust the BoQs accordingly in coordination with the donor.- Sudden upsurge of the COVID-19 cases in Syria (community transmission) and restriction of inter-governorate movements thus disrupted access to project locations. IPC measures for TGH staff in order to avoid a potential increase in the cases among the organization. In case of local lockdowns, TGH counts with field staff in order to ensure a permanent present.- Intervention of the authorities among the selection of beneficiaries. Transparency in the selection of the beneficiaries based on criteria previously agreed upon. - Impact of fuel on rehabilitation, monitoring, pumping activities. TGH carries out regular market analysis in order to try and predict the fluctuation in the fuel prices. TGH also ensures that the facility will be operational with the collaboration of the local authorities by negotiating with them the provision of fuel.
