Project Summary

The project aims to provide gender-based violence response services and mitigate risks for affected populations in the al-Haffa region. The interventions included in this project will contribute to reduce the risks of gender-based violence, enhancing the availability and utilization of comprehensive and life-saving response services to prevent the negative consequences of gender-based violence. This will be achieved by creating a safe space for women and girls. The support services include individual and group psychosocial support, psychological first aid, management of gender-based violence cases, vocational training, and recreational activities. An integrated mobile team will be established to provide services for gender-based violence, sexual and reproductive health. Integrated services complement the services provided in the safe space by reaching the most vulnerable populations and reducing barriers to access services such as long distances to prevent and respond to gender-based violence. Medical services provide essential awareness services for displaced persons, returnees, and host communities, as well as referrals to higher levels of care, including gender-based violence case management in the safe space. This will enhance easy and safe access for women and girls to gender-based violence services, including pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups.
Purchase and distribute emergency supplies to mitigate risks of gender-based violence, in the form of dignity kits.
Disseminate awareness messages on forms of gender-based violence, protection from exploitation and sexual abuse, and community mitigation measures.
In the mentioned safe spaces, Al-Batoul’s policy of sexual exploitation and abuse will be activated through complaint mechanisms of complaint boxes and hotline inside these safe spaces and through the mobile team as well. As in Al-Batoul, All staff will be trained on this policy and will sign a PSEA code of conduct.
Gender-based violence is a terrifying reality and a violation of human rights for women and girls globally. The risks of violence, exploitation, and sexual abuse escalate in emergencies, while national systems, including healthcare, legal systems, social and community support networks, are weakened. The disruption of these systems can limit access to essential services, including sexual and reproductive health services and legal services, creating an environment where perpetrators escape punishment and go unpunished.
When systems and services are disrupted or destroyed, women and girls face increased risks of human rights violations such as sexual violence, intimate partner violence, exploitation, sexual abuse, child marriage, and deprivation of resources. Gender-based violence has significant and long-term effects on the health, psychological well-being, social welfare, and economic well-being of women, girls, their families, and local communities.
The Hafa region has been severely affected by the ongoing Syrian war and the earthquake that occurred on February 6, 2023, in Syria. There has been an increase in displacement and homelessness, as well as economic hardships leading to heightened risks of gender-based violence, especially harassment, sexual violence, and early marriage, prevalent due to displacement. Reports from the United Nations in 2023 estimated that around 7.3 million people, mostly women and girls, were affected.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

AL-Batoul Association’s intervention started in Latakia Governorate in 2022 (with offices in the cities of Latakia and Jableh). Intervention operations increased after the earthquake in 2023. Regarding the targeted area in the project, which is the Hafa region, the population is estimated to be 12,000 individuals, with 300 returning families and 700 displaced families. The number of earthquake-affected families is around 81, comprising approximately 3,200 individuals, spread across 6 villages. The percentage of women and girls is approximately 60%.The Safe Space project aims to address several key issues faced by vulnerable women. Some of these issues include:-Increase in the number of women at risk of gender-based violence.-Presence of multiple families in one house due to poor financial conditions, preventing individual families from securing a safe place.-Loss of income sources for most incoming men and local residents, leading to increased domestic violence due to men being at home most of the time.-Higher percentage of women without providers due to consecutive crises.-Increase in cases of psychological stress and depression by about 70% among incoming and local communities.-Rise in early marriage rates due to economic conditions and displacement by approximately 40%, as parents marry off their daughters to alleviate financial burdens as a coping mechanism for the crisis.
