Project Summary

Humanitarian and protection needs, and risks have been widened, expanded and deepened by the 15 April war in Sudan. Affected populations in Khartoum, Gederaf, Al Jazirah, North Darfur, West Darfur and South Kordofan continue to face significant challenges to access safety, dignified life, freedom, and essential documentation and enforce their rights to housing, land, and property. The 15 April 2023 war has heightened pre-existing risks facing civilian population on the move, in camps, gathering sites, hibernating or transit points.
This protection project will aim to support displacement affected communities and individuals on the move, in camps, gathering sites, in hot spots and in at-risk locations - to claim their rights and access humanitarian and protection services through an integrated NRC protection intervention of Information and Legal Counselling Assistance (ICLA), Site Management, and Protection from Violence. Under ICLA, this will be by; 1) promoting the exercise and enjoyment of housing, land and property rights through creating awareness on HLP rights and contributing towards conflict prevention and non-escalation through promoting the peaceful resolution of disputes over land and natural resources. 2) Improving access to legal identity and civil documentation which will in turn result in the enjoyment of basic services such as education, health, and financial inclusion. Furthermore, the project will 3) work to improve living conditions and access to services in site and site like settings through Site Management through increased participation of women, youth, elderly and people living with disabilities in site governance, refer complaints and feedback to humanitarian service providers at site/camp level.
The HLP component will 1) ensure that displacement affected communities are better informed about their HLP and legal identity rights through information sessions, information education and communication (IEC) materials, mobile counselling, individualized legal assistance and support for collaborative dispute resolution 2) enhance understanding amongst authorities, community leaders, and humanitarian actors on HLP rights and the importance of legal identity and civil documentation through integrated capacity building and 3) promote HLP mainstreaming through adoption of due diligence approach before humanitarian interventions which have an implications on land use and ownership.
For Site Management, the project will work with committees for women, youth, elderly, and people living with disability (PDW) to ensure participation in site management. Build the capacity and awareness of local actors such as community representatives, government officials and local NGOs on Site Management and integrated protection elements. Provision of space for all actors for inclusive engagement. A Complaint, Feedback and Referral Mechanism (CFRM) will run throughout the project in both mobile and static modality.
Protection from Violence (PfV) will focus on protection monitoring, protection risk mapping and assessment, and protection advocacy, community-based protection towards mitigating protection risks and violence, providing individual protection assistance, and capacity building of different actors (staff, partners, local authorities, community leaders) towards improving safe and inclusive humanitarian and protection response, safe access to basic and protection services, respect of rights and protection of civilians, among others, and information dissemination and awareness raising on rights, entitlements, available services, and CFRMs.
The project will reach target unique beneficiaries of 55 910 (60% female and 40% male) vulnerable displacement affected individuals with a budget of USD$ 7 136,640. This updated project contributes to SO1 livesaving and SO2 Protection of the HRP. Gederaf and Al Jazirah state were added as an adjustment too.

1 - Needs

Sudan is experiencing a worsening, deepening, and widening humanitarian and protection crisis since the 15 April 2023 outbreak of war. 4.7Million are IDPs (IOM DTM). Since 15 April 2023, over 1million people are internally displaced (IOM DTM). Over 345 000 people are displaced from Sudan to neighbouring countries like Chad, Eretria, Ethiopia, and Egypt. (UNHCR, 2023). According to Sudanese Doctor’s Union, 865 people died due to the violent conflict between SAF and RSF. Electricity, water, and telecommunications infrastructures as well as businesses and homes, have been damaged, looted or and destroyed. Prices of goods and services soured up heavily. For instance, according to NRC’s observation (May 2023) in Wad Madani, Al Jazirah State, prices of basic goods have risen by 40 – 100%, with bottled water being sold at double the normal price and fuel prices surging to 40,000 Sudanese Pounds (US$67) on the black market, compared to 2,500 Sudanese pounds ($4.2) previously at fuel station. In addition, WHO reports that about two-thirds of hospitals are not functional, with 61% of health facilities in Khartoum closed and only 16% operating as normal, leaving millions of people without safe access to basic health care. This is mainly due to direct violent attacks, occupation by fighting parties, access, lack of electricity, access to water supply or fuel, or stock out of medicine. Humanitarian access continues to be utterly comprised especially with insecurity, beauracratic requests like armed escorts, cumbersome visa challenges, and travel permits (USAID, Sudan,May 2023). And yet operational challenges hamper the delivery of aid at scale – especially limited functionality of markets, limited money transfer options, and lack of cash. The ceasefire agreement signed in May is relatively holding. According to the updated 2023 Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan for Khartoum, West Darfur, South Kordofon, Gederaf, and Al Jazirah : 10.4M are People in Need; 7.767M are targeted, and there is average percentage increase of 64% in targeted populations from the initial plan. The Protection Cluster has 5.4M People in Need and 3.6M people targeted for whole Sudan. According to the Sudan Protection Cluster, there was a reportedly significant increase in the protection of civilian incidents in the Darfurs: armed conflict, armed attacks, robberies, intercommunal clashes, and looting affecting thousands. Prior to the 15 April 2023 violence,29 localities were in high priority for response. Humanitarian access is challenging.Consequently, due to the violent conflict, and legacies of conflict, children are at risk of being recruited into armed elements. Psychosocial needs soured. GBV and sexual violences risks soured too. the clashes have had severe impact on women and girls who have been exposed to sexual abuse, forced prostitution, and forced labour. Men and boys have been susceptible to forced conscription.. Unresolved localised and subnational conflicts remain a threat to civilian populations. Therefore, there is need to prioritise response to hotspot localities, at risk localities, those hosting displaced persons, and in transit with community-based protection, community level protection monitoring, analysis, advocacy, and individual protection assistance. Lack of internal organization and representative community management structures, which indicates that humanitarian responders are defaulting to self-appointed representatives to serve as interlocutors and informers on needs, reducing participation of PSNs. Protection advocacy on protection related HLP, camp site management, and supporting local actors remains imperative.

2 - Activities/Outputs

This project has an ICLA, Protection from Violence, and Camp Site Management Rresponse components. The ICLA response will provide up-to-date, relevant and accurate information on HLP rights, legal identity and civil documentation - NRC will raise awareness about the importance of legal identity, civil documents, and the procedural requirements associated with securing either of these rights. Information sessions are generally brief and generic touching on the above thematic areas and conducted with the aid of information, education, and communication (IEC) materials. Following the information sessions, NRC will provide counselling on HLP rights, legal identity, civil documentation. Legal counselling is carried out to explain the more complex requirements needed to exercise ones HLP rights or access legal identity and/or civil documents. As part of its counselling services, ICLA staff will also facilitate referrals to other service providers to help beneficiaries’ access essential services and other protection interventions. Where the beneficiary is particularly vulnerable, more comprehensive legal assistance will be provided. Legal assistance covers a wide range of activities depending on the circumstances of each case including accompaniment to relevant authorities, support with complicated administrative procedures, financial legal assistance and collaborative resolution over conflicts related to land/natural resources. Lastly, integrated capacity building of local authorities, community leaders and humanitarian partners through trainings, workshops and provision of technical and material support on HLP and legal identity including civil documentation will be provided. Site Management activities cover three areas: site governance and participation, capacity building, and information management and coordination. For site governance and participation, NRC works through with community leadership to increase the active participation of women, youth, the elderly and PWDs increasing engagement with new/pre-existing site-level committees, contributing to area level coordination networks. In addition, community led projects will be implemented based on the needs identified within sites. Awareness raising and information sharing on seasonal issues (flood and fire mitigation) and available services from other service providers, and information available, increasing engagement and awareness of the IDP population. Through trainings for other humanitarian service providers and organisations, NRC hopes to build capacity and understanding of Site Management and how it can support and improve service provision. Information management and coordination covering 4Ws, the CFRM system and other products, while regular site and area level coordination meetings with IDPs and stakeholders feed information from the sites into coordination mechanisms. Protection from violence component within NRC will contribute to three Protection Cluster HRP and NRC strategic objectives through the following: 1) Identifying and referring Persons with Special Needs for specialised services, 2) Establishing and or strengthening existing community based protection structures for violence prevention and protection risk mitigation, 3) supporting projects of community based structures like local p 4) conducting protection assessments and monitoring aimed at informing and improving humanitarian and protection needs assessment, response and advocacy; 5) conducting protection advocacy interventions aimed, 6) capacity building and 7) information dissemination.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection mainstreaming in NRC is integrated applied through our Safe and Inclusive Programming approach, which is the responsibility of all technical sectors and program teamshumanitarian staff. This includes: ● Do No Harm: NRC will review the different modalities and processes for intervention as the situation evolves to ensure they are accepted and safe for the community. ● Community Engagement: community consultations were undertaken to identify key threats, worries, and opportunities on the part of the community. These consultations will continue through the project cycle and will inform design of our intervention. It will also be one of the key parameters to weigh the benefits of our intervention vs the different risks it entails. Before project implementation the project will undertake a gender sensitive assessment (and in most cases NRC conducts gender sensitive conflict/situational analysis) that will analyse potential protection risks associated with the programme activities to inform safe and inclusive project targeting and implementation strategies. The assessment will consider different needs and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls, and of specific groups that may be vulnerable, marginalised, or discriminated against. The project design prioritises participation of vulnerable groups such as women and persons with disability ensuring that they are able tocan safely access services and assistance and removing any barriers that may prevent full and effective participation. ● Inclusion: While the project will be a blanket coverage for all Persons of Concerns (POCs), there are segments of the population that will require additional support and/or specific assistance. This includes people persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other groups that communities themselves will identify as in need of help. To this end, NRC will have a dedicated team to engage in these specific cases when they arise to ensure all are able to access and enjoy the assistance to be provided. The project design ensures that that activities have a ‘protection lens’, intentionally addressing safety, dignity, inclusive participation, diversity, rights, and vulnerabilities. ● Accountability: the Sudan country program is in the midst of establishing its Complaints and Feedback Mechanism. The mechanism will have different channels to ensure all stakeholders of NRC programming are able to share complaints and feedback whilst taking into account digitization opportunities.. Regular monitoring activities with the affected populations around their perceptions of safety, dignity, and agency practice will be carried out. ● PSEA: the Sudan country program has a zero-tolerance policy and approach to reports/incidents of PSEA. Data protection: NRC has SOPs in place for the safe handling of beneficiary and partner data. NRC regularly builds the capacity of its staff, partners and stakeholders on safe and inclusive programming to promote principled and safe interventions

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NRC actively participates in the Protection Sector in Sudan and will collaborate with other related sectors or sub sectors such as the GBV sub sector to ensure that relevant cases are referred according to the established and updated referral pathways. In West Darfur, NRC chairs the INGO forum and has taken lead in developing interagency referral tools and SOPs. Internally NRC will ensure that all pathways, complaints and feedback are shared according to the established inter agency communication channels. At the national level NRC also actively engages in the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) Working Group.
