Project Summary

The overall object of this project is to support affected communities in addressing protection risks and strengthen the Housing, Land, and Property rights of the displaced affected communities including internally displaced persons, returnees, and vulnerable communities in South Sudan through preventative and remedial mechanisms. The proposed interventions aim to provide timely, effective, and quality lifesaving protection responses and service provision to promote enjoyment of Land, and Property rights.
The proposed project will target refugees, returnees, IDPs, and vulnerable host community members in South Sudan for integrated General Protection and HLP assistance.
To achieve the project objective, NRC will focus on two key components i.e. 1. ) General Protection; 2) Housing, Land and Property.
General Protection will be through awareness raising, Protection Monitoring, and Protection Case Management, persons with specific protection risks will receive individualized assistance (Individual Protection Assistance – IPA), including psychosocial support and, as necessary, material assistance. Following the initial rounds of community engagement and awareness raising, NRC staff will work with volunteers in affected communities, training them on peer-to-peer psychosocial support techniques, guiding community-level PSS efforts, and creation of peer support networks. Community-based Protection Community-based protection structures are established or enhanced, and activities implemented.
NRC teams will continue to assess the protective environment for shifts in risks and negative coping mechanisms and adjust awareness-raising messages to better suit the needs of targeted populations. Initially, messages and broader activities will emphasize responding to acute protection needs; eventually, greater primacy will be placed on mitigating new risks and establishing some resiliency at the community level.
Prevention of HLP rights violations will be approached using community structures in dispute settlement, advocacy and through information, counselling and legal assistance The project will assist displaced persons in claiming and exercising their HLP rights and attain durable solutions through the prevention of further displacement by supporting them to overcome legal obstacles related to accessing and controlling housing, land, and property rights. The project will strengthen dispute resolution for community structures through collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR) training to ensure alternative dispute resolution mechanisms are used to resolve HLP-specific cases in the first instance and litigation and advocacy. To further enhance collaboration and multisector responses to forced evictions, the project will work closely with the protection and shelter cluster and other humanitarian actors. Land tenure secured through communal land agreements will be implemented before construction of any humanitarian infrastructure and alternative land will be negotiated and secured for populations at risk of HLP violations.
As the community-based protection systems are often male-dominated and often discriminate against women, NRC will prioritize female beneficiaries during information sessions and legal assistance considering their vulnerability levels. Community participation processes will be used to identify beneficiaries and ensure interventions are both inclusive and community driven. Field level Community engagement through consultations with the affected communities before and after the implementation of project activities. This approach will be used to strengthen community networks while gender will be mainstreamed within all the initiatives.
NRC proposes to target 43,000 beneficiaries (refugees, Internally Displaced Population, Returnees, and Host Communities through HLP and General Population and Housing, Land, and Property Rights Assistance.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains one of the most severe with more people in need of humanitarian assistance. The Protection Cluster estimates 5.53M People in need as of 2023 and 2.4M people are more vulnerable and are target for Protection services response. The conflict in Sudan has resulted in influx of people seeking safety in South Sudan these include South Sudanese returnees, Sudanese and other nationals refugees fleeing the conflict and additionally to internally displaced people (IDPs) and vulnerable host communities.This has led to Increased HLP challenges in areas of return due to illegal occupation, land grabbing and forced eviction, also led to increase in human rights violations, limited access to justice and the rule of law. Areas of focus are Akobo, Bor, Leer, Aweil East, Renk, Koch, Twic, Aweil South and Aweil NorthHLP issues faced by IDPs range from inadequate housing, forced eviction, restitution of land and property left behind coupled with insecurity of land tenure and limited access to agricultural land. Secondary and illegal occupation of property owned by displaced populations is very common and has the potential to fuel conflict, creating major land disputes resulting in secondary displacement and fresh tension. Competing claims over land are not easily resolved taking into consideration the complexities of the land sector in the country. In South Sudan most areas lack a coherent and unified land administration system. The land legislation, though progressive, is also largely unused and multiple rules, procedures, institutions and authorities are frequently competing with one another over jurisdiction in land administration and management. Most of the displaced population lack national identity cards, which further limits their access to rights and services. There is a huge gap between eviction prevention and response. Weak legal and policy frameworks and weak governance structures do not provide IDPs and returnees with adequate protection to ensure their enjoyment of HLP rights. There is a need to ensure that HLP rights violations are managed to protect all those in need. Thus, addressing protection concerns and supporting activities such as shelter and education services that enhance the protective environment of the most vulnerable displaced communities is vital.The areas targeted by this response were acutely impacted by flooding, Conflict and returns. All the areas are largely affected by pre-existing protection burdens, including heightened levels of intercommunal violence and large IDP populations, returnees and refugees. Returns from displacement sites and neighboring countries are ongoing and steadily increasing, which is likely to pose additional challenges to long-term social cohesion and Protection risks, such as increased competition over resources such as land. Land use is still a major concern. It is a source of income, subsistence, and survival, as well as an indicator of community, belonging, and location. Because of its cultural and historical significance, the land is inherently contested and politicized, and access to housing, land, and property is unequal, far beyond economic constraints.The needs for the response were assessed through the ongoing activities of NRC's emergency response and static teams' gaps to respond to Protection needs in several areas across the country. The locations specified in this project were those that were found to have high existing or potential protection and HLP needs and insufficient resources to support affected communities. The Targets for this grant have been set based on NRC's previous experience with response and include estimates of what teams can reasonably address in hard-to-reach areas in the project time frame. Once additional information becomes available and NRC teams are better able to understand the extent of protection and HLP needs in the targeted locations, NRC may undertake a more sustainable response.

2. Project activities

NRC will roll out systematic protection monitoring activities for target locations to better understand their protection needs and enhance evidence-based and data-driven protection responses. NRC will Reach 15,000 people with critical awareness-raising messages to improve protective service seeking and mitigate the worst protection risks resulting from flooding. This will include mass media (radio, pamphlets, and other sources identified by community members) to share messages about displacement-related protection risks. Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) will be provided as one-off or timebound assistance, with limited follow-up. It is specific, intentional, and designed to achieve a protection outcome for the client. 1500 individuals facing specific protection risks will supported through Cash, material assistance or facilitated referrals. To promote community participation and ownership, NRC will enhance and establish 24 community protection committees and train 120 community protection points. To build and enhance their capacity for protection, all the community-based protection structures underwent training on basic community-based protection, basic protection monitoring skills, identification of persons with specific needs, PSEA, basic GBV, and child protection concepts, safe referrals of sensitive cases, and collaborative dispute resolution. NRC will provide training to Humanitarian workers, Relevant authorities, Community Volunteers, Teachers, Social Workers and Community Members on Protection information gathering, dissemination, secure storage and archiving, Assistance in the development of referral systems, PSEA, AGD approaches and Safe and inclusive programming and 300 persons will be trained. Conduct HLP Assessments and Research: The project will invest in understanding the protection and HLP issues affecting the target populations. The findings will inform area-based interventions.Capacity Building: HLP teams will facilitate training activities on tenure security, due diligence, HLP legal frameworks, Land administration and management and women's HLP rights among others. The trainings will target government officials, community leaders and local authorities in the management and administration of HLP.Conduct awareness raising on HLP. Owing to the limited knowledge on HLP among community members that operates as a barrier to the enjoyment of HLP rights, awareness raising activities will be conducted to increase knowledge on HLP. Information will be provided through mass awareness sessions, IEC materials, radio talk shows, drama and door-to-door campaigns. Provide legal aid services. These will be provided through legal counselling and tailored legal assistance. This will include mobile legal aid clinics targeting female- and child-headed households, and those with or headed by disabled persons and elderly individuals identified and profiled during HLP and protection assessments. Support security of tenure documentation. HLP teams will identity and support target populations in need of security of tenure documents to obtain them. The teams will use cash interventions and accompaniment to administrative bodies for processing the required documents. Provide technical and material support to HLP structures. HLP teams will identify knowledge gaps in HLP institutions and respond to these gaps by providing technical support and where feasible provide material support. Conduct advocacy on HLP. Advocacy initiatives in the form of dialogues, meetings and bilateral engagements with relevant HLP stakeholders will be conducted to influence policy reforms, change in practice and behavior to improve protection of HLP rights. Participate in protection cluster coordination. The protection and HLP teams will engage in protection cluster led initiatives such as meetings, leaning events, assessments etc. These will facilitate linkages, coordination, and first tracking of protection and HLP priorities within the HRP.
