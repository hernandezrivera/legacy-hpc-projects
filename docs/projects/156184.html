Project Summary

• 825 Tons of wheat crop will be purchased from local farmers whom harvested their crop in June or from local market to support their wheat production and their income, hence support their resilience. • From the purchased wheat crops, 125 tons of wheat will be cleaned, sterilizes and packed to be distributed to 1000 vulnerable farmers including families headed by women and/or disabled along with agricultural baskets contain: pesticides, herbicide, fertilizer, and rodenticide. The agricultural baskets will be distributed at 18 affected villages at Al Rouj Valley in Idleb district (8 rain fed villages at the South West and 10 irrigated villages at South East of the Al Rouj Valley) using the following criteria for 8 rain fed villages and the beneficiaries' list of 10 villages of farmers benefited from the QRC irrigation project: -Households (HH) who relies on agriculture as a source of income and show interest in this project and its instruction, with access to 5 dunums. -HH headed by females. up to 50% of the total beneficiaries -HH having a person or more with special need. -Large household with more than 5 individuals in the family. -Vulnerable IDP’s and local resident households whom were affected by this crisis. -HH must not receive any agriculture support from other NGOs for this year. The farmers will start planting the seeds in November of 2019, and harvest the crop in May 2020; during this period technical engineers team will conduct capacity building training and give advice on improved production and good farming practice in cereal production such as (soil management, plant protection, fertilization with special focus on organic fertilizers that can be produced using local resources, weeding, control of wheat rust, use of crop residues, post-harvest handling, as well as storage and marketing practices) 5 times during the growing season at 18 different villages. The training helps farmers produce their maximum capacity of harvest by minimizing their losses, in addition to reinforcing a skill set which extends well beyond one agricultural season to ensure high wheat production and yield. Those farmers will be supported regarding their income, and strategic wheat crops will be available for people in the next year which will be reflected on resilience of local economy to have more sustainable assistance. • The second quantity 700 tons of wheat seeds will be sent to mills to produce 560 tons of flour that will be baked to distribute 650,000 bread bundles to 4000 affected and vulnerable IDP families in 33 camps in rural Ma'rat An Numan up to 6 months including families headed by women and/or disabled. Bread distribution criteria will be: Households with 5 or less individuals will receive one bread bundle (1 kg), and more than 5 will receive two bundles˶ • Kids paradise (KP) will be responsible for all procurement according to their standard policies.KP will be responsible for sterilizing and distributing 125 tons of wheat seeds that will be used for planting 500 hectare, purchasing and distributing agriculture inputs, and provide technical agriculture capacity building to 1000 vulnerable farmers including families headed by women and/or disabled, in 18 villages belongs at Ariha & Idleb sub-district at Al Rouj Valley. • Implementing partner INSAN CHARITY (IC) will be responsible for implementing activities aimed to improve food security for IDP's in camps of Ma'ret An Numan and enhancing livelihood opportunities for vulnerable farmers in host communities in Al Rouj valley. IC will determine the quality of 825 tons of wheat crop that will be purchased (testing seed samples using Standard Operating Procedures before purchases) from farmers or local market for bread distribution and agricultural input. IC will be responsible for milling of 700 tons of wheat seeds, storing 560 ton of flour it in their warehouse, contracting bakeries, and distribute 650,000 bread bundles to 4000 families at 33 camps .

Needs

on April 2019, INSAN CHARITY has conducted need assessment on 4000 HH IDPs in the camps in rural Maaret Alnuman . According to the need assessment • 77% of breadwinner are males while 23% are female from whom 84% are widowed. • 50 % of the IDPS stated that the most important needs for them is bread while 48% stated food baskets Need Assessment Report is attached in GMS.On the other hand, On May 2019, kids Paradise has conducted need assessment for farmers in 10 villages at A Rouj valley under rain fed conditions, Analyzing primary and secondary data we obtained from survey led to the following: Due to the deteriorating of economic activities and the loss of jobs led to most people in the areas of study to depend on agriculture as a main source of income (especially needy families). However, the high cost of production, caused the reduction in the economic return for the investment in the agriculture field. in addition to that Most of farmers relies on borrowing to purchase agriculture supplies and wait until harvesting, to pay off their loans (Report is Attached). Kids Paradise also contacted key players of NGO's and INGO's such QRC, Hand in Hand, Big Heart, Acted to insure no overlapping of services occurs. Also, a beneficiaries list for 10 villages (2 villages overlap with the 1st need assessment) received support from the irrigation project for QRC, which will be used to maximize the End Line impact for the QRC project and strengthen the End line outcome for this project to compare the impact of Agricultural Basket on rain fed vs Irrigated wheat production (list is attached).

Protection Risk Analysis

1) PSEA by humanitarian workers impact:high in terms of reputation, trust mitigation tools :( There is separate complaint mechanisms for men and women, since in the needs assessment it was noted that women and girls feel safer with segregation. One of the M&E officers will be female. This will make access to information easier for women and girls, where they will feel safer and more comfortable to ask questions. - Ensure there are a female staff present during distribution -Monitoring the behavior of workers on site, and making sure they are abiding with humanitarian principles and code of conducts.2) risk of GBV : impact :high in terms of reputation,trust mitigation tools :KP and IC will conduct their extension and training sessions, insuring women, and minorities are comfortable with mobility to maximum benefit from the livelihood opportunity, with zero tolerance to sexual harassment.Providing, as necessary, sex-segregated distribution sites and monitoring these sites.3) Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves /with humanitarian workers mitigation tools :(Ensure distribution is well-organized .)4) interference with beneficiary targeting/Beneficiaries lists not regularly updated by the local councils.impact:high as it leads to inclusion and exclusion errors mitigation tools :( conduct need assessment by KOBO and apply vulnerability criteria.)5) risk of coming to the distribution centers impact :high mitigation tools : (People with wheelchairs whom do not have family member to help will also be accessible by delivering the bread to their tents- Scheduling distribution at times that are easily accessible and safe for women, girls and other at-risk groups .

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Assumption: No more IDPs displaced Risk: Increasing in the waves of displacement. Mitigation : Regularly update beneficiary lists and increasing bread quantity in collaboration with partners2- Assumption: Distribution will be implemented smoothly Risk: conflict between IDPs and host community because host community will not receive humanitarian assistance Mitigation : explain clearly the mechanism for selecting the beneficiary list, also we will rely on local authorities, civil society leaders to explain the criteria for selection and resolve any dispute. provide information written, verbal and illustrated like banners, beneficiaries cards .... etc to inform people who qualifies for assistance.3- Assumption: Safe rented warehouse Risk: bombing warehouse Mitigation : looking for a safe place and structure that is located inside a basement and is built with reinforced concrete 4- Assumption: fuel price is stable Risk: Increasing fuel price due to security deterioration. Mitigation : sign contract with the bakery based on stable prices throughout project duration.5- Assumption: Beneficiaries will use bread as it is intended for Risk: Beneficiaries selling bread Mitigation : Applying distribution criteria from 1 to 5 persons (1 bread bundles) and more than 5 (2 bread bundles) 6- Assumption: Security situation is stable in project locations Risk: The area gets exposed to security or military crisis Mitigation : The area that we will work the project at, is considered secure and no major security issues were encountered. However, we will monitor the situation closely to secure our team and the material planned for distribution. In case of security issues arising, operation site will be changed after consultation with OCHA.- Assumption: Quality material is available in local markets Risk: Quality material is not available in the local market for any reason Mitigation : Purchase from neighboring market or cross border through Turkey. 4- Assumption: Agricultural inputs are available in local market Risk: Agricultural inputs are not available in the local market for any reason Mitigation : Purchase from neighbouring market or cross border through Turkey. 5- Assumption: project will go well without issue Risk: Beneficiaries selling seeds, kits, tools to benefit for cash. Mitigation : KP team and local authorities will explain goals for the project, and before the distribution, beneficiaries will sign an agreement not to sell. Also, regular field visit by M & E team will reduce any irregular selling activity6- Assumption: Training will be conducted on time without delay Risk: Security issues such as shelling or local fighting that could affect the training. Mitigation measures : Delay the training couple days, until things calm down, or move the Implementation of activities to different location, after consultation with the cluster, OCHA.
