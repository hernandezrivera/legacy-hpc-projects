Project Summary

Through the project WFP will provide cash and food support to 140 400 refugees in the targeted regions of the Far North, East, North and Adamaoua, in line with WFP Cameroon Country Strategic Plan (CSP 2022-2026). Targeting will be refined alongside physical verifications to ensure that only the most extremely food insecure households will be assisted. The project proposes to continue providing lifesaving support to extremely vulnerable CAR refugees households to ensure they are able to meet their immediate food security and nutrition needs, as well as essential needs.
The transfer modality of food assistance to the beneficiaries will be initially in-kind food through unconditional monthly food distributions to support refugees’ households’ access to adequate and nutritious food, therefore prevent them from adopting negative coping practices. The full ration of the in-kind distributions will provide 1, 095kcal energy per day per person for the beneficiaries who entirely rely on humanitarian food assistance. A minimum percentage of 50% of beneficiaries will be gradually moved to cash assistance at the end of 2024. Mechanisms will be put in place to evaluate the evolution of household’s needs to ensure a graduation approach from one form of assistance to the other.
This assistance will be coupled to the nutrition prevention and treatment activities, WFP will provide support for children aged 6–59 months, this will include blanket supplementary feeding, counselling and support for moderate acute malnutrition treatment and improvement in adherence to treatment; mother and child health and nutrition programmes; and gender and nutrition- informed social and behavior change communication as part of an integrated nutrition package. The daily ration for the specialized nutritious foods per assisted child will be 100 g.
Communication activities will be intensified to inform on the temporal nature of food assistance and the need to graduate towards early recovery and resilience activities as well as SBCC messages formulated to accompany food security and nutrition activities.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Cameroon currently hosts around 300,162 Central African refugees. In 2019, almost 20,580 Central African refugees were registered (births and new arrivals) in Cameroon. Almost 76,000 refugees are settled in the sites built by UNHCR and more than 183,000 have settled in host communities in numerous villages in the three border regions (East, Adamawa, North).Regular large influx of refugees has been common in the East following major political events happening in Central African Republic since 2005, with the biggest waves seen in 2008 and 2014 (more than 107,000 persons). By end 2021, new influx of more than 25,276 refugees had arrived in the East Region following tribal tensions and clashes between non-state armed forces. This has continuously exacerbated pressure on natural resources, especially land and food, stimulating conflicts between pastoralists and farmers. Insecurity along with criminality has equally increased, with more regular cases of kidnappings and robbery recorded. In 2023, the refugees were estimated at 277,649 spread across 3 of the 4 departments in the Eastern region, more precisely, the Lom & Djerem, Kadey and Mbomba & Ngoko departments. Their presence has increased the pressure on already insufficient social services exacerbating food insecurity. According to the Cadre Harmonize exercise of November 2023, 2.5 million people are projected to be food insecure in Cameroon with an estimated 373,736 in IPC2 and 3 in the East Region with consistent risk of falling into IPC 4&5 in an event of shock.In the Far North region, around 67000 Nigerian refugees who fled the Boko Haram insurgency live in the Minawao camp, with the remaining living within host communities. Against the background of high vulnerability levels, more than half of refugee households’ resort to negative livelihood coping strategies to meet essential needs. According to the statistics of UNHCR in October 2023 (Monthly _Statistics_31102023.pdf), 61% of the population living in the Minawao Camp is composed by minors.The results of the SMART/SENS surveys conducted in December 2022 by the Ministry of Public Health, UNICEF, UNHCR and WFP showed a deterioration of the nutrition situation for children aged 6-59 months in the Far North, North, Adamawa, and East regions. It also showed alarming prevalence of wasting among Central African refugees living in camps, and a very high prevalence of stunting (exceeding emergency threshold) in the whole country, with a maximum of 1 in 2 children affected among the refugees and Internally Displaced Persons (IDP). Severe underfunding for nutrition interventions, leading to a lack of supply of ready to use therapeutic food (RUTF), negatively impacted the response to malnutrition in 2023 (Cameroon: Humanitarian Dashboard-January-March 2023). The prevalence of acute malnutrition in children under 5 years old among off-site and on-site refugee populations in the regions of Adamawa, East, North and Far North regions of Cameroon were alarming with the Adamawa and East regions recording the highest rates (17.4%) which exceed the intervention threshold of 10% for off-site refugees in these regions and the emergency threshold of 15% for refugees in camps for both regions. The MART/SENS survey equally showed that all refugee populations in the four regions, except for Minawao camp, have higher rates (at the emergency threshold) for severe acute malnutrition while all strata involved in the survey had emergency thresholds for chronic malnutrition. UNHCR and WFP joint assessment monitoring survey conducted in 2019 revealed that 44% of households are headed by females and an average household is made of six persons with four children under the age of 18. Moreso an intention of return survey conducted by UNHCR revealed that not up to 30% of CAR refugees have an intention of returning to CAR.
