Project Summary

In South Sudan, conflict, insecurity and climate shocks remain significant drivers of humanitarian needs (SSHNO 2023). Indeed, World Relief South Sudan recognizes the poor understanding of the link between good hygiene behavior and safe water to desired health outcomes. The poor understanding in South Sudan continue to largely being exacerbated by undeveloped rural WaSH practices and systems, thus resulting in high mortality and morbidity rates across the country. Fangak, Ayod, Pigi/Canal and Koch Counties in greater Upper Nile region have been particularly affected by this phenomenon and made prone or risk level heighted and as a result, communities in aforementioned counties are consistently exposed to water-related diseases and soaring morbidity rates.
Much of these are due to myriad reasons including the roles of government, humanitarian actors, communities and private sector. The government has struggled with weak capacity and inadequate resources to properly install and monitor the maintenance of WASH infrastructure and services. Humanitarian agencies have installed thousands of WASH infrastructure with insufficient strengthening of community management structures and little regard to increase community ownership. Communities have not adequately taken up their role in administering their WASH infrastructure, resorting instead to unhygienic practices when water points or latrines break, thereby increasing exposure to water-related diseases. The private sector and local institutions lacked the means & incentives necessary to provide high quality services in support of rural WASH needs including poor WaSH governance, conflicts, displacement and supply chain.
In response, WRSS will work with donors, communities and governments to address these needs premised on the “Theory of Change that if you increase understanding of the link between hygiene, sanitation and safe water services to health behaviors and if you strengthen the structures and systems to support communities with these practices, then communities will play an active role in managing these services because they ultimately want their families to be healthier”.
Together with others, WRSS will pursue to improve hygiene practices through intense messaging using key influencers and mass campaigns focus on safe water chains, hand-washing at critical times, latrine use, and solid waste management. Response will be linked to educations, health and nutrition within the areas to ensure change agents are fluent on hygiene messaging. Hygiene awareness will be used to create demand for WASH infrastructure, economical and efficient latrine options while supporting communities to develop capacity to proactively manage and maintain these facilities. WRSS will establish community led supply chain for spare parts to assist maintenance with intent to work alongside the state and county governments, taking up coordination roles, monitor quality control of repairs and rehabilitations.
WRSS is also committed to upholding the dignity of all the people it serves protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. WRSS Child Protection Policy and related documents will be put to use for strengthening protection mechanisms and building staff capacity to identify, respond to, and prevent abuse and exploitation of children. WRSS has also established CFM infrastructures and trained throughout its program areas to mitigate related risks.
Gender will also be mainstreamed in all WaSH establishments including Water User Committee (WUC) formation and strengthening and sanitation activities, including themes such as SGBV, developing co-empowerment strategies for men and women, and the involvement of women in decision-making.
With inclusive approach, WRSS will support safety audits to ensure safe and equitable access to WASH services. The elderly, youth, middle-aged and those with special needs will receive recognition of their conditions that can limit to WASH services.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to suffer complex shocks of floods, displacements, food insecurity, inflation, and lack of access to basic services and disease outbreaks. At least 6.1 million are in need of WaSH assistance. While the political and economic uncertainties continue to linger, and civilian’s means and capacity to restore livelihoods and curb humanitarian consequences like excess morbidity and mortality are overly constrained. South Sudan’s humanitarian needs overview (2023) revealed that Fangak, Ayod, Pigi/Canal and Koch are counties classified as extreme Severity of humanitarian conditions. estimated 69,000 people across greater Upper Nile as affected by violence of which 35,474 were children exposed to major risks, including family separation and trauma. Over 1 million people got affected by floods due to torrential rain and flooding across 36 counties of South Sudan further exacerbating the already dire living conditions of the population and propagation of communicable diseases, maternal mortality and neonatal health that continue to be the leading causes of morbidity and mortality in South Sudan and in particular greater Upper Nile where access to basic services remain unmet. In the target locations, key barriers to securing clean and safe water are lack of collecting and storage vessels, inadequate knowledge to use the PUR and Aquatab. WRSS conducted KAP survey revealed popular vessels used for water collection are jerry cans (35.9%) and buckets with lids (25.3%), and the most popular method of water treatment is the use of Aquatab/PUR. Multi-purpose soap is also highly sought for general hygienic behavior. In Fangak has 175285 people in WaSH needs and severity is 5. Only 11% have access to basic sanitation whereas 73.5% had access to hygiene promotion and soap and 75% had access to improved water sources. KAP survey (WRSS March 2023) revealed 44.7% knew ways of diarrhea prevention, 60% knew at least three (3) of the five (5) critical times to wash hands, and only 54.4% practiced it. 43% of households’ handwashing facilities whereas 27.2% had soap. Drinking water source mainstay remains significant challenge with even more proportion drawing water from surface water sources (rivers, streams, ponds, puddles). Hepatitis E cases reached above the epidemic thresholds as declared by MoH in September 2023 with 19% CFR (Epi-week 40). According to OCHA’s Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2023, Koch County has 80,099 people with humanitarian needs of which 630318 are WaSH related needs. WaSH infrastructure and systems are significantly underdeveloped. WRSS KAP survey revealed that 16.7% households had designated handwashing location with water, and 15.4% had handwashing facilities with both water and soap. South Sudan WaSH cluster HRP dashboard 2023 revealed Koch County as severity 2, individuals reached with improved drinking water sources was 25207, reached with sanitation interventions was 13949 and 39.2 % reached with hygiene promotion and soap.According to South Sudan WaSH cluster 2023 HRP dashboard, Ayod county WaSH severity is 3 and 178,800 people have significant humanitarian needs. Only 29.31% have been reach with basic sanitation, and 60% with improved source of potable water and 58.8% reached with hygiene promotion and soap. Women and girls with improved access to water and sanitation after audit safety was 53997 individuals.In Canal/Pigi County, HRP dashboard 2023 update revealed WaSH severity 5 with over 101295 people who have significant humanitarian needs. Only 17.2 % have been reach with basic sanitation, and 80% with improved source of potable water and 78.4 % reached with hygiene promotion and soap. Women and girls with improved access to water and sanitation after audit safety was 40.7% individuals.Response to these myriad of WaSH needs may include increased access to improved water sources/ water treatment goods, increased access to sanitation facilities and improved hygiene practices.

2. Project activities

WRSS intervention strategy would align with strategic objective to contributing towards reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality in the priority counties and Providing access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties through WASH integrated programming with health and Nutrition subsequently allaying hunger reduction and climate change sensitive approach (Adhere to thresholds of environmental markers). Response to myriad of WaSH needs will include increasing equitable access to improved water sources/ water treatment goods, increase inclusive access to sanitation facilities and improved hygiene practices both at institutional and community levels. WASH non-food item (NFI) distributions will be based on the need of the beneficiaries. WASH NFIs will be distributed through community and nutrition programs to optimize desired public health outcome. Community distributions will respond to acute needs, based on validated assessment or public health concerns that emanate from flooding or displacement. Distributions through the nutrition program will target children or pregnant lactating women (PLWs) enrolled in the program to provide a long-lasting supply for those families with intent to impact recovery and prevent relapse. Distributions will also prioritize extremely vulnerable households such as female-headed households, households with a member who is disabled, elderly, and pregnant or lactating women. Hygiene kits received from the core pipeline will be distributed in compliance with response strategy including the HeV WaSH response strategy. Distributions will be standardized in each location, but based on the needs of beneficiaries in that context. WASH infrastructure rehabilitations, including water points and sanitation facilities will be provided in strict adherence to sphere and national cluster standards including water quality monitoring. In view to ensure ownership of the response across all spectrum of beneficiaries, WRSS will facilitate equitable formation of WaSH resource groups ( Water users Committees; Community handpump mechanics etc.) and provide targeted trainings and basic equipments for operation and maintenance of established WaSH system,jobaids will be provided to selected community members as volunteer community hygiene promoters for hygiene promotion, and who will also liaise with health and nutrition facility staff to target beneficiaries who seek health and nutrition services. Community Hygiene Promoters will also closely liaise health and nutrition extension workers such as community nutrition workers, Boma Health Workers (BHWs) to reinforce community-based outreaches including referrals for safe services. At community levels, door-to-door campaigns, clean water source campaigns, participation in global WaSH events such as Global handwashing day; World Toilet day, menstrual hygiene management day and World Water day to accelerate awareness linking poor sanitation; poor water quantity & quality and poor hygiene practices to diseases outbreaks; morbidities and mortalities, and to ensure collaborated transmissions of key WaSH messages are inculcated in health, nutrition and basic learning institutions. World Relief will also ensure there are deliberate mechanisms for meaningful access to services; applicable “Do no harm” plans, optimized inclusion and participation of beneficiaries, and decision making among program participants are significantly simplified & inclusive. Emphasis will be made to ensure age-gender and diversity considerations are optimized throughout the project cycle. WRSS will decommission water and sanitation systems that have aged and poses public health risks to program participants. Equally important mechanisms will be established to adequately focus on complainant response and feedback mechanisms, PSEA/Child protection policy as well as internal controls and compliant thresholds.
