Project Summary


The proposed project is aimed to immediately response and address to humanitarian needs of recently internal displaced people (IDPs) that affected by recent clan armed conflicts and recurrent droughts in the regions of Sool and Sanaag. The recent sub-clan armed conflicts erupted in number of villages under Lasanood district in Sool region, this include Dhummay, Dharkayn, Dabotaag and Tukoraq villages. The sub-clan armed conflicts had also occurred in El-afweyn, Fadhigab and Dararweyne of Sanaag Region, According to report of local NGO called SVO in Sool region these clan militias fighting caused death, injuries destruction of properties and huge internal displacements. In addition to this, the consequences of drought is perceptible in most of villages of these regions where urgent needs of internal displaced population is shelter and Non-Food Items (NFIs) to protect their vulnerable families from sun, cold wind and rains. Furthermore, poor shelters put at risks to women and girls to experience various protection problems such like rape, sexual violence, domestic violence and lack of their privacy. New IDPs reside in various villages of Lasanod, Erigabo and El-Afweyn districts.
Therefore, the purpose of the project is to provide lifesaving and life-sustaining assistance to people in need for shelter with prioritizing the most venerable men, women, boys and girls through procurement and distribution of non-food items (NFIs) and solar lamps. CCBRS will also conduct awareness raising and capacity development on community peaceful coexistence & social cohesions and minimizing of incidents of GBV and child protection violations.
CCBRS will conduct rapid need assessment to identify and register project beneficiaries with consideration of most vulnerable families such as large families, female and child headed households, persons with special needs. CCBRS's selection of beneficiaries, distribution and implementation strategy will be gender and age-sensitive to ensure that most vulnerable groups to fully access to lifesaving assistance and services. The project plan is to target a total of 4,000 households who will directly benefit from NFIs distribution and 750 from solar lamps distributions, the beneficiaries will thus consist of 28,500 individuals including (7480 women, 7320 girls, 6700 men and 7000 boys). The project will also sensitize and develop their capacity a total of 720 people that consist of (female 210, male 267, girls 113 and boys 130).
The project will be linked with other projects/programs of cluster members in the ground such as UNHCR, UNOCHA, NRC, and DRC and will also be involved local authorities in project planning, selection of beneficiaries, distribution and monitoring.

Needs

Over the past five years, as result of climate change, Somaliland which is an arid and semi-arid environment is repeatedly experienced episodes of recurrent droughts that mainly affected to the agro-pastoralists and nomad communities. It affects enormous percentage of the population in different ways including loss of life, crop failures, food and water shortages that may lead to malnutrition, health problems and mass displacement. Most affected areas include remote areas of Sool and Sanaag regions. In this year, although Deyr rainy season began across many parts of Somalia in which the forecast of Greater Horn Africa Climate Outlook Forum (GHACOF50), indicates a high probability of average to above-average rainfall between October and December 2018. But according to humanitarian Dashboard issued in October 2018, reported that humanitarian need persist. Many nomad communities migrated to other regions of Somaliland to search water and grass for their animals. Elderly people, persons with disabilities, women and children were left behind while men and youth travelled with animals. However, thousands of internal displaced families resided in outskirts of main towns and villages, those families have multiple problems in living and need emergency response. Moreover, most of districts or villages locate in remote areas have very rough roads and security challenges that interrupted humanitarian response to be on time. Despite this, sequence sub-clan and territorial dispute armed conflicts frequently happened in Sool and Sanaag regions make situation difficulty. According to LNGO called SVO that operates in Sool region, the clan fittings caused huge displacement of pastoral nomad communities who have already struggling with consequences of the recurrent droughts. However, the displaced people live in makeshift huts that made of old clothes and cannot protect them from rain, sun, wind and cold. Furthermore, GBV and child protection violations become more concern.

Activities

As part of the response strategy of the project, CCBRS has planned the following project activities: • CCBRS will conduct needs assessment in selected 12 villages/IDP settlements in collaboration with relevant clusters and local authority to identify most vulnerable households in need of shelter/NFIs and Solar Lamps. • Community committees of target villages are trained into project goal, objectives and implementation strategy. • CCBRS will establish and train shelter/NFIs distribution community committees to help effective distribution of NFI materials. • CCBRS will carry out shelter induction workshop to make understand target communities to understand mode of NFIs and Solar lamps distribution and proper usages. • CCBRS will procure 4000 NFI kits, transport and distribute to most vulnerable households. • CCBRS will also procure 750 solar lamps and select and distribute to most vulnerable IDP households • Post distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted to target villages to ensure the efficiency and effectiveness of distribution and if most vulnerable households are addressed. • CCBRS will conduct 12 sensitization workshops on community peaceful coexistence and cohesions at IDP sites to contribute peacebuilding process, traditional and religious leaders, youth, women and other influential people will be targeted. • 12 awareness raising workshops will be held at IDP settlements on prevention and response to GBV and child protection violations. So that to reduce the risk of GBV and child protection violations in the IDPs and help their vulnerable survivors. • CCBRS will establish and train GBV and child protection community committees from target project sites. The committees will help CCBRS staff to timely identify, assess and refer GBV and child protection survivors.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project activities will take place in the newly established IDP settlements of Sool and Sanaag regions. The security of IDP sites is quite safe and building resilience and community peaceful coexistence can create durable solutions of the project. CCBRS has a long experience for implementation of community based/oriented projects to generate long-term sustainability and durable solutions. Generally, rapid internal displacement make changes to community based support structures and positive resilience to recover from adverse conditions. In regards to the cause of internal displacement of clan armed conflicts and recurrent droughts, CCBRS will thus build community support structures and cohesions to improve and readjust their living conditions and self-reliance. The distributed Non-food items and solar lamps will be the beginning of constructing their durable shelters. Since the origin of most of IDP households is from pastoral nomad community and they have already been living in simple shelters then the distributed NFIs will give a momentum to rebuild their durable huts. Through awareness raising and capacity development, the community will be prepared to solve their problems, transform clan conflicts and improve their resilience and resume their living with energy and productively. The capacity of community support structures and local authorities will also be strengthened to prevent GBV and child protection violations and to support their vulnerable individuals and families.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Sool and Sanaag regions have been experienced to a diverse humanitarian crisis over the past two decades including episodes of recurrent droughts, clan armed conflicts and territorial disputes between Somaliland and Puntland that resulted in huge displacements, poverty and protection violations. Moreover, most of districts and villages under these two regions locate in remote and poor infrastructure roads that make difficulty to receive timely for humanitarian assistances. Therefore, competition of little resources such as grassing, access to water points and land possessions are the basis of sub-clan armed conflicts in both Sool and Sanaag regions. Therefore, in order to reduce the risks of clan conflicts and strengthen resilience to recurrent droughts CCBRS will do community awareness raisings and mobilizations to create community peaceful coexistence, revitalize indigenous conflict transformations and encourage local measures to mitigate persistent droughts. CCBRS will also distribute NFIs and solar lamps to improve shelter conditions of displaced communities. The project will engage various community sectors and make sure that all different gender and age groups to be part of the beneficiaries The project will also sensitize community support structures and local authorities to minimize risks of GBV and CP violations to enable to assist whose vulnerable survivors with timely identification and referral to appropriate services.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

CCBRS is a local NGO which is more sensitive to gender issues in order to ensure that women to play important role in all social mainstream activities. Nevertheless, more than 60 percent of CCBRS staff are significantly women. Since, the organization implements projects focusing on empowerment and protection of women and children we provide important role for women to provide care and compassionate for survivors. Therefore, CCBRS will promote strategy that different gender and age groups will equally benefit from support services and distributed NFI materials to ensure that nobody is left behind. CCBRS will significantly encourage inclusion of women and children in all established community committees which involve in decision making process for example distribution committees for NFI materials and GBV and child protection committees.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Clan armed conflicts and border disputes between Somaliland and Putland is major security challenges resulted in community internal displacements in particular Sool and Sanaag regions. The Sub-clan armed conflicts in El-afweyn district of Sanaag region and number of villages under Lasanood district of Sool region cause usually a massive population displacements. The war affected communities are lacking to basic needs, particularly poor shelters and protection risks to women and children are major challenges. CCBRS has professional staff in peacebuilding and have already integrated peace building initiatives into all its projects. We have therefore, planned to include this project some awareness raising activities and capacity building workshops to promote community peaceful coexistence and social cohesions. Youth, traditional &religious leaders, women and parents will be used as a change agents of conflict transformations to which culture and local norms will be the basis and pillars of peacebuilding interventions. The project will also create activities driven to social collaboration and cohesions to build community trust and friendship. Psychosocial support and community counselling will also be a part of the project to strengthen coping and resilience mechanisms of the war affected population.
