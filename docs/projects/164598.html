Project Summary

The project will be implemented in 4 locations in northwest Syria within sub-districts. The first location is in Idleb city, where the severity scale is 5
and the people in need for protection services is 207,600. The second location is in Dana city where the severity scale is 6 and the people in need for protection services is 712,400 children. The third location is in Azaz city where the severity scale is 6 and the people in need for protection services is 251,700 children. The fourth location is in Dart Azza city where the severity scale is 4 and the people in need for protection services is 86,300 children. After signing memorandums of understanding with the local councils in those locations, 4 Child-Friendly Spaces will be established to provide psychosocial support for 6500 children (G: 3250 B: 3250) and parenting programmes for 800 people (W: 400 M 400),Those will be implemented in CFSs, schools, community centres as well as will be provided via mobile teams. The project will also provide awareness-raising and community events to prevent and respond to child protection issues for 2000 people (W: 500 M: 500 G: 500 B: 500). The project will support 40 adults (W 20, M, 20) members of the Child Protection Networks that will be established to ensure the community’s active participation to prevent and respond to child protection issues. The project will also provide case management services for 300 vulnerable children including CAAFAQ, UASC and CWDs, in addition to 40 (W 20, M, 20) trained on child protection in line with child protection minimum standards. Also, risk education from humanitarian risk-education actors will be provided for 13816 (G: 5900, B: 5900, W: 1008 M: 1008). AFAQ will ensure that the planned interventions in this PD consider the needs/interest of girls and boys, women, men and their unbiassed participation. The project will avoid perpetuating inequalities based on gender and avoid discrimination. Fairness to both girls and boys, men and women will be promoted so that ultimately there is equitable benefit from the project. AFAQ ensures protecting the rights of members from affected communities and ensures that no harm is caused because of its activities. AFAQ staff sign a code of conduct that includes protocols to ensure that sensitive information is kept confidential. Consistent with AFAQ’s standard approach to humanitarian programming, the project is designed to observe the Do No Harm principle. Monitoring and evaluation will be progressive and ongoing.

4 - Needs

Based on MRM4Syria 2020,the UN verified 1,792 such violations against children in 12 of 14 governorates of Syria, illustrating persistent trends of violence though not the full scope of protection concerns affecting children.Events leading to family separation indicate the interconnectedness and diversity of vulnerability factors with death (24% for boys and 22% for girls), economic need (20% for boys), child marriage (22% for girls) and child recruitment (14% for boys) among top-cited reasons for family separations.Recruitment and use of children by parties to the conflict continues and is alarmingly becoming an accepted practice in some parts of Syria.The Syria MRM verified 519 incidents of recruitment and use of children (93% boys), with 96% used in combat roles, and 20% under the age of 15.One in two people in Syria live at risk of explosive hazards.Based on current population estimates, 11.5 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 2,562 communities reporting explosive hazard contamination in the last two years.Explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities, depriving them of livelihoods and sources of income, increasing vulnerabilities. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Child Protection: Activity1: Make community-based child protection for girls and boys available in targeted locations. Protection Risk: -Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigating measures: -Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. -Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Activity2: Make specialized child protection services, e.g., case management, for girls and boys available in targeted locations. Protection Risk: -Risk of increasing violence in the home and community for girls and boys receiving specialised services. Likelihood: High, Impact: High. Mitigating measures: - Training practitioners and service providers on doing no harm. - Ensure support of relevant authorities to provision of specialised services. Activity3: Strengthen technical capacity of human resources to respond to child protection concerns in Syria. Protection Risk: Impact of activity is minimal on children's protection. Likelihood: Low, Impact:Low. Mitigating measures: -Link with other capacity building initiatives to ensure synergy.-Support development of a cohort of core child protection trainers in Syria.-Encourage follow-up, mentoring and coaching initiatives. -Designing/supporting the development of uniformed/interagency training packages to standardize capacity building. Activity4: Conduct risk education for at-risk groups. Protection Risk: -Familiarisation may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigating measures:- Emphasis on "Stop, Warn, and Report" messagging in risk education.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks that are beyond the project’s control might be the physical security risk due to conﬂict activity, in addition to barriers for certain groups in access centers (limitations on movement). The risks might also be that services provided in centers not adequate to address the scale of protection issues and sustainability of centers given the current funding environment. The project would mitigate these risks by collaborating with key members on accessibility mainstreaming of centers and assessing the impact of community centers through baseline and end-line surveys, in addition to advocating with donors for direct funding to Syrian organizations to ensure continuity of centers. The project will also ensure center staff is trained on PFA and ﬁrst aid. Moreover, there is a risk that is beyond the project’s control, which is targeting the centers by airstrikes during providing the services. The project would mitigate these risks by community engagement from the onset of the project, from the assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders. The mitigating measuresmight also be safety measures for low visibility and underground centers so that the centers are safe from the airstrikes. Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. The project will mitigate this risk by ensuring a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed, in addition to ensuring consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to the location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Ensuring safety audits for access are conducted for each ﬁxed site or mobile service location.
