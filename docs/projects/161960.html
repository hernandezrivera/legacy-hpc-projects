Project Summary

PULSE operates a Multi- Purpose Child and Adolescent Centre in camp 9. Boys and girls represent 55% of all refugees (UNHCR Population factsheet, 15 September 2019). The project will address child protection needs and risk through providing:
a) 70 children access to CFS
b) 300 children and adolescents with life skills trainings and resilience activities (Sewing, Computer, English, Youth Clubs)
c) 600 parents with positive parenting sessions
d) 1000 community members with key protection risks messages
The goal is to provide a quality, need-based intervention that leads to conscious internalization of protection messages and basic life skills. Children and adolescents become aware how to stay safe and where to ask for help. They learn social skills: how to cooperate with each other, take decisions together and express their needs. PULSE’s learning priorities: protection, hygiene and health, safe water and food, environmental awareness, cooperation and self-directed learning.
Child protection issues need to be addressed in an entire community. PULSE will organize awareness raising sessions and positive parenting sessions to strengthen Community Based Child Protection. Community will participate in social events such as photo and art exhibitions that tackle protection issues and encourages dialogue and discussion about community’s needs and challenges.
Apart from fixed groups (aimed at organizing long-term, participative activities) MPC offers an open space where children and adolescents come to learn and play under the supervision of trained staff (currently ongoing). They can use library equipped with learning materials (books, games) and an outdoor playground (with a variety of sport equipment). Some of visual aids (e.g. puppets that support emotional learning) are developed during sewing workshops. PULSE provides a safe space supporting self- directed learning. Trained staff is available to guide and teach using non-formal education approach by creating learning opportunities. The majority of library visitors are adolescents (there is a great gap in educational offer aimed at adolescents). Children and adolescents visit the library in their free time, according to their availability (some of them need to help in their families and cannot participate in fixed activities).

Needs

Refugee children report feeling unsafe and vulnerable to violence, including sexual violence (Rohingya Refugee Response Gender Analysis, August 2018, Joint Agency Research Report). That is why it is crucial to address the whole community to understand what is violence and abuse and what should be done to prevent it. Based on results from different studies, approximately 5-10% of Rohingya households engage in child marriage and two-thirds of these households consider it a normal practice, while approximately one-third use it as a coping strategy to either protect girls or reduce economic burdens on the household (July 2019 UNHCR’s SPP 5 and August 2019 ISCG’s MSNA). By creating a safe space for children and adolescents PULSE gives a chance to them to develop their skills and to become aware that any form of abuse is not acceptable. Children and adolescents learn how to stay safe and their parents, together with community leaders participate in positive parenting and awareness raising sessions. According to findings from Joint Multi – Sector Needs Assessment (Refugees and Host Communities, Key findings for NGO Platform, 31 October 2019) there is low attendance rate in education facilities which affects adolescents aged 12 and over, but particularly adolescent girls. Main barriers are: marriage, lack of time (individual is needed to help at home) or it’s perceived not useful/ age appropriate. Education is supporting child protection. The moment adolescents are in a TLF they are safe and they also have a chance to learn protection messages. PULSE addresses this gap trying to reach out to adolescents through life skills sessions, youth clubs, library activities and social events giving them a chance to spend their time in a safe space. Adolescents can decide how many days a week they visit MPC, what activities are best for them and in which role they want to be involved (as a visitor, participant or facilitator’s support). They are encouraged to use available resources on their own and to support each other by peer learning. Adolescent’s presence in MPC creates an opportunity to both “directly” and “indirectly” disseminate key protection messages (that are part of learning materials, centre’s displays and decorations and MPC’s staff is trained to disseminate the key messages through informal conversations). All MPC activities and space aim not only at providing practical experience (prevocational trainings, sport and social activities) but also at sharing key protection messages in an understandable and practical way.

Activities or outputs

The project will support: - 70 children and 300 adolescents (facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress) to have access to well-coordinated and gender-sensitive quality child protection services, including 600 parents and caregivers. - 1000 adults to promote a community-based approach to the response, support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services for persons at heightened protection risk, including girls, boys, women and men of all ages and with diverse needs and vulnerabilities, with the ultimate aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks, strengthen the resilience of affected communities, and place communities at the center of the response.This will be achieved through the following six activities:1.Child Friendly Space which provides children to participate in recreational and psychosocial activities in a safe space which will be provided to children aged 3-6 (2 groups, 35 children each, 2 hour session). 2. Life skills and prevocational trainings will improve access of children and adolescents to practical skills combined with protection mainstreaming (total number of participants: 120)-Sewing (48 adolescent girls aged 12-18yrs) -Computer (total number of participants: 40: 2 adolescent girls group aged 12-18, 10 girls in each group and 2 adolescent boys group aged 12-18, 10 boys in each group.- nglish (total number of participants: 32: 1 adolescent girls group aged 12-18, 16 girls in a group and 1 adolescent boys group aged 12-18, 16 boys in each group). Each sessions starts with protection message. Sessions support other activities in MPC (sewing workshops prepares toys for CFS and for positive parenting sessions, computer workshops create posters and descriptions for awareness raising sessions and social events, English vocabulary includes basic child protection vocabulary transmitted though social stories). 3.Youth Club for adolescent boys and girls aged 12-18, total number of participants: 180The Club will follow Plan’s International curriculum of Champions of Change (activities tailored to individual needs as per gender). Groups for adolescent girls (3 x 30) and boys (3x30) will be created. 4.Positive parenting sessions: 600 parents and caregivers(supported by “No- limit generation” videos). Parents learn how to talk to children without using violence, how to handle own frustrations and support child’s development. Each group (50 participants) meets once a week for 4 weeks in a row (12 groups in a year). Each session lasts 2 hours. 5.Awareness raising sessions: 1000 adults(twice a month). Sensitizing community to the main threads (e.g. human trafficking, child marriages, DRR mainstreaming) and providing educational sessions. 6.Social events (photo and art exhibitions, sport tournaments). Once a month to presents the results of activities for children (CFS) and adolescents (prevocational trainings and youth club) to the members of the community. All activities will be tailored to the specific gender, age and disability needs of children, adolescents and adults promoting their inclusion and participation. Children, adolescents and adults will learn protection messages and DRR mainstreaming. There is a need to integrate gender transformative approaches across the response balancing with the principle of “do no harm”. Activities involve interaction with vulnerable children. MPC team follows Reporting Procedures from PULSE’s Child Protection Policy, Code of Conduct and PSEA procedures. There is a reporting mechanism and the MPC manager is a focal point for incidents. All activities will be coordinated with CPSS in Cox’s Bazar. Project’s representatives will participate in regular meetings in Cox’s Bazar and in the field level. The work will be coordinated with Protection Sector in Camp 9, CP Focal Point, WASH, CiC and SM.
