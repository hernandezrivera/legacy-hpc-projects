Project Summary

WFP will provide unconditional food-assistance to the most food-insecure, crisis-affected populations. Life-saving general food assistance (in-kind food and cash-based transfers) will be delivered to IDPs, returnees and host populations in North-East Nigeria (Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States). Early recovery and resilience to shocks will be promoted through asset creation and preservation, increased livelihoods opportunities, and enhanced agricultural value chains. Joint multi-sectoral assessments and Emergency Food Security Analysis will be conducted regularly to monitor the nutrition, food security, and markets situation in crisis-affected states.
A gender-transformative approach that reflects the needs of women, men, girls and boys will be adopted. WFP will mainstream protection throughout its work, supported by a solid understanding of the operational context and protection analysis. Training and capacity building of staff and partners in protection and humanitarian principles will include on-the-job coaching. WFP will ensure that affected populations (women and men) will be consulted throughout the programme cycle; beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be tailored to their preferred means of communication. Beneficiaries will continue to be empowered with knowledge on their food assistance entitlements and their rights, including WFP’s zero tolerance policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be embedded in M&E tools, complaint feedback mechanisms and training of partners and staff.
The WFP protection and AAP unit is made up of two national protection officers and two international protection officers. This would need to be correct as it currently states 6 national protection officers and three international protection officers.
WFP will continue the collection of key information on beneficiaries and assistance provided as part of its M&E activities. Monthly monitoring will include distribution and activity site management; beneficiary and entitlement verification; and ensuring adequate protection and gender measures. Lessons will be regularly drawn from monthly process and biannual outcome monitoring and reporting, and periodic reviews and evaluation to inform strategic and operational decisions.
WFP will further consolidate partnerships to enhance national and state-level emergency preparedness and response systems, including in food security and nutrition assessment and monitoring, early warning, protection, gender analysis, targeting and registration processes, safety nets, cash-based transfer capacities and supply chain.
Where security and access to land allows, increasing livelihood activities will complement food assistance to support access to productive assets, income generating activities and self-reliance in the long-term. Assistance will be coordinated with government distributions, to ensure food assistance is complementary and fills gaps. 1 - Needs

The persistent and widespread humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria continues to cause repeated displacement, protection concerns and unrelenting food and nutrition insecurity. Millions of children, women and men bear the brunt of the conflict, which has decimated livelihoods, crops, livestock, infrastructure, education and basic services. According to the October 2019 Cadre Harmonise analysis (projection for June-August 2020), approximately 3.8 million people in the BAY states are projected to be food insecure and considered to be facing crisis or emergency situations. The October 2019 Cadre Harmonise confirmed a worsening of the food security situation compared to findings of the CH analysis for the same period in 2018. The marked deterioration in the food security situation is linked to the upsurge in insecurity as evident in renewed attacks by non-state armed groups which have led to a new wave of displacements, limited access to farming and grazing land essential for livelihood opportunities. This situation has stretched communal resources due to increased dependency from IDPs and returnees. Of the 1.832 million people that remain displaced in the three most affected states (Adamawa, Borno and Yobe), 79 percent are women and children. In recent months, thousands of people in dire need of assistance have newly arrived at areas of relative safety, placing increasing pressure on humanitarian actors to meet their most basic food, nutrition and other needs. Notwithstanding, the continued assistance by the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community to deliver food and nutrition assistance as well as livelihoods support, access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets and livelihood activities remains limited. Given the current levels of food and nutrition insecurity, below-average crop production, disrupted livelihoods, and high staple food prices, food security and nutrition situation remains extremely fragile. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their food and nutrition needs. WFP regularly conducts protection analysis including Do No Harm, protection risk assessments and protection monitoring within the context of the provision of food assistance. Protection and gender specific questions have also been integrated into vulnerability food assessment tools and rapid intersectoral assessments to ensure that protection and gender is captured in food security assessments. Depending on the context, one or more of the following targeting criteria can be considered in agreement with the communities: people with disabilities with no support[1]; heads of household with a disability; households headed by older people with no support; child headed households, separated and unaccompanied children; heads of household taking care of a large number of children or relatives; households headed by females with no support; households headed by widows with no livelihood source/support; households with a large number of dependents; households with no livelihood activity/source; households reliant mainly on risky income generating activities such as firewood gathering; newly displaced individuals (less than 3 to 6 months) residing in a camp and not integrated with communities; households headed by people who are chronically sick; households found to be adopting negative coping strategies such as beggars (when considered by the community as an abnormal consequence of shock).

2 - Activities

(1) Provide unconditional food assistance and income-generating activities to food-insecure IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by crises [contributes to FSS Objective 1]. In 2020, it is planned under this activity to reach up to 920,870 beneficiaries with GFD (in-kind food, CBT) in BAY States. Food assistance will be maximized from the beginning of the year up to the lean season and will decrease during the final quarter of the year (post-lean season and harvest period). (2) Provide conditional transfers to food-insecure persons, including women, young people and smallholders [contributes to FSS Objective 2]. In 2020, it is planned under this activity to reach up to 250,000 beneficiaries with conditional food assistance, increased livelihoods opportunities, asset creation and preservation, and natural resource management in BAY States. In 2020/21, a planned gradual increase in the number of people assisted to reach 200,000 and 250,000 beneficiaries, respectively. (3) Support the technical capacity of the federal, state and local actors in IMS, vulnerability assessment and mapping, M&E, safety net management, food technology and fortification, supply chains, nutrition and EPRP, integrating gender [contributes to FSS Objective 1, 2, 3]. To continue focused support to the most vulnerable conflict-affected communities and LGAs, WFP leverages evidence from the Cadre Harmonise and other supporting information such as the EFSA and market and livelihood assessments carried out by WFP. Multiple factors are considered with priority given to the most food-insecure LGAs (in CH Phase 3 and 4), including the most vulnerable people living in camps and fully dependent on WFP support, and accessibility and coverage by partners.The criteria below are used to classify beneficiaries that receive lifesaving and livelihood support in order of priority: IDPs beneficiaries living in camps; newly arrived displaced people; IDPs with local communities returnees and host communities. WFP will provide livelihood and income generating support to IDPs and host communities using food assistance for assets (in-kind food and cash based transfers). The activities undertaken shall build resilience and be shock responsive in nature falling under the range of WFP’s livelihood typology e.g. agri. based, infrastructural rehabilitation, VSL...etc. The selection of the activity type shall be informed by contextual analysis and community based participation. Targeting through a community based approach shall enable further prioritisation of the most vulnerable. The type of businesses for which cash grants are targeted are informed by seasonal livelihood programming that entails an analysis of the seasonal activities that are unique to the operational context and thereby, appropriate for selection. In short, it forms the basis for various businesses that are appropriate for the context. The activity selection process, targeting and prioritization criteria, and transfer modality selection ensures social cohesion aligned with the “do no harm” principle. The selection of both IDPs and host communities to receive resilience building shock responsive support assures overall community integration in the end. The selection of activities and transfer modalities are informed by WFP’s operational context, beneficiary choice and community-based targeting/prioritization. Gender and protection analysis also informs the above mentioned selection/approach. The appropriate geographical targeting is also informed through the FSS and the CH results, complemented by WFP food security assessments and IOM DTM. Targeting and prioritization of beneficiaries and activities are collectively derived through the participation of Partners and the communities to avoid duplications and minimize assistance gaps. WFP collaborates with IOM through a data sharing agreement assures a harmonized registration approach via an electronic data exchange.
