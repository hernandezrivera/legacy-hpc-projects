Project Summary

The proposed project will target IDPs and host communities, school going children, women, vulnerable groups including HIV victims and youth in Mvolo Payam, Yeri Payam and Bogori Payam in Mvolo County will be reached through Water supply, Sanitation, Hygiene promotion activities involving training, awareness campaigns, Schools hygiene club formation and awareness sessions in Schools.
• Water supply will include borehole rehabilitation/repair, hand pump mechanics training, restructuring and training of Water Sources management committees for the rehabilitated/repaired water sources.
• Formation of School Hygiene Clubs, hygiene sessions for pupils, menstrual hygiene management sessions for female students above 12 years old and School-led Total Sanitation training for teachers, provision of dignity kits to schools girls of menstruating age.
• Community WASH activities will target host communities and include community targeted hygiene promotion activities, household water treatment sessions for vulnerable groups and media campaign. Much of the hygiene promotion activities will have more focus on hygiene education than promotional activities with many of the target beneficiaries sensitized on the importance of good hygiene practices, such as hand washing and prevention of diarrhea diseases.
• Hygiene promotion activities will continue with more focus on promotional and demonstration activities like latrine use and maintenance, safe water chain, and households’ visits. The program will support activities that will ensure its sustainability such as intensifying information campaigns, education and promotional activities. TARGET Association will use community-based approach to implement these activities, continued collaboration with host community and schools using life skill education and strengthening committees and hygiene promoters to support follow-up activities.

Needs

Mvolo County is located in the former Western Equatoria state, bordered by lakes state to the north, Maridi County to the West, Mundri Counties to the South and East. It had a population of 48,000 People (2008 Sudan Census), but current population projection by County government is put at 92,000 people. The County is predominantly inhabited by the Jur tribe, who are entirely crop farmers annually, armed pastoralists from Yirol and Rumbek, come to Mvolo in December - June to access pasture and water for cattle. Conflict in the northern Payams of Mvolo causes displacement, destruction, looting of property and fear which interrupts the farming activities. Influx of IDPs from Bahr-el-Grindi, Kokori, Dari and Lesi Payams to Mvolo and Yeri Payams added huge pressure on available food in the host community, making both host and IDPs vulnerableTARGET Association has been working in Western Equatoria since 2015 as community based promoter of girls’ child education. Girls’ children supported from Western Equatoria under TARGET Association program in the State; hail from different background including Mvolo County. Thanks to these previous experiences, TARGET Association has identified few major challenges which communities in western Equatoria; most especially Mvolo County are facing on daily basis, mainly in water supply, which is clearly not sufficient. Several existing boreholes have not been rehabilitated for a long time, Directorate of Water and Sanitation (DWSS) does not have spare parts for rehabilitation and the ones which have been repaired by non-governmental WASH partners do not meet the water demand. The hygiene awareness of the population in Western Equatoria is low, particularly in areas outside the main towns of Mvolo, and among the IDP populations, which often come from quite isolated rural areas.Kokori has 2 Boreholes for about 5,000 people and both are not functional. It takes 2 hours to access the water well and stream. In the locations visited the assessment team found 24 Boreholes, out of which 15 are not functional. The whole county of Mvolo has a total of 37 Boreholes among which 20 are not functional. Lack of water is a major challenge in Mvolo contributing to the poor hygiene and sanitation as well as the vulnerabilities Yeri is a Payam. Estimated population given was 19,319. Yeri has a PHCC which provided the medical services. Open defecation is common in all areas visited and this may cause hygiene related illnesses when rains increase and contaminate the water sources and food. ). The majority of the population is dependent on streams, some seasonal in nature. The lack of protected water sources is currently keeping many children away from school because thirst makes children reluctant to focus on classes, thus has many negative aspects.

Activities

TARGET Association will intervene in Water supply, Sanitation, Hygiene promotion activities involving training and awareness campaigns in the community, Schools hygiene club formation and awareness sessions • Water supply including borehole rehabilitation/repair, hand pump mechanics training and Water Sources management committees for the rehabilitated/repaired water sources.• Formation of School Hygiene Clubs, hygiene sessions for pupils, menstrual hygiene management sessions for female students above 12 years old and School-led Total Sanitation training for teachers, provision of dignity kits to school girls of menstruating age.• Community WASH will target host communities and include community targeted hygiene promotion activities, household water treatment sessions for vulnerable groups and media campaign. Much of the hygiene promotion activities will have more focus on hygiene education than promotional activities with many of the target beneficiaries sensitized on the importance of good hygiene practices, such as hand washing and prevention of diarrhea diseases. • Hygiene promotion activities will continue with more focus on promotional and demonstration activities like latrine use and maintenance, safe water chain, and households’ visits. The program will support activities that will ensure its sustainability such as intensifying information campaigns, education and promotional activities. TARGET Association will use community-based approach to implement these activities, continued collaboration with host community and schools; using life skill education and strengthening committees and hygiene promoters to support follow-up activities. To ensure that the intervention is suitable to the current situation in the areas of concern, water sources will be assessed and included in the project on a rolling basis, in close collaboration with DWSS. Water point’s repairs, rehabilitation will be outsourced to the outside supplier, which will be closely monitored and supervised by TARGET Association WASH Engineer and DWSS staff. Formation and training of Water User Committees will provide the sustainability and strengthening of the Operation & Maintenance system of water sources, as a community-led approach to increase resilience to adequately deal with emergencies and with the aim to create a strategy for reduction of borehole spare parts subsidy, taking into special consideration the active participation of women in the decision making processes. A Water User Committee (WUC) will be formed in all locations where TARGET Association will take care of water sources to ensure maintenance and sustainability of the water sources. TARGET Association will also organize three training for hand pump mechanics in the three payams of operation. The workshops will concern hand pump repair and maintenance, and go more in-depth on the technical issues. Schools are usually very crowded places where the spreading of micro-organisms that cause diseases is very common. When water, sanitation and hygiene conditions are poor, instead of safeguarding children from transmission of infectious diseases, school environment becomes a health hazard. Therefore, hygiene education has to go hand-in-hand with safe and well maintained water and sanitation facilities to make schools safe places for children’s development. To ensure sustainability of hygiene awareness in schools, TARGET Association shall initiate formation and training of School Hygiene Clubs in schools. Each Club will be established by 15 pupils and 1 teacher, who will be equipped with specific knowledge and booklets on hygiene promotion. Training on School-led Total Sanitation for teachers will be organized to equip them with tools to work with their students. TARGET Association WASH Team will visit all the schools in the selected payams and carry out hygiene promotion sessions according to the life skills-based hygiene education method and context specific hygiene and sanitation

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Several consultations were made by TARGET Association staff on the ground in western equatoria with the community members who are target beneficiaries of the proposed project.The consultations were made with 3 different focuses WASH needs to be addressed, protection mainstreaming and access; The project aims at preventing gender inequality which often leads to GBV by engaging male and female community members in discussions on current gender roles, stereotypes and stigmas. These discussions will nurture a better understanding between women and men and lead to more equal and respectful relationship.The specific needs of girls, boys, women and men will be taken into consideration throughout the project cycle in the following ways: Referral pathway will support in the identification of cases and report them to partners specialized on protection. TARGET Association will implement the intervention following the principle of do not harm and protection mainstreaming. Following TARGET Association Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding and GBV Policy, all beneficiaries will be informed through dissemination of key messages and awareness sessions about the reporting mechanism related to CSG and GBV incidents.Lack of access, especially during rainy season when roads are closed and airstrips accessible only by helicopter, make delivery of assistance by humanitarian actors logistically complex and challenging In Mvolo, insecurity and some hostilities can constrain freedom of movement of both, civilians and humanitarian actors, requiring complex negotiation with state and non-state actors for access to humanitarian space. The remoteness of some locations and the difficulty in access, especially in the raining season, has resulted in a lack of service provision, which has increased health and protection concerns.
