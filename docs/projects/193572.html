Project Summary

In line with the strategic objectives, priority needs, and most vulnerable populations established by the Health Cluster and HNO, and considering the regular SWOT analysis conducted as part of our standard project cycle management procedures, as well as the periodic need assessments and risk analysis conducted by our staff and its experience in the area of operation, in addition to the feedback of the beneficiaries of our intervention and our partners, the main outcome of this intervention is to improve access to non-specialised and specialised cross-sectoral prevention and emergency care services at the community and health care level for adults and children experiencing suicidal ideations and behaviours in Palestine, considering two main outputs: 1) Adults and Children presenting suicidal ideation and behaviours are detected and referred at community and medical services levels at an early stage and are provided with an ad hoc support plan that includes access to support services; 2) Suicide, as a major public health concern, is addressed through national and international awareness and advocacy efforts.

Data collected in 2021 showed that 25-38% of households have at least one member reporting self-diagnosed mental distress. In addition, PCBS (2019) reports 29% of Women in oPt had experienced violence (psychological, sexual, economical), 9.1% of these women would need specialized MHPSS services beside protection services. According to last WHO´s fact sheets on adolescents, 13% of children and adolescents experience mental disorders. If untreated, these conditions severely influence children’s development, facing major challenges with stigma, isolation, and discrimination, as well as lack of access to health care and education facilities 43% of the Palestinian population are children and adolescents and there is a need to strengthen mental health services provided to them. A 2019 mixed study conducted by MdM-CH on the risk factors of attempted suicide patients in the WB found that 52% of the total attempted suicide patients were youth, ages 16-25 years old. COVID-19 enlarges this group to include infected patients, as the virus causes various mental and neurological complications. The project proposes activities based on the objectives mentioned above, aiming to:

1. Increase the capacities of medical staff and communities for the proper detection and referral of vulnerable cases to specialized MHPSS services, through specific actions at institutional (hospitals and PHCs/CMHCs) and community level (CBOs and religious leaders) through the implementation of tailored trainings in coordination with the MoH.

2. Integrate MHPSS services at Primary Health Care Centres (PHCs) / Community Mental Health Centres (CMHCs) to better detect, respond, and refer risky behaviours by emphasizing on mhGap intervention, with special attention to the provision of care for youth.

3. Enhance the capacities of Emergency hotlines in detecting, supporting, and referring people presenting self-harm and risky behaviours.

4. Support or update of the mapping of service providers (Health, MHPSS and Protection - PWD & GBV) done by other actors.

5. Promote psycho-education and awareness raising about youth vulnerability (especially girls & PWD) with regards to self-harm, risky behaviours and stigmatization of suicide, involving CBOs, religious leaders, and schools.

The project will coordinate with the MoH, the Health Cluster, the WHO and other partners working on suicide prevention and management, to consolidate efforts and complement the work already done. The project activities will be implemented in the WB (Area A, B, C, H2), EJ and the GS, in an effort to reach people in need of MHPSS interventions geared to the needs of women, adolescents, ex detainees, GBV victims, people presenting suicidal ideations or behaviours and PWDs in the most vulnerable and marginalized communities.

1 - Needs

Mental Health and Psychosocial issues represent one of the most significant public health challenges across the oPt. Although the precise extent of mental disorders is not clearly known, according to PCBS’ last data collected (2014), the oPt presents a suicide mortality rate (deaths per 100,000 population) of 1.69 for men and 0.92 for women, data that is far from the estimation done in 2022 by the MHPSS Technical Advisory Group: more than 621 000 people are in need of mental health and psychosocial services in the occupied Palestinian territory. The findings of a study published in 2017 (The Burden of Mental Disorders in the Eastern Mediterranean Region, 1990-2013) indicate that oPt has the largest burden of mental disorders among the Eastern Mediterranean Region. In 2019 WHO estimations of mental disorders in conflict settings show that more than one in five people (22.1%) in post-conflict settings has depression, anxiety disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder or schizophrenia. Adapted to the Palestinian Population, and considering the escalation of violence in hot zones (e.g. EJ, H2, GS, etc.), we can consider that in Palestine, more than 250,000 individuals are in-need of lifesaving MHPSS interventions. A study published in 2017 (Suicidal ideation and planning among Palestinian middle school students living in GS, WB, and United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA) camps. International journal of pediatrics and adolescent medicine, 4(2), 54-60), explored suicidal ideation among Palestinian middle school students in grades 7, 8, and 9 and analyzed data from a Global School-based Student Health Survey. The study found that the overall prevalence of suicidal ideation and/or planning was 25.6%. Males were more likely than females to report suicidal thinking (Itani et al. (2017)).The 2021 UN Women Gender Alert highlights how conflict disproportionately impacts the mental health of women in general and women with disabilities in specific, especially in the most vulnerable communities. SAWA and AISHA, the main national helplines providing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) tele-services to suicide attempt and self-harm patients, report an increase in the last years of the total number of counselling sessions for suicide attempters. According to their statistics, between July 2021 and June 20221, a total of 475 people (309 women) were presenting suicidal ideations or behaviours. Due to the frequency of escalations of violence in the WB, EJ and the GS, the provision of medical assistance services has become a priority. Helplines indicate a clear increase in calls related to suicidal ideation during escalations and in the 2 months following these incidents. According to the WHO (2019 – Suicide in the world), suicide takes 800,000 lives worldwide annually, accounting for 1.4% of all deaths and it’s the 18th leading cause of them. According to the same source, suicide is the second leading cause of death among youth 15-29 years of age around the world. Police and government records in the Arab World report annual completed suicide rates of 1.1/100,000 to 6.2/100,000 (Karam et al., 2008). WHO indicates that 79% of suicide occurs in low- and middle-income countries. In Palestine, police public statistics demonstrate an annual increase in suicide rates since 2016. Although we are witnessing the last phases of the COVID-19 pandemic, it has led to a surge in mental health issues across the globe (WHO 2020). Social isolation has caused reduced physical activity, and reduced cognitive stimulation, which in turn may cause an increase in the risks of cognitive decline and dementia (ibid). A 2021 WHO & Juzoor study emphasizes the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the mental health of youth and adolescents in Palestine. It shows that this group has suffered increased levels of psychological distress caused by the pandemic and the continuous lockdowns. Over 60% of the 1,800 surveyed adolescents exhibited poor to very-poor mental wellbeing. It revealed significantly elevated rates of psychological distress, among this group, compared to before the pandemic and lockdown, and negative impacts on family’s financial situations, family relationships, social connectedness, motivation, and learning. Furthermore, the pandemic disrupted all forms of health provision in Palestine (Health Cluster HNO). More specifically, the disruption has impacted mental, neurological and substance abuse victims, which WHO 2020 attests as life-threatening; particularly when impacting service provision for attempted/ suicide patients. The pandemic has caused a widespread disruption to critical mental health services for vulnerable people (72% disruption to services of children and adolescents, and 61% disruptions to women requiring antenatal or postnatal services). Finally, literature within the eld of psychology emphasizes that understanding the risk factors for suicide and suicide methods is very significant towards the development of effective preventive strategies. On this basis, MdM-CH conducted an assessment study in 4 Palestinian Public Hospitals in the WB to identify specific risk factors and suicide prevention strategies. The results highlight a crucial gap in the detection of people at risk, due to severe psychological distress, risky behaviours and self-harm, as well as to the paucity of cross-sectoral responses. It recommends consolidated, crosscutting, efforts to build a sustainable system of detection, alert and referral of people in need of urgent lifesaving MHPSS interventions, and advocates for further prevention national strategies. Taking into account the findings of the study conducted, the experience in previous years working in suicide prevention and management, as well as MoH and WHO’s recommendations, this project between proposes evidence-based activities tailored to the unique and volatile Palestinian context.

2 - Activities

Based on the specific objectives described above, and as a continuity top our Suicide Prevention and Management project, active since 2018 and coordinated with MoH, the project proposes the following activities listed below: 1.Trainings for medical staff: ►Practical trainings at ER departments in public and private hospitals, located in the West Bank and the Gaza strip, according to MoH identification and considering the 10 public facilities were the previous phase of this project took place: Hebron, Bethlehem, Yatta, Jericho, Nablus, Ramallah, Tulkarem, Qalqelya and Salfit, conducting trainings on soft skills and stress management related to suicide prevention and managements, emphasizing youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, suicide attempts, and the need to provide inclusive healthcare and safe access to services for PWD and GBV survivor groups. Between 2021 and 2022, 96 ER staff were trained at these hospitals ►Practical on-site training for psychiatrists: Train MoH psychiatrists (13 psychiatrists and 2 resident psychiatrists) on the medical and psychotherapeutic treatment for Suicidal and at risk behaviours following a scientifically based and culturally adapted approach. A special section will be designated to the topic on suicide amongst children & adolescents (with a focus on vulnerable groups: PWD and GBV survivors, chronic mental health patients and substance users); ► Trainings for Mental Health professionals at Community Mental Health Centers (CMHCs) from Bethlehem, Hebron and East Jerusalem on youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviours, suicide attempts, and the need to provide inclusive care and safe access to services for PWD and GBV survivor groups; ► Supervision sessions to trained staff (ER and Psychiatrists). Identification of facilities and participants in the trainings will be done following MoH indications. 2. Tailored trainings for staff and volunteers from Community Based Organizations (CBOs), religious leaders and stakeholders on suicide prevention and management, with special attention to the needs of the most vulnerable groups identified above. 3. Support to the main helplines providing remote MHPSS services (AISHA and SAWA): ► Covering of cost of part of the staff; ► Specialized trainings about youth and PWD vulnerability to self- harm, risky behaviours, suicide ideation, YLDs, and plans; ► Support with the case management of complex cases, especially when identifying self-harm, risky behaviours or suicidal ideations. 4. Collaborate in the mapping activity of all service providers (Health, MHPSS, and Protection-PWD & GBV) conducted by other partners to create a revised comprehensive tool that can be used by the participants in our trainings. 5. Awareness raising on mental health and suicide: ► Awareness activities about vulnerability (especially youth & PWD) with regards to self-harm, risky behaviours and stigmatization of suicide, targeting CBOs, religious leaders, teachers, counsellors and key community members in marginalized areas on mental health and suicide, including information on groups at risks of suicide to become actors of support at community level to prevent social stigma against and misconceptions on suicide; ► Awareness activities targeting most vulnerable youth and adolescents, GBV survivors and PWDs; ► Workshop for Protection (GBV, CP-MHPSS) cluster members about suicide prevention and recommendations of best practices; ► Quarterly coordination with the International Association for Suicide Prevention (IASP), the Ministry of Health, and the WHO. All the activities will consider the needs of the most vulnerable groups identified before, including specific actions for each one of them.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MdM-CH’s Feedback and complaints mechanism are in place in Palestine as part of the M&E process, following WHO globally recognized complain mechanisms, procedures, and standards (rules of conduct for abusive behavior as part of MdM prevention alert and management standardized system since 2018). All our beneficiaries and partners receive a card with information on how to provide feedback & complaints using different mechanisms, to guarantee the accessibility to provide feedback or complaints for all of them. Feedback & complaint channels are handled exclusively by designated office staff. Response is provided regardless of whether corrective measures are feasible to be implemented or not. Complaint & feedback is handled on the basis of sensitivity: Less sensitive complaints & feedback are handled on country level, with support from our focal point at HQ. Sensitive complaints (PSEA or corruption) are handled on an HQ level, with support from field staff. MdM CH is part of the oPt´s PSEA network. As part of the process to be a member, our procedures were reviewed and validated, and one staff was designated officially to handle all the F&C channels, to guarantee full confidentiality. All complaints and feedback are taken seriously and, if further investigation is needed, other professionals might be involved in handling the case depending on the matter raised. Since 2020, Sawa Helpline is one of the main reporting channel for PSEA for the entire oPt, meaning that any beneficiary from the West Bank, East Jerusalem or Gaza can use the phone number 121 to report misconduct. The results of MdM-CH’s community surveys and the results of its regular Beneficiary Satisfaction Surveys (BSS) for MHPSS ER, have informed the design and strategy of the proposed intervention.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

At all stages of the Health Project Cycle management: Diagnosis, Implementation and M&E, protection mainstreaming is a cross-cutting theme for MdM-CH’s programs and projects. MdM-CH understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programs followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards regarding MHPSS & PSEA. We follow strong PSEA policies to protect beneficiaries and staff, following international standards under the umbrella of the PSEA network. MdM CH and the partners remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard a number of steps and measures are adopted to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening PIN capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. Moreover, to further enhance protection mainstreaming the project will ensure that beneficiaries of the intervention will be capacitated on the following topics, according to their level of expertise: MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines). GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards). Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following Unicef and Sphere guidelines, as well as MdM guidelines). Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR (Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO. PSEA IASC guide our feedback & complaint mechanisms. Further, SAWA has developed internal guidelines and policies that are regularly updated and improved to reflect the realities on the ground. In this respect it is important to mention that SAWA and AISHA have direct contact with hundreds of beneficiaries each day. According to their estimations, two thirds of the consultations are follow up calls which means that the callers trust the counselor and express the wish for continued counseling. Since the counseling is based on the method of active listening, the counselors do not impose their own solutions on the caller and they do not try to find solutions for the caller's situation. It is the beneficiary that is at the center (survivor-centered approach) throughout the consultation and it is the caller who independently takes the decision on the next steps to take. Also in cases of referrals to other partners or authorities, the counselor triggers the referrals on the beneficiaries' own wish and DO NO HARM policy is strictly followed by counselor. In case when the counseling is done with children, additional policies apply (Child Safeguarding Policy), so that the consultation follows all standards on how to work with minors.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MdM-CH is basing their interventions on ethical and humanitarian principles. This project aligns with the principal approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter (and SPHERE MHPSS). Ethical principles in healthcare include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneﬁcence, the promotion of others’ well-being, commonly known as “do no harm,” as described above and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the second source of values and norms that animate both applicants’ actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conﬂict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, physical ability, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actors. As for AISHA and SAWA, they are free and anonymous helplines where anyone who calls can receive the services they offer regardless of gender, age, religion, sexual identity or socio-economic status.
