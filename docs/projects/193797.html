Project Summary

The escalation of conflict in Sudan has caused the destruction of civilian infrastructure and has forced people to flee their homes seeking safety, protection and assistance. Despite the recently declared ceasefires, hostilities between the SAF and the RSF have persisted since 15 April 2023.
Almanar aims to address life-saving and life-sustaining protection needs among crisis affected families, while also enhancing the capacity of stakeholders to strengthen the protective environment through provision of protection, child protection and GBV services in Khartoum, White Nile, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, East Darfur states. Almanar will ensure engaging men, women, girls and boys, older people, disable PSNs ensuring the full and equal respect for the rights of all individuals, IDPs, Refugees and host communities, regardless of age, gender, ethnic, social, religious or other background in the activities.
General protection activities will be presented through legal aid assistance, activate referral pathways to receive needed support, Establishment of gender balanced CBPCs and train them on protection essential functions, GBV and PSS.
Conduct protection monitoring will be conducted and advocacy interventions will be supported through organizing networking meetings and forums to discuss community issues related to protection and seek possible solutions with decision makers. Also, awareness sessions will be held in addition to capacity building trainings for community members and technical staff.
Under GBV SS, Almanar will rehabilitate and operationalize women centers to be utilized as a safe space. Community Based Committees will be established and trained on GBV principles and standards. Women & girls will be supported with startup capital for Income Generating Activities and vocational skills, Women empowerment/life skills training, case management, consultation and psychosocial support in addition to raise their knowledge through awareness raising campaigns on GBV related issues, psychosocial support and first aid, GBV survivors referral pathway, legal aid/awareness and gender. CMR Training will be provided targeting health providers in addition to PSEA training to project staff & suppliers on PSEA basic concepts.
For CP, Almanar plan to run Child Friendly spaces/ multi-service centers and equip them with needed furniture, stationaries and equipment. Girls & boys who have experienced Gender based violence, abuse, and neglect will be provided with needed services according to their needs, will be registered for Case Management and/or referral and will provide them with legal assistance in detention. Capacity building training will be done targeting men and women on child protection and the minimum standards.

1 - Needs

According to 2023 SUDAN REVISED HUMANITARIAN RESPONSE PLAN (REVISION ISSUED ON 17 MAY 2023), The situation in Sudan has significantly worsened since the last update on humanitarian needs was released in November 20221. Starting on 15 April 2023, fighting between the SAF and the RSF has killed hundreds of people, forced more than 218,000 to flee across borders and displaced over 730,000 people inside the country in the first four weeks of the conflict. The number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent increase.In a region already significantly affected by conflict, insecurity, displacement and high levels of humanitarian needs, civilians have been caught in the cross-fire of ongoing violence and face targeted attacks, including reported cases of sexual and gender-based violence. Vulnerable population groups are increasingly exposed to protection risks with immediate as well as longer-term harm to their physical and mental wellbeing. This includes an increased number of women, girls and at-risk groups10, who are exposed to risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). The deteriorating security situation is also limiting access to lifesaving GBV services, including GBV case management, clinical management ofrape (CMR), psychosocial support and referrals. Safe spaces for women and girls, including to access life-saving services and obtain referrals, also limit options for support.As per GBV AoR Sub-Sector Sudan Situation Brief, all forms of GBV are increasing since 15 April, the GBV sub-sector in Sudan and service providers have received surging reports of cases of GBV, including sexual violence, particularly against internally displaced persons (IDPs) fleeing from one state to another and when homes are being looted, as well as an increased number of domestic violence cases. There are also extremely high risks of sexual violence and of exploitation as women and girls are displaced, in transit, in temporary shelters, and while awaiting visas at border crossings, and as the prices of basic goods – including food, water, and fuel – are skyrocketing and cash is almost impossible to obtain. General protection needs are most severe in the hotspot localities of Khartoum, Darfur, Kordofan, and Blue Nile. conflict is exacerbated by politics at the national level; the proliferation of firearms, water and other resources, herders and armed groups. The Protection Cluster’s 2023 PiN is 3.2 million, of whom 56 per cent are children, and 22 per cent are women. In Sudan, there are 3.7 million IDPs, of whom 2.1 million need assistance, about 666,000 of the 1.2 million returnees need assistance, and there are also 413,000 vulnerable Sudanese represented within the country’s PiN. About 83 per cent of the 3.2 million PiN reside in Darfur, while the remaining people in need are mostly in South Kordofan, Blue Nile and West Kordofan states. The Child Protection Cluster's 2023 PiN is 3.8 million. Up to 60 per cent of the people in need of protection services in Sudan are children who continue to be exposed to protection threats. About 3.5 million children (51 per cent female, 49 per cent male), including 15 per cent of children with disabilities, need immediate child protection services. Across Sudan, GBV continues to be a life-threatening concern, exacerbated by a variety of factors including conflicts and recurring disasters. According to the first nation-wide, qualitative GBV assessment, “Voices from Sudan 2021”, physical and sexual violence are prevalent both inside and outside the home. Sexual violence is more pervasive in rural and conflict-affected communities including refugee camps. The severity of GBV needs is very critical in 131 localities across all states in Sudan. GBV survivors have inadequate access to services and reporting mechanisms.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle and integrated in the assessments to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fire bricks to alternative constructive methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. As the main element of the project, the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities as well as with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management. Throughout the project cycle gender will be considered to access to, and management of natural resources to receive consultation from both men and women.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

One of the most significant factors, which is increasingly recognized as an integral component of UN programming, is the gender equality and protection of girls and women. Inequalities between the sexes typically reflect, and can increase, vulnerabilities of young girls, teens, may face different protection risks, have different needs and choices, and possess different skills, knowledge and coping strategies. The project activities, will aim to ensure community participation through the project cycle, capacity and vulnerability analysis, reduce the level of exploitation to women and promote protection of girls in communities and families with the support of legal and traditional protection systems. The project will use and advocate for Minimum Standards of Protection package amongst the targeted communities throughout the project implementation process. This project through the referral pathway will ensure that women, girls and boys victims of S/GBV, elderly, disables and their families and guardians are fully aware of available services around them. Almanar will ensure that the service locations are safe and accessible to all without discrimination, and ensure that the beneficiaries receiving services in dignity and confidentiality.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The existing community committees and community leaders will be actively engaged at all levels of the project including management, monitoring and evaluation. Complaints handling mechanisms will be established to ensure that complaints are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. These committees are expected to be gender and ability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project. Almanar staff is trained and signed the sexual exploitation and abuse policy and committed to it with close monitoring by the supervisors.
