Project Summary

FSL I aims to contribute to increased food security for vulnerable displaced and host community households in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. FSL I comprise of two components: Household Food Security and Bakery Support.

Within the Household Food Security component NGO X targets an average of 35,000 HH, 175,000 food-insecure individuals per month with in-kind food assistance (food kits) or cash modality (food restricted vouchers) and 4000 HH, 20,000 individuals with one off ready to eat kits. The regular food assistance is to the value of 70% of the food SMEB (Survival minimum expenditure basket) or 70% of the Sphere minimum standards of 2100 kcal/p/day.

The Bakery Support component provides an approximate 300,000 individuals living in supported bakeries’ catchment area with bread at a reduced and stabilised price. This is achieved through subsidising up to 50% input (flour and yeast) of bread production thus supporting local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditional upon the selling of this bread in the local markets at a reduced and stabilised price agreed with and monitored by NGO X’s Cash and Markets, and Monitoring Teams. Approximately 22 bakeries are targeted to benefit from this assistance in FSL II.
NGO X will implement the proposed project in partnership with NGO Z. Please note that NGO Z will input into the OCHA HPC separately under this project. This decision was agreed between both parties and submissions have been coordinated to ensure there is no duplication with regards to either budget or beneficiary targets.
NGO X will target 5,296 children 6-59 months with daily energy and micronutrient dense nutrient supplements for approximately 6 months in IDP camps which are also targeted with regular food assistance.
NGO X has secured total funding for this project. NGO X has permission to operate in all proposed locations.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2020, there are 7.9 million food insecure people in Syria. NGO X conducted a needs assessment in July-August of 2019 in Idleb province. Data was collected in 161 villages across 13 sub-districts (in 4 districts). Findings reveal that most sub=districts in NGO X’s Area of Operation (AoO) have medium to high levels of food insecurity. 36.9% of IDPs were reported to be insecure versus 29.1% of host residents; and 37.2% of females were reported to be insecure versus 28.9% of males. Azaz: In October 2018, NGO X conducted an RNA in IDP camps in North Aleppo which indicated that access to cash and income generation was one of the top three priority needs by survey participants. The strategies that camp-based IDPs use to cope with economic insecurity include relying on less preferred and less expensive foods, reducing the portion size and meal frequency per day and adults reducing their meal intake in favor of child feeding. Afrin: In September 2019, NGO X undertook a food security needs assessment in the Bulbul subdistrict of Afrin. The average diet diversity score in Bulbul subdistrict is 5.51 and the majority of households mainly consume cereals, bread, sugar and oils/fat and very little fresh food which means that their diets are low in protein and multiple micronutrients. Only 2% of HHs had received food assistance, 74% of HHs are employing livelihood coping strategies (LCSI). Within these surveyed HH, pregnant and lactating women and children 6-59 months have lower diet diversity score. Needs assessments have shown that IDPs are most in need of food assistance. Since January, Aleppo received 300,000 IDPs across 21 sub-districts and 476 communities. IDPs living in ‘last resort sites’ – IDP camps – are among the most vulnerable groups. In a vulnerability assessment carried out by NGO X in rural Idleb (October 2018), IDP households were identified as more likely to have lower Food Consumption Scores (FCS) and Household Diet Diversity Score

5 - Implementation of activities

The household food security component will support food security for HHs through (Cash and Voucher Assistance) CVA or via direct food distribution. Each household will receive 5 rounds of assistance. The bakery support will advance the following outcomes: households have access to diverse foods in adequate quantities; communities have access to bread at affordable prices; recently displaced households are able to meet their emergency food needs. FS I is comprised of three components: community food security, household food security and emergency food security. NGO X plans to transition households from kits to vouchers as conditions allow, scale up its use of electronic vouchers, and continue supporting bakeries. In Idleb, Western Aleppo and Northern Aleppo, NGO X and its partners will continue to apply its household selection methodology developed and refined using vulnerability assessments, community feedback and NGO X’s contextual analysis. NGO X will provide blanket distributions in IDP camps targeted by NGO X in Northern Aleppo. This approach is mandated by AFAD (camp management) due to the overall vulnerability of camp residents. NGO X will seek to negotiate a targeted approach in camps in Northern Aleppo during the life of RESTORE. NGO X will target 5,296 children 6-59 months with daily energy and micronutrient dense nutrient supplements for approximately 6 months in IDP camps which are also targeted with regular food assistance. In Afrin, NGO X will work in off camp settings and target using the methodology implemented in Idleb.NGO X will pre-position 8,000 RTE kits (adequate for approx. 4,000 households) in NGO X’s warehouses to be able to respond within 72 hours. Routine monitoring including distribution monitoring and post-distribution monitoring will be conducted for all distribution-based activities. For the bakery component, bakery feedback surveys, shopkeeper satisfaction surveys and bread beneficiary survey will be conducted.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, supported by continued adherence to NGO X's acceptance strategy and the JOPs - Sufficient and timely access to required inputs through procurement, reliable supply and available transportation continues to be achieved; Border crossing remains open- Food inflation and currency exchange rates remain relatively stable- Cash transfer channels remain open and sufficient- Local markets continue to function. Risks: 1. Access restrictions prevent humanitarian actors providing timely emergency relief to the affected population. 3. Closure of the border for commercial shipments or non - renewal of SC would have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities 4. Tension between host community and IDPs or between included/excluded IDPs might lead to conflict between target groups and complaints against the humanitarian organisations. Mitigation: NGO X will apply a conflict-sensitive approach by means of the following: • Context analysis of all programme delivery locations is updated regularly to ensure that: • NGO X is aware of all possible changes in the area and will adapt the programme in accordance with the new changing environment.• NGO X keeps updated on any new actors which may have an impact on the context in the AoO, and NGO X assesses the positive or negative connections between new actors and existing stakeholders. Staff will be informed about NGO X’s communication strategy with new stakeholders to eliminate the risk that NGO X legitimises any one actor or group in conflict. • NGO X will assess all actors to ensure that any possible procurement procedure which is conducted in the programme area will not empower any one group/actors/family. • Field staff in Syria and Turkey will be trained on Conflict Sensitivity. • N Aleppo: Security and risk assessments have been carried out. A conflict sensitive approach will be applied.
