Project Summary

This project is planned to be implemented in 7 localities of Central Darfur (Zalinge, Azum, Golo, Nertiti, Rokero, Bendesi & Umdukun), & 6 in West Darfur (Genina, Kernik, Kulbus, Habila, Beida & Sirba), 6 in Blue Nile (Kurmuk, Geissan, Bau, Wad Almahi, Tadamon & Damazine), and 4 in Khartoum (Bahri, Khartoum, Um-Durman, & Jebel Awlia) targeting 294,760 crisis-affected people (66,144 women, 63,550 men, 84,183 girls, 80,882 boys). The project focuses on improving vulnerable households’ access to sustainable WASH services to reduce the morbidity and mortality attributable to poor water, hygiene and sanitary conditions among IDPs and conflict-affected people. These vulnerable residents are prioritized based on the most severe needs related to WASH borne epidemics, floods, food insecurity, undernutrition, COVID-19, and access to WASH services.
Firstly, this project aims to improve access to basic water services for crisis-affected populations. Under this objective, the interventions ensure drinking water from an improved source with a collection time of not more than 30 minutes roundtrip, including queuing. People targeted with basic water services will benefit from new installations and maintenance, operations, and rehabilitation of existing handpumps, water yards, protected wells including check-dams, and hafirs. KAP assessments by WRS in 2020 show that in the dry season, women and children walk far distances to fetch water. Women and girls face explicit risk of sexual violence as they go about their daily task of collecting water. Therefore, to mitigate such risks, the project will ensure that water sources are close to homes. Furthermore, the project will ensure that the different committees either established or strengthened by the project consist of at least 40% women.
Secondly, the project aims to ensure improved access to adequate, protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services for conflict-affected populations. Interventions will ensure the installation, rehabilitation, and use of ventilated improved pit latrines (VIP), pit latrines with slabs, composting toilets, and flush/pour pit latrines where possible. Household latrines (basic), latrines shared between two households (limited), communal latrines, and institutional latrines will be considered. For sustainability, community volunteers will be trained on CLTS. To ensure effective behavior change, community based hygiene promotion volunteer networks will be established and supported to disseminate hygiene messages and monitor household hygiene practices.
Thirdly, to ensure access to hygiene promotion services, water quality and environmental health interventions will be provided to 208,000 crisis-affected populations. The targeted population will receive hygiene promotion messages focusing on handwashing with soap through household visits, campaigns, and media coverage. Half of the targeted population will be provided with soap and jerry cans. Handwashing facilities will reach people in communal setups. Women and girls who are part of the newly displaced population will receive personal hygiene materials such as reusable sanitary pads or culturally appropriate menstrual hygiene supplies. Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) affected children, along with their households, will be reached with WASH supplies such as soap, jerry cans, buckets, and point of use water treatment materials, including aqua tabs and PUR along with appropriate hygiene promotion messages in the OTPs and Stabilization Centers (SCs). People located in the epidemics and flood-prone areas will be reached with PoUWT and water treatment at the source, followed by water quality monitoring to check on the contamination levels. Environmental health interventions, such as solid waste management and vector control, will be implemented in risk-prone areas.

1 - Needs

The WRS 2019/2020 KAP and SMART survey gathered data related to health, nutrition, & WASH in Central & West Darfur. The assessment depicted that 64% of people are fetching water from improved water sources. However, during the rainy season, this rate drops down from 64% to 55.2% due to use of unprotected sources from hafirs/surface water, from rivers, from shalal (waterfalls), from wadis and from rainy water harvesting. The average household (HH) water consumption is 41.2L/household/day. Considering an average HH size of 6.6 persons, this is an average of 6.2L/per person/per day, below the recommended 15l/h/d SPHERE minimum standards. Furthermore, majority of community members 77% in Umdukhun fetch water from unprotected sources, posing more danger of contracting water related ailments due to contamination from open defecation. The residents travel an average of 7.4 kilometers and spend around 3.75 hours waiting time to fetch water. Children and women bear the brunt of daily water collection and are perennially exposed to accidents and sexual violence to and from the distant water points. Only 20% of the population own traditional latrines, while 80% of the households practice open defecation. Nutrition findings depicted that the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate is 11.4%, severe acute malnutrition (SAM) is 1.6%, and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) rate is 9.8%. GAM was higher among boys than girls at 15.4% and 7.5% respectively, and the SAM rate among boys is 2.3% and 1.0% among girls. By August 2019, UN reports indicated that 6.3 M people in Sudan are vulnerable and experiencing crisis or worse levels (IPC 3 & above) of food insecurity and are in need of food and livelihoods assistance in 162 localities of 17 states. The IPC report indicates that over 90,000 people in Blue Nile state (25% of Kurmuk, 23% of Geissan and 17% of Tadamon locality populations) are experiencing crisis or worse levels of food insecurity, and are in need of urgent support. As per the recommendation from the 2020 comprehensive assessment conducted by WRS in Damazine, Bau, Kurmuk, Geissan and Wad Almahi, the communities in the FGDs mentioned that, in cases where protected water sources are unable to meet the needs of the community (because of malfunctioning pumps, dry water sources or overcrowding at the protected source), it is recommended that water purification solutions be provided to families that are drawing their water from unprotected sources. Moreover, adapted water containers for the transport and storage of water at the household level should be provided. The use of latrines, whether communal or for individual family use only, is infrequent in many communities. Open defecation is reportedly widely practiced, even in communities where latrines have been built. Community members report that the existing latrines are not segregated by gender; cannot be locked from the inside, are unsafe, unclean and lack privacy. Furthermore, they say that they have not received instruction as to how to build safe latrines, nor any public health messages regarding their utility. It is imperative that the government, in collaboration with development partners, plan and execute a public awareness campaign. Resources will then be needed for the construction of the latrines. There are also very few hand washing facilities reported in any of the communities. Again, it is imperative that public health messages be transmitted to the communities to inform about the health risks associated with unclean hands. Finally, there are few rubbish pits in any of the communities. As with latrines and hand washing facilities, public health messages need to be transmitted that inform the population of the health risks related to scattered rubbish, and the importance of disposing of household waste in a safe manner.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project takes the maximum care not to cause damage to the environment. For activities having an impact on the environment, the project design required mitigation measures, which will be implemented parallel to project activity implementation. The environmental markers guideline was reviewed to ensure that the project is designed in a manner that is sensitive to the environment, and that necessary mitigation measures were taken as per the guidelines. To ensure environmental compliance and adhere to no-do-harm principles, WR will provide training on integrated water resources management and create community awareness on the environment. The trained community members will further create awareness to the beneficiary communities on the efficient use of water resources and protection of the environment. Besides, the water management committees will be monitoring ground water for water projects supported by the project and WR, to avoid depletion of ground water and to take joint measures in enhancing the recharge of ground water. Emphasis has been given to installing solar energy instead of supporting diesel energy, because solar is environmentally friendly, cost effective, and efficient. In addition, the ferro cement screen household latrine is environmental friendly technology which will ensure no trees are cut, rather that the technology will utilize sand mineral which is readily available in the area. The side slabs are thin and portable, and reusable in their lifetime as opposed to wooden superstructures. Additionally this project will address the issue of environmental health by establishing solid waste management committees among the targeted villages. Furthermore, through FSL sector WR is supporting tree nurseries in the project proposed localities and the seedlings produced in the nurseries will be planted in the project operational areas.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

While providing WASH services and facilities, this project will consider girls' and women's security and safety. Their engagement will be critical while identifying water points and new water sources to save them travel too far to unsecured places to fetch water. Furthermore, this project will ensure that the communal and institutional latrines are gender-segregated, safe with latches on the doors, and easy to access in secure places. The project will link women and girls to income-generating women's groups to receive training on making sanitary pads and soap. Additionally WRS will install and construct suitable facilities to suit the aged and disable population. WRS will uphold the five minimum commitments, including consulting or engaging the affected population in assessments, designs, implementation, monitoring, and inclusion of women and girls. Efforts will be made to strengthen feedback mechanisms and post-distribution surveys.
