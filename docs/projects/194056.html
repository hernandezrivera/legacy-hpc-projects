Project Summary

IMC will continue implementing its integrated health, nutrition, and WaSH interventions in five states, West, Central, and South Darfur, South Kordofan, Sennar, Jazeera, and the Blue Nile states covering around 1,250,645 people plus those who were displaced due to current conflict. The proposed intervention will address gaps in the provision of primary health care and nutrition services. Under the health intervention, International Medical Corps will be providing basic primary and nutrition services, including outpatient consultations, reproductive health services (antenatal care, delivery service, post-natal care, and family planning), and immunization through 6 health care facilities across the targeted 5 states. In addition to facility care, community-based health interventions including; health and hygiene education, referral of cases, and campaigns will also be provided. In collaboration with SMOH, WHO, and HAC, IMC will respond to seasonal outbreaks such as AWD, measles, and malaria in all targeted areas. Health facilities will be prepared with the necessary human resources and equipment (including laboratory equipment and materials) to enable them to respond to outbreaks and related emergencies. IMC will be implementing community-based interventions such as the promotion of child and maternal health, health education, community mobilization for EPI, mass MUAC screening, and other activities through local partners. Under the nutrition intervention, IMC will support SCs, OTPS, and TSFPs, IMC (in conjunction with the Ministry of Health) will provide nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups- children under 5 and PLWs- among the IDPs, returnees, and host population and refugees in the targeted localities. Treatment will be provided based on the national guidelines for the management of acute malnutrition.(MAM). Children 6-59 months found to have SAM without complications will be treated in the OTPs and those with medical complications or children below 6 months will be transferred to SC where they will receive medical and therapeutic nutrition. To promote optimal IYCF practices, including exclusive breastfeeding, timely complementary feeding, and dietary diversity, individual counseling and group sessions for caretakers of children 0 to 23 months will be provided. Nutrition, health and hygiene education will also be provided for caregivers of all children admitted in the CMAM program, women attending antenatal and post-natal services, and when immunization services are provided. Key messages will be on recommended feeding and child care practices, improving dietary diversity using locally available foods, and improved water, sanitation, and hygiene practices. Community outreach activities will result in improved timely access to treatment, program coverage, and community engagement and contribute to the prevention of malnutrition. The health facilities will be made fully functional by the rehabilitation of the structure, provision of drugs, nutritional supplies, medical equipment and capacity building of State Ministry of Health (SMoH) staff.
IMC will provide integration of MHPSS services into health in Blue Nile and South Kordofan States. Integrated comprehensive MHPSS services will be provided in static health facilities and the community by skilled MHPSS workers. IMC MHPSS workers and MOH general health staff trained on WHO mhGAP-HIG selected priority mental, neurological, and substance use disorders will provide care and management of MNS conditions including case detection, follow-ups, and referrals, with supportive supervision from the MHPSS Coordinator to ensure delivery of quality care. To further enhance the delivery of quality comprehensive MHPSS services relevant stakeholders will be trained on other MHPSS-related topics such as Psychological First Aid (PFA). IMC will ensure the provision and availability of essential psychotropic medication at the health facilities.

1 - Needs

The Health systems in Darfur, South Kordofan, and the Blue Nile remain weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health services and respond to outbreaks. In Central Darfur, it is reported that 26% of the health facilities are not functional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) coverage in health facilities is only 30% in Central Darfur. Only 40% of Clinics in Central Darfur States provide Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) Services. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervision, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow-up to ensure adherence to protocols. Detection and referral of pregnant mothers are weak because of low coverage of community outreach activities and lack of transport. The clinics are not yet fully staffed and still lack the necessary equipment and physical structure to provide comprehensive health services. Water supply, medical waste management and infection control practices are identified as a gap in the clinics. The community outreach activities need expansion to more villages. . Dispensaries do not have enough shelves and storage capacity is low. Health workers in targeted clinics need training on IMCI which is an important skill for better treatment of childhood diseases. In the sphere of mental health needs, there is a critical lack of mental health services throughout Sudan with existing services highly centralized. Sudan has only two psychiatric hospitals in Khartoum, and only seven out of the 18 states in Sudan have psychiatric treatment facilities (hospitals or in-patient units), leaving the wider population of over 40 million with no access to mental health and psychosocial support services. Additionally, there is alack of trained and qualified mental health professionals, weak or no community-based mental health and psychosocial support approaches, indicative of the dire state of MHPSS services in the country (WHO MH Atlas for Sudan 2017).According to the Ministry of Health, in Sudan, some 3 million children suffer from malnutrition annually (Global Acute Malnutrition) out of which over 936,000 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition.South Kordofan and Blue Nile States have been affected by the persistent conflict between government forces and rebel groups since 2011. Two million people are affected by the conflict since then and more than 250,000 refugees fled to Ethiopia and South Sudan. The United Nations estimates that armed movements in South Kordofan and Blue Nile resulted in additional displacement of 545,000 people in areas under their control. The conflict has disrupted traditional livelihood activities and eroded community resilience to withstand shocks though the areas were known to have good agricultural productions before the conflict. Among displaced people, women and children are the most vulnerable and at the greatest risk of being exposed to violence. Healthcare facilities were destroyed in both states as a result of the fighting. Lack of basic services and food scarcity perpetuates displacement and migration of people out of the country, mainly to the camps in South Sudan. The humanitarian context in South Kordofan is further complicated by arrival of South Sudanese refugees in the state increasing the humanitarian need and straining the already compromised basic services infrastructure, including health services. There are new arrivals of around 5000 refugees in South Kordofan in December 2019. According to S3M II 2018 survey result, seven out of the 18 states have malnutrition prevalence rates above the critical threshold as per the WHO standards (15%). Nationally, GAM prevalence is at 13.6 %. Per the current situation Sudan, IMC proposed 3 states from hotspot priority and two states from high IDP receiving locations .

2 - Activities/Outputs

Health: Deliver minimum basic package of primary health care services (including maternal and child health) and support referral to secondary health care. Support and conduct routine or acceleration interventions for immunization. Strengthening Early Warning, Early detection and reporting of AWD(EWARS). Procurement, storage, and distribution of drugs and medical supplies. Conduct health education training for health staff. Conduct awareness/orientation sessions at the health facility for the community. Nutrition: Provision of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases of U5 children and PLW (incl. refurbishment of OTP/TSFP, provision of RUTF/RUSF, MUAC screening, referral service for SAM with complication case etc.). Conduct training for nutrition workers, community volunteer on CMAM, IYCF etc. Conduct community awareness campaigns on CMAM, IYCF etc. Establish a mother support group for the promotion of IYCF. Through a tailored approach to service delivery based on the needs of the different vulnerable groups, the proposed action specifically attempts to address disparities and differences in access to health, nutrition services among vulnerable populations, including women, children and the elderly. Children and women's health and nutritional needs are a specific focus of this Action and women, in particular, are solicited as catalysts for behavior change in the communities through their participation in various community committees. IMC's ongoing collection and analysis of health and nutrition data are disaggregated by age and sex. IMC will continue to ensure the impartiality of program services while enabling the identification of individuals and families with specific vulnerabilities related to age, sex, disability, family size, health status, socioeconomic status, or other factors. International Medical Corps will continue to work with federal and state-level MOH and actively participate in federal and state-level relevant sector clusters (Health, Nutrition) meetings. IMC implementing integrated health, nutrition and facility based WASH. of the total life saving activities implemented by IMC nutrition program, 10% contribution with resilience. WaSH: Provision of safe and sufficient amount of water, medical waste management, provision of sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion in the health facilities and catchment population

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IMC has established community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) involving the holding of monthly community meetings facilitated and documented by IMC staff; use of suggestion boxes put at each clinic where beneficiaries can insert their complaints or suggestions, also using logbooks where suggestions can be written and shared with IMC staff. IMC will inform the communities about the availability of these channels during routine health and nutrition education sessions and how they can send their suggestions and complaints. Complaints and feedback collected through these channels will be reviewed in the Monthly Community Feedback Response Meeting at the site level. The complaints, suggestions, and issues received will be compiled and responses provided by the project staff to selected members of the communities through the monthly community meeting, when possible, as per the guidelines and practices related to the pandemic and the need for social distancing and restrictions for community meetings. As per IMC’s CBFRM guidelines, IMC will ensure that feedback received through the mentioned channels will be collected regularly and documented in the complaint database/log, and a response to the feedback shall be provided within the appropriate time. A CBFRM Committee of selected staff was established at each project site. This committee is responsible to track and document complaints and ensure that responses are provided on time. Sensitive categories of complaints will be kept confidential in order to safeguard those making or involved in the complaint. Only a limited number of authorized staff handle sensitive complaints and have access to information about the complaint. The CBFRM data will be stored in a secured database in the format ofInternational Medical CorpsIntegrated Emergency Health, Nutrition and WASH Services for Conflict Affected Populations in Darfur1, Blue Nile and South KordofanOriginal Date of Submission: April, 2021protected datasheets, and the CBFRM focal points will manage data entry and access-permissions. Information related to Sensitive categories of complaints is only disclosed on strict-need to know basis.International Medical Corps has established policies and procedures for the reporting of sensitive complaints, including those related to allegations of Safeguarding and physical and/or sexual abuse. Only specially trained staff investigate allegations of These sensitive categories of complaints. All sensitive categories of complaints are fast-tracked and referred to the HQ Case Management Team within 24 hours. Sensitive complaints are handled according to applicable internal policies and their reporting mechanisms, so that confidentiality and fast-tracking can be undertaken. As part of the complaint procedure, the immediate health and safety needs of survivors and/or complainants will be evaluated and addressed according to the country office Safeguarding Action Plan (SAP) and the Safeguarding focal point.
