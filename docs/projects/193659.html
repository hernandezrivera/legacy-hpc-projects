Project Summary

Aligned to the WASH cluster objectives 2 and 3 and in line with the framework of the OPT country 2023 HRP strategy under strategic objective 2 and 3 this project aims at improve communities’ access to water & sanitation facilities and securing sufficient and safe drinking water quantities for 1,470 households (6,764 individuals) of the targeted communities and families living in Area “B” and “C” of the West Bank for household purposes, including 3,330 women and girls in two locations namely in Jammala and Rujeib in Ramallah and Nablus Governorates respectively, in addition it aims at mitigating the risk of flooding during winter through the supply and installation of storm water systems for areas in serious needs as well as improving the capacity of the solid waste service providers via the provision of solid waste bins and improving sanitation conditions for the most vulnerable communities in area “C” by providing household latrines for 1,670 households (7,684 individuals) of the targeted communities and families living in Area “B” and “C” of the West Bank, including 3,765 women and girls in 9 locations namely in Kafr Ni’ma and Wadi Salman in Ramallah Governorate and Deir Ballut, Marda, Deir Istiya, Iskaka and Wadi Qana in Salfit Governorate.
The initiative responds to needs highlighted in the WASH 2023 HNO and it is also fully in line with the oPt. Response plan, contributing to the WASH Cluster Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians communities as well as contributing to WASH Cluster Objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks. Access to safe WASH service is a main concern in the West Bank, where according to Water sector regulatory council more than 500,000 Palestinians in the West Bank are receiving less than 50 l/c/d through the piped water service and according to PCBS more than 60% of the households in the West Bank are not connected to sewer services.
Proposed interventions and priorities were coordinated with WASH cluster coordination team, area focal points, technical focal points and governmental counterpart focal points, the initiatives have been endorsed by PWA and the Joint services council for solid waste & waste water in Salfit Governorate, it is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) and in compliance with the Water Master Plan for Area C. The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing with “Goal 6: Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”.
Further, the initiative allows to reduce and more effectively address the core vulnerabilities of communities in need, improving WASH services for unconnected and underserved communities in area “C” and “B” of the West Bank, precisely in Jammala in Ramallah Governorate, Rujeib and improving WASH and solid waste services in 9 locations namely in Kafr Ni’ma and Wadi Salman in Ramallah Governorate and Deir Ballut, Marda, Deir Istiya, Iskaka and Wadi Qana in Salfit Governorate.
All project activities will be closely coordinated and implemented in consultation with and approval of relevant stakeholders including PWA, and the WASH cluster.
By its completion, the project is expected to serve 14448 individuals including (3586 girls, 3703 boys, 3146 women, 3289 men, 368 elderly, and 356 disabled).
The project will also result in Installation/ rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks and HH connections, in addition to the construction/ rehabilitation of storm water network, lakes, ponds, or rainwater drainage system/s, the provision of domestic solid waste containers/bins, and the provision of household latrines.

1 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO, more than 700,000 people across the West Bank reside in areas that are either not connected to piped water services or are very poorly supplied. and according to the Water sector regulatory council more than 500,000 Palestinians in the West Bank are receiving less than 50 l/c/d through the piped water service and according to PCBS more than 60% of the households in the West Bank are not connected to sewer services. All Palestinians living in Area C are directly affected by the Israeli restrictions and control over WASH-related infrastructure and water sources. As per the 2023 HNO, water scarcity in Area C is one of the main factors leading vulnerable families to poverty and food insecurity, as well as further deteriorating living conditions and hygiene practices. Currently and over the coming next year PHG and the Water regulatory council in coordination with PWA and WASH cluster are working on quarterly assessment at community levels for early warning indicators for WASH in the West Bank and Gaza strip as well as annual WASH assessment at service providers level. it was found that there is a need and priority to rehabilitate and expand the water network in Jammala and Rujeib in Ramallah and Nablus governorates respectively, and by reviewing the history of WASH-related problems in Jammala village, after visits and coordination with the local authorities, it was found that pollution and health incidents have been accrued in 2019 were caused by the leakage of sewage water from cesspits to the public water network, which is more than 35 years old, and the water losses from water network reaches more than 60% and the LCD is less than 50 liters. PWA has been already started partially implementation to rehabilitate parts of the water network in 2022, and this project aims to complete the rehabilitation works of the remaining parts of the water network in Jamalla including water transmission pipelines, internal distribution network and house connections, the same for Rujeib as endorsed by PWA to rehabilitate parts of the water network especially in areas not connected to sewage network in the community to reduce potential pollution from cesspits and to improve WASH services conditions. In these communities, water insufficiency can affect negatively impact HHs especially on women who prioritize the use of water for domestic uses rather than personal hygiene, generating negative protection impacts increasing and contagion of COVID. Increased access to WASH facilities, such as water networks and institutions WASH facilities, stands as the humanitarian response to improve communities living conditions. Lack of WASH facilities affects also the hygiene and dignity of women and girls during their menstruation, critically jeopardizing their sense of privacy and personal safety.Access to water is not the only need in such areas- the 2023 HNO the figures indicate a real need at these communities in both sanitation services, and flooding risk mitigation measures on community level. The lack of such services is causing several health, environmental, and social issues which requires urgent intervention to avoid the negative impact caused by this, the proposed locations were identified as under high risk of flooding impacts due to the increasingly frequent extreme weather events in winter, in combination the poor maintenance of stormwater networks and drainage systems. Vulnerabilities to flooding includes risks of displacement and destruction of their properties, and severe health and environmental consequences if the stormwater mixes with the overflow wastewater and solid waste. Hence, a flood mitigation and prevention intervention is highly needed in these 4 communities. More than 90 tons of solid waste are produced per day across Salfit Governorate, this amount of solid waste is managed by number of municipalities along with one Joint Service Councils. Solid waste management (SWM), including collection, transportation, and disposal, is amongst the most critical challenges facing service providers in Salfit Governorate. The lack of waste equipment and tools such as waste bins and collection trucks as well as sanitary land fill has undermined SWM. The lack of resources, outdated equipment and tools, and limitation of collected fees have taken a further toll on the ability of service providers to properly manage solid waste in a healthy and timely manner, which by default has yielded number of bad practices. Many health risks related to the inadequate management of solid waste could be induced; rats, dogs, snakes and other scavengers are attracted to garbage, particularly in hot climates. The Joint services council for solid waste & waste water in Salfit Governorate is responsible about the solid waste management in 14 communities in Salfit Governorate, the JSC is suffering from lack of resources, outdated equipment and tools, and limitation of collected fees, only three collection trucks are working on collection and transferring solid waste from these 14 locations which means high level of waste accumulation and bins are overloaded and not enough, therefore, the initial suggestion to alleviate the current problem of waste accumulation was to provide more waste bins of 1100 liters capacity to accommodate more waste for more long time, at least for three days which is the collection cycle based on the available resources.

2 - Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated and coordinated with the relevant stakeholders, including cluster coordination team, area focal points, technical focal points and governmental counterpart focal points to avoid and overcome work duplication, as well this cooperation and coordination will be maintained and improved at implementation stages at all levels with all stakeholders and organizations. The approach of implementation for this project will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women , municipality , local committees , the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any (local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the field particularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning . the design of the systems will be done under the supervision of PHG , and expected to be supported by PWA and the Joint services council for solid waste & waste water in Salfit Governorate, Funder representative, and member form the community whom will be as members in a steering committee of 3-5 persons , they will be updated/reporting back in periodically meeting by PHG engineer / head of office /representative for the progress and obstacles faced through implementation , and recommendations form next periods , the SC will support and help to find solutions to improve work and progress . the tendering process will go through the PHG procurement system, procurement plan will be designed for this purpose. The M& E officer at PHG, will follow implementation stage by stage through the M&E module and will insure achieving outputs and will measure indicator on regular bases. Although the vulnerability index is low comparing to the WASH vulnerability map 2022, PHG has carried out in-depth and specific assessments in each of the targeted locations, by conducting rapid assessments at community and household levels, field visits and some observations. It was found that these locations are in urgent need of the proposed interventions, for example, in Wadi Qana area, some households do not have latrines, and women and children are forced to use the latrines of relatives or neighbors, or even walk a long distance, and the same applies to solid waste in the proposed locations and in Salfit governorate in general, there are only 3 waste trucks serving The governorate only and the insufficient number of containers, which caused the accumulation of waste in most of the governorate’s communities. Contacts have been made with the Joint Services Council for Waste Management in Salfit. It has been found that the problem is very large, especially the accumulation of waste and insufficient containers, as well as the problems of drowning and flooding in the proposed sites field visits were made to the drowning sites, and some pictures and videos were viewed that document the problems of drowning in the streets and homes in the winter season in the past years. Regarding the water problem in both Jammala and Rujib, contact was made with the PWA and it was confirmed the urgent need for the proposed intervention there, as the water loss rate is very high and the network is very old, and there is a leakage of sewage into the network and the occurrence of pollution.The main activities that will be implemented under the project which will target Jammala village in Ramallah Governorate, Rujeib town in Nablus Governorate and 9 locations namely in Kafr Ni’ma and Wadi Salman in Ramallah Governorate and Deir Ballut, Marda, Deir Istiya, Iskaka and Wadi Qana in Salfit Governorate can be summarized as follows: 1- Supply and installing of about 13,565 meters of water pipelines with different diameters for the benefit of 6764 inhabitants of them 1674 girls, 1470 women, 170 elderly and 165 disabled persons, 2- Supply and installing of about 200 water house connections 3- The construction/ rehabilitation of 4 rainwater drainage systems for the benefit of 5634 inhabitants of them 1400 girls, 1230 women, 144 elderly and 140 disabled persons. 4- The provision of 190 domestic solid waste containers/bins for the benefit of 2000 inhabitants of them 500 girls, 435 women, 52 elderly and 50 disabled persons 5- The provision of 10 household latrines for the benefit of 50 inhabitants of them 12 girls, 11 women, 2 elderly and 1 disabled person.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PHG aim with all of its projects is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. In conjunction with this policy, PHG has ensured these practices by focusing on female heads of household, empowering the most vulnerable of families. By doing community outreach, PHG is able to help normalize female participation in public spaces. By providing home visits involving female evaluators, women can feel more safe in their own homes. PHG will secure with community representatives and local authorities safety and protection methods and measures, especially at a time of the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic at the community and school level by adoption of restricted health protocols while providing service, working in the field and during meetings including social distancing and prevention tools to reduce The spread of COVID-19. PHG and community representatives will conduct a thorough risk analysis for proposed activities in each targeted location especially in area “C” to identify potential risks and propose mitigation measures, including: - Community legal status: to assess if there are military orders or other legal concerns, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will not trigger the community legal case in a harmful way. - Community location related risks: to assess the community surrounding risks, like the existence of settlements, military base, firing zones and bypass roads, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will be applicable and operational in such context, not exposing beneficiaries to further risks. - Relations and sensitivity inside and outside the community: to assess the different cultural and social relations inside the community and in its surrounding, to ensure that the proposed interventions will not increase the social sensitivity or tension between the community, households, and with the surrounding communities. - Type of activity: to assess the community capacities and needs to follow up on activities such as the communities’ ability to operate and maintain the water networks which can contribute to the sustainability of the delivered assistance. In addition to doing no harm, the participatory approach contributes to the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. In order to further inform its assessments and implementation, PHG continuously works on understanding gender roles and household dynamics. By doing so, PHG believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. PHG has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All PHG staff have the obligation to adhere to the organization code of conduct. PHG will ensure that its activities, especially connecting communities with the pipe water service, will not cause overcrowding of the limited water sources and will not cause competition between the communities regarding water supplies, through the coordination with PWA and water service providers. Moreover, and in cooperation with community representatives, PHG will ensure that the project fully covers the water needs of communities and households, avoiding more tensions between the community families and other surrounding communities. Safety of the students of targeted schools will be improved, through increased adequate access to water and sanitation facilities. The proposed project ensured community participation -promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community– since the identification of the needs and the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in increased empowerment at the individual and community level. Particular attention was given to input/feedback provided by women, whose participation is strongly encouraged in any decision-making process. Whenever possible the implementation will be led by community members through a participatory approach, nevertheless, the project will also instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measures to meet “Do No Harm” requirements.
