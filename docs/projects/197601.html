Project Summary

Mercy-USA through its field team will target 4500 HH with USD$150 one-off emergency cash per HH (MPC) to cover the survival needs of the affected people. This intervention is aligned with MPC guidance notes – 2023 to ensure the value covers the agreed SMEB value of 150USD.
The fund for this project is secured (100%) to be implemented during 2023
Mercy-USA will respond to any sudden/onset crises born from natural hazards (flooding, fire, snowstorm) or man-made (displacement) across NW Syria with coordination through FSL, SNF, WASH clusters and Cash Working Group to avoid any overlap.
Mercy-USA will conduct vulnerability assessment and verification for the BNFs in the affected sites before the cash distribution and will ensure the response to be implemented within 7 days from the emergency to ensure effective response is in place.
. Targeting: Will be based on the HNO severity scale and align with the FSL targeting, taking into consideration the nutrition-sensitive criteria PLW, child under 2 years and elderly.
Mercy-USA M&E team will verify the BNFs, monitor the distribution, and conduct PDM after 2 weeks of each distribution.
Mercy USA will have contract agreements with multiple vetted Financial Service Providers (FSPs) to cover all targeted locations for timely response to any emergency, so the FSP has the capacity for cash distribution with huge amount.
Locations will cover broad area in NW Syria at the district level: Ariha, Harim, Idleb, Jisr Ash-Shugur, Afrin, Jebel Saman where Mercy-USA has full access.
The project will be implemented directly by Mercy-USA without IPs
PTT will be possible option for the MPC response in Afrin by transferring to the FSP who will be distributed to beneficiaries under supervision of Mercy-USA team.
Women and men are given equal respect, and have equal opportunities. Relevant sex-disaggregated monitoring data will be collected to assess the project’s indicators and objectives. Protection mainstreaming will be applied through the project activities.

3 - Needs

Based on the draft HNO 2023, PiN is 15.3M, 50% male and 50% female, In NW Syria, the number of people living in last-resort IDPs sites (1,421 sites) reached 1.8 million individuals by October 2022.Last year, 30 per cent of IDP sites in north-west Syria flooded, affecting over 540,000 people . Self-settled sites are the most vulnerable to extreme weather conditions, due to lack of site planning, infrastructure, and camp management systems. In camps in north-east Syria, there were 71 fires reported resulting in injuries, deaths and damage to infrastructure.According to the ISIMM, in the past year, 30 per cent of IDP sites in north-west Syria flooded, Informal settlements/camps are the most vulnerable to extreme weather conditions, due to lack of proper site planning, infrastructure, and camp management systems. CCCM cluster just reported 12 affected IDPs sites due to flood during the last week of November 2022, the sites are self-settled, and they are most vulnerable to bad weather conditions during the winter season. The most need was heating fuel, shelter, food and safe water. In The NW Syria Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) 3rd – 10th Oct 2022 REACH: MEB November value: 152 USD- increase of 11% (compared to September) and The SMEB value in NWS is currently the highest recorded since JMMI monitoring began in 2016. the informal USD/TRY exchange rate increased only slightly by 2% to 18.62 TRY. These are the highest exchange rates recorded in NWS through the JMMI.

4 - Implementation of activities

Based on Mercy-USA Rapid Market assessment conducted in Dec 2022 shows: 70% of the assessed retailers have the ability and capacity to restock the food items on daily basis, 5% on weekly basis, 25% several times a week.Mercy-USA conducts rapid cash feasibility assessment in area (market accessibility, functionality, risk analysis), while engaging all stakeholders include BNFs, and coordinating with other NGOs planning responses in same area to avoid overlap and ensure complementarity. Coordination will be with related stakeholders including local authority, camp management, IDPs representatives through existing communication channels (WhatsApp), which is a key platform to de-duplicate, create synergies, find opportunities for external referrals, harmonization, and “graduation”/transition/exit approaches. and other organization as well as with FSL, SNFI, and Wash clusters and CWG through the Skype group and emails, Following BNFs verification and determination that MPC is most timely and appropriate modality, Mercy-USA distributes one-off emergency MPCA based on the agreed value of 150 USD in cash per HH along with pre-vetted contracted FSPs in USD as the FSP have the capacity, in very limited.Targeting: Will be based on the HNO severity scale and align with the FSL targeting, taking into consideration the nutrition-sensitive criteria PLW, child under 2 years and elderly.M&E team will verify BNFs, monitor the distribution, and conduct PDM after 1 month of distribution

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:1.Decreased availability of cash in project locations, meaning that FSPs are unable to cash out or deliver cash assistance2.The closure of borders and/or key markets creates a problem of supply for other markets and industries,resulting in price increases and product shortages3.Beneficiaries are pressured into paying ‘taxes or fees in relation to CVA’ out of their aid transfers4.COVID-19 outbreaks lead to reduce FSP activity5.Heightened visibility for beneficiaries/exposure 6.Cash not being equally shared/distributed within the HH7.Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV,SEA, discrimination) Mitigation measures1.Mercy-USA will consider a shift in modalities, or to use a combination of modalities (cash, vouchers, and in-kind)2.Mercy-USD will conduct regular market monitoring to assess price and availability, and programming should be adjusted as needed3.Mercy-USA will conduct awareness to the beneficiaries that the assistance is free of charge and they are not required to pay ‘fees’ or ‘taxes’ out of it, this information will be printed out on the banner in each distribution site, complaint feedback mechanism will be in place4.Mercy-USA will arrange to longer distribution period with limited number of beneficiaries per day, as well as door-to-door distribution for most vulnerable household exposed to COVID-195.Engage with relevant authorities as appropriate. 6.Vary the payment days 7.Ensure close monitoring by female aid workers


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA Risks: • Overcrowded during the cash distribution which may cause SEA • Mixed distribution lines for males and females • All distribution staff are males • The cash distribution is not accessible to all gender groups • No shared clear information about timing and amount of cash • No clear procedure regarding loss of card • Taxation on the MPC assistance • No complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) in place Risk mitigation: • Ensure extend distribution days to avoid overcrowding • Separate lines for males and females in all distribution process • Ensure gender balance in the hiring process, ideally to be 50% of the team are female • The cash distribution sites will be chosen to be accessible to all gender groups especially female HH and disables • Clear information about timing, cash amount, and procedures about loss of card of the distribution will be shared with all beneficiaries beforehand and will be existed in the banners in the distribution sites • Sensitization about the scope of the project to all communities with highlighting “This assistance is Free” and this statement will be printed on the beneficiary cards. • Different CFM channels will be in place such as WhatsApp, hotline, email, phone as well as physically by MEAL team. • PSEA awareness materials (such as posters and pamphlets) and reporting hotline will be visible to beneficiaries at the distribution site


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: -Markets are not functioning which may affect the use of MPC -General daily security threats -Danger to staff and beneficiaries including conflict between beneficiaries. - Delays in fund dispersal - Exchange rate fluctuations between USD and TRY and SYP -Spread of COVID-19 in NW Syria which may cause complete lockdown -FSPs offering over-the-counter services do not have sufficient capacity to meet higher caseload -FSPs forced to reduce staff and office hours, limiting over-the-counter service -Limited trading; market shutdown -Critical items unavailable due to market closures, increased demand and hoarding Mitigation: -Regular price monitoring and markets assessment to ensure this intervention will not cause harm to the local markets -Currency is USD and in case of huge currency fluctuation, Mercy-USA might increase the transfer value in coordination with CWG or change the modality-Consider market-based programming, supporting critical markets/traders (actors across the supply chain, including small retail shops) traders directly, by providing them capital to augment their stock of essential goods FSP vetting will be done in coordination with donors to exclude any FSP listed in the sectioned list of OFAC for US treasury, SAM, UNSC. Local Authority interference in cash distribution, mitigation: by signing MOU with LC and camp management to clarify roles and responsibilities.
