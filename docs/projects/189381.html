Project Summary

NRC’s planned and proposed Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) intervention for 2022 is designed to address the emergency WASH needs through mobile response and contribute to improve health, nutrition and protection outcomes through NRC static WASH response. The proposed interventions are to reduce prevalence of malnutrition, contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality, provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster, disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility and durable access to WASH amongst 200, 000 IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in in South Sudan.
The proposed WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with NRC’s health , nutrition and protection programming and teams, applying an integrated approach to uplift people’s lives and contributing to South Sudan HRP and WASH Cluster objectives and the 2022 Humanitarian Response. Recognizing WASH services to be key drivers of health and malnutrition outcomes and women and girls to be the primary users of WASH services, the project aims to reduce GBV risk by ensuring adequate and needs based provision of WASH services that address key protection concerns. NRC’s integrated approach will further address high levels of malnutrition in targeted locations.
NRC will consult and engage communities in preliminary assessments and design process to ensure all NRC’s interventions are tailored to local context and to establish appropriated water and sanitation facilities at community, institutions and household level. To enhance and ensure sustainability and proper usage of the facilities comprehensive training will be provided to female and male members of Water User Committees (WUCs) and Health & Nutrition facility staff. Solid waste management training will also be provided to solid waste/hygiene committees to implement community-driven solid waste collection and disposal campaigns. NRC will also construct incinerators for medical waste disposal at health facilities.
To ensure durable and continues access to safe water ; NRC will establish / rehabilitate small scale solar powered water supply schemes , new boreholes through cost effective manual Borehole drilling. The proposed water supply interventions will increase the safe water access to health, nutrition and protection centers , disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WAH facilities and route to WASH facilities. NRC will construct own driven household / family shared latrines to ensure that immediate and long-term sanitation needs are adequately met and facilities are gender and culturally appropriate. The approach will enhance the ownership and sustainability of the facilities.NRC will also promote good hygiene practices and awareness through training of community hygiene promoters who then will be conducting hygiene promotion at their own communities , community hygiene campaigns, hygiene promotion campaigns and sessions at intuitional levels and provision of hygiene kits, WASH-NFIs and soaps to the vulnerable communities specially in hard to reach areas.

1 - Needs

The WASH cluster 2022 Humanitarian Need Overview sectoral analysis revealed that; total of 6.15 million people is in need of access to safe water, improved sanitation and hygiene facilities. The conditions are worse in Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. The main WASH cluster objectives and strategies for 2022-2023 is to Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties ; Thus, NRC will deliver WASH services through static response and mobile emergency response in all NRC existing counties across all ten states in South Sudan. Access to water and sanitation is projected to remain a major issue in 2022 , further aggravating the risk of malnutrition. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera, AWD, etc. Hence to significantly to contribute to achieve WASH Cluster objective 1 :- Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition; NRC will implement integrated WASH , Health and Nutrition response in targeted counties in all ten states.Moreover; the WASH Cluster HNO further reveled that • Only 39 per cent of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34 per cent in rural areas (FSNMS, 2021). • The main four states namely Unity, Warrap, Jonglei and Upper Nile, where 33 per cent of households travelling for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities (FSNMS, 2021). • The HNO found that 49% of the populations / households are relying on surface and unimproved water such as river, canal , stagnant water as their main water sources for drinking and secondary usages and particularly 71% of households are reported rely and using river water. (FSNMS, 2021).• The rampant open defecation is further creating public health risks especially in areas where people rely and using surface water.• Water storage and water collection containers are limited and only 14% of households reporting having at least one jerrycan and /or bucket to safely store water (FSNMS, 2021).• Protection to access the water points is another concern; 18 per cent of households, both rurally and in urban settings, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water in the two weeks prior to data collection (FSNMS, 2021).The issue is particularly acute in one of the NRC static location of Akobo where 40% households reported feeling unsafe while access and collecting water (FSNMS 2021).• The assessment reveled that ; 80 percent of households did not have access to latrine ; the lack of access and open defecations are rampant mainly in Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei states ; there has been some cultural barriers to use the constructed latrines is also another challenge to improve people sanitation practices.• Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in South Sudan. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Overall, only 16 per cent of the households reported owning soap and this figure was as low as 4 per cent and 11 per cent in Jonglei and Upper Nile States respectively (FSNMS, 2021).• Lack of awareness, prevention and control measures for prevailing Covid-19. Through proposed WASH interventions in water supply (water point rehabilitation, construction and water point disinfection), sanitation (latrine construction, waste removal) and hygiene promotion. NRC further aims to improve and strengthen ongoing WASH initiatives and expand into further IPC 3,4 and 5 locations in all targeted counties.

(Health and Nutrition facilities , schools and Protection centers) Environmental Health : • Conduct safe Solid Waste Management disposal , drainage and debris clean up campaigns- 20 Campaigns • Construct / rehabilitate incinerators / ash pits at health facilities - 5 Facilities.• Construct/ Rehabilitate Waste disposal / Sharp pits – 5 units Hygiene Promotion: • ToT training of 100 Community hygiene promoters (40% male and 60% female) in targeted payams/bomas.• distribute hygiene kits – 4,000 HKs• Distribute WASH –NFIs kits – 10,000 Kits • Conduct integrated WASH, nutrition and health awareness raising sessions and awareness • conduct special “Women and Girl’s menstrual hygiene education and promotion” sessions on good menstrual hygiene practices• form and train School Hygiene Clubs - 20 schools.• Establish handwashing facilities in institutions and common places.• Conduct WASH global events in schools (GHD , World water day, etc) – 10 Schools.• At least 5 (total - 20) hygiene and sanitation promotion campaigns per project site, 20 campaigns: • Prepare and implement cholera prevention measures through hygiene promotion campaigns, • Broadcast Covid-19 awareness, prevention and control messages using Jingles in local languages using Mega phones.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedbacks will be collected , analyzed and used to adapt and shape the project design if required and implementation. NRC will conduct post distribution monitoring to assess beneficiary’s satisfaction on NRC kits such as WASH NFIs and Hygiene kits distribution focusing on quality, quantity and timely distribution. The findings will be used to adjust the ongoing and future kits distribution to improve beneficiary’s satisfaction. Moreover, beneficiaries feedback will be received for all NRC activities including water points establishment and rehabilitation , intuitional and household latrines , waste management systems and modality of conducting hygiene promotion sessions and campaigns ; the findings and feedback will be used to adjust and improve the quality of future programming.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NRC will use different strategies and sources to collect WASH feedback and complaints from most vulnerable members. Trained community hygiene promoters , health and Nutrition volunteers and water user’s committee members are tasked to collect the feedback or complaints from most vulnerable members who would not be able to reach or lodge the complaints established feedback mechanism. Moreover, the feedback will also be collected during the house to house visits to monitor Household latrine construction , conduct post distribution monitoring and other Household surveys from vulnerable members specially who are not Iterate , person with disabilities , women and girls. Vulnerable members feedbacks will also be collected during the distribution at distribution venues where complain desk will be present and represented by respective community leaders / volunteers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?
7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NRC will conduct the kick off meeting includes sensitization and mobilization of the project prior to start of project, through consultations with community leaders, both formal and informal. Chiefs, project management committees, water user’s committees and women/youth groups will be provided with full details of assistance including water, sanitation and hygiene promotion activities. The project introductory meetings in all project areas include explanation and orientation on how to make feedback and complainants and how the feedback and complaints will be analyzed and actin will be taken against the information gathered from the beneficiaries. Moreover; NRC staffs and local authority will be trained on how to orient, collect, record and keep confidentially on beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints received.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Construction of household and institutional latrines provide safe access to sanitation facilities and reduce exposure of women and girls to potential violence and insecurity while walking long distances for OD. NRC female staff and volunteers will conduct registration, verification and HP session at the HH level to avoid that women and girls travel to distance venues for WASH assistance. NRC will also establish multiple distribution points for WASH NFIs and HKs distribution to ensure women and girls can easily access without harassment or violence. NRC will make sure all beneficiaries are consulted and informed about NRC’s interventions through meetings, especially through FGDs with women and girls conducted by NRC trained female staff and volunteers to get their perception and contribute to WASH design, site selection and implementation. NRC will also explain implementation modalities and intervention timelines to target beneficiaries, specifically those designed to mitigate exclusion of women and girls from consultation and decision making process. As part of accountability to affected communities, NRC will employ two different types of complainant and feedback mechanisms - responsive and proactive, permitting direct and indirect beneficiaries to provide feedback and/or lodge complaints. Moreover, PDM will be conducted to allow beneficiaries to provide their feedback on satisfaction with quality and quantity of goods and items included in the kit. All beneficiary feedbacks and ideas are taken into account to adjust and realign ongoing and future WASH interventions. There might be chances of impartial and need based selection of beneficiaries by influences of external stakeholders / local authority directly or indirectly by local authority on humanitarian assistance. NRC will use clear selection criteria will be explained to local authority and beneficiaries before the selection process and registration and verification of beneficiaries will also be conducted at house to house to avoid any impartial and double assistances of same beneficiaries.
