Project Summary

This project aims to support the CCCM response to the immediate needs of the most vulnerable displaced populations in the northwest of Syria by providing multi-sectoral assistance in camps and improving the basic infrastructure and shelter conditions in IDP sites through:
i) Maintaining and managing three reception centers in A'zaz, Al-Bab, and Mezenaz, Aleppo;
ii) Maintaining and managing the planned camp in Mareet Elkhwan, Idleb;
iii) Providing 17,200 family tents in 88 IDP sites with tent insulation and ground insulation support, including replacement of 5,400 family tents;
iv) Ensuring 7,000 family tents are available to members of the CCCM cluster for the contingency stock; and
v) Improving site infrastructure and facilities of 40 informal settlements.
The project will also contribute to improving the management quality, accountability, and communal coping mechanisms of IDP sites through:
vi) Enhancing camp management capacity of site managers of 40 informal settlements through the provision of site management support;
vii) Enhancing camp management capacity of NGOs through the provision of CCCM training; and
viii) Enhancing the communal capacity of 40 informal settlements to address fire risk through the provision of fire safety measures and fire safety and first aid training.
Finally, the project also aims to inform the CCCM response of cluster members through:
ix) Collecting information about IDP displacements and IDP site profiles, including multi-sectoral gap analysis, and reporting to cluster members.

Needs

According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 6.2 million people are internally displaced in Syria. There are an estimated 871,150 IDPs living in last resort camps, informal settlements, transit/reception centres, and collective centres (including schools, residential building, and warehouses). Of these, almost half (420,103 IDPs) are in the northwest of the county, the majority of whom are located in Idleb Governorate. According to the CCCM Cluster, throughout the IDP sites in northwest Syria, children make up 54 per cent of the population or about 226,856 individuals. The CCCM Cluster has also tracked 1,401 female-headed and 466 child-headed households. Furthermore, there are 1,573 acute or chronically ill people, 1,620 elderly persons, 1,451 persons with physical disabilities, 389 persons with mental disabilities and 76 separated children in need of assistance. According to the August 2019 population figures shared by the CCCM Cluster, 15.1% of the estimated populations of 6,174,977 individuals in Idleb and Aleppo governorates are young children (<5), 35.5% are children (5-17), 45.5% are adult (18-59), and 3.9% are over 59 years old (elderly). During sudden displacements, IDPs have often no choice but to seek shelter in planned camps, informal settlements, transit/reception centres and collective centres. Between 1 January 2018 and 22 December 2018, the CCCM Cluster has tracked almost 678 thousand IDP movements across northern Syria. During this time period, an estimated 18% of displaced IDPs resorted to or transited through, IDP sites. Across Syria, the IDP site population increased by an estimated 16 per cent, with such increases witnessed almost uniformly across all parts of the country. To cope with sudden displacements, the CCCM Cluster has actively promoted the establishment and management of Reception Centres for sudden displacements in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. These centres have hosted 42,034 IDPs (monthly cumulative sum) since September 2017. The planned camp IOM manages in Mareet Elkhwan, Idleb, also has received 1,221 individuals (282 families) from congested and vulnerable IDP sites since its opening in September 2018, contributing to alleviating the burden on already overstretched IDP hosting capacity in the region. Out of those living in IDP sites, approximately 89% are residing in informal settlements (IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM), October 2018). There are thirteen major clusters of IDP sites in the area: Atareb, A'zaz, Jarablus, Bab Al Hawa, Dana, Darkosh, Deir Hassan, Harim, Karama, Liyajlikum, Qah, Salam and Sarmada camps clusters. With more than 291 IDP sites grouped in 11 clusters, Idleb has the highest concentration of IDP camps in all of the Syrian Governorates. Out of twenty-four IDP sites in northern rural Aleppo, the A'zaz cluster camps hosts nineteen IDP sites. Due to its proximity to Turkey and relative safety, A'zaz has continually received the flow of IDPs throughout the conflict. Established in late 2016, the Jarablus camps cluster currently has three informal settlements. Compared to formal camps, collective centres, and reception centres (which do receive some form of relief assistance), informal settlements have intermittent or non-existent basic service provision, as well as poorer standards of sanitation and housing infrastructure. Furthermore, the majority of informal settlements have not been integrated into ongoing joint humanitarian response planning due to access issues and a lack of reliable information. Heavy rains and serious floods throughout December 2018 and January 2019, have affected a large number of these informal settlements, making living conditions for IDPs even worse. Therefore, there are urgent needs of providing multisectoral humanitarian assistance to displace populations in reception centers and planned camp as well as to improve living conditions and management quality of IDP sites, especially in informal settlements, in NWS.

Implementation of activities

The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with CCCM Cluster Coordinator, by the IOM Turkey cross-border team based in Gaziantep with technical and administrative support from IOM Sub-office in Gaziantep as well as the IOM main office in Ankara which includes all activities related to procurement and management of the warehouse. For the contingency stock of family tents, IOM will undertake all operational activities including procurement, warehousing, transportation of the items, and payment of IP distribution costs. Cluster partners wanting to utilize the stock will need to place a request through the established Review Board system. Upon the approval, IOM will disburse the items to the cluster partners for distribution. IOM also provides secretarial and administrative support to the Cluster Review Board. For all the other activities, IOM will work in partnership with well-experienced NGOs in each specific activity in camps and camp-like settings as implementing partners, selected from active members of the CCCM Cluster. For tent insulation activities, IOM will procure, warehouse, and transship tent insulation materials and plastic sheeting to IPs in Syria. Third-party monitoring mechanisms will be utilized to monitor and evaluate project activities. Warehouse verification, distribution and post-distribution monitoring activities will be conducted by third parties for at least 30 per cent of the overall distributions and activities. The disaggregated number of beneficiaries will be verified through regular reporting by cluster members through the 4W reporting format to the CCCM Cluster and through IOM weekly progress reports. Exit interviews with men and women beneficiaries will be also conducted at the project sites. IOM will also engage all different groups of target communities through regular meetings and transparent information management throughout the implementation. IOM will apply the IOM Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) frameworks, including community feedback mechanism, focus group discussions and joint community-IOM post service monitoring, to all project activities and will work to build the capacity of project partners in AAP throughout the project lifespan. Based on feedback from the affected population, the project is modified as much as possible to better address the needs of camp residents. Gender and age are key vulnerability criteria (e.g. female-headed household, households with 2 or more children less than 5 years old) for beneficiary selection of some activities, such as the selection of beneficiaries relocating to the planned camp. Even for projects which use a blanket approach for all camp residents, such as tent insulation project, gender and age information will be used to tailor the modality of assistance. Moreover, targets indicators are disaggregated by sex and age. For example, # of IDPs living in IDP sites provided with life-saving assistance will be counted by men, women, boys, girls. Men and women largely receive similar benefits, which will be verified by post-distribution satisfaction surveys.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risks 1: Continued operations of reception centers, planned camp, and any possible coordinated relocation assistance will create negative pull and push factors for IDPs. Linked to this is the risk the Reception Centers will become ad-hoc camps if IDPs perceive it to be a long term solution. Risk 2: Increased risk of inter-community tension between Collective Centre residents and host communities due in part to an unequal level of assistance given to Collective Centre residents in comparison to host communities.Risk 3: Intentions surveys and/or needs assessments will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs living in camps and contribute to assessment fatigue. Risk 4: Distribution of tents will lead to more spontaneous and informal settlements, which itself poses risks associated with the absence of official site management and coordinated services. Risk 5: Targeting of communities and beneficiary selection will fuel unintended inter or intra community tensions. Risk 6: The management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities.Risk 7: Construction contractors could create some tensions or harm based on their salary payments. Mitigating measures:1: i) Ensure intentions of IDPs arriving to the Reception Centers and planned camp are recorded to assess duration of stay, and intended final destination (including return); ii) Ensure comprehensive service mapping of IDPs sites in the region is undertaken, intentions results are shared with operational partners and service providers to better coordinate services at intended destinations; iii) communication with IDPs to assist them in making informed decisions and ensure their movement is voluntary, safe and sustainable; and iv) Ensure any assistance offered to IDPs departing sites is not done randomly but is coordinated. Mitigating measure 2: i) Ensure that the Collective Centre is all needs based and that CC residents are amongst the most vulnerable (See CCCM Guidance on-site verification). ii) Not servicing CC in isolation but providing wider services.Mitigating measure 3 i) Ensure that the intention surveys and needs assessments are as light as possible and done ideally at registration or shortly after; ii) Ensure healthy communication with the people of concern to avoid raising unrealistic expectation; and iii) IOM will strive to employ women enumerators to achieve gender-balanced teams.Mitigating measure 4: i) Maintain the CCCM guidance and position on the camp establishment and camp support ensuring that camps are the last-resort, and ii) Any requests by agencies operational inside Syria for distribution of tent will be requested follow CCCM guidelines to minimize this risk. IOM will also consult with the CCCM review board to ensure due diligence on coordination of sites is adhered to. Mitigating measure 5: To mitigate against tension and project disruption, IOM will engage the target community through regular meetings, transparent information management and beneficiary selection, and throughout implementation. Mitigating measure 6: IOM through its implementing partner will ensure that any management structure is representative of the entire community with strong selection criteria.Mitigating measure 7: Portion of unskilled construction workers are composed of residents. Furthermore, the project will ensure that partners work in line with humanitarian principles and follow the principles of Do No Harm. IOM and its partners will ensure that its actions and interventions do not adversely affect individuals or their communities, our partners or colleagues, and that they do not expose them to harm. IOM will also apply the IOM Accountability to Affected Populations frameworks, including community feedback mechanism, focus group discussion and joint community-IOM post service monitoring, to all project activities and will work to build the capacity of project partners in AAP throughout the project.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Staff mobility and access restrictions to affected population: Active fighting, shifting of the conflict lines and potential closure of the border might prevent delivery of humanitarian assistance. o Partners will elaborate on different plans for deliveries allowing for flexible dispatching of goods when certain areas are inaccessible. Contingency areas in other locations have been pre-identified with the CCCM cluster during project design. o The security assessment will be undertaken before the planned transportation and distribution of items as well as appropriate risk mitigating measures will be put in place. o No UN staff will cross into Syria, which will lower the profile of the activities and ensures the safety of both UN and IP staff. o ‘Duty of care’ strategy in place for local partners. • Aid diversion by armed groups. o IPs are encouraged to report any incidents of aid diversion and interference incidents by the armed groups. o IOM utilizes third-party monitoring mechanisms to conduct warehouse verification, distribution and post-monitoring distribution activities to mitigate the risk. o Targeting of most vulnerable beneficiaries will also be a key under this action. Recruitment of female staff within distribution teams will be considered. • HLP disputes on project sites. o HLP Due Diligence Guidelines has been/will be used to minimize the risks associated with land ownership. o All partners will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate ownership of land, and discuss with owners and available site management. o For sites in which the landowner refuses heavy physical works such as ground insulation, IOM may only focus on light works such as internal insulation and weatherproofing. • Risks mitigation measures specific for contingency stock of family tents. o Tents will be released to trusted NGOs who have a proven track-record inside northern Syria. o Risk will be managed through the use of the Cluster Review Board mechanism to determine which community tents will be released for. One criterion by which the Review Board makes its decisions is a question of access and security threat in a particular area of the community. o IPs are requested to distribute within two weeks of the delivery to their warehouses inside Syria to minimize the threat of looting of warehouses. o IOM has rigorous monitoring and evaluation tools in place to ensure dispatch, transportation, and delivery of tents to partners inside Syria.
