Project Summary

The project will target Manicaland and Mashonaland provinces as they have the highest number of people that will be food insecure at the peak of the lean season at 825 215 (Manicaland) and 577 954 (Mashonaland Central) (ZimVAC, 2019). Further, the project will target the following districts namely Chimanimani, Chipinge, Mbire, and Goromonzi through the provision of small grain seed, garden vegetable seeds, setting up of community and school nutrition gardens and the rehabilitation and mechanization of key livestock infrastructure. Chipinge and Chimanimani districts have been selected due to their experiences with Cyclone Idai that resulted in heavy losses in crop productivity and having more than 6% of households that are supporting an orphan and a physically or mentally challenged individual. The current project will ride on the existing project funded by ADH in Chipinge and Chimanimani. Mbire and Goromonzi districts have been selected as they have a high food insecurity prevalence of 62513 and 141 252 individuals respectively. Help from Germany is already running projects in the aforementioned districts. The total caseload for the project will be 25 000 beneficiaries (5 000 in Chimanimani, 5 000 in Chipinge, 10 000 in Mbire and 5 000 in Goromonzi).

1 - Activities

1. Inception Workshop and Community feedback mechanisms The implementation of the project will start with an inception workshop to sensitize beneficiaries and all relevant stakeholders on project objectives and interventions. The identification and selection of wards for the interventions will be guided by the local authorities. The selection will use the rural participatory approach in line with the vulnerability criteria developed by Help that is Food-insecure households in the selected districts i.e. poor households, households without livestock, assets or alternative sources of income, enlarged households, female and child headed households, households that have access to land but no resources to utilise it. The identified communities will participate in selecting the beneficiaries for crop inputs, poultry stock, goats or sheep and cattle supplementary feeds. With this participatory community-based approach, beneficiaries will be quickly mobilized, selected and registered. The setting up of grievance redress mechanisms shall encourage the participatory approach and ensures fair practices throughout the project. 2. Distribution of agricultural inputs 5000 vulnerable households with labour capacity will be selected and registered. Help will procure agricultural inputs and distribute to households. The ration per household will comprise of either 5kg sorghum or 10kg maize seed, 100kg basal fertilizer, 100kg top dressing fertilizer, 200 cuttings of orange fleshed vitamin A sweet potato and Nua 45 bio fortified sugar beans.3. Facilitate the establishment of 80 community gardens The project will support vegetable production through the establishment of community gardens. The overall 80 community gardens will be supported. The community garden selection will be done in collaboration with the local authorities. The selected sites should have reliable water source, be accessible and 30m away from the river bank where water source is a river. After site selection, the gardens are supported with the fencing materials and the community provides labour. The final beneficiaries for the gardens will be selected by the communities in line with the selection criteria highlighted above, which ensures that the most affected HHs will benefit. The selected garden beneficiaries will receive vegetable seed packs and trained on best agronomic and horticultural practices for vegetable production. 4. Facilitate the establishment of school nutrition gardens The project will support the establishment of school nutrition gardens for learning purposes and complimenting Government of Zimbabwe (GoZ) feeding programmes. The overall 20 school nutrition gardens will be supported with vegetable seed packs, fencing material seed packs, garden tools, drip kits, water tanks and solar water pumping kits. The selection of schools will be done in collaboration with the local authorities and the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education. The selection criteria will be as follows: schools with high enrolment and have a reliable water source close to the school. 5. Small livestock production support Beneficiaries will be selected in line with the vulnerability criteria highlighted above i.e. poor households, households without livestock, assets or alternative sources of income, enlarged households, female and child headed households, households that have access to land but no resources to utilise it and afterward registered in a beneficiary database. The distribution of the including chickens, goats or sheep and will be accompanied by training on good husbandry practices. Households targeted to receive poultry each get 6 birds and 25kg poultry feed. Households targeted to receive goats or sheep each get 3 animals. 6. TrainingTraining the vulnerable smallholder farmers on how to improve yields, manage crop pests and livestock diseases. 7. M and E Joint monitoring of all project interventions with GoZ will be conducted though out the project period.
