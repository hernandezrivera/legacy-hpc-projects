Project Summary

The project will work to support the Syrian students who affected by the crisis, since the current situation in camps getting worse and the student suffer from cold and floods, Afaq try to find emergent solution for students to get access to non-formal/informal education and raising its quality, as well as providing remedial and catch-up education for the dropped out, through providing safe environment of education, in addition, to enable those who are out of school to return to schools, and those are at risk of dropping out to continue their learning.
The safe learning environment will be demonstrated by the following:
1. Rehabilitation of the schools which existing in the Jarablus cluster to become more effective for education and more interactive place for students, addition to hygiene facilities in the targeted school to become valid for healthy use, with taking gender issues into consideration while designing bathrooms, separate the bathrooms (males - females) addition to paint the classrooms with Delightful colors to create a creative attractive learning environment, encourage values of tolerance, child rights, human rights, and civil peace, through drawings and posters suitable for class and age
2. Training the teachers and administrative staff on "Principles of psychological support -Classroom Management" -Dealing with children and adolescents-Modern teaching methods-Education in emergency situations-school management skills”.
3. provide the students(female) with the dignity kits with taking the girl's monthly needs into consideration (kits for females contain special needs of females) and provide the students(male) with the hygiene kits contain special needs of males)
4. Providing students "male-female" with stationary, schoolbooks and necessary bags for the education process, in addition, to supply the school with necessary visual aids and electronic devices.
5. Provide children (boys-girls) with psycho-social support through activities conducted by specialized staffs, in addition, to assign a protection team and train them.
6. Provide schools with heating devices and fuel
7. Installation child-friendly spaces and covering the floor with Special material "rubber " to protects the Childs from shocks when they play
8. Awareness-raising campaigns targeting residents of the Jarablus cluster and the neighboring camps about the importance of education and going back to schools, the culture of peace and security, disadvantages of early /forced marriage, extremism, child recruitment, and children labor, in addition to how to deal with remnants of war.
In addition, a gender equality supervisor will be assigned to ensure implementing all the project's activities with taking gender issues in consideration

Needs

According to HRP 2018 an estimated 1.75 million children, or almost one-third of school-agechildren (aged 5-17 years) from the 2015/16 school-year were out of school and a further 1.35 million were at risk of dropping out.Around 150,000 education personnel from all governorates, including teachers, left the education system, which has negatively affected the quality of education for all children. Since the beginning of the crisis, over one in three schools are damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter.Schools, account for an estimated 33 percent of collective centers are reported as the most common building type used for IDP sites in Syria Increased internal displacement has put the current infrastructure under enormous pressure and highlighted the need for light school rehabilitation.The comments and discussions within the education sector and the camp sector have shown the weakness of education services in the camps " The education system inside camps is overburdened and overstretched to the extent that schools and learning environments are unsafe, overcrowded and under-resourced. The key impediments for children accessing to education are including safety and security along roads to and from schools and alternative learning spaces; Lack of teaching and learning materials; poor quality teaching; damage of education infrastructure.The education in emergencies is the safeguard of dignity and preserves of life, where the learner (male/female) will become less vulnerable to sexual or economic exploitation and will be able to know and understand the ways to save their life, add to that education Supports the right of health.“Some areas are without a curriculum or have seen changes to the curriculum, and many facilities have been damaged or in need of rehabilitation” according to HNO report.Moreover, According to ACU's survey, there are 72 functional schools distributed within 62 IDP camps only among all 226 assessed camps in Aleppo and Idleb governorates, and there aren't any schools in the remaining 164 camps. Furthermore, according to HNO, the number of people in educational need at Jarablus is 23,763 (male-female)AFAQ team keen to find a solution for EIC, where there is an urgent need, especially in Jarablus. As the number of camps in Jarablus is increased to 10 camps according to AFAQ assessment with only one school in the area. The team in Al Jabal camp at Jrablus is following up the movement of displaced people on a permanent basis and within the last survey has indicated the following: There is in " Al Jabal camp” 1250 displaced family (1423 male - 1727 Female - 3100 children under 18), as well as the number of students which are at the educational age 2075 students (1202 girls- 873 boys) aged between 6-14 and 25 children with special needs (12 Female 13 male) The Statistic which carried out by AFAQ team has indicated that percentage of students who have dropped out of the education has exceeded 86% of the total number of students in the education age within the area in which the survey has taken place.As well as, it has been recorded 126 workers in the education field (72 female - 54 male) living in the camps that have been surveyed.The survey which carried out by AFAQ has shown that there is no school in work within the previous community.

Implementation of activities

This project will work to support the Syrian students who are affected by the crisis to get access to non-formal/informal education and raising its quality, as well as providing remedial and catch-up education for the dropped out, through providing a safe environment of education, in addition, to enable those who are out of school to return to schools, and those are at risk of dropping out to continue their learning.This project will target, 2075 students (1202 girls- 873 boys) aged between 6-14 with remedial and catch-up education and 25 children with special needs (11 Female 13 male), to re-integrate them into an education system and promote the following - Provision of a safe learning environment in the camp to encourage parents to send their children back to school. - Rehabilitating the school in Al Jabal camp in Jarablus to improve its capacity in order to become a safe environment for learning - Improving education’s quality through building the capacity of the education and management staff, as well as supplying schools with explanation tools and suitable aids - Provide remedial and catch-up education - Protecting children through providing PSS to both students and parents and raising their awareness of the importance of education and disadvantages of early marriage, child recruitment, in addition to concern for children with special needs and work on re-integrate them within the rows of the educated students. -provide the students(female) with the dignity kits with taking the girl's monthly needs into consideration (kits for females contain special needs of females) and provide the students(male) with the hygiene kits contain special needs of males) - Providing students "male-female" with stationary, schoolbooks and necessary bags for the education process, - Awareness-raising campaigns targeting residents of Al Jabal camp and the neighboring camps in Jarablus (1423 male - 1727 Female - 3100 children under 18) about the importance of education and going back to schools, the culture of peace and security, disadvantages of early /forced marriage, extremism, child recruitment, and child labor, in addition to how to deal with remnants of war.We will hold an evaluation in the middle of the project by internal evaluation by the official monitoring and evaluation officer of our organization. At the end of the project, we will hold an assessment to measure achieved results and compare it to the status quo ante, before the project’s intervention.Opinion Boxes will be placed in each school for students, along with the “Winner of the month” Journal in school.Here are some examples of how to monitor some of the indicators:# of students (girls and boys) who are enrolled in remedial & catch-up education (periodic checklists of students from the school directors).# of class that have been processed # of members of the teaching staffs (male-female) who have attended training workshops and the degree of their development (by a competent coach who will periodically visit schools and meeting with teachers).
