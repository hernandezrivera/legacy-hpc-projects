Project Summary

According to UN OCHA – Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020, The lack of protection from violence, abuse and neglect remains a key consequence of the current humanitarian crisis in Somalia for up to 3.2 million people. Out of those, two million are living in displacement and 1.2 million people belong to host communities. Out of the estimated 3.2 million people 64 percent are children, and 48 percent are women and girls
Catholic Relief Services (CRS) proposes to scale up protection services to vulnerable Internally Displaced persons and other crisis-affected Somalis in Mogadishu, Afgooye Corridor, Baidoa, Xudur and Cadaado,.This package will include access to integrated prevention of gender-based violence (GBV) and comprehensive services for survivors, including children who are victims of GBV; child friendly spaces that promote protection. CRS will ensure transparency and accountability to ensure high quality project deliverable and partner with Save Somalia Women and Children (SSWC) to implement the activities as part of the wider localization government strategy
The proposed project is aimed at addressing and reversing the impacts of the current humanitarian and protection related crises compounded by the impacts of Corona virus on the lives of vulnerable people in the targeted areas. The main objective of the project is to complement CRS existing programs funded by USAID and fill the existing critical protection gaps in the above proposed areas reaching 66,400 direct beneficiaries. CRS will account for the specific protection needs of men, women, boys and girls, as well as disabled persons, older persons, marginalized groups, and other groups with specific vulnerabilities within beneficiary communities at all stages of the project cycle, and will gather data disaggregated by sex and age, as well as disability The main objectives of the project will be:
A) Target populations access comprehensive primary care and have reduced morbidity.
B) The risk of GBV among vulnerable groups (including women, men, boys, girls, disabled) is reduced and survivors of GBV access comprehensive services.
The project’s sub sectors and activities will correspond with the objectives of Somalia’s National Development Plan: Health (NDP 7.2); and Protection (NDP 7.8);

Project Activities

The project will address priority community needs through the following activities a) Prevention and Response to Gender-based ViolenceThis will include strengthening the capacities of the CRS supported existing safe houses and crises centers. in Hodan (Mogadishu), Afgooye Corridor, Baidoa and Cadaado. Community sensitization on prevention and response on GBV will be conducted and project staff and CHW trained on GBV prevention and responseb) Provision of psychosocial support Trained counsellors will be recruited to provide psychological support and counselling. They will follow up the procedure of the case management in a confidential manner and will respect the rights and the safety of the survivor. c) Procurement of dignity kits for survivorsDignity kits including sanitary pads and other basic and essential supplies will provided to the GBV survivors admitted and discharged from the safe houses and crises centers. d) Procurement of solar lights for street lighting in hot spot areas CRS and its partners will hold community consultations with elders, men and women to identify the optimal placements for the lights before installation. Latrines and water points will be presented as default options though communities may decide on other placements.e) Strengthening community-based referral mechanisms CRS and its partner (SSWC) will closely work with IDP camp focal points and cluster members to ensure a robust referral mechanism between the women’s health clinic and the surrounding IDP camps and neighborhoods. SSWC will continue to refer victims of GBV to the SOS main hospital in Mogadishu for treatment. Any transfer of child patients from one facility to another will be documented and the child will be accompanied by a family member or facility staff.f) Provide primayr health care for, with special attention for pregnant women and young children to reduce mobidity and mortality

Mitigation Measures

1. Exclusion and Marginalization of Women, girls and Boys.Women, Children and members of minority clans might face risks of being excluded in decision making processes on protection issues that affect them. Generally, men and grand mothers are decision makers and mostly influence views and opinions of other household and community members thus affecting decision outcomes. CRS will ensure that there is equal participation of women, men, old, disabled and children in all proposed protection activities. Through outreach and awareness sessions, CRS will sensitize and empower women, children and other vulnerable groups on their rights. Strong beneficiary selection and inclusion criteria will be developed to ensure vulnerable groups are not left out 2. Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanismsThe project will ensure transparency and accountability to affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them will be discussed. CRS will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming. The project teams will be trained to understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. CRS Set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that does not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff.Protection staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability will be considered) and all project staff will be trained on protection mainstreaming and protection staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. In coordination with other protection actors CRS will enhance the capacity of community workers to monitor, report and refer protection concerns (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures3. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areasThere is the high risk of displacement and evictions of the most vulnerable persons in the IDP camps especially possible post-election violence. CRS will closely work with the existing community-based structures and local authorities to address the challenges through the land tenure system, lease land for more than 10 years or buy permanent land for them. CRS will continuously advocate work for more funding in order to address such risks as they arise.
