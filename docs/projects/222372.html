Project Summary

Save the Children (SCI) proposes an emergency food and livelihoods assistance project to alleviate the urgent needs of conflict- and flood-affected populations in Abyei and Maban counties. This initiative will provide crucial support to the most vulnerable households in IPC4+ locations through direct cash transfers for essential food needs and emergency livelihood support, including emergency food production by distributing main and dry season agricultural kits (Crop kits, Vegetable kits, fishing kits, tools), establish kitchen and nutrition gardens in the target locations, training for community animal health workers to provide treatment and vaccination support to livestock dependent households affected by conflict and climatic shocks. SCI will integrate key messages on gender equality, protection, early warning systems, environmental management, climate change, conflict sensitivity, hygiene, nutrition, and disaster risk reduction into project activities, aiming to alleviate immediate suffering, strengthen community resilience, and promote long-term food security and livelihoods in the affected areas.
The proposed interventions will be complementary to the existing health , nutrition and WASH programme being implemented by SCI in the selected locations. The targeted households will be inclusive of internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities' households meeting the defined vulnerable criteria. The most vulnerable youths at risks, female headed households, widows, elderly, people with disability, low-income households, food insecure households, households taking care of chronical ill members, orphans, separated children, households with high dependency ratio will be considered.
The project will reach out to 12,000 people (at least 60% female, 15% people with disability), with urgent live saving food assistance and support emergency food production, to address their immediate food gaps whilst reducing illness and mortality as a result of the current food crisis.. Save the children will coordinate with other actors in the areas of location to avoid duplication and ensure harmonization of cash transfer values, targeting and strengthen complementary.
The accountability to the affected population mechanisms will be strengthened to ensure beneficiaries are aware of the project activities, aware of complaint feedback mechanisms, can safely access distribution sites with dignity to enable safe programming.
The design of the proposed project is informed by findings from the need assessments, multi-sectoral assessments, IPC 2024/2025 analysis, Inter Agency Rapid Needs Assessment reports, humanitarian updates and project evaluations. The proposed project directly contributes to two of the HRPs’ strategic objectives 1: Vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs ;FSL Cluster Objective 1 & 2: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5) and FSL Cluster Objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock.

1. Needs

The dire food security situation in South Sudan, particularly in Abyei and Maban -Upper Nile, demands urgent humanitarian intervention. A projected 2.1 million children are at risk of acute malnutrition, a significant increase from the previous year. The crisis is driven by factors such as conflict, climate change, and economic instability. Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) faces significant food Insecurity challenges, classified in IPC AMN Phase 4. An estimated 224,000 people (49.1 percent of the state’s population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC AFI Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between December 2023 and March 2025. In all six counties in Warrap and the Abyei Administrative Area, the situation is likely to deteriorate during the lean season of April to June 2025. Conflict disrupts agricultural activities, displaces populations, and hinders market access. Limited infrastructure, particularly poor road access, further hampers development and exacerbates food insecurity. A Multisector Needs Assessment-August 2024 conducted by Save the Children International (SCI) revealed that a significant portion of the population struggles to afford food, with 32% of households unable to meet their food needs. Malnutrition is a major concern, with 30.6% of households lacking access to nutrition facilities and 1.4% unaware of their existence. Poor infant and feeding practices, limited healthcare access, and insufficient health and nutrition interventions are primary contributors to malnutrition. The 2024 IRNA report show that many households are relying on wild fruits as a coping mechanism for food shortages. Livestock, a crucial livelihood source, has been frequently raided during intercommunal conflicts, further exacerbating the situation. Insecurity has led to widespread displacement and disrupted livelihoods, affecting fishing communities, beekeepers, and those who gather thatching grass, firewood, and wild fruits. The lack of essential livelihood kits, such as crop seeds and fishing gear, has further hindered recovery efforts for the 2024 season. The ongoing conflict, coupled with failed crop production due to prolonged dry spells and pests, has severely disrupted livelihoods and coping mechanisms. Respondents consistently expressed concerns about food shortages, livestock losses, and water scarcity, even in relatively safer areas.Maban- Upper Nile: In the peak of the lean season, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate in Upper Nile State with an estimated 1.02 million people (65.1 percent of the state’s population) likely to be in Phase 3 or above, of which 10,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), 330,000 people will likely be in Phase 4, and 683,000 people will likely be in Phase 3. The deterioration in food insecurity is driven by the depletion of cereal stocks by majority of households, a rise in staple food prices due to low local supply to markets and inaccessibility to markets during rainy season, limited income-earning opportunities, persistent macro-economic challenges in the country. The deterioration in food security is compounded by the impact of the ongoing crisis in Sudan and its effects on supplies to markets and influxes of returnees and refugees in Upper Nile. The state is considered the main entry for majority of returnees from Sudan, and this is likely to put more burden on the local communities’ resources.Save the children has operational presence in Abyei and Maban implementing multisectoral programmes; the proposed project will be integrated to other ongoing projects to enable maximization of impacts. SCI will prioritise the following locations in Abyei – Mading Achueng , Awuolnhom payams and in Maban – Banashowa, Khor El Amer, Jinmagda and Bunj Payams.

2. Project activities

12,000 vulnerable men, women, boys and girls from acutely food insecure households in Abyei – Mading Achueng , Awuolnhom payams and in Maban – Banashowa, Khor El Amer, Jinmagda and Bunj Payams will be provided lifesaving food assistance using CVA modality, enabling them to better meet their food and nutritional needs, reducing mortality and illness as result of food shortages. The will be supported to sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock. The following cluster objetivess and activities will be implemented;Cluster Objective 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5). This will be achieved through improving access to lifesaving food using unconditional cash assistance and conditional cash transfer to households capable of participating in rehabilitation of priority productive assets selected by the targeted communities. Through these participating households will be able to earn some incomes to enable them to initiate some livelihoods and meet basic household needs. A combination of this will help reduce vulnerability to food insecurity and malnutrition for children and their families. The Cash for food assistance will be complemented with key messaging on cross cutting themes; gender, protection, nutrition, hygiene, sustainable management of natural resources including strengthening of the early warning systems to meet the immediate needs and enable them to be resilient to shocks. Activities 1.1 Distribute multipurpose cash (MPC) to the most vulnerable households in IPC4+ locations where markets are functional. 1.2 Sensitization of beneficiaries on MPCA and other cross cutting themes; gender, protection, nutrition, hygiene, sustainable management of natural resources. FSL Cluster Objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. This will be achieved through provision of agricultural and agricultural and livelihood kits coupled with training on climate smart agriculture, kitchen and nutrition demonstration gardens inducting food preservation and nutrition sensitive food cooking demonstration. This will enable households to meet their food needs and other basic needs. Activities 2.1 Distribution of dry season agricultural kits2.2 Distribution of main season agricultural kits2.3 Support to fisheries2.4 Establish/Re-establish kitchen Garden sites at Nutrition Facilities/School/Household2.5 Livestock Treatment support2.6 Livestock Vaccination support2.7 Training on Agricultural practices2.8 Training on livestock management practices2.9 Training of community nutrition volunteers and mother to mother support groups on nutrition education and SBCC.
