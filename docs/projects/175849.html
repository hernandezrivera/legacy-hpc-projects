Project Summary

SPEDP is proposing the provision of life-saving health services to address acute health needs of 49,065 IDPs and returnees (15,226 girls, 13,400 boys, 11,329 women, 8,500 men, 450 older person and 160 persons with disability) in need in Yei, Morobo, Kajo-keji and Terekeka County. The project will deliver a comprehensive package of essential primary healthcare services aimed at treating, detecting, responding to and preventing communicable diseases to increase resilience among the internally displaced persons and returnees.
Specific objectives are to 1) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities 2) Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems 3) Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. To achieve these objectives, SPEDP will run four community-based mobile primary healthcare units (PHCUs) and two static primary healthcare centers (PHCCs) in Kajo-keji, Morobo, Yei and Terekeka County for a period of twelve (12) months. The services will increase access to and utilization of essential health services to reduce mortality, morbidity and suffering related to the burden of communicable diseases in IDPs and returnees settlements. They will promote safety, dignity and equitable access to timely and quality health services through the treatment of epidemic-prone diseases; immunization of children and pregnant women; provision of basic emergency obstetric care (BEmONC) and improved referral pathways. In addition, SPEDP will use an integrated disease surveillance system to monitor, detect and respond to epidemics including Ebola virus disease (EVD), Covid-19 pandemic and other epidemics. The project will streamline mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) by strengthening referral pathways to manage the victims (survivors) of sexual gender-based violence (SGBV). PHCCs will provide consultations, antenatal and postnatal services and facility-based deliveries assisted by skilled birth attendants to reduce excessive morbidity and mortality among the target population. Furthermore, mobile teams will be deployed to scale up access to and utilization of essentials healthcare services to women, children and people living with disabilities in all locations including hard-to-reach areas. Outreach immunization campaigns will be conducted in communities and mobile facilities, supported with social mobilization services to sensitize the beneficiaries, improve family healthcare practices and health-seeking behaviors. Similarly, information, education and communication (IEC) materials will be employed for messaging to increase and sustain community demand for healthcare services among the population in need. More importantly, appropriate and approved Covid-19 guidelines will be employed at the health facility and community levels to mitigate the negative impact associated with the Covid-19 pandemic.
In compliance with the National and International Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 and Health Cluster approach, SPEDP is employing integrated response with the Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihood, WASH and Protection to ensure protection, inclusion and gender-mainstreaming of services for the host community, IDP and refugee returnees in Yei, Morobo, Kajo-keji and Terekeka County. The developed monitoring and evaluation framework will be implemented to monitor the project performance by collecting data and share reports throughout the project cycle. Surveys, periodic assessments including feedback mechanisms and sharing of findings will be established. SPEDP will implement this project at a cost of USD 650,000 (USD 13 per beneficiary).

1 - Needs

Following intensified violent confrontation among the armed groups, and localized inter-communal violence in Central Equatoria, there has been increased influx of IDPs and returnees in addition to the host population. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) and refugee returnees are spontaneously settling in IDP-like camps in Yei, Morobo, Kajo-keji and Terekeka County . In January 2021, SPEDP and humanitarian partners conducted needs assessment and documented 24,000 IDPs in Korijo IDP camp, Kerwa, Gorbeleng, Tululu and Ajio villages of Kajo-keji County. In addition, Rodo, Hudaji, Kansuk (Nyepo payam), Likamerok (Lire) and sokare (Liwolo) villages have also received 13,909 returnees (6,272 children, 6,154 women of which 1,700 are breastfeeding women, and 1,483 men). Another population of 1,987 displaced households are living in spontaneous settlements in the villages of Pure, Logo, Mapu, Goloko, Kangapo 2 and Merewa, Liwolo Payam. Displaced communities in Sunyu, Merewa and Nyepo Payam are highly vulnerable to epidemic disease outbreaks due to low immunization coverage, weak health system, poor hygiene and sanitation. Routine immunization coverage among these IDPs and returnees is very low (Penta3 43%, measles 42% and oral polio 20%). Immunization campaigns have been interrupted by Covid-19 restricted movements in Morobo, Yei and Terekeka County. In addition, cases of malaria, acute respiratory infections (ARIs) are rising among IDPs. Yet, recent IDPs and new returnees have no access to basic primary health care, clean water, sanitation services and are desperately in need of life-saving health services; access to clean water, sanitation, mental and psycho-social support services. Only 4% in these IDP settlements have access to communal or shared latrines.Health needs among the vulnerable population in Yei, Morob, Kajokeji and Terekeka County are at severity IPC Phase 4 higher than IPC Phase 3 in 2020. Approximately 12% of IDPs and 13% of returnees live in settlements with no access to health care services. Health infrastructure is poor and no financial support for rehabilitation. Most of the health facilities in the vicinity are destroyed, looted and/or abandoned. Some mobile facilities operated by humanitarian partners are not adequate and located at more than hour walk away from the IDP settlements; not accessible to the most vulnerable population especially the elderly and people with disabilities. Only 19% of births are assisted by health workers. Consequently, morbidity and case fatality rates of communicable diseases are very high among the affected population. Three in four child death are due to preventable communicable diseases. This situation is promoting increased burden of diseases and these counties are prioritized by SSHF 2021.Furthermore, a persistent surge of armed conflict at sub-national level is exponentially increasing cases of sexual abuse, rape and gender-based assaults against women and girls in IDP settlements. About 74% of IDPs and returnees are reportedly not feeling safe in their current settlements. Yei County is a hotspot of rape and sexual slavery . As women and girls collect firewoods in bushes, they are subjected to a high risk of rape and other forms of gender-based violence (GBV). Out of all incidents of GBV reported in the area, 97% are women and girls. Seven cases of rape (2 girls and 5 women in July 2020) were reported in Jalimo, Kajo-keji County. In early February 2021, ten incidents of GBV were documented in Kerwa, Ajio and Korijo. One case of child marriage was also registered too. These victims of GBV are suffering from high level of psychological distress, low self-esteemed but no access to medical and mental psycho-social support services. Covid-19 pandemic and its associated impact, have worsened mental health situation of the affected population and constrained the availability of already severed mental health services in counties. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP staffs are aware, trained and adhere to the UN Secretary-General's Bulletin on Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13) This project staffs will strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising sessions around PSEA with women, girls, boys and men in communities of concern as well as with other stakeholders and design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities. Beneficiaries will be made aware of their rights and entitlements through community meetings, focus group meetings and information sheets distribution to beneficiaries. In particular, SPEDP will identify and support feedback channels that communities prefer while ensuring that feedback mechanisms are sensitive to age, gender and diversity (AGD) and accessible to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities and others at high risk. Besides, SPEDP will also coordinate complaint collection and responses with other partners within these proposed project locations and establish clear tasks and duties (who will do what, where, and how) since it is critical to put in place an effective interagency complaint mechanism that permits and encourages individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially, and safely.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Communities will be encouraged to identify different means and methods/channels of communicating information on the project. Where relevant and appropriate platforms such as Whatsapp and SMS will be used. People with low literacy will be consulted to suggest the appropriate means of communicating feedback. Women and girls will be consulted to identify gender-responsive content and channels. They will be guided to outline strategies for soliciting regular feedback from communities (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries). This will be done through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by the project officer. Information obtained will be communicated to the project management to adopt it and incorporated into the implementation strategies and work plans accordingly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life of the project. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase and will be the primary responsibility of the Project Management Committee to implement, with support from project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback information will be received, adopted by the Project Management Team, and incorporated as part of adaptive programming to strengthen implementation strategies and work plans. Information on the action(s) taken regarding the beneficiaries' feedback, will be communicated back to communities by the PMC through scheduled consultation meetings, ensuring ongoing feedback mechanism loop/communication. The project team will manage all complaints and recommendations from the communities and provide feedback immediately. The project team will use project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to complaints.

Ongoing orientation for new and existing staff and affiliates will be completed. SPEDP adheres to a zero tolerance policy of SEA. 8.9. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area - In order to mitigate local disputes around the land ownership following clearance operations, SPEDP will liaise with local authorities, traditional and religious leaders even before the teams start operating. A handover certiﬁcate will be issued to local leaders after the land being cleared and a community meeting will be called to ensure transparency and accountability. Additionally, SPEDP will closely monitor the socio-economic impact of its work by utilizing its own methodologies and dedicated M&E mechanism to ensure that targeted local communities remain at their locations and use the cleared land for agricultural or hunting purposes in an eﬃcient way. This will help to build their resilience and pave the way for self-suﬃciency at community and household levels. It will also reduce the level of vulnerability towards humanitarian aid. 8.10. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in the project target area - Risk will be mitigated by prioritizing accreditation of EORE Teams in a timely manner (refresher training and ongoing capacity development). EORE Teams are equipped with mobile phones of key focal points at UNMAS and NMSS should immediate assistance be required. Further, EORE Teams carry identifying materials for themselves and communities to be able to mark any UXOs discovered.
