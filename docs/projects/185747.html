Project Summary

Rohingya camp: UNICEF will continue implementing components of community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) through its 26 integrated nutrition facilities (INFs) in the 19 camps. The project will combine facility, community and household-based approaches to facilitate improved access for the most at-risk groups. At the INF level, UNICEF and partners will target to admit 7,000 children with SAM for treatment. All nutrition centers will be equipped with disability friendly facilities including access during monsoon. Caregivers of children under 2 will be screened to identify needs for breastfeeding support and will be provided IYCF counselling. The project will strive to achieve optimal IYCF practices by involving men and other family members taking an active role in infant feeding and family activities and influence women on decision making. All staff and volunteer will be trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, and PSEA awareness messages will be displayed in the nutrition facilities. Furthermore, a comprehensive complaint and feedback handling mechanism will be established, and a service referral linkage will be established to the protection agency. Monthly Growth Monitoring and Promotion sessions will be conducted focusing on 0-23 months children along with WFP implemented preventive Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme where children 24-59 months are being targetted for GMP. Considering the COVID-19 situation, all activities will be adapted ensuring protection of beneficiaries and service providers from COVID-19. All community mobilization activities will be conducted through community nutrition volunteers. These volunteers will do follow up visits with an emphasis on absentees, defaulters and non-responders from treatment. Strategies for behaviour change communication will include mother-to-mother support groups, peer groups for adolescent girls as well as courtyard sessions targeting community leaders, male caregivers and other stakeholders. Pregnant and lactating women and adolescents will be given iron folic acid to prevent anaemia. Early childhood development services will be mainstreamed as part of essential basic services of health and nutrition since early childhood development is seen as a holistic approach including protection, stimulation, health and nutrition. In addition to that, attitude of parents, male caregivers, service providers and community people on nurturing care will be promoted as well. Existing contingency plan will be revised according to the need, and partners will be trained to tackle any unanticipated weather incidents including fire outbreak.
Host Community: To ensure quality nutrition services at facility-level, upazilla nutrition supervisors and nutrition counsellors will be deployed in the union health and family welfare centres, and upazilla health complexes. Frontline health and nutrition service providers will be trained on a comprehensive package of nutrition and essential nutrition supplies will be procured and distributed to the targeted facilities. The services in the host community will involve community engagement and mobilization by the multipurpose health volunteers (MHVs). MHVs will be trained on comprehensive package of nutrition who will mobilize Community Groups and Community Support group members to strengthened community level nutrition services. The community engagement activities will mainly focus on practical demonstrations for mothers and caregivers on improving the quality of complementary foods through cooking demonstrations and mukhe bhaat ceremonies. Furthermore, UNICEF will collaborate with department of agricultural extension and the department of livestock services and local NGOs to build the capacities of beneficiary households on sustainable homestead gardening and backyard poultry rearing to improve household level food security.

1 - Identified Needs

Refugee Camp: A SMART survey conducted in December 2020 showed the level of acute malnutrition situation remained critical at 11.3 per cent (in terms of GAM), having slightly increased from 10.9 per cent since the previous SMART survey in October 2019. Survey results further reveal malnutrition aggravating factors such poor dietary diversity and anemia continue to persist among young children. As of 2019, more than a third of children were not exclusively breastfed and only 26.4 per cent of children aged 6-23 months were receiving a minimum acceptable diet. Inappropriate IYCF-E practices indicating a serious concern among the Rohingya population. The pandemic has exacerbated pressures on local food pantries, leading to a potential elevation in the food insecurity condition. The 2020 Refugee Emergency Vulnerable Assessment (REVA 4) revealed the prevalence of population below the accepted food consumption increased from 42 per cent in 2019 to 50 per cent in 2020. Considering that it is increasingly important to scale-up and strengthen preventive services while continuing acute malnutrition treatment. Besides, strengthening effectiveness and quality of service provision is also a priority, requiring hard and valid evidence to be used for a gradual adjustment to the programme in a changing environment. Host Community: A February 2021 SMART survey in Teknaf and Ukhia identified a GAM prevalence of 9.3 per cent with approximately 8,879 children under five suffer from acute malnutrition. The same survey estimated the rate of SAM to be 0.7 per cent in both upazilas, with 2,229 children are affected, is a serious concern. Level of stunting (20.7 per cent in Ukhia and 25.8 per cent in Teknaf) indicates a poor socioeconomic conditions and possible early exposure to illnesses. The same survey revealed 73.5 and 60.0 per cent of mothers respectively in Ukhia and Teknaf exclusively breastfed their children which is an improvement compared to around 50 per cent EBF rate in 2018. Survey findings indicated a minimum acceptable diet of 36.8 per cent in Ukhia and 19.3 per cent in Teknaf, very low in the Teknaf upazila, as did the score for minimum dietary diversity which is 41.7 and 25.2 per cent respectively in Ukhia and Teknaf. The pandemic has worsened the food security situation: the 2020 REVA saw acceptable food consumption decrease from 79 to 67 per cent both in Ukhia and Teknaf. In the host community, nutrition services are delivered through the health care system: community clinics, Upazilla Health Complexes and home visits by the community nutrition workers. COVID-19 affected the accessibility and availability of nutrition services due to lockdowns. Corresponding to that improving the quality of nutrition services through strengthening health system is a priority. At community level creating awareness on optimal IYCF practices will require community engagement activities therefore develop caregivers and mother’s capacity as they will ‘learn by doing’.
