Project Summary

The project duration will be 12 months from 1st January 2022 to 31st December 2022. It will contribute to the Education Cluster objectives ECLEDU/CO1, CLEDU/CO2, and CLEDU/CO3 to improve access to safe and protective quality education. The geographical locations of the project are aligned to the Education Cluster priorities 1 and 2 counties.
The project will focus on provision of safe and protective quality education services to emergency-affected boys and girls aged 3-17 years in South Sudan. At least 342,400 children, adolescents and youth (40% girls) aged 3 - 17 years will have access to quality education services. The project will establish 160 temporary learning spaces; rehabilitate 200 temporary learning spaces; establish WASH facilities for 40,000 children (40% girls); rehabilitate WASH facilities for 80,000 children (40% girls); train 3,000 teachers (30% females) on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies; train 3,000 community education committees/PTA members and local education officials (30% females) on school management, PSS and DRR; and organize 200 school campaigns for back-to-learning.
Through the project, a total of 346,368 emergency affected people will be targeted. This target comprises boys, girls, adolescents, youth, teachers, and PTA members. Ensuring access to safe and protective quality education services for all children (boys and girls), particularly the most vulnerable, those affected by emergencies including children with disabilities is essential. Without the provision of education services, the future of children (boys and girls) in South Sudan is at stake.

1 - Needs

Myriad factors including conflict, displacement, floods, COVID-19 pandemic, poverty, and insecurity worsened the dire education situation in South Sudan, resulting to poor educational outcomes and high number of out-of-school children. UNICEF South Sudan (2021): Getting girls back to the classroom after COVID-19 school closures cited in the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2022) reports that prior to COVID-19 school closures, about 2.2 million children were already out of school. Due to the high poverty levels, coupled with instability and long distances to school, some parents prefer not to send their children to school but rather keep them at home to help with economic activities and domestic chores. The education crisis increases the risk that more out-of-school boys will be recruited by armed actors while girls face an increased risk of early marriage. The Ministry of General Education and Instruction (2019), Preliminary report on National catchment mapping for out of school children in South Sudan estimates 2.8 million, or over 70 per cent children aged 6 -17 are out-of-school in South Sudan, putting at risk their futures and the future of the country . According to SSAMS (November 2021), the gross enrolment decreased by roughly 7% between 2020 (2,727,209) and 2021 (2,523,204). Therefor, the proportion of out-of-school children in South Sudan is likely to remain high. These figures are even higher in pastoralist communities, as traditional schools are not effective in reaching out to them since the communities are often on constant move with their cattle across the country. The trend in female enrolment and drop out is particularly disconcerting with the Gender Parity Index (GPI) going from 0.86 in pre-primary to 0.75 at primary and, ultimately, 0.54 at secondary level. In the strict patriarchal society of South Sudan, the destiny of the girl-child is often determined from birth with the boy-child as the preferred heir to preserve family lineage and pride. Girls are raised to accommodate their male family members; cooking, cleaning and serving in preparation for their role as wives and mothers. This early childhood socialization process along with other societal, environmental and economic challenges increases the risk of girls being victims of sexual and gender-based violence both within and outside of school settings, pregnancy, early and forced marriages. Thus, in turn leading to persistent obstacles affecting girls’ education opportunities and learning experiences as they transition from adolescence to adulthood. According to education statistics, only 33 per cent of all girls in the country attend primary school, with a high dropout rate of 13 per cent. These trends have led to a low rate of female literacy and inhibit girls and women from achieving their full potential as equal members of society. The Education Cluster (2021) South Sudan Education Needs Assessment cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021 . School functionality has also been severely impacted by flooding and county education officials reported over 190 schools closed due to flooding. The quality of education has been affected by poor service conditions for teachers; limited opportunities for professional development, very low and delayed salaries . The inadequate learning environment and shortage of basic teaching and learning materials further hinder teachers from providing quality education. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (202, Children with Disabilities (CwDs) face numerous challenges in accessing quality education. In the Education Needs Assessment, most schools reported having no mobility or assistive devices. Over half of schools also report their latrines are inaccessible for CwDs and teachers are not trained to foster or deliver inclusive education and school communities are not sensitized on the need to create enabling environments for CwDs.

2 - Project activities

To address the main educational barriers and priorities described in the needs analysis, the project will carry out the following activities:1. Enroll children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) in Pre-primary, Primary and secondary schools/learning centers.2. Establish temporary learning spaces and classrooms in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019.3. Rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms.4. Establish gender segregated WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls).5. Rehabilitate gender segregated WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls).6. Provide emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials.7. Provide teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education.8. Train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children.9. Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change.10. Organize school campaigns on back-to-learning.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Information from beneficiaries' feedback will be analyzed and and used for making strategic decisions especially during site selection for temporary learning spaces, supplies distribution planning, meeting the needs of vulnerable groups, and adapting project assistance.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from the most vulnerable members of the communities will be collected through community structures such as the Parent-Teachers Associations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be provided to beneficiaries through posters, social mobilization sessions, public announcements, radio talk shows, open meetings, PTAs, and through local authorities. Community mobilizers will also play a crucial in information sharing with the beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will ensure that the affected communities have access to information and are aware of the feedback and/or complaint mechanism. During feedback collection sessions, UNICEF will ensure opinions of the vulnerable groups are heard and appropriate actions to address their needs clearly communicated. Other feedback mechanisms will also be availed to girls and boys with special needs.
