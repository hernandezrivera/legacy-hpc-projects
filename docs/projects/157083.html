Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to improve access to quality education for 1000 refugee children in three schools in Jouri camp.
Specific project objectives are:
1. To improve learning environment through construction/rehabilitation of classrooms, WASH in schools (rehabilitation/construction of latrines and water points; and hygiene promotion activities through Sanitation Health Clubs) and capacity building of School Management Committees;
2. To increase enrolment and retention of school-age refugee children through enrolment campaigns and provision of retention support (school fees, textbooks, uniforms and female hygiene kits) for children.
3. To improve learning outcomes through re-training and provision of incentives for teachers, and provision of teaching and learning aids material (chalk, pen, exercise books and instruction charts) for target schools.
Special focus will be directed to inclusion of girls and women who are disproportionately at risk (due to their refugee status), thus vulnerable to early marriage, child labour and exploitation, and SGBV

Needs

Just 41 per cent of school-aged South Sudanese refugee children are enrolled in school. School drop-out is high, which compounds refugee children’s vulnerability to exploitation, abuse and other protection issues. The absorption capacity of public schools is limited, with a lack of classrooms and WASH facilities, trained teachers, textbooks, school supplies and seating. Refugees are required to pay school fees at the same rate as national residents; however, many families are unable to sustain this due to a lack of access to income. There is a need for school construction and rehabilitation, provision of school uniforms and broader retention support to help refugee families keep their children in school. Limited educational opportunities increase children and youth’s vulnerability to early marriage, pregnancy, substance abuse and other negative coping mechanisms. In the participatory assessments refugee communities cited prevalence of child labour, and early marriage. Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) are the most vulnerable. Moreover, there are limited recreational and social programming targeting refugee children and adolescents, which has a serious negative impact on their development and psychosocial well-being.A UNHCR Participatory Needs Assessment (December 2018) highlights the following education needs in the 9 camps in White Nile: - Syllabus are not completed in time.- High school dropouts (especially girls, who end up being married off or exploited through various forms of child abuse) are high due to financial constraints. Majority of girls at puberty stage stay away from schools when experiencing their monthly periods.- Widespread shortage of school materials such as school uniforms, exercise books and text books. - Teachers are absent from the schools most of the time.- Poor sanitation enviroment and limited number of latrines in the refugees schools.- Lack of school recreational activities.- Insufficient sources of safe and clean drinking water for the schools.- Refugees are not able to provide breakfast meal for their children who attend schools ,thus poor class attendance and participation- The few classrooms are in dilapidated conditions and are congested.

Activities

Key project activities will include: 1. Construction of six and rehabilitation of nine classrooms for three primary schools.2. Construction of 18 latrines for three schools.3. Construction three water supply systems in three schools.4. Conducting of hygiene promotion activities by strengthening the Sanitation and Health Clubs, which will conduct solid waste management campaigns and awareness raising activities through performance arts.5. Capacity strengthening of School Management Committees through trainings and networking activities.6. Conduct periodic enrolment campaigns aligned with school opening dates7. Provide retention support (school fees, textbooks, uniforms, hygiene kits for girls) for children.8. Provide training/re-training for teachers on the curriculum9. Provision of incentives for teachers in three schools.10. Provision of learning and teaching aids material (chalk, pen, exercise books and instruction charts) to the target schools.
