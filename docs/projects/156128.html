Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Syria has resulted in loss of productive capacity of the farmers. Alseeraj intends to restore this capacity through training and input support. Through this intervention, Alseeraj targets to improve the food security situation of the target population by at least three months.
Beneficiaries will be provided with seeds, tools for farming and training to ensure that this is achieved.
Alseeraj will procurement the inputs from the local market to activate the local vendors.
Targeted beneficiaries will be organized into Vegetable Farmer Groups.
These households will be contacted and prioritized for the groups.
This is to enhance the sustainability of nutrition interventions.
It is important that once treated and cured, children who were once malnourished remain healthy. By providing sustainable access to nutritious food through vegetable production this problem will be addressed.
Beneficiaries caring for this malnourished and other children already have a burden of increasing the difficulty of access to healthy food for their children.
Most caretakers are women, and they care for their children either through harvesting wild foods and vegetables which exposes them to more danger of sexual abuse, or through general food distribution, which is irregular and unsustainable.
Including these caretakers in the farming, therefore, reduces their burden of walking long distances to get the wild food and vegetables, also increases food availability at the household level in a more sustainable manner.

Needs

The crisis in Syria continues to have a profound impact on people across the country. Civilian infrastructure lies destroyed or in disrepair, limiting access to shelter, basic services and livelihood opportunities. Women are exposed to many risks, including limited access to reproductive health and GBV services. There are increased patterns and number of people with disabilities. Close to eight years of crisis have triggered immediate suffering and at the same time severely undermined the ability of communities to recover.•• The proportion of people who are food insecure at the national level is 5 percent less compared to the 2017 HNO. An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity.From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend over 65 percent of their expenditures on food, 57.2 percent of food-insecure households reported to have outstanding debt, and 65 percent of food-insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency harmful coping strategies.(HNO 2019).Almost 40 per cent of the surveyed households report to be IDPs or returnees, 74.5 per cent of which reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Among them 478,282 displaced persons living in last resort camps are of particular concern as they have limited access to diversified diet or income opportunities.(HNO 2019).With an 83 - 89 per cent of the population living below poverty lines, and with a cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 and 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to the agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable.125 126 A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 per cent of an unskilled labourer’s monthly salary and 50-80 per cent of a public service employee’s monthly salary, thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria.

Implementation of activities

1- During the first 15 days we will start by contacting the LC to provide us with a raw data about the PNF's, then by categorization method we will select the targeted PNF's, and then ask the LC to approve the PNF's lists, after that our team will conduct a fields trip to the PNF's to insure the data provided by the LC.2 - In the next month we will start the procurement process for the agriculture kits and conduct vocational training ( agriculture training ) for the PNF's.3 - It will take 15 days to distribute the kits for all PNF's.4 - During the cultivation of corps our team ( M&E and Field officers ) will conduct field trips to monitor the use the kits and give any consulting needed for the PNF's.5 - Before the beginning of the harvest we will conduct a marketing training to help the PNF's marketing their goods.6 - During the harvest our team will conduct field trip to monitor the process and the quantity produced by each PNF's

Protection Risk Analysis

This project will partner with local vendors who manages the Agricultural supplies pipeline to secure vegetable kits to be distributed to the beneficiaries. similarly, the project will collaborate with the logistics cluster to have inputs delivered in their lands.this will ensure that inputs are made available for use at the appropriate time, the vegetable kits need to be available before the rainy season. farmers kits beneficiaries need to use the materials provided to make nets of pipe water and be ready to start the agriculture process as at the start of the project.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Livelihood inputs will be provided in a safe and timely manner to food insecure and vulnerable households to encourage/ support recovery and build resilience in livelihood security.• Security situation will remain normal to enable communities access land for farming. • Rains remain normal for cropping. Droughts or floods could negatively impact on the project.
