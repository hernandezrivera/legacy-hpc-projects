Project Summary

The project will provide essential and life-saving integrated health assistance to vulnerable communities in Awdal, Bay, Bakool, Banadir, Bari, Galgaduud, Hiraan, Lower Juba, Mudug, Nugaal, Sanaag, Sool, Toghdeer, Woqooyi Galbeed and aims to reach a total of 455,658 IDPs and host communities contributing to 16% of the cluster target in the targeted geographies. This will be achieved by providing both fixed and mobile health services supporting 4 regional hospitals, 11 Referral Health Centers, 103 Primary Health Centers/Health Centers/MCHs, 140 Primary Health Units as well as 101 mobile clinic sites.
The project will seek to contribute to a 5% reduction in excess morbidity and mortality rates from crisis-driven diseases and outbreaks among target vulnerable communities by end of 2021 while scaling up provision of regular health services and providing safe, dignified and meaningful access life-saving assistance (provided directly or through referral) with sensitivity to age, gender and disability status.
This will be achieved by supporting the Somalia governments and communities to be able to delivery essential package of health free services to communities with acute humanitarian needs using developments programs as a platform for immediate responses. The proposed project has also been designed with full awareness of the prevailing gender norms and deeply rooted gender inequalities in Somalia that limit women and girls from accessing basic services such as health and nutrition, and expose them to a higher risk of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV). In order to address these challenges, Save the Children will identify and work to remove the physical, cognitive and social barriers by collaborating closely with the communities and particularly the influencers and gatekeepers. We will also address the intersectionality of vulnerability factors, for example women with disabilities or from minor clans who are likely to face multiple levels of discrimination. Save the Children will also include appropriate child safeguarding measures at every phase and within every programming activity.

Project Activities

Save the Children proposes a multi-sectoral, integrated health, nutrition and WASH response to respond to vulnerable, IDPs, and marginalized communities in Somalia. Save the Children will work with MOH to improve access to quality lifesaving health services in static health and outreach sites to deliver an integrated package of promotive, preventive and curative health services. SC will build the local capacity by providing training and technical support to local government, health facility staff, the Community Health volunteers, representatives of local communities to raise awareness, promote nutrition, and strengthen linkages between the community and health facilities. In addition, SC will support to increase awareness and sensitization on COVID-19 amongst vulnerable populations in target areas using the RCCE strategy. Mitigation Measures

In order to address protection concerns, Save the Children will undertake the following; Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, straybullets, crossfire or violence. Install lights outside and on the access route to the facilities. Ensure that the health services are respectful and inclusive of cultural and religious practice and cultural and gender sensitivities, we will also consider separate waiting areas for males and females Save the Children will work to have a proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, we may consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women will be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available. Employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children. Be aware of how power dynamics between a health staff and the patient can affect the patient’sresponse and attitude towards the staff/organization and service provided. Save the Children will ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private: Separate examination rooms from public spaces or the waiting area (eg. Establish dry walls or at leasta curtain) Establish an information sharing protocol for survivors of abuse that avoids exposing her/him tofurther trauma and that all efforts are made to ensure her/his confidentiality and wishesconcerning the care provided. Do not share information on the identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g.names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). Keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use - applyeffective data protection measures. Avoid community tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. Consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether healthinterventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. To prevent improper use of medical supplies, ensure proper storage and that labels and instructions on their usage is in a language or pictorial form that is understood by users. Design and clearly mark separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females and account forpersons with disabilities and children. In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidentsconcerning hospitals to advocate against attacks on hospitals and military use of hospitals
