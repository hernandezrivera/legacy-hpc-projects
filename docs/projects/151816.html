Project Summary

SUMMARY: By providing a package of low-intensity mental health interventions and psychological support services (MHPSS), WHO Somalia will begin to address the acute mental health needs of the most vulnerable populations affected by violence, displacement, natural disasters, and disease outbreaks in Somalia –internally displaced persons (IDPs) and frontline health workers. WHO will train local health personnel who work with crisis-affected populations using curricula for three well-established and proven effective WHO models: Psychological First Aid (PFA), Program Management Plus (PM+), and Thinking Healthy. Over the course of 12 months, WHO will also supply essential psychiatric medicines to key health facilities --those known as 'sentinel sites'-- located in the most high risk areas covered by the Early Warning and Response Network (EWARN) for epidemic-prone diseases.
MENTAL HEALTH RAPID NEEDS ASSESSMENT: WHO will carry out a rapid assessment of available MHPSS services at the EWARN sentinel sites and MHPSS training needs and capacity gaps among community health workers (CHWs) working in the catchment areas of these facilities. WHO staff conducting the assessment will also look at the supply and administration of psychiatric medicines at pharmacies in crisis-affected areas to identify shortages and training needs.
PFA TRAINING FOR FRONTLINE HEALTH WORKERS: WHO Somalia staff will train frontline health workers in PFA, the most basic psychological intervention support by WHO worldwide. The trainee pool will be composed of members of the Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), members of the Integrated Emergency Response Teams (IERTs), CHWs, and emergency medical personnel in urban Mogadishu. These are the groups of frontline health workers that work most closely with crisis-affected populations as well as those most likely to need to administer PFA to their own colleagues due to the intense stress of their working environments.
PM+ TRAINING FOR IERTS, CHWS, AND LOCAL NGOS: WHO Somalia staff will train all members of the IERTs, CHWs, and staff from local NGOs working with crisis-affected populations (IDPs, survivors of conflict-related violence and SGBV, and communities affected by disease outbreaks) in the PM+ model, a low-intensity psychological intervention for adults impaired by distress in communities who are exposed to adversity due to conflict and natural disasters.
SUPPORTING THE MENTAL HEALTH OF CRISIS-AFFECTED MOTHERS OF YOUNG CHILDREN: WHO Staff will train female CHWs in regions in an adapted version of WHO’s Thinking Healthy model. Thinking Healthy is targeted intervention by CHWs for new mothers with and at risk for adverse mental health outcomes. This low-intensity model has proven effective in reducing common symptoms of depression and increasing women’s functional capacity to care for themselves and their infants under difficult circumstances. WHO Somalia will adapt the curriculum to focus more heavily on the challenges faced by mothers caring for young children (not just infants) in emergency settings, especially in the context of displacement.
SUPPORT FOR EMERGENCY PSYCHOPHARMACOLOGY: WHO Somalia will provide a 12 month supply of essential psychiatric medicines to all health facilities that currently act as EWARN sentinel sites. WHO Somalia staff will conduct refresher training sessions for the staff psychiatrist, the facility pharmacists, and other responsible and qualified personnel in the administration and management of these medicines. WHO will also translate treatment guidelines into the Somali language and provide these to each facility in the network.

Activities

MENTAL HEALTH RAPID NEEDS ASSESSMENT: WHO staff will conduct field missions to the selected health facilities. During these missions, they will interview facility personnel, patients, and community members, conduct surveys, and assess the physical pharmacy space, equipment, and inventory of psychiatric medicines, at each health facility. PFA TRAINING FOR FRONTLINE HEALTH WORKERS: WHO Somalia staff will organize the PFA training workshops to take place in multiple regions, and each training workshop will cover the WHO PFA training curriculum. PM+ TRAINING FOR COMMUNITIES IN CRISIS: WHO Somalia staff will organize the training workshops to take place in multiple regions for IERTs, CHWs, and local NGOs, and each training workshop will cover the full WHO PM+ training curriculum. THINKING HEALTHY IN AN EMERGENCY: WHO staff will conduct these training sessions in multiple regions. Each training session will cover the Thinking Healthy curriculum with an extra day of group discussions and role-playing exercises around providing MHPSS to mothers in Somalia’s complex emergency. SUPPORT FOR EMERGENCY PSYCHOPHARMACOLOGY: Activities under this activity cluster will include procurement and delivery of essential psychiatric medicines, development of treatment guidelines in the Somali language, and the delivery of refresher training sessions for the responsible and qualified clinical/pharmacy personnel in each supported sentinel site.
