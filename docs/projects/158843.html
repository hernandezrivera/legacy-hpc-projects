Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect food production capacities and income sources in locations that are most at risk due to the chronic electricity deficit in the Gaza Strip. The rationale of this project is to target areas most impacted by the increasing electricity deficit in order to protect current levels of food and agricultural production, livelihoods and food security by providing vulnerable agricultural producers in the Gaza Strip with a consistent, clean and renewable source of energy to guarantee sufficient electricity for essential food production. This intervention responds to the first need under the Agri Livelihoods section in the Food Security Sector Response Plan, which indicates the need for interventions that prioritize responses to the electricity shortage in the Gaza Strip. The intervention falls under Strategic Objective 3 of the HRP 2018-2022 as it invests in solar energy sources to ensure that targeted farmers have a consistent electricity supply as and when required in order to cope with frequent energy cuts as part of the protracted crisis. As an emergency intervention, this project aims to minimize damages and losses to domestic crop production in the current season and to enable normal levels of cultivation in subsequent seasons. In doing so, this project will protect agriculture based livelihoods, food security and domestic food production that are at risk given the chronic electricity deficit and will enable normal levels of cultivation if electricity supply to the Gaza Strip is further reduced.

1 - Needs

Gaza’s farmers have been impacted considerably by the ongoing chronic electricity deficit, particularly in the southeast of the Gaza Strip where a significant proportion of local agricultural produce is sourced. Given the need for approximately 10 hours of electricity per day for the irrigation of crops, farmers had barely been managing to sustain agricultural production on eight hours of electricity per day. With electricity supply down to a mere three to four hours per day and the possibility of further reductions , Gaza’s farmers are faced with the choice of either substantially reducing the scale of agricultural production or investing in costly diesel generators with which to pump the required amounts of irrigation water. According to Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) estimates, the cost of one cubic metre of irrigation water has increased by up to 40 percent since Gaza’s sole power plant ceased operations in April 2017, before resuming at very limited capacity, with fears that the cost of irrigation water will increase further should the supply of electricity decrease further. Moreover, according to the MoA, up to 60 percent of agricultural land across the Gaza Strip remains at risk of being set aside due to the lack of electricity with which to pump water for irrigation. As of September 2019, energy demand in the Gaza Strip has exceeded 500 MW while supply has stagnated at 200 MW. This imbalance between available supply and demand has resulted in acute energy shortages leaving residents with a range of 5 and 15 hours of energy access per day. Current energy is partially supplied by Israel on average of 120 MW, while domestic energy by the Gaza Power Plant (GPP) generates approximately 70 MW, supported by the ongoing donor-supported provision of fuel aid. The overall impact of the increasing electricity deficit, which currently remains at more than half the current need, in terms of the extent of damages and losses to Gaza’s agriculture sector, has the potential not only to reverse the gains made by reconstruction and recovery efforts since the end of the 2014 conflict but also to further jeopardize agriculture based livelihoods, to exhaust the coping mechanisms of farming households and, consequently, lead to a sizeable reduction in local agricultural production which will in turn have a negative impact on food security levels in the Gaza Strip. Preliminary findings from a participatory assessment of solar energy needs in agriculture by FAO indicate that the private sector maintains interest and capacities to investment in narrowing the energy deficit. The contribution leveraged from this proposal will be designed in a way to also reflect and strengthen a portion of investments by the private sector related to energy.Moreover, according to the findings of a field assessment conducted by FAO in 2018 in response to Gaza’s deepening electricity crisis, farming communities in the Gaza Strip reported that input costs for planting had increased significantly as a result of the chronic electricity deficit. Increased water scarcity has further impacted the quality of crops for the summer season and has negatively affected prices in local markets as well as the competitiveness of Gazan produce in the West Bank and Israel. Women farmers are most impacted as they bear ever increasing levels of responsibility both inside and outside the home. According to the Gender Alert Report (2015) by UN Women and OCHA, women’s limited share of assets makes them increasingly vulnerable during difficult economic times. Overall, the work of rural women in the agricultural sector is under recognized and women farmers are considered to be “helpers” regardless of the level of responsibility they have for the household’s agricultural work. Data from PCBS indicate that 18.2% of economically active women work in agriculture and constitute 30% of agricultural employees in Gaza, thereby masking biases with regard to recognizing the contribution of women

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable farmers whose livelihoods are endangered. The selection of farmers is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries in risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

FAO’s approach provides a framework for addressing and integrating issues such as gender equality, protection, the inclusion of the aged and people living with disability, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse by FAO and partner staff. Throughout the process of beneficiary selection, disability and age will be a factor taken into consideration. Beneficiaries with this vulnerability will be prioritized. The project also seeks to engage with targeted stakeholders and mainstream FAO’s commitments in each phase of the project cycle. FAO maintains its commitments through regular stakeholder engagement and participation, two-way communication, transparent disclosure and a grievance mechanism.
