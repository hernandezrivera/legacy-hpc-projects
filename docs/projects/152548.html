Project Summary

Overall, the Medair ERT ES/NFI team aims to reduce morbidity and mortality, alleviate suffering and enhance dignity amongst displaced populations throughout South Sudan. It maintains the flexibility to provide emergency responses to help save lives and strengthen communities. Medair has many years of experience in providing emergency relief to the most vulnerable populations in South Sudan. Operating with a multi-sectoral approach, Medair’s Emergency Response Team has been responding health, nutrition, water & sanitation, and ES/NFI needs in different parts of the country. With its expertise in ES/NFI, the emergency response ES/NFI team provides swift relief for thousands of people across the country each year. The team is equipped to react quickly and provide assistance to the most vulnerable, and interventions can last as long as 3 months.
Medair works in close collaboration with the Shelter and NFI Cluster and other partners and is an active member in multiple coordination meetings. Activities are always coordinated through the Shelter and NFI Cluster to align response strategies with other humanitarian organizations. Activities include the distribution of non-food items and emergency shelter kits (e.g. tarpaulins, blankets, mosquito nets, and cooking kits) in order to build resilience of the affected populations.
Additionally, Medair acts as ES/NFI State Focal Point for former Central Equatoria State to facilitate the coordination of responses among different partners and ensure gaps are identified so needs across the state can be addressed adequately and in a timely manner. This role also involves capacity building of national NGOs who can then participate fully in ES/NFI activities and become effective Cluster partners.
Medair actively participates in the Needs Analysis Working Group as well as the Access Working Group.
Medair’s Emergency Response Team has worked in different areas and contexts in South Sudan. A strong security management system and years of experience in negotiations enables the teams to deploy fast to challenging settings in logistically hard to reach areas.

Needs

Despite improved security, there is still significant risk that populations will be displaced by armed groups, cattle-raiders and intercommunal fighting. Emergency Shelter and NFI assistance is still urgently needed to serve those who have fled their homes and left their belongings behind. An estimated 1.8 million people are currently displaced (UN OCHA, July 2018), and most are in need of some form of ES/NFI assistance. In particular, IDPs outside camps require basic household items, especially those who have relocated several times. In general, those who are newly displaced will have priority over protracted displaced populations. Women and children will remain particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to lack of shelter, and in some instances host community members will also need support due to pressure on their limited resources to help IDPs. Additionally, with the recent signing of the R-ARCSS peace agreement in September 2018 that is potentially ushering in more political stability, it is expected that some of the 2.47 million refugees living in neighbouring countries (UN OCHA, July 2018) might return to South Sudan in 2019. These returnees as well as returning IDPs will need NFIs to enable them to settle in new locations. Medair aims to address the needs of the most vulnerable IDPs particularly pregnant and lactating women, female-headed households, the elderly, and the disabled. In this specific context, Medair responds to recently displaced populations fleeing conflict, a major disease outbreak or malnutrition rates above emergency levels. If warranted, Medair will also support some of the most vulnerable host community members in order to build their resilience and ease community tensions. The goal is to support those in need by giving them ES/NFI items that will help them thrive without eroding their existing coping mechanisms.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

When the team deploys for an assessment, they initially meet with community leaders to inform them about the objectives of the assessment as well as the fact that the assessment does not automatically result in an intervention. The same message is consistently shared during the assessment at HH level, as well as during FGDs and other meetings held. Based on the needs found in the assessment, Medair will decide in collaboration with the cluster whether an intervention is appropriate. Local authorities and community leaders will be informed about the outcome.Typically, before a distribution occurs, mobilisers are sent out to the communities to alert the affected population about the time and date it will take place. The quantity and type of items are mostly not communicated at this stage in case of last minute changes or delivery delays or shortage of goods. On the day of the distribution, the beneficiaries are informed at the distribution site about the items and quantities per household they are entitled to receive. Pictures of items are also used to cater for illiterate beneficiaries. During the distribution, the team ensures there are demonstrations of the items from the kits to show the beneficiaries how to use them correctly. Medair actively involves the community in these demonstrations and trainings (e.g. how to use mosquito nets) and community members are identified to demonstrate the usage of items. In this way they can continue disseminating the messages in the community even after the Medair team has left, which helps to ensure that items are used appropriately. If there is an existing Care Group network Medair aims to also involve the lead mothers of the care groups to use these networks for information sharing and training on usage of the distributed items. The lead mothers are volunteers and are motivated to serve their own community, Casual labours which are needed to carry out the distribution are recruited locally.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Depending on the context and the population group, messaging will be tailored to suit the situation, especially when hygiene kits for females are distributed. This involves specific messaging and information. Additionally, if during the assessment/verification people are found to be using items incorrectly (i.e. mosquito nets used as fishing nets), the team will conduct targeted sensitisation to influence behaviour change. These trainings are done through community members to ensure that messages to the community are context specific and culturally appropriate.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All Medair staff receive regular training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.Distributions are conducted during the day in accessible, open, public places. When possible, Medair will collaborate with a protection partner on the ground to ensure appropriate measures are implemented to keep the beneficiaries safe. In sensitive areas with high risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and no protection partner present, Medair will advocate for a mobile protection team from other partners to join in the assessment and intervention.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The assessments and PDMs include input from local authorities, community leaders and elders, women and youth groups (teens, not children), church leaders, and other NGOs. Additionally, focus group discussions are held before the intervention. Exit interviews are conducted at the end of the intervention to inform the design of the following interventions.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

There is a feedback desk at every distribution to address and document complaints and any other feedback. Any complaints or positive feedback are recorded in a feedback mechanism document. The team aims at resolving complaints immediately (e.g. lost token, damaged item, etc.). Where this is not possible, the feedback received informs and shapes future interventions.Post Distribution Monitoring will be conducted within 2 weeks to 4 months after the distribution wherever possible, to inform future distributions.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Monitoring and Evaluation tools with community engagement include exit interviews and PDMs. The PDMs involve household (HH) surveys, focus group discussions (FGDs), and key informant interviews (KIIs).

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All ES/NFI tools include protection components to follow the “do no harm” principle. The composition of the protection risk analysis will be adapted according to different contexts and specific needs and risks (e.g. SBGV). For sensitive areas and when there is no protection partner on ground, Medair coordinates with protection partners to conduct in-depths protection assessments alongside the NFI assessment. Distributions are arranged so that conflicts/tension can be reduced or avoided, coping mechanisms are not eroded, and designated areas are safe and accessible. The most vulnerable beneficiaries are separated and served first so they do not have to wait a long time. Over the last year, Medair has actively increased the capacity of the ERT in protection risk analysis and mitigation by organising internal briefing sessions and sending staff members to external training run by protection experts. Medair plans to hire a Protection Officer to increase its capacity in protection mainstreaming in the programme design and implementation. This will be a full-time senior position in the ERT to ensure appropriateness in assessing protection needs and further build up the team’s competency in identifying and mitigating protection risks.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

When preparing for a distribution, Medair consults with local leaders and authorities to find the most suitable location to conduct the distribution. It considers space needed for the distribution, safe entry and exit points, shade for the waiting area, etc.Furthermore, Medair also organises focus group discussions to collect inputs from community leaders and elders, women and youth groups (teens, not children), church leaders, and other NGOs. The team aims to address identified access barriers. E.g. casual labourers are employed to guide blind recipients to the distribution point and assist them when exiting the site. As mentioned before, Medair plans to hire a Protection Officer to increase the team’s capacity in ensuring protection mainstreaming. This new position will additionally help to address the barriers to access of the populations.

Again, Medair coordinates with a protection partner on the ground whenever possible. The Protection Officer, Medair plans to hire, will further increase the team’s expertise to appropriately address inequalities and protection threats.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

During ES/NFI distributions, the team will identify a central location with the community chiefs, partners on the ground, and local authorities in order to ensure fair accessibility for beneficiaries (considering distance, available latrines, ample space, shade, water points, etc.) The team takes into account the physical security of beneficiaries and the items at the site, and also ensures the place can be accessed by disabled individuals (flat ground, no narrow passageways, no steps, etc.) A feedback desk is also implemented in order to allow beneficiaries to voice any concerns including access concerns, and these issues are reported and analysed for future improvements.During PDMs and exit interviews, the team will interview HHs and conduct FGDs to understand any challenges faced when traveling to and from the distribution site or if the location itself caused problems in order to make changes for safer, more inclusive, and more effective interventions. Both tools (PDM and exit interviews) ask the beneficiaries: Was anyone excluded from the assistance? If yes, who was mainly excluded? (men, women, boys, girls, elderly, people with disability, other).

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The team conducts assessments/verifications every time to determine if an intervention is needed and if it’s feasible. The following questions are included: Do you feel safe here? How would services coming to this location affect safety? This helps determine if a distribution would cause more harm than good if implemented. The PDMs and exit interviews also ask specific questions to the beneficiaries about whether they felt safe before, during, and after the distribution. This includes: Did you experience any safety/security issues on the distribution? Did you feel unsafe because of the S/NFIs you received? The PDM asks: How long did it take you to collect the items? How did you come to the distribution site? Did you have to pay or exchange anything to go and come back from the distribution point or to receive the items?

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity is one of Medair’s values. As such, it is an organizational priority in all interventions, and staff are trained to understand what this means in how to treat beneficiaries. For PDMs, the beneficiaries are asked: Did you feel you were treated with respect by the staff during the response? Did you see/know if the most vulnerable population were given priority during distribution? Are you currently able to better meet your family’s basic household needs using the S/NFIs you received? This way, we can measure the team’s treatment of others, make improvements if needed, and see whether the support is promoting the dignity of the affected population.
