Project Summary

The aim of CMD’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2024 is to provide a gender-responsive and disability-inclusive increased school enrolment and attendance for 7, 256 (3, 265 girls & 3, 991 boys) children and youth , including 725 (326 girls & 399 boys) children with disabilities (CWDs), in a manner that improves the quality of their education and promotes their protection, mental health and wellbeing in 25 schools (5 ECD, 15 Primary & 5 Secondary) through the facility of 100 (25 female & 100 male) teachers and the support of 150 (50 female & 100 male) PTA members in crisis-induced situations across Duk & Akobo Counties in Jonglei State, Ulang & Longechuk Counties in Upper Nile State and Pibor County in Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA).
Of the total target 7, 506 (3, 340 female & 4166 male), children account for roughly 96.6% or exactly 7, 256 (3, 265 girls & 3, 991 boys), spread in 25 target schools (5 ECD, 15 Primary & 5 Secondary). The total number of children with disabilities (CWDs) stands at 725 (326 girls & 399 boys)), constituting 10% of 7, 256. Children originating from the Host, IDP & Returnee segments of the target population account for 50%, 29%, and 21% of 7, 256 respectively. These translate into 3, 628 (1, 632 girls & 1, 996 boys) from the Host; 2, 104 (946 girls & 1, 158 boys) from IDP; and 1, 524 (685 girls & 839 boys) from Returnee families in the 5 communities of Duk, Akobo, Ulang, Longechuk & Pibor Counties.
100 (25 female & 75 male) teachers and 150 (50 female & 100 male) SMC/PTA members will support the target 25 schools to strengthen the technical, organizational, and institutional capacity of County Education Departments (CEDs) to inject local energy that builds professional and community capital to support children’s learning & achievement in the five Counties. CMD will work in an integrated manner and exploring linkages that exist with WASH, Health, and Nutrition Clusters. By working together, we shall include an integrated component of WASH, Health & Nutrition, specifically addressing critical WASH gaps in the schools through rehabilitating toilets and hand-washing facilities; and promoting hygiene to avert disease outbreak. Health-wise, we shall also target actions to prevent HIV/AIDS and STIs through raising awareness and reflections on prevention and response, thus, improving the sexual and reproductive health of girls and women, boys, and men. Furthermore, dignity kits are provided to girls in upper grades (P5-P8) and secondary schools as a preventive measure to assure school attendance and completion in any given academic year.
CMD will focus on reducing risks and strengthening protective factors for SEA victims/survivors and mainstream effective female inclusion in all stages of project design and implementation. Particular attention will be given to tailoring activities that respect cultural norms and traditions for female beneficiaries. The Project will involve women, girls, and other at-risk groups in all aspects of programming while being duly cautious where their inclusion poses a potential security risk or increases their risk of SEA. There are significant risks associated with SEA documentation, in relation to data protection. Failure to ensure information security can expose survivors, in particular, to harm, e.g. reprisal attacks by perpetrators, stigma and ostracism by their families/ communities. This means that SEA documentation must be carried out with great care.
The key strategy is to use primary and secondary schools acting as buffer spaces against acts of sexual, physical, or psychological violence inflicted on children due to stereotypes and roles or norms attributed to or expected of them on the basis of their sex or gendered identity; and promote crisis-affected children’s academic achievement through gender-sensitive delivery of curriculum, classroom management, teacher conduct and schoolyard dynamics.

1. Needs

The scale of need in South Sudan, a territory roughly the size of France, is almost inconceivable. Its human development indicators are bottom of the scale, confirming fears that the current cost structures for service delivery are severely constrained. According to the 2023 INFORM Risk Index, South Sudan is ranked as the second most vulnerable country globally to the impacts of natural hazards, including droughts and flooding (IOM, 2023). Climate change combined with short-term changes, such as a change in the seasonality of rains, have indirect effects on peace and security (OCHA 2022). The capacity of government authorities to respond to the many ongoing emergencies throughout is limited at best. Continued disparities in access to lifesaving education services are evident, particularly where donor support has tended to target more accessible and stable locations. An estimated 2.8 million people risk physical violence, rape, and other forms of gender-based violence. Millions of children cannot go to school because their schools were damaged, occupied by people who have been forced from their homes, or due to insecurity and violence. Children risk recruitment into armed groups, abduction, and possible trafficking (UN OCHA, HRP, 2023). The vast majority of headteachers (96%) reported a rise in at least one type of protection case during school closures. Teenage pregnancies (70%) and early/forced marriage (64%) were the most common, with half of respondents (50%) also reporting an increase in child exploitation cases. GBV and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) also increased, with 40% and 28% of headteachers reporting an increase, respectively (Education Cluster, 2021). Between 2019 and 2020, the most commonly reported reasons for school closures were flooding, looting by armed group and insecurity in or around schools. The top education interventions to support schools re-opening were reported as rehabilitation of infrastructure, teacher salaries, and school grants (Education Cluster, 2021). In terms of accessibility for Children with Disabilities (CwDs), 40.9% of the surveyed schools reported that their classrooms were not accessible for Children with Disabilities (Education Cluster, 2021). While 79.3% of headteachers reported that teachers in their school have received training on the South Sudan New Curriculum, only 28.8% said teachers in their school had received subject/content related training and just 25.1% had teachers who had been trained in inclusive teaching practices (Education Cluster, 2021). The most recent sober statistics are a yield of the Education Cluster Strategy for 2023-2025. Accordingly, 2.8 million children are out of school; 23% of schools have classes under trees; 77% of schools reported a lack of separate toilets or washrooms for girls and boys; 60% of classrooms and 50% of latrines are not accessible; less than 25% reported having a general protection referral mechanism; and only 34% of head teachers reported teachers had been provided with PSS training (Education Cluster Strategy, 2023-2025). The Christian Mission for Development (CMD) has, therefore, sought to close equity gaps and improve quality of service delivery to the needy. The geographical areas (Duk, Akobo, Ulang, Longechuk & Pibor Counties) covered by CMD are amongst the most severely impacted by high levels of displacement due to the recent civil war and associated calamities in South Sudan. Through this HRP, CMD intends to provide rapid structured education activities during emergencies as a coping mechanism to keep children in schools and enroll out-of-school learners. This will significantly make schools a buffer zone against harm and insecurity to vulnerable children; address equity gaps and improve quality of learning.

2. Project activities

Key response activities are as follows: establish 25 learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards; rehabilitate 15 learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards; rehabilitate 20 gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE and sphere standards; enrol 7, 256 (3, 265 girls & 3, 991 boys) learners - Pre-Primary, Primary & secondary; enrol 1, 000 (400 female & 600 male) learners - Life Skills for Youth; distribute dignity kits to 1, 000 adolescent girls; conduct awareness raising on key issues for the girl child and her transition to post-secondary education; distribute assistive devices to 725 (326 girls & 399 boys) children with disabilities; establish reporting (toll free lines, suggestion boxes, designated focal-points etc) mechanisms in 25 schools across 5 project locations; conduct training of 100 (25 female & 75 male) teachers and staff on child safeguarding and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanism; provide school meal to 7, 256 children; train 100 teachers on PSS; provide PSS service to 7, 256 learners; distribute ECD Kits to school children in 5 ECD Centres; distribute Recreation Kits to children in 15 primary and 5 secondary schools; distribute teachers Kits to teachers in 25 schools; train 100 teachers on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills; support 100 volunteer teachers with financial incentives; and conduct training for 150 (50 female & 100 male) SMC/PTA members and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
