Project Summary

Over the past 3 years, Lebanon has been facing political and economic crises which significantly affected the country’s political and socio-economic stability. The country is struggling with electricity blackouts, fuel and water shortages, and the near collapse of the banking, education, livelihood sector and public services. Inflation is reaching triple digits with a continuous deterioration of the exchange rate. The prices of basic food goods have been soaring while the purchasing power has been decreasing, leaving the majority of the population with little means to cope with extra hardship. The value of Survival minimum expenditure basket has significantly increased since the beginning of the crisis from nearly 1, 01 million LBP in October 2019 to almost 9,9 million LBP in September 2022.
The aforementioned challenges coupled with gender inequalities is deeply affecting the lives of women, men boys and girls. The crisis tends to limit women, men, boys and girls' access to services, notably for women and children, as well as persons with specific needs including sexual gender-based violence (SGBV) survivors and persons with disabilities (PWDs). In addition, the crises increases the risk of Gender based violence especially among women and girls. According to the GBVIMS data of Q3 2022, female survivors continue to constitute the majority of survivors seeking services given the disproportionate exposure of women and girls to GBV. 94% of all survivors reported in Q3 of 2022 were female, and 6% included men and boys reporting GBV incidents similar to previous quarters with a significant increase in children seeking GBV services. The most reported type of GBV in Q3 was physical assault and psychosocial/emotional abuse accounting for 35% and 32% of all reported incidents, respectively. Incidents of violence, including rape and sexual assault present the third most reported type of GBV incident making up 17% of all incidents.
This project will provide GBV services for the most vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinian, and Migrant population, including women, men, girls, and boys. It will address GBV needs through provision of GBV prevention and response services including psychosocial support, case management aligned with cash and voucher assistance, referral to safe shelter and awareness raising. The project will address legal needs for GBV including child survivor cases through provision of legal services, including legal counselling and payment of legal fees.
Through this intervention, WVL and Tabitha will implement multiple prevention activities, starting with gender responsive outreach targeting men, boys, women and girls to provide them with GBV, PSEA and SRH awareness raising sessions complemented by menstrual hygiene kit distribution.
The project will conduct a safety audit to ensure that intervention is safe, accessible and responsive to the needs of all groups. In addition, the safety audit will help prioritize the intervention.
The project will operate through WVL and Tabitha static intervention inside the Alread community centers or through mobile intervention in the North Gov. Mount Lebanon, the south, Bekaa, Baalbeck el Hermel, Beirut ana Akkar.
WVL and Tabitha will also coordinate their work with the stakeholders of each area to ensure the success of the intervention and be able to reach out to the most vulnerable population.
Based on the national SOP for GBV case management. Tabitha will implement the case management component while building WVL's capacity on GBV safe identification and referral. In turn, WVL will handle the prevention component by implementing the FPSS and non-focused PSS for cases, survivors, or at risk of GBV in the different centers located in the mentioned areas. Legal support, referral to safe support and other specialized services will complement the case management process.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Based on the national SOP for GBV case management, this project consists of providing GBV services for the most vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinian and Migrant population, including women, men, girls, and boys. It will address GBV needs through prevention and response services including psychosocial support, case management with cash assistance, referral to safe shelter and awareness raising. The project will provide legal assistance and counseling for GBV including child survivor cases.WVL and Tabitha will implement multiple prevention activities, starting with gender responsive outreach targeting men, boys, women and girls to provide them with GBV, PSEA and SRH awareness raising sessions complemented by menstrual hygiene kit distribution. As part of the project, a safety audit will be conducted to ensure that interventions are safe, accessible and responsive to the needs of all groups. In addition, the safety audit will help prioritize the intervention. The project will operate through WVL and Tabitha static intervention inside the existing community centers or through mobile intervention in the North, Mount Lebanon, South, Bekaa, Baalbeck el Hermel, Beirut and Akkar. WVL and Tabitha will also coordinate their work with the stakeholders of each area ,to ensure the success the intervention and ensure reaching out to the most vulnerable population.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

This project will base the intervention on the national SOPs using vulnerability/needs assessments, a network of outreach workers from the community. WVL and Tabitha will mainstream protection in all their activities by targeting the most vulnerable, enhancing their safety & dignity, and protecting the rights of participants without contributing to discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. Activities create a space for discussion and peer mentoring which allows different groups to reflect on their similarities rather than differences. Project activities are designed to include any vulnerable individual, regardless of their gender identity, disability, race, religion, or socioeconomic status. The project will follow WVL and Tabitha's safeguarding and PSEA policies and procedures that outlines zero-tolerance towards sexual exploitation and abuse and the sets the guidelines for reporting and responding to any incidents. 

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

WVL and Tabitha will be adopting a full-fledged accountability system, the Community Response mechanism (CRM), to ensure all participants and their respective communities are adequately informed about the project, are consulted and actively participating in the implementation of the project, and have access to safe feedback and response mechanisms. Information will be disseminated through SMS, flyers, helplines or through information sessions held at the field level. Moreover, WVL’s Accountability Unit has selected several channels to raise complaints and provide feedback: World Vision's Helpline, Helpdesk during field activities, Face to face with the Accountability Team, Face to face with the project team, Trusted Community Member, and Online Form. Complaints relating to the implemented project will be handled internally and closed within the timeframe while other complaints will be referred through RIMS.
