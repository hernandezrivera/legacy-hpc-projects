Project Summary

The project “Improving nutrition status of children 0-59 months and women of childbearing age among vulnerable households within Kapoeta East and Kapoeta south Counties Eastern Equatoria state” seeks to improve the nutrition status and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices among the vulnerable households in the two counties. AFSS intends to improve the nutrition status of children 0-59 months and the Pregnant Lactating Women (PLW) through integrated approach (Nutrition specific and sensitive). The nutrition-specific activities will seek to increase access to and coverage of appropriate treatment of moderately and severely malnourished individuals per the national Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) guidelines. These will include community mobilization and sensitization, active case finding by CNVs, passive/onsite nutrition assessment by the nutrition and health workers, enrollment of identified MAM(14,373; 7,474 F & 6,899M) ,SAM(4460; 2319 F & 2141 M) and PLW (13,685) cases into the nutrition programme for appropriate care, capacity strengthening of staff, health workers, (69; 20F & 39M) CNVs (170;68F & 102M) and MtMSGs,(66). Dispensation of Specialized Nutritious Foods and therapeutic foods (CSB++,RUSF and RUTF) . The Preventive activities will be geared towards addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition such as; poor child care practices and sub-optimal MIYCN practices. These will include ; MIYCN counseling both children 6-23months(30,991) and PLW (15,495). Vitamin A supplementation for children 6-59 months(65,859; 34,247F & 31,612 M), De-worming for children 12-59 months (54,233; 28,201F & 26,032M) Promotion of the production and consumption of diverse micro nutrient-rich foods such as dark green leafy vegetables (66 2 per facility per year) using contextualized climate-smart techniques; Rearing of small animals like guinea pigs and rabbits to increase the consumption of high biological value animal proteins. AFSS will also continue to promote optimal WASH practices through training MtMSGs and school going children on liquid soap making; MIYCN practices through Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) approaches such as onsite Nutrition and Health Education(NHE), food and cooking demonstrations (396; one per facility per month) and conducting community nutrition dialogues (8, 1 dialogue per county per quarter) as well as awareness campaigns among other
To enhance outcomes and sustainability, AFSS will support home-based care for children with MAM and uncomplicated SAM using locally available formulations like Sorghum Peanut Blend (SPB); capacity building of the health and nutrition workers; strengthening on the existing structures in the communities including the Mother To Mother Support Groups (MTMSG). To promote sustainability and continuity of the project, AFSS will work closely with the government stakeholders through engaging them in the planning phase, capacity strengthening, community mobilization, routine project monitoring as well as review of the project progress.

1. Needs

The Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 2 set an ambitious agenda for the world to achieve zero hunger by 2030. However, many countries including South Sudan (SS) are not on track attributing it to the several years of conflict and the resulting fragility, weather extremes as well as effects of the COVID-19 pandemic which adversely affected the food and nutrition security. There are increased risks of malnutrition and related health challenges among children especially those under five years of age (Sarah Karaja et al. 2021) and pregnant and lactating women. The women and children of South Sudan continue to suffer from higher levels of acute malnutrition as of the 2023 South Sudan IPC report indicated that1.65 million children 6-59 months are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and are in urgent need of nutrition services from July 2023 to June 2024. The total number of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases are estimated at 0.48 million and 1.16 million respectively. In the Eastern Equatoria State (EES), Kapoeta East (KEC) and Kapoeta South (KSC) counties are being classified in phase 3 in IPC AMN between October 2023 to March2024 and the situation will deteriorate to phase 4 between April 2024 to June 2024 hence many children under five & PLWs will become malnourished. The major factors contributing are; high prevalence of diseases, poor access to improved drinking water and sanitation and poor infant and young child feeding practices. In this regard, the management and prevention of acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) becomes paramount. Concerted efforts from the government of South Sudan and nutrition partners through the National Nutrition cluster (NNC) are in action to reduce the prevalence of acute malnutrition and consequently prevent malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality among children under five years of age and PLW . AFSS has been at the forefront of this in the counties of Kapoeta East, South, Maridi and Ibba through the implementation of nutrition programs using protocols stipulated in the national guidelines for Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) along with the nutrition sensitive approaches. Despite the interventions, under-nutrition among children has remained prevalent and there is still need for bold actions to address the situation. Therefore, AFSS proposes to continue implementing the nutrition programs in KE& KS Counties. The same integrated approach (Nutrition specific and sensitive) will be utilized with the aim of addressing the malnutrition situation in the 2 counties. The nutrition-specific activities will seek to increase access to and coverage of appropriate treatment of moderately malnourished individuals per the national CMAM guidelines. Nutrition-sensitive activities will be geared towards addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition such as; poor child care practices, sub optimal MIYCN and WASH practices through SBCC, and practical approaches such as food and cooking lessons, supporting households to produce and consume diverse nutritious foods including micro nutrient-rich fruits and vegetables using contextualized climate-smart techniques. To enhance outcomes and sustainability, AFSS intends to support home-based care for children with MAM and uncomplicated SAM using locally available formulations like Sorghum Peanut Blend, capacity building of the health and nutrition workers; strengthening on the existing structures in the communities including the Mother to Mother Support Groups. Prior to the onset of the projects, the prior obligations or pre-requisites that will be met include obtaining permits or approvals from relevant authorities, and ensuring that there is adequate funding for the project. The likely risks that might inhibit the achievement of the mentioned objectives include the inadequate funding, poor infrastructure, lack of community participation and insecurities.

2. Project activities

The nutrition interventions will target all children aged 6-59 months, pregnant women in the second trimester and Lactating Women with children less than 6 months of age. These will be identified from the communities and at the health facilities with the support of the Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs), MtMSGs and Nutrition Assistants with the aid of MUAC tapes, WH/L Z-scores and assessment of Oedema. Beneficiary monitoring will be carried out twice in a month on a bi-weekly basis. Management of the individuals enrolled into the programme will be in accordance with the South Sudan CMAM protocols. Community mobilization and sensitization will be carried out routinely alongside the active nutrition screening by the CNVs. Beneficiary nutrition and demographic data will be captured in the OTP/TSFP Ministry of Health Nutrition Registers that will be stationed at the health facilities providing nutrition services. Nutrition specific activities will include community mobilization and sensitization, active case finding by CNVs, passive/onsite nutrition assessment by the nutrition and health workers, enrollment of identified MAM/SAM cases into the nutrition programme for appropriate care, capacity strengthening of staff, health workers, CNVs and MtMSGs, Dispensation of Specialized Nutritious Foods (CSB++ and RUSF) under TSFP. While the Nutrition sensitive activities will include; Promotion of the production and consumption of diverse micronutrient-rich foods such as dark green leafy vegetables using contextualized climate-smart techniques; Rearing of small animals like guinea pigs and rabbits to increase the consumption of high biological value animal proteins. This will be done through collaboration between AFSS and the Food Security and Livelihoods implementing partners. They will play a vital role in supplying farm inputs that include seeds and garden tools as well as capacity building on vegetable production and value addition. AFSS will also continue to promote optimal WASH practices through collaboration with WASH implementing partners that will provide capacity building among MtMSGs and school going children on liquid soap making; MIYCN practices through Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) approaches such as onsite Nutrition and Health Education(NHE), food and cooking demonstrations and conducting community nutrition dialogues as well as awareness campaigns among others. To ensure protection among the targeted beneficiaries, AFSS will liase with the protection department within the counties of implementation to provide a safe referral pathway for the identified victims. Additionally, through the gender and protection department, AFSS will put into place the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism(CFM) that will be situated within the communities and at the nutrition sites. To promote sustainability and continuity of the project, AFSS will work closely with the government stakeholders through engaging them in the planning phase, capacity strengthening, community mobilization, routine project monitoring as well as review of the project progress.
