Project Summary

Health: The integrated primary health care interventions will be provided with key objective to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with disease among the children under five years, men, women, adolescent boys and girls, women of child bearing age, elderly men and women and people with disability. ARC has been implementing a comprehensive integrated primary health care life-saving intervention supporting 24 primary health care centers in South and East Darfur. Under this project, ARC will continue to provide basic primary health care services; treatment and diagnosis of communicable and non-communicable diseases, reproductive health, support of referrals for both medical and obstetric emergencies to tertiary level, emergency preparedness and response, disease prevention activities through health promotion activities by community volunteers, routine immunization, provision of essential drugs, medical and non-medical supplies, clinical management of rape to gender-based violence survivors and provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) services. ARC will also strengthen community-based approaches to promote positive health-seeking behaviors, recognition of illnesses, and care-seeking practices through trained community structures, community relief development committees and health, nutrition and WASH volunteers network. The project will strengthen institutional capacity through on-the-job training and mentoring to implement sustainable and affordable health interventions that meet the needs of men, women, boys, girls, children, elderly, and persons with special needs.
Nutrition: The nutrition project is designed to meet the needs of nutrition services for internally displaced people and vulnerable host community in Belil, Alsalam, Gereida and Dimso localities in South and Assalaya, Adilla, Abukarinka, Elferdous and Yassin in East Darfur states. ARC will provide nutrition services integrated to the primary health care facilities supported by ARC in South and East Darfur states. The nutrition activities will be implemented in an integrated approach with health and WASH. The goal of this project is to contribute to the reduction in nutrition-related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high-quality multi-sectoral life-saving nutrition interventions for children under five and pregnant and lactating women who are below the emergency threshold. The project will support 23 Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programs, 23 Outpatient Therapeutic Programs and 3 Stabilization Centers (SC) including referrals to secondary health care and provision of essential drugs. ARC will follow CMAM approach to implement the intended interventions. ARC will use the mother support group strategy to promote appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and Social Behavior Change and Communication (SBCC) among the target communities. ARC will also enhance the technical capacity of ministry of health staff through conducting training on CMAM package, IYCF and training on monitoring and reporting of the nutrition activities. All activities will be implemented in close collaboration with local institutions/ line ministries and communities such Community Relief and Development Committees (CRDC) to ensure sustainability and active community participation.

1 - Needs

Health: As per the draft of HRP 2021 health sector, the worsening economy of Sudan and protracted health emergencies resulted in an increasing number of people who can not access basic health services and also a decrease in the health system capacity to deliver the needed essential healthcare to the population. In addition, Sudan has been affected by the global COVID-19 pandemic that further strained the capacity of healthcare providers and affected access to essential health services due to nationwide lockdowns, re-allocating of health resources, and disruption of global supply chains that also impacted the availability of medicines and medical supplies nation-wide. During 2020, the cost of healthcare increased by 90% due to inflation with availability of essential medicines ranging between 15% to 50%. As of November 2020, the total COVID-19 confirmed cases reached 14,090 including 1,115 associated deaths. The high case fatality ratio CFR of 7.9% reflected the prevalent issues of low capacity in case management, lack of intensive care cadre and equipment. Furthermore, the lack of infection prevention and control (IPC) training and personal protective equipment (PPE) contributed to the 338 positive cases among health staff. As per the draft of HNO 2021, prioritization of the localities based on the severity of key lifesaving sectors (health, wash, nutrition, and FSL), the localities where ARC is operational in East Darfur and South Darfur have been classified as severity level 4. Nutrition: With reference to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), an estimated 12.8 million people (28 percent of the population), including 7.4 million women and girls, are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. There are approximately 2.5 million IDPs in Sudan, and about 1.07 million refugees from South Sudan, CAR, Eritrea, and Ethiopia. The continuing economic crisis, exacerbated by COVID-19, the high inflation and erosion of many families’ purchasing power has resulted in high levels of food insecurity, and severely curtailed livelihoods. A decline in the economy is disrupting health services, including the availability of medicines with only 15 percent of essential drugs available in the country. The ability of households to meet and access basic needs and services including purchase of food has been severely affected. South Darfur and East Darfur states are classified as among the states with the highest malnutrition rates with a recorded global acute malnutrition rate (GAM) of 16.43, which is above the World Health Organization’s (WHO) emergency threshold of 15 per cent. The GAM rates in localities being targeted by ARC are either above or very close to the WHO emergency threshold of 15 per cent with the highest rates recorded in the localities of Yassin (22.09%), Adilla (20.17%), Assalaya (19.99%) and Dimso (19.96%). Also, the survey revealed high prevalence of anemia in Abukarinka, Adilla, Al Salam, Assalaya, Beleil, and Gereida localities.It is also worth noting that the humanitarian situation in both East Darfur and South Darfur remain complex due to ongoing inter-tribal conflicts. Recently, an inter-tribal conflict between Massalit and Falata has erupted in Gereida locality and resulted to new displacements in the Gereida camps. Moreover, prevention measures, including lockdown order, were put in place to prevent COVID-19 spread but this had a negative impact on food availability and access, particularly on prices, on income and food expenditure. The conflicts and displacements and COVID-19 prevention measures eventually will lead to acute food insecurity and malnutrition.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ALIGHT places protection at the center of its programming by undertaking efforts for lifesaving activities throughout the project cycle and consistently working with the affected communities to ensure all services are delivered based on needs without harming the target beneficiaries. ALIGHT has comprehensive and collective approach to mainstream protection in order to enhance the overall ability of our staff to analyze, prioritize and respond effectively to respond protection needs of the affected populations including Refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities. ALIGHT provides special consideration to making its services (Livelihood, WASH, Health and Nutrition services) accessible to everyone as well as persons with disabilities and people with specific needs and ensure their accessibility across the age categories and vulnerability scenarios, with special provisions for pregnant women/ new mothers and infants, older, women headed families, widow’s orphans and severely ill and people with disability. ALIGHT has a dedicated protection team to work with all thematic sectors to strengthen the capacity of staff in protection mainstreaming but not limited to child protection, older persons, and people with disability, GBV prevention and psychosocial support. The protection team will support project teams in adopting practical ideas for, protection of identified vulnerable people in all ALIGHT programs as well as continuously monitoring protection outputs in all sectors.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ALIGHT is focused on implementing systems and methods that reveals customer experience of organizational services so that it can align a response and re-establish trust between ALIGHT, refugees, IDPs and the host communities. Trust works in both directions and must be built on the foundation of honesty and mutual accountability. ALIGHT commitment to accountability to the affected population will focus on the following; - governance, transparency, feedback, participation, design, monitoring and evaluation. ALIGHT will work with community structures in increasing the role of the community participation in maintaining the services. In addition, through the established community feedback and response mechanism, ALIGHT will continue to facilitate community consultation meetings to ensure active community participation, transparency and handling community feedback. The community members and beneficiaries provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities provided by ALIGHT and other development partners in their communities in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible to improve accountability across ALIGHT operational areas. ALIGHT’s Feedback/Complaint Response Mechanism (F/CRM) provides a formal channel through which ALIGHT beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can register voices and complaints. The F/CRM is centrally operated through feedback forms and a toll-free hotline number from all mobile phone networks companies (Sudani, Zain and MTN). ALIGHT has developed awareness materials including banners and flyers placed at ALIGHT project service delivery points such as health centers, water points etc. with the hotline number and explanations on how the FCRM works as well as information about our accountability standards and practices to which customers should hold ALIGHT accountable. In addition, through the MEAL department, ALIGHT conducts regular customer interviews and post distribution monitoring using standard survey feedback forms. As per ALIGHT procedures, the analysis of feedback information from communities is fed directly to senior management for action. These mechanisms have proven to be an effective tool in providing beneficiaries and communities with a voice and ultimately improving ALIGHT operations and identifying misconduct within the organization. A well-structured FCRM also enhances lessons learned and good practices across our network. ALIGHT is a signatory to the Joint Framework for Action on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.
