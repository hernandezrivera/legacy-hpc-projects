Project Summary

The provision of quality primary healthcare to conflict affected communities within Mbonge, Tombel and Ekondo titi Health district is aimed at reducing excess morbidity and mortality by improving access to primary healthcare through mobile clinic and CHWs models. The project will consider ensuring that activities related to health promotion, prevention, care and treatment services at the primary level will be well implemented. Caritas Kumba will collaborate with the Divisional delegation of Public health in ensuring appropriate implementation of all activities, promote effective monitoring and referrals. The project will target 51,400 affected people in Tombel, Ekondo titi and Mbonge Health districts in the South West region. The affected population will consist of internally displaced persons (70%) and the host population (30%). This project will enable the affected populations to have timely access to essential primary health care, mental health care, trauma care, improved epidemiological surveillance for diseases of epidemic potential, provide support for the response to outbreaks, given the opportunity to women to have access to skilled birth attendants and post-natal follow-up, and ensure clinical management of GBV cases. Essential primary health care will be made available by by using mobile clinics and community health workers in areas where health facilities are not functional. and refer cases with medical complication to appropriate treatment centers. Clinical psychologists will be deployed to provide mental health care to victims of mental trauma, patients in need of trauma care will be referred to centers with qualified trauma surgeon to provide trauma care to victims of physical trauma. The project will improve access to primary health care for persons with disabilities by assuring access to timely essential health care to this group and by providing support to rehabilitation facilities to take care of their needs. Assistive devices to improve the quality of life of people living with disabilities will also be provided to those who are in need. Epidemiological surveillance will be strengthened by expanding the early warning alert and response system using the events-based surveillance to Ekondo titi, Mbonge and Tombel health districts.
Caritas mobile clinic team which shall be comprise of well qualified Doctors, nurses, midwives, laboratory technicians, psycho-social support specialist shall be made up of two mobile clinic teams with about over 100 well trained Community health workers. Each team shall have an ambulance that can be able to ply the bad roads within the rainy seasons. The teams will undertake general consultation of inhabitants in communities within these health districts, sensitization of inhabitants with the use well defined social and behavioural change communication (SBCC) strategy, counselling and provision of psycho-social supports to conflict affected persons, treat patients diagnose with diseases through drug dispenser and refer cases that needed secondary health care within health facilities.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The prolong crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon has caused continue displacements with most families no longer in possession of their livelihood supports. this has caused most inhabitants to be practically unable to meet up with health care bills and hence engaging in to negative coping strategies to support their needs. In addition, health facilities as well as health care providers have been targeted by the fighters hence allowing existing health facilities to be destroyed, ill-equipped and void of quality staff to support the provision of quality health care services. According to OCHA in March 2023, 1.8 million persons were in need of quality health care services in the NW and SW regions of Cameroon. Most of whom have been affected epidemics, physical and mental trauma, vulnerable women and adolescent girls lack access to safe deliveries and have difficulties to access essential health care. Considering the low funding allocated for health care services in the NW/SW regions that was less than 15% of the requested funds in 2023, the low percentage of humanitarian actors on health care provision in the South West that had projects running, it has been projected that the number of persons in need of health care services may have double in 2024 in the South west region. Mbonge and Ekondo titi helath districts have been very challenging in terms of transportation means hence making it difficult for a lot of healthcare providers to access these areas for health care provision. The main difficulty in the North-West and South-West is the high number of non-functional health structures. In addition, attacks on health personnel have considerably limited human availability of technical material and equipment in both regions. In 2021, in addition to the COVID-19 pandemic, the South-West region recorded several outbreaks such as cholera, yellow fever, measles, and monkey-pox. Meanwhile, many pregnant women and children did not benefit from routine vaccination activities. This account for the need to improve on health care promotion and prevention activities to change the behavior of inhabitants toward vaccination and sharpens their awareness on preventive approach to healthcare. Emergency transportation, referral and counter-referral of cases remain a challenge in the access to essential healthcare, including sexual and reproductive care in areas with ongoing strives resources. The difficult means of transportation and the attack on healthcare allows the mobile clinic and Community health workers models of care to be the most recommended within crisis affected communities in Mbonge, Ekondo titi and Tombel health districts.
