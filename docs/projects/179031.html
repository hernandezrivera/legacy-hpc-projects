Project Summary

The project aims to improve the food security situation for the most vulnerable families in the targeted camps by providing live-saving humanitarian food assistance for 8524 vulnerable families (42,924 individuals: 8,256 men, 9,399 women, 13,279 boys, 11,990 girls)
That was achieved through free bread distribution (The essential food of the Syrian families) on a daily basis for continues 6 months in Aleppo governorates while each person received 300 g of bread per day based on the FSL cluster recommendations
BINAA contracted with 4 bakeries to the targeted areas and supported them by the sufficient flour and the operational cost in order to produce the needed amounts of bread, then the bread was distributed to the beneficiaries on a daily basis.
BINAA took the COVID-19 precautions in all project phases starting from beneficiary selection till the bread distribution according to BNAA COVID-19 Guidance

4 - Needs

Based on HNO2021, the food security situation in Syria has steadily worsened over the past three years, the 56 percent increase in the number of food-insecure people from 7.9 million in 2019 to 12.4 million by the end of 2020 is unprecedented. Two out of three Syrians are in need of some form of food and agricultural assistance, with an additional 1.8 million at risk of falling into food insecurity. The number of severely food insecure people has more than doubled, from 0.6 million in 2019 to 1.27 million people in 2021 (six percent of the population). During the design phase of the project, BINAA conducted a rapid need assessment. The assessment covered 51 camps in 6 subdistricts (Aghtarin, Ghandurah, AlBab, Suran, Azzaz, Jarablous). The main objectives of the assessment were to Identify needs, affected groups, and geographical areas and interventions requiring immediate assistance and develop an evidence-based strategy and implementation plan for improving the situation for the vulnerable people in these communities. A semi-structured questionnaire with Key Informants was designed to address the above objectives. Key informants were selected among the local council members and camp management office who are well informed of the situation in their communities. The assessment results indicate an urgent need for food security interventions, especially the bread supporting activities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project was achieved through the following activities:1.1 Community engagementThat includes coordination with AFAD, coordination with the clusters, and other active organizations on the ground, in addition to sign the MoUs with camps’ management 1.2 beneficiaries' selection and verificationWe conducted house-to-house verification visits to 100% of the shared lists by local councils, and we analyzed the collected data and identified the portion of bread per family per day based on the number of family members, then we issued the final beneficiary list for each camp.The beneficiaries list was updated on a monthly basis. 1.3 Contracting flour providersWe selected the flour provider through a comprehensive procurement process as we contracted for the needed amount of flour.We have conducted laboratory tests for the received flour in order to ensure that it matching to the contracted specification. 1.4 contracting with bakeries and provide them with the flour and operational cost in order to prepare the needed amount of breadwe contracted with 4 bakeries (2 bakeries in Jarablus subdistrict and, 1 bakery in Aghtarin subdistrict, 1 bakery in Al Bab subdistrict ).The bakeries were selected through a comprehensive procurement process 1.5 Distribution of the bread to the 8,524 targeted families households (42,924 individuals: 8,256 men, 9,399 women, 13,279 boys, 11,990 girls) on daily basis for continues 6 months. 1.6 Conducting awareness campaigns to prevent the spread of COVID-19We conducted of hose to house awareness visits against COVID-19 in the targeted camps to 26% of the families in the targeted camps 1.7 Monitoring and evaluation of the project activities through M&E field team.
