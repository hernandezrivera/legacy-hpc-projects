Project Summary

The Project will be implemented over a period of 12 months and targets around 4867 individuals residing at vulnerable and marginalized communities at 2 governorates of the West Bank (Qalqilya and Tubas) whose main dependence on Farming and herding is considered as the main source of livelihood. The project aims at enhancing the livelihoods of farmers and herders in the targeted communities and facilitate their access to land and water resources for enhanced productivity, promoted income and reduced food insecurity as it considers a wide range of interventions targeting both Farmers and Herders as follows:
1. Rehabilitation of water harvesting cisterns
2. Rehabilitation of agricultural roads
3. Rehabilitation of greenhouses
4. Distribution of drought-tolerant seeds
5. Rehabilitation of animal shelters
6. Rehabilitation of the drip irrigation system
7. Distribution of dietary kits

1 - Needs

Palestinians in the West Bank are subject to a complex system of control, including physical (the Barrier, checkpoints, roadblocks) and bureaucratic barriers (permits, closure of areas) which restrict their right to freedom of movement. The expansion of Settlements, restrictions on access to land and natural resources and ongoing displacement due to demolitions, in particular, are ongoing. Ongoing violent incidents throughout the West Bank pose risks to life, liberty and security, and – security considerations notwithstanding – concerns exist over reports of excessive use of force by Israeli forces. Over 60 percent of the West Bank is considered Area C, where Israel retains near exclusive control, including over law enforcement, planning, and construction. Most of Area C has been allocated for the benefit of Israeli settlements or the Israeli military, at the expense of Palestinian communities. This impedes the development of adequate housing, infrastructure, and livelihoods in Palestinian communities. Structures built without permits are regularly served with demolition orders, creating chronic uncertainty and threat, and encouraging people to leave. Where the orders are implemented, they have resulted in displacement and disruption of livelihoods, the entrenchment of poverty and increased aid dependency. The humanitarian community has faced a range of difficulties in providing aid in Area C, including the demolition and confiscation of assistance by the Israeli authorities. Referring to the targeted 2 governorates of the West Bank and based on OCHA's Vulnerability Profile, the majority of Area C 40 targeted communities mounting to around 12434 residents in suffer from medium to high vulnerability and they mostly depend on agriculture (Farming and Herding) as the main source of livelihood and while the price of Water Tankers per cubic meter ranges mostly between 15-25 NIS and sometimes even from 25-50 NIS. The Majority of Area C targeted communities suffer highly from settlers violence affecting herding and farming activities and they mostly suffer from access restrictions to land and access to markets. Regarding the herding activity, 31 communities of Area C in the targeted governorates decreased their holding of sheep since the year 2000. Many of the targeted localities suffer from monthly to weekly violence and abuse from settlers. Furthermore, the majority of the targeted communities suffer from medium to high vulnerability levels in terms of accessing their lands as well as high concerns regarding access to water in terms of quantity and quality. On another matter, Women in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers in this sector. Food insecurity increases the vulnerability of women and they are hugely impacted by the lack of water and food and eventually are immensely impacted in their livelihoods. Furthermore, and in correspondence to the Clustering Plan of the Prime Minister Mohammed Shtayeh, the project targets Qalqilya and Tubas that are set as agricultural clusters where the pillars of the plan are to address the effects of destruction resulting from the occupation and access to agricultural land, water, and natural resources and markets, as well as increasing investment in the agricultural sector and activating the role of start-up agricultural institutions. The plan includes several steps such as the management of natural resources such as the transfer of water to the eastern areas, supply and installation of major water lines, support and rehabilitation of cisterns, reclamation and protection of land, rehabilitation of roads and construction of others. Therefore, protective measures in their remedial, responsive and environment building are required to correspond to the needs of those targeted communities in the 2 governorates to enhance their livelihood and ensure their protection against severe conditions and to reduce their vulnerability.

2 - Activities

The project activities range from plant production interventions to livestock and animal production interventions benefiting both herders and farmers. Furthermore, the proposed activities aim at facilitating Farmers and herders access to resources including land and water to optimize their production, enhance their income resources, enhance their livelihoods and eventually achieve food security from local production and potential return from selling their products. The activities take into account the necessity to safeguard farmers and herders' lives against vulnerabilities caused by the Israeli Occupation measures such as demolition and confiscation as well as settlers' violence and abuse. Furthermore, Protection mainstreaming is considered a priority in this project incorporation of the principles of “do no harm”, non-discrimination, and the application of a human rights-based approach to carrying out the project activities. It also works towards preventing violations and promoting accountability capitalizing on community engagement including women and children and accountability to the affected population with regards to different age categories and gender. The activities of the project target male and female farmers where females account for around 49% of the beneficiaries and the activities capitalize on their enormous contribution the agricultural sector while the project also prioritizes women-headed households when the beneficiaries are selected. Activities of the project are illustrated as follows: 1. Rehabilitation of water harvesting cisterns2. Rehabilitation of agricultural roads3. Rehabilitation of green houses4. Distribution of drought tolerant seeds5. Rehabilitation of animal shelters6. Rehabilitation of drip irrigation system7. Distribution of dietary kits

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project considers protection mainstreaming a priority incorporating the principles of “do no harm”, non-discrimination, and the application of a human rights based approach into carrying out the project activities. It also works towards preventing violations and promoting accountability capitalizing on community engagement including women and children and accountability to the affected population with regards to different age categories and gender. The following principles for DNH in its design and implementation as follows: 1. Transparent dialogue and beneficiary communication , 2. Promoting community well-being and support , 3. Capitalizing on the community based resources from development and emergency preparedness projects, Making provision for those community members who cannot participate in food/cash for work projects, 4. Focusing on intersection of household criteria with socio economic criteria for beneficiary selection
