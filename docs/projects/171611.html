Project Summary

Driven by the impacts of climatic shocks and insecurity over the last two years, large-scale destitution and displacement continue to diminish food access and limit recovery efforts of the most vulnerable populations. While little rainfall has been experienced since May, the combined effects of flooding, and Desert Locust and other crop pest infestations have adversely impacted the 2020 Gu season crop production, with production dropping 40 percent lower than the long-term average for 1995-2019, according to FSNAU estimates. This is further exacerbated by the rapidly spreading Corona Virus (COVID-19) pandemic, which has led to market and transportation disruptions, as well as decline in remittances, livelihood opportunities and humanitarian assistance, directly affecting food security and affordability. As a result, 3.5 million people are expected to be in crisis and emergency (IPC3&4) through 2021 and in dire need of humanitarian assistance (HNO, 2020). Around 2.6 million IDPs living in over 2000 sites, in crowded living conditions in urban and semi-urban areas, are among the most at risk to COVID-19, and in dire need of immediate and long-term assistance. Additionally, the total acute malnutrition burden or Somalia is estimated at 849,900 children under the age of five years likely facing acute malnutrition through August 2021, including 143,400 likely to be severely malnourished. (FSNAU, September 2020)
The project focuses on the delivery of basic lifesaving and protection/restoration of livelihoods of the target population with a special focus on all locations with FSC prioritized persons in need, including IDP camps and host communities that are classified in IPC Phase 3 & 4 in terms of response.
The project is aimed at improving the ability of vulnerable HHs to meet their basic needs, supports protection and recovery of livelihood assets through life-saving UCT, animal treatment services, provision of locally preferred high quality seeds kits and trainings, and rehabilitation of community productive assets through CFW to support the resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks and prevent them from slipping into acute food insecurity. The proposed project aims to target at least 12,585 HHs (75,510) agro-pastoral and IDP households in Baidoa, Banadir, Belet_Xaawo, Burco, Cabudwaaq, Doolow, Luuq and Owdweyne districts in crisis and emergency with delivery of life saving interventions in line with the strategic objectives of the HRP and FSC response plan.
The project is informed by the centrality of protection and a gender, age and diversity-sensitive approach which is mainstreamed throughout the project cycle. Moreover, the project reflects the principles of COOPI Protection Policy and is built consistently with COOPI Protection SOPs and Protection Mainstreaming guidelines. The project will be guided by the principles of DO NO HARM, ensuring safety and dignity of target population is upheld and at the same time providing for accountability, participation and empowerment.
In summary the following activities will be carried out:
1. Provide UCT to 4,285 HHs that are faced with severe levels of food insecurity (crisis and emergency) with the aim of increasing immediate access to food for 3 months.
2. Rehabilitation of community productive assets through CFW targeting 1,900 vulnerable HHs.
3. Provision of locally preferred high quality seed kits, trainings and fuel for irrigation for 900 flood affected female and male headed vulnerable riverine farmers in Gedo region.
4. Provision of locally preferred high quality seeds kit (drought tolerant) and trainings for 2,500 female and male headed vulnerable agro-pastoral farmers in Bay region.
5. Emergency livestock treatment (treatment and deworming) campaigns for animals belonging to 3,000 HHs will be dewormed and treated against endemic &prevalent diseases.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfer as a response modality has proven to be efficient, it increases transparency and is scalable to meet rapid surges in needed support. From the donor or agency standpoint, it is a cheaper and more efficient alternative or a complement to commodity distribution, notably food aid. From the beneficiary perspective, it enhances dignity and the degree of empowerment/independence, enabling them to take control of the relief themselves and to adapt it to individual needs in a timely manner so as to cater for the immediate lifesaving necessities. Given in-kind’s inability to address the overarching needs of communities facing crisis, cash transfers have become more preferable among beneficiaries with the flexibility offered. Furthermore, with the increase in mobile money penetration in Somalia, cash transfers have increasingly become more popular in the modern era, unlike in-kind transfers which involve more costs, are not as safe as mobile money transfers, and take longer to distribute. Markets in Somalia have remained functional and there is adequate infrastructure to enable this modality to work efficiently.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Given the crucial role of markets in people’s livelihoods and the fact that transfer modalities are largely market oriented, COOPI has prioritized market assessments in its food security analyses/needs assessments so as to understand market dynamics in shaping programme requirements. The project design has placed great emphasis on market functionality as a means towards determining the CMBs of the various target locations with reference to FSNAU market data and CWG transfer value recommendations. This has been seen as a guide towards developing efficient and effective responses to crises, particularly for populations in IPC 3&4. Monitoring of the markets will be done regularly through local market price monitoring, the CWG and FSNAU, and this will be helpful in ascertaining if markets in target locations are sufficiently elastic and competitive in order to respond to increased demand with sufficient quantity and quality of goods without resulting in inflation.

Mitigation Measures

• COOPI will support initiatives that promote self-reliance and access to basic services, such as livelihood inputs, rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets as well as disaster preparedness, that can help reduce the need to seek such support elsewhere. • The proposed intervention is intended to not only alleviate the target communities of the impacts of shocks through short-term cash transfers, but also to build their resilience while at the same time providing increased livelihood sources and strategies as well as access to productive assets for beneficiaries. This is expected to reduce instances of displacement in search of livelihood opportunities.• COOPI will engage with influential stakeholders, such as regional or international organizations or relevant government ministries/local authorities, to encourage and support their involvement to minimize the risks of displacement in target districts/villages.• Physical verification exercises will be conducted by the project staff to ascertain the weaker members of the target beneficiaries are not excluded. • The project staff and village committees will be selected to represent all groups within the community including members from marginalized clans. • The project staff and members will be trained on protection mainstreaming while all COOPI staff signed and adhere to COOPI’s Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect the humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. • COOPI has an established feedback and complaint mechanism where the beneficiaries can call to air their grievances and their issues responded to. All the target beneficiaries will be informed of the complaint mechanism and the telephone numbers through sensitization and banners posted at the project locations.• To ensure that women are empowered to influence decisions and outcomes, the project will ensure at least 30% representation of women in all the local institutions. COOPI will identify and prioritise the Women, girls and boys as the most vulnerable groups in the community. • Providing them with accessible and timely information on project deliverables, selection criteria and COOPI targeting procedures.• The project team will consist of a gender balanced team ensuring there are enough women available as contact points for women in the community, as this is more culturally acceptable in the communities.• Inclusion of women, girls and boys during implementation and monitoring of the project, a participatory and gender sensitive approach will be adopted to allow equal opportunity to all. • COOPI will carry out a detailed analysis of data relevant to the project and identify the power dynamics in the target areas including who has access to food, assets and inputs and who is able to influence decisions on food, agriculture and livelihoods to identify any barriers to access, discrimination against particular groups• COOPI will utilize site information from needs assessments and CCCM assessments in IDP settlements. Both tools will be mainstreamed to collect GBV risks and will be shared with technical staff and other stakeholders to reduce and mitigate GBV where possible.• The principles of “do no harm” will be prioritized in the design of this action. Activities such as cash for work will be sensitive to disabled people, pregnant and lactating mothers, IDP and marginalized communities, and will provide flexibility in work norms. Cash will be transferred on a mobile money platform to mitigate risks and address protection and privacy of the beneficiaries.• COOPI will prioritize safety & dignity of the beneficiaries by ensuring that the beneficiary lists and their personal data are kept confidential and safe from unintended use. This will ensure that personal data is handled carefully and used only to address immediate needs. COOPI will ensure that lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing support
