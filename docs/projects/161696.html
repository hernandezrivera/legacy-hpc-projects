Project Summary

To continue to support conflict-affected families and meet humanitarian needs, CRS proposes a modification to extend the second phase of its “Multi-sectoral Support for Conflict-affected Families in Borno and Yobe States” project, initially launched in September 2016, and funded by the United States Agency for International Development’s (USAID) Office of Foreign Disaster Assistance (OFDA). Through a multi-sectoral approach, CRS will continue to provide vulnerable HHs with lifesaving support, by meeting basic shelter and WASH needs while also addressing adverse coping strategies by strengthening their agricultural-related livelihood options. In the modification 74,954 individuals (13,628 HHs) will be targeted for, shelter, NFI and WASH assistance. In addition, CRS will support 15,000 existing agriculture and food security beneficiaries in 3,000 HHs through the 2019 harvest.
CRS will prioritize IDP, returnee and female-headed households while also extending support to vulnerable host community members. CRS will work with local stakeholders to identify and quickly respond to newly displaced people’s needs to save lives and restore dignity.
This comprehensive rapid response emergency project modification will continue to cover those Local Government Areas (LGAs) most affected by the conflict, namely Jere, Kaga, Magumeri and Gubio LGAs (Borno State) and Gujba, Gulani LGAs (Yobe State), and expand to Mobbar LGA (Borno State). In addition, through the modification CRS will engage with other humanitarian actors, government and stakeholders to map and assess the private water vendor market system and initiate pilot activities to improve the delivery of sufficient safe water through the market. This modification will extend the project an additional 10 months from July 1, 2018 to April 30, 2020.

1 - Needs

Shelter: It was estimated that 3.5 million people will require shelter and non-food items (NFIs) assistance in 2019, showing a significant increase over 2018 and representing the second and third highest priority need across all IDP households. In camp-like settings, the majority of IDP HHs are living in emergency (37%) or self-made/makeshift shelters (35%), with others in host family houses (9%), government buildings (7%), individual houses (6%) and schools (5%). The CRS’ recent assessment in Magumeri that showed in Magumeri community members observed crowding conditions due to the influx of new arrivals. Buildings and houses were in disrepair, missing doors, windows and in some cases collapsed roofs and walls. Damaged, incomplete and makeshift structures expose households to rain, wind, sun and harsh temperatures. NFI: NFIs remain the second most reported need of affected people in NE Nigeria, in host communities and in camp settings alike. While over 47,000 HHs benefited from humanitarian NFI support in 2018, support fell far short of the 200,000 HHs in need. CRS’ beneficiaries have reported mattresses, blankets, solar torches/lamps, cooking kits, farming tools and sleeping mats as priority items. WASH NFIs have been identified by CRS beneficiaries as a critical need, including important hygiene items needed to prevent disease outbreak. For example, a lack of containers to carry and store water has been identified by HH respondents as a main barrier to water consumption and the carrying out of basic daily activities. Similarly, CRS’ purchasing pattern data from the first phase of the OFDA-funded project shows that when provided with NFI vouchers, families most frequently purchased laundry soap, bathing soap, sleeping mats, cooking pots and basins. WASH: In CRS program areas, 8-16% of accessible people do not have access to 15 liters of water per day, and in some locations more than one quarter have to travel more than 30 minutes to access water (Kaga – 25%, Magumeri – 29%, Gubio – 38%), falling short of Sphere recommended standards. CRS consistently finds poor access to water during its targeting assessments, illustrated by a recent assessment of 6 sites Kaga covering 17,070 people that showed that existing functional water points supplied only 12 liters of water per person per day, with the Magistrate and Aisha Buhari sites averaging only 8.9 and 5.9 L/p/d respectively. According to IOM’s assessments, people practice open defecation in 33% of camp-like settings and 47% of host communities. Few communities have hand washing stations or gender-separate latrines, and existing latrines are in poor condition. CRS’ recent assessment in Kaga showed a gross latrine coverage rate of 70 persons per drop hole, with individual sites as high as 117 at Magistrate. A transect-walk assessment in 12 sites in Magumeri showed that open defecation is common and only a few households have traditional pit latrines without superstructures. In the same communities, there was no dedicated site for solid waste disposal. Garbage and stagnant water around the household areas and vectors such as rodents and insects could be observed in the community. Economic recovery/water markets: While most humanitarian actors in Nigeria were, and are still today, involved in rehabilitation of nonfunctional boreholes as well as drilling and development of new ones; to date, no humanitarian actors have engaged in mapping the private water sector in North East Nigeria in a systematic way nor have actors identified how this market can be potentially used to improve WASH outcomes. CRS conducted in February 2019 a consultation with a variety of WASH actors and confirmed this fact; CRS also found out that there is limited understanding of how to target and work through the existing private water sector, which is extensive in Maiduguri and throughout most Local Government Areas (LGAs) in North East (NE) Nigeria, to meet water needs of the affected population.

2 - Activities

This modification will provide an integrated package of immediate life-saving and sustaining WASH, shelter and NFI interventions for conflict-affected households in Gubio, Jere/MMC, Kaga, Magumeri, and Mobbar LGAs. The modification will target new beneficiaries in the WASH and Shelter sectors. The modification will also provide ongoing support to 3,000 current agriculture beneficiary HHs in Gubio, Magumeri, Gujba and Gulani LGAs through the end of their harvest to reinforce training messages from the current project and monitor their progress. Similarly, throughout the modification period CRS will provide ongoing operation and maintenance in existing WASH communities according to the NE Nigeria WASH Sector’s strategy. Shelter:-assist 4,000 HHs with shelter (emergency/a1, Bama/A2, or building repairs)-train 80 committees on disaster risk reduction, provide each committee with a fire safety kit-train community leaders, land owners and IDPs on housing land and property rights, facilitate land agreements and building repair agreementsNFI: provide 4,000 HHs with e-vouchers for the purchase of Shelter and WASH NFIWASH:-repair 10 water points-train water management committees and supply repair tool kits-construct 440 latrines and 440 bathing spaces-establish 70 latrine cleaning committees and supply them with cleaning and maintenance kits-organize community clean-up campaigns and provide tool kits for the clean-up-distribute 4,000 female hygiene kits to women and girls of reproductive age-distribute 3,000 cholera prevention kits-conduct hygiene promotion sessions with 74,954 individuals through HH visits, child to child sessions, FGDs, community meetings and other approaches.
