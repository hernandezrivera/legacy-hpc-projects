Project Summary

The proposed project for 2021 will contribute to the WASH sector 2019-2021 strategy. Project will be achieved by increasing sustainable use of gender sensitive emergency WASH services such as water supply and sanitation and hygiene promotion sessions, and ensuring that conflict affected populations receive immediate assistance and operation and maintainace support. The project will provide WASH assistance at IDP in camps and community level as well as in health facilities and schools. It will enable male, female, boys and girls to have improved access to safe drinking water in secure places, enhance their knowledge on WASH practices, prevent COVID 19, reduce prevalence of diarrhea and ultimately also lead towards improved nutrition security. This will be achieved through a combination of interventions such as construction/rehabilitation of water points, Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), formation and training of WASH COMs with 40-50% female and building capacity of government authorities to effectively provide lifesaving assistance and have improved capacity to meet the immediate needs of newly displaced or returning populations. Throughout the course of the project, the active participation of women in the design, implementation and sustainability of interventions will be ensured and protection needs of women and girls will be identified and integrated into programming

1 - Needs

BAY states, the epicenter of the crisis was the worst hit and has recorded arrivals of refugees from neighboring countries. Furthermore, According to Round 33 of IOM DTM report, 2,118,550 IDPs or 436,058 households were recorded as displaced out of which 53% are female, 47% male. In addition, the Borno state return plan is to different committees to relocate IDPs to various LGAs including Marte, Ngoshe (Gwoza), Pulka, Damasak and Kirawa in Borno State. According to the REACH, multi-sectoral rapid needs assessment (MSNA, November 2019), 44% of households reported severe or extreme severity of needs related to water, sanitations and hygiene (WASH), while in Yobe, 49% of the households were in severe or extreme severity of WASH needs. While in Northeast Nigeria regions, windstorm and rainfall (flooding) had adverse effects in camps and host communities since June 2020 till date. IOM, SEMA and UNOCHA reported that about 6,799 households (29,243 individuals), 57 sites, 2,780 shelters and 180 toilets were affected and damaged during this period in 17 LGAs in Yobe and more than 50 IDP camps in Borno State. Furthermore, in Nigeria, cholera/acute watery diarrhoea (AWD) is a continuous problem in the three (3) targeted states during past years. According to NCDC, 13,887 cholera cases were reported in Borno state and 2275 cases in Yobe. Borno state is considered as one of the high-risk states for cholera/AWD transmission, where a total of 1881 cases were reported since January 2020 and WASH sector identified some of high-density populated villages and IDP camps are high risk hot spots for cholera/AWD.

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Improved access to safe drinking water supply for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno states.In close coordination with the Government and the WASH sector-working group, the response will be deployed, integrating protection concerns and adopting a gender sensitive approach to adequately determine the needs of the male, female, boys and girls. Populations will be provided with access to a safe water supply (water trucking /quick repair of water points with chlorination, new drilling and rehabilitation of boreholes with solar pumping system/hand pumps) that prioritizes first distribution to vulnerable groups (women, children and elderly people). Water quality testing including free residual chlorine will be tested for all water supply to ensure the quality of the water. The IDP camps will be supported with operation and maintenance of water points to ensure continues safe water supply. Where every possible WASHCOM will be formed and trained with 40-50% of female in the committee to ensure the needs of the women are considered during the implementation. Activity 02: Improved access to sanitation service for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Multi-unit communal gender separated latrines will be constructed in IDP camps in safe places with light to ensure the safe access. Male and female Hygiene and sanitation volunteers will be trained and supported to monitor the cleaning and maintainace of latrines. Solid waste management will be conducted through distribution of waste bins, trainings and coordinating with BOSEPA for waste collection. In communities where returnees, host communities and IDPs have been displaced for extended periods, the project will focus on household latrines. Following a transect walk focused on the negative aspects of open defecation, the Community Lead Total Sanitation approach will be launched. The approach will ensure that community mobilizers and project staff integrate gender-related dimensions to ensure women and girl’s participation while developing community action plans. Beneficiaries will commit their own resources, including locally available materials to construct latrines. Local artisans and skilled technicians will be trained to build standard structurally sound latrines. The design will ensure that pour flush/VIP latrines for health facilities and schools are accessible to people with disabilities and take into consideration the needs of women, children and elderly. The structure will include ramps, be child-friendly, have hand railings, be lockable, gender segregated and have containers for menstrual hygiene management. Activity 03: Improved access to hygiene promotion and gender sensitive hygiene kits for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno stateThe interventions are targeting hard to access areas with new arrivals in locations with limited coverage of critical lifesaving WASH services, emergency hygiene and sanitation sessions conducted to demonstrate measures such a handwashing, good hygiene practices and the safe disposal of waste, with the aim to mitigate against the spread of water borne disease. An experienced consultant and trained ACF staff will undertake a study on the barriers communities face to carry out hygiene practices. This will inform the selection of messages highlighted in the Behavioural Change Communication strategy that will be launched in partnership with the Rural Water and Sanitation Management Agency (RUWASA). Handwashing stations will be established next to constructed latrines and utilized during emergency hygiene sessions covering personal hygiene, domestic hygiene, the proper use of hygiene kits and WASH infrastructure. The project will also commemorate world days of significance such as Global Hand Washing Days, Menstrual Hygiene Management Day, World

The multisectorial needs assessment will be conducted with community leaders and female beneficiaries’ members including disable person and the outcome will be discussed with beneficiaries. Since women are key actors all along the water chain, project team will ensure that girls, women and disable person are part of the decision making process for the selection of location for water distribution, rehabilitation of borehole or wherever possible, AAH team will ensure the representation of women to be 50% in the water management structure, thus encouraging them to become active members.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In addition, as described below, AAH has a comprehensive feedback and complaint response mechanism (FCM) with well-trained accountability personnel to ensure responsiveness of feedback and complaints to affected population, transparency to project stakeholders and information-sharing with different groups.The mechanims is structured to integrate the people affected by the crisis into key decisions and processes and to have effective communication and feedback channels. When RRM is triggered in any community/location, the team will hold community discussions to ensure communities/beneficiaries understand the purpose and intended impact of the project, to agree on two-way accountability measures and responsibilities and to determine community obligations and participation in project implementation and monitoring. The team will place banners showing access to the feedback mechanism toll free phone lines, complaint boxes, complaint booths and feedback forms that are administered during feedback days at the community level.
