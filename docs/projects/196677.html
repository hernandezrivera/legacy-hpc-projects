Project Summary

The project will target 1500 families with regular (monthly) food assistance through two appropriate modalities:
1- Cash for food: GAN will target 750 most vulnerable families in village and camps with cash for food, the cash intervention is feasible in these camps as they all have access to different main markets in the targeted communities, eight rounds of cash for food will be distributed, each round consists of 20 days for distribution and 10 days for validation and PDM, 60$ will be allocated for each family as recommended by FSL cluster and CWG, the data gained from the FSL cluster indicate these areas as high priority ranking and no partner plan to target the same vulnerable and underserved camps, according to the needs assessment, the first priority is food, the proposed modality is a cash for food, as it is considered one of the most effective modality since the markets are available near camps.
2- E-voucher for Food: GAN will target 750 most vulnerable families in the village with food voucher with the aim of saving lives and ensuring continued and regular access to food for 8 months for most vulnerable groups, beneficiaries could replace the monthly top-up assistance (60$ per family) with the main food items from the markets contracted with GAN and will be able to buy among 40 specified items with restricted amounts that allowed us to fulfill the calories min standard set by the WFP.
The project will target the IDPs with emergency food assistance through four appropriate modalities:
1- Providing cooked meals to 750 families of newly IDPs in the targeted locations 30 times respectively within 30 days, this intervention is considered as a rapid response for affected IDPs suffering from lack of resources, instability, and don’t have cooking facilities, GAN designed the cooked meals based on its experience along past 10 years, and each beneficiary will receive 500g of high-quality cooked food daily for 30 days (this period of this type of assistance is recommended by FSL cluster).
2- Providing RTERs to 750 families of newly IDPs in the targeted locations enough for 5 persons for 7 days, this intervention is considered as a rapid response for affected IDPs suffering from lack of resources, instability and don’t have cooking facilities.
3- Providing food baskets to the beneficiaries (750 HHs) who have received Cooked meals/RTERs as a next step toward securing their food security which will last for 3 months.
4- Bread bundles: provide bread to 500 HHs in the targeted camps for 30 days frequencies.
GAN will use beneficiary selection criteria that prioritize vulnerable HHs, including female or children-headed households, those with elderly or disabled family members, chronic ill family members, pregnant, and households with more than six persons.
Monthly market assessment in terms of availability of the items, prices, and accessibility will be conducted to ensure the local markets are functioning and all beneficiaries have good and equal accessibility to the different goods.
GAN will monitor the distribution, according to the M&E plan
Information will be collected using: Verification of the eligibility of beneficiaries: through a visit to the beneficiary residence and interview face-to-face using Kobo questionnaire.
PDM: GAN will conduct PDM assessment after each redeem process/distribution, GAN will choose a sample of beneficiaries (5-10%) to gauge beneficiary feedback in relation to timeliness of the provision of assistance, quality and quantity of assistance provided, the distribution process, behavior of the distribution staff, satisfaction with the project.
The M&E field officers will also be present within the distribution points to receive complaints, and they will periodically submit their reports to the head office to assist in decision-making.
The feedback mechanism will ensure accurate information about the implementation mechanism and the beneficiary's satisfaction.

3 - Needs

The FS situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated. In 2023, an estimated 68% of total population will need some form of food assistance. (2M people living in camps who are deemed to be 100% food insecure). 7in10 Syrian families is food insecure with the highest concentration in Idleb 74%The impact of Syria’s crisis continues to hinder people’s ability to meet their immediate food needs and livelihoods, in a context marked by very complex drivers and contributing factors. This includes economic worsening and weakened local currency, soaring food prices. Overall, the unemployment rate has increased sharply, with negative consequences on income sources and purchasing power. With their buying power decimated, families are forced to resort to harmful coping mechanisms to meet their food and nutritional needs.Households, especially female-headed, reported facing food shortages and engaging food coping mechanism to maximize the use of little available resources. For instance, households adopting IPC crisis or beyond food coping mechanisms reached as much as 50%. Furthermore, due to lack of access to food or money to buy food, as many as 15% of households had to withdraw children from school, increasing risks for child labour and related protection concerns.52% of households made difficult decisions of restricting food consumption for adults in favour of children, while 63% households reported reducing the number of usually accustomed meals. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

GAN is a member of PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA, GBV), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available at distribution points. GAN recognizes the rights of all stakeholders, particularly the vulnerable, to access humanitarian programs without the risk of sexual exploitation or abuse. GAN intends to prevent abuse by implementing procedures to protect beneficiaries through best recruitment practices, staff induction, creating an open and aware culture and assigning clear management responsibilities, ensuring that victims are supported, and perpetrators held to account. Information on the referral pathway will be circulated through posters and banners at the distribution points. The distribution points will be inside the camps itself, during daylight hours, and safeguard that there is appropriate segregation for women to create a culturally sensitive and safe operational environment


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: - The MTA using the e-voucher modality are available and functioning.- Markets are functioning properly. - Adequate presence of vendors to support the voucher program - Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries - COVID context is continued Risk: 1: the risk of that one money transfer service is responsible for transferring the cash amount: Likelihood: low, Impact: high. GAN has a prior fundamental condition for a money transfer agent: the payment is conducted after distribution. 2: COVID-19 spread in target communities: Mitigation: GAN team takes all necessary measures to avoid the transmission and spread of the disease infection, such as wearing a face mask, gloves, providing hand sanitizers, maintaining social space spacing, and measuring the temperature of the beneficiaries before entering the distribution point, GAN will use the latest version of recommendations in regards of distributing NFI under the cotext of COVID-19 3: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk that markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access, or other unexpected issues. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. Mitigation strategy: GAN confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allows the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on a regular basis by the program team4:Cholera spread: Raising awareness and how to prevent and treat it, adding soap to the assistance
