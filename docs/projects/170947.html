Project Summary

Project Objective:
To enhance the capacity of agricultural assets of vulnerable farmers in the southern governorate of the Gaza Strip who are subjected to humanitarian crises to restore livelihoods.
Special objectives:
1. To increase agricultural assist productivity of 360 Dunums of open field holed by (at least 20 female farmers their age between 20-65 year and 160 males' of the headed household farmers their age between 20-65-year-old).
2. To rehabilitated the greenhouse of 100 farmers in the targeted area ( at least 10 female farmers their age between 20 -65-year-old and 90 male of the headed household farmers theirs age between 20-65-year-old).
3. To rehabilitate agricultural water wells and install a 100 kW solar power for four wells in the targeted area (at least 8 female farmers their age between 20-65-year-old and 72 male of the headed household farmers their age between 20-65-year-old).
4. To provide 4500 M3 of water storage by rehabilitating 50 ponds (at least 5 female farmers their age between 20-65-year-old and 45 males of the headed household farmers their age between 20-65-year-old).
The project activities will be implemented in Rafah, Khan Younis, and the middle area.
All project activities will be implemented by the parallel in all regions and completely coordinated with the Ministry of agriculture and the Food Security Sector.
The estimated number of direct beneficiaries is 330 farmers (At least 35 female farmers and 295 males' headed households) while the total number of the farmers and their families is 1815 members.
The project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach and all targeted households will be selected according to protection-related and socio-economic vulnerability criteria.
The project's objectives match the Food Security Sector (FSS) objective #(1) The livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security, and productive capacity restored/enhanced,
Moreover, project objectives aligned to the HRP 2021oPt, Strategic objective country #( 3): The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.

1 - Needs

Two million people living in Gaza are witnessing a continuous decline in their living conditions, the level of food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Gaza, also increased the rates of unemployment, which exceeded 49 % in the second quarter of 2020( PCBS 2020 ). Moreover, the funding crisis is represented also by the FSS HRP funding appeal shortfall, with only 43% of the overall FSS budget being funded as of mid-year 2020, Since Mar 5th, 2020, a state of emergency was declared in the Palestinian territories to counteract the spread of the (COVID-19). The food insecurity is deepened by the COVID-19 pandemic, both in terms of the number of people being affected, and the severity of food insecurity. Food insecurity in the oPt is already high, particularly in the Gaza Strip Nearly 72 percent OR 1.6 million people, live in the Gaza Strip food insecure. (FSS-HNO2021). In July 2020, Al Nakheel Association held the workshop to determine the priorities of the agricultural sector in the light of COVID-19, In the presence of male farmers and female farmers from all sectors, agricultural associations, and the Ministry of Agriculture. As the results of the following :1. Provide agricultural inputs and financial assistance to affected farmers from all sectors due to the facing accumulating debts 2. Farmers are still facing a deficit of water for irrigation due to the deficit of power supply for the functionality of their water wells, 3. The irregularity of water availability caused severe losses to farmers as the production sharply decreased in terms of quality and quantity. Besides, 4. Farmers in border areas (ARA) are still suffering from limited access to their lands to cultivate, also the damages derived from the storm. 5.The volume of agriculture and food sales remained low compared to pre-COVID-19 levels for small-scale producers, especially cattle breeders (milk producers) and poultry breeders, the market access is limited and sporadic amidst restoration of movement restriction and lockdown measures affect the farmers directly. Al Nakheel has been working together with Al Manal society for development the rural women ( Al Manal) in the middle area. and the Palestinian Center For Organic Agriculture ( PCOA) in Rafah to assessed humanitarian needs using the several methods; Semi-structured interviews executed with headed house females farmers their age between 20-65 years (random samples), field visits to some owners of the water wells, During October 2020 tow focus group was held one in Rafah area and the other in the Middle area with precautionary measures to help prevent the spread of COVID -19vwith the participation of MoA, women's associations' representatives. 44 small-headed household farmers (19 females and 25 males) with different marital status, their age between 20-65 years, this age group have been selected because they form the most economically active age group, two of the participant is people with disabilities, Some of them are landowners or land hold and some of them work with their relatives in their father's or grandfather's land ownership. Participants concluded their needs as follows: 1.(95) % of the participants focused on the low productivity of open fields and the greenhouses due to man-made and natural disasters. They focused on the necessity of increasing their land production capacity as well as protecting their plants from Insects and weather fluctuations. 2. (80)% of participants indicated agriculture water availability and accessibility issues as an urgent need due to the high price of cubic meters of water (about 2.5 NIS for one M3). 3. All the interviewed agricultural water wells' owners complained about the high cost of operating their wells by fuel. 4. All participants confirm the sales remained low and the poultry breeders were affected by COVID-19 the market access was limited and sporadic amidst the restoration of movement restrictionand Milk producers no longer didn't find a place to sell their milk. Their cows were always in need of the fodders, and other veterinary medications. 5. The people with disabilities indicate that they are actually in a bad economic situation and their needs for the source of income for secure food to their families while most of the income is spent on healthcare and they could not invest in the rehabilitation of their farms. 6. Females focused on their rights to benefit from agricultural assets of their fathers or grandfathers - equal with the males. Additionally, according to the COVID-19 Emergency Situation Report 19 mention, the Agriculture and livelihoods continue to be severely affected in the Gaza Strip: while traders had inputs in stock, these remained partially inaccessible due to movement restrictions. Farmers and breeders are buying on credit, and traders suffer from insufficient financial liquidity, placing them at risk of losing their ability to import agricultural inputs. To conclude, the farmers have horrible damage and their needs have never been changed since 2019, and their increase in the number of affected and vulnerable farmers of which they accumulated debts. The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities.

2 - Activities

This intervention aims to increase the production capacity of the agricultural assets owned by the small male and female farmers who are most vulnerable to humanitarian crises in Rafah, Khan Younis, and Deir Al Balah. The activities are designed in coordination with MoA and FSS to eliminate the duplication of the beneficiaries from other agriculture projects, and Implementation of the activities will be taking with health measures to prevent the outbreak of COVID-19. the selection beneficiaries criteria based on the identified needs, gender equity, sensitivity without any bias, and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.to ensuring equal benefit. Although traditionally most farmers are male, But attention will be paid to targeting farms headed by women or those that employ women, this project will target women farmers who own or rent or hold agriculture assets from their fathers or husbands. Al Nakheel and their partners will encourage women to apply to benefit from the project activities In addition to Al Nakheel will announce the priority will be for the female farmer and the female beneficiaries will get additional marks when evaluating the needs and selecting the beneficiaries on other hand the legal opinion will be consulted to ensure minimum risks on the implementation is likely to happen.Furthermore will be verified by the local committees to ensure that selected 35 female farmers having the support documents that proving the female farmers and these agricultural assets are holding it. in addition, the final list will be developed and passed to the MoA to get their feedback on the selected beneficiaries. Activity # 1: Rehabilitation of agricultural assets for 180 farming households (at least 20 female farmers headed household and their age between 20-65-year-old). The project will create opportunities for vulnerable and poor farmers, two dunums for each farmer to rehabilitation 360 dunums( 100 Rafah, 160 Khan Younis, 100 Middle Area), through tilling the soil in addition, to fertilize the soil with natural manure on 6 cubic meters per dunums and extension of irrigation network for each dunum and providing vegetable seeds. Output 1# 360 dunums of high productivity soil and 1800 tons at least of vegetables. Activity # 2: Rehabilitation of greenhouses for 100 farming households (at least 10 female farmers headed household and their age between 20-65-year-old).The project will create opportunities for vulnerable and small scall farmers, one dunum greenhouse for each farmer to rehabilitation 100 greenhouses ( 30 Rafah, 40 Khan Younis, 30 Middle Area), through the agricultural inputs for one dunum (1000 m2) greenhouses ( nylon, irrigation system, Beechwood, Wood gutters, seeds, seedlings ). Output #2: 100 greenhouse rehabilitated and of high productivity soil and 1200 tons at least of vegetables. Activity 1 & 2 will be responded to by the cash modality. The cash distribution will be done in three installments as follows: - 40 % first installment - 40 % second installment - 20 % final installment. The cash distribution will be through ATM, The beneficiaries will receive electronic cards and will be issued to the beneficiaries after their selection, and they will be delivered to them by the bank employee, as coordination will be made with the bank to deliver the cards to the beneficiaries in a safe way. Activity # 3: Rehabilitation of water wells. Partial Rehabilitation for operating four agricultural wells to improve the efficiency of discharge water and installing renewable energy for the agricultural water wells. (1 Rafah, 2 Khan Younis, 1 Middle Area) each well will serve 20 farmers; this activity will target 80 farmers (at least 8 female farmers and 72 males of the headed household farmers). Outputs#3: improving water access to 400 dunums at a low cost. Activity # 4: Rehabilitation of 50 water ponds. The plastic water harvesting ponds rehabilitated with plastic sheeting, shadow net, safety fence installing renewable energy ( 25 Rafah, 25 Khan Younis ) - 50 farmers (at least 5 female farmers headed household and their age between 20-65-year-old). and 45 males of the headed household farmers). Outputs # 4: # M3 of water storage will be provided. Three local committees will be formed one for each area will ensure community participation in beneficiaries’ selection and identification of needs. the committees will be At least 50% of the members will be women. and representing people with disabilities ( PwD) by Local NGOs representative. The local committees will participate in planning, implementing, and monitoring, and evaluating the project activities. This will ensure good participation of women in all the project cycle. Each partner will be responsible for implementing activities in his area, in addition to Al Nakheel will be responsible for the project in general and the project activities will implement in an overlapping manner to ensure the completion of all project activities within 12 months.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is a key driver to maximize positive impact. The proposed project mainstreams protection, including: Al Nakheel and their partners follow the Code of Ethics and Conduct ensuring that the beneficiaries are treated with respect and dignity, and to prevent abuse. The Partners through its nature of work understand the concept of "do no harm" and consecrate their effort to provide the received an agricultural asset to the local community to strengthen peace among them. Therefore, the staff is highly recommended not to use their power during the project implementing stages and not to be an element of the conflict. And not to use this agricultural input to support their sides in the conflict to prevent harm to the community. Partners follow the Rights-Based Approach Manual as it deals with beneficiaries as rights holders rather than service recipients and empowering them to protect and claim their rights.Partners use an effective complaint system so that beneficiaries can give feedback on their treatment and quality of servicesPartners follow the humanitarian principle of DO Not Harm through preventing and activities that may cause negative outcomes on the target beneficiaries. The project will be adequately announced in the targeted communities using various outreach tools to give equal opportunities to benefit from the project.The targeted groups will be selected based on transparent selection criteria that ensure humanitarian assistance is delivered without bias, and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and vulnerabilities.The selection of beneficiaries will fulfill the transparent and clear criteria for the project to be reducing suffering, maintaining human dignity, and reach the most valuable people. Gender has been strongly incorporated into the design of the project. the partners adopted “Zero Tolerance” regarding any action causing GBV, all the staff members signed, and trained on GBV and harassment prevention policies PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse).The gender focal point in Al Nakheel will be responsible for monitoring and follow up on any Gender-related complaints. The gender risks facing project implementation are protection risks associated with cash programming in Gaza including an increase in domestic violence and/or intra-household power imbalances (i.e. who controls and spends the cash) and the exclusion of women or individuals with special needs in the process of awarding the cash (e.g. due to secondary social status and restrictions on movement) Mitigation measures include:- use of female staff members to make female household members more comfortable.- in-person visits if households are not able to collect cash.- a beneficiary orientation that informs male and female household members about the potential risks especially for female-headed households as well as an introduction to the Community Accountability and Reporting.- on a case-by-case basis, staff will conduct household visits for orientation.- training staff on the detection of potential protection risks.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project aims to provide service to the most vulnerable farmers with coordination with MoA and FSS according to a set of selection criteria ensuring equality, impartiality, and with no discrimination moreover, The project activities will be published and communicated in the targeted areas to ensure all farmer targets have equal impartial access to these activities. Al Nakheel and their partners will give equal opportunities to apply through the announcement of the project in the target area by various outreach tools including (printed announcement, social media pages, website, etc..) the registration will be through open applications to ensure registration of all potentially eligible breeders, the selection criteria will be included in the announcement. Additional priority in the selection will be given to the female-headed household as well as people with disabilities to empower them and strengthen their ability to face their challenges. This will be according to the following steps : 1. High coordination with MoA in order names of affected and registered in it and the FSS to make the cross-checking to avoid any duplication. 2. Explicit and crucial selecting criteria announced to the community. 3. Conduct workshops in the presence of local community representatives include women association, key persons, and farmer of both sexes and Al Nakheel and their partners representative. 4. Forming neighborhood committee to assist the project management in reviewing applications, intervene as necessary. 5. Preparing application form embodied the main items of selected criteria. 6.
