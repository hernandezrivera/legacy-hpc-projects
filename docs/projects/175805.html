Project Summary

Around 1.4 million South Sudanese children under five years old (U5) and another 483,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021. Continued conflict induced insecurity in Koch and Fangak counties pose security risks and create difficulty of movement from place to place. Flooding has contributed greatly to food security deterioration at household level and has impacted health and nutrition service provision at both locations. People in Fangak County continue to be displaced from low lands affected by flooding and have moved to relatively dry high lands. Nutrition center structures and WASH facilities in Fangak County are damaged by the ongoing flooding and rehabilitation of infrastructure is needed to continue providing services. IRNA conducted in Fangak county in early February 2021 showed, worsened Food insecurity. Many households in this county continue to be displaced and lose livestock assets due to flooding. Proxy GAM rate for Fangak county for 2021 calculated based on predictive modelling techniques is 17.8% whereas latest SMART survey shows SAM prevalence of 3.3% among children under five (ACF, Sept 2019). In Koch county, 5 WR run Nutrition sites were inaccessible since the start of the rainy season until late October 2020 and Boaw is still cut from the rest of the county due to the ongoing flooding. Proxy GAM rate for Koch county for 2021 calculated based on predictive modelling techniques is 17.8% and the latest Mass MUAC screening conducted by WR in Koch county revealed that a proxy SAM of 1.9% among children under 5 children.
To respond to the immediate needs of the population, WR will scale up community-based management of malnutrition (CMAM) programming and expand lifesaving nutrition services to reach hard to access areas and underserved communities. The project will target 17,188 as direct beneficiaries who show signs of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in children U5, MAM cases of PLWs and care givers of children U5 on Maternal Nutrition and Infant and Young Child Nutrition through MIYCN in 8 OTPs and TSFPs, as well as one outreach nutrition units in Koch County, Fangak County. This project will establish two new outreach units in Koch County to provide outreach nutrition services to communities with limited access due to distance away from static sites. In Fangak County, WR will be support 6 static Nutrition centers and two new Outreach Unit unit integrated into a mobile health unit to reach those who are displaced due to flooding. A gender-targeted and nutrition-sensitive service delivery approach will be used and Nutrition will be integrated with WASH, health and FSL where women headed beneficiaries will be prioritized for FSL and WASH support. Ultimately, the project will aim to immediately reduce morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable children and caregivers, while promoting longer-term recovery and resilience measures into their communities.
Activities will be monitored through well-coordinated supportive supervision strategies together with the County health and nutrition department, MOH and UN Agencies. Activities will be monitored by nutrition project staff with support from Juba office program team. Reports will be shared to nutrition cluster and other reporting partners. Community based compliant mechanism will be established with in the health facility and at community level to ensure accountability to communicate affected and communicate entitlement of nutrition products to beneficiaries

1 - Needs

Nutrition needs are high in all three locations given the compounding conditions of 2020, continued violence, extreme flooding, and the COVID-19 pandemic, which shut down certain services around the country. All of this has had an effect on the levels of food security and hence deteriorating nutrition rates. Given the high flood levels since mid-2020 to present, most WR-run Nutrition sites are inaccessible because of flood waters, all the more given their proximity to the Sudd. Nutrition center structures and WASH facilities have been destroyed by flooding and need to be rehabilitated for continued use. An initial rapid need assessment (IRNA) conducted in Fangak County in early February 2021 revealed the worsening food insecurity situation. WR staff conducted mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) screenings from a sample size of 187 children U5 (90 male; 97 female). Of those assessed, 21 children (11 male; 10 female) were SAM cases and 47 children (19 male; 28 female) were MAM cases. Many households in this county continue to be displaced and lose livestock assets due to flooding. The proxy GAM rate for Fangak County for 2021 calculated based on predictive modelling techniques is 17.8% whereas the latest SMART survey shows a SAM prevalence of 3.3% among children U5 (ACF, Sept 2019). In Koch County, 5 WR-run Nutrition sites were inaccessible since the start of the rainy season until late October 2020, and the Boaw Nutrition site is still cut off from the rest of the county due to the ongoing flooding. The proxy GAM rate for Koch County for 2021 calculated based on predictive modelling techniques is 17.8%. The latest MUAC screenings conducted by WR in Koch County revealed a proxy SAM of 1.9% among children U5. In Koch County, an IRNA conducted in August 2020 revealed that sanitation and hygiene practices were ‘poor’ in the county as most affected households do not have a pit latrine or hand washing facilities. These challenges coupled with ongoing flooding increase the chance of fecal contamination and therefore increasing the risk of repeated illnesses especially for those who are malnourished. Elsewhere, the ICCG mission in February 2020 revealed that Leah Village of Gany Payam lacks basic services including nutrition. Intercommunal conflict, conflict induced insecurity and flooding have all contributed to the displacement of families in Koch and Fangak counties from their original locations to other hard to reach areas. To improve the quality of nutrition interventions, WR is planning to scale up the program with approaches to reach every family affected by the humanitarian crisis.Regular training and supervision will continue to be provided to enhance the capacity of staff and ensure quality of service. COVID-19 restrictive measures and other related measures continue to negatively impact the economy and affect household food security levels. It is expected that the second wave of COVID-19 in the country might tighten the restriction measures and further deteriorate the national economy. This would continue to negatively impact household food security and increase malnutrition rates among children U5. WR has noted that COVID-19 measures are not strictly practiced by communities and WR has continued to strengthen risk communication messaging and create awareness at nutrition centers and during house-to-house visits. The COVID-19 pandemic has affected service provisions at the nutrition centers. Activities like group MIYCN counseling, and early childhood development through play has been halted for the time being.

2 - Project activities

WR is supporting OTP, TSFP, MIYCN and other nutrition sensitive interventions in 8 static Nutrition sites in Koch County and 9 static and one mobile outreach units in Fangak County. To respond to the needs of families displaced by flooding and those with limited access to services, WR will establish two integrated outreach units supporting Leah village in Gany Payam, Koch County where there is no existing health or Nutrition facility and villages which are affected by flooding and two integrated outreach units supporting families displaced by flooding in different villages in Fangak. To scale up the Nutrition service and improve quality of services, WR will rehabilitate 2 Nutrition sites in Koch and 6 sites in Fangak which are damaged due to flooding. Community-based MIYCN counseling and support approach will be used to improve optimal MIYCN practices. A total of 4,109 (205 Male: and 3,905 Female) caregivers children aged 0-23 months are targeted for MIYCN individual counseling. Awareness raising activities on COVID-19 prevention will be incorporated into facility and community prevention interventions at all locations and. SAM and MAM children U5 will be identified based on their nutritional status using national criteria of CMAM protocols, and WHO reference standards. Children 6-59 months who meet admission Criteria for SAM: MUAC< 11.5cm, and edema+ or ++ and for MAM: MUAC<12.5cm, without edema for children U5 and PLWs, MUAC<23 will be admitted to OTP and TSFP respectively. The project will target 935 (421 Male; 514 Female) children U5 through OTP and 4,387 (1,974 Male; 2,413 Female) children U5 and 2,399 PLWs through TSFP in 8 OTP and TSFP static Nutrition centers and 4 outreach Units in Koch and Fangak counties. Admitted children will receive medical investigation, and routine medication based on treatment protocol. Supplies for the OTP/TSFP program will be received from the Nutrition cluster core pipeline (UNICEF and WFP) and WR will ensure regular monitoring and early request of supplies. WR will continue supporting national immunization, Vitamin A Supplementation and deworming campaigns. MUAC screening will be done at facility and community level by project staff and CNVs. Mother MUAC self-referral will be strengthened by continuously building the capacity of mothers on MUAC measurement. WR will conduct one round mass of MUAC screenings in the post-harvest season in each location, and recommendations will be shared with the Nutrition Technical Working Group (NTWG). Supportive supervision at nutrition sites will be done by project staff, and refresher and on-the-job training will be given to 68 (39 Male; and 29 Female) project staff and CNVs to build their capacity. Reports will be collected, analyzed and shared. WR is piloting care group programming which integrates nutrition, health, WASH and FSL interventions to enhance knowledge and practice of care givers on infant and young child feeding (IYCF), hygiene promotion and maternal care. Care group promotors and volunteers will be recruited and trained regularly and deliver messages for groups of 8-10 mothers and caregivers every 2-4 weeks. Kitchen gardens, cooking demonstrations, and selecting admitted children and PLWs to be receipts of FSL programming activities will done. Hygiene messages will be incorporated into regular awareness raising activities in the nutrition program. Activities will be monitored through well-coordinated supportive supervision strategies jointly with the CHD, MOH and UN agencies. Nutrition project staff and Area Coordinator with support from the Juba office will also do internal monitoring. Reports will be shared with the Nutrition cluster and other reporting stakeholders. A community-based compliant mechanism will be established within the health facility and at community level to ensure accountability to affected populations and to communicate entitlement of nutrition products to beneficiaries

The indicators to measure this projects success under this objective will be; proportion of girls and boys admitted with SAM tested and treated for malaria. proportion of girls and boys admitted with acute malnutrition in nutrition sites provided with LLITN for malaria prevention, number of girls and boys admitted with severe acute malnutrition received WASH kits, nutrition sites in IPC 4 which have access to Water facilities, number of nutrition sites with kitchen gardens for demonstration, number of nutrition sites with Gender Based Violence (GBV) safety audits conducted at least once and number of GBV survivors supported and referred to GBV response services through nutrition sites.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief takes Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) seriously at all levels. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. WR will also build the capacity of the health facilities and nutrition sites to treat women and girls who are survivors of rape and other incidents of sexual or gender-based violence. For the community to report any incident of PSEA, WR will establish community-based beneficiary feedback mechanisms and will do consultation with community members and community leaders on how reporting should be done, to whom it should be reported and how the compliant is handled. The community will be clearly informed that any reported case will be kept confidential and will be dealt by responsible individuals at WR. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that program teams which consist of combinations of locally hired national staff, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. WR will continue to leverage its relationships to advocate for the safety of beneficiaries regardless of their tribe or political affiliation.WR will ensure that feedback mechanisms are safe, accessible, and the preferred option for beneficiaries by offering a variety of options. Beneficiaries will be sensitized on the different people to whom they can report an incident. By allowing for multiple choice contacts, the beneficiary may choose based on their comfort level.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

People living with disabilities are often discriminated against in a society. Health care access is especially critical to this demographic, yet they often are not able to seek any support from humanitarian actors because of stigmas. Through health project staff, WR will work to identify program beneficiaries with disabilities early on and help them get the necessary health support. Identified cases will be given priority attention and enrollment into any of the programs to get the appropriate service support needed. People living with disabilities might find it difficult to access health facilities and WR will ensure accessibility to the health facility is fully possible for people with disability. People who are not able to travel and need household visits and support will be identified based on their needs and will be followed-up with accordingly.Vulnerable individuals, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities are likely to be discriminated against in accessing nutrition services. Distance is a possible barrier to inclusion. The project team will ensure that these individuals are particularly targeted during registration. The project staff will be inducted on protection mainstreaming to ensure that inequalities are minimized, inclusion in project activities is enhanced and all population groups are protected from exploitation and abuse. This will be achieved by ensuring equitable gender inclusion, targeted inclusion of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups including children and elderly individuals. Community leaders, religious leaders and local leaders will be consulted and advised as to the focus of the program’s intervention related to the inclusion of people with disabilities, and will be requested to provide their full support in WR interventions related to case identification and referral. They will be requested to participate in meetings and group awareness raising sessions. Beneficiaries living with disabilities will also be consulted to give feedback on the service provision and will be part the planning and service improvement process. Using the project staff, project report will disaggregate data of persons living with disabilities and keep record to be shared with relevant actors.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meeting with the volunteers and community-based committees as well as focus group discussions. WR sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meetings at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plans and opportunities, community concerns, and risks. This feedback is discussed with the management team in the country office and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs. A variety of mechanisms are used to give beneficiaries a choice in how to provide inputs and express concerns. • First, local staff hired at the community level, are trained on how to receive feedback from participants, serving both as staff and as trusted community members. • Second, program management staff at the field level conduct regular Focus Group Discussions with project participants and relevant community members and leaders. To ensure the comfort of project beneficiaries, various discussion sessions are conducted with segregated groups of individuals with particular sex, age, and minority (e.g. non-dominating clan or sub-tribe) identities. • Third, CNVs/CHPs are able to receive feedback from participants one-on-one, both at the nutrition centers and during home visits in settings where participants may feel safer sharing their comments in the privacy of their own home. This also ensures that elderly, disabled, or other people confined to their homes have the opportunity to submit concerns. • Fourth, beneficiaries are informed of their right to submit complaints during community-level meetings in case they feel more comfortable in a group setting or directly to a WR staff member at a project site.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Local staff, clinical staff and the Area Coordinator will be able to immediately respond with small requested changes in the activities to improve the suitability and desired outcomes for the affected populations, based on their feedback. The Quality Specialist and the Program Director will also analyze monitoring data collected to suggest changes that can be made to improve the project, in collaboration with the Area Coordinator and Technical Advisors. Apart from this, during the project period community meetings will be conducted for influential community leaders, church leaders, the CHD, and women leaders to build trust and confidence toward the project and to get feedback from the community on the services delivery.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Women and young children particularly are at risk as a result of project activity implementation. Rape and sexual abuse when walking to nutrition sites is especially of concern. The project team will discuss with the Boma Nutrition Teams to ensure and encourage men at each household to escort women and girls in their travel to health facilities to reduce the potential for sexual and gender based violence and other protection concerns. As well, WR will ensure where possible that the Nutrition centers are an accessible distance to the targeted community, and the nutrition project team together with partners’ protection team will establish a system for effective community awareness and referral pathway. Children receiving malnutrition treatment are likely to be discriminated against at household level as the caregiver may redistribute or sell the RUTF provided to the child. The project team will ensure that these caregivers are particularly targeted and following maternal, infant and young child feeding counseling. Nutrition project staff through the care group program will also ensure caregivers of children U5 are fully targeted and followed-up on regularly via group meetings and individual household visits.Vulnerable individuals, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities are likely to be discriminated against in accessing nutrition services. Distance may be a possible barrier to inclusion. The project team will ensure that these individuals are particularly targeted during registration. The project will employ one protection officer and two assistants. Their role will be mainly to ensure inequalities are minimized, inclusion in project activities is enhanced and all population groups are protected from exploitation and abuse. This will be achieved by ensuring equitable gender inclusion, targeted inclusion of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups including children and elderly individuals. The project staff will be inducted on protection mainstreaming in addition to the above. With the recent surge in COVID-19 cases, the project will make sure the response takes into account preventive measures through procurement of PPE for frontline staff, increased community awareness through messaging and distribution of IEC materials, and screening of clients at all service points. Intensifying Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities, including where possible distribution of face mask to vulnerable populations, will be prioritizedSafety of beneficiaries is of paramount importance to project implementation. World Relief has a security focal point who works closely with other protection actions; a security risk analysis will be conducted to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries. This will be particularity so for distributions which act as pool factors and have a potential of exposing beneficiaries to attacks and injury. The Boma management committees will be pivotal in advising the safest mode and venue for training and inputs delivery. The project will conduct post distribution monitoring which will measure among other parameters how accessible the inputs were to beneficiaries. The project will measure timeliness of inputs, ease of access of beneficiaries to Nutrition centers and time taken in queuing for inputs. The project team will serve beneficiaries in the most respectful and dignified way possible. This includes respecting their culture and way of life. Where change in practice is envisaged, for instance in the use of different varieties, mode of cultivation or consumption of other foods, this will be explained in the most respectful manner and beneficiaries will be allowed to make their own decisions whether to adopt or ignore what has been availed to them.
