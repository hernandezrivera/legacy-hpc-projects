Project Summary

CARE intends to provide emergency food assistance complemented with emergency food production and livelihoods support to reach an estimated 157,693 people including 49 percent men and 51 per cent women of different age groups with and without disabilities in the targeted locations. The intervention would contribute to the strategic objectives of the HRP and all the three FSL cluster objectives to save lives through food assistance, promote emergency food production and livelihood support and enhance and maintain agro-pastoral community emergency coping capacity in South Sudan. CARE will deploy sets of proven approaches and outstanding experience focusing on linking emergency food security interventions with medium- and longer-term livelihoods and market-based approaches. CARE places women and girls at the front and center of all our work to enhance impacts of all interventions conducted across humanitarian and development projects. CARE beliefs than when women and girls have equal access to resources, they lift their families and communities out of poverty. In this project CARE will focus assistance to those who need them most including child headed households, persons with disabilities, women headed households within the areas of displacements, vulnerable host communities under IPC 3+ food insecurity levels, returnees and refugee caseloads. Households and vulnerable populations within the severity food insecurity areas of severely flooded and drought and conflict affected areas would be the priority locations of our project targets. In the programme cycle, CARE would provide emergency food assistance through cash and voucher (CVA) system to populations falling in the IPC 3+ and areas of extreme concerns including Jonglei, and Unity states complemented nutrition sensitive programming and kitchen gardening around the Nutrition facilities to promote appropriate feeding best practices. While in the areas of very high and high concerns respectively, CARE would provide emergency food production and early recovery livelihoods support and resilience programming to build absorptive and adaptive capacities of crises affected population. CARE International is a global facilitator of village savings and loan associations (VSLA) scheme for recovery and resilience building for humanitarian affected populations. In this cycle, CARE would diversify and restore the livelihoods of affected women, men, girls and boys including people with and without disabilities to enable them to access an alternative source of capital investments and improve savings. In the Equatoria, particularly Eastern Equatoria (Greenbelt) and Western Bahr el Ghazala, CARE will promote the Farmer Field and Business School (FFBS) approaches to provide capacity building supports to smallholder farmers, herders and producer groups to increase their productivity, improve resilience, adapt to climate change, diversify diets, and boost nutrition. CARE’s FFBS system is a participatory, women-focused extension approach that helps women farmers build skills necessary to increase production; access markets and sell at competitive prices; collaborate with each other; and engage in beneficial and efficient decision-making processes. It also transforms the status and recognition of women by providing the support they require to be successful farmers, businesspeople, leaders, and agents of change. The VSLA saving model will be integrated across all the FBBS and producer group systems.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, since 2019/2020, trends of severity and magnitude of food insecurity have worsened pushing many communities and households above IPC 3+ food insecurity thresholds. The protracted crisis since December 2013 and consecutive years of flooding, severe localized conflict events and continued indirect impact of COVID 19 pandemic had significantly limited commercial and humanitarian flows of food commodities and services, while current economic crisis (food commodity price hikes) is impacting people’s ability to access markets. Increased sub-national violence in many parts of the country, particularly in Jonglei, Unity, Western Equatoria (Tumbura) and Eastern Equatoria (Magwi) states have continued to disrupt economic livelihoods, market and agro-based farming opportunities. The ration cuts by WFP in 2021 from 70 to 50% and the reduction of duration of food rations from 12 to 9 months have affected IDPs the most due to funding gaps that have also disrupted people’s livelihoods and food security. According to the recent Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) sectoral analysis, an estimated 8.3 million (including the refugees) people are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022. This indicates an increase of 7 per cent above the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. The counties with greatest severity in food insecurity amplified by compounded shocks and areas of extreme concerns in 2022 include: Canal/Pigi (10%), Fangak (35%), Pibor (10%) all in Jonglei, and Leer (5%), Mayendit (5%) in Unity state and Tumbura (15%) in Western Equatoria. Several counties in Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria, Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap states are also in extreme concern, very high and high concern respectively according to the report. The main drivers of severity of food insecurity are floods and localized dry spells, inflation and high food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence and household stressors (such as death of the bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services), which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. In South Sudan, 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry. Climate induced shocks (floods events, localized dry spells and conflicts for grazing areas and farming) have increased violent conflicts among agropastoral communities and have led to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the agricultural productivity. Among the most affected people are child headed households, persons with disabilities, older people and women headed households. South Sudan ranks amongst the top ten countries in the world most vulnerable to climate impact, including droughts and flooding. Flooding has been a major driver of displacement followed by conflicts. An estimated 835,000 people in 33 out of 78 counties in 8 states between May and December 2021 were reportedly displaced and more than 37,000 tons of crops were destroyed and nearly 800,000 livestock were killed. Households in the severely flooded areas of Unity (208,000), Jonglei (278,000) and Upper Nile (126,000) will require high levels of support in 2022. Similarly, households in the most drought affected pastoral and conflict affected farmers in Eastern Equatoria (Greater Kapoeta) and Pibor areas would also require high levels of support inform of emergency food assistance complemented with emergency livelihoods and livestock support across farming, livestock and fishing communities for medium and longer term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity to mitigate such extreme levels of food insecurity. As of December 2021, there are 2 million displaced persons across South Sudan either due to flooding or conflicts.

2. Project activities

Result 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for 18,398 vulnerable households (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) including 110,385 men, women, boys, girls of different ages and people with and without disabilities in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines). 1.1. Conduct community consultations for needs verification/prioritization and beneficiaries’ selection process 1.2. Beneficiaries' identification, verification and registration exercise 1.3. Rapid Market assessment 1.4. Form project management and targeting committees 1.5. Food/cash distribution to the most vulnerable households (restricted, unconditional cash) 1.6. Conduct PDM.Result 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support to 5,257 IDPs, Host communities, returnees and refugees’ households consisting of 31,539 men, women, boys, girls, elderly people and people with and without disabilities in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines). 2.1. Beneficiaries identification and registration 2.2. Conduct livelihood inputs availability assessment 2.3. Distribute crop, vegetable and fishing kits (direct distribution and input trade fairs) 2.4. Conduct small ruminants (shoats) restocking 2.5. Train on livelihoods (crop, vegetable and fishing) 2.6. Sensitize households (esp. women) on messages regarding proper nutrition, hygiene and protection, especially for lactating / pregnant women and for children 2.7. Conduct PDM. Result 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen 2,628 IDPs, Host communities, returnees and refugees’HHs ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines). 3.1. Conduct community consultation and community based participatory planning 3.2. Beneficiaries identification, verification and registration 3.3. Community and household assets creation process 3.4. Formation of voluntary saving and loan associations and training on IGAs and VSLA approaches 3.5. Provision of startup grants to IGAs and VSLAs 3.6. Formation of farmers’ producer groups and training on GAP, NRM through FFBS. 3.7. Capacity building of government extension workers through refresher trainings 3.8. Training on draft animal technology (ox-plough) and supply of ploughs to farmers’ groups 3.9. Conduct rapid market assessment 3.10. Provide unconditional cash/food to support household assets creation 3.11. Conduct training for women groups in nutrition-sensitive and climate resilient agriculture techniques. 3.11 Establish demonstrations and kitchen gardening around existing Nutrition facilities to promote nutrition sensitive feeding and social behavior change.
