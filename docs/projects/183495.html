Project Summary

In 2021, Palestine refugees, already suffering with the humanitarian and socio-economic impacts of the 15-year blockade, were confronted with the additional impacts of the May hostilities, which lasted for 11 days, and the persistence of the COVID-19 pandemic
Having witnessed death and injury, damage or loss of property, IDPs faced trauma and require psychosocial support, with coping mechanisms already eroded by years of blockade and previous experiences of violence. The majority of IDPs reported the need for further MHPSS support. Of the 98,500 children and youth who participated in UNRWA’s summer activities for children conducted in the July 2021, 8.7% were in need of additional mental health care.
While much of the explosive ordnance from the escalation has been removed, the potential for renewed conflict remains high, giving rise to the need for further ERW Risk and Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) messaging.
To mitigate and respond to the protection concerns and risk factors arising from the escalation of hostilities as well concerns exacerbated by COVID-19 (including GBV, CP, MHPSS), UNRWA will seek to increase its protection capacity through a series of multifaceted initiatives focusing on MHPSS, child protection, GBV and ERW risk reduction.
Underpinning the initiatives will be a significantly increased focus on coordination with the Protection cluster and a community based approached that includes Palestine refugees as partners in decision making.

1 - Needs

In 2021, Palestine refugees, already suffering with the humanitarian and socio-economic impacts of the 15-year blockade, were confronted with the additional impacts of the May hostilities, which lasted for 11 days, and the persistence of the COVID-19 pandemic. The blockade of the Gaza Strip has entailed tight control over all aspects of life since 2007, severely restricting the movement of people as well as goods. These restrictions limit the opportunities for people living in Gaza to realize basic human rights and development standards, including the realisation of the right to health. Years of socioeconomic decline, conflict and blockade have have caused tremendous levels of destruction of physical infrastructure, loss of human life, and have contributed to serious deterioration of the social fabric. The Protection Cluster HNO highlights that already high levels of psychosocial stress, especially among children,have been further exacerbated in Gaza by the pandemic and escalation. GBV, Child Protection and MHPSSAs noted in the Protection Cluster HNO, “during 2021, child protection needs in the oPt have been compounded by the continuing COVID-19 pandemic, as well as an increase in conflict-related violence during the May 2021 escalation in Gaza. Against the backdrop of multi-sectoral and household vulnerabilities contributing to child protection risks, there are considerable needs for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) interventions including specialized case management, and approaches targeting adolescents and caregivers, particularly males. The Protection Cluster MSNA reports that large swathes of households in Khan Yunis and North Gaza report psychosocial distress. Separate from the escalation, the protracted protection crisis has led to a multitude of protection concerns and issues, including but not limited as regards GBV, Child Protection and an MHPSS. Children exposed to conflict-related violence including detention remain a most vulnerable group. The Protection Cluster MSNA reports that of 11% households report that women and girls avoid areas because they feel unsafe there. PSEA remains a heightened risk. To mitigate the psychosocial pressure, poverty and environmental degradation affecting many children in Gaza as a result of conflict, blockade and severe socio-economic conditions, UNRWA in 2021, with support from the United Nations Children Fund (UNICEF), launched a summer and after-school sport and recreational activities programme under the title ‘Keeping Kids Cool’ (KKC) targeting 150,000 children. The project aimed to support the children in Gaza, to strengthen their physical, mental and emotional well-being and to build their resilience.Of the 98,500 children and youth who participated in KKC activities, 8.7% were in need of additional mental health care. Reports received through UNRWA’s Social Services hotlines confirm the Protection HNO Narrative’s assertion that “access to GBV services – already compromised throughout the oPt by the occupation and COVID-19 restrictions - has been weakened by the recent escalation”. In its second situation report (May 2021). UNFPA notes it is essential to ensure continuity of essential GBV response services such as MHPSS and health services. ERW risk awarenessAs noted in the Protection HNO Narrative and UNMAS, the recent escalation in Gaza has left severe contamination of explosive remnants of war (ERW), explosive ordnance and exacerbated an already volatile living space with ERW from past escalations. Damage to basic infrastructure in Gaza is severely affecting the provision of electricity, piped water and essential services. Explosive ordnance which has failed to explode, and now lies buried in the rubble of damaged buildings or in farmland and poses an immediate risk to the population. Children out playing are also particularly vulnerable, as well as internally displaced persons as they are situated in locations that are less familiar and may not have the same access to normal community communications, which might share ERW locations when they are identified. There is an urgent need to ensure that humanitarian actors and the general population receive risk education to reduce risk from ERW. Mine action partners have previously noted a marked decrease in sensitization towards ERW contamination, highlighting the need for intervention. In addition, the potential for renewed conflict remains high, giving rise to the need for further ERW Risk and Conflict Preparedness and Protection (CPP) messaging, especially but not limited to children.

2 - Activities

In line with key needs highlighted in the Protection Cluster HNO and MSNA, to mitigate and respond to targeted protection concerns and risk factors arising from the escalation of hostilities as well concerns exacerbated by COVID-19 and the general protection crisis in Gaza, UNRWA will seek to increase its protection capacity through a series of multifaceted initiatives focusing on child protection, GBV and ERW risk reduction. Protection response mechanisms in place to prevent and mitigate effects of conflict related violence: Child Protection and MHPSS response The below interventions will be premised upon the inclusive participation of women, men, girls and boys as partners, including the elderly and people with disabilities in needs assessments and specific design and targeting of interventions. Pre-existing youth Committees, Parent Councils, Womens Programme Centres, UNRWA school parliaments will be engaged in this process. Where gaps exist, particularly in the area of people with disabilities and the elderly, UNRWA will engage local community members to ensure participation. Gender, age and disability targets/indicators to be further finessed after needs assessment completed. Geographic areas of focus tbd based on MSNA analysis, current UNRWA data and planned needs assessment. The MSNA notest that 14% of all households reported having at least one household member with a physical and/or cognitive difficulty. Disability target set at 14%. Through training of its staff and expanded coordination with Protection Cluster and sub-cluster members, UNRWA will expand its case management and referral system mechanisms across all programmes in general and schools and health centres in particular, special attention will be given to child protection and GBV initiatives. Children experiencing high and critical domestic protection risks such as GBV and child abuse, as well as children severely affected by conflict related violence, in particular, will receive targeted child protection interventions including, but not limited to PSS and external referrals. 700 children and caregivers affected by conflict related violence and violence in households will benefit from child protection interventions (300 boys, 150 girls, 150 women, 100 men, including 98 children with disabilities). 400 children affected by conflict related violence benefitting from specialized individual case management (200 boys and 200 girls, including 28 children with disabilities). UNRWA will, as possible, coordinate closely with active CP AoR partners providing specialized child protection case management services to bridge the gap in service provision and complement UNRWA’s services. 250 UNRWA non-protection specialist front-line staff will receive training on protection mainstreaming principles, protection interventions, and referral systems focusing on child protection and GBV will be conducted (100 men and 150 women). 200 non-GBV and non-child protection specialists will receive training, including on remote service delivery, contingency planning and/or GBV Minimum Standards. Protection and Disability Mainstreaming UNRWA will establish four area based Protection Mainstreaming Working Groups across the Gaza Strip aimed at strengthening protection mainstreaming (inclusive of disability) throughout UNRWA humanitarian response and service delivery. All forty staff involved in the Area Protection Mainstreaming Working Groups will receive training in protection mainstreaming. Explosive Remnants of War risk awareness UNRWA will expand its ERW risk education by training its emergency shelter staff and key school staff and will expand its partnership with UNMAS to meet its objectives. 100 staff will receive ERW Risk Training and through a training of trainers programme, over 100,000 students will be trained (50 men and 50 women, including 14 PwD).Up to 100,000 IDPs will receive awareness raising in case of future conflict (disaggregation tbd based on assessment of where highest risk is). Advocacy 25 advocacy initiatives to highlight and raise awareness on the protection context affecting Palestine refugees will be provided. Monitoring and Evaluation UNRWA’s Monitoring and Evaluation Unit and implementing partners will be engaged to design a framework of monitoring evaluation, including regular in person visits to project locations by M+E and UNRWA Protection staff. UNRWA will employ surveys, focus group discussions and interviews with beneficiaries to monitor the progress and results of the activities. Feedback will be incorporated into the project cycle. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach will be further undertaken from project design to review by project staff to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.PSEA UNRWA commits to all mandatory activities with the following: 1. The adoption of a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. 2. UNRWA has trained PSEA focal points to receive complaints in multitude of ways, including a 24/7 Whatsapp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. UNRWA commits to all mandatory. PSEA focal point and UNRWA leadership a regular and contributing member to PSEA network. 3. PSEA awareness raising material will be disseminated to all project recipients. In addition to mandatory requirements, UNRWA will employ additional initiatives: project staff will be trained and all trainings will included a a targeted component on PSEA. UNRWA regularly engage with HCT oPt PSEA network and project staff will engage with UNRWA’s representative to this body via training and through project monitoring.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

UNRWA is unique among United Nations agencies in directly providing essential services, mainly primary education, primary health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement and microfinance. Of the two million Palestinians living in the besieged Gaza Strip, some 70 per cent are refugees, dependent on UNRWA for humanitarian assistance. Given its mandate of direct service delivery, the most effective way to reach beneficiaries and address the protection risks described is through direct service provision by staff such as Teachers, Counsellors, Social Workers, Nurses and Doctors.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Generally, in UNRWA's Protection Strategy the prioritization of the do no harm principle is explicitly stated as the key protection standard of safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit includes extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. Specifically in relation to this project, UNRWA will collect disaggregated data (by gender or specific groups) as part of project monitoring to detect the effects of activities on inequality or exclusion in these groups; introduce specific DNH indicators into the project monitoring framework, depending on the project and its context; disseminate Do No Harm tools; invoke its well established process during its monitoring and documentation procedures of child protection and gbv cases of obtaining informed consent (and assent in the case of minors) for recording and use of information that affects them. UNRWA will employ an enhanced risk matrix prior to embarking on any individual response deemed high risk or critical. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. Finally, UNRWA developed guidelines for remote documentation and monitoring and has also established guidelines to prioritise field work in the context of COVID-19 risks.
