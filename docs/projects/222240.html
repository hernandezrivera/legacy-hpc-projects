Project Summary

This project will target returnees, vulnerable host community members and IDPs to have access to agricultural inputs (tools and seeds) specifically short season, flood adapted and drought tolerant seeds. This support will enable the beneficiaries to produce short term varieties of crop to support their household’s food needs.
The project activities will focus on emergency food production, postharvest handling and building the resilience of the hosts and displaced persons through VSLA and nutrition messaging. The crop seeds delivered will be multiplied in the community. For the environment protection, solar irrigation pumps will be promoted under organized farmer groups.
Malteser International will conduct vulnerability mapping with the involvement of Payams representatives and local community leaders (Boma Chiefs and villages Chiefs).
The project will target 1,250 beneficiaries’ households (3,750 persons): 1,250 hhs with agricultural support and 150 households in disaster risk reduction and anticipatory approach. The beneficiary’s selection will be based on criteria such as: returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host community (widows; disable persons…) at household level. The project will train farmers on good agronomic practices and climate smart farming techniques. Additional awareness raising will be conducted on Mother-infant -young and child nutrition to promote good nutrition.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. Since April 2023, over 860,000 individuals have fled the Sudan crisis into South Sudan, majorly transiting through Renk, where the two collective centers are heavily overcrowded. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above.The deterioration in food insecurity will be driven by the depletion of cereal stocks by majority of households, a rise in staple food prices due to low local supply to markets and inaccessibility to markets during rainy season, limited income-earning opportunities, persistent macro-economic challenges in the country. The deterioration in food security is compounded by the impact of the ongoing crisis in Sudan and its effects on supplies to markets and the influxes of returnees and refugees in Upper Nile. The state is considered the main entry for majority of returnees from Sudan, and this is likely to put more burden on the local communities’ resources. The deterioration in the food security situation is likely to be mitigated by the availability of some wild foods, fish, and increased access to livestock products such as milk as animals are brought closer to the homesteads.
