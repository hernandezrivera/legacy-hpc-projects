Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to continue answering the most urgent humanitarian
needs along the contact line while looking for medium to long term solutions to ensure an
improved access to quality GBV response services for the most vulnerable people affected
by the protracted crisis in Eastern Ukraine (Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast).
The project will particularly target vulnerable women and girls, affected by the on-going
conflict by ensuring access to GBV prevention and response services.
The objectives are as follow:
- Increased availability and access to essential GBV services for conflict
affected population in Governmental Controlled Areas of Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast
- Improved capacities of health staff and other local service providers for the provision of
support to GBV victims and of MHPSS services to conflict-affected men and women, in
GCA of Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast
- Improved awareness of the population on GBV and risks
- Strengthened referral pathways for GBV survivors

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine constitute a significant humanitarian crisis with at least 1.5 million internally displaced persons, two-thirds of whom are women (GoU Ministry of Social Policy – January 2019). Women make up 66% of IDPs (UNHCR 2015). Many young men have left the country to escape conscription (Spear et al. 2016, p. 22) and many women lost their husbands in the conflict, increasing the number of female-headed households. Higher levels of poverty are experienced by households with children (one in three is below the poverty line), single mothers with young children, unemployed women, women in rural areas, and women over 75 (Spear et al, 2016, p. 60). Differences in payment between men and women have led to differences in pensions, to the detriment of older women. On the other hand, there has been a tendency for women to return to more traditional gender roles and patriarchal models of marital relations as a survival strategy (Ukrainian Centre for Social Reforms 2016, pp. 6-7) and many women have become the only carers for children, the elderly and the disabled and other domestic roles, decreasing their freedom and livelihoods. Domestic violence remains largely a hidden problem and incidents frequently go unreported (UNFPA 2015). The conflict has also undermined the ability of males to respond to social expectations and have led to increases in harmful behaviours such as alcohol consumption, drug addiction and high-risk sexual activities, resulting in increasing reports of domestic violence (UNOCHA 2015, p. 10). Reliable statistics on the prevalence of GBV are not readily available. This may be in part due to the lack of a clear legal framework, especially for sexual violence other than rape. A very important step towards strengthening actions aimed at eliminating violence against women and girls in Ukraine is the ratification of the Council of Europe Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence against Women and Domestic Violence (Istanbul Convention) by Ukraine. The Convention was signed in 2011 but has not yet been ratified, thus Ukraine is not legally bound. According to the UNFPA survey carried out in 2015, there is no common practice to seek assistance from specialized institutions in cases of violence. The overwhelming numbers of survivors have not applied for psychological or legal support, neither have they applied for medical assistance. The main reason was, according to respondents, unawareness of the availability of services, absence of proper institutions in communities and mistrust in service personnel. Some expectations of help were expressed for the assistance of psychologists and representatives of NGOs and women’s support groups. Experience of Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) is leading to significant health issues among survivors (12% of married women reported some sort of physical injury perpetrated by their partners), including issues of reproductive health. Population awareness on available services for GBV survivors and specific service providers remains low; there is no proper planning of safety strategies – almost a half of IPV survivors do not know how to deal with a repeat situation. Thus, there is a need for awareness raising campaigns, targeted at disseminating information on safety planning and coping strategies for IPV. The UNFPA assessment of the needs in improving GBV policies provided grounds for recommendations on institutional policy reforms, capacity building of multi-sectoral service providers, improving the system of support of GBV survivors and expansion of information campaigns for those at risk of GBV through awareness raising programs (safety planning, support of the 24-hour hot lines at the regional level, fostering the practice of applying for psychological and social support) and educative work with disadvantaged families and vulnerable population groups.
