Project Summary

The conflict has broken down solid waste management services with trash now commonly piled in heaps or discarded in nearby valleys. The accumulation of rubbish, coupled with the lack of public services for rubbish removal is becoming a threat to public health, deteriorating the health and environmental situation and further exacerbating difficult living conditions. To address this need, EPDC will support solid waste management systems. Building on previous successful solid waste management project in Rural Damascus and support of more than 800,000 people with waste management and health awareness campaign, EPDC is suggesting to duplicate its intervention and use cash-for-work to provide solid waste removal and health awareness campaigns in 8 vulnerable municipalities in Dar'a

Needs

Continued destruction of the economy and cessation of trade has led to high levels of unemployment; the unemployment rate was estimated at 53 per cent in 2015 but has increased the last two years. Repeated shocks, including multiple displacements and reductions in livelihood opportunities, are contributing to an uncertain future for the majority of Syria’s population. As a result, people are resorting to harmful food and livelihoods-based coping strategies that often exacerbate the protection risks households are exposed to. Harmful coping mechanisms include spending savings, borrowing money, reducing non-food items, and depleting productive assets. There are reports of high proportion of households engaging in irreversible coping strategies to meet food needs, including selling property or land and engaging in high risk activities. In rural communities, households are no longer able to make a living through agriculture alone due to low productivity and incomes, as well as poor access to cultivable areas due to insecurity.It is estimated that 9.8 million people are in acute need of early recovery and livelihood support and an estimated three in four people of working age are not involved in any income generating activities. The proportion of the population in Syria living in extreme poverty (less than US$2 per day) doubled from almost 34% pre- crisis to approximately 69% today.The conflict has broken down solid waste management services with trash now commonly piled in heaps or discarded in nearby valleys. As in many cases in Dar'a, the movement of IDPs has exponentially increased per capita waste disposal per person, per day. The accumulation of rubbish, coupled with the lack of public services for rubbish removal is becoming a threat to public health. To address this need, EPDC will support solid waste management systems in the camps.

Implementation of activities

Activity 1: Solid waste removal and management through cash for work modality Based on meeting with the governorate office, and mayors of Dar'a municipalities, and after assessing needs in selected area, the project will provide solid waste management services to the selected 8 municipalities in Dar'a, building on EPDC Education and Health intervention in the area. This will be conducted by subcontracting the removal of household rubbish daily from community bins. 181 Daily laborers, using cash-for-work modality, will collect garbage daily and remove rubbish from the streets, yards, and the rubbish collection area for 6 months. Laborers are given masks, boots, and gloves to ensure their safety when conducting garbage collection, and will be provided with training on safety measures during the cleaning process. The project will also ensure foggy pesticide is sprayed in relevant locations in the municipality, significantly reducing the proliferation of the harmful flying and creeping insects and rodents. Finally, health awareness campaigns will be conducted in each village on health topics such as proper disposal of waste, personal and community cleanness for project sustainability. This will be conducted in several sessions to maximize the population's participation with special focus on women participation. By doing so, the project aims at achieving better health outcomes for the population, and mitigate the risk of respiratory and dermatological diseases

Protection Risk Analysis

EPDC will be assessing in advance and adopt mitigation measures, in terms of selection criteria and to minimize the risks, these should include adequate distributions of employment chances, including the involvement of local authorities where appropriate. Livelihood projects might trigger GBV and domestic violence, measures to prevent, monitor and respond to gender-based violence or sexual exploitation associated with livelihood will be considered and relevant considerations may include involvement of family intervention rather than individual to minimize exposing women and girls to GBV within their families. To prevent improper use of cleaning chemicals and chlorine, EPDC will ensure proper storage and that labels and instructions on their usage is in a language or pictorial form that is understood by users. Bbeneficiaries and personal data will be confidential and safe from unintended use – EPDC will apply effective data protection measures. Health awareness campaigns messages will be reviewed to ensure cultural appropriateness. Physical risk due to waste removal might occur thus protective equipment will be provided as a mitigation measure. Moreover, placement of female staff to conduct assessments and communicate the health campaign with women and girls. Safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation (that have been committed by community members and/or staff). This will reduce the potential for exploitation by staff, as well as providing a greater opportunity for protection issues to be picked up by a broader range of staff with different expertise.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: ● Conflict lines shift due to conflict activities, affecting the safety/mobility of program staff and areas of proposed intervention● Loss of support from the local governing authorities or councils● Beneficiaries do not want to participate in the project● Duplication or gaps in activities due to miscommunication amongst humanitarian agencies and stakeholders● High fluctuations in prices of items needed for the project as the waring context escalates● Limited availability of medicines ● Delays with distributions caused by checkpoints/delays in obtaining necessary approvals● Lack of full time medical staff and specialization Assumptions:● The local security situation remains stable within this current time and beneficiaries will not face large scale displacements from the target areas.● The local governing authorities support the project in a continuous manner● Coordination and communication lines with other agencies, other services and with local authorities stay in place● Acceptance and engagement of targeted communities. It is assumed that through investment of resources, sound project design and use of appropriate techniques, the benefits of the action will be accepted by the community. Mitigation:• EPDC will implement a robust contingency plan to fluidly relocate selected vulnerable project locations in the event of a shift in security context which could compromise the selected areas.• EPDC will continue to utilize their strong relationship and reputation with the Ministry of Health and Directorate of Health to identify other opportunities although the risk is deemed very low.• EPDC will meet local government officials on a regular basis and update them on the progress of activity implementation. They will also involve them if there are any modifications from the initial plan.• EPDC will disseminate information about projects within the community and will also organize community events to build acceptance and provide services, in coordination with local authorities.• EPDC will remain an active member of the coordination system at the national level led by UN OCHA. At the local level, EPDC will continue to coordinate with humanitarian actors already implementing projects in the areas of operation.• EPDC continues to strengthen its on-site Field Monitoring capacity to ensure a standardized but flexible approach for verifying programmatic and operational aspects of each hub-level program
