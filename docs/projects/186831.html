Project Summary

OCHA plays a critical role in monitoring humanitarian developments at territorial level; coordinating the response and providing key analysis to inform decision making. The current coordination architecture includes 12 Local Coordination Teams (LCTs) and 3 Humanitarian local Teams (HLTs) in the border departments of Guanía and Vichada with OCHA leading five of these LCTs. OCHA also provides information management support to LCTs in other areas led by other UN agencies. Information gathered from all territories is centralized and analyzed regularly to generate information products related to the various emergences taking place in the departments and the status of the response. OCHA leads inter-cluster coordination efforts, a new Access Working Group as well as an inter-agency working group to analyse displacement and confinement figures which are published monthly. OCHA also co-leads a working group that analyses conflict dynamics and the classification of armed groups. OCHA also supports the HCT in strategic decision making. It maintains close coordination with the Refugee and Migrants Response coordination mechanism in country to ensure coordinated actions as well as with the RCO and the Peace Mission to facilitate linkages between humanitarian, development and peace building efforts in country. OCHA also maintains close relations with the OCHA office in Venezuela and the focal points in Peru and Equator, particularly in relation to analysis of cross-border dynamics and coordination of some response efforts. OCHA will continue to play a key role in planning, coordinating and monitoring the COVID-19 response together with WHO. Furthermore, OCHA supports the coordination of the response to natural disasters with the state’s National Unit for Disaster Risk Management to complement Governments efforts and fill potential gaps in the response. In 2021 OCHA will maintain its presence with coordination staff in 11 departments - those most affected by humanitarian crises.

3.1.1. Provea brevemente información al respecto - máximo 1.000 carácteres

OCHA places a high value on the involvement of local stakeholders in the Humanitarian Programme Cycle, conducting regional workshops in the most affected regions to design a response based on the actual needs on the ground. As a coordination body, OCHA's beneficiaries are the 130 humanitarian actors involved in the response and that are part of coordination mechanisms at national and territorial level. Other beneficiaries include local and national institutions as well as the broader international community, which benefits from OCHA's information and communication efforts.

3.2.1. Provea brevemente información al respecto - máximo 1.000 carácteres

To mobilise a response to all people in need, OCHA constantly analyses the dynamics in the territories, establishing a presence where new needs arise and coordinating the response to the people that are potentially left behind by an insufficient institutional and/or humanitarian presence.

4 - CENTRALIDAD DE LA PROTECCION
4.1. ¿El proyecto analiza y busca abordar los riesgos de protección, incluyendo los riesgos enfrentados por personas generalmente más vulnerables (género, edad, discapacidad)?

Yes

4.1.1. Provea brevemente información al respecto - máximo 1.000 carácteres

Given significant protection needs in country, OCHA ensures the centrality of protection in its overall coordination effort. OCHA ensures protection needs and analysis are regularly shared. OCHA continues to improve its monitoring system called Monitor - which is an open platform that reflects real time information on events taking place in country impacting the country's affected population - to be able to identify the protection needs, to advocate for their affectation and to provide the information needed for humanitarian actors to promptly responds to the emergencies. OCHA plays a key role in identifying the most vulnerable in country, among the women, children, indigenous communities and Afro-Colombian, ensuring a response is mobilised to respond to their needs. Indigenous people are particularly vulnerable due to the widespread persistence of structural poverty, which is exacerbated by the fact that in some areas the majority lives in in close proximity to armed groups.

4.2. ¿El proyecto garantiza la seguridad y la dignidad de las personas beneficiadas y tiene en cuenta el principio “No hacer daño”?

Yes

4.2.1. Provea brevemente información al respecto - máximo 1.000 carácteres

Due to OCHA's coordination role, OCHA does not have any direct beneficiaries. However, this does not prevent OCHA from proactively securing that the humanitarian response follows the do-no-harm principle, by understanding the impact of the aid in the respective regional and cultural context. As such, OCHA employs local staff that is familiar with the regional context, facilitating a humanitarian response in regions, where other actors have little to no presence, among them the Amazonas, guiding their response in a manner that humanitarian activities have little to no unintended negative effects. Furthermore, due to OCHA's lead role in the allocation and design of CERF UFE and RR funds, OCHA guarantees that the people receiving the assistance are involved in the project design. Transparency is increased through their tracking and monitoring of the humanitarian response, making the response transparent and accesible to the public.

4.3. ¿El proyecto identifica las barreras y asegura accesibilidad universal e imparcial a la ayuda y los servicios por los diferentes grupos de población?

Yes

4.3.1. Provea brevemente información al respecto - máximo 1.000 carácteres

In its coordination role, OCHA secures that the Humanitarian Response focuses on the needs of the people affected and the gaps of the institutional capacity on the ground, ultimately securing an imparcial prioritisation of the activities under the HRP within the capacities of the humanitarian actors. Furthermore, the persistence of access restrictions prevents the affected population form receiving the needed assistance. In order to identify, raise awareness and reduce these restrictions, OCHA established an Access Working Group, which aims to analyse key access constraints and implement a number of activities to mitigate them.

4.4. ¿El proyecto incorpora un sistema para prevenir y combatir la explotación y los abusos sexuales (PEAS) que incluye una estrategia de sensibilización y mecanismos apropiados de denuncia?

Yes

4.4.1. Provea brevemente información al respecto - máximo 1.000 carácteres

OCHA regularity advocates for the importance of PSEA. Although the agency has no direct beneficiaries, it secures that CERF UFE funding is only disbursed to agencies and implementing partners that implemented the necessary PSEA complaint mechanisms and advocates for humanitarian partners to have regular PSEA workshops.

Whenever possible, situation reports and needs assessment include the data of people with disabilities. During the Humanitarian Needs Overview 208,000 people with severe needs were identified.

Yes

6.1.1. Explique brevemente (máximo 1.000 carácteres)

Humanitarian response is due to its fundamental characteristics for the most part short-lived. However, a close collaboration with development and peace actors is key in Colombia, as the structural, rural poverty and inequality is a fertile ground for a persistence of the armed conflict in the country. OCHA thus coordinates the humanitarian response with these actors to achieve a medium to at best long-term impact, targeting in particular regions with structural deficits and population groups, such as indigenous communities, among others.
