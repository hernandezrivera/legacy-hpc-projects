Project Summary

Syrians face daily threats from ongoing hostilities, widespread explosive ordnance contamination, a significant deterioration in living conditions, and erosion of adaptive capacities. The continued worsening of these conditions contributes to the exacerbation of humanitarian needs. People’s physical and mental well-being is severely affected. During protection interventions, it will reached 5700.
Armed conflict, displacement, social practices and worsening poverty continue to fuel harmful coping mechanisms and strain the capacities of families and communities to protect children, especially adolescent girls and boys: child labor, including its worst forms, has been reported in all governorates. In Raqqa on 14 December 2023, Ashti Center conducted a comprehensive assessment involving community leaders to assess protection needs. The evaluation analysis revealed a deterioration in the protection environment, resulting in an increase in the number of children exposed to abuse and exploitation, including child labor and neglect, as well as increased psychological distress among children and communities affected by the conflict.
Individuals interviewed identified key sources of distress and mental health issues including economic hardship, displacement, domestic problems and lack of safe spaces. About 41% of participants stated that the adverse economic situation greatly affected children’s behavior, 90% indicated that children excluded from services were girls, and 59% reported that children with disabilities were the most excluded group, followed by newly arrived children with 20% of the sample. .
In addition, the city of Raqqa received many women returning from Al-Hawl camp, according to statements issued by the Social Affairs and Labor Committee in Raqqa and the Conflict Resolution Committee in Raqqa concerned with the affairs of those returning from Al-Hawl camp (a committee composed of active figures in society, tribal sheikhs, and technocrats, in addition to representatives of the local authority and civil society) The number of families in Raqqa reached more than 300 families (270 caregivers and 636 children), families who left on regular return trips.
These factors have a gendered and disproportionate impact on girls and women in northeast Syria and specifically in Raqqa, which is manifested through various forms of gender-based violence, especially early/forced marriage, domestic violence, sexual harassment and/or violence, and deprivation of resources, psychological and/or physical abuse, in all aspects of life.Structured psychosocial support (PSS) interventions will be provided to different age groups. Which will enhance confidence and resilience for children and caregivers who care for children. NGOs will ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities by adapting psychosocial support programs, changing modalities where appropriate, and rehabilitating safe spaces to maximize accessibility for persons with disabilities, along with the presence of disability-sensitive water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities and GBV within the safe space. NGOs will provide their services and activities through different modalities.
Ashti Center will also conduct awareness sessions on gender-based violence in the targeted communities. Community capacity building will be a focus, including the establishment of child protection committees and clubs, and community initiatives to ensure community ownership of services. Children's rights awareness campaigns will also contribute to the quality of programs.
Training will be provided to Ashti staff and front-line staff of humanitarian organizations, including identification and safe referrals, child protection, and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
Ashti will coordinate with humanitarian organizations operating in Raqqa in order to provide training on safe identification and referral for workers in these humanitarian organizations.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Through this project, NGOs will link services for vulnerable individuals via referrals made using updated service maps. The NGOs will provide specialized child protection case management. The NGOs will create safe spaces, provide structured and unstructured PSS for caregivers and children, and conduct awareness sessions on child protection and gender-based violence topics. The NGOs will provide training courses for community actors. About SIR, PSEA, PFA and PSS trainings. NGOs will monitor common child protection concerns and risks of gender-based violence by analyzing case management and referrals data to inform program design to address needs. NGOs have a strong monitoring and evaluation system built on regular data collection, analysis and reporting, with accountability and data monitoring in mind. Tracking tools created by NGOs will be used to track IPP delivery outcome data to beneficiaries using registration records, attendance records, well-being improvement measurement tools and the Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS+). In addition to quality participation and beneficiary satisfaction questionnaires at the end of all sessions of psychosocial support activities. The NGO will inform beneficiaries and the community of the NGO's feedback mechanisms to allow them to communicate their feedback on program delivery, needs and concerns. The intervention on gender-based violence will be through providing awareness-raising messages to women, men, girls and boys on gender-based violence and working to activate a referral pathway between service providers and 4W reports. Gender-based violence messages will be delivered through awareness-raising sessions for women, men, girls and boys.Protection risk analysisRisks: Barriers to beneficiary access to specialized services due to physical barriers services as well as standard restrictions on accessing services for women and girls.Likelihoods; Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Using the community outreach method to support the vulnerable individual with referrals and ensuring integration with others, taking into account the rehabilitation of the safe space to be disability-friendly and where people with disabilities and different genders can access the disability and sensitive WASH facility in the safe space. Risks: Stigma that vulnerable children and survivors of GBV may face as a result of receiving a specialized case management service or being referred to specialized GBV case management. Likelihoods: High Impact: High Mitigation: Investing in building a relationship with the community by distributing psychosocial support groups or providing awareness sessions as a non-specialized service targeting all members of the community. Ensure that staff making referrals are trained in safe identification and referral of cases. Clear messages about what services should be in place and used by staff providing services and activities. In addition to working towards greater integration.
