Project Summary

The project aims to improve surveillance of epidemic prone diseases, access and utilisation of integrated health services by 1) communities, which are not currently covered by the HPF support 2) Communities that are located far away from the nearest health facility in Aweil South County of Northern Bahr el Ghazal state. This will be achieved through expanding the reach of essential primary health care services through both static and mobile outreach points. The project will seek to address both the demand side and service delivery side barriers to access and utilisation of health services by the targeted communities. Demand side barriers will be addressed through community engagement, health education and health promotion activities to empower communities to demand quality health services, be active participation members in health decisions that affect their communities and to build positive health seeking behaviours. The service factors will be addressed through building the skills of health workers through training (both in class and on the job) mentorship and supportive supervision, provision of the some of the required resources for the health facilities and strengthening accountabilities at both the health facility and County health department level.
The health workers will receive training/refresher training on the following packages: 1) BEmONC 2) referral of SGBV 3) Clinical Management of Rape, reproductive health services including adolescent reproductive health and FP 4) screening at Community level for MAM and SAM and Management of Acute Malnutrition at the facility level. The project will strengthen existing programmes like EPI and IMNCI and others as outlined in the Basic Package for Health and nutrition Services through refresher training, provision of Job Aids and tools, supportive supervision and mentorship of Health workers developing health worker clinical competencies and skills. To improve vaccination of children, the project will also work with Boma Health workers (BHWs) to mobilise care givers of children under the age of one to take children for vaccinations at the health facility and at outreach points and to refer children with danger signs for further management at health facilities. BHWs will also follow up on Vaccination defaulters and through this network of BHWs, the project seeks to improve surveillance and referral for epidemic prone diseases. To raise awareness, promote identification of people with mental health problems and create demand for mental health services, the project will implement Low Intensity Mental Health Interventions such as Psychoeducation, Community Messaging and awareness use community health structures to Identify people with mental health problems and refer them to the health facility and community support systems. The project will also seek to improve the screening and referral of people with disabilities using the Washington group of questions at every point of contact with health service provider.
In view of the COVID 19 Pandemic and other diseases that are prone to out beaks, the project will work with both facility and community based health workers to improve on surveillance (prevention and control of epidemic prone diseases including outbreak detection, investigation, response) and reporting of suspected cases. Community Health workers will receive refresher training on risk communication messages about COVID 19 and other epidemic prone diseases. Facility based Health workers will be mentored on triaging, passive and active surveillance and emphasis will be placed on timely reporting and responding.

1 - Needs

Despite great progress made at reducing all-cause mortality and morbidity in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) State by the HPF project and other partners in the last 2 years, NBeG continues to experience very high incidence of malaria with some communities not having access to functional health facilities making access to prompt treatment for fever with 24hrs difficult. Under these circumstances, pregnant women and young children are affected most. Malaria is endemic in the entire country and 100% of the population is at risk. However, substantial differences exist in the intensity of transmission and the duration of transmission season. Malaria incidence currently stands at 337 cases/ 1000 populations which is showing an increasing trend in comparison to the 2013 level of 171 cases per 1000/population. The number of deaths have also significantly increased from 1312 in 2013 to 4873 in 2019. The malaria Indicator Surveys data has shown a shift in transmission intensity from intense transmission in the southern part of the country in 2013 to the northwest part in 2017. Aweil South of NBeG state falls in the region identified by the Malaria Indicator Survey as having increased malaria transmission intensity thus contributing more to the Malaria burden in the country. According to the NBeG 2020 annual EPI performance bulletin, NBeG state annual Penta 1 coverage was 82%, which is an indication that communities have good access to immunization services. However, the Penta 3 coverage for the same period was at 67% and the DOR was 18%, an indication of poor utilization of EPI services. One of the contributing factors to poor utilisation of health services especially EPI services is distances to the nearest EPI service point. The longer the distances to the health services point, the lesser the utilisation of health services at that health facility. Aweil South has number of highly populated communities that are hard to reach with health services because of distances between the communities and the health facilities. Aweil South issupported by HPF and IRC was providing Nutrition interventions in Aweil South.. Despite having support from these partners, the coverage and utilisation rates of essential services such as FP, Immunization, institutional deliveries and OPD consultations at some health facilities are still low owing to geographical, cultural, financial and health system barriers. Aweil South has 12 Health Facilities and only 9 are supported, Among the 12 in Aweil south Health facilities are 2 PHCC but only one is supported by HPF. All the health facilities not supported by HPF have interrupted services due to lack of staff, drugs and commodities leaving communities with limited or no access to health services. HPF Budgetary limitations have also affected access to integrated health services through mobile clinics by these communities. For the period 2020, Aweil South is one of the counties identified to have inadequate food supply and requiring food support. Inadequate food supply predisposes Children under the age of5, pregnant and lactating women to poor nutrition and consequently poor health increasing their vulnerabilities to infections. Poor surveillance systems is known to contribute to delayed detection and reporting of any suspected cases. This calls for implementation of an innovative project to scale up surveillance, access to and utilization of health services by communities that are disadvantaged by various factors.

2 - Project activities

The Project approach to increase access to, and utilisation of, health facility-based services will address both supply side and demand side barriers to service delivery. Supply side improvements will include: 1) Strengthening the surveillance systems (2) developing health worker clinical competencies and skills to provide the basic package of health and nutrition services; trainings on BEmONC, HBB and kangaroo care,IMNCI, PMTCT,SGBV and Clinical Management of Rape, IYCN and including IPC as part of every training. (3) strengthening health service provision : OPD Consultations for common diseases, basic preventive nutrition services through maternal infant and young child nutrition Screening and referral targeting women of reproductive age, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). (4) Strengthening utilization of health facilities by pregnant women for ANC, delivery, Postnatal care and family planning: Screening for Mental illness and disability and referral to higher levels of care and support, strengthening routine diseases surveillance, Support to Mass drug administration and NIDs. Provision of EPI services Vitamin A supplementation after every 6 months, and IMNCI as Part of BHI at community level, Strengthen provision of CMR services respond to epidemics and disease outbreaks. Provision o mobile and outreach health services (3). Supervision and mentorship of health workers for service quality improvement and monitoring attendance of health workers at posts; (3) health facility maintenance; and (4) ensuring availability of essential medicines and equipment. (4) Demand side interventions to support increased utilisation of services will include community engagement, health education and promotion at community level, and improving referral to health facilities through the Boma Health Initiative. As part of our approach we will offer patient-centred services that are sensitive to local customs, community needs, geographical and social factors, and protect the basic rights of individuals, their dignity, privacy and respect. (5) The project will also support the procurement of supplies, last mile delivery of commodities to HFs and BHTs, inventory management and drug stock reporting, prepositioning of drug stocks during the rainy season, and collaboration with other health partners to avail supplies and streamline distribution systems under the CHD as the accountable lead. To strengthen prevention of COVID -19 we will train health workers on triaging and BHWs on Home management of Flue like symptoms.. (7) Management of human resources for health: We will work with CHDs to ensure that all CHD team members and health workers have their job descriptions reviewed and updated based on national guidelines and recruit critical staff at HFs in agreement with CHDs. Any new recruitment will follow South Sudan Human Resources for Health guidelines. We will collaborate with CHDs to ensure a review of the data in the human resource information system (HRIS), to ensure integrity of the system. We also will collaborate with SMoH and CHD to reconcile HRIS with South Sudan Electronic Pay Roll System and ensure CHDs regularly review the pay roll for its integrity. We will Support CHD to develop Micro Plans for EPI and hold programme progress review meetings and quarterly planning with partners. Support the CHDs to develop and maintain an asset register of equipment, vehicles (including ambulances) and health facility buildings, routinely check their state, and have plans in place for their maintenance. Support minor renovations and provision of Wash facilities at health facilities

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will use the media and Boma health workers, adolescent and young adult peer educators, churches, TBAs and religious leaders to reach out to communities with information on PSEA. The project will support experts on PSEA in the state to hold Radio talk shows and key messages will be developed and shared with community members during meetings and printed in local language. The project will support the commemoration of key events like the 16 days of activism against gender based violence as a way of reinforcing other efforts to disseminate information on prevention of sexual exploitation and Abuse

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project on inception will educate communities on channels available to launching complains and grievance redress mechanisms. To ensure that individuals with disabilities and those not literate can give feedback on the services they receive, Boma Health Committees at communities will be empowered through training to engage with community members to get their views and perceptions. The project will incorporate service satisfaction surveys through focus group discussions with people with disabilities) and anonymous suggestion boxes at health facilities, Boma Health Workers will continue to educate communities about their role in improving service provision through making suggestions and recommendations. Deliberate identification of marginalized groups such as women, people leaving with disabilities, people with mental disorders and their care givers and adolescents in the community to be members of various project committees will be done to ensure that their voices and feedback is taken note of.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Building on existing mechanisms such as all-inclusive county review meetings, Boma Health Committees, Health Facility committees, men’s group meetings and community leaders meetings, the project will capitalize on these platforms to reach out to beneficiaries with information about the project and to receive complains from the beneficiaries. We will work with BHCs and BHWs to engage local leaders, to raise awareness about the project, its purpose and target to ensure understanding by the communities and informed participation in project activities. We will remain mutually accountable on responsibilities and conduct quarterly performance reviews including budget reviews with CHDs. We will collect feedback (quarterly) from beneficiaries, HF staff and CHDs to understand their perspective on our implementation, and to enable issues to be highlighted (including positives) on the running of interventions. For monitoring purposes, this feedback will be documented with clear responses on the concerns raised and as required adapt programme strategies and approaches to fit the changing context. Remain impartial; engaging with all project stakeholders and beneficiaries in an inclusive way that is non-partisan and non-discriminatory. Recruit project staff in a transparent and fair way.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

During bimonthly review meeting, the feedback from communities about project activities is used to realign the project assistance to address the issues raised by community members

8.11. Mitigation Measures

We will maintain a risk register that is updated quarterly and reviewed by Malaria Consortium’s Global Management Group (GMG) during monthly operational calls. The risk register will detail: programme, financial, contextual (political and economic) and safeguarding risks; their impact, likelihood, acceptable risk level and existing controls; and the mitigating actions, timelines for these actions, and the person responsible for implementing them. These risks will be factored into our quarterly work plans by identifying any potential causes of delay and ensuring we have actions in place to mitigate against them, to ensure delivery of the contract on time and within budget. Mom Health Teams (BHTs) will work with local leaders and administrators to mobilise communities to support people with mental illness and disabilities. BHTs will engage men’s groups on issues of GBV, to make them aware of the special health needs of women and the importance of their involvement in family health affairs. BHT training will cover non-discriminatory practices, interpersonal communication, and their role in sensitising communities about the negative effects of stigma
