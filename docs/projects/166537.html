Project Summary

Zimbabwe has not been spared from COVID-19 pandemic and on the 17 March, President Emmerson Mnangagwa declared COVID-19 a National Disaster following the worldwide spread of novel coronavirus, which has been rapid across continents and regions. By 12 May 2020, Zimbabwe had recorded 37 confirmed cases, 4 deaths and 12 recovery cases. Cumulatively, a total of 12,869 rapid screening tests and 11,058 PCR diagnostic tests had been conducted. Zimbabwe has been going through economic challenges for over two decades, ranging from unemployment, poor economic performance, financial crisis and the liquidity crunch, which exacerbates the vulnerable situation thus reducing its capacity to invest in water and sanitation infrastructure. The COVID-19 pandemic, has further put a strain on the ailing economy making the population in both urban and rural areas more vulnerable. People living in poverty with or without access to water, sanitation and healthcare services face the greatest risk from the COVID-19 pandemic as they may not be able to observe prevention guidelines due to the limited resources and their need to continue with livelihood activities for survival despite the imminent threat. There is inadequate surveillance and laboratory capacity, scarcity of necessary human resources and limited financial means to address a global pandemic of this nature and magnitude. In addition to the new public health crisis coupled with the weak public health system increase the vulnerability of the populations to the pandemic.
Zimbabwe still faces a number of challenges including providing clean potable water and sanitation services for the citizens and promoting good public health and hygiene. The water supply situation remains critical in both urban centers and rural areas due to the recurrent droughts that the country has experienced and low awareness on COVID-19 among the citizens. Promoting hand-washing and general hygiene are critical routes to minimize the spread of COVID-19. Non availability of hand-washing facilities in public places, health care facilities, public shopping malls and worship places increase the risk of spreading of the corona virus among the citizens.
The proposed project is aimed at supporting public health responses to contain the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic by decreasing morbidity and mortality. The proposed project will support 6 health care facilities and reach a target population of 190,481 women, men, girls and boys with hygiene messages in Makoni, Rusape town, Mutare City and Mutasa districts of Manicaland Province. The proposed project will help to create an enabling environment for the COVID-19 response through immediate interventions aimed at improving governance, human rights and gender equality, coordination, social cohesion and services for the affected populations. The project aims to increase awareness on COVID-19 and support short-term interventions that prevent the spread of COVID-19 among the affected populations of the targeted domains.

1 - Activities

The proposed project seek to implement the following activities to support the project objectives:• Support the setting up of hand washing stations at various public places.• Support mass media campaigns on COVID-19 prevention using various channels of communication (billboards, radio shows, radio jingles etc) taking into account literacy levels and access of different groups.• Support the health system to ensure availability of basic water and sanitation services in the key prioritized health care facilities. • Providing access to critical handwashing facilities including soap and alcohol-based sanitizers, WASH- related hygiene kits.• Ensure field and front-line staff engaged in WASH humanitarian responses have adequate access to PPE and practice key risk reduction practices like social distancing.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through the following indicators:* Number of men, women, boys and girls with access to quality clean water.* Number of people reached with key health and hygiene messages.* Number of households reached with WASH hygiene kits.* Number of people reached through provision of sanitary facilities.
