Project Summary

Between May 2023 and January 2024, INTERSOS conducted Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments and protection-specific context analysis in sub-districts of implementation across Hama, Idleb and Rural Damascus. Extensive protection risks were identified. For CP, physical, psychological and emotional abuse, child labor (including worst forms of child labor and resulting in risks of UXO related injuries due to the nature of the work), neglect, lack of legal documentation and child sexual abuse. Psychosocial distress and unaddressed psychological needs are prevalent in affected communities, in addition to trauma-specific psychological distress caused by conflict and earthquake. Neglect and abuse of vulnerable people (PwD and elderly) and denial/restricted access to services and resources. For GBV, intimate-partner violence and wider domestic and family violence, sexual abuse and exploitation and early and forced marriage were identified. In 2024-2025, INTERSOS will continue integrating its protection programme with livelihood (HSYR24-ERY-212363-1), education (HSYR24-EDU-212411-1), and health (HSYR24-HEA-211951-1) to ensure a holistic response to the increasingly complex needs of the local populations. Targeting RD, Hama and Idleb - possibly expanding to Deir-ez-Zor, INTERSOS will carry out a comprehensive protection response (CP, GP, GBV and MA), contributing to preventing and mitigating protection risks, building the resilience of affected communities and improving the psychosocial well-being of affected communities through stand-alone and integrated protection interventions. Expectations of response in areas with limited services and potential tension in community (Likelihood H: Impact M) will be mitigated through mapping and coordination to balance coverage based on needs, clear selection criteria prioritizing medium-high protection risks, and community participation. Female participation being restricted by family/community (L:M I:H) will be mitigated providing women/girls with socially-accepted activities as safe entry points and training staff on protection mainstreaming. SEA by staff/partners during service provision (L:M I:H) will be mitigated through PSEA trainings, clear PSEA policy/strategy, and ensuring accessible reporting mechanisms. Inadequate BNF’s data management (L:M I:H) will be mitigated by enforcing data protection SoPs, setting clear red lines with line ministries, and ensuring functioning CFRM. INTERSOS has global PSEA/CP policies applying to all personnel and suppliers, incl. trainers. A country-specific strategy ensures SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP declaration and attend PSEA/CP induction/refreshers (PSEA/CP trainings are also provided to partners). In-country PSEA Referent and 6 PSEA FPs are appointed to receive reports about alleged SEA acts and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Reports can be made in person, to a dedicated email address, by phone or through INTERSOS CFM boxes. Complaints are then referred to the PSEA Referent and investigated. Risks and mitigation measures are in-detail elaborated in INTERSOS Contingency Planning Process for target governorates. Programme risks are mitigated through robust monitoring and reporting mechanisms, capacity building for staff and partners, and clear engagement protocols with SARC and line ministries. Safety and access risks are mitigated via security assessments, capacity building on relevant protocols, and close coordination with key actors (OCHA, SARC, line ministries, other partners, community networks). Close supervision of the compliance with internal SOPs, rigorous monitoring of financial expenditure and regular revision of financial plans, frequent due diligence and pre-qualification processes, regular market research and procurement in stocks reduce financial and procurement risks.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Promoting integration between Protection and Livelihood, Health and Education responses, INTERSOS will implement the proposed action through 2 modalities: in partnership with SARC or directly under sub-MoUs with MoSAL or MoE, depending on target locations and best modality to ensure integration with other Sectors. Protection activities will be implemented through a static-mobile approach and will include training for IPs and line ministry staff/volunteers, community members/groups, education personnel, health workers/CHVs and PTAs on core protection principles (GBV, CP, safe identification and referral, inclusion and legal awareness through SARC). Awareness raising on core protection topics, tailored to the protection risks identified through INTERSOS community based protection response (with direct implementation modality,) and in partnership with SARC including legal and mine risk awareness. Establishing community groups and supporting community led initiatives, including through PTAs. GBV risk mitigation through ICM and prevention/empowerment activities for women/girls, alongside awareness raising activities for all genders. Delivery of CM services (both through direct implementation modality and with SARC as implementing partner, with INTERSOS providing technical supervision and support.) Provision of IKA for those BNFs identified as most vulnerable/at risk and/or PwDs (dignity/education/hygiene/winter kits,) and MIKA. An M&E plan will be developed with quantitative/qualitative indicators to measure achievement rate/impact of each intervention. Needs assessment/community consultations (through FGDs, KII, PDM) will be regularly conducted to foster active participation from the community. Protection and gender considerations will be mainstreamed throughout all Sectors of interventions. INTERSOS Protection staff and Health staff will be working in collaboration on the delivery of dignity kits and MHM awareness sessions (as entry point for SRH and GBV). INTERSOS gender mainstreaming approach aims to avoid causing further harm, ensures meaningful access, accountability and equal participation of affected individuals. INTERSOS is currently working with different modalities: 1. Partnership with SARC; 2. Under sub-MoUs with MoE and MoSAL, allowing for direct implementation of a full range of protection activities, targeting the most vulnerable groups. Activities implemented under these sub-MoUs include ICM for GP and CP, structured (rolling out UNICEF MHPSS Manual) and semi-structured PSS, protection-related awareness sessions - based on findings from community mapping exercises and tailored to the specific protection risks of the targeted community, and community-based protection. In 2024-2025, INTERSOS plans to reach 71,914 individuals with 2,983,182 USD budget requirement, including 40,368 individuals (1,672,472 USD) in 2024 and 31,546 individuals (1,310,710 USD) in 2025.
