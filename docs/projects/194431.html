Project Summary

In response to identified priority needs of and protection service provision, DRC will deliver this integrated protection (General Protection, HLP, GBV, and Child Protection) project with solutions mainstreamed for conflict and displacement affected communities. The aim is to creating a protective environment for women, men, girls and boys of South through safeguarding, respecting rights and finding solutions. DRC will implement protection activities including in hard to reach locations through both static, outreach and mobile modalities. In doing so DRC aims to contribute to the overarching intersectoral strategic objectives for the 2023 HRP through contributing to the protection cluster strategic objectives too.
The proposed sub-sectors and activities will ensure provision of critical protection related assistance through an integrated approach to address priority needs of targeted communities, women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas. The project will mainstream durable solutions whilst enhanced protection assessments and monitoring will inform protection and overall humanitarian response.
DRC also currently has 2 Mobile Protection Teams that respond to emergencies in hard to reach locations where there are limited services and high needs thus under GP. These mobile teams will also provide General Protection services. The target locations are country wide, hard-to-reach and recommended by the Protection Cluster for short response or semi-static response – in coordination with the Cluster. GBV prevention and response activities will be implemented through static and outreach in Unity and Upper Nile State. Child Protection activities by DRC will be implemented through partners focusing but focusing in Jonglei State and Greater Pibor Administrative Area. Proposed activities in this integrated protection project are as follows:
1. Protection monitoring, assessments and analysis. This with include safety audits, conflict analysis, and rapid gender and inclusion analysis for the project.
2.Information Dissemination and Awareness raising on rights, entitlements, assistance opportunities GBV issues, Child protection issues, HLP rights, available services, Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), Peacebuilding issues, etc.
4. Strengthening community-based protection structures like women committees, Community Protection Committees, community-based protection networks, Community based child protection structures and networks.
5. Comprehensive Case Management (GBV) and (CP) and provision of psychosocial support
6. Integrated GBV prevention and response with GBV risk mitigation
7. Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) in-kind of material or cash.
8. Comprehensive Case Management for GBV survivors and Child Survivors.
9. Supporting community co-existence, facilitating peacebuilding dialogues,
10. Capacity building of staff, partners, and local authorities and leaders on GBV core messages, CPiE, Peacebuilding, ADR, HLP, according to available IASC standards, PSEA Cluster SOPs, DRC tools, among others.
Evidence-based programming and advocacy across the response in will be supported through regular protection monitoring and protection assessments based on pre-identified triggers, with mechanisms to ensure inclusion of persons with specific needs such as persons living with disabilities, older people, young men at risk, adolescent girls at risk, and female headed households.

1 - Needs

2022 saw a deterioration in the humanitarian situation in South Sudan in general, and Upper Nile in particular. The country was confronted by social and political instability due to violence and a series of interconnected shocks, including conflict, persistent and unprecedented flooding as well as inflation. This has led to massive displacement, further straining scarce resources, livelihoods, and basic services, and increasing protection risks, particularly for the most vulnerable groups. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), over 8.9 million people are estimated to need some form of humanitarian assistance and protection in South Sudan. In Upper Nile, violent conflict erupted in August 2022 resulting in widespread destruction of property and livelihoods, loss of life, family separations, distress, and displacement. On August 2022, heavy armed clashes commenced in Tonga town and Nyiluak payam in Panyikang county between forces loyal to General Gatwech on the one side (SPLA-IO and the White army are also included in this group), and Agwelek Forces led by General Olony on the other. The forces led by Gatwech are thought to be primarily of Nuer origin, whereas the Agwelek forces are considered to be primarily of Shilluk ethnic origin. The conflict resulted in the mass displacement of approximately 17,000-21,000 individuals from Tonga town and surrounding villages to Fashoda, Malakal town, and Malakal Protection of Civilians (PoC) site. As the security situation remains fragile, the movements of IDPs continue to be unpredictable.As a result of the fighting, the situation between IDP population from the Shilluk and Nuer ethnic groups in the Malakal PoC site remains tense. Furthermore, repeated shocks have weakened community-based structures and services, pushing families to adopt negative coping mechanisms that expose the vulnerable, particularly female headed households, PWDs and girls, to violence, exploitation, abuse, abduction, child labour, and forced marriageDRC continues to monitor numerous protection risks and threats, including revenge killings, attacks, conflict-related sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), forced displacement, breakdown of social and leadership structures, ethnic tension, and family separation within the targeted areas. The overall protection environment in Upper Nile remained fragile with 230 separate protection incidents documented from the first to the third quarter of 2022. In the Unity state, human rights violations committed by armed groups remain rampant. Incidents include killings, cattle raiding, looting, abduction, and widespread sexual violence affecting women and children. UNMISS reported 131 cases (113 women and 18 girls) of rape and gang rape in the state from February to May 2022, including the case of a nine-year-old girl who was gang-raped to death. Violence causes displacement, with at least 44,000 civilians thought to have fled their homes due to conflict in the second quarter of the year4. In addition to the conflict-related tensions, the state experienced unprecedented flooding. While some seasonal flooding is common, 2021 and 2022 saw a mass increase in flooded areas. The nature of flooding has also changed, from being just rainwater to floodwater from unknown sources. Roads in and out became impassable and boats and the airstrip serve as the only means to connect the state with the rest of the country. Internally displaced persons (IDP) camps are below the current water level, protected from floodwaters only by dykes. Most counties across Unity State were affected by the flood, displacing people to higher lands in Rubkona and Bentiu towns. This has exposed vulnerable persons to more hazards and risks. According to IOM DDT Baseline , a total of 195,678 are thought to be displaced across the state.

2 - Project activities

DRC in South Sudan will implement an integrated protection project with activities for HLP, GBV, GP, and CP activities. Broadly, the activities to be implemented are protection monitoring, assessments and analysis; Providing Individual Protection Assistance (IPA); Information dissemination and awareness raising; strengthening community-based protection mechanisms; Case Management; Running Women and Girls Friendly Spaces, and supporting protection and inclusion mainstreaming in other sectors. Protection Monitoring, assessments, audits and analysis: The aim of the protection monitoring activities is to identify protection risks faced by persons of concerns, analyze the trends in order to know the perpetrators, and who is affected. This will ensure appropriate interventions through referral or direct support by DRC as well as support evidence-based protection advocacy. Specific activities will include FGDs, KIIs, direct observation, and documentation of protection incidents at existing Protection Desks. Reports will provide an overview of the protection environment. Similarly, the reports will be shared on a need-to-know basis with partners and clusters for evidence informed programming and advocacy. This project will conduct a rapid gender and disability analysis too. Individual Protection Assistant (IAP): IPA is a responsive and remedial action whereby immediate protection concerns at the individual or family level are identified and addressed. IPA aims to address protection risks that are time sensitive and require immediate action. The assistance will prevent, reduce or mitigate exposure to protection risks, or limiting the effects of violations on victims/survivors, through individual protection assistance (material in-kind, or cash-based assistance). IPA will be provided as a last resort, on a case by case basis, when referrals to other services providers to meet the immediate need are not feasible due to lack of available services or incapacity to provide services by other partners. A monthly Cash Transfer for firewood targeting the most vulnerable and at-risk households in Unity State. IPA targeting will be informed by protection monitoring and assessments or referrals. General Protection, HLP for documentation purposes and for legal assistance, and GBV case management and referrals. Information dissemination and awareness raising: This will include on available GBV, Protection, Child Protection services, the prevention and mitigation of specific protection risks (including on PSEA) identified in the community and relevant rights and entitlements informed by protection risk analysis. Child Protection, GBV, HLP, GP and Mobile Response Team will apply this activity. GBV team will use other methods like SASA! SASA Together! and Girl Shine! DRC will use GBVIMS and monitoring analysis to identify trends to include in awareness raising. Economic empowerment and risk mitigation. DRC will run 10 Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) – according to South Sudan WGFS guideline - providing integrated GBV services including adolescent reproductive health education; PSS, supporting and distributing locally made safe dignity kits, supporting Income generating activities. Girl Shine will be to empower adolescent girls. Safety audits and awareness will be part of GBV risk mitigation. Community Based Protection: To ensure community participation, DRC Protection in Unity State and Upper Nile will continue to work with various existing Community Based Protection networks to strengthen the capacity of community-based structures in order to improve their self-protection capacities and strengthening positive coping mechanisms as well contributing towards localized community cohesion. In Jonglei, through the partners (CIDO and WCH). DRC GBV team will continue to work with and strengthen existing women committees in Unity and Upper Nile State. Through the GBV Teams and Mobile Response Teams, DRC will distribute dignity kits.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Protection interventions are provided through a variety of modalities, including cash and in-kind. Cash and in-kind assistance will be provided as IPA support where referrals to other services is not feasible as well as achieving protection outcomes. Cash items will be provided based on a risk vs benefit analysis assessment. Cash will be provided only if such protection risk can be solved only by cash. Other instances where cash support will be applicable is (cash is needed for transport to a life-saving service which the person would otherwise not be able to access) deemed safe for the person in need to move to a safe space/location or access life-saving services but does not have the means to do so. This assistance (cash and in-kind) will be on a case by case basis and assessment and with the aim to reduce, prevent or respond to a protection risk. Continuous risk assessment of the cash assistance will be done to identify, mitigate and manage associated risks of CVA and to ensure conflict sensitivity.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DRC is implementing a Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanisms in all project locations, which would be capable of handling complaints related to any of its activities, and will have several entry points for lodging complaints comprising of in-person office visits, Let us know box (suggestion box) and through community visits (either orally at or formal written complaints, at both the FCRM desks or to other DRC staff), and through intermediaries working with DRC e.g. implementing partners and community representatives. Having several mechanisms allows for beneficiaries to choose a method that is most comfortable and practical for them and ensures there is at least one mechanism in place in all locations. DRC Staff have been trained to handle personal complaints and inquiries in a confidential manner, and protection issues are referred to DRC protection and GBV team or to protection partners to ensure sensitive issues are handled in safe and professional manner without causing further harm. Moreso, DRC also work with and through community based structures like Elderly, Disability Groups, Women Groups among others of which these platforms are opportunities for providing and giving feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project have an activity of information dissemination and awareness raising. Part of the deliberate key messaging is about disseminating information about the project and about available feedback and complaint mechanisms. The project will capitalise on community meetings, meetings with community representatives, including youth, female and disability representatives to share the information about the project and available mechanisms for complaints and feedback. Besides meetings, the project will also do awareness campaigns during the project period where rights and entitlements about the project is raised. Where possible, the project team will provide information to the local leadership and religious representation for disseminating information to the general public. Experiences of DRC in South Sudan indicates that communicating with and through community based protection structures and local peace infrastructures is an appropriate way. To ensure that the messaging is contextually relevant, safe and appropriate, the methods and content of the information will be tailored accordingly. For instance, local languages will be used. Translated pamphlets can be shared. Messages can shared over the local radio frequencies.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DRC has zero tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse. Every staff, consultant, and partner of DRC will have to sign a code of conduct for participating with DRC activities. All staff will be required to inducted or trained on PSEA and Code of Conduct of DRC. The training could be virtually or physically. Through trained staff and information dissemination on the humanitarian code of conduct, etitlments and available goods and services - the project will disseminate this information during community meetings, psychosocial activities, Women and Girls Safe Spaces, awareness campaigns, trainings, - about the right and entitlement to safe assistance without exploitation. The project will also share available complaints and feedback mechanisms which can be used by the beneficiaries. By participating in meetings and campaigns organised by PSEA Taskforce of the specific location, beneficiaries will gain more information about PSEA too. The project may also design, review and disseminate safe and appropriate Information and Education Materials (IEC).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DRC has a functional Feedback, Complaints, Response Mechanism (FCRM) that allows beneficiaries to provide and receive feedback and complaints regarding the activities being implemented in their respective locations. The DRC F-CRM desks are set up to receive complaints and questions from beneficiaries and follow-up with feedback, including referrals to external service-providers or to the DRC protection desks when appropriate, either immediately or within a reasonable timeframe, depending on the nature of the complaint. DRC has a standardized FCRM form where all feedback and complaints are captured. All data captured on the FCRM forms is then uploaded onto a password protected database where all feedback and complaints are tacked until they are closed. Furthermore, beneficiary complaints and feedback will also be captured when conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM). Beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to provide feedback or make a complaint. Any feedback related to gross misconduct serious breach of the Code of Conduct, then that feedback provided (and according to principles, standards and policies of DRC) may inform appropriate management decision about the project and project staff, gaps and opportunities for capacity building too.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

In order to manage mitigate interference from local authorities, DRC will proactively engage the local authorities in a transparent manner about the project, humanitarian principles, and approaches. In collaboration and coordination with OCHA and the Cluster, DRC will ensure a coordinated response and approach. DRC will also maintain a working and informed relationship with local authorities through regular meetings. In addition, DRC will also include local authorities in targeted capacity building opportunities to enhance their understanding and procedures too. In order to mitigate PSEA associated risks, DRC will continue to update and implement its PSEA implementation plan, as well as proactively participate in the PSEA taskforce at National and state level. Moreso, DRC is an member of the PSEA Taskforce in Upper Nile and Unity State. In that regard, DRC will work with the taskforce coordination mechanisms to ensure mechanisms are put in place to enable affected populations to reports cases of PSEA and to the PSEA Coordinators through established Community Based Complain Mechanisms. DRC will also work with the taskforce to create awareness in the community on the available channels for reporting. DRC will also engage and consult specialist groups like women groups, disability groups, and community-based protection/child protection/ women groups/networks to ensure access and visibility of minority and marginalized groups and individuals. In addition, DRC will also capitalize on providing IEC materials in multiple formats to ensure accessibility. And in line with DRC policy of Gender, Age and Diversity Mainstreaming, the project will ensure diverse and inclusive team members. To ensure safe delivery of the project without causing further harm, the project will also apply a risk reduction approach up to activity level where any associated risks are identified and mitigated, hence adapting modalities of the activity. Risks and benefits will be weighed in such a manner that the project will ensure that it is principled, mainstream protection and gender, reduces GBV risks, conflict sensitivity and does not cause harm. Finally, DRC will regularly train the project team in different subjects of the intervention to ensure quality and standards is maintained. Topics may include on GBV, Protection Mainstreaming, AGD Mainstreaming, Disability Inclusion, Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) and PSEA, among others.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

DRC plans to implement the following GBV activities in Unity State and Upper Nile: Comprehensive GBV Case management; awareness raising and information dissemination; running Women and Girls Safe Spaces; distributing dignity kits; conducting safety audits; and providing IPA for survivors besides referrals.DRC proposes to provide specialized, comprehensive and survivor-cantered approach GBV case management services for GBV survivors and individuals at risk of GBV in line with the IASC GBV Guidelines for Case Management, as well as DRCs internal SOP on GBV Case Management. Whilst the priority target population for global GBV case management approaches and services have been built are women and girls, given women and girls constitute the overwhelming majority of survivors, DRC adheres to the principle of non-discrimination and therefore will provide GBV case management services for men and boys if required. DRC will also focus on how men and boys can access the case management services. A GBV service mapping will be conducted in target locations and in consultation with GBV SC, on a quarterly basis to map out available services and enhance referrals. GBV response staff will work closely with other actors providing health and legal services to strengthen the referral pathway, including coordination to ensure primary health centers have staff trained on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and are equipped with post exposure prophylaxis (PEP) and Emergency contraceptives (EC). DRC is a data gathering organization for the Gender Based Violence Information Management Systems (GBVIMS) and will continue using GBVIMS for data management. In Unity State DRC will pilot the roll out of GBVIMS+ having been trained by the global team on this in February 2021. DRC will continue engaging in GBVIMS trend analysis meetings with the GBV SC and continue to engage in internal GBVIMS trend analysis to inform DRC programming particularly prevention and risk mitigation activities. DRC will also provide Group Psychosocial Support Services (PSS) in the current 10 WGFS in Unity and Upper Nile State and to establish new WGFS in the new IDP Sites for the new displaced communities due to flooding. The group PSS activities will be organized based on the identified need of women and girls, by GBV staff. PSS activities will be closely linked with livelihood programs, also implemented by DRC. As the flooding affected the main source of income of women (firewood collection), lost their livelihood opportunities including their livestock, DRC will provide monthly Cash transfer for firewood targeting the most vulnerable households. Cash transfer for Firewood came is the most preferred and effective modality of responding to this flooding emergency need especially in Unity State. DRC will provide monthly cash support per Household for Fuelwood. The reason behind providing monthly is to reduce risk for loss or theft by vulnerable women residing in IDP camps. Providing cash monthly will also help DRC to monitor if the cash is being used on intended purpose. As part of women economic empowerment and risk mitigation, DRC will train women on producing local Dignity Kits, specifically Sanitary Pad, which will be assembled with the soap, which is currently produced under the Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) program. In Unity and Upper Nile State, DRC will conduct information dissemination and awareness raising activities aiming on behavioral changes on the gender inequality and power imbalance between male and female which reduces GBV. Different awareness raising methodologies including SASA! Will be used.
