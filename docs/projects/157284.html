Project Summary

The broader goal of this project is to increase access and quality of basic education to Internal Displaced Persons (IDPs) & new arrivals as well as prioritising essential actions that address barriers facing girls in enrolling and participating in the school environment.In addition the project would also actively mainstream promotion of environmental friendly actions such as solid waste management, tree planting, live fencing and community environmental awareness sessions through use of school health and hygiene clubs. While the primary target is IDPs and new arrivals, nearby children from host communities,returnees or nomads can also access rehabilitated or built (semi permanent) education facilities in conflict affected rural communities.The 12 month project would be implemented in central jebel Marra benefiting 13,400 children (6,076 boys & 7,324 girls) and 6,060 adults (3,400 women & 2,660 men). The specific objectives of this project are as follows;
1) Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies
2) Improve the quality of education (formal and non formal) to ensure continuity of relevant education during and after emergencies for the most disadvantaged and vulnerable pre school and primary school aged children and adolescents
3) Strengthen the response capacity of education actors (PTA, headmasters, government officials and partners) to mitigate the impact of emergencies including natural disaster and conflict on education service delivery (PROTECTION, DURABLE SOLUTIONS). In close coordination with UNICEF and partners on the ground, SORC will ensure strategies to eliminate all forms of violence and girls and women and allowing children protective and conducive environment.

Needs

The 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview ranked Jebelmarra as one of the areas facing severe challenges in the education sector & requiring urgent humanitarian support. These findings are also corroborated by SORC monitoring reports and information from other agencies that reveal enormous constraints especially with regards to school dropouts and early marriages . Some of the traditional and cultural practices such as early marriage, caring and home nursing duties & women and girls constituting the bulky of agriculture labour force promote exclusion of girls from the school environment. It was observed that Jebelmarra area do not value education of girls hence most girls are not in school as compared to boys. Almost 4 million children of school age do not attend school and 55.6% of these are girls (UNICEF).Lack proper sanitation facilities in schools expose girls to more risks such as sexual abuse and sexual exploitation as compared to boys.Long walking distances (10 15km) has resulted in parents not willing to send children especially adolescent girls (13 17 years) to school in fear of sexual attacks and other forms of harassment. In golo girls’ primary school enrolment was less than boys (48% compared to 52%) while in out of a total enrolment of 5,964 students, 31% are girls and 69% are boys .In all the 3 localities,levels of school drop outs are very high and this is particularly fuelled by conflict & destruction of education facilities.However it has also been noted that during agricultural season majority of parents withdraw adolescent children (13 17 years) from school to provide labour in the fields.In most instances girls as compared to boys who are withdrawn fail to return owing to various reasons such as early marriage & increased household chores . Recurrent conflict & flooding in Central Jebel marra results in looting, destruction and collapsing of basic education facilities as well as displacement of children. The has seen existing facilities being overstretched & overcrowded classrooms with the average classroom holding 80 pupils (golo villages standards) are common. I statistics state there are more than 150 temporary (open) classrooms in the 7 areas.The majority of schools in the communities lack proper facilities for teachers. Over 60% of teachers in golo do most of their work (planning & marking) in the open and such poor conditions have partly contributed in schools failing to attract and retain qualified teachers .Moreover, most of the make shift schools are made up of local raw materials (trees) that demand continuous rehabilitation and this increased deforestation & desertification.Overcrowding in worsening the environmental challenges.
