Project Summary

The worsening situation in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Unity can be mainly attributed to the protracted conflict in the country and the unfavorable rainfall patterns attributed to climate change. Panyijar County was affected by heavy flooding. The Humanitarian Needs overview released in February 2022 states that the cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, and intensified existing vulnerabilities of affected people. The proposed project seeks to provide water and sanitation services to affected households in Panyijar. The intervention will target host communities of the area, returnees and IDPs. The projects will expand on existing WASH infrastructure and services to facilitate access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities. Water inventories will be conducted in consultation with the respective district administrations to determine where drinking water points, public latrines and handwashing stations, for instance in schools, health centers public spaces within the communities, need to be constructed, rehabilitated, and/or expanded. In collaboration with the local private sector, such critically needed WASH infrastructure will be constructed, rehabilitated, and/or expanded, and trainings for their operation and maintenance will be conducted with the involved communities. For the latter, mobilization of community engagement will be achieved through the establishment of water user and sanitation committees. Moreover, elderly and disabled people will be assisted in building their latrines and menstrual and/or general hygiene kits will be distributed to the affect population (in particular women and girls). To prevent the spread of (water borne) diseases and increase hygiene awareness, of hygiene promotion and Covid-19 campaigns will be conducted, including the provision of relevant IEC and PPE material where relevant.

1 - Needs

The Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report, Panyijiar County, Unity State of August 2021 indicated that due to flooding displacement caseloads are likely to increase in Panyijiar with a high risk of continued disease outbreak as elderly, disabled, women and children will continue to be exposed to hard living conditions with limited access to basic needs, sanitation facilities, and information on protection services. It is also important to consider the increase of WASH needs owing to COVID-19 which is expected to continue in to 2022 and possibly beyond. This gives rise to the need to address critical WASH needs of the affected population. the project aims at addressing the need for emergency latrines, rehabilitating handwashing facilities and addressing hygiene promotion activities. Assessments by IRNA in 2021 revealed that most of the sources of water, mainly boreholes, were submerged by flood water limiting access to potable drinking water resulting in people travelling long distances to seek access to usable water. This situation also gives rise to insecurities and protection issues. The WASH severity classification (May 2021) report insecurity as one of the factors that has affected water access with 16% of households (Nationally) having reported feeling unsafe while fetching water. The project proposes to address the lack of portable water through rehabilitation of 16 sites of water supplies and 10 emergency latrines and bathing facilities inline with the Humanitarian Needs overview (February 2022) which identified poor water and hygiene (WASH) services as one of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in South Sudan. The project will also distribute personal hygiene material for 1500 women and girls in the reproductive age. Given that the project can only address some of the needs in the most deserving locations, focus will be on water supply, distribution of NFIs and hygiene sensitization. Geographical focus will be in Panijiar counties. Crosscutting issues like gender protection and inclusivity have also been considered in the design and implementation of the project. All population sectors including marginalized groups like the elderly and the disabled will be given an opportunity to participate, benefits and influence decision making in the project.

2 - Project activities

The project will start with a water inventory conducted in consultation with the district administration to determine where drinking water points, public latrines and handwashing stations (schools, health centers, communities) need to be constructed, rehabilitated, and/or expanded, and analyze local capacities and systemic gaps to operate and maintain them; Construction rehabilitation and expansion of drinking water points, public latrines and handwashing stations in collaboration with the local private sector (drilling/construction contractors, hand pump mechanics, artisans) and conduct trainings for their operation and maintenance by explicitly addressing need and knowledge gaps identified during inventory; Each borehole will be equipped with simple hand pumps. Local pump mechanics will be trained on maintenance of hand pumps and wells, which will ensure the usability of the water points beyond the project duration because a shortage of essential water supplies would otherwise put the already vulnerable population in a humanitarian emergency again. The project will also target to rehabilitate and construct 20 wells and drinking water points to improve the access to drinking water for 98,000 affected people. In addition to that, the project will select interested and committed households and support them in rehabilitating and constructing simple pit latrines affected by floods. Two toilet blocks (female & male) will be constructed with 2 stances. The project anticipates that approximately 14,000 individuals will benefit from these toilets. Complementary to the to the emergency latrines, WHH SSD will provide handwashing facilities to ensure that hygiene measures are practiced after using the toilet.; Mobilize community engagement through the establishment of water user and sanitation committees WHH will conduct hygiene trainings for community engagement groups and WatSan committees to ensure frequent cleaning and proper maintenance along the camp sites for the all WaSH-related services. However, WHH will accompany the teams over a longer period to ensure follow-up’s and to guarantee accountability; Implementation of hygiene promotion and Covid-19 campaigns, incl. provision of relevant IEC and PPE material as well as menstrual and/or general hygiene kits Households will be trained on fundamental hygiene practice (e.g. personal hygiene, proper use of latrines, latrine’s safety and cleanliness, etc.). The project will target beneficiaries through hygiene promotion sessions, hygiene messages and promotion materials will be harmonized in line with WASH cluster guidelines on related key messages.In addition, WHH under this activity and for a purpose of adaption to COVID-19 preparedness and response will seek engagement with the government public health department to join for these campaigns and sessions to support spreading all relevant information and outreach to mitigate the risk of COVID-19 in the project areas. Hygiene messages and Information, Education, Communication (IEC) materials will be coordinated with WASH and FSL Clusters and WFP to ensure harmonization. This is also to ensure a wide coverage by reaching to the catchment population during the general food distribution activities funded by the WFP in the project area. In addition, and to ensure the best outreach, volunteers will be selected and trained in community hygiene sensitization and promotion. They will in turn reach out to affected households through door to door and public gathering hygiene promotion.

3 - Project indicators

OUTPUT: Access to essential water supply and safe sanitation facilities in vulnerable communities has improved to meet the demand. Success of the project will be measured by the number of WASH services/products (water points, latrines, handwashing points, etc.) made available to the targeted population, disaggregated by gender, age and disability. The project will conduct hygiene trainings for community engagement groups and WatSan committees to ensure frequent cleaning and proper maintenance along the camp sites for the all WaSH-related services Sucess of the activity will be the percentage of water systems/facilities in targeted locations that have functional and accountablemanagement systems in place. The project will target individuals through hygiene promotion sessions, hygiene messages and promotion materials will be harmonized in line with WASH cluster guidelines on related key messages and success of the activity will be demonstrated by the number of households who correctly describe three measures to prevent WASH-related diseases. The hygiene kits distribution activity will be measured by the percentage of targeted women and girls of menstruating age provided with access to appropriate materials for menstrual hygiene management.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries will be incorporated in the project management structures to ensure there are full participants in project implementation oversight and decision making. Beneficiary feedback will be collected through feedback mechanisms and directly during deliberately organized feedback meetings at community level. Welthungerhilfe has a practice of ensuring that all projects and locations have a feedback analysis committee chaired by respective project managers. The committee analyses feedback immediately it is received and decides on responses. Responses are then directed to the responsible units for implementation which includes adjustment of approaches and communication to affected parties. The project manager follows up to ensure implementation and refers issues to higher levels and third parties as may be deemed necessary and appropriate. The response mechanism has clear guidelines of protecting information sources.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Multiple channels of communication including hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint desks at all project activity sites will be availed to communities. The project will additionally organize regular feedback meetings for special interest groups at community level. Project staff will be trained on how to handle special interest groups’ complaints with complaints desk and hotlines being made gender and age sensitive.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

At the point of engagement, all WHH staff are required to undertake the PSEA course as part of their induction. WHH staff are contractually bound to appreciate and disseminate the organizations policy on PSEA. Refresher trainings for staff will be held at project inception and staff prepared to disseminate to communities and report on violations. Communities will be informed during project inception meetings and all contact sessions with project staff. Moreover, reporting on PSEA will be an Integral part of community trainings on the feedback and complaint mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project inception meetings to be attended by communities and stakeholders willbe held at all locations. Issues to be covered in the meetings will include explanation of project objectives, methodology, activities and beneficiary selection procedures, responsibilities, and the rights thereof. Participants will additionally be sensitized on the project complaints and feedback mechanism and its application. Contact details and reporting tools for the mechanism will be shared and their use explained. Regular reminders on the importance of the complaints and feedback mechanism will be communicated during activity implementation and monitoring. Project communication including adverts and visibility materials will have messaging on CFRM as well as thereporting hotline contacts.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The IRNA assessment report of August 2021 found that Women and girls are exposed to risks of GBV as they walk long distances collecting water and firewood from relatively non-flooded villages. Through the rehabilitation, of old water facilities and installation of new infrastructure, the project will contribute towards reducing cases of GBV as the provision of water in nearby locations ensures their safety and security through reduced exposure to potential abuse.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will construct facilities that are context and climate sensitive in case of future disasters that cause displacement. The project beneficiaries will also be linked to other ongoing WHH projects that are working on creating dykes that prevent flood water causing massive population displacement.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The HNO of February 2022 identified lack of access to WASH services as a contributor to increased likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout. The project will provide WASH services in the form of water facilities, sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion thereby contributing to a reduction in the overall humanitarian needs of people in the targted area.
