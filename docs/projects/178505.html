Project Summary

The education system has been severely affected by the decades-long conf ict. One out of
three schools is either damaged, destroyed, or utilized for purposes other than education. Moreover, an estimated 180,000 educational personnel have left the system. The COVID-19 outbreak has had major implications on the education of school age children.
This project aims to increase safe and quality access to education to crisis affected children aged 5-17 years by rehabilitating damaged schools in a gender and disability sensitive modes and provision of non-formal education in community centres. School rehabilitation will focus on areas hosting large number of returnees, as well as priority locations identified by Education Sector.
The rehabilitation activities will target light and moderately damaged classrooms and WASH facilities with gender sensitive and disability friendly approach to safeguard the protection of boys and girls and ensure adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities. UNHCR pays specific attention to the future adolescents and youth populations that are able to contribute to resilience and social cohesion in their communities through facilitating their access to education. In line to AGD, AAP and rights-based approach, UNHCR will reach out to children and their parents during project monitoring activities and through focus group discussion seeking feedback including receipt of complaints regarding the project. Resources permit, UNHCR plans to rehabilitate 63 schools, and some 90,000 students will bene t from non-formal education activities.

4 - Needs

The education system has been severely affected by the decades-long conflict. One out of three schools is either damaged, destroyed, or utilized for purposes other than education. Moreover, an estimated 180,000 educational personnel have left the system.. The COVID-19 outbreak has also had major implications on the education of children with schools closed or working at reduced capacity for the majority of 2020. According to the 2021 Needs and Response Summary, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out, numbers that will certainly increase due to an overall increase in the school-aged population and the overall deteriorating situation.UNHCR’s education interventions focus on supporting access to formal education, through rehabilitation of schools and provision of accelerated education programmes in community centres to promote school enrollment and retention. This approach is also in line with UNHCR’s global education strategy.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposal builds on the achievements made in past years, and contributes to Sector Objective 1, Availability and Access and Sector Objective 3 Resilient Systems. UNHCR's interventions in education in 2021 will be line with the “No Lost Generation” initiative launched in 2013. With over 2.1 million children and youth out of school even before the pandemic, concentrated and joint efforts are essential to mitigate additional dropouts and ensure out-of-school children are back at school. UNHCR will participate in the Education Sector Working Group led by UNICEF and the Ministry of Education to coordinate education interventions and will respond to the identified needs with due consideration of security and resource limitations. UNHCR education interventions in 2021 will include: non-formal education programmes such as remedial classes and accelerated education, education activities during summer, preparation for placement tests to re-enrol in school and recreational activities. UNHCR education interventions will also be in line with Ministry of Education and World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines related to COVID-19. Additionally, school rehabilitation and teacher training programmes conducted under the Returnees pillar will also benefit IDPs and host communities.UNHCR will continue to explore innovative means of enhancing monitoring, and the use of innovative tools. The operation has developed a Project Reporting and Monitoring Database which is rolled out in 2021. Furthermore, UNHCR Syria is considering the expansion of third party monitoring, especially to locations where UNHCR lacks the capacity or security clearance to access. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Inaccessibility to education due to lack of safety and insecurity including contamination with explosives and mines. Mitigating measures: Ensure flexible and context informed mechanisms for school rehabilitation project to adjust to a changing context. Ensure that relevant authorities are aware of and are engaged with the projects and are committed to ensure basic safety/ access for project implementation and ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security measures including in mine risk education. Risk 2: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools Mitigating measures: Close coordination with local education authorities, community leaders and school management resulting in clear agreements on every actors' responsibility to create and maintain a safe learning environment. Regular monitoring to verify adherence to these agreements.Risk 3: Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Mitigating measures: Raise awareness among staff of UNHCR and its partners, as well as communities about SEA and that humanitarian aid and services are rights and provided free. Apply mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff members. Design specific complaint mechanisms and ensure adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems & provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities & feedback mechanisms.Risk 2: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply. Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning & implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price & reduced target. Revision of targets (reduction) as needed when price inflation exceeds resources available.Risk 3: Threat of military offensives and disturbance in safety and security of some project areas or changes of security situation restricting implementation. Mitigating measures: Prioritise rehabilitation activities in safe & accessible locations. Ensure that projects on school rehabilitation in insecure locations, if any, have security plans for staff and project beneficiaries have sufficient resources to consent to those plans.Risk 4: Rehabilitated schools not operational in a timely manner. Mitigating measures: Close coordination with relevant authorities to ensure the school is in the national plan and resources (funds, teachers) are allocated for this school, before rehabilitation work is started. Close monitoring/follow up by UNHCR, it's partner & communities
