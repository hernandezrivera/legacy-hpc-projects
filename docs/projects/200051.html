Project Summary

With Growing triple burden of malnutrition (undernutrition, both stunting and wasting; deficiencies in vitamins and other micronutrients; and overweight and obesity) among women, children and adolescents which affects health, growth and development.
70% of young children are not exclusively breastfed; 90% of children aged 6-23 months lack dietary diversity and meal frequency; over 40% of all women and children, including Lebanese, are affected by anaemia. In October 2021, 53% of children skipped a meal due to insufficient food in the house, up from 37% just 6 months before. 453,000 Lebanese women and children need urgent nutritional support.
Nutrition and child development should be prioritized as part of the emergency response plan, hence UNICEF will be working under the ERP appeal through a multi-system approach from food, health, social protection, education and water and sanitation systems to protect and promote adequate diets, practices and services to prevent all forms of malnutrition among children and women and support optimum early childhood development. In 2021, the percentage of childcare service providers encountering families with payment difficulties has doubled, reaching 41%. Additionally, due to the local currency devaluation and the significant increase in fuel prices, the monthly fees per child are no longer sufficient to cover the operational expenses of childcare providers. Therefore, to remain affordable, childcare providers are often constrained to compromise on quality which can negatively affect the healthy development of children, while children in rural areas, camps or informal settlements have little access to ECD.
Building on this momentum of a strengthened enabling environment for nutrition and child development, UNICEF will work with the government and partners to reach the most vulnerable children and women with an essential service package for prevention, early detection, referral, and management of all forms of malnutrition and development delays. Maternal and child nutrition during the first 1,000 days coupled with a nurturing care framework – from conception to age 2 years – remains core to the response.
This will be done by strengthening national capacity across multiple sectors and systems through better evidence, policies, and facility and community-based solutions and programmes.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Sector Objective 1: Young children U5 and their caregivers have access to and demand for services aimed at prevention of all forms of malnutrition and the associated developmental risks.UNICEF will support vulnerable PLWs and caretakers of children under 2 years with IYCF counselling integrated with hygiene promotion. Support, promote and protect early childhood development among caregivers of children under 5.Sector Objective 2: School age children and adolescents have access to and demand for services aiming at prevention of all forms of malnutrition– particularly among girls.UNICEF will promote knowledge and skills on healthy nutrition among school age children and adolescents, also benefiting from anemia prevention with adolescent girls receiving MMS.Sector Objective 3: Women have enhanced access to services aiming at prevention of all forms of malnutrition– particularly during pregnancy and lactation.Counseling services, education, awareness, and distribute MMS to prevent and address micronutrient deficiencies and stunting. Provide malnutrition treatment for PLWs.Sector Objective 4: Children have access to growth monitoring, screening, early detection and treatment of wasting in early childhood.UNICEF will provide preventive nutritional and micronutrient supplementation and vitamin A supplements to children U5.Support & procure needed treatment supplies of children with severe and moderate acute malnutrition. UNICEF will continue leading the sector coordination

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

UNICEF is committed in advancing PSEA and Child Safeguarding (CS) minimum standards within Implementing Partners (IPs) working under the ERP umbrella through the roll-out of the PSEA/CS capacity assessment followed by trainings, provision of technical support on PSEA and CS. Additionally, the communities coping mechanisms will also be assessed to mitigate adverse impacts on their safety. This is in line with Core Humanitarian Standard that encompasses the ‘Do no harm’ principle. UNICEF, through its mandate as sector lead U for nutrition sector will support partners to include the needs and voices of children, PLWs and persons with disabilities at all stages of the response. UNICEF, from a vantage point of co-leading the child protection sub-sector will ensure protection concerns for children are identified and addressed in line with the strategies outlined in UNICEF’s Core Commitment for Children for humanitarian action.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNICEF has made Accountability to Affected People (AAP) a priority in its Strategic Plan, UNICEF is adopting an institutional strategy and action plan for scaling up AAP. UNICEF’s responsibility to ensure programmes generate meaningful and relevant results and outcomes for girls, boys, women and men, in accordance with their specific needs, priorities and preferences. It involves working in ways to protect, facilitate and enable them to exercise their rights, including the right to safe, fair, equitable access to quality services and accurate, reliable and relevant information, the right to share their views and opinions about the quality and effectiveness of programmes, and participate in decisions that affect them. UNICEF will scale up the use of the SMS based 24x7 U-report system to ensure community feedback is collected and analyzed systematically. The U-report will complement other forms of community based beneficiary feedback and complaint handling mechanisms.
