Project Summary

Somalia has seen conflict, desert locust, and serious flooding since June affected 191,800 people in Hirshabelle, South West, and Jubaland states as well as Banadir region. Most of the 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia live in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Safety and security concerns remain a high priority for all affected populations, with displacement contributing to immediate and heightened exposure to violence, exploitation, and other forms of abuse. Serious risks and threats to safety and security. This project seeks to address dire humanitarian needs of protection for the IDPs and poor host communities in Banadir (Kahda, Daynile and Dharkaynley districts). The project is planned to include the protection mainstreaming guidelines to ensure that the beneficiaries access humanitarian aid at a convenient time and points considering the place of distribution as well as time. The project will involve all the target stakeholders to avoid conflicts and to facilitate effective targeting and inclusion of the most vulnerable households in the target locations. SCC proposed this project that will be implemented in a period of 12-months to benefit 3000 women, girls, and men in Banadir region. The project aims to sustain the provision of emergency protection and lifesaving services to reduce risks for women and girls at the target locations. The intervention will contribute to build resilience and restore dignity for the beneficiaries to gain control of their lives through the provision of case management and material support for the affected women and children in the target villages and IDP Camps.
The proposed project is to result in GBV timely response to the women and girls affected and displaced by the recurrent conflicts, COVID 19 and droughts, and in search of services and livelihood opportunities in the Banadir region. The displacements combined with COVID19 restrictions significantly presents protection challenges, particularly for women and children, as households lose traditional socio-economic safety nets and frequently experience extremely poor living conditions in Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) sites. There are significant information gaps on the specific protection vulnerabilities faced by IDPs and host communities in the target locations and SCC outlined the need for strengthened GBV integrated responses in Banadir region. The intervention will include the following activities: Activity 1.1: Awareness raising forums and campaigns for individuals targeted with rights-based public outreach and awareness-raising activities. Activity 1.2: Capacity building on protection for duty bearers, service providers, and community leaders. Activity 1.3: train and establish Community-based protection committees in Banadir region. Activity 1.4: Provision of training to GBV/Health workers to ensure clinical care for sexual assault survivors accesses health facilities with trained GBV/Health staff. Activity 1.5: conduct gender forums involving local authorities, law enforcement, women representatives, traditional community leaders, state duty bearers, service providers, and community leaders engaged in protection. Activity 2.1. provision of life-saving assistance to rape survivors through Cash voucher support to cope with post-trauma stress. Activity 2.2: Dignity kit distribution to women and girls of reproductive age (15-49Yrs) including rape survivors. Activity 2.3: Provision of solar lanterns to female households in the IDP camps.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The provision of cash or voucher is an appropriate option in this project as it empowers the affected survivors to decide on their own on how to meet their own needs using available local resources and improves their status In emergencies. the method is an appropriate response to the survivors meettheir immediate needs for food and non-food items, and to support the recovery of livelihoods. distributing cash can often meet people's immediate needs more quickly and appropriately than the direct distribution of commodities. Cash gives for the beneficiaries' choices and thereby preserves their dignity. Commodity distribution often poses logistical and security problems, and - in the case of food aid - it may disrupt local markets.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Cash interventions are an appropriate response for providing resources to people only when the market is functioning. In that case, SCC conducted a market assessment to consider a minimum standard for cash interventions. The market assessment was aimed to determine if the preconditions for cash interventions were met. The organization’s assessment has closely checked the availability of the stocks and prices and we have talked to the different traders if they have adequate stock to meet the demand or whether seasonal issues are impacting stock levels in the market and we came to know whether there is often enough to decide if cash programming was appropriate and whether the markets will be badly impacted by an in-kind distribution of goods. At the rapid market assessment, the traders were assessed to know if there was a market close to the beneficiaries, or will there were any major costs to the beneficiaries for transporting goods back from the market? were the needed items available in markets close by or could local traders bring them in. were there seasonal issues with the supply of the required items? were the needed items available in sufficient quantity to meet the demand? Could items be purchased by beneficiaries at a reasonable price?

Mitigation Measures

All SCC staff, field officers and all other and volunteers part of the implementation process like camp committee focal points, training facilitators, teachers and contractors will sign clauses relating to SEA Code of Conduct (CoC) to mitigate the risk of SEA cases which may occur in any place any time. Any infringement of the code will result in the termination of the contract and other relevant charges. SCC has implemented a similar project in the target location before and used stakeholders' participatory approach to establish possible risk reduction and discuss mitigation approaches where necessary to reduce risks in the course of humanitarian assistance. SCC shall apply the same approach in this project where applicable using the already established stakeholder networks to assist in establishing other networks in other target locations. Through these networks, the risks to the project implementation will be identified through involvement by the project team, appropriate stakeholders that will include an evaluation of environmental and security factors, organizational culture, and the project management plan including the project scope. Careful attention will be given to the project deliverables, assumptions, constraints, cost/effort estimates, resource plan, and other key project documents. A risk Management Log will be generated and updated as needed and will be stored electronically and as a hard copy printed on monthly basis in the project file. After identifying the risks SCC will assess the range of possible project outcomes and a matrix will be used to determine which risks are the top risks to pursue and respond to and which risks can be ignored as there is no big implication to the project outcomes. The probability and impact of occurrence for each identified risk will be assessed by the project manager, with input from the project team and the SCC project team will put an effort to reinforce child safeguarding in the target locations that SCC had already other projects running like education and WASH, in relation to building effective partnerships for children, particularly with teachers/head teachers, CECs WASH committees and community leaders have to ensure safety strategies with children to improve better environment and a better risk reduction mechanisms through improved risk management. These groups will develop a coordination team to avoid risks exposed to violence during service provision as a result of the safety in their target locations and environment.For the land dispute and evictions, SCC will enhance record keeping by recording details of the demographics of land tenure users, including gender, clan, and whether they are a refugee or an internally displaced person (IDP); Develop a code of conduct and ethics and accountability mechanisms; Formalize the ad hoc committees established at the IDPs settlement and district levels so that their decisions can be appealed at the regional level; Link the land disputes to the courts by filing all documents decisions with the regional court and establishing a Justice in Land Committee in each district.
