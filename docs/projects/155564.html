Project Summary

This project aims to improve WASH and meeting Basic Needs in the South West Region for Conflict-Affected Communities in Cameroon. To achieve this objective, the project proposes to rehabilitated 20 water points to improve access to water for displaced and host communities of the Fako and Meme divisions in the South West of Cameroon. In order to limit open defecation and prevent waterborne diseases, the IRC will built 100 emergency latrines, 100 emergency showers and 100 family latrines. 500 WASH kits and 500 NFI kits will be distributed to the most vulnerable households. The IRC will engage 80 community health workers (CHWs) in this project to ensure community participation and dissemination of messages on good hygiene practices in topics such as: key hand washing moments, water treatment at home, food hygiene, latrine use and maintenance, prevention of waterborne diseases, and environmental hygiene. The IRC will also train members of water management committees (WMCs) to ensure the sustainability of water points. In total, 40,300 individuals will be benefit from the WASH project including 32,240 IDPs and 8060 hosts.

Needs

IRC proposes to improve access to water and sanitation for IDPs and host communities in Meme, Fako and Ndian divisions, South-West Cameroun. Since 2016, Cameroon’s North West and South West regions have experienced social instability and violence, negatively affecting the area’s socio-economic situation. The socio-political crisis gradually turned into insecurity and armed violence as early as October 2017. Escalated tensions and multiple conflict outbreaks between the area’s Separatist Militias (SM) and the country’s defense and Security Forces (SF) have affected civilians; contributing to the internal displacement of many within the two regions. The number of households forced to leave their villages or even the country in search of safer areas has grown rapidly and steadily. The South West Region is the focus of crisis, with approximately 246,000 people displaced within region as of August 2018, of which 68% are estimated to be women. According to the evaluations conducted by the various partners, particularly UNICEF in 2018 in the south-west, it was generally noted that there was little access to quality drinking water in the majority of localities. In the Southwest, although most households have access to a safe source of water, either tap (82%) or protected well (18%), more than half of the population has access below the Sphere standard of 15 L / person / day recommended. the quarter reaching only 5 L / person / day. Only 57% of the population has hygienic latrines (latrines with handwashing / cleaning mechanisms, etc.). As a result, nearly half of them are at risk of becoming infected and suffering from diarrheal diseases at home through unhygienic latrines.
