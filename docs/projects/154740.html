Project Summary

Civilians in the accessible liberated areas in Ninewa and Anbar are faced with a difficult humanitarian situation. Large-scale destruction, explosive device contamination, and lack of basic services, all poses a threat to the population. Most of the liberated areas are contaminated with explosive ordnances, including unexploded ordnance, as a result of the conflict. In addition, IEDs are intentionally placed by the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) in and around schools, health centres, public- and residential buildings, as well as along main routes and roads.
NPA’s mine action intervention in areas contaminated with improvised landmines and IEDs comprises of survey, clearance, and risk education activities with special competence in working in areas contaminated by improvised landmines and IEDs. NTS activities are aimed at collecting and processing information on areas contaminated with improvised landmines and IEDs, and threat impact assessment among highly at-risk populations. NPA provides risk education integrated with NTS, aiming to reduce the vulnerabilities of highly at-risk groups of girls, boys, women and men. Technical survey and mine clearance of highly prioritised areas contaminated by improvised landmines and booby-traps, will improve community safety, allow livelihoods to be built again, homes to be renovated and following detailed surveys of the task areas coupled with strong community liaison, it will give the returning population the confidence to use the cleared land and transit safe access routes. For technical survey and clearance operations, NPA will primarily deploy manual clearance and technical survey teams with special competence in working in areas contaminated by improvised landmines. NPA's manual teams will be supported by a mechanical team that will increase safety and efficiency of operations through safe disruption of improvised mines and booby-traps, creating rapid access to suspect areas, assisting in rubble removal in high priority house/compound clearance tasks, and verification of suspected hazardous areas.
Long Term Objective: To facilitate the safe return of refugees and IDPs and enable the local population to safely resume normal activities, free from the threat of mines, cluster munitions, and other explosive weapons, and significantly contribute to Iraq government in fulfillment its obligations as state-party to the MBT and CCM.
The immediate Project objective 1: Survey and clear priority hazardous areas (in NPA’s area of operations), releasing land of improvised landmines and explosive devices, allowing access and return.
The immediate Project objective 2: Reduced vulnerability of the population living in the affected communities by improving their knowledge and promoting safe behavior in explosive contaminated areas.

Needs

Iraq is one of the countries in the world most severely affected by landmines; cluster munitions remnants and other explosive remnants of war. According to the Mine Action Review’s publications “Clearing the Mines 2018” and “Clearing the Cluster Munition Remnants 2018”, Iraq is among the four most contaminated countries in the world. The already serious contamination has been made significantly worse by the recent fighting in Iraq and enormous quantities of improvised mines and explosive devices in areas recaptured from ISIL. Starting in early 2014, ISIL advanced through the Iraqi governorates of Anbar, Diyala, Ninewa, and Salah-al Din, reaching as far north as the border of Dohuk governorate. The conflict and subsequent military operations by the Iraqi Security Forces (ISF), Kurdish forces, and militias to retake captured areas have displaced over 3.4 million Iraqis of various ethnic backgrounds and religions, while others chose, or were forced, to stay in their communities under the control of ISIL. Indiscriminate attacks and use of explosive weapons in populated areas are the primary causes of civilian casualties and displacement in Iraq. The various conflicts and shifting frontlines have led to contamination of explosive remnants of war in the country. Anbar, along with Ninewa, Kirkuk, Diyala and Salah al-Din governorates, is the most affected governorate in Iraq. Civilians in the accessible liberated areas in Anbar are faced with a difficult humanitarian situation. Large-scale destruction, explosive device contamination, and lack of basic services, all poses a threat to the population. Most of the liberated areas are contaminated with explosive ordnances, including unexploded ordnance, as a result of the conflict. In addition, IEDs are intentionally placed by the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) in and around schools, health centres, public- and residential buildings, as well as along main routes and roads. Despite the dangers, people are returning to claim their lives back.Based on the Multi Cluster Needs Assessment, conducted in July 2018 amongst a representative sample of 12,261 conflict-affected households nationwide, 22% of IDP households citing explosive hazards as a top reason for not intending to return (in camp), 18% of IDP households citing explosive hazards as a top reason for not intending to return (out of camp), 6% of households with members reported to be disabled due to explosive hazards (returnees). Vulnerable people in liberated areas continue to be severely impacted by the presence of IEDs, landmines and other ERW, while their safety is continuously put at risk, especially in the case of children. While disaggregated casualty figures are not available, according to casualty figures recorded by the United Nations Assistance Mission for Iraq (UNAMI) in 2017, at least 3,298 civilians were killed and another 4,781 were injured in acts of terrorism, violence and armed conflict in Iraq. The gap between current Mine Action assets available for deployment in areas liberated from ISIL and the needed assets to seriously address this problem in the areas that are accessible for survey and clearance so far is enormous.

Activities

1. Non-technical survey (NTS): Assessment of the scope and impact of contamination threat in accessible liberated areas: data collection and mapping. 2. Technical survey (TS) and clearance of explosive hazards in areas with reported accidents or where people are already exposed to the risks. -Technical survey and clearance of explosive hazards in communities prior to returnees’ arrival.3. Provide information to highly at-risk groups of girls, boys, women and men how to report hazards and how to stay safe. The primary/priority activities that will be implemented within this project are survey and clearance of explosive hazard contaminated areas, which the ultimate goal is saving lives and returning land to productive use. NPA’s clearance activities will improve the lives of those affected by contaminated land as it will remove the risk from IEDs, improvised landmines and other ERW, as well as decrease the level of fear that land users associate when using contaminated land. NPA’s clearance activities will allow livelihoods to be built again, homes to be renovated and following detailed surveys of the task areas coupled with strong community liaison, it will give the returning population the confidence to use the cleared land and transit safe access routes. As the mine, cluster munition, IED and other ERW problem is so large in the country; mapping and narrowing the extent of the contamination will greatly reduce efforts and resources required to clear it. Non-technical survey, assessment of the scope and impact of contamination threat through data collection and data mapping, is essential to determine contamination level, impact to the local population and ensure future clearance projects can take place in a methodical and cost-effective manner. NTS also supports and assists the GoI, through close coordination with national, regional, and local mine action authorities in achieving their main priorities with regards to mine action emergency response. As complementary activity, integrated with survey, NPA will conduct risk education to the people affected by explosive hazard contamination. Raising awareness of the risk of explosive hazards is aiming to reduce civilian casualties and promote safe behavioural change among the most vulnerable communities (returnees, IDPs, stayers), through the delivery of tailor-made Risk Education sessions, while creating an increased feeling of safety and protection among beneficiaries.The above activities will be implemented across Ninewa Governorate (areas of Hamdaniya and West Mosul) and Anbar Governorate (Areas of Ramadi, Falluja, Heet and Haditha).

How is your project accountable to affected population

NPA activities are conducted with the consent of the beneficiary populations, based on the affected community’s needs. Community Liaison activities are conducted to build a relationship with the beneficiary communities. NPA applies a socio-economic impact assessment methodology to its mine action work, referred to in NPA as the ‘Impact Assessment’ concept. Impact Assessment ensures that the needs at both the household and community levels have been identified prior to, during and after the clearance activities. This is ensured by holding community meetings and separate interviews with households directly affected. Community based risk education will be conducted based on the community needs assessment, that will include identification of risks in an explosive hazard contaminated community, identification of impacted group of population. Based on the assessment a community-based risk education will be implemented including providing of RE sessions for affected groups, through meetings with local population at community’s centres, presentations by method door to door. A gender sensitive approach at all stages of the project assists with identifying and responding to the needs for gender mainstreaming in humanitarian disarmament. NPA will continue to involve women, men, girls and boys in the critical information gathering process. Women, girls, boys and men will be consulted and acknowledged throughout the programme processes. Post Clearance Impact Assessment is conducted in order to evaluate the actual impact with the expected impact, ensure community satisfaction and lessons learned for the future. Staff solicit feedback and a complaints mechanism exists within the national mine action authority so that grievances can be lodged.
