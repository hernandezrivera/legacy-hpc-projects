Project Summary

Guided throughout Protection and Do no harm Principles, multiyear and multisectoral approaches, SIF (Secours Islamique France) proposed interventions for 2023 will directly contribute to the Early Recovery and Livelihood Sector’s priorities and objectives aligned with the sector logframe by delivering comprehensive sustainable dignified long-term solutions while attempting to prevent early marriage and child labour by a) Support the Maintenance & improve equitable access to basic services b) Increase access to Livelihood and job opportunities. c) Strengthen social cohesion by empowering communities to lead early recovery processes together. SIF multiyear interventions are stretching over 8 governorates identified according to the severity of the needs, their level of accessibility, and SIF’s technical, social assessments and expertise in Dara’a, As-Sweida, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Idleb and Der Ez Zor.
In line with the results of consultations with the local communities and relevant authorities, and taking into account sector priorities, the proposed activities will contribute to building the resilience of 94,100 community members through:
(1) Rehabilitating 2 VT schools in Rural Damascus and Homs.
(2) Rehabilitating 2 PHC centers in Homs and Aleppo.
(3) Providing and installing 590 solar panel devices in Der Ez-Zor, As Sweida, Homs, Hama, Aleppo and Rural Damascus.
(4) Debris Removal of 1,000 m3 in Idleb.
(5) Rehabilitating/enhancing 1 garden/park in Aleppo.
In 2022, a total of 331,478 direct community members benefited from SIF Early Recovery and Livelihood programmes.
Throughout the project cycle, SIF team will be guided by the accountability indicators, and participatory approach will be implemented through community based structured dialogues with the groups of different ages, gender and backgrounds, particularly those with special needs on ERL issues to ensure that any protection concerns are detected, addressed and resolved.
Projects monitoring and evaluation are ensured by SIF technical teams composed (not only) by the engineers and MEAL teams with regular field visit prior, during and post implementation to furthermore to evaluate the impact of the intervention throughout its cycle.
SIF will minimize protection risk of vulnerable population, and will facilitate a safe and dignified return and reintegration of IDPs and returnees along with creating suitable, safe living conditions, job opportunities for vulnerable communities in cohesion to our multisectoral programmes in Education, Protection, Shelter & Wash. SIF takes into account local capacities and encourage the contractors in recruiting workers from local communities. Accountability to affected population will be realized through strengthening community-based complaint mechanisms, improved participatory assessments and enhanced outreach through SIF MEAL and technical teams.
SIF will implement this project in close coordination with various stakeholders on the ground for an effective response (MoLAE, MoWR, NGOs, contractors, SARC, Clusters, local committees, refugee, IDP/returnee and host community leaders) in order to bridge gaps in available services and ensure better access of People in Needs to services.
SIF observes the sector guidelines and donor requirements. SIF is fully committed to the SPHERE standards and humanitarian principles based on which SIF’s internal policies and procedures have been developed to guide project implementation. SIF will capitalize on its extensive expertise in implementing ERL projects in the targeted locations.

3 - Needs

2023 marks 12 years into protracted complex humanitarian crisis in Syria with a prognostic of 13.8 million people in need of every sort of assistance in a country where over 90% of the population lives below poverty line. The severity of the humanitarian needs is deepening due to the spiral inflation and economy deterioration, the lingered impact of the sanctions affecting evermore the response by the humanitarian organisations & the continued effect of COVID 19 pandemic. The access to essential services remains critical need in both rural and urban areas. Prioritising ERL response would create the equitable linkage to other services such as Health, Wash, Education, Protection & Shelter for communities in inclusive and safe environments. The ERL intervention represents the main response articulating the provision of comprehensive lifesaving and adequate life sustaining assistance in a country where the crisis ravaged the local economic resources and infrastructures. The following needs were identified based on SIF assessments conducted in the last trimester of 2022 aiming to define our intervention for 2023:- Debris removal 102,000 M3. - Rehabilitation of 4 Vocational Schools- Rehabilitation of 8 PHC centers- And installation of 3,165 Solar Devices Through this intervention, SIF contributes in safeguarding the main 3 pillars of the humanitarian aid: Protection, Safety and Dignity.

4 - Implementation of activities

From HR mobilisation, processing Workplan, identification & selection of communities as well social & technical assessments are conducted by SIF in collaboration with the MoLAE, SARC, and based on SIF pre-defined objectives criteria. Results of technical assessments are endorsed by an appointed Committee composed of representatives: Municipality, governorate, local community members & SIF. The Committee will be also responsible of monitoring the implementation of the activities and issuing the handover documents. The Procurement, tendering process-contractors are governed by SIF guidelines-SOP ensuring transparent process, and liable intervention with our policies of anti-fraud, code of conduct, child safeguarding & PSEA. SIF intervention creates diverse short to longer terms employment opportunities for local communities, alongside 150 national team members enabling SIF to engage in direct implementation of its operation articulating neutral, impartial & independent action of humanitarian aid in spite of significant challenges bred by this complex humanitarian crisis. To ensure accountability & sustainability, SIF grants a period to each Municipality in case repairs are needed from post-implementation to exit strategy. This multiyear response is complementary to Shelter, Wash, FSA, Education and Protection interventions in the targeted areas, fostering an integrated approach to address needs and encourage community self-reliance and resilience.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Discrimination or perception of discrimination may occur if assistance isprovided to a specific group/ individuals while others are excluded. Likelihood:Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) To ensure uniform and transparent beneficiaryselections procedures which are agreed among the sector members and governmentauthorities and are clearly communicated to the community. (2) Ensure thatcomprehensive service mapping has taken place that risk mitigation andcommunication with communities are proactive and informed. (3) Ensure standardizeassistance package and guideline that are followed by the sector. Risk 2: Risk ofgender based violence (GBV) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) while accessingthe infrastructures. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: (1) conduct safetyaudit to ensure safe access of beneficiary to Shelter infrastructures sites. (2) ensureclose coordination and consultation with local communities on the accessibility ofinfrastructures in order to receive their inputs and understand their perspectives inrespect to safety of beneficiaries. (3) Consolation with women, girls, boys and menshould be held. (4) sensitizing others sectors in gender sensitive measures in ShelterAssistance (5) ensuring locks on each door to provide privacy to girls and women andreduce risk of sexual abuse.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual exploitation of beneficiaries in return of promise for assistance.Mitigation measures include the following: (1) SIF ensures that specific PSEAindicators are included in the monitoring framework. (2) Vetting process is conductedduring the recruitment of a candidate at application and reference stages. (3) The codeof conduct, which includes the strict prohibition of SEA, is signed by everyone workingfor and with SIF, including staff, SIF volunteers and contractors. After the selection ofthe contractors, as the same time of the signature of the purchase order, the contractorsigns the SIF Code of conduct which is then attached as an annex to thecontract/agreement. (4) SIF employees are trained on PSEA and a PSEA focal point isassigned in all governorates targeted by SIF. Awareness sessions of the communitymembers on PSEA are conducted every year. Sessions includes the definition of thewhat is SEA and channels of reporting/whistleblowing. (5) SIF community-basedcomplaint mechanisms is accessible to affected populations in all response locations.(6) A process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of allegations of SEA hasbeen developed by SIF.
