Project Summary

The proposed intervention will improve access to quality education, increase access to critical WASH inputs and enhance child protection mechanisms in schools for drought- and conflict-affected students and their surrounding communities in Belet Weyne,
, Bulo Burto, Bossaso, Iskushuban, Qandala, Caluula, Garoowe, Eyl and Gaalkacyo districts of Somalia. The project will target 12, 100 children (8,685 non-displaced and 3,415 displaced). Among these 12,100 children 6,655 will be boys and 5,445 will be girls) Education specific activities will include the provision of education supplies, emergency take-away food rations for students to meet their minimum caloric needs, emergency teacher incentives to motivate teachers, and sanitary kits for adolescent girls to encourage their regular attendance at school. Specific WASH activities will include increasing schools’ water storage capacity through the provision of PVC storage tanks, safe water delivery to schools, the construction and rehabilitation of latrines and water storage tanks. Specific Protection activities will include case management for separated and unaccompanied children and GBV survivors, mapping and sharing of existing child protection service providers and their respective services to school administrators and communities, strengthening child protection systems in schools, including the recruitment and training of child protection focal persons at each school, linking schools to referral pathway outside the school and facilitating the development of school-level child protection action plans. Relief International (RI), through child protection committees, will deliver messages to schools and communities on key protection concerns affecting the community. These messages will target prevention as well as the response in the event protection concerns arise. RI will provide training to CECs on their roles and responsibilities, food and water management, disaster risk reduction (DRR), civic skills, and child protection in the respective schools. To ensure water availability in the targeted schools, RI will truck safe water to schools but CECs will manage its distribution. Further, the CECs will be trained on DRR to build their capacities and improve school preparedness against future disasters. Child- and gender-friendly latrines in the targeted schools will be rehabilitated or, where necessary, constructed to minimize disruptions in learning for girls and boys. RI will coordinate a back-to-school campaign with CECs and religious leaders to encourage school enrollment with an emphasis on children affected by displacement. RI, in collaboration with the appropriate ministries, will map existing referral pathways and service providers and link schools to these services. Where pathways are weak or non-functional, RI will strengthen them to ensure effective response to child protection concerns. To monitor the effectiveness of the child protections systems implemented and capture lessons learned, RI will facilitate joint review workshops with schools, service providers, and relevant government ministries. Through engagement with ministry officials and CECs, RI will leverage the respective agency apparatus and community assets to raise awareness among children on child protection and the established referral mechanisms which will serve to strengthen child protection in schools and the larger communities.

Mitigation Measures

"Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement"The displacement crises in Somalia due to prolonged drought has severe effects on the lives of people. This has led to separations and child protection issues. The project specifically targets displacement affected communities, including IDP settlements. Based on an awareness that children from displaced families are likely to be enrolled in school, further, ‘back to school’ campaigns will particularly target in IDPs settlements, including door-to-door visits to households in IDP camps will also contribute the enrollment of children in schools. In areas where formal school facilities are not available, temporary learning spaces will be constructed/ rehabilitated to cater for the IDP population. In formal schools which have seen a recent influx of displaced children, RI will construct additional classrooms to accommodate the additional population. Teacher incentives, WASH provision and distribution of dry food rations, will act as an incentive to stay, thus helping prevent further displacement, and will meet the needs of those already displaced who are often most in need with a lack of available of assistance. Availability of water, food and educational service will further contribute to avoid displacement. The school child protection assessments and related analysis will consider displacement as a key area of concern: identifying any issues relating to discrimination by teachers or pupils on the basis of displacement status, and exploring the particular child protection challenges faced by girls and boys affected by displacement. RI conducts Post Distribution Monitoring, enabling us to verify that whole school populations have been reached with services such as food provision, ensuring displaced children and teachers are not excluded. Data collected by RI at the school and individual level is disaggregated by displacement status wherever possible."Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age"RI acknowledges that there are differential risks for girls and boys and across the age cohorts. The project is designed to ensure the speciﬁc needs of girls are met in recognition of the particular challenges they face. For example, all school latrines will be built according to a girl-friendly design. In addition to the economic and contextual factors faced by girls in general, adolescent girls across Somalia are strongly encouraged to drop-out of school to marry; girls start menstruating and the majority have been subjected to FGM, the combination of which leads to adverse physical and psychological effects, with knock-on impact on their education access and performance. Activities that will particularly help meet the related needs including the provision of sanitary kits and menstrual hygiene information, and training of female teacher mentors to provide psychosocial and mentoring support to the girls. CECs will be trained on the different needs and challenges faced by girls and boys, and appropriate responses. School Development Plans developed by CECs will have speciﬁc activities targeting girls, including actions to be taken to keep girls in school and target drop-outs for re-enrollment. In training of CEC members, RI will insist on gender spilt amongst participants as much as possible, recognizing the importance of female representation in school community management. Community sensitization campaigns, in which CECs will play a leading role, the value of education for both girls and boys will be emphasized. Child protection assessments in schools will include consideration of the speciﬁc issues faced by girls in the school, including areas such as harassment. As mentioned above, Post Distribution Monitoring exercises will be conducted to ensure that no children are being excluded from receiving services on the basis of gender or age. All data collected by RI at the individual level is disaggregated by gender, and further by age where appropriate

Please provide further explanation

The project aims to support the larger formal education system by empowering communities and relevant ministries in its management.Our education project is durable in that it- puts children in schools and retains them there- works with the relevant ministries in each region/state- trains CECs giving them long-term skills which will contribute to sustainabilityThe project will redesign the CEC training manual to ensure it is relevant to the required roles and responsibilities of the CECs with regards to child protection and school management. School staff orientated in child protection and safeguarding Support will be given to schools through the respective ministries to ensure referral mechanisms are in place and continue to be operational beyond the life of the project. Through the holistic approach, by the end of the project, a range of stakeholders connected to the school will be pushing for change to be sustained – teachers, ministry officials, community members and children themselves.Relief International will continue to engage with the Somalia Child Protection Working Group and the Protection Cluster, and will exchange learning with group members. Tools developed through the programme will be shared with the group, as well as lessons in best practice regarding child protection in schools. Therefore it is expected that other actors will replicate systems and the impact will be spread to schools beyond the target schools, and sustained beyond the project lifetime
