Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide life-saving and essential health and nutrition services for conflict and disaster affected people in need in White Nile and Gedaref States. The targeted localities are Aj Jabalain , and As Salam/ Ar Rawat in White Nile; Al Fao, Alfashaga, East Galabat, Al Mafaza, Ar Rahad, Basundah, West Galabat, and Madeniat Al Gedaref in Gedaref State.
The project will directly support 65 health facilities with the deployment of health/nutrition workers, provision of essential supplies and standardized service delivery, and scale-up of nutrition services in the targeted localities to mitigate and prevent morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition. Medical Teams International (MTI) will focus on the immediate life-saving nutrition needs that include Out-patient treatment and in-patient treatment of severe acute malnutrition, treatment of moderate acute malnourished under-fives and global acute malnutrition among Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in prioritized localities. Preventive nutrition Infant and Young Child Feeding practices, emergency supplementary feeding programme (e-BSFP) for under-fives and PLW, within the crisis-hit populations, Food based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) for under 2 children and PLW at risk, provision of micronutrient powders to children under 5 years (Home Fortification) and salt iodization, treatment and prevention of anaemia, Vitamin A supplementation, and capacity building.
To provide life-saving health and nutrition services in difficult-to-reach locations, it will also deploy one mobile health unit in White Nile.
This project will directly help 696,280 beneficiaries in total ; 319,500 refugees, 30,000 IDPs, 346,780 Host community/ Nationals. All demographic groups; 362,066 females, 334214 males, 139256 children U5, 557024 adult, 20888 elderly, 1044 disabled and 34814 PLWS are among the beneficiaries. The services would be specifically designed to encompass preventive, promotional, and curative actions. The approach is comprehensive including the linkage of services from community outreach to secondary health care ensuring a continuum of care at all levels.
The project will also contribute to improving the capacity of local communities and MOH towards enhancing prevention and health care-seeking practices, mitigating, and responding to disasters and outbreaks. This humanitarian intervention will be carried out in close cooperation with MOH, UNHCR, COR, UNICEF, WFP, IOM, the local community, and other partners. To make sure that beneficiaries are getting high-quality services that fit their needs and ensure their active participation and engagement through the process, the project will include accountability measures such as feedback channel sat all levels and KPC surveys priority localities. The project will also rely on core humanitarian principles and common humanitarian standards. The project activities and indicators are aligned with the overarching humanitarian framework and objectives. Robust monitoring and quality assurance mechanisms will be laid out to ensure the success of this project.
