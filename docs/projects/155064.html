Needs

"While 2018 has seen significant improvement in the level information sharing with the Rohingya refugees, several key gaps remain. The dissemination and thus the access of beneficiaries to this information is still limited especially for the most vulnerable such as elders or person living with physical disability. The use of a single information dissemination channels remains wide and thus limits the reach of these messages to all segments Rohingyas communities. In many cases, information sharing take place in a “top-down” manner with limited rooms for feedbacks by the beneficiaries. If most camps are now covered with at least one complaint and feedback mechanism, there is limited standardization of the collection of the complaints collection and on the response process. A strong focus on the quality of both the information disseminated as well as the information dissemination and sharing mechanisms such as the Feedback & Information Centers is required to ensure that beneficiaries have both a meaningful access to information and opportunity to feedbacks. Communication with Communities is challenged by the language barrier that remains between humanitarian actors and beneficiaries. The engagement of host community and the development of adequate CwC strategy for host community will be required as this is an identified gap in 2018. This need will continue to grow as tensions are likely to rise between host and displaced communities in the light of the unlikely large-scale return of Rohingya to Myanmar. Community engagement at large will remain a major gap to be addressed in 2019 based on the ongoing work and discussion lead by the Site Management Sector and RRRC in terms of community governance. This will lead to continuous research on existing social cohesion and fracture lines both within the Rohingya population as well as between the host and displaced communities. In 2019, a strengthened and stable CwC working Group will be essential for the coordination of the CwC response to meet the information needs of both refugees and host communities, in coordination with all other Sectors. The evolving humanitarian and political context will require continuous advocacy for greater accountability to affected populations and community engagement. "

Activities or outputs

"1. Support to Coordination - Support the CwC Working Group Coordination team to advocate for CwC inclusion across sectors - Coordinate collective service mechanisms - Provide sectors with the technical advice and guidance needed to create cohesive communication and messaging to all affected populations- Lead research initiative on the changing information needs of both displaced and host communities as well as on social cohesion2. Communication with Communities - Provide a humanitarian information service for radio networks designed to add value by providing a comprehensive and coordinated information-based response to the emergency- Conceptualize, pre-test and produce a wide range of communication outputs for Site Management Support agency (SMS) - Develop and disseminate an audio drama program to explore longer-term, more deep-rooted issues connected to attitudes and social norms- Establish and maintain Feedbacks & Information Centers (FIC) to provide localized and reliable source of information, communication and accountability means (at minimum one FIC per 12,000 individuals in all camps in IOM SMS AoR). - Establish and run information sharing and dissemination mechanisms in camps such as Radio Listening Groups, Town Halls meetings, Sensitization campaigns, outreach works, etc. - Conduct regular Focus Group Discussions on all relevant topics.- Analyze on weekly & monthly basis at camps and AoR level all feedbacks (formal & informal received), minutes of meetings (Camp level coordination meetings, blocks meetings, etc.), etc. to ensure identification and prioritization of action required (referral to partners, IOM actions, etc.) 3. Community engagement - Support and advise Site Management Support (SMS) agencies in the roll-out of the Community Representation Guidelines - CRG (from SM Sector/RRRC)- Develop & implement jointly with SMS teams a communication strategy for the roll-out of the CRG using existing CwC mechanisms, focusing notably on the election process - Organize dedicated consultations with the communities at all the stage of the process to ensure inclusion of feedbacks and real-time adjustments of the process & planned activities- Document the roll-out of the CRG, especially at its pilot phase"
