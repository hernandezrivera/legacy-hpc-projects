Project Summary

The ongoing project will continue to support the GBV Protection sector in Borno state by providing a comprehensive approach in implementing complete Gender-Based Violence packages and accountability to affected people in the hotspot locations of Gwoza LGA in Pulka and Konduga LGA. Malteser International will provide GBV case management programming including caring for survivors of GBV, Mental health, and Psychosocial Support Services (age-appropriate) Including Counselling, psychoeducation, Art therapy, adult literacy section, recreational activities.
Through the provision of GBV case management and specialized services immediate needs of survivors (women and girls) formerly associated with armed groups & forces/trafficking will be addressed, women and girls safe space facilities, and referral (one-stop centers) will be equipped to strengthen the capacity of case management for the immediate response to GBV related cases. Malteser International will provide and distribute standardized, culturally appropriate dignity kits to address the critical needs of survivors who are either affected by displacement-related issues or domestic violence. The ongoing program will support and proactively participate in field-level coordination, periodic service mapping, and update location-specific referral pathways. Peer support, mentoring/coaching, skills building, and empowerment targeting adolescents and adults as well as relevant capacity building and engagement with stakeholders and security actors will be integrated during program implementation. The program will establish linkages to meaningful and protective livelihood activities and promote resilience by organizing specialized skills building and empowerment actions (Tailoring, Cap knitting, soap making, beads).
The project will integrate GBV risk mapping/safety audit into GBV response to reduce the risk of GBV in the community such as early/child marriages, survival sex SEA, etc, and mainstream it into the WASH program to mitigate risks of GBV at related water and sanitation facilities. Conduct awareness and capacity building for staff, community leaders, gender champions, and non-GBV staff including the water user committee on GBV services and how to make safe referrals to appropriate services.

1 - Needs

Malteser International identifies humanitarian needs and responds accordingly by relying on the primary source of information through conducting its own needs assessment and secondary source of information from the Humanitarian Needs Overview conducted by UN-OCHA as well as relevant information from GBV protection sub-sector and multi sectoral need assessment. According to the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, More than 1.5 million people IDPs, members of the host communities, and returnees—need GBV prevention and response services across north-east Nigeria. All forms of GBV are mostly underreported. In particular, reporting an incident of sexual violence (rape or sexual assault) is hindered by the risk of stigmatisation, retaliation, shame or marginalization and oppression from the community, as well as lack of survivor-centred service provision after referrals. Physical assault, sexual violence, denial of resources, opportunities and services, and psychological and emotional abuse are among the violations most frequently reported, recurrently perpetrated by intimate or former partners (domestic violence). Girls and boys under the age of 18 years are also at risk of GBV, mainly occurring within the context of child sexual abuse and child/early marriage. There exist significant gaps in service provision as survivors do not have access to comprehensive GBV services. Besides, there is a clear need to strengthen GBV service provision, prevention and risk mitigation mechanisms, particularly for women and girls. Provision of safety measures (including safe energy options) are needed to reduce women and girls’ vulnerability; fetching firewood has been identified as posing high risks to GBV, especially for girls and women. Increased access to empowerment and livelihood opportunities will further mitigate sexual exploitation and abuse, denial of resources and intimate-partner violence due to constrained financial resources. Also, there is a need to address widespread intimate partner violence often exacerbated by conflict and negative social norms. Given increased and multiple displacements due to the deteriorating security situation vis-à-vis planned returns, it is expected that the risks and exposure to GBV will escalate. Reports of intimate-partner violence are mainly attributed to the stress by partners who are unable to provide for the family are expected to increase due to the economic constraints aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Whilst the coverage of GBV services increased in 2021, the need for GBV services and risk mitigation have simultaneously increased, thus outweighing the capacity of existing actors to respond. This calls for not only greater capacity-building of existing actors but also scale-up of the number of actors responding to the GBV needs of the affected population across geographical locations most in need.Malteser International actively participated in the data collection for the multi-sectoral needs assessment (MSNA) of 2021. In close cooperation with REACH and the inter-sector working group, Malteser International conducted needs assessments in its project implementation areas and submitted the data for compilation. In addition, Malteser International has been carrying out regular service monitoring as well as participatory methods, such as focus group discussions, taking into account the differing needs of men, women, girls, boys, people with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Malteser International is currently implementing protection services at Pulka, Gwoza LGA, and Mashamari, Konduga LGA providing psychosocial support, GBV case management, and skill acquisition/empowerment to GBV survivors. Aligning with its protection program Malteser International will ensure streamlining of overall protection strategies in its WASH activities to reduce risks to vulnerable populations through its designs and activity implementation. Malteser International Nigeria will strive to reduce the risks from exposure to GBV and protection issues through creating a safe, dignified and friendly environment for women and children making facilities safely accessible to individuals with special needs. Malteser International will adhere to the guidelines set by the IASC for Integrating Gender-Based Violence mainstreaming in humanitarian action and the Humanity and Inclusion guidelines in the design of facilities and services for people with disabilities. To ensure safety and dignified access to facilities for women, children, and people with disabilities, Malteser International will undertake the following program approaches • Construction/rehabilitation of latrines which are gender-segregated for women and men and constructed at suitable locations and distances which will be discussed and agreed upon with community members. • Setting up separate women and men’s committees in Focus Group Discussion (FGD) to provide spaces for the participation of and accountability to the community about the design, implementation, information sharing, and opportunity for feedback. • A gender-sensitive approach to MEAL will be adopted to ensure that the program is having intended impacts to meet the needs of women, men, boys, and girls while addressing sexual exploitation and abuse. • Installation of strong locks from inside doors of latrines to ensure privacy and protection while latrines are used by girls, boys, and women. • Installation of waste bin inside the latrine designed for women and girls to dispose of sanitary items appropriately, disposal of the content from the waste bin to the communal disposal sites will be a part of the regular cleaning and maintenance schedule which will be supervised by female hygiene and sanitation volunteers. • In each block of 5 latrines rehabilitated, 1 latrine will be designated to people with disabilities. This latrine will be provided with a door that is wide enough to ensure access to those with limited mobility and their caregivers where suitable; handles and other utensils to support mobility will also be installed to facilitate the use of the latrine. To ensure the project enables equal and impartial access to WASH services with consideration of people with special needs, MI will undertake the following activities: • Hygiene promotion volunteers will be dispatched to homes of the people with limited mobility and elderly people to present the content from the community level hygiene and sanitation activities as well as obtain their perspectives for the design of the services and facilities to be more accessible to their special needs. • Hygiene kits and other items such as chlorine tablets will be delivered to individuals with disability/ limited mobility to their homes, rather than to general distribution sites for the general population • Hygiene promoters will use IECs for special presentations of hygiene practices to individuals with impaired hearing, if any, in order to ensure equal/ equitable access to messaging

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In ensuring integrity and accountability in our work as per the Core HumanitarianStandard, feedback from beneficiaries will be received via the following channels;•Community Volunteers will be the primary channel through which MI receivesfeedback from the camp about its Services. Community volunteers are functional in allcamps where MI is rendering WASH Services. •Complaint Desk: The M&E will station acomplaint desk at the distribution site to record requests, complaints, and feedback frombeneficiaries during NFI distributions. M&E officers will resolve simple complaints at thedistribution spot and refer a complex one to the team after distribution. •Feedbackboxes (suggestion boxes): will also be used to gather feedback from beneficiaries andother stakeholders. The boxes will be located inside the camp. Complaints/suggestionswill be retrieved at the end of the month by dedicated MI staff in the presence of theCamp Leaders. •Protection caseworkers will use WGSS to facilitate educational andrecreational activities and collect feedback from survivors after rendering services toascertain the survivor's satisfaction with the support provided and welcomesuggestions if any. •Additional informal channels for capturing feedback, requests, andcomplaints from camp and host communities include periodic community meetingsand focus group discussions conducted by staff from the (WASH, WGSS caseworkers, and M&E department), regular post-distribution monitoring visits provide additionalopportunities for IDPs to share feedback. Awareness and Information will becommunicated to beneficiaries through community meetings, FGDs, or face-to-faceinteractions with volunteers and staff. The Feedback Response Mechanism operateson the principle of confidentiality, meaning that complaints made through themechanism are not shared beyond people who need to know about them to addressthem.
