Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2022), more people are likely to experience severe food insecurity in 2022 than in 2021 with an estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). The purpose of the project is to mitigate humanitarian crises in South Sudan by ensuring provision of needs-based emergency aid to prevent or decrease morbidities, mortality and acute malnutrition especially for vulnerable people in acute humanitarian crises triggered by flight and displacement, droughts, floods and anticipated periods of hunger. The Humanitarian Needs Overview published in February 2022 states that climate change continues to impact different sectors, particularly agriculture, which is one of the most climate-sensitive economic sectors. Above normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2021 led to prolonged flooding, which impacted areas that had not flooded in previous years. Implementation of project activities is planned for Panyijar where the community has not been spared from shocks triggered by, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services. Pankiar in classified in IPC Phase 4. The project aims to address issues of climate change through training of 20 DRR committees to develop vulnerability mappings for their specific context on boma (village level) or payam level (sub county level), depending on context. The committees will present a cross-section of the population, since all ages and genders will be represented. The project will therefore target 56,000 beneficiaries in total (33,600 in 2022 only) in the form of IDPs, returnees and host communities and more specifically, Women and children headed households, including women in reproductive age and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), unaccompanied minors, people with disabilities, elderly and chronically ill with various activities that include distribution of NFI kits, cash for work, agriculture training, distribution of ruminants and support to livestock health. The project will seek to reduce deterioration of people’s physical and mental wellbeing coping mechanisms. Target groups will additionally be linked to existing WASH interventions implemented by Welthungerhilfe and other agencies so that they can benefit for the services.

1 - Needs

The project will be implemented in Panyijiar where the Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report for Panyijar County, Unity State of August 2021 highlighted that the food insecurity situation remains elevated due to insecurity, the impact of COVID-19, persistent poor macroeconomic conditions, and the impact of flooding on livelihoods. The FAO Flood Impact Report of December 2021 showed that Unity state suffered the highest livestock loss in the country with the 354,725 losses out of a population of 2,589,634 making up a loss of 13.70%. the state is also reported to have suffered a crop production loss of 137 metric tons. The floods caused massive displacement of communities which will result in high post-harvest losses for any surviving crop due to poor post-harvest handling and storage facilities. The needs in Panyijiar are quite high as indicated by the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2022 which reported that that the county has 90,000 people out of of population of 120,000 classified as people in need (75%). 66% of the people in need fall in the severity phase, 20% in the extreme severity phase and 14% in the catastrophic severity phase. Based on the IPC projections, the food insecurity situation in Panyijar is classified as an emergency (IPC 4) with very high likelihood of households sliding to the worst acute food insecurity phases if humanitarian food assistance is not provided on time. 2 - Project activities

The project activities are in alignment with the cluster objectives to prevent famine, enhance production and strengthening ability to absorb shocks. Under CLFSC/CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3). The project will support 24,500 by undertaking cash for work/cash for asset activities for extended basic needs and early livelihood recovery. The cash will provide households with the opportunity make make their own decisions in regards to food needs and other coping mechanisms. In meeting the CLFSC/CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5), the project will distribute agricultural seeds for food production amongst targeted beneficiaries and will also support them, through agriculture training. Livestock support will be provided through provision of small ruminants (which are not prone to livestock diseases), fodder production, livestock vaccination and basic medical care for livestock to preserve the existing herd. In contribution to meeting CLFSC/CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks the project will provide on-the-spot-training on emergency agricultural practices and development of emergency preparedness plans for early detection of droughts, floods, famines, pandemics, animal diseases, plant diseases, and pests. Under the project Output A; Provision of local and immediate emergency assistance to enable the protection of especially children, women, elderly and people with disabilities in humanitarian crises.Activities will include:(1) Creating district/county-based vulnerability mappings/profiles, (2) Early Warning training, public awareness and education to the communities on critical aspects of disaster mitigation.(3) Developing emergency preparedness plans and establishing community-based mechanisms for early detection of droughts, floods, famines, pandemics (Covid-19, cholera outbreaks), animal diseases (e.g. foot-and-mouth), plant diseases, and pests (e.g. Fall Army Worm and locusts) (4) Distribution of NFI kits.(5) Live-saving activities in the form of water transport and purification. Under the project Output B: Enabling the targeted population to cover individual basic food and non-food needs in the short-term include:(1) Protecting essential livelihoods through distribution of agricultural/garden kits, (2) Training and reinforcement on emergency agricultural practices for short-term food production and(3) Cash for Work/Cash for assets (this activity will help to increase theincome of the target group, strengthen their resilience while at the same time creatingcommunity assets and boosting the local economy,.(4) Distribution of agricultural seeds (restoration of livelihoods to a small degree)(5) Distribution of small ruminants which are not prone to livestock diseases and therefore easier to manage at household level.(6) Fodder, vaccination, basic medical care for livestock to preserve the existsing herd.(7) Destocking livestock (Emergency slaughtering for immediate food access) (8)Agricultural training.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will undertake cash for work activities. Cash will allow households to make their own decisions and priorities thereby contributing to their dignity. Cash is a less visible form of assistance than in-kind aid, reducing the risk of extortion or theft. Cash also acts as a strong incentive for quality work in construction of dykes within the identified areas. Cash transfers will be implemented to target groups participating in Cash for asset activities. Welthungerhilfe is opting for cash because it can be used for multiple purposes by communities thus improving livelihoods beyond the food dimension. Use of cash avoids transportation and handling costs of materials with and offers the possibility of utilizing the cash earmarked for such costs on other activities. Welthungerhilfe has standing agreements with cash transfer agents to ensure security of the cash particularly during its transportation sites where beneficiaries receive it.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

South Sudan has a relatively upcoming market. The cash for work component will incentivize functionality of the market. Availability of cash within communities provides an opportunity for the market to access multiple streams of inflows of income who's trickle down effect helps to enhance the market further. Being a member of the South Sudan cash working group, Welthungerhilfe receives regular updates on markets from the clusters which have been used in the design of the proposed intervention. Welthungerhilfe participates in market monitoring data collection in all areas of operation and in case in case of a sudden limited functionality of markets or unsafe access to markets, WHH will change the delivery modality from cash to in-kind approach where feasible and advisable.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All issues will be attended to in the shortest possible time and escalated if need be for decision making. The feedback will be handled by different levels including project management committees to influence needed adaptations to project assistance. Feasible adaptations will be communicated to all relevant stakeholders and implemented as appropriate.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Internal mid-term reviews and lessons learnt workshops will be held to ensure all feedback is considered accordingly. Individuals from the moist vulnerable groups will also be provided with the opportunity to represent their groups issues in some of the meetings and workshops. Channels of communication including hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint desks at all project activity sites will be availed to communities. The project will additionally organize regular feedback meetings for special interest groups at community level. Project staff will be trained on how to handlespecial interest groups’ complaints.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Project inception meetings, activity mobilization meetings, information posters and loud hailers will be used to provide information on PSEA. All WHH staff are contractually bound to appreciate and disseminating the organizations policy on PSEA. Refresher trainings for staff will be held atproject inception and staff prepared to disseminate to communities and reporton violations. Moreover, reporting on PSEA will be an Integral part of community trainings on the feedback and complaint mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through feedback interactions which may be conducted through meetings, mail boxes, and even one to one sessions with concerned persons. Regular monitoring surveys, including post-distribution monitoring will also provide opportunities for information sharing. Welthungerhilfe will deliberately organize feedback meetings at community level. Normally, all Welthungerhilfe projects and locations have a feedback analysis committee chaired by respective project managers. The committee analyses feedback immediately it is received and decides on responses. Responses are then directed to the responsible units for implementation which includes adjustment of approaches and communication to affected parties.
