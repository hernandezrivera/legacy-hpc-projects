1 - Needs

Since 2010, the armed conflict in North-East Nigeria has heavily affected the populations of the Borno State. In addition, frequent epidemics, including the impact of Covid-19, extreme climate events together with the reduction of livelihood resources have devastating consequences. The number of internally displaced persons (IDP) in the North East is up to 1.9 million . In 2021, the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance is up to 8.7 million, including 1.2 million in areas that are difficult to reach or inaccessible to humanitarian actors. Monguno has a total population of 186,018 individuals. Of this number, 84.5% (representing 157,199 individuals) are internally displaced people (IDPs) coming from the surrounding LGAs. Of the total IDP population, 89% live in IDP camps while the rest is living in the host community. Women and children constitute 80% of the total IDP population living in camps. According to the population Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) conducted by IOM in December 2020, Monguno itself is hosting 9% of the total IDP population in Borno State.

2 - Activities

To cover GBV issues, PUI wants to train 240 women, men, adolescents, and community leaders on gender-based violence by enhancing inclusive access to general protection information through community members’ awareness. PUI will implement a gender-inclusive strategy involving 600 referrals and accountability practices training for 300 individuals and reinforce community awareness reaching additional 5,760 persons through recreational activities provided by PSS teams in women and girls community safe spaces. By targeting 480 adolescent girls and 120 pregnant women with urgent needs, PUI will offer tailored SRH information, including safe delivery and motherhood. Furthermore, to allow survivors and/or at risk of GBV individuals to express themselves, PUI foresees to assist them with individual counseling in safe spaces. PUI strategy envisages involving local community leaders by training them on protection related topics to guarantee a continuity of the awareness on these themes. Moreover, targeted women and adolescent girls will receive support with dignity and menstrual hygiene kits.PUI will implement key nutrition and health-related messages dissemination. 20 Mothers Care Groups (MCGs) will be set up and trained by 10 community nutrition mobilizers (CNMs) through 24 bi-weekly sessions focusing on the IYCF training model. PUI will provide IYCF counseling to 300 mothers of 0-23 months to improve their knowledge of young child feeding practices. Moreover, screening of 3,277 children 6-59 months for malnutrition will allow referral of SAM with medical complications to PUI SC supported under BHA funding and SAM without medical complication to one of the nearby OTP sites. Children referred to SC will also receive child stimulation activities in PSS mother and child space. PUI will manage 300 children with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) through the Tom Brown model of supplementary feeding using local nutritious ingredients (soya bean, millets, groundnuts and sorghum) to prepare in the form of powder to make porridge for children 6-59 months. It will be coupled with refresher training of health staff on the management of acute malnutrition. PUI will provide free of charge Outpatient consultation for all ages to 37,130 IDPs and host communities in Gana Ali PHCC and with free of charge referral linkage to secondary health facilities for emergency cases needing higher-level care with full referral support. PUI will provide comprehensive service package for SRH based on the six objectives of IAWG . While supporting immunization services for children and pregnant women with Tetanus Toxoid in collaboration with the Ministry of health (MoH), PUI will provide incentives and transportation to MoH staff and keep promoting immunization of vaccine-preventable diseases. PUI will procure and deliver essential and drugs to manage communicable and non-communicable diseases and SRH patients. Moreover, facility based health and nutrition education coupled with community health promotion will be enhanced using a network of community outreach workers already supported under BHA. They will identify health-related cases and refer them to the health facility.On the logic of implementing a nexus approach, PUI wants to support 300 households by training them on managing small businesses before providing them with a 2,187 SEK capital grant to start an Income Generating Activity (IGA) of their choice. Beneficiaries will receive technical support for the management of their IGA monthly, and they will be encouraged to join a Savings group. Households will be selected from PUI Nutrition and Protection services beneficiaries as their economic vulnerability is a major risk factor for relapse and deterioration. IGAs will allow the targeted beneficiaries to cover their basic needs, hence contributing to decreasing their exposure to protection and malnutrition. During the initial phase of IGA launch beneficiaries will be provided with food assistance.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

PUI strives to ensure mainstreaming of protection principles and respect for the safety and dignity of people in all its programming and sectors of intervention, in order to minimize unintended adverse effects of its interventions. All interventions will be based on a thorough analysis of the needs and gaps identified through participatory assessments conducted with different groups of the targeted populations. The targeted populations will be consulted in all phases of the project cycle (design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation) to ensure that the response is adapted to the needs and capacities of the affected populations. In this process, different groups from diverse backgrounds will be engaged to ensure meaningful access to services, participation and empowerment of the communities. Moreover, PUI closely works with the community and local leaders to select sites and ensure the beneficiaries' day-to-day safety. In order to facilitate meaningful access, PUI will adopt multiple approaches, including sharing information about the available services with the populations of concern and ensuring the disability-friendliness of the facilities and activities. About disability inclusion, PUI will also diversify the communication and information-sharing channels to improve communication with people suffering from different types of disability (e.g. pictorial aid, mega phones, etc.). PUI will train its staff in protection mainstreaming and raise their awareness of the protection principles, CoC, PSEA, etc. to ensure compliance with safeguarding principles and the principle of “do no harm”. PUI will pay special attention to specific vulnerabilities in order to reduce barriers to health-care access by implementing an active and efficient triage of patients to ensure the most vulnerable are given priority. In all interventions, gender dynamics/norms will be thoroughly analyzed and mainstreamed in the activities (e.g. separated waiting areas (male/female) when feasible; gender-balanced teams to cater for the different needs of both males and females, etc.). PUI attaches high importance to the safety and confidentiality of the beneficiaries and ensures that all the staff are aware of these principles, specifically data protection protocols. In addition to GBVIMS information sharing protocol, PUI has developed its internal data protection protocol, signed and adhered to by all relevant staff.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The current community-based feedback system consists of multiple entry points: the helpline, the mobile helpdesk, the community volunteers' network, the satisfaction surveys, and the monthly community committees’ meetings. These channels are reviewed yearly following beneficiaryfeedbacks to ensure the mechanism is inclusive, context-specific, and accessible safely and confidentially by women, girls, boys, and men.Further, accountability refresher training ensures that field staff and volunteers know the feedbackmechanisms, procedures, and accountability principles. Accountability teams are composed ofboth male and female members who speak the most spoken language of the area of intervention to allow beneficiaries to express their concerns easily. When attending the helpdesks in the health facilities, accountability team ensures to provide awareness to beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries regarding the various channels available for complaints and feedbacks.
