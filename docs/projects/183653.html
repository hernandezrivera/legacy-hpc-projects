Project Summary

Project Summary
The project” Protection and Support of FHHS With Disabilities and Survivors of Cancer
Subjected to GBV through Multisectoral Responses” was designed in order to provide
multi-sectoral service to FHHS affected of last Israeli escalation and COVID-19 women with disability and women with cancer in the Gaza Strip” The change which can be attributed to the implementation of the project or the project will contribute to ultimate outcome 1000: Alleviated suffering, protected and maintained human dignity, and lives saved for vulnerable FHHs (suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs, women with cancer, and GBV survivors) 18-59 and >59 years, in poorest localities in the north, Gaza, Middle area, Khanyouins, and Rafah governorates of the Gaza Strip in Palestine. That change will be achieved 'only if ' the following intermediate outcomes will be realised:
1) Increased performance and change behavior among GBV service providers in
offering gender-responsive assistance for vulnerable FHHs (suspended women, wives of
mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with cancer, and GBV survivors) 18-59
and >59 years, in poorest localities in Gaza, Middle North Gaza, Khanyouins and Rafah
and affected of COVID-19.
2) Increased use of gender-responsive assistance including structured group psycho-social support, family therapy, awareness-raising workshops, group legal counseling/awareness, referral to receive additional legal support services, dignity kits,
temporary shelter assistance, case management (individual psychosocial counseling,
psychotherapy, and medication, individual legal counseling, legal representation,
economic enablement, reproductive health, and social services) by vulnerable FHHs
(suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with
cancer, and GBV survivors) 18-59 and >59 years and their families. These intermediate
outcomes will be achieved 'only if ' the following immediate outcomes will be realised:
1) Increased access to staff ToT training on Hawa Elzahra and case management
approach for GBV service providers to promote recovery and resilience of vulnerable FHHs
(suspended women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with
cancer, GBV survivor) 18-59 and >59 years and their families.
2) Improved access to gender sensitive structured group psycho-social support, open
recreational days, family therapy, awareness-raising workshops, group legal
counseling/awareness, dignity kits, temporary shelter assistance, case management
(individual psychosocial counseling, psychotherapy and medication, individual legal
counseling, legal representation, economic empowerment, reproductive health, social
services, and referral to other service providers to receive additional services) conforming
to cultural norms of users for vulnerable FHHs with and without disabilities (suspended
women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, WWDs and women with cancer, GBV
survivor) 18-59 and >59 years and their families.
The project will be implemented by the main applicant AISHA. The project directly targets 2000
FHHS, 500 men, and indirectly 15,000 community persons. The project will target the most
vulnerable FHHS and their families in the localities: Rafah: Rafah, Rafah Camp Gaza: Al Mughraqa (Abu Middein), Ash Shati Camp, Gaza City
Deir Al-Balah:An Nuseirat Camp, Az Zawayda, Deir Al Balah Khan Yoins: Al Mawasi,
Khanyouins camp, Khuza'a, North Gaza: Beit Hanun, Beit Lahiya, Izbat Beit Hanun,
Jabalya, Jabalya Camp.
Aisha will use Mix modality (In – person and remote modality) to respond to the
needs through crises and emergency.

1 - Needs

The results of the violence survey in the Palestinian society carried out by the PSBC showed that 38% of woman who are married or previously married in the Gaza Strip have experienced at least to one type of violence. Gender Alert 2021, identified that Gaza’s 2 million residents continue to suffer from a relentless humanitarian crisis, exacerbated greatly by the May 2021 escalation of hostilities. While 44 per cent of surveyed women and 80 per cent of persons with disabilities, reported not feeling safe in their homes. (HNO 2022), Women and girls with disabilities are particularly at risk of violence. Being physically cut off from employment prospects leaves them with few options for supporting themselves financially. Women and girls who are survivors of GBV often face social stigma that can limit their access to GBV essential services. (HNO 2022) 1.1 million of the population in Gaza is considered food unsecured and in need for assistant. MSNA 2021, 22% of surveyed HH reported temporary loss of employment and 6% permanent loss of employment as a result of covid-19 measures. 50% reported a decrease in their income. The escalation on top of worsening socio-economic situation increasing the vulnerability of the furthest left behind groups (FHH, WWDs, cancer survivors) who represented 11% of the Gaza population (PCBS 2019). The recent escalation let to more female-headed of households that 40 women and 63 Palestinians children killed, almost 398 women and 600 Palestinian children wounded, 113,000 persons displaced more than half of them were women and girls.A rapid assessment on COVID-19 carried out by the UN Women revealed that domestic violence has increased due to the conditions created by the COVID 19, which in most cases resulted in lock down of survivors of violence with their abusers. With families in lockdown, helplines are seeing a surge of cases requesting a multitude of services. The findings of the assessment also showed that security and justice services, including protection, violence against women cases are not considered essential under the state of emergency with the main shelters not taking in new women and courts limiting litigations procedures for cases of the emergency law violations. Additionally, the report concluded that front-liners are working under stressful conditions, putting their emotional and psychological well-being at risk. According to a survey carried out by AISHA Association on the impact of COVID-19 crisis on PSS conditions of women, 2 out of 3 respondent women reported experienced violence during home isolation. The report further showed that more than half of women are at risk of violence and have no access to protection services. Another factsheet commissioned by Atfaluna Society for Deaf Children showed that 84% of respondent families reported increased anxiety and 89% reported that their mental health well-being was negatively affected due to COVID-19 crisis. In addition to the medical needs, the closure of schools, limitations on access to work places, and the imposition of quarantine and curfew, particularly in overcrowded households, alongside general uncertainty, are expected to increase mental and psychosocial distress, as well GBV. The impact on women, boys and girls is further influenced by the layered socio-economic and political realities which they live under. Pre-existing socio-cultural factors contribute to the formation and implementation of health emergency response. FHHS in the Gaza Strip being vulnerable group had a bad impact of their lives. Aisha through its work with affected people of COVID-19 since March-2020 highlighted the damage of family’s entities, with families staying at home, household tension will increase and higher incidence of GBV against women and children was recorded. Aisha conducted a specific needs assessment on 14.10.2020 in the 5 governates affected of Covid-19.The results of the assessment, gender alert 2021, and HNO the needs of different groups disaggregated by gender and age as follow:Women, FHHs, WWDs 18-59 years:• MHPSS support through easy methods to reach• Case management services and multi-sectorial interventions • Family therapy method and social integrations• Dignity kits• Cash assistance• Shelter assistance• legal services including consultations, awareness and representation • Awareness raising workshops on gender and protection pathways • Screening, diagnostic, treatment, home visits and rehabilitation• Physiotherapy• Specialized mental health services• Individual counseling• Awareness raising workshops on Gender and COVID-19 Men 18-59 years:• awareness raising workshops on Gender, procurations measures through Covid-19, child protection measures.• Service providers:capacity building training on providing remote PSS, protection measures, risk mitigations and referral pathways.

2 - Activities

Activities will be implemented following implication of conflicts and COVID-19 safety measures set by local authorities to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries. A mainstream protection of beneficiaries in last conflicts and COVID-19 context through prioritizing safety and dignity and do no harm will be applied ; professionalism of AISHA staff in delivering MHPSS services, ethical considerations of confidentiality, privacy and informed consent; meaningful access to MHPSS services particularly for female headed of household – FHH and persons with disabilities; cancer, accountability, participation & empowerment; and applying protection against sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment guidelines developed with UN WOMEN support. AISHA will also apply COVID-19 safety measures set by local authorities to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries. ACT1. Detect 2000 FHHS 18-59 (200 out of them are women with cancer, 200 WWDS who are in need for Spatialized MHPSS support from AISHA hotline, website and AISHA FB and direct approach to AISHA Centre. Act2. Provide MHPSS for 2000 women and adolescents girls through AISHA hotline and Zahr online application for counseling and case management and its mobile application. The cases will be received PFA, individual counseling and consultation on social, psychological, legal issues upon their need, and referral to AISHA clinics to receive specialized MHPSS services including psychotherapy, medication or multi-sectoral services will be through the counselors ACT3. Provide case management to 200 vulnerable FHHS (30 out of them WWDS, 70 women with cancer) identified with multi-sectoral needs including group PSS (20)groups of 10 cases/group, (5)life-skills workshops for 20 group of 15 women, individual psychosocial counseling, psycho-therapy and medication for (100)cases, individual legal counseling, legal representation (100 case), economic enablement (60) case, family therapy (30)cases, and referral services. ACT4. Temporary shelter assistance provided for 70 evicted families of targeted FHHs or at risk of eviction due to deteriorated socioeconomic situation and implication of conflicts and COVID-19 pandemic. Selection criteria include: families headed by women with and without disabilities, size of the family, families lost their house and income because of implication of conflicts and COVID-19 pandemic, evicted families or at risk of eviction, and GBV survivor. ACT5. Provide dignity and Hygiene kits to 200 vulnerable FHHs (30 out of them WWDS, 70 women with cancer)ACT6. Organize (100) awareness raising and sensitization workshops (2 hours each workshop) involving 500 women and (500) men from the families of vulnerable FHHs (out of them 100 men with disability) violence, GBV risks and protection pathways. Act7. Provide emergency health care services for (200) FHHS (pre-conception counseling, antenatal care, quality care for obstetric and newborn emergencies, post-natal and family planning, gynecological and obstetric problem diagnoses and treatment, health counseling, lab test, pharmaceutical services). Act8. Provide screening, diagnostic, treatment, home visits and rehabilitation (physiotherapy, artificial silicon breast in addition to medical breast bra, artificial hair, and primary health care kits containing iodin-burne cream-cotton-alkohol) interventions to 200 women survivors of breast cancerAct9. Provide Multi- purpose cash assistance- MPCA to 100 FHH including women with disability and women survivors of breast cancer according to their needs. Selection of Female head of households for MPCA will be according to criteria like FHH whose main bread winner has lost their livelihoods due to the COVID-19 restriction measures or as a result of the escalations of May 2021, whom are at risk of sinking deeper into poverty without assistance. AISHA will be responsible for the selection of (FHH, WWDs and cancer survivors18-59 Yrs) in coordination with the MOSD. Households will be selected and prioritized considering the criteria among others: female headed household, WWDs, Cancer survivors/ breadwinners, income and expenditures variations, source of income, debts holdings, food security level, daily worker status, household composition, coping strategies, financial stress. (MPCA) will be paid for 3 months will be transferred to selected women to provide financial access to cover their basic needs. The transfer value of 328 USD corresponds to recommended transfer value (1'046 NIS) by the Cash Working Group (CWG) and the FSS. Assistance will be received through Palpay ATM cards where right holders will be able to redeem their assistance through the Bank of Palestine ATM locations or/and direct purchase in accredited stores. MPCA will be linked with management visits to ensure that the cash assistance and associated responsibilities in deciding spending priorities and access to markets do not create further harm within the household. ACT10. Conduct capacity building training for 60 service providers (including project staff) in self-care, stress management and in remote service delivery, contingency planning and/or GBV Minimum Standards and PSEA. (4 groups each group 15 persons, 25 hours each group, total of 100 training hours). The training will be conducted in person after evaluating the situation. Considering protection and safety measures such as large rooms, good ventilation, lighting and Hygiene measures for both place and target group with no change on the budget. ACT11. Conduct digital campaign to raise awareness regarding rights and dignity of FHHS affected of Conflicts and COVID-19 including (3 radio sessions, 1 radio spot, 2 fact sheets amplifying the women voices, the needs of GBV and the gaps of services in front of the duty bearers, 3 tweeting days, 1000 leaflets, 30000 SMS, 5 educational videos about GBV, protection measures, women rights, stress management & depression will be shared on social media platforms via paid adv.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

In 2021, the Israeli escalation compounded by the impact of the COVID 19 pandemic, violence against women have been increased that according to the latest available Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) survey on violence which predates the COVID19 pandemic, 29.4% of (married or previously married) women between the ages of 18-64 experienced violence in the twelve months preceding the survey – 37.5% in Gaza, 56.6% experienced psychological violence, 17.8% physical violence, 8.8% sexual violence, 32.5% social violence, 41.1% economic violence, with 8% experienced the emerging issue of cyber violence. According to gender alert and survey conducted by Aisha Association, it shows the urgent need to provide specialized CP/ MHPSS services, case management, economic enablement, capacity building, and awareness raising working with the following groups: women, FHHs, WWDs, men, and service providers.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

AISHA applies PSEA manual and code of ethics and conduct. - AISHA hasinfrastructure adaptations including fitting ramps and railings to facilities so that allPwDs can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity. - The location ofAISHA’s facilities and the routes to them are safe. - AISHA provides WWDs and GWDsassistive devices and signs language interpretation services to ensure full access ofdirect beneficiaries to services.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AISHA advances access approach that is consistent with the humanitarian principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence and humanity). - AISHA follows the guidelines in its "Risk management plan including risk analysis and risk mitigation" and "Security and Safety Manual" to protect the lives of their staff during emergencies. - AISHA provides case management as multi-sectorial services at an adequate time when all women and girls and their families can realistically access them. - AISHA facilitates access to the services for persons with reduced mobility (persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) through making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. - AISHA facilitates access to MHPSS services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g. the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled) through providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations. - AISHA will raise awareness of target women and men about gender, GBV and their rights - AISHA uses their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls in ARAs to GBV services to leave no one behind. - AISHA adapts activities in accordance with hygiene and safety measures. - AISHA involves grassroots CBOs and local communities in these localities.
