Project Summary

This project has been designed to responds to the needs for humanitarian life-saving shelter and NFI assistance to IDPs and host community HHs in Azaz, Albab, Dana, Maaret Tamsrin, Harim, Armanaz, Atareb, Idleb, Kafr Takharim, Mhambal and Qourqeena subdistricts in Aleppo and Idleb governorates targeting 7500 HHs (37500 individuals, 9750 Men, 10500 Women, 8250 Boys, 9000 Girls).
The locations are prioritized as per the HNO 2021 data and according to prioritization of BINAA to the location as per the eld teams’ communication with the local councils and their eld visits to some villages and camps.
The project will target the IDP’s sites in the targeted locations, (5000 HHs (25000 individuals); Men 6500; Women 7000; Boys 5500; Girls 6000) in order to improve living conditions for conflict affected IDP HHs though supporting IDPs sites with emergency response activities like NFI distribution (5000 Heaters and 320 liters/HHs of heating fuels), tent winterization, insulation, levelling, graveling pathways (over 150 camps).
For targeted communities in mentioned subdistricts, BINAA will provide shelter repair and functional upgrades for damaged houses valued at an average of $650 for 1500 HHs (7500 individuals) (1950 Men, 2100 Women, 1650 Boy, 1800 Girl) (60% IDPs and 40% HC). The repair works will enable the target communities to use local materials and work through local contractors to improve their living conditions, fostering a sense of ownership, responsibility, and resilience while supporting local markets. BINAA will also working on upgrading 250 unfinished buildings accommodated by 1000 IDPs HHs (5000 individuals) (1300 Men, 1400 Women, 1100 Boy, 1200 Girl) and providing safe and dignified housing conditions. valued at an average of $850 for each HH.
Shelter repair beneficiaries will be IDP and HC HHs living in an inadequate shelter that does not meet minimum SPHERE minimum standards for square meters of covered living space per person and has inadequate privacy or protection from the elements, settlement planning and WASH facilities. We will target HHs that have light to medium damage but remain structurally sound. Works include doors and windows repair or replacement, partition installation, sealing cracks and holes in walls, and WASH facilities repairs. The works will ensure increased privacy between male and females, host and hosted families that enhance gender sensitive response. Furthermore, for HHs with disabled persons, additional works will be included to respond to their needs by including disability friendly entrance and toilet facilities (e.g. use of a simple ramp with a handrail for using wheelchairs at the building entrance and seating provisions for toilets). The unfinished buildings rehabilitation varies between the number of rooms in each unfinished building, installation of masonry partitions, repair of WASH facilities within the building. To enhance gender sensitive response, necessary adaptations that particularly affect women and girls will include appropriate lighting, separate toilets and showers for men and women, accessible facilities for people with disabilities (disability friendly entrance and toilet facilities), areas for bathing, washing of clothing, and partitions to create spaces for different households and additional partitions within households to create privacy for men and women.
To participate in the project HHs and building owners should agree on the terms of assistance and sign a contract that allows the IDP household to stay for one year for free to ensure that HLP guidance are considered and utilized. To bene t from the project, all beneficiaries should present valid HLP documents that verify the ownership of properties.

4 - Needs

BINAA conducted a rapid needs assessment to design this project. The assessment and based on the HNO 2021 shared by the CCCM Cluster in north-west Syria, the project will cover 150 existed camps (16 in Aleppo (Afrin, Azaz and Jebel Saman ) and 134 in Idleb(Harem and Idleb), 5000 IDP Households (25000 individuals) recognized as IDP sites by CCCM cluster in Idleb and Aleppoto develop evidence-based strategy for ensuring an effective intervention including the winterization and weatherproofing needs. The results showed that 49% of tents in assessed camps need insulation and weatherproofing and 62% of them need ground levelling. The site's infrastructure needs improvement in all camps especially facilitating access of elderly and people with disabilities to the facilities of the camp. Pathways to WASH and other facilities in most camps are bad or very bad.72% of targeted camps are suffered from dirt roads which make a big challenge for residents during winter in addition to lack of heating equipment and fuel during winter for most IDPs HHs living in the camps site..40% of newly IDPs are living in the damaged houses, unfinished buildings, schools, temporary and makeshift camps which aren't secure protection and dignified for living conditions of their HHs.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

During the implementation of project activities, BINAA staff will ensure compliance with five key messages on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Feedback mechanisms will also be established and announced to the targeted populations. Gender and age disaggregated data from project coverage and impact will be collected, analyzed and routinely reported on, while project activities are monitored for improvements in self-reliance as well as beneficiary satisfaction and resilience of both women and men. The targeted population will be involved in different stages of the project management cycle, either by contacting the Key informative persons like Local council or camp management or by direct communication with beneficiaries through the ME questionnaires during baseline, endline, PDM, PIM, and the implementation and distribution monitoring activities, and through the focused meeting which will discuss the reaching consensus about the proposed activities and laying the foundations for active participation and involvement during implementation of the project and to prevent any harm may the intervention cause.
