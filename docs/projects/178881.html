Project Summary

Olive Branch Organisation throughout this intervention is going to addressing the education needs and bridges the gaps of the education sector according to the strategic objectives of both the HRP 2021 and Education Cluster guided by an analysis of the severity and properties of needs based on the assessment findings and key indicators.
The project’s activities are aiming to improve the access of 60,164 children (29,971 G, 25,243 B, 450 CwDs (200 G, 250 B) 3 -5 and 6-17 years old and 2,758 education actors (1,718 W, 1,040 M), in Idleb governorate includes rural areas 15 sub-districts to quality inclusive education and learning opportunities with safe and protective environment by providing a package of formal and non-formal education services, the response prioritizes vulnerable children, adolescents / youth, girls and boys, and CwDs, in 15 sub-district locations: Benesh, Idlib, Taftanaz, Maretmesrin, Sarmin, Harim, Salqin, KaferTakharim, Armanaz, Aljanudiyeh, Ariha, Ehsem, Jisr Alshoghur, Dana, and Muhambel, which are located within the high-level of severity 4 and 5 according to 2021 HNO, with strong coordination with relative authorities, Local councils, IDLIB Education of Directorate
OB will focusing to bring children back to formal education through a variety of interventions ensuring that they have access to learning opportunities in 138 formal schools, with establishing 15 NFE TLSs to provide ECE (Early Childhood Education) activities for children age group 3 -5 years to address the numeracy literacy basics of ECE as a pre-school.
Obj1: increasing the availability of and access to safe and equitable formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth 3 -17 age.
OB will conduct a package of education services starting with Back-to-school companies in the 15 selected areas targeting 52,500 (22,500 M, 30,000 F) children/youth with their parents/caregivers to raise their awareness of the education importance, Provide the 138 schools and 15 ECE TLSs with minor repair based on the each school’s damage assessment including 1,656 classrooms, maintenance for the wash facilities gender-based in the 138 schools and 15 TLSs, Provide school furniture (Desks, whiteboards, tables, chairs, cabinets, heaters), provide 138 schools and 15 TLSs with running costs (drinkable water - cleaning tools & materials - buffet materials- stationery), provide 138 schools and 15 TLSs with heating fuel in winter, Provide School bags includes stationery for 60,164 children (29,971 G, 25,243 B, 450 CwDs (200 G, 250 B) appropriate the 3-5 and 6-17 years old, Provide 55,664 children (29,971 Girls, 25,243 Boys) and 450 CwD (200 G, 250 B) 6-17 years in the 138 selected primary and secondary schools with formal education (SIG curriculum) grades 1–12, and PSS sessions to promote the wellbeing, in addition to provide 690 kits materials of education, 690 kits of PSS/recreational materials for 138 schools, Provide 4500 children (2000 B, 2500 G) 3 -5 years in the TLSs with BLN-ECE that encompasses literacy and numeracy education, physical, social and emotional care, intellectual stimulation, provide 15 TLSs with 75 ECE kits.
Obj2: enhancing the quality of formal and non-formal education services.
OB will be paying the incentives to 1,119 Teachers and supporting staff (682 M, 352 w) working in the 138 schools and 15 TLSs, Building the capacity of 2,758 education actors (1,718 W, 1,040 M), Teachers and supporting staff on child-centered, gender-sensitive inclusive education, codes of conduct, PSS and referral mechanisms, Integration of Children with disabilities in Education Response, INEE, TICC. In addition to training the schools' protection supervisors to improve project quality and compliance with child protection and humanitarian principles, establishing 138 PTAs (2070 members 1500 w, 570 m) that the main role of the schools’ PTAs is to build strong working relationships among parents, teachers, and schools, in support of students.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2020, in 2020 an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out3. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID0-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase. OB M&E team conducted rapid assessment in the selected 15 areas in IDLIB Governorate, that based on triangulation of the information with the reports of HNO 2020 and EoD of Idlib 202, the main key findings:138 of the functional school need support, % 87 of 138 functional schools need minor repair (Doors, windows, wash facilities), % 68 of 138 functional schools need school furniture (Desks, whiteboards, tables, chairs, cabinets, heaters, safety equipment), 40 % of the educational staff need incentive support, 138 need running cost including heating fuel, % 85 of schools’ staff need capacity building training, 138 schools need recreational materials, there are 55,664 children (29,971 f, 25,243 m) affected in the 138 assessed schools, 85 % of schools’ children need textbooks in formal education, The 138 schools need support with teaching materials, 90 % of the young children 3-5 age are unlikely to attend early childhood education due to lack of ECE centres (95% of school have no ECE), While early learning is not compulsory, due to its ability to enable children’s readiness for school and enhance life-long learning, it is a priority.

5 - Implementation of activities

in Aug 2021 minor repairing will be provided for 138 schools including establishing and maintenance for the wash facilities gender based with inclusive the needs of the CwDs, in parallel there are school furniture will be provided includes based on the assessed need of each school, once the welcoming school are ready, OB will provide capacity training for the schools' staff and protection supervisors on a package of professional topics related to the INEE and EIE, establishing 138 school PTAs, BTL campaigns will be conducted in a community level of the 15 selected locations throughout distributing Flyers, Brochures about the importance of education that will encourage parents to bring their children back to schools, on 1/9/2021 the Formal tutors (SIG curriculum) will be started for 55,664 children (29,971 G, 25,243 B, 450 CwD (200 G, 250 B)) 6-17 years in the 138 selected primary & secondary schools in addition to PSS and recreational sessions until the end of Dec 2021, paying incentives for 954 education staff, providing running monthly costs, including heating fuel for two months of winter and purchasing and distributing school bags includes stationery for 60,164 children (32,671 G, 27,493 B), in other hand there are 15 NFE TLSs will be established in a selected locations to provide ECE for 4500 children 3-5 years, with providing child safe environment including furniture and educational and recreational materials, ECE sessions for kids will be started in Jul until Dec 2021 with providing running monthly costs including incentives for 165 TLSs staff, OB MEAL team with management will follow up the progress of the activities to make sure all is in place with quality.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1. Violence against education facilities and education providers, OB’s Safety and security department prepared contingency plan actions with evacuation plan with conducting regular drills for the children and staff, with providing safety equipment (fire extinguishers and first aid kits), all attacks will be reported through MRM and cluster mechanisms Risk2: Violence, physical punishment or bullying in schools, OB keen to train all staff of schools and TLSs on code of conduct with ensure the commitment, with taking serious actions with misconduct cases, teachers and school personnel will be trained on prevention and reporting of violence, physical punishment and bullying Risk3: Students are not sufficiently protected from adults or older students in learning centre/school, OB have Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place and these regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed, make sure that all students and staff aware about the feedback and complaint mechanisms Risk4: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) by aid workers, OB have PSEA policies in place to cover standards of behaviour for the OB’s staff and representatives, to address risks at the organizational level and the project level Risk5: Insufficiently safe learning facilities, OB will ensure the TLS/school safe and protected, Sufficient water and sanitation facilities are provided and maintained, WASH facilities will be sex and age segregated and accessible to people with disabilities, facilities will be kept clean. Students are trained on good health and hygiene practices with awareness raising in Covid19 prevention measures, referral system between learning facility and the relevant health centre.

Olive Branch organisation’s Staff, volunteers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Olive Branch makes sure that these Prevention measures in place:1. all staff, volunteers and associated personnel sign the OB’s code of conduct which includes PSEA issues. 2. all staff, volunteers and associates receive induction and (where relevant) annual refresher training on PSEA and staff members are aware of their obligation to report SEA/misconduct.3. HR and relevant hiring bodies check employment history during interview and questions related to PSEA asked during interview, 4. criminal records check is conducted where possible and new employees sign contracts which include PSEA and the organisation code of conduct, 5. complaints mechanisms developed to receive and respond to reports of SEA - CSG with train all OB's staff, employees, volunteers, and contractors on these mechanisms and tools to make sure they are aware how and when to use it.6. The awareness-raising sessions on PSEA will be conducting for children by classroom teachers or schools' management. In case any violates the CoC/CSG; OB will follow the investigation procedures in order to take the appropriate disciplinary actions based on OB’s CSG - PSEA policy.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Learning facilities near to the M4/M5 road mostly could be attacked by bombing or airstrike. (Med/High) Mitigation: OB safety and security field team will conduct trainings, awareness -raising and drills appropriate for the age of the involved students in addition to the staff. Topics includes self-care, first aid, mine risk and unexploded ordinance awareness, fire drills, evacuation drills and stay-and-shelter drills, refer the IDPs to another education service according to IDP destination Risk 2: COVID-19 pandemic:(High/High) Mitigation: OB have developed policies to be followed in COVID-19 including prevention measurements, conducting hygiene awareness, with providing learning facilities with cleaning materials, divide students into shifts in case the school not able to apply the social distance between the students, in addition to following the recommendation of WoS re-opening schools. Risk 3: Re-Closure of learning facilities(High/medium) Mitigation: Transform the physical learning to distance learning throughout OB learning platform, train the education staff on the distance learning modalities and methods, and approaches.Financial risks are low as we have a well-established financial control, procurement, and anti-fraud policy to ensure the duties are sufficiently segregated for the benefit of transparency, control, and avoidance of any frauds. Since the materials will be sourced in the relatively stable local markets or across the border if required, there is low risk of impact from inflation and price fluctuations. In terms of disruption to our operation due to shortage of required items, we estimate the risk to be low in that the required items and tools are available in the market.
