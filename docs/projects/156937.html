Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to enhance advocacy female humanitarian workers’ roles in both organizations and communities. This will be done through Orange hosting the Syrian Women Humanitarian Network (SWHN) to implement the following outputs:
1. Extending the Syrian Women Humanitarian Network:
This will be done through the networking and the advocacy that target the female humanitarian workers inside Syria to join the SWHN and contribute in the network activities.
2. Setting up an Information Management System:
This will be done through the establishment of female humanitarian workers Information Management Unit (IMU) led by SWHN. The aim of (IMU) is to collect information through the SWHN member organizations about the needs of those workers. The IMU will be supported to carry out analysis of the information and data collected and sharing these information with relevant stakeholders including SWHN members, UN agencies, relevant national and international non-governmental organizations. The information system will be invested to enhance the understanding of female humanitarian workers overall situation including career development, HR rights, gender equality within the humanitarian agencies.
3. Female Humanitarian Workers capacity building:
This will be done through identifying the female humanitarian workers’ capacity building needs. This output includes providing them with training programs and workshop events that include different components according to their current or aiming employment roles. These training programs will aim to enhance and develop the roles of females within their organizations and strengthening their ability to develop and enhance their careers.
The capacity building program will also aim to enhance the role of the female humanitarian workers to take a leading role within their communities and organizing initiatives at their community level.

Needs

Nearly eight years since the conflict in Syria began, over 250,000 people have been killed, 6.5 million have become Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and an estimated 4.8 million registered Syrian refugees1. The humanitarian crisis inside Syria has resulted in one of the most complex and tragic in history. Besides many other implications, the ongoing violent conflict has resulted in a change of gender relations in Syria over the last years. Due to the consequences of the conflict, many men are in one way or the other absent from their families, either because they have been killed, they are in prison, have migrated or are involved in fighting. Others have lost their income-generating possibilities as carpenters, smiths, electricians or construction workers since markets and supply chains have collapsed in many areas. Infrastructure is destroyed, and costs for inputs are soaring due to the siege and widespread violence. Industrial activities, production and small- and medium- size enterprises have seriously suffered. Hence, a significant number of men are shifting toward working in humanitarian aid activities.Many agencies and Organizations took action responses towards the Syrian crisis. However, no gender balance was considered at the very beginning phases of these responses. Later, the need of female employees was raised due to direct connecting with large number of women and female beneficiaries. Particularly in sectors were sensitive topics existed.On the other hand, decision makers and senior management didn’t take any serious reaction toward this case which caused preventing ambitious females to develop their careers and take higher important role within these agencies and organization. Females were not given opportunities in the key vacants and capacity development programs. Moreover, this was applied specifically at the field level.After more than seven years in the involvement of Syrian women in the humanitarian and community work in the organizations in Syria at field level, it does not appear that the reality of women workers gives positive indicators for the appropriate representation of them at least numerically and even in the decision-making positions. On the other hand, the high level of harassment and abuse and the increase in the number of cases of functional marginalization and gender-based discrimination.

Implementation of activities

1- Conducting advocacy campaigns through events, conferences, and over social media that targets the female humanitarian workers.2- Setting up a communication plat forms for female humanitarian workers.3- Dialog and debate events organizing to be attended periodically by the female humanitarian workers.4- Conducting capacity needs assessment for women within NGOs and humanitarian organizations.5- Setting up a data collection system.6- Implementing a capacity development programs according to the needs assessments findings.7- Developing female humanitarian workers’ needs, work conditions, and status in Syria with proper statistics and data analysis.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Advocacy has no direct impact on women and raises unnecessary expectations.2. Challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development.3. Lack of women access to specific communities due to security, traditional or legal constraints

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Ensure evidence based and targeted advocacy activities including sharing relevant information on needs of female humanitarian workers in NW Syria.2. Ensure that capacity building initiatives do not end with training; through direct application of knowledge.3. Mobilize women from the same targeted agencies and organizations.
