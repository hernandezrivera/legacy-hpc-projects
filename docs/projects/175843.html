Project Summary

UNCDR proposes to target conflict-affected and GBV survivors, Host community, IDPs and returnees living in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut. The purpose of this project is to prevent protection risk and provide quality service in a timely manner to women, girls, men, and boys in the three Counties cutting across 6 payams. The target population is 12,500 (4,800 Women, 3,700 Girls, 2,000 Boys, and 2,000 Men). The project will run for a period of 12 months, with a budget of USD 375,000. This is a continuation of a series of projects UNCDR has been implementing including GBV in emergency in Canal/Pigi and Child protection in Ayod and Maiwut for the last three years and is also a build-up following the lessons learned over time. UNCDR will strengthen community-based structures that have been developed together with other protection partners and update the referral pathway. The other activities to be conducted include: case management , WGFS programming ,creating awareness in the community on the effects of GBV to the whole community to build resilience and mitigate GBV risks and direct effects it causes on the survivors, training men and women in the community on PFA/PSS, GBV principle guidelines, training more frontline workers on case management, PFA and PSS, directly support survivors of GBV through direct support depending on their needs, coordinate with other actors to update referral pathways and conduct appropriate integration and referrals to Health, Nutrition, WASH, Child Protection and Education sectors. In addition, UNCDR will conduct comprehensive case management to the women and girls of GBV survivors, establish WGFS, COVID-19 awareness, children, women and men will receive messages on COVID-19, which emphasize handwashing, social distancing as key prevention method for transmission of the virus.
UNCDR maintains strong presence in Maiwut and Ayod has it been implementing protection project on the target counties for the last 3 years with support from UNICEF. UNCDR will scale up its operation especially in the hard to reach areas of the locations where the caseload is high.

1 - Needs

Protection is among the top priority concerns, GBV is pervasive in South Sudan. As a result of structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships, with women and girls especially those who are internally displaced or living near cantonment sites, or with a disability continue to suffer from GBV. Flooding, armed conflict in pockets of areas, sub-national violence and COVID-19 are escalating the risk of GBV including CRSV and SEA. The condition also compounded reduced access to sexual and reproductive health services for women and girls. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 have also contributed to a rise in GBV incidents. Based on nationwide FSMNS+ assessment, the risk of GBV is identified high as 49% of surveyed households expressed concern on GBV and 34% of households travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water. Similarly, 49% of affected women and girls who are in need of services have no access to GBV services and only 12% of the community have awareness on available GBV services. Based on the 2020 GBVIMS data analysis, 29% of GBV survivors in need of safe house did not obtain the service because of lack of safe houses. Increased risk of GBV coupled with gaps in response services necessitates an urgent need for strengthened primary GBV prevention, integration of GBV risk mitigation in other sectors, and scaling up GBV response services. Provision of safe housing, dignity kits and livelihood support continue to be a high priority. According to IRNA report conducted by the partners in August 2020 heavy rains severely hit an area, leading to expansive heavy flooding and a colossal displacement of many residents in different parts of Ayod and Canal/Pigi. There is an alarming and acute need for protection, especially SGBV, This report indicated that 15,263 HHs totaling to 91,579 individuals are the current most affected with females’ population comprising of over 65% since the onset of flooding. The emergence of a large number of vulnerable women-headed households within conservative and patriarchal communities has heightened the acute exposure of women and children to protection threats, especially GBV cases such as sexual violence, SEA, intimate partner violence, economic violence, forced marriage and harmful traditional practices like blood compensation for the girl child. In addition to SGBV perpetrated within the community, respondents of the IRNA reported that women and girls had been attacked on routes from residential areas to safer higher grounds and raped, items looted and young girls abducted. IOM-DTM report of December, 2019 indicates that there are more returnees expected to arrive in the New Year 2020 in Maiwut arriving from Ethiopia. Ideally, returnee women and girls are dependent on the community for essential resources which causes mental distress. About 8 SGBV cases were reported in Maiwut. Women and girls fear to report rape cases due to the weak judicial systems. This puts them in a position of increased vulnerability in an environment where the only access to justice is via community-based justice adjudicated by the male Boma chief or administrator who often does not favor women and girls. The report further says that the entire population of women-girls-boys and men are affected by civil war, inter-clan and revenge killings and that much of the work is done by women and girls which include fetching water and collecting firewood from bushes that put the safety of these girls and women at high risk of sexual violence. According to the CTSAMM report of October 201 on cantonment sites, there is a need to mitigate GBV risks of women and girls living near these sites through proper coordination of partners to target community leaders on GBV mitigation strategies. There is a need for GBV awareness-raising to women, girls, boys, and men on behavior change in support of their female counterparts. GBV services are covers fewer payams in Maiwut, UNCDR will increase access to GBV services.

2 - Project activities

UNCDR intends to conduct the following activities: Activity 1. Provide comprehensive case management to GBV survivors/individual PSS and follow up for women and girls GBV survivors using appropriate and ethical guidelines in women and girls’ friendly spaces in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut Activity 2. UNCDR will conduct awareness on the availability of GBV services and use of referral pathway in coordination with other sectoral partners in WASH, FSL, Nutrition and Education. UNCDR will plan the awareness meetings with Health, WASH, Education, Nutrition and FSL partners to integrate GBV prevention in sectoral activities using GBV guiding principles and mainstreaming guidelines in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut. Activity 3. Establishment of 3 women and Girls' friendly space in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut to cover more beneficiaries in both project locations with GBV services such as case management, PSS livelihood referrals and provision of dignity kits. Activity 4. Strengthen the capacity of static service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut through training on case management and PSS. Activity 5. Conducting training of male and female front-line service providers on protection risks mitigation and early warning systems. Activity 6. Conduct monthly community dialogue forums for EMAP with male community leaders, local authority and community-based protection committees to strengthen protection mechanisms and mitigate GBV risks in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut. Activity 7, UNCDR will provide legal service and awareness assistance to the GBV survivors. In strengthening this activity, UNCDR will train the police and the justice authorities, including the traditional justice mechanism.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

While implementing the project, UNCDR will ensure mass campaigns and awareness –raising on issues of PSEA to the beneficiaries with the information of preventing and responding to the acts of sexual exploitation and abuses.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNCDR will ensure equal participation through conduct monthly meetings with most vulnerable members including individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls where group discussions will be held, UNCDR will also ensure and design accountability feedback mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In line with the core humanitarian standards and IASC Guidelines, UNCDR will incorporate its accountability framework through the establishment of the beneficiary feedback mechanisms (grass-root and outreach dialogues, consultation communication channels and suggestion boxes for use beneficiaries) to enable feedback through and /or after all our activities. As part of the feedback mechanisms, complaints lodged by the community or beneficiaries are discussed and resolved at the community level, field office level or at Juba level depending on the complexity of the complaint. As a way of ensuring accountability to beneficiaries, UNCDR will seek the participation of the targeted communities throughout the project cycle. UNCDR will put a special emphasis on persons with special needs. They will be involved in developing criteria for beneficiary selection, assist in selecting beneficiaries and endorse the beneficiary list for any kind of assistance under the project. The inclusion of women in all our consultations, planning, and implementation, the evaluation will be a priority, as a means to promote their participation. UNCDR seeks to ensure that people, particularly those who are most vulnerable within host communities, IDPs and returnees, are able to have their voice heard on issues that are important to them. Have their views and wishes genuinely considered when decisions are being made about their lives or included in decision making. Their rights to get services, their rights to freely get back their lands of ownership and their rights to social justice among other privileges. This message will get across to all relevant authorities, community leaders, and youth group. Monthly meeting with IDPs, returnees and host community leaders and members of the community protection centers to convey get the feedback from the community on the project activities and share the planned activities in order to increase the level of community participation during the project implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedbacks will help the beneficiaries to adapt the project activities Feedbacks helps the organization to be accountable to the beneficiaries through beneficiary’s feedback. The organization will be guided on the specific needs for the target population in accordance to their local context.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project activities are restricted by the global outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic, for instance, limitations on personnel travel, Large gathering keeping social distancing. - Cattle raiding especially during dry season, revenge killing, conflict/violent inter-communal leading to death, abduction of women and children, mass displacement of the community or affecting the organization from operating. Floods during rainy season that may led to inaccessible of roads, which may affect transportation of construction materials and/or commodities to project sites. - Resistance from local community leaders and authorities due to stigma surrounding the prioritization of women and girls, people with disability in the project activities Mitigation: Community acceptance of UNCDR’s activities. No resistance to the recognition of gender equality and SGBV by community leaders and/or members. The project is based on a protection risk analysis generated from the assessment report that was conducted by UNCDR in the month of August 2020. We analyzed the protection risk women and girls face when they are involved in their daily chores that include; fetching water and firewood in the bushes and even when they go for a call of nature given the nature of their surrounding considering the open defecation practice that is common in Ayod, Canal/Pigi and Maiwut.
