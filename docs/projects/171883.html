Project Summary

As the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine continues, available data on landmine/explosive remnants of war (ERW) casualties continues to document frequently occurring accidents. In the period between June 2014 and June 2019, a total of 1126 landmine/ERW accidents were recorded by DRC-DDG from open news sources. From the total of 2060 individual casualties recorded, 1,909.
2018-2019, with support from UNICEF DRC-DDG implemented MVA project. The project covered four out of six core pillars as per the UN Policy on Mine Victim Assistance (MVA) in Mine Action*: (a) data collection including needs assessment, (b) establishment of response methodology, (c) provision of direct assistance through the case management and (d) strengthening of the protective environment for survivors.
* UN Policy on Victim Assistance in Mine Action: (a) data collection, (b) emergency and continuing medical care, (c) physical and other rehabilitation, (d) psychological and psychosocial support, (e) social and economic inclusion, and (f) laws and public policies.
A notable achievement of the project was the completion of the child landmine/ERW needs assessment, conducted by DRC-DDG during the period between Sept-Nov 2018, which covered the GCA of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. The findings of the assessment conveyed the profiles of child landmine/ERW survivors, their path from the moment of accident to recovery, existing gaps and barriers in the process of rehabilitation and capacities of service providers (governmental and non-governmental) to meet the needs in accordance with the six pillars of MVA (defined in the UN Policy on Victim Assistance in Mine Action): (a) data collection, (b) emergency and continuing medical care, (c) physical and other rehabilitation, (d) psychological and psychosocial support, (e) social and economic inclusion, and (f) laws and public policies.
The findings of the pilot project in 2018 informed the need for continuation of the MVA intervention and a 10-month project was launched in Mar 2019 with a key development which included the change in the scope of the intervention resulting in equal assistance for both, families with child survivors and adult survivors of mines/ERW who are also primary caregivers of children.
The action is formed by the UN Policy on Victim Assistance in Mine Action and built around five key components all inspired from this.
The 1. component envisions strengthening of communication between national governmental actors and local service providers through the facilitation of joint meetings, round tables discussions to ensure the provision of better support to the mine/ERW victims and their families. The 2. component consists of direct support to victims through case management and targeted individual assistance (in cash or in-kind). Victims will be closely followed by DRC-DDG staff who will ensure needs are met to the largest extent possible which includes: emergency and ongoing medical treatment, psychosocial support, physical rehabilitation (e.g. prosthesis) and social/economic inclusion. Part of this support will be secured through referrals and follow-up with relevant governmental/non-governmental services. The 3. component aims at strengthening national capacities through completion of a detailed physical rehabilitation assessment (focusing on technical aspects of prosthesis production and physiotherapy). In the 4. component, DRC-DDG will deliver a number of trainings in the selected communities (targeting schoolteachers, school children, and key community representatives) on how to improve inclusion of persons with disability into social and economic life. Following this, community-based initiatives focusing on such inclusion will be supported where the effects are deemed to be highest. Finally, to complement this, advocacy efforts will be reinforced to promote recommendations, developed as part of the Needs Assessment report.

1 - Needs

In this section, the analysis of needs was developed based on the findings from Mine Victim Needs Assessment report, developed by DRC-DDG in partnership with UNICEF, and direct work with mine/ERW direct victims and their families over the course of implementation of case management activities. The majority of the assessed accidents were registered in rural areas of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts. The findings indicate that rural areas tend to have lower coverage of government services such as health care, police and general access to social services. Therefore, the population of these areas, who are at a higher risk of suffering mine/ERW accidents also have a lower prospect of receiving timely and high-quality needs‐based assistance. In terms of support to mine/ERW victims, gaps and barriers were identified for all MVA pillars. Key findings include: 1) no centralised and standardised system for the collection, maintenance and analysis of data on mine victims is in place 2) survivors are at risk of being left with uncovered needs when it comes to ongoing medical care, other than physical rehabilitation 3) information on and access to physical rehabilitation services is very limited and mainly impractical cosmetic prosthetics are provided to persons with impairments 4) psychological counselling is rarely available and deemed not to be necessary 5) generally victims have a poor understanding of their rights according to national legislation and local official entities interpret those same laws differently. DRC-DDG continues identification of adult mine/ERW survivors in conflict-affected areas in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts. During the case management activities, DRC-DDG has identified that adult victims experience similar issues in accessing support services on their path to recovery as child mine/ERW survivors. Based on the aforementioned findings and drawing on previous global experiences in holistic MVA programming, DRC-DDG has developed a multifaceted response, detailed in this proposal.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

DRC Protection team will ensure accountability to the affected people and communities at all stages of the project cycle. Guided by the Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) which goes in line with the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability. The proposed action forms a vital contribution to the overall mine action response in Ukraine and synergies are expected with DDG mine action programming. The holistic approach, envisioned by DRC-DDG for MVA interventions, is in line with UN Policy on Victim Assistance in Mine Action, which is built around five key components which are drawn from the MVA pillars. The proposed action will focus on the following interventions which address the existing gaps in MVA provision: Outcome 1: Mine/ERW victims (including persons with disabilities) have access to a tailored support in the aftermath of the mine/ERW related incident. Activity 1. Advocacy Round Table with Oblast and Local authorities. Support to the systems and standards: Support the state experts’ visits to the key regional government service providers to strengthen the communication and the understanding of reforms and the new legislation, and to ensure better support to MVA at the regional level. Activity 2. Advocacy Roundtable with National authorities. Support to the law and public policies - reinforcement of advocacy efforts based on the findings and recommendations from the needs assessment report. The proposed interventions would include round table meetings with state and regional government stakeholders responsible for the implementation of MVA pillars in order to raise awareness about the gaps in legislation and develop steps to address them. Activity 3. Targeted Individual Assistance: Direct support to victims: Case management support and targeted individual assistance through which victims will be closely followed by DRC-DDG staff who will ensure that their needs are met to the largest extent possible. This would include: emergency and ongoing medical treatment, psycho-social support, physical rehabilitation, including the provision of prosthetics and socio-economic inclusion. Activity 4. Detailed physical rehabilitation needs assessment which will focus on technical aspects of prosthetics’ production and physiotherapy. The assessment will be conducted by an international expert in rehabilitation and will include concrete recommendations. The findings of the assessment will be disseminated to the relevant health providers and state actors. Outcome 2. Communities from mine/ERW affected Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts, GCA are equipped with knowledge to create safer and inclusive environment for mine/ERW direct victims and persons with disabilities. Enhance protective environment will include further work in mine/ERW and conflict-affected communities and will aim to remove the barriers for social reintegration for persons with disabilities - raising awareness about rights of persons with disabilities and developing concrete actions to address these barriers. Activity 1. Provide of inclusion training activities in mine/ERW effected communities for key figures – teachers, workers of social sphere, administrators.Activity 2. Provide of inclusion training activities in mine/ERW effected communities for pupils.Activity 3. Supporting Inclusive Community Projects in targeted communities.
