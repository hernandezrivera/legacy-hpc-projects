Project Summary

"This proposed project will be for one year and will enable COAST Foundation to contribute to achieving the objectives set by the ISCG FSS sector by addressing the needs of the Host population in Ukhiya and Teknaf. But the project has developed for one year and will be continued next two years with indicative direction and intervention.
At the moment, COAST Foundation is working on a project that aims to increase food security in host community regions in Teknaf and Ukhiya. This project has evolved to continue providing services and assist more vulnerable and low-income people in the affected host community areas in 2023. Women's participation in the project's conception, management, and monitoring has been given priority. The project will primarily target women, and women with disabilities in project activities addressing their needs and support, in order to fulfill the needs of the most vulnerable people's food security.
The project’s major activities include; the project will provide training to a total of 1000 women and 5000 family members on crop agricultural gardening, crop production, and training on fisheries and livestock animal rearing which will enhance their food security. The project will also provide conditional cash investment support to start their initiatives. The project will organize and work on market linkage, private sector engagement, etc. Project beneficiaries will be supported and trained in Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), landslide early warning systems, etc. The project will work to improve natural resource management through tree plantation and reforestation activities. The community will be aware of PSEA, complaint and reporting mechanism, child marriage, social cohesion and peacebuilding, and protection and safeguarding issues.
The project will also ensure community participation in all stages of project cycle management. A community consultation meeting will be organized for the selection of beneficiaries. The project will consider and ensure regular beneficiary feedback events and learning-sharing meetings. A total of 1000 families from the selected two unions will be the direct beneficiaries of this project and 5000 will be in-direct beneficiaries. Women-headed households, families have low families income, and the person with disabilities members in families suffering from an overall financial crisis due to the Rohingya influx will get priority.
The project will conduct community consultation meetings and most affected families will be selected as beneficiaries. However, some criteria will be followed for selecting beneficiaries i.e. affected by the influx, lost everything or partially, not receiving similar services by any actor, etc. COAST will conduct community consultations meeting for setting beneficiaries' selection criteria. The project will also take surveys to know the beneficiary's needs by the section criteria.
The project will be coordinating even more effectively incoming years with Food Security Sector by participating Sectoral meetings, workshops, trainings, field visits, timely submission of 5Ws timely. The project will also keep effective coordination with EETWG, ADWG. Project Focal person will participate regularly. COAST will also participate market day, fair and other joint event to showcase project achievement.
"

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"This project will address the needs of the affected host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf. COAST has conducted an assessment of livelihood opportunities where we have found that the host community and Rohingya communities both are the most vulnerable in terms of food security. As many actors are working in camps areas, COAST Foundation will address the needs of affected host community areas and continue the services. Deputy Commissioner (DC) office also suggested working more in vulnerable host community areas. According to the assessment, COAST has found that the host community doesn’t have enough income to live. Both 76% of respondents from host communities reported that they have don’t enough income to live and do not have good food for their families. 56% of respondents informed that they lack the skill to earn in their house like IGA and another 50% of respondents have no financial ability to start any IGA. Women are most vulnerable in case of poor financial conditions. They had to reduce the number of meals compared to males. According to the ACAPS – NPM ANALYSIS HUB, Thematic report, 30 August 2022, the impact of COVID-19, has reduced livelihood options for both communities. According to the report it has found that in the host community, households with a high dependency ratio, and households with oneperson aged five or older who needed daily support consistently continued becoming morevulnerable to food insecurity (REVA 2018 and 2019; J-MSNA Host 2019 and 2020; ACAPS 04/10/2020). In 2021, more FHHs (48%) were found to have inadequate food consumption than male-headedhouseholds (36%) (REVA 01/2022). In the host community, men had better access to the local labour market than women, enabling them to access better food supplies. On the other hand, according to Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis report (round 1), Teknaf was identified as a sub-district with the least resilience index (36.2). The low resilience index of Ukhia and Teknaf is mainly characterized by their limited ownership of assets like productive assets, non-productive assets, and land. Moreover, usage of fertilizers and organic manure in agriculture production is also limited there. So, targeting Ukhiya and Teknaf Sub-district is very rational according to the reference reports. "

This proposed project is absolutely aligned to the Rationalization Principles. As the aims to improve inter-sectoral coordination mechanism and service delivery to host communities. This project will be coordinating with the Government part like UNO and DC office coordination as this project is only focus in host community. AS COAST has been working in targeted geographical locational for long time. COAST will consolidate the services to geographically contiguous areas. In host community targeted areas, COAST has other program like child protection project and micro finance project which will help COAST to work in coordinating way. As this project has been implementing last three years and planning to next year, so COAST will try to continue the services. So, this project is aligned with rationalization principle 3 and 4.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will consider no harm for doing anything. The project will prioritize the safety and dignity and avoid causing harm. Project will also consider the unintended adverse effects of your intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Beneficiaries will have meaningful access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without discrimination, gender, disability or age-related barriers. Paying special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, e.g. Persons with disability, elderly, children, etc. And the project the set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can be involved in all participatory aspects of project cycle from design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation and establish measures for affected populations to measure the appropriateness of interventions, and organizations can address concerns and complaint

3.2 - Gender

Women empowerment will be given in priority and their participation in economic activities will be ensured. Gender role in the community will be transferred as the knowledge and skill of women have increased. In family decision making process, the women will be involved and participated.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project will provide PSEA, complain response mechanism (CRM), child safeguarding training. These training will help the communities to prevent themselves from any negative incident by the project staff and community people by awareness and actions.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will target considering the diversity like include most marginal groups, ethnic and minority group in project activities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

COAST will ensure the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) conducting community consultation meeting, involve them in project design and monitoring and influence on project activities. Besides, Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be carried out by hiring enumerators after distributions. This activity will help the project to know the community perception, feedback, further recommendations and impact of the distribution in the community. As COAST is local organization and born in Cox’s Bazar, so this project will demonstrations of localization by engaging local community in implementation of the project. COAST is active partner of FSS sector and attend coordination meeting at sector. The project will be coordinating with FSS in regular basis. 5W, Sitrep and other required reports will be shared for better coordination.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

For environmental sustainability, DRR training, tree plantation/reforestation, Climate Resilience Adaptive Technologies, Contingency Fund to Response any disaster and emergency will be ensured. Every year, cyclones, floods and landslides occur in these areas. Besides, host community people often faced by severe natural disaster like flash flood, landslides, cyclone and others. To get respond from community people and better preparation, COAST will provide DRR, climate change awareness and using climate adaptive technology and environmentally sustainable training. These training will help the communities to reduce the loss resources due to disaster. 10 Hectares of lands will be reforested by tree plantation initiatives.
