Project Summary

This project will target small farmers in need of humanitarian support in order to strengthen their resilience within their main source of income
Its aim is to provide food security and live in dignity in the face of political volatility, to which farmers may be a direct victim, which is reflected in further poverty, unemployment and gender-based violenceThe agricultural assets of small farmers will be rehabilitated and provided in three governorates from Gaza Strip to enable them to provide livelihoods, with taking into consideration the targeting of both sexes and the inclusion of persons with disabilities in project activities
So the Project Goal:
1) To improve access to water for agriculture.
2) To strengthen the productive capacity of the affected farmers from the last Israeli attack on Gaza Strip to produce fresh vegetables.
The project contributes to Country Strategic Objective SO3 and FSS Objectives
1&2.

1 - Needs

The Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) reported that more than 20,000 dunums of vegetable’s lands all over the Gaza Strip have been severely affected and damaged due to the Israeli attack in 2014, and only less than 50% of them have been rehabilitated (FSS damages and response report, March 2017). In April 2017, the Gaza farmers faced one of the worst energy crises in its history having an average of only 2-hours power on and 22 hours power cut. Farmers considered those who have been harshly affected since they depend on electrical power to irrigate their lands. The sharp decreases in electricity resulted losing approximately 60% of irrigation water needed for vegetables. The damages were doubled considering the planting seasonality and the hot summer. This led to (according to MoA report, May 2017) about 60% losses (about 90 million American Dollar) in production especially in the Khan Yunis and Rafah governorates; the Gaza Strip vegetables basket .ACAD field visits show that the majority of agricultural lands in the Gaza, Khan Yunis and Rafah governorates were not rehabilitated. According to MoA needs in September 2017, there is still 17,000 dunums in need of agricultural interventions in the three governorates. This doubled crises led to a significant increase in inputs prices (mainly irrigation water) on the farmer’s burden leading him to huge losses and lead to some farmers destroying their crops due to their inability to complete the season. Consequently, the owners of these lands became vulnerable. The protracted crisis of blockade into Gaza with very limited resources and inputs, is also an obstacle facing farmers causing additional burden with the increased prices of inputs followed by limitations on exports. In March 2017, ACAD conducted two focus groups in Gaza governorate and in Khan Yunis with the affected farmers (15 women and 15 men) and interviews with the planning directorate of MoA and the related MoA directorates to assess the main needs of the affected farmers. The recommendation we got was to achieve a kind of complementarity of activities. Water wells rehabilitation, As a result, ACAD plan is to conduct the activities in three governoratesACAD will closely coordinate activities with MoA, FSS and other organizations working in the targeted governorates, naming PUI, CARE, UAWC, MAAN and PARC, as well as other actors. 2 - Activities

• Forming a community committee (Community Mobilization) • The committee will consist of members from entrepreneur farmers, municipalities and ACAD project staff as well as MoA representatives. The aim of the committee is to determine the selection criteria of the beneficiaries, review the list of the applicant farmers, identify and finalize the beneficiaries list, conduct field visits to the final list. - This community will also cork on conflict resolution if occurred in the community, it will also assist ACAD team to provide a high sense of • accountability towards the communities through a well-established feedback and complaint mechanism. Through strong coordination with FSS, MoA and other NGOs working in the area ACAD will ensure no duplication in providing service nor beneficiaries. ACAD will ensure fair presence of a female in the committee hence can ensure female farmers are well represented in the project committee. Project committees in the targeted communities will ensure effective communication with them. • Tendering, Purchasing and distributing the agricultural inputs • ACAD will purchase the inputs for the damaged open lands rehabilitation including irrigation nets, organic fertilizers, seasonal seeds or seedlings(Seeds will be planted according to the season as peas - cabbage - potatoes)- ACAD will contract with local suppliers according to the proper agricultural standards. One Hundred and fifty (150) farmers (2 dunums each) will be provided with the required rehabilitation inputs according to their needs and under the supervision of the community committee and project staff. • The skilled workers will coach the unskilled ones during the process of rehabilitation in order to acquire them new skills of design and implement works on the land. 9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing ACAD humanitarian interventions. ACAD premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential the principles of protection in the project are part of humanitarian actionPrinciples governing humanitarian interventions for ACAD. It almost opened its projectDesign a "No Harm" approach to ensure that project activities are not delivered to cause harm to the target population by facilitating access to resources without any risk to their safety as well as taking into account no environmental damage as a result of project, As well as ensuring that there are no damages in the process of completion of the project and acceptance by the beneficiaries - as well as ensure any damage related to financial changes to the project inputs, which may adversely affect the quality of production inputs if not taken into considerationTherefore, to ensure that no harm is done to the beneficiaries, a mechanism for handling complaints, feedback and feedback will be clarified through the local committees that will contribute to conveying the complaints to the project staff - by specifying addresses and mobile numbers, the beneficiaries of the project can communicate if any damage occurs.Define a complaints fund in the local committees areas through which the beneficiaries can submit complaints within the project areas to ensure the ease of the beneficiaries To avoid any harm during the project activities, evaluation and follow-up will be carried out on a daily basis to ensure that services reach all beneficiaries.The follow-up process will be carried out by the project team as well as members of local committees in the project areasIn addition, field visits will be conducted for beneficiaries, field survey and implementation of public meetings and focus groups to measure the impact of the project on the lives of beneficiaries.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

At the community level, three (3) local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their active participation with target groups throughout the project, to enhancing community participation and reduce any potential risks at the community level when project implementation . Capacity-building of targeted committees to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through guidance sessions to inform target communities of the services provided during the project as well as on safety measures. In addition, the design of the project takes into account the principles of gender discrimination and protection by emphasizing women's participation in the project committee as well as exploring the needs of women and people with disabilities. Furthermore, ACAD will ensure the safe exit of the project by providing more sustainable solutions to the identified needs and proposed intervention through a holistic approach that ensures that women, men, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups targeted as part of our interventions. Committee will be sensitized to feedback mechanisms and complaints management process. Through strong coordination with the FSS, and other NGOs working in the region will ensure that ACAD does not duplicate service delivery or beneficiaries. It will almost guarantee the fair presence of females in the committee and this ensure that women farmers are well represented on the project committee. Project committees in the target communities will ensure effective communication with them, and working on ensure access to all beneficiaries according to a specific criteria without discrimination based on sex, marital status or health status (with or without disabilities) 'vulnerable are selected through: - Allocating a percentage of some persons with disabilities to benefit from the project activities- Verify the names with the Ministry of Agriculture and government institutions working in the field of support and integration of people with special needs- Carry out field visits to reach the largest number of persons with disabilities- Conducting a field survey, registering cases and providing agricultural activities commensurate with their circumstances- Provide a database of the competent authorities on the numbers of persons with disabilities in the project areas of both sexes and try to reach them within the criteria of the project to work with them with fairness and justice- Involve people with disabilities in the process of designing, implementing and evaluating the project
