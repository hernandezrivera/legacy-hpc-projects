Project Summary

This project aimed to achieve two overarching objectives which include: 1) Ensure the provision of GBV prevention and risk mitigation measures among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in Juba (Northern Bari, Rajaf, Wonduruba, Rokon, Lobonok and Lirya), and Mvolo (Mvolo and Yeri) counties, and 2) Reduce the suffering of women, men, girls, and boys who have experienced various forms of GBV and reported to service delivery points in Juba (Northern Bari, Rajaf, Lobonok, Wonduruba, Rokon and Lirya), and Mvolo (Mvolo and Yeri) counties to 16,234 beneficiaries who includes. With total envelope of $487,039 of which AYA have already secured $200,000 for GBV programming in Juba- 2021.
Active Youth Agency (AYA) intends to continue running and establishing women and girls friendly spaces in Juba, and Mvolo continue providing survivor-centered psychosocial support to survivors of gender based violence and other abuses, conduct and strengthen case management system in all the operation areas, establish or strengthen referral pathways which forms basis of essential information about services available and contact persons, provide livelihood opportunities through cash and voucher assistance to people having protection concerns and risks, implement community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk mitigation which will involve working with other clusters, engage men and boys in accountable practices through using EMAP approach and conduct capacity building to other frontline service providers on basics of gender based violence. AYA foresee changes if the proposed activities are implemented in totality.
Active Youth Agency (AYA) in past projects and especially WASH-GBV project implemented in Juba have built capacities of its staffs to handle GBV cases with quality assurance put in place. The organization was trained by Cash Working group on Core skills for Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) and a member of Cash Working Group in South Sudan. AYA is GBV Prevention Technical Group (TRG) Co-coordinator, supports coordinating prevention activities across South Sudan. Being strong National NGO implementing Engaging Men through Acountable Practice (EMAP), AYA will use its valuable skills and assets to implement EMAP in Juba and Mvolo counties. AYA is GBV Working Group Co-coordinator for Mundri. The organization will use the coordination skills to mainstream GBV across sectors which include WASH, Livelihood, Nutrition, and Education among other selected sectors. The organization is implementing CASI (Caring for Child Survivors of Sexual Violence Initiative) supported by International Rescue Committee in Juba. This comprised of different capacity building initiatives that started in July 2020. AYA is also among the few Data Gathering Organizations (DGOs) in South Sudan and also selected to pilot Gender Based Violence Information Management System Plus (GBVIMS+). Therefore, there is already existing capacity in terms of case management and quality GBV programming. There are policies available which includes Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse, Child Sage-guarding, Code of Conduct and the organization is in process of developing gender equality policy that will be out in two months’ time. Therefore, staffs are oriented on the policy.

1 - Needs

Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate protection services or will have protection concerns in South Sudan in 2021. An estimate conducted by Protection Cluster in 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO). However, entire people in need (PIN) estimated to be 8.3 million2. These attributed to rising food insecurity (IPC classification), insecurity and sub-national conflicts, economic crises or rise in commodities, floods, disease outbreaks that is supported by weak health systems and institutions, persistent gender inequality, early and forced marriage, and gender based violence that mostly affect women and girls3. Weak legal frameworks across the country affects the survivors of access to or and achieving justices. This makes out-of-school adolescent girls and boys to suffer the violence quietly without perpetrators being apprehended. The main drivers of GBV Prevention and Response needs include limited physical infrastructure for handling survivors of Gender Based Violence, sub-national violence and recurring flood, increased displacement places an extra burden on limited available services. The recent conflict that displaced people from Tindilo,Tali Payams ,and Terekeka to Juba and Kedi’ba and possible return to Tali and Tindilo. The violent conflict that displaced people to Lirya Payam and Lobonok, Wonduruba and Ketigiri respectively also majorly contributed to the overstretched need for services especially GBV prevention and response. The reports of harassment, abduction, and killing remained at the peak in areas5 (IOM-EP&R assessment-July 2020).Due to economic hardships and other deep rooted social norms, early and forced marriages remained on rise in the targeted counties6 as well as other negative practices that affects women and girls such as denial of essential economic opportunities and ownership of resources as well as accumulation of wealth (IRNA Mundri W/E, Mvolo and Maridi- March 2020, IRNA Lirya- July 2020). Lack of services remained the challenge to already struggling community. IOM’s survey on the perceptions of women collecting water show only 34% of women always felt safe, and 20% rarely felt safe, with Juba identified as one of the highest risk areas (IOM, 2018). The COVID-19 pandemic further acerbated the risks of violence against women and girls. This is because the movement and other restrictions caused an economic downturn, increased stress at home, the need for use of water increased leading to more risks of gender-based violence for women and girls. Since household chores including fetching water, cooking and caring for children and other dependents is role of girls and women, and boys enjoy the privileges and can undermine household and community adoption of hygiene practices. Women and girls in the project targeted areas as any other females in South Sudan lack decision-making powers in households and community level. Women taking up roles at the community level are not encouraged, especially for leadership. This along with lack of menstrual hygiene management materials makes girls reduce the days they attend in class leading to poor performance. On the other hand, the survivors of Gender- Based Violence as result of the issues linked to WASH, conflict, famine and other harmful gender norms receive little to no support from communities. Communities lack knowledge of consequences of violence and services available, and GBV survivors face barriers to access the services that do exist due to stigma and fear of retaliation. The WGFS that AYA is managing in Juba has been accessed by many GBV survivors during the COVID-19 pandemic and now survivors and the wider community are gaining the knowledge about the availability of services. It is important to strengthen and replicate to other targeted locations of project intervention in 2021. Therefore, this project intends to work in Juba County, Terekeka County and Mvolo County.

AYA will form and engage men in two locations (Juba and Mvolo) with 5 men and women dialogue groups in each location. 15 participants per dialogue group to respect COVID-19 rules. 1200 (600w/600m) beneficiaries targeted to benefit.8. And provide capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to front-line service providers. This training will target AYA Community Mobilizers (70, 35w/35m), staffs of women and girls friendly spaces, other staffs from different service providers to be trained on different GBV topics (66, 39w/27m).

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market assessments have been conducted by the use of MIRA and it is integrated into preparedness and planning for this project. For example in Juba, markets are functional and available goods are readily on the local markets. There are available vendors who will be contracted to support delivery of cash to beneficiaries which includes using cash vouchers, mobile phones such as Nilepay, and there are many small banks to deliver cash assistance. Therefore, there has been basic understanding of market structure, capacity of traders, prices of commodities or services in the different market locations in project area and financial service providers. There has been already MEB calculated for Juba and there is available information in FSL Cluster that AYA makes its use to support the process. In Juba County and Northern Bari, Rajaf Payams, Markets can be easily accessed by beneficiaries. 90% of beneficiaries can access market within 20 hours. While in Mvolo, only two Payams of Yeri and Mvolo have functional markets.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash assistance is cheaper than in-kind support. People are free to choose what they want to use their money for. Study has shown that cash assistance restores dignity and choice even in most difficult circumstances. Therefore, giving cash help to easy burden on the staff. The major aim of the cash transfer in our context is to meet basic needs of individuals who might have protection concerns. It can help them get to health facility, provide for food during referral and provision of life-saving support.Given the difficulties in transportation and movement in South Sudan, essential services will be difficult to reach the beneficiaries in early and timely manner. Cash will also support the empowerment of local market and enhances the market functionality based on the assessments conducted in regards to market. Cash assistance is cost effective.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AYA has PSEA Focal Point and the organizational staffs are sensitized on the policy. There is also Code of Conduct AYA has developed and signed by every staff. There will be continuous awareness to the community about the involvement of AYA’s staff in exploiting the vulnerability of the community and also availing the contacts of the Focal Point at any stage of activity implementation. If found, there is already body set to investigate or refer.AYA will help the communities to access equal services, during meetings share about Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and work with partners to report cases related to SEA to enable the community to receive aid without fearing any harassment or abuses. There is already IOM poster on PSEA that will be used. AYA’s new staffs will be given code of conducts to sign alongside the contracts. Education and enforcement of code of conduct is vital which AYA will conduct by ensuring that all new staff and volunteers are oriented and also that there is an annual refresher session for existing staff and volunteers. AYA has the Focal Point on SEA and the organization is capable of doing its own investigation. There has been no report involving our staff. However, community does get awareness and there has been sharing that AYA uses its channel of referral. The feedback will be communicated to the community through different channels which include meetings with community leaders, meeting with direct affected beneficiary and women in women and girls friendly spaces. AYA will organize specific feedback sessions with the key concerned individuals.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

AYA will have feedback sessions with the community leaders, beneficiaries including persons with disability and old age to communicate the results of complaints received from the beneficiaries. This will help to instill the trust and above all the centrality of protection where recipients of aid are at the center of the aid delivery.Different modalities will be used in disseminating the feedback using the appropriate channels and methods of delivery. This includes using sign language, communicating and meetings to enable every category of community to feel that their complaint is heard and been addressed. AYA will also work to address the complaints through going to other partners for meeting and discussing the feedback if it concerns the services of other partners.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

There is already complaint mechanism in place that AYA use for collecting complaints, analyzing and disseminating the complaints for decision making purposes. The complaint desk is established in each women and girls friendly space, it can be inform of suggestion boxes, client satisfaction survey for the consumers of AYA's services or focus group discussion. The table is also placed and marked complaint desk. This helps the beneficiaries feel comfortable to launch their complaints. There are also already established phone contacts given to community for launching complaints against the activities of Active Youth Agency. This therefore, will be communicated and addressed as it arises.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The complaints will be used in redesigning the activities of Active Youth Agency (AYA). The views of beneficiaries will be incorporated to reflect the best practices and therefore, making the aid friendly to all the beneficiaries. The regular project management meetings, weekly, monthly and quarterly plans will take in consideration the complaints or feedback from the beneficiaries regarding to the services offered by AYA. The client satisfaction survey will enable the Case Workers to know what they do best in Case Management and their supervisors will support conducting specific in-house sessions that takes in consideration the feedback from the community and key beneficiaries of the assistance.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Proper planning, consultation of every group of beneficiaries, disseminating PSEA messages, making every staff sign Code of Conduct before starting any engagement with the community, strengthening community complaint mechanism, dissemination of humanitarian principles to the key stakeholders, training staffs on key GBV guiding principles and conducting assessments prior to the distribution of items. AYA will orient all of its volunteers on six principles of PSEA. This will include all the staff either part time or full time volunteers. Community awareness shall be done to communities on issues pertaining sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. The contacts for the focal points shall be given to the community members to enable reports of any act constitute SEA. Therefore, the proper feedback mechanism to the community shall be established to generate and disseminate some feedback to the communities. All these will help to mitigate the risks that are anticipated.
