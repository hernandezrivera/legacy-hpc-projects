Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the improved resilience of conflict-affected populations in Northwest Syria by enhancing access to livelihood opportunities and basic services for women and men in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. IYD will achieve this objective by the implementation of the following integrated activities:
1-IYD will target 30 camps located in Aleppo and Idleb governorates to rehabilitate the main roads between the targeted camps and surrounding communities so that we can prevent the flood and ensure good accessibility to the targeted camps. The graveling process will cover 30,000 M3 in the targeted camps. Based on IYD technical team, the provided thickness of graveling will be 0.5 M.
2-To further increase the benefit from graveling the camp's roads, prevent collecting the rainwater above the graveled roads, and ensure the stability of the graveling, IYD will establish 10,000 ML of closed drainage systems in the targeted camps and surrounding communities.
3-Asphalting of 5 roads (10 KM) to keep the link between the targeted camps and surrounding communities in the targeted area based on the location assessment which will be conducted by IYD. By the end of this activity, 5 roads in camps and surrounding communities will be asphalted. The planned thickness of the provided asphalt will be 7-10 cm
4-On the other hand, IYD will launch CFW activities by conducting a market assessment to determine which type of activities needed to be supported with labor. Under CFW activity, 3000 BNFs, including women and PWDs, will be supported with temporary job opportunities. The CFW activity will be divided into 10 rounds, and each round will last for 20 working days by targeting 300 BNFs based on the locations assessment that will be conducted by IYD to select the most priority works by population
5-Furthermore, with the chronic lack of livelihoods, the project activities will scale up emergency livelihood support focused on capacity building of the targeted people, restore local manufacturing, mitigate the impact of COVID-19, and enhance food security NWS by providing vocational training to 1800 individuals in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. IYD will conduct a vocations assessment to select the most 20 preferable vocations by population. The VT activity will be divided into 6 rounds, and each round will last for one month by targeting 300 BNFs. By the end of each training, the capacity for the targeted BNFs will be improved so that the trained vocations will help the BNFs to enhance their income.
6-Following to vocational training activity, IYD will provide business support to 500 new businesses support for the best-trained BNFs to initiate their new jobs. In addition, providing 500 apprenticeship opportunities to support the local market.
7-Based on market assessment, IYD will rehabilitate 10 local infrastructure projects by vendors and CFW workers like (Canals, irrigation systems, markets, storage facilities, bakeries,etc.), therefore, the accessibility to the public services and markets will be easier
Here we should mention that high coordination will be made with Cash Working Group and the NGOs working on the ground to ensure CVA coordination and harmonisation. The CVA activities will foster social cohesion as they will include both IDPs and host communities who will be together while implementing these activities. In addition, community/local stakeholders at the area of implementation will be consulted on project design, especially on the daily wage rate, the number of work hours per day, and project duration. CfW daily wage rates will be considered not to distort the local labour market and draw workers away from local employment opportunities. Cash for Work Minimum Recommended Actions Guidance for NWS will be used as a base while developing the CVA activities. The market assessments that will be conducted will consider all market functions/proximty/etc

3 - Needs

IYD conducted a comprehensive needs assessment in the governorates of Aleppo and Idlib . the results are: - The majority of assessed communities have an unemployment rate of (51-75%). The following trades (Grain, real estate, seeds and building materials) are available in the assessed communities. - Based on the interviewed KIIs, there is a need for training on MS office, data science, photography, and android applications. - The worker's daily average wage is (15 TL), and the average number of working hours is 7. - The most needed support is providing vocational training and providing cash for work activities. - There are local trainers to provide the (theoretical, practical) training with an average wage (80 TL), while to establish a new business, (7,500 TL) is needed as capital. - The preferred professions for females are sewing and food industry, and the preferred ones for males are solar energy, electrical equipment’s and beekeeping - Most assessed communities and camps have dirt roads. - The majority of targeted camps have been affected by the last flood. - Gravelling the roads inside the camps and constructing drainage systems can effectively alleviate the flood incidents' occurrence every year in the assessed camps - The access of the surveyed camps’ inhabitants to the surrounding communities is quite difficult during winter because of the heavy rain and floods, and gravling the roads linking the camps to the nearby communities is an effective solution.

4 - Implementation of activities

As soon as the project fund is secured, IYD will coordinate with local authorities and leaders, announce the project, and start the activities. 1-Graveling activity: IYD will gravel 30,000 M3 ( 0.5 thickness) of the main roads between the targeted camps and surrounding communities in the targeted area. 2-Drainage systems: IYD will establish 10,000 ML of closed drainage systems, in addition to rehabilitating the 5,000 ML of the damaged sewage systems in the targeted areas. 3-Asphalting activity: IYD will asphalte 5 roads (10 KM-10 cm thickness) in the camps and surrounding communities in the targeted area based on the location assessment which will be conducted by IYD. 4-CFW activity: IYD will conduct a market assessment to determine which type of activities needed to be supported with labour. 3000 BNFs will be provided with temporary job opportunities into 5 rounds based on the location assessment. 5-Vocational training activities: To mitigate the impact of COVID-19, and enhance food security, IYD will conduct a vocations assessment to select the most 20 preferable vocations by population. Later providing vocational training for 1800 individuals into 6 rounds, 300 BNFs per round. 6-Business activity: IYD will provide business support to 500 new businesses support for the best-trained BNFs to initiate their new jobs. 7- IYD will rehabilitate 5 local infrastructure projects by vendors and CFW workers like (Canals, irrigation systems, markets, storage facilities, bakeries

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: GBV Risks: The backlash from family or community on women participating in project activities and preventing them from traveling to other areas and accessing services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Highly. Mitigation: • Community engagement from the onset of the project, from the assessment phase, and throughout all project cycles. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders. • Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities. Risk 2: Sexual exploitation and abuse by the project staff, suppliers, contractors, against female beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Highly. Mitigation: - All staff and other contractors will sign the Code of Conduct and receive mandatory training on PSEA. - Conducting PSEA awareness sessions for the beneficiaries. - Establishing complaints mechanisms and addressing these complaints. •Risk 3: The project activities may cause GBV risks for some women, girls, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups during their participation in these activities either through a cause of action or failing to take necessary actions. Likelihood: low. Impact: High Mitigation: IYD will contribute to reducing risks, by raising awareness, providing GBV risk mitigation training, and linking survivors to services directly and through a referral system. IYD will work with all gender groups, communities, and local authorities to transform harmful gender norms and attitudes that perpetuate GBV, and promote equitably


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

•IYD has adopted the inter-agency Code of Conduct Which condemns the SEA ( Endorsed by the Humanitarian Liaison Group - 2020) for the Syria cross-border response, which is intended for all staff and representatives of humanitarian organizations as well as their associated contractors, and volunteers •IYD is an active member of the PSEA network, and it is already familiar with the network’s monthly reporting tool where IYD reports its PSEA monthly activities such as (the number of beneficiaries reached by the distribution of awareness-raising materials, the number of beneficiaries reached by providing PSEA awareness-raising sessions, the number of field staff with direct interaction with beneficiaries who trained on PSEA) •IYD has established an internal SEA reporting and investigative mechanism which will not tolerate an atmosphere of impunity toward SEA. Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards of conduct •Complete PSEA Checklists: The organizational checklist was be completed by the management and the HR unit in communication with the CEO. Also, the Field Observation Checklist for PSEA Mainstreaming will be completed by IYD's program managers who will utilize these tools in all our interventions during the implementation and monitoring stages to identify gaps and risks related to PSEA


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Institutional: Low collaboration between IYD and the actors involved in the project leading to a slowdown in the implementation of activities. However, IYD’s strong presence and good name will enable it to overcome this challenge• Operational: Limited capacity of service providers leading to a delay in the supply process. To mitigate this, IYD will keep following up with the service providers day by day, to ensure their commitment to the contracts signed with them•Security Risks: Interference from the military controlling groups or the local councils in the targeted areas, and deterioration of the security situation. IYD has a strong relationship with neutral communities’ leaders and will highly coordinate with them for the prevention of any interference attempts in the project activities or IYD’s work as a whole. IYD will coordinate with the active actors on the ground to select the safest area to implement this project; additionally, IYD’s safety and security team will keep following up the security situation and send updates to the field team to ensure their safety• Aid diversion risk whose mitigation will be Staff vetting, Vetting for supplier,etc. All CVA-specific risks will be identified and mitigated based on Northwest Syria Cash and Voucher Assistance Risk and Mitigation Matrix developed by a number of International and Syrian NGOs
