Project Summary

The spread of the conflict in Tigray into neighboring regions of Afar and Amhara during 2021, has left millions of people, displaced and non-displaced, in need of assistance and protection in these two regions of Northern Ethiopia. In response to this, UNHCR harmonizes its IDP response with the Cluster system, the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the Sustainable Development Goals, the Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement (1999) and the Kampala Convention (2009) to support PoCs.
Recognizing that contexts across the region is volatile with unique needs per location, UNHCR Shelter and Non- food item response is planned to address the needs of people in all phases of displacement, with a focus on the emergency phase, care, and maintenance of shelter for people in protracted displacements and supporting sustainable returns and recovery where the context allows. The planned interventions include consideration of shelter preparedness, response, and shelter recovery support to account for both returns and secondary displacement and will be conducted in close coordination with multi-sectoral stakeholders to ensure that approaches are integrated, and that broader needs are addressed. Holding the protection of affected populations at its core: Do no harm incl. accountability, participation and empowerment of affected people, enhancing resilience, coordination, and context-driven responses, principles will be mainstreamed throughout our responses as per the ESNFI cluster strategy. Through this interventions, including provision of in-kind assistance and cash when feasible, UNHCR planes to reach 80,533 HH (402,664 Individuals) across the Amhara and Afar regions through the below responses.
Emergency Shelters (Communal, family shelters or Emergency Shelter kits) provision Non Food Items and ESNFI provision. For the displaced who have returned to their original homes, support such as Shelter Repair kits either through Cash or In-kind with technical guidance on building better and addressing Housing Land and Property (HLP) will be the focus. Shelter Repair is also planned for displaced populations who are unable to return to their place of origin due to protracted displacement, where government allow to settle in a secondary location. Capacity building of staff, authorities, affected populations and advocacy for those with shelter and household needs is also considered. All the ES/NFIs component will be undertaken in consultation with the IDPs and host communities to ensure it meets their most urgent needs. The IDP hosting communities will also benefit from the responses. As per the cluster guideline 10-15% of the vulnerable host communities hosting IDPs will be targeted. Acknowledging any responses that exclude groups and individuals, increase violence and SGBV risks and increase risk of exploitation, UNHCR will undertake essential actions to reduce risks and mitigate Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) associated with shelter and NFI responses. Awareness-raising sessions will be delivered to ensure that the communities are made aware of the protection risks in shelter and NFI response and how best to mitigate them. UNHCR will continue to use IASC GBV guidelines & related tools for SEA risk assessment and mitigation, including its newly developed GBV policy guidelines, National action plan and Victim centered approach for addressing sexual misconduct to make programmes safe and accessible to all women, girls and other at risk group. Most often persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected by disasters and conflict and they face multi-layered barriers to accessing humanitarian services. As an effort to enhance inclusive humanitarian action principles, UNHCR will provide awareness-raising and training of those at the forefront of service delivery and from the overall target reserving a minimum of 15% of kits (tailor made for disables).

1. - Projects activities

The activities that are going to be undertaken under each response type will be carried out in close coordination and collaboration with all stakeholders including the affected population. In general, the main activities of the project include: Targeting of beneficiaries, Establishing the response approach, communication of the response, Organizing the distribution/construction site as per cluster guideline. Accordingly, the targeting of beneficiaries will be done based on the cluster endorsed vulnerability criteria of Female headed households, Child headed households, Households with a high dependency, Households caring for one or more members with chronic diseases, Elderly (60+) without family support, People living with disabilities, Households with pregnant or lactating women, Households with children under the age of 2 years and Households living in sub-standard shelter conditions (like in collective centre, partial or fully destroyed shelter) and the stage of the shelter in the area of return In consideration of safety of all involved, physical access for the most vulnerable community members, well-being of the community, access to facilities and additional factors response approach will be established. Before and during intervention (distribution and/or construction) UNHCR will ensure effective communication with affected communities on date, time, place, type of items and quantities to be expected, what documentation beneficiaries should bring etc based on the response type through available means of communication like, banners, mass communication and etc. With lesson learned from previous intervention, best practices and as per the ESNFI endorsed guideline each responses will be carried out through the implementing partners with close coordination with DRM and the affected population. In addition UNHCR through its implementing partner will provide the required technical guidance and support to beneficiaries. To gather feedback from the target population on the impact, relevance of responses meeting their needs UNHCR will undertake review of distribution through Post distribution/construction monitoring Survey.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The role of the local and national actors remains central, and UNHCR will continue to cooperate with actors and seek to understand better national and local rules and regulations. Shelter assistance requires a well-coordinated response that ensures effective communication and achieves synergies among other sectors. The expansion of multi-sectoral approaches will ensure that shelter strategies optimize the well-being of displaced people. SGBV, child protection, education, water, sanitation, and hygiene(WASH), health, nutrition, livelihood, and environmental issues are mainstreamed into the process of delivering shelter solutions.The project will be implemented in coordination and collaboration with authorities, Emergency Operation Centres (EOC) and appointed site focal points, the ES/NFI Cluster, as well as other relevant Clusters (protection,…) and partners, including IOM and OCHA. The goal is to ensure an effective, coordinated and harmonized approach among and with cluster partners; in order to fill gaps in protection and assistance, to avoid duplication and for sensible use of resources. UNHCR also works with National NGO for shelter and NFI program intervention as per the cluster’s localization strategy. These organizations play critical role by bringing in the indigenous knowledge and local capacity, increasing direct access to local communities and hard-to-reach areas

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

UNHCR applies its corporate age, gender and diversity policy in all its programming, in addition to its strong data protection policy. UNHCR’s experience in applying these policies in practice will ensure the appropriate, safe and confidential channels required for the implementation of its integrated programme. UNHCR applies a participatory, community-based and age-gender and diversity sensitive approach to ensure the needs of all parts of the affected population are taken into consideration during planning and implementation; affected populations are consulted and engaged during all stages of the project and included in the monitoring of the activities. Beneficiaries are involved throughout the project cycle and information will be shared freely and openly. Beneficiary selection will be conducted in a consultative, transparent and neutral manner with community leaders and representatives, to avoid the perception that any one interest group is being unfairly favored over another. Various methods will be put inplce to receive complaints, including establishment of complaints hearing committees comprised of beneficiaries, suggestion boxes that beneficiaries can give feedback or share any concerns or complaints and receive feedback or response. UNHCR will also be carrying out post distribution monitoring activities to receive feedback from IDPs in connection with the assistance provided as well as on the evolving needs of the population.
