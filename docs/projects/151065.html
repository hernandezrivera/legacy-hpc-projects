Project Summary

There are 2.6 IDPs in Somalia and the risk of GBV is on the rise.According to the GBVIMS managed by the protection cluster 76 per cent of recorded Gender-Based Violence (GBV) incidents were reported by IDPs.perpetrators of GBV often do not get prosecuted.
The objective of the project is to strengthen case management for survivors of Gender-based violence(GBV). WOCCA will be working in Middle Shebelle. The objective of the project is to strengthen case management for 600 survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV) and Family reunification and IDTR for 200 UASC.The project staff who will be providing immediate assistance to the survivors and vulnerable population will be trained on case management and survivor centered approach.
WOCCA will use the recent data collected in Somalia service mapping to link and coordinate with other service providers to provide the survivors with immediate care.The 300 survivors and vulnerable person at risk will be also provided with tailored material assistance, basic emotional support and follow up on the services provided. We will also be providing 1000 solar lanterns to reduce risks to the most vulnerable members of the community and also increase outreach and build networks by training traditional birth attendants, imams and elders to promote awareness of GBV risks and to mitigate exposure to Gender-based violence.The community members (10,000) will be given awareness raising and basic GBV knowledge. The service providers,teachers,community leaders and religious leaders will be trained on GBV risks and referral pathways.The youth at risk will be trained on life skills education to allow them cope up with peer pressure.

Needs

Based on the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview Acute protection needs in Somalia stem from acts of violence, exploitation, abuse, coercion, and deprivation, especially in situations of conflict and displacement, violating International Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law, including grave violations against children. The impact of the crisis is aggravated due to an unconducive protection environment characterized by limited responsive service provision, weak or lacking protection systems, impediments on access to services, low awareness of basic rights, and existing gender discriminatory and harmful socio cultural norms and practices which disadvantage marginalized groups such as persons with disabilities, women and children disproportionately.Based on the Gender-Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) managed by the Protection Cluster indicates that 76 per cent of recorded Gender-Based Violence (GBV) incidents were reported by IDPs. From all the reported cases, 96 per cent of GBV survivors are women and girls. Forced child recruitment is again on the rise, especially in areas under the control of non-state armed actors in southern and central Somalia. It presents not only a protection concern but has also triggered displacement as families seek to shield children from forced recruitment. According to the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) on grave violations committed against children in times of conflict (CAAC), from January to August 2017, a total of 1,202 children have been recruited by armed groups, 95 per cent of them in southern and central Somalia. Nomadic pastoralists who have lost all their livestock, present a particularly challenging case in terms of return or local integration or settlement elsewhere. Some communities, often marginalized groups, have seen their land taken over, rendering principled approaches to durable solutions challenging.

Activities

Activity-Case management for SGBV (500) survivors including referrals by the case workers and recording cases in the GBVIMS. -Family reunification of UASC (200) through Identification, Documentation, Tracing and Reunification and recording their cases in the CPIMS.-Provide material assistance 150 UASC and 300 GBV survivors and persons at risk based on agreed individual assessments. -WOCCA will also use the referral pathways in place to refer survivors who give consent to legal assistance.-GBV and CP caseworkers will receive training on caring for survivors focusing on child survivors as well as incorporating training on survivor centered approach.-6000 community members sensitized in person and through media on Child protection and GBV risk, consequences, available services, mitigation and increase their knowledge and understanding of basic human rights among the affected communities. -Provision of portable solar lanterns to 5500 of the most vulnerable IDP households who are most at risk of GBV during the evening period-Community outreach training for Traditional birth attendants 70, community elders 40, imams 40 and WASH committees 30 on GBV/Child protection core concepts and referral mechanisms-Refresher training for 200 community based focal points including identified new ones on GBV/CP and ethical referral to help in the outreach program.-Protection mainstreaming training for 100 service providers to ensure a coordinated effort for timely and efficient referral of cases. The training will also target new service providers captured in the newly updated service mapping.-Conduct training and capacity building for 50 teachers and community health workers on GBV/CP core concepts, safe and ethical referral-Train 50 local authorities on basic core concepts of GBV and child protection as well as training basic human rights, protection guiding principles, safe and ethical referral.-Train 50 adolescent and youth at risk on Life skills education

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Protection still remains a huge concern in Somalia. SGBV cases is still on the rise according to the GBVIMS managed by the protection cluster 76 per cent of recorded Gender-Based Violence (GBV) incidents were reported by IDPs. WOCCA has established direct relations with clan elders, protection and GBV cluster members and local authorities in targeted regions to gain access, enhance program awareness and monitoring on issues including complaints/feedback about operations. WOCCA closely engages local leaders and government officials to avert any security risk and possible threats. The project will help in training the community members comprised of men, women, girls, boys and even religious leaders on GBV risks, referral pathways, and services provided by WOCCA and other actors in the area. The project will also include vulnerable groups like the IDPs, women and youth in awareness raising to make them feel not let out. The project will have reporting mechanisms like suggestion box and mobile phones where the hotline will be shared with the IDPs to make it easy for them to report cases of SGBV to WOCCA for immediate help. The project will leave behind a community that is well informed and educated reducing the number of people undergoing GBV risks. WOCCA will train community leaders on psychosocial support meaning they can give proper counseling to those in the community with GBV issues even after WOCCA exits showing a long term solution. The community support workers can easily direct survivors for medical referral because they will be trained on referral pathways in the area. The religious leaders are the most respected in the community with the training they will be given on GBV they will continue to guide the community not to do any harm especially when it comes to rape, FGM and child marriages. The religious leaders will also train the community what Koran says on SGBV and the consequences of outdated practice like FGM.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The estimated number of IDPs according to the HRP 2018 stands at 2.1 million due to the armed conflict that has taken decades in Somalia. The children face SGBV risks such as early marriages, rape, FGM, separation from their families and being recruited as child soldiers. Children also get separated from their families while women also face challenges such as being separated from their husbands, their husbands being killed in the armed conflicts and rape.IDPs displaced by the recent floods, drought and ongoing conflict in the targeted regions face serious protection risks particularly women, children and marginalized groups. They are at risk of exclusion from accessing basic humanitarian services at the camps. Some of the factors that may lead to exclusion of vulnerable and marginalized IDPs from accessing services include; poorly defined vulnerability and targeting criteria of beneficiaries by humanitarian actors, discrimination from host communities, lack of access to information on the available services and beneficiary entitlement, IDPs lacking opportunities to voice their opinions and concerns which is often done through camp leaders or community elders and might not accurately reflect their views. The project will therefore work towards mitigating the risks of exclusion.The project will also Support provision of protection services to affected communities, including in hard-to-reach areas and in IDP sites, targeting the most vulnerable, especially those at risk of exclusion. Protection of affected communities and access to protection services will be enhanced through regular protection monitoring and analysis, strengthened referral pathways, more systematic protection mainstreaming, expanded community engagement, capacity-strengthening of humanitarian partners and increased evidence based advocacy with duty bearers. Family Reunification will be done for unaccompanied children through IDTR based on the principle of do no harm.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The IDPs are mostly sidelined by the host community and majority clans in Somalia. IDPs are traumatized people who have undergone loss of land, family members and children and their work due the prolonged armed conflict. IDP families and children face multiple risks of exclusion and marginalization based on gender and age.The project will be training the community leaders on Gender equality, human rights and the risks of gender based violence on women, men, boys and girls. The participants of the training will be comprised of both gender (women and men).Material assistance will be given to all survivors of GBV, it does not matter which age group or gender they are. The Unaccompanied children or displaced children will also benefit from the project in that IDTR will be done for them, their family traced and reunified with their family. The youth and adolescents will be given life skills education to help them cope with peer pressure, overcome drug addiction and get skills of life. The religious leaders, the service providers in the area, the teachers and health workers will all be trained on GBV risks and mitigation measures and referral pathways to enable them help the other members of the community in awareness raising. The members from the minority groups will not be left out also they will receive services just like the members from the majority groups. The project will principally be keen on gender and age inclusion of all groups in service provision.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Somalia has so far lost an estimated 1 million children to armed conflict and violence recruitment. The situation has affected with boys being abducted as young soldiers who face extreme abuse, torture, extreme training and sometimes used as suicide bombers. Girls on the other hand are abducted as soldiers’ wives and laborers, they often undergo SGBV and torture at some points.The project will encourage youth at risk to stay in school and those who are former child solders to attain life skills. Awareness raising and life skills education such as coping with stress will be given to all boys and girls to discourage them from violence and stay in school. IDP households who at risk of SGBV in the evening period or their children are at risk of being abducted or enticed into armed violence will be provided with solar lanterns for lighting and security purposes.Most importantly the school teachers, caregivers, other service providers and religious leaders will be trained on children rights and child protection, psychosocial support and referral pathways to ensure that they are able to quickly identify and refer children at risk, abused and unaccompanied children for further support. WOCCA is also working with DDG to support processes like traditional conflict resolution mechanisms that are more representative and capable of solving civil disputes and aligning with the formal justice sector in South-Central Somalia. This project targets both police, the armies as well as the community at large.
