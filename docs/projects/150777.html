Project Summary

The designed action is in line with the HRP strategic objective 3 and the cluster objective 3 (Capacity to respond to natural or man-made hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner is increased). The 2014 war was devastating in the Gaza Strip; more than five hundreds thousands people were forced to displacement. Tens of thousands found refuge in the public and UNRWA schools. The later were not at all well prepared to receive such huge numbers of IDPS. UNRWA decided to upgrade 50 of its schools in order to make appropriate the refuge for IDPs. OCHA, UNICEF and INGOs concerted and found that it was crucial also to upgrade public schools. 32 schools were selected all over the Gaza Strip. Up-to-date, 18 were funded by ECHO for upgrades and 14 remained in need of upgrading works in shelter and WASH facilities. OCHA coordinated with all key stakeholders the upgrade process and distributed the 14 schools between key actors working in upgrading public schools. PUI will target three public school for upgrading them according to the needs identified in shelter and WASH. There three schools are distributed between Gaza north, Gaza city Middle area.

Needs

During the 2014 conflict, nearly 485,000 people were internally displaced. During the conflict, some of 285,000 IDPs were hosted in 93 UNRWA schools and around 48,000 IDPs in 24 PA schools (excluding informal shelters) However, Gaza lacks well-equipped and safe formal shelters. Based on the after action review of the 2014 Emergency Operations Center and further lessons learned discussions by the Gaza inter-cluster coordination group (ICCG) and all clusters, a number of alternative shelter solutions have been explored. The conclusion was – in coordination with MoEHE and MoSA – that schools are the most feasible option for IDPs in Gaza. 1- Hard component: Identifying and upgrading schools to be used as designated emergency shelters (DES)2- Soft component: Develop sector-specific standard operating procedures (SoPs) & management tools for the operation of DES in a gender sensitive manner, integrating GBV prevention & protection standards and provide training to shelter managers. Engagement with IDP (men and women) in the shelter management and decision-making to ensure equal access to humanitarian assistance, registration and access to information. (Most of the soft component activities are under implementation) The proposed project is to upgrade 14 governmental schools to serve as DES during emergencies; targeting people fleeing their homes due to security concerns or due to a lack of services in their original residence. After the emergency phase, the schools will be converted into Collective Centers if needed. HNO 2018 addresses the needs of IDPs as a priority, including safe shelters affording them enhanced protection during a crisis and meeting their basic humanitarian needs. Led by OCHA/IDP working group in cooperation with UNRWA, MoSD, MoEHE and the relevant clusters, a selection criteria were set and an initial list of 65 schools was presented by MoEHE. Out of this list, 32 schools were selected, based on five additional criteria Location, Capacity, Access, Existing facilities/infrastructure and Security/safety. A multi-sectorial technical taskforce (TTF), including Oxfam, ACF, NRC, PUI, UNICEF, GVC, UNDP and others, in cooperation with the relevant authorities assessed each school in April-May 2016. Key informant interviews with the relevant stakeholders were also held. Recently, in November 2017, Oxfam and ACF have reviewed and updated the cost estimate of the remaining proposed DES shelter.Considering UNRWA shelter standards, the TTF developed two tools: 1. A technical assessment tool to assess the security, capacity and suitability of the nominated schools. 2. SoPs considering all sectors’ needs, were developed in consultation with all clusters, mainstreaming protection, gender and GBV in line with IASC guidelines. This resulted in the inclusion of specific considerations such as assigning separate showers and toilet units, lighting, safe spaces for women & children, cooking spaces. Privacy and culture concerns were considered (for example: pre-fabricated partitions inside shelter rooms). The needs of persons with disabilities were considered through accessibility measures, such as ramps. The proposed upgrading activities are designed to meet the different needs of the IDPs with considering the diversity of ages and gender by affording the basic needs for men and women, young and old displaced people, which including but not limited to the need for toilets block for men, toilet block for women, showers for women, showers for men, sunshade open area for children, adapted toilet and shower for persons with disabilities and ramps for the entrance, partitions between the different families accommodated in the same the classrooms, etc. The assessment analysis by the TTF was reflected in the scope of the work to ensure that all the requirements to enable the schools to be used as DES in an emergency were met. The final scope of work was shared with all clusters & specific agencies for final review.

Activities

The nominated schools were prioritized according to agreed-upon criteria, including but not limited to hosting capacity, budget & geographical distribution. The final outputs (scope of work, budget, design & project time frame) were presented to the ICCG and shared with stakeholders, including PWA, CMWU, MoSA & MoEHE. The total number of beneficiaries is estimated at 22,500 IDPs in the 32 schools with an average of 703 IDPs per school.It’s worth to mention that all the targeted schools’ students and teachers will benefit from some elements of the project inside the schools. This will include signs, access improvements to disabled people, ramps, backup generator, additional stairs, rails for stairs, essential maintenance, fire extinguishers, etc. The total number of students who will benefit from this elements of schools upgrades project are 34,885 students and teachers (19,396 girls and 15,489 boys students) and 825 female and 644 male teachers. Currently 18 schools are upgraded by UNICEF, NRC, Oxfam, ACF, GVC and PUI through ECHO, DIFID and the OCHA Pool fund while KFW has agreed to add the DES shelter components to an ongoing school building project. The partners (UNICEF, Oxfam, NRC, ACF, PUI, GVC and UNDP) are looking for funding to upgrade the 14 schools remaining and to complete outstanding components in the ongoing project. In close coordination among the partners (ACF, Oxfam, UNICEF, PUI, NRC,GVC and UNDP), the proposed intervention will cover WASH, Shelter, food security, and energy components for 14 schools to meet the needs as outlined in the assessment by OCHA/IDP working group, clusters and humanitarian agencies (Handicap international, Islamic Relief, GVC, NRC, Oxfam, ACF and PUI) in cooperation with UN agencies (UNRWA, UNICEF, UN-Women, UNDP and WFP) who provided technical advice to ensure that the design of the DES guarantees the safety of displaced children, boys, girls, women and men. • The WASH components of the intervention are designed to ensure access to clean and safe water, sanitation and solid waste facilities in each shelter which will include but not be limited to (rehabilitation of existing sanitation facilities, establishment of additional sanitation & hygiene facilities (construction of water wells and brackish water desalination plants with a capacity off 10 to 20 m3/day; procuring and deploying standby generators, domestic and drinking water storage tanks, solid waste containers, and laundry facilities). Toilets and showers will be provided in sufficient numbers for both females and males.• The SHELTER components will include maintenance works for the existing buildings, lightning, external ramps, extinguishers, installation of laminated film on windows for safety, and fabricated temporary partitioning to ensure privacy for women and girls.• The FSS intervention will include provision of stoves and protected gas cylinders to allow for preparation of light meals for babies and children.All shelter key equipment and hardware, including backup generators, should be protected by security fencing for children safety and to protect against unauthorized access. Climate conditions summer vs winter will be addressed during the upgrading as much as applicable. PUI will rehabilitate 3 schools (Al Shati Martyrs secondary school, Abdelkareem Al Aklok primary school, and Hamad secondary school) in Gaza for a total of 2,373 IDPs. The implementing partner will supervise the contractor works, liaise with PUI engineer on activities progress and monitor the upgrading process under the supervision of PUI M&E manager. The total cost of the construction and rehabilitation works (32 schools) is estimated at 7.664 USD Millions while the available budget for the achieved and ongoing works in the 18 schools is 3.34 USD Millions (excluding admin/operation cost) and accordingly the partners are seeking to cover the gap in 14 schools with a total construction /rehabilitation cost of 4.324 USD Millions.

Indicators

At the beginning of the project, each organization will develop its monitoring and evaluation plan to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets, in coordination with the ICCG, IDP working group and clusters. This plan will describe the entire M&E system for the project. Moreover, it will include indicators, roles and responsibilities, what forms and tools will be used, and how the data will flow through the organization. In order to follow up on the progress of activities, regular field monitoring visits will be conducted by M&E officers on a weekly basis to follow up on the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. Indeed, Monitoring and Evaluation officer will issue a written report summarizing the main findings after each monitoring visit.Interim and final report including updates on the progress of activities and the achievement levels of different indicators will be submitted by the implementing organization to the donor on agreed dates. Regarding the accountability, implementing organizations will maintain ongoing discussions with all concerned stakeholders, including but not limited to potential beneficiary households, the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), the Palestinian Civil Defense, and Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MoEHE), as well as the relevant water authorities, in order to ensure that the proposed activities are implemented properly taking into consideration the real needs of the affected population. Consultations are also held with the management teams of the designated emergency shelters. Furthermore, each organization will implement its own feedback and complaint mechanism to collect any complaints or suggestions regarding the implemented activities. All implementing partners are considering communication with community elements in the upgrading process. Partners met with the affected people as a result of the recent conflict in different occasions and kept them updated through official meetings with their representative who attended different bilateral meetings or in the IDP WG meetings lead by OCHA. Communication with neighboring communities adjacent to the DES will be consulted and their concerns should be addressed during the design and upgrading of DES. In addition, partner’s agencies received CwC training carried out by BBC Media Action in coordination with OCHA. The training will help implementing agencies to develop/improve communication strategies and tools during the project phase.
