Project Summary

The project will provide lifesaving WASH assistance to IDPs, Returnees and Host community affected by conflict, disasters, malnutrition and disease outbreaks to mitigate WASH related GBV, Nutrition and Health risks. This will apply gender responsive and safe programming approaches including gender risk analysis for activities. The project will adopt community engagement approaches through FGDs, peer to peer talks engaging girls, boys, women, men and teachers on MHM in school, community led hygiene awareness creation campaigns through the CHPs and community leaders. WASH in schools will be promoted through reinforcement of school hygiene clubs.
The WASH needs for disabled persons and other at risk groups shall be incorporated into the project. Light infrastructure interventions have been prioritized that include repair and rehabilitation of water supply and sanitation facilities and provision of WASH NFI kits to create greater impact among the target population. In addition, training of water management committees (with at least 50% female membership) will be promoted to build their capacity to manage and maintain their WASH facilities.
Emphasis will be placed on female headed households, PLWDs, PLW HIV/AIDS and the elderly. The needs of the population in terms of WASH is directly related to the number of IDPs in the community, which always puts a strain on existing services and water sources.
In conflict-affected areas, WASH infrastructure in IDP camps are often overstretched by the new arrivals thus need to be upgraded/maintained due to the protracted situation. These influxes in areas that are already underserved in terms of access to water and sanitation facilities are creating additional strain and health risks whilst emergency response struggles to meet sectoral standards in time.
The project shall foster self-reliance among targeted populations to ensure cost effective and sustainable response across all projects. This is due to the increasing demand for natural resources in densely populated areas, coupled with climate change which continues to challenge sustainability of water resources and their exploitation.
The project shall target 30,000 most vulnerable people that include women, men, girls and boys and persons living with disabilities.
These are communities affected by crisis, food insecurity (high GAM rates) and disease outbreaks, with a strong focus on the most vulnerable members of the community.

1 - Needs

ACD has been working in South Sudan since 2018 delivering humanitarian, development assistance and resilience programming. ACD has a long and strong presence in parts of Greater Equatoria. The South Sudan WASH Cluster Severity Mappings and People in Need figures indicate that less than 30% of South Sudanese population have access to clean water while 7% of the population has access to adequate sanitation — the lowest rate of sanitation access in the world.Severity mapping indicates that locations are severely constrained on WASH/GBV issues, With below average access to WASH services and are severely affected by flooding situation that has led to the destruction of the available WASH infrastructure. The current wind of stability in South Sudan is building trust, causing returnees to return home. The returnees have exerted pressure on the existing WASH facilities where they have settled. Some of the villages have no protected water sources, while the existing water sources are dysfunctional due to lack of maintenance. Similarly, lack of sanitation and hygiene awareness has created rampant open defecation thus the need for safe sanitation practices, hygiene behaviour change and construction of latrines. Female learners face the risks of harassment at schools lacking safe sanitation facilities and while women including girls are exposed to rape and other forms of gender-based violence (GBV) risks while walking long distances to fetch drinking water from protected sources. In some locations, people were displaced from their protected water sources thus need improvement in access to water points for women and girls. There is also the need to improve access to safe sanitation facilities and hygiene behavior change through hygiene promotion and distribution of WASH NFIs. Adolescent girls face risks of being attacked in location which have no safe sanitation facilities while women are exposed to high GBV risks while walking long distances in search of water from unprotected sources such as seasonal rivers and surface ponds. ACD will work to support in coordination with civil authorities in the target areas, rehabilitate existing water sources to improve access to safe drinking water. This is through working with community leaders, women and youth groups to create awareness towards attitude and behaviour change aiming at adaptation of good hygiene and sanitation practices. Formation and training of elected water management committees will ensure proper usage of the water sources, capacity building community resources persons as village pump mechanics will support repairs and sustainability of the protected water sources, while trained Community hygiene promoters and nutrition volunteers will strengthen community awareness towards prevention of common water and sanitation related diseases. High levels of malnutrition have been attributed to poor WASH especially amongst under 5s and PLW. Pastoralist displacement is linked to poor WASH services, creating scenarios of high disease burdens. The situation has further been exacerbated by COVID 19 restrictions, which have reduced humanitarian interface with persons in need. Provision of WASH services will play a big role in mitigating increase in malnutrition, disease, WASH related protection issues as well as displacement.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ACD has a standing policy on PSEA to which all stakeholders under engagement must adhere to. The organization will ensure that information on prevention of SEA to women, boys, men and girls is passed on. This shall be done through FGDs, continuous dialogue and having consultative forums with the different gender group. All messaging in all sectors will have information on PSEA, including banners, posters, T Shirts, IEC materials amongst others. These will be provided in the language well understood by the target populations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Several channels will be in place to collect maximum feedback from the most vulnerable stakeholders including interviews with KIs, focus group discussions, suggestion boxes, establishment of hotlines and PDM exercises. PWDs will be attended to specially, as well as the elderly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will ensure that feedback from the most vulnerable community members is provided through the established CFM mechanism. This shall be carried out through holding continuous meetings with the beneficiaries through door-to-door visits, having coordination meetings with key stakeholders, community leaders and government authority to ensure that all issues raised are amicably addressed. The project will ensure timely and continuous monitoring of project implementation and collect feedback from the beneficiaries through conducting post distribution monitoring and WASH KAP survey. Person to person talk sessions will be conducted through use of the local language so as to collect the views and issues relating to the project from the most vulnerable persons. FGD sessions and dialogue sessions will be conducted with persons living with disabilities, not so literate persons including women and girls to ensure that their views are captured and are addressed. ACD has existing Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRM) that provides for follow-up action on issues reported and are affecting the beneficiaries and thereafter provide the necessary feedback. The CRM approaches will be adopted and used under this project to ensure that all interventions are community centered and encourage community dialogue, consultation and their participation in delivery of WASH activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be periodically collated and documented to assist in program design and adjustments. The project will ensure that girls, boys, women, and men (including older people and those with disabilities) have opinions feed into the feedback systems put in place. This will promote corrective actions towards addressing their specific needs and improve quality of the project as well as address any emerging needs. Regular meetings with community representatives will be held to provide a platform where ACD will regularly inform and update community representatives on the planned, progress and achieved project activities including timelines.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

ACD will ensure that women and girls are considered during gender risk analysis sessions for the various activities to be implemented. This shall be done through FGDs, continuous dialogue and having consultative forums with the target group. The project will ensure that displaced women and girls are not exposed to sexual violence as a result of staying in highly insecure environment. WASH facilities shall be sited in secure and safe locations identified by the target women and girls through gender risk analysis sessions to be carried out. The project will ensure that allocation and distribution of WASH support services will be done through openness and transparency while involving all the key stakeholders during beneficiary identification and selection. This will help promote peaceful coexistence among the target community through equitable allocation of WASH resources. Collaboration with authorities will help reduce risks around bureaucratic impediments.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

At least one of the ACD WASH team will be female in order to ensure women and girls feel more comfortable to come forward with any concerns. GBV risk assessments, conducted through FGDs, will be conducted before all interventions. ACD plans to collaborate closely with a GBV partner to ensure that their expertise is drawn upon to create GBV risk analysis of all activities. ACD will refer to this partner any reports of cases of GBV, in order that they can provide support to survivors. ACD will have a CRM focal point to ensure that beneficiaries can objectively and confidentially report complaints and feedbacks, this focal point will follow-up with the relevant ACD staff or partner in order to provide an answer to the complainants for minor complaints while major concern complaints are directly forwarded to the AC/AME team in Juba.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Prioritization of households facing severe food insecurity will be done during this project. Provision of WASH services will reduce the disease burden caused by poor WASH services, thereby enabling households to be more productive.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

ACD will specifically deliver WASH assistance with the aim to mitigate against WASH-related GBV risks. In doing so, we will specifically ensure that the needs of especially vulnerable women and girls are prioritised and addressed during activities (including persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly) are prioritised and addressed during interventions. Distribution sites will also be chosen taking into consideration any specific protection concerns that might arise during the focus group discussions.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Provision of timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water and sanitation to vulnerable and affected populations and Increase of knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH- related diseases greatly reduces malnutrition amongst under 5s.
