Project Summary

The main goal of this project is to increase access and also scale up lifesaving emergency healthcare services to vulnerable populations in Nyirol County with a specific emphasis and focus on the major causes of morbidity and mortality. This is in line with the Health Cluster Log frame 2020 for South Sudan which emphasizes three objectives i.e. reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, improving access and scaling-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable population and increasing access to services for the survivors of SGBV, disabled and mentally ill.
Therefore, the emergency that this project will address is the high rate of mortality from malaria, AWD, pneumonia, SAM with medical complications and outbreaks of measles, cholera and kala-azar. In the volatile context of Jonglei, the project will target IDPs recently displaced and newly displaced by conflict, and IDPs and host communities in areas with limited access to health services. Health cluster data shows people in need are 20,732 in Nyirol, including 6,476 IDPs integrated into host communities (Health Cluster PIN Oct 19). In Nyirol, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) has persisted due to seasonal flooding which destroyed crops, limited food and income sources among displaced households and disrupted delivery of assistance and market functioning. Additionally, insecurity, cattle raiding and intercommunal conflict resulting in the loss of lives, disrupted livelihoods and restricted access to wild foods and fish (S. Sudan IPC Snapshot September 2019).
In order to achieve its results, the project has prioritized PHCC, PHCU and mobile team clinical packages. The strategies include: Emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic diseases which will involve prepositioning of supplies for emergency response; Outbreak investigations in order to establish a disease causal organism verified by laboratory tests; Case management for selected diseases; Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases; Implementing infection prevention and control measures; Capacity building of health care workers to improve the quality of health service provided; Disease surveillance both active and passive and outbreak response through close monitoring of the EWARN reporting; Deployment of mobile medical teams to reach the population further from the health facilities; and Capacity building of health workers on vaccine management and administration.
In order to improve quality of services and to attract more clients, the project will focus on static integrated facilities with renovations; Staffing and supplies of key medical materials; Management of SAM with medical complications at SCs; Integration of WASH in health facilities; Training health workers on SGBV and provision of CMR services at the health facilities. Further, the project will increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mentally ill through provision of MHPSS and CMR services in health facilities; Capacity building of health personnel to follow the standard treatment protocols; Referral of the survivors to safe houses; Provision of rehabilitation services for disabled within the project sites; and, Surveillance of attacks and reporting on vandalism of PHC infrastructure, theft of equipment, essential medicine and supplies.
It is envisaged that a successful implementation of these activities would significantly result in the overall decrease in morbidity and mortality arising from the epidemic prone diseases and improvement of access and scaling-up of integrated quality essential health services. It will further increase access to services for SGBV survivors, disabled individuals and people with mental health disorders. Besides improving resilience among vulnerable populations, the activities are further expected to benefit from an integrated response involving key sectors such as health, WASH, nutrition, FSL and protection.

1 - Needs

The Health Cluster Log frame 2020 for South Sudan emphasizes three objectives namely 1) reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, 2) improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable population and 3) Increase access to services for the survivors of SGBV, disabled and mentally ill. These objectives indicate the wanting condition of PHC services in South Sudan. Whereas challenges to health care provision in South Sudan are more or less similar, there are specific counties where the services have been compromised and hence bear a higher burden of disease prevalence. Nyirol County falls within this category. Health cluster data shows people in need are 20,732 in Nyirol, including 6,476 IDPs integrated into host communities (Health Cluster PIN Oct 19). The gaps identified by the Health Cluster is corroborated by other reports. For instance, through assessment and experience, CMA has established that the critical health needs in Nyirol are:• Increased access to essential PHC services, as well as scaled up capacity to treat medical complications associated with malnutrition, poor sanitation and SGBV.• Improved access to specialized and integrated services for the vulnerable women, men, girls and boys• Scaling up support to the most vulnerable as well as at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks including promotion of emergency health services and protection for people with disabilities.• Strengthening a prompt and quick response to outbreaks such as to cholera, kala-azar and other highly infectious diseases through mobile approaches. In Nyirol, poor access to healthcare services has been due to multiple factors including, conflicts, and displacement. Additional stress on the PHC system is due to malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine preventable and communicable diseases. Similarly, seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including cholera and measles, and the continued endemicity of malaria pose additional challenges. These disease outbreaks tend to last longer hence weakening already vulnerable people’s ability to cope with multiple shocks. Conflict has caused continued breakdown of health facilities and resulted in interruption of essential services and remains one of the main drivers of morbidity and mortality in this county. Health issues are further being compounded by malnutrition as conflict continues to disrupt the cereal markets. In relation to acute food insecurity, there are indications that the former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. Here food insecurity remained high during the peak of the 2019 lean season despite improvement in overall security conditions. In Nyirol, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) has persisted due to seasonal flooding which destroyed crops, limited food and income sources among displaced households and disrupted delivery of assistance and market functioning. Additionally, insecurity, cattle raiding and intercommunal conflict resulting in the loss of lives, disrupted livelihoods and restricted access to wild foods and fish (S. Sudan IPC Snapshot (August 2019-April 2020 issued 11th September 2019 pg 5). Currently, Nyirol is experiencing unprecedented flooding and it is rated as Heavily Affected, and the fifth worst affected county (WASH Cluster meeting minutes, 25th October 2019). These factors indicate a sharp deterioration which requires intervention. The rise in SGBV has heightened the need for comprehensive care of victims of SGBV. Women, adolescent girls and children are most at risk. This reality coupled with conflict, insecurity and flooding causes severe restrictions to access health services. Special medical outreaches and community-based protection measures are needed to ensure PLW and children have access to services.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

In this project, it is acknowledged that a durable solution is achieved when beneficiaries can enjoy their human rights without discrimination of any kind and when these legal rights are fully recognized and supported in the delivery if this project. In addition to the foregoing, and in as far as this project will be undertaken under the umbrella of UNOCHA, and in support of and in respect of authorizations provided by the GOSS, and as CMA delivers the assistance offered by the donors in respect of these authorizations, then the rights of beneficiaries will be legally recognized under this project. Understanding that achieving durable solutions includes recognition of the human rights of beneficiaries, this project will be implemented in collaboration with government representatives at county level (recognizing that government actors must be supported to take a leadership and coordination role in promoting and facilitating solutions), and with other humanitarian actors (UN Agencies, INGOs, local NGOs) working in Nyirol County. This project reasonably assumes that the inclusion and subsequent participation of many actors is the surest way of ensuring that the beneficiaries' rights are subsequently legally recognized.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

In this project, it will be acknowledged that a durable solution is achieved when IDPs no longer have specific protection needs that are linked to their displacement and such persons can enjoy their human rights and entitlements without discrimination resulting from their displacement. Therefore, working towards durable solutions means diminishing gradually the needs and vulnerabilities of displacement affected communities, while strengthening their capacities, skills, and increasing their resilience, so that displaced persons can increasingly enjoy their rights. It is further noted that the IASC Framework identifies three options to achieve this goal, which are underpinned by the principles of safety, dignity and non-discrimination: right to return of displaced persons to their place of origin or habitual residence; acceptance by host populations to settle and integrate in areas where displaced persons have sought refuge; and the right to seek settlement elsewhere in the country. In recognition of the above, in this project CMA will work very closely with the community leaders, local authorities as well as INGOs and NNGOs whose interventions cover the protection sector and coordinated by the protection cluster. This will help ensure that all the displacement-affected populations will have access to, and adequately benefit from the project. Further, the project will actively seek to engage both men and women of displaced population in this project so they have opportunity to participate in planning, implementation and evaluation of project activities, ensure displaced populations are aware of and have access to the complaints and feedback mechanism, and sufficient protection to enable free access to project services and benefits.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will be focused on health in collaboration with nutrition and WASH service providers as coordinated through the Health, Nutrition and WASH Clusters, respectively, and through the MOH primary health and nutrition care system. In some locations the collaborating partner will be CMA also delivering health and nutrition programming. In other locations, other collaborating humanitarian partners will be delivering the WASH, education, FSL and protection interventions, all of which operate under the locally available government systems. This includes state, county and payam authorities representing both the GOSS administrative system and the various sector ministries. Further, as an active humanitarian actor in Nyirol County, CMA will collaborate with, and participate in the existing forums for state and county coordination involving representatives of both government and humanitarian actors, to ensure effective and timely coordination with all humanitarian actors delivering programs in the targeted locations of this project. Through these links, the project expects to foster durable solutions in the context of health care interventions. As has been the practice of CMA throughout its involvements in South Sudan, at the community level, the project will link with the traditional leaders and authorities, local opinion leaders and women leaders, ensuring links with and inclusion of, displaced and disabled populations. The project will also link with and engage the various structures of committees and representative of both traditional groups, church groups and groups formed through health, nutrition, WASH, education, FSL and protection interventions so as to quickly and thoroughly increase equitable access to integrated preventative and lifesaving emergency health care services for crisis affected IDP and host populations Nyirol County.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

CMA has provided health services in Nyirol County since 1997 and integrated health with FSL and nutrition work. During this period, CMA has always ensured that its project work aligns with all national objectives and all sector protocols as defined through the various sector cluster programming mandates and the sector ministries and department of the GOSS. As in other sectors, with this health project, CMA will ensure that activities and implementation approaches adhere to the technical protocols of the Basic Package of Health and nutrition Services (BPHNS) as prescribed by MoH, and with all guidance provided by the Clusters in relations to social protection, inclusivity, dignity and respect for all groups of target beneficiaries without discrimination and by addressing human rights, and reducing vulnerabilities and risks of the populations served. This project will also work within the framework of a multi-sector integration approach bringing in humanitarian actors delivering nutrition, WASH, FSL, education and protection interventions as promoted by GOSS, and sector ministries as well as the operational agencies of the UN, INGOs, local NGOs working in Nyirol County. Similarly, CMA will use the existing channels to ensure effective and timely coordination with all humanitarian actors delivering programs in the targeted locations of this project. Complementarity will be achieved as the health project will be fully integrated with nutrition and health services and by coordinating and collaborating closely with other humanitarian actors delivering education, protection, WASH and FSL projects/interventions. This approach is expected to deliver strong and durable results through the health project.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

In the target county, protection of vulnerable populations remains an ongoing concern. Currently, there is no conflict between opposing military forces nor any inter-tribal conflict. As such the targeted County demonstrates a safe and stable environment for the delivery of humanitarian services. Challenges occur within communities and ensuring that all populations who need humanitarian services feel free and safe in accessing them. In relation to the Health sector, SGBV is the most critical and common protection concern, especially among IDPs, and children, adolescent girls, women, and disabled people of host and IDP populations. These are the most vulnerable groups of concern. Understanding that a sufficiently safe and stable environment at the community level is essential for sustainable humanitarian impacts, it is also evident that durable solutions that provide a safe environment need to be anchored in the political-will of leadership at all levels of community and government. However, humanitarian actors must also implement measures and act in a do-no-harm approach to ensure all populations have unimpeded access to the health services. In seeking to address long-term safety concerns, this project will implement the following measures:1. work closely with the community leaders, local authorities and NGOs in the protection sector to coordinate appropriate and needed protection measures which affect a seamless access to health care services;2. ensure facility-based safety audits are conducted as per Health Cluster protocols and implement any required changes to approaches and install gender-appropriate modifications during the health project implementation;3. engage affected populations of host and IDP populations including people with disabilities so that they participate in the planning, implementation and evaluation of health activities delivered under this project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

A sustainability strategy is known to improve a community’s resilience, empower beneficiaries with assets and be a springboard for improved quality of life, hence most important in delivering durable solutions. In order to advance sustainability, the project will:1. Prioritize sustainability by establishing the principles and metrics and have them built-in at the design phase of the project e.g. by seeking to appreciate local cultural dynamics, calculating initial levels of capacity, which influences the degree to which a project will deliver long-term intended benefits.2. Empower a properly trained and motivated local workforce by improving long-term mentoring, ongoing practical learning e.g. CMEs and livelihood considerations as well as consistent training.3. Facilitate more integrated partnerships, as they produce longer-lasting impacts and noting that national and local government agencies are critical stakeholders facilitating, enabling, and maintaining health projects. Involving local governments removes obstacles to health care implementation and lends legitimacy to health projects. CMA will work with the MoH to catapult issues onto the national agenda and enable a credible transition plan to local structures by establishing a system of supervision and support for health facilities that, over time, becomes ingrained in the community and continues once the externally funded project ends4. Consider the broader health ecosystem and establish partnerships aimed at the inclusion of all the stakeholders working across different sectors so as to address some of the barriers to health access. 5. With the understanding that a sustainable health system is one that keeps people out of hospitals and supports them to maintain good physical and mental health in their community, this project will strengthen prevention, promotion and public health activities in collaboration with the community leaders and the various stakeholders.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

In this project, CMA will provide mechanisms through which affected populations i.e. most vulnerable community members e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls, can receive the information they need to make informed decisions on accessing health services, provide feedback on the health interventions, share concerns and submit complaints. CMA will work closely with all stakeholders e.g. local authorities, community leaders, beneficiaries etc. to ensure their inclusion in program decision-making.These structures will enable affected populations to participate in planning, implementing and monitoring the delivery of nutrition services. CMA’s field teams will undertake FGDs and KIIs as part of the planning and monitoring process, conduct monthly meetings with host community and IDP leaders and leaders of payam and boma health committees to report on health programming and obtain feedback, and conduct discharge exit interviews with a sample of the beneficiaries to obtain feedback from those who receive health services. Through these regular consultations, the key stakeholders will have the opportunity to effectively participate in planning and monitoring and learn their entitlements for accessing health services and the processes for implementing the full complaints mechanism. Therefore, all nutrition sites will have a feedback and complaint mechanism established as a pillar of project approach to the AAP with an in-built mechanism that the project will consult with users (namely most vulnerable community members e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) on an individual, KIIs and FGD basis as well as through community meetings to establish a viable, transparent and effective mechanism for delivering feedback and complaints and to seek input to finding resolution to complaints.
