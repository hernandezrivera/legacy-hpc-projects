Project Summary


The UNICEF child protection project will focus on strengthening access to quality preventive and responsive child protection services that address the needs, rights and dignity of children and their caregivers in the conflict affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY). The emergency response will prioritize the most vulnerable including at-risk children and their families. UNICEF’s efforts to address child protection and gender-based violence in emergencies is part of its over-all strategy on prevention and response to all forms of violence against children (VAC).
Results will be achieved by providing integrated child protection case management services for children including family tracing and reunification for UASC and delivering quality and sustainable socio-economic reintegration services to children formerly associated with armed groups as well as other vulnerable children and their families. In addition, conflict-affected children will have access to community-based mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) including structured recreational activities and adolescents’ life skills education. The project will also contribute to strengthening capacity of service delivery to GBV survivors. Child and adult survivors of conflict-related sexual violence and GBV will in addition have access to comprehensive minimum lifesaving services including medical, psychosocial, legal and safety. Children and caregivers living in or returning to locations contaminated by unexploded remnant of war (UXO) will be provided with explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) in schools, child friendly spaces, transit and displace camps with the support of the Mine Action sub sector. UNICEF response will target almost 53 percent of the overall child protection sub sector target for 2023. The geographic locations will be prioritized according to the severity of need and access.
In addition, the project actions will further focus on child protection system strengthening through the roll out of the child protection law in the BAY states, registering under-5 child births, and enhancing the capacity of a professional social service workforce to improve quality service delivery. The UNICEF child protection response will be integrated with other sectors, such as education, WASH, health and nutrition, for increased and comprehensive delivery of services to conflict affected children and caregivers.
Using a nexus-approach, this project will also support the efforts of peaceful coexistence, transitional justice, reinforcing existing local and national capacities including a focus on climate change mitigation where possible.
Grave violations against children affected by conflict will be monitored and reported through the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM 1612). Data derived from the MRM will be used for high-level advocacy and to inform programming. The signed Handover Protocol (HoP) between the Government of Nigeria and UNICEF will be used to prevent or reduce the detention of children encountered by military and security forces during armed conflict, by identifying and transferring children allegedly associated with armed groups within a limited period to the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MWASD).
Finally, UNICEF will use the platform of the Group of Friends (GoF) on children and armed conflict (CAAC) to share information on the impact of conflict on children, raise awareness, support fundraising initiatives, and undertake joint strategic advocacy actions to prevent and end grave violations against children in Nigeria.

1 - Needs

 The armed conflict in north-east Nigeria can be characterised as a protection crisis. Armed groups and security forces both continue to commit crimes under international humanitarian law and international human rights law and have a severe impact on the protection of children. In 2021, the United Nations verified 250 grave violations against 208 children (92 girls) in north-east Nigeria. Most violations of recruitment and use by Boko Haram affiliated or splinter groups, the Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP). The insurgency in Northeast of Nigeria took an unprecedented turn in July 2021 leading to large number of individuals, including fighters, exiting armed groups and presenting themselves to security authorities, largely in Borno State. According to the Borno State Government, over 82,237 fighters and their families have surrendered since July 2021, out of which 16,577 were active male fighters, 24,499 women and 41,161 children (https://www.nannews.ng/2022/11/30/x-raying-handling-of-surrendered-terrorists-rescued-women-and-children-in-borno/ - 30 November 2022). As of 03 December 2022, a total of 17,973 individuals (70% women and children) are living in three transit camps (Hajj, Shokari and Bulumkutu) in Maiduguri. Sustaining this momentum of exits presents challenges to effective process of handling and treatment of the individuals led by government authorities and ensuring their reintegration into communities amidst an ongoing conflict, an internal displacement situation and significant intergenerational grievances stemming from the longstanding conflict. Forced displacement and returns impose high risk on separated and unaccompanied children. Boys and adolescent male risk forcible recruitment by armed groups, or suspicion of association with armed groups by authorities. Civilians continue to suffer death and injury from explosive ordnance, including the use of explosive weapons in populated areas. Protection needs are formidable with women and girls under the threat of violence, abduction and rape. Gender-based violence (GBV) including sexual violence and forced and child marriages continue to be reported and are attributed to the conflict, insecurity and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. In 2021, about 2,500 cases of GBV survivors were supported through case management in the north-east. In 2021, the GBV Area of Responsibility reported that 98 per cent (4,300 cases of the 4,388 total) of the reported incidents of GBV in BAY states were perpetrated against female survivors and two per cent against males. The risk of SGBV in conflict contexts including north-east Nigeria, is exacerbated by several factors. These include the breakdown of family and community support systems; the presence of armed forces or combatants in displaced settlements; weak legal systems and law enforcement; abuse of power by individuals in positions of authority; laws and practices that reinforce gender discrimination; inadequate access to shelter, food, water and fuel; and inadequate access to livelihood opportunities. Two per cent (88 out of the 4,388 cases) of reported and documented incidents of GBV were perpetrated against survivors with a disability. With over 5,000 children, including UASC, waiting in transit camps to return to their communities, their effective reintegration is hindered by the impact of armed conflict on societies, such as the absence of livelihood opportunities, weak economies and markets, and a breakdown of social infrastructure, including schools and health facilities. The recent wave of children, youth and women exiting from armed groups lack the support needed to reintegrate due to the highly fragile and unstable security and political environment, weakened state institutions, law and order, impunity and insufficient resources. Women and girls with disabilities are at equal or greater risk of GBV compared to their peers without disabilities,

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

UNICEF and its partner will collaborate with other agencies, communities, and stakeholders to develop a more holistic environment of protection for vulnerable groups, displaced populations, and children at risk. The proposed Programme is focused on mainstreaming protection. UNICEF will deploy the principle of “Do no harm” and employ appropriate measures to avoid exposing people to further harm as a result of project actions and ensure that the environment and way in which assistance is provided do not expose people to further hazards, violence or human rights abuses or violations. UNICEF/partner will manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized, UNICEF/partner will ensure that the affected population have access to humanitarian assistance against any form of direct or indirect discrimination. UNICEF will engage with the target population at all stages, and the participation of all in the design and targeting of interventions, in particular vulnerable and marginalized groups, women, girls, boys, men, people with disabilities, old age, women headed HH, child headed HH and pregnant, lactating and mothers married girl children. All implementing partner staff, will undergo gender-based violence (GBV) and protection from sexual exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training before they begin working in the program. This is to both help prevent GBV and PSEA but also to spot potential warning signs of gender exploitation and abuse in targeted communities. UNICEF and its partners will establish safe and accountable GBV reporting and feedback mechanism in their targeted communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNICEF has established several safe and accessible community-based reporting mechanisms in targeted communities to report against PSEA and other violations. UNICEF also uses its short text messages (sms) based U-Report tool to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback or complaints. The U-Report 24x7 Hub receives complaints, reports, and feedback from communities, analyzes the data and shares with relevant sections in UNICEF and its partners for action. As reports and complaints regarding other services handled by different agencies come in, they are shared with the Sector to act. Each case is followed up to provide feedback to the community members who laid complaints.
