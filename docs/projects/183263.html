Project Summary

Globally, undernutrition is estimated to cause 45 per cent of all deaths in children annually1. A child experiencing wasting or stunting is at heightened risk of dying; a child who is both wasted and stunted has a 12-time higher risk of dying from common childhood infections2. Limited data are available on the nutritional status of the vulnerable groups in Lebanon. In 2004, stunting was at 16.5 per cent, while wasting at 6.6 per cent for Lebanese children under five years of age. In 2009, exclusive breastfeeding for infants below six months was 14.8 per cent, one of the lowest in the world. Over 72 per cent of children were no longer breastfed by the time they reach their first birthday. Complementary feeding practices were extremely poor, with only 41.6 per cent of infants infants introduced to complementary foods at six months. Adolescent girls and women in particular could also more vulnerable to malnutrition. Women with both a low body mass index and short stature are highly prevalent.
SC will encourage and support an integrated approach across our sectors, to address the many underlying causes of malnutrition so to increase access to safe and nutritious food, WASH, child protection, education. In the crisis settings, we will focus on the prevention, detection, and referral of acute malnutrition and protecting, promoting, and supporting IYCF practices among the most vulnerable populations.
We will provide support to mothers and caregivers to navigate difficult conditions in a crisis setting so they can feed and care for their youngest children safely. This is delivered through different actions at the household and community level.
Additionally, our staff will help detect cases needing specialized support using the family MUAC approach. We will train frontline staff across the different sectors to support women and their children while promoting recommended IYCF practices. We will also train volunteers and community workers to conduct support and education sessions for families with young children on IYCF topics.
Targeting
The Project is targeting pregnant women, adolescent girls, children <2 years of age in the family and their caregivers. As such, only families with the target populations will be eligible to receive the support. Project will target families with eligible populations among the most vulnerable. Household with children <5 years of age and pregnant women that are found suffering from any form of malnutrition including anemia will be referred for treatment, supported with IYCF and maternal nutrition counselling and linked with the FSL programme. The project will provide cash support to the HHs found with pregnant women and children <5 years suffering from malnutrition with the support of the Nutrition interventions.
Specific vulnerabilities including children or caregivers with disabilities, female-headed households, and other characteristics will be included and scored in the selection tool. Outreach to these specifically vulnerable families is prioritized to ensure programming reaches the most underserved, and this is supported by SC’s Gender & disability Specialist.

Mother Support Groups
The healthcare system aims to provide preventative and curative services that will, in a comprehensive and concerted way, prevent, reduce, and treat malnutrition. SC believes that successful Nutrition efforts require full engagement and active participation of community members and the key influencers in the family. There is a need to step up community participation focusing on the most vulnerable communities, increase support for women participation and those with disabilities, and invest in initiatives that will contribute to effective behavioral change.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

At the start of all projects SC undertakes a risk assessment, including a focus on ensuring the protection and safeguarding of children and adults. All staff are trained on PSEA and safeguarding and sign up to SC’s code of conduct & safeguarding policies, with regular refreshers on reporting procedures. SC is committed to mainstreaming diversity and inclusion across all its projects and programs. Gender Equality Markers are completed for all project and use of GBV Minimum Actions at the design stage with a close monitoring and on-the-job coaching at the field level. SC works towards creating gender-sensitive to transformative and inclusive/accessible programming with strong focus on women, girls, and diverse groups' empowerment. SCI's approach includes conducting regular gender and disability analysis, framing the analysis recommendations and outcomes into practical action planning, setting gender equality and social inclusion indicators in the MEAL framework.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability to affected populations is central to SC’s work and ensured through feedback and reporting mechanisms, regular engagement and communication with stakeholders including children. Focus group discussions are held with adults and children to ensure full understanding of the project, as well as to hear any feedback. In particular for this project, communications to cash recipients will ensure clarity in the purpose and application of the cash assistance. Families should be made aware of GRM faculties and field teams will always distribute hotline number cards during cash distributions. A Q&A document will be circulated with the field staff (across sectors) and the call center staff to support them in answering frequently asked questions by the community that are related to our cash programs. The Q&A should be continuously updated to reflect any trends in the field, or any recurrent questions received by the staff that are not included in the document.
