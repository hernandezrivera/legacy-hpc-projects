Project Summary

This project aims to maintain and enhance access to essential healthcare services, focusing on non-communicable diseases (NCD), including mental health (MH), with emphasis on gender-based violence. Project activities will include technical support through adopting the HEART approach for the management of cardiovascular diseases (CVD), and updating and revising the national gender-based violence guidelines. Interventions will also contribute to building the capacity of 900 healthcare workers, out of which 440 are women, on the management of different NCDs, including training on mhgap intervention guideline, with focus on child and adolescent MH and GBV. To maintain service provision for vulnerable NCD patients, WHO will procure essential supplies needed for NCD management to cover the MoH needs for 4 months.
An estimated cumulative of 48,400 patients, including 23,716 females women and 2,420 people with disability, will benefit from conducted activities.
All project activities have been coordinated at various levels with all relevant partners, counterparts, and Health Cluster working groups.

1 - Needs

According to the Health Cluster, around 1.6 million Palestinians, including 778K females and 79K people with disability (PWD), across the oPt were identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2023- two-thirds of them are in the Gaza Strip. Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are the leading causes of death worldwide, accounting for 65% of the total Gazan deaths, in 2019. NCD deaths are caused by four main disease groups- cardiovascular diseases (CVD), cancers, chronic respiratory diseases, and diabetes mellitus. The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that 93K Gazans require assistance for NCDs, including 45K women and girls and 4,650 elderly. NCDs also cause substantial morbidity and disability, placing an increasing burden on individuals, families, society, and the health system, as a result of high healthcare costs, loss of productivity, and societal stress. However, recent acute emergencies, including the COVID-19 pandemic and recurring military escalations, have required prioritization of emergency interventions, sometimes even at the expense of the management of chronic diseases. The lack of medical supplies, including diagnostics and therapeutics, and referral restrictions, are main reasons for delays and interruption in the treatment of chronic diseases. Untreated NCDs can lead to serious complications, such as renal failure, disability, and amputations. The Gaza Central Drug Store (CDS) reported that, at the end of September 2022, 86 essential primary healthcare (PHC) medicines out of the total 132 essential medicines list (65%) were at less than one month’s supply. In addition, 70 items (36%) of the essential 194 consumables needed for cardiac catheterization and open-heart surgery were also at zero stock levels. To address these needs, WHO has been implementing evidence-based interventions to reduce and prevent NCDs including the Package of Essential Non-communicable Disease Interventions (PEN), in all districts of the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. WHO mainly focused on strengthening early detection of NCDs through introducing and strengthening screening programs, improving patients’ registration, and building the capacity of staff in NCDs surveillance and health promotion in around 370 health centers. Building on previous work, the project aims to implement the HEARTS technical package, which provides a strategic approach to improving cardiovascular health and would support the Palestinian MoH to strengthen CVD management in primary care. Strengthening the health information system for adequate NCD surveillance; and maintaining the provision of essential drugs, equipment and supplies are also vital to bridge the gap in needs. Mental health and psychosocial (MHPSS) issues also constitute one of the largest, least acknowledged health problems in the oPt. According to the 2022 World Mental Health Report, COVID-19 has sparked and amplified serious mental health problems. A great number of people have reported psychological distress and symptoms of depression, anxiety or post-traumatic stress, with worrying signs of more widespread suicidal thoughts and behaviors, including among health care workers. Although the precise extent of mental disorders in the oPt is not known, the findings of a study published in 2017 indicate that the oPt had the largest burden of mental disorders within the Eastern Mediterranean Region. According to the 2023 HNO, the Health Cluster identified an increase in numbers of people needing MHPSS support to 359,877 people, including more than 176K women and girls, and 18K PWD. The protection cluster has also identified an increased need for MHPSS interventions. Of particular concern is the situation in Gaza, where, since the start of the pandemic, there have been 44 attempted suicides involving children, compared to 8 in all of 2019. An estimate of 198,000 children and over 242,000 adults in Gaza, including caregivers, are also in need of various MHPSS services. Additionally, insecurity related to employment, housing and income, as well as the increase in violence, adversely affect mental health and well-being for Palestinians in the West Bank (including East Jerusalem), rendering 139,227 people in need of humanitarian assistance related to MH. The results of the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) demonstrated that 20-38% of surveyed households have at least one member showing signs of psychological distress during the 30 days before the data was collected in July 2021. Furthermore, COVID-19 and the May 2021 escalation in conflict resulted in an increase in reported GBV cases. In 2020, in the context of increased financial pressures, unpaid household work and restricted movements outside of households during COVID-19, providers of helplines for survivors of gender-based violence reported a 70% increase in calls. Additionally, SAWA, an emergency helpline, reported a 135 per cent increase in caseload in May 2021 compared with May 2020. Women and girls in communities impacted by conflict and displacement, i.e. Gaza, Area C and East Jerusalem, tend to suffer from higher incidence of GBV, including more frequent incidents of intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and exploitation. Women and girls with disabilities face additional barriers that limit their access to services, putting them at additional risk. In Gaza, 58 per cent of persons with disabilities lack information about GBV prevention and response, including how and where to access relevant assistance and services during emergencies.

2 - Activities

This project is fully aligned with the needs and priority interventions identified in the Health Cluster HNO and HRP for 2023. Through its planned activities, which are in line with the Strategic Health Cluster Objectives 2, WHO will contribute to enhancing access to essential health services, by improving NCD service provision in Gaza, and enhancing mental health and GBV services across the oPt, with specific emphasis on gender-based violence. Activities will aim to minimize the equity gap in service availability and accessibility and to achieve an equitable access to essential services for the vulnerable population, including females, elderly, and people with disability, across the different age groups. Capacity building activities will also provide equal, and non-discriminatory opportunities for participants. Through its different interventions, WHO will promote protection (confidentiality, dignity, respect,..) and accountability to the affected population. Proposed interventions include: 1) Provide essential medical supplies (medicines, disposables, blood components, etc.), to cover the needs of Gaza patients with diabetes mellitus (DM), cardiovascular (CVD) and chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases (COPD), for a period of 5 months. 2) Enhance the health information system at PHC level to strengthen NCD surveillance in Gaza, including through the provision of needed equipment and supplies. 3) Expand the integration of the HEARTS approach at Gaza PHC centers 4) Conduct capacity building activities for 500 healthcare workers, in Gaza, on the management of DM, and CVD (including HEARTS approach) 5) Provide and strengthen preventive activities in PHC and raise community awareness to combat NCDs, in the Gaza Strip 6) Build the capacity of 400 MOH and UNRWA health workers (200 in Gaza and 200 in WB) on the mhgap intervention guidelines, with a specific emphasis on child and adolescent MH and GBV. 7) Procure 5 essential psychotropics for MOH, to benefit 3,000 patients in Gaza and another 3,000 in the WB 8) Carry out awareness raising campaigns to decrease stigma related to mental health and mental disorders in the community, targeting 400 people (200 Gaza and 200 WB) 9) Revise the National GBV protocol 10) Conduct orientation and awareness raising workshops on GBV, for 40 health managers in WB (20) and Gaza (20). 11) Conduct training for 200 emergency healthcare workers on the GBV protocol

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

WHO is committed to ensuring a people-centred humanitarian response that is accountable to the affected population. For this project, the affected people will participate in the following ways:- Lists of procurement of supplies and equipment will be drafted and shared with the Health Cluster and endorsed by the MoH, to ensure that there is no duplication and procurement responds to the most acute needs of the health system.- Where possible, WHO will continue to conduct regular visits/meetings to collect feedback on the quality and availability of services from patients and health staff. WHO will consider the feedback and identify and implement remedial actions to improve response, in consultation with affected people and counterparts. This also includes any SEAH complaints. WHO will continue to advocate through the Health cluster for strengthening the feedback mechanism for the entire response. - For advocacy, feedback will be used to strengthen processes and activities, and complaints will be fully investigated, with the involvement of impartial bodies where required. All complaints related to serious ethical concerns will be raised through WHO’s ethics mechanisms.- WHO will seek feedback from the affected people on the materials produced, through risk communication and community engagement (RCCE). Affected people are also part of the RCCE task force and will participate in the design and implementation stage. Feedback will be used to modify the materials.- Feedback on training topics, content, and modalities will be collected from healthcare workers and will be used to contextualise the training and adapt it to address the need, taking into account the health system's capacity.- Feedback from end beneficiaries will be used to guide response activities and inform decision-making, including modification of policies, where needed and appropriate.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The centrality of protection is at the heart of WHO emergency response as mandated by the IASC. WHO works to ensure the prevention of harm to all persons it engages with, including through upholding due processes of consent, the protection of confidentiality and the proper collection, communication, storage, dissemination and disposal of information on affected persons. Additionally, activities supported by WHO will contribute to ensuring that beneficiaries are not exposed to further contamination risks through adherence to IPC. WHO will also ensure that all interventions are context-appropriate and culturally sensitive. Affected people will be consulted on this where and when appropriate. The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse has also been integrated across the WHO-supported projects through mandatory training of all staff on PSEA and seeking feedback from the affected people, which will be acted upon. WHO’s response team ensures that PSEA is integrated into the response from an early stage, to build the capacity of national PSEA focal points, and to ensure continuity of activities on the ground despite access constraints. Based on lessons learned from the independent report of the abuse perpetrated in the DRC, WHO oPt will organize a training session for staff, and has rolled out the inter-agency Code of Conduct in alignment with the national, and sub-national PSEA network and encouraged all staff and personnel to sign. WHO willparticipate in the GBV sub-cluster as well as the national and sub-national PSEA networks through a designated focal point. Currently, plans are under way to roll out more trainings, work on prevention, and to strengthen inter-agency feedback and reporting mechanisms, as well as case management and response services through linkages with GBV actors. In harmonization with the SOPs and ground work laid by the national and sub-national PSEA network to identify and mitigate PSEA risks, WHO is planning an agency specific risk assessment that is complimentary and identifies the specific risks associated with WHO’s scope of work, and guides a plan that is fully integrated within the IASC PSEA strategy and plan for oPt. To ensure safety of medical care and mitigate the risks of medical errors, WHO interventions will focus on boosting the capacity of health staff to early-detect and adequately manage cases of NCD, including MH and GBV. Interventions will also strengthen the national health information system, including NCD surveillance, to enable secure and safe collection, storage and sharing of medical information and records between the different levels of care, which is critical to minimize medical errors. Moreover, proposed activities will benefit from the WHO patient safety project, which promotes and strengthens the detection, assessment, understanding and prevention of adverse effects or any other drug-related problem, including through promoting collaborative working, and engaging patients and families as partners in safe care. Through its different interventions, WHO will promote protection (confidentiality, dignity, respect...) and accountability to the affected population. WHO will work on raising the awareness of the affected population on their rights when benefiting from services related to GBV, MH and NCD, including their right to keeping the confidentiality of their information and medical records, and not to use any of their data without prior informed consent. Mechanisms for feedback (including a hotline, email address, surveys, focus groups, and a complaint box) will be communicated to both suppliers and beneficiaries to empower beneficiaries to claim their rights. Capacity building activities will also provide equal, and non-discriminatory opportunities for participants.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project activities will clearly focus on the provision of life-saving health interventions to the most vulnerable, including women, the elderly and people with disabilities (PWD) based on the latest available impartial assessments. PWD will be considered as one of the main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project. All indicators will be gender-age disaggregated where possible. WHO will continue to work to strengthen the empowerment of affected persons, including through the provision of platforms to promote patient voices as well as capacity building with health service providers to promote participatory mechanisms within health services design and delivery. WHO also works with the Ministry of Health and partners to strengthen monitoring and analysis of equity and barriers to health access, focused on vulnerable groups and protected characteristics.
