Project Summary

This project responds to the acute health needs of populations experiencing severe food insecurity categorized under IPC4 and severities level 5 located in Canal Pigi, Akobo, Morobo, Kapoeta North, Jur River, and Mundri East counties in South Sudan.
According to an FSNMS, October 2020 report1, these populations have faced persistent crises overtime, attributed to multiple shocks; subnational violence, flash floods, Macroeconomic factors, and COVID 19 restrictions.
Health Action Africa (HAA) has designed this project to complement the already ongoing HAA Health in emergency projects, supported by the SSHF, RRF/IoM, UNFPA, NMCP-MoH, and UNICEF in these Counties to continue mobilizing resources to meet these identified needs in 2021.
County Context Problem Statement:
The December 2020 Integrated Food Security Phase classification placed Pigi Canal County in Jonglei State under IPC4, and in January 2021, the (HNO)2 reported by OCHA similarly indicated that 103,82 people need humanitarian assistance in this County. The extreme needs are related persistent food insecurity and limited access to optimal health care services WASH related morbidities and Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV). The (IRNA Jan 2021 Pigi Canal4) indicates, more children were acutely malnourished in addition to the already existing high GAM rates marked at 24.7% in the SMART Survey conducted by IMC
Similarly, the Nov 2020 IRNA assessment on Akobo County conducted by CARE and partners confirms that, persistent trend of complex emergencies; inter-Communal violence, long history of armed conflicts, and flash floods. Deterioration of WASH infrastructure and access constraints to health care amidst reported protection and SGBV threats increased morbidity.
Kapoeta North and Panyikang Counties encountered related shocks, floods, and weak social infrastructure including dysfunctional health facilities. More than 75% of the Health infrastructure is dysfunctional (situation report Jan 2021 OCHA)5
The escalating armed conflicts in Morobo directly impacted access constraints to health care services (VAD , 2020).
In Both Jur River and Mundri East Counties, prolonged armed conflicts, chronic vulnerabilities, escalated extreme needs, especially essential health services. Morbidity and mortality are high because most health facilities are either damaged, destroyed, or looted (IRNA Sept 2020)6 Mundri County.
Proposed Health Intervention Model:
HAA will use both Mobile and static response emergence packages guided by the Health cluster and Basic Package for Health and Nutrition (BPH&N) .Detail breakdown of the beneficiaries is in the log frame.
Mobile services includes; Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response, Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and Vitamin A supplementation, mobile OPD Consultation and treatment for common ailments; malaria, diarrhea, AWD, ARIs, and other epidemic-prone diseases, basic management of SGBV survivors (PEP, STI treatment, wound care) and Clinical Management of rape (CMR), ante- and postnatal care services including check-ups, vaccination against Tetanus for pregnant women , IP&C , risk communication for COVID 19 and EVD prevention.
In the static facilities; (PHCUs/PHCCs and County Hospitals; routine OPD consultations and treatment of the identified cases, expanded management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC), Maternal & newborn health; clean and safe normal delivery, manual vacuum aspiration (MVA), post-abortion care (PAC), PMTCT and CMR services, routine Immunization services. Support CHD in COVID 19 Infection Prevention & Control (IP&C) and other diseases of Public Health concerns; cholera, measles, EVD, AFP, support CHDS in county level planning and coordination.
The project therefore response to the overall health cluster sectoral objectives; 1, 2 &3

1 - Needs

The main survey provides outcome indicators for the IPC analysis (FSNMS, October 2020)1. Additional evidence on nutrition indicates that counties categorized in IPC 4&5 were significantly affected by sub-national and localized violence and flooding, which destroyed Key infrastructure, including health facilities, basic Infrastructure & livelihoods. There is limited access to optimal health care services; for example, the immunization coverage for measles and vitamin A supplements are at around 40%, indicating poor health services in general. More than half of the health and nutrition centers have been destroyed or looted either during the conflicts or floods. The Dec 2020 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification placed Pigi Canal County in Jonglei State under IPC4. In Jan 2021, the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)2 reported by OCHA similarly indicated that 103,82 people are the populations in need of humanitarian assistance in this County. 35% of this Population, translating to 36,340 populations, faces extreme conditions related to persistent food insecurity, and inaccessible healthcare, lack of WASH services, and above all high protection threats especially Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV)The increasing needs are mostly driven by rising food insecurity compounded by severe shocks according to the 2020 December IPC Report3 persistent floods that deplete capital assets and livelihood, weakens people’s health and nutritional status, thus exposing them to more common morbidities such as malaria, acute watery diarrhea, respiratory tract infections, and other diseases. More children were acutely malnourished in addition to the already existing high GAM rates marked at 24.7% in the SMART Survey conducted by IMC 2019. The IRNA Jan 2021 Pigi Canal4 report indicates that pregnant and lactating mothers' nutritional status has drastically deteriorated. There is a high hunger scale score, 92% of the assessed households’ records encountered animal mortality, no milk source, and barely any harvest since the floods have submerged croplands. These Populations depend on wild gatherings and fish.Similarly, the Nov 2020 IRNA on Akobo county confirms that: the persistent trend of complex emergencies, attributed to multi-hazards such as; inter-Communal violence, long history of armed conflict, and flash floods caused a general break down of the social and economic infrastructure. Common causes of increased morbidity are directly attributed to the deterioration of WASH infrastructure, inaccessible health care and SGBV threats. Kapoeta North and Panyikang Counties encountered related shocks, floods, and weak social infrastructure. Health facilities experience frequent medical supply stock-outs, lack of Human Resource for Health. According to the South Sudan response scale up for highly food insecure areas, more than 75% of the Health infrastructure is dysfunctional (situation report Jan 2021 OCHA5).Amidst the escalating armed conflicts in Morobo, Populations that moved to safe places still experience floods, displacement, and localized social pressure due to the increasing voluntary return population from DRC Congo and Uganda(VAD , 2020). In Both Jur River and Mundri East Counties, the prolonged armed conflict, chronic vulnerabilities, and weak essential services have escalated the need for humanitarian assistance, especially essential health services.. Morbidity and mortality are high because most health facilities are either damaged, destroyed, or looted (IRNA Sept 2020)6 Mundri County. This project thus targets to addressed these identified health needs by using both Mobile and Static facilities of interventions in aligned to cluster sectoral objectives; 1, 2 & 3 to provide access and utilization of health services, reduce excess morbidity and mortality , protection threats /Sexual Gender based violence(SGBV) and mental health

2 - Project activities

HAA will provide health care services through mobile and static facilities in Canal Pigi, Akobo, Morobo, Kapoeta North, Jur River, and Mundri East Counties IPC4&5 in Payams, where HAA dispenses emergency health services to continue the humanitarian assistance in the hard to reach areas currently supported through SSHF, RRF/IoM, UNICEF, NMCP-MoH, and WHO in 2012 Mobile response services; Guided by the the Basic package for Health & Nutrition and the Health cluster activity guidelines, the acitivities will include; Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response. In all mobile centers and outreaches, there will be Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and Vitamin A supplementation. HAA will conduct mobile OPD Consultation and treatment for common ailments; malaria, diarrhea, AWD, ARIs, and other epidemic-prone diseases, basic management of SGBV survivors (PEP, STI treatment, wound care) and Clinical Management of rape (CMR), ante- and postnatal care services including check-ups, vaccination against Tetanus for pregnant women and distribute folic acid/ferrous phosphates, Sulfadoxine Pyrimethamine pregnant mothers as well as LLITN. At community level, HAA will conduct social mobilization for health to facilitate local engagement for environmental hygiene and sanitation services Community Health Workers (CHWs) and Home Health Promoters (HHPs) will undergo refresher training to enhance their skills on health education awareness, counseling, promotion of immunization, nutrition, and prevention of diseases, good personal hygiene.In the static facilities (Primary health care centers (PHCUs/PHCCs and County Hospitals), HAA will use the standard case definition guides and case management protocol for prevention & treatment in Primary Health Care Centers and Hospitals (MoH)- South Sudan guideline to manage common morbidities during the project period. HAA will conduct OPD consultations and treatment of the identified cases, Expanded Management of SGBV survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, prevention care for tetanus, Hepatitis E, and mental health care. Specific reproductive health services in the static facilities will include Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC), Maternal & newborn health; Clean and safe normal delivery, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC), manual vacuum aspiration (MVA), post-abortion care (PAC), PMTCT and CMR services. HAA will also dispense inpatient care, minor surgery and observation ward, injury care, and mass casualty management services. HAA will coordinate and support COVID 19 Infection Prevention & Control (IP&C) and other diseases of Public Health concerns (cholera, measles, EVD, AFP). collaborate with the County Health Department and State level working groups such as the EPI working groups, EVD & COVID 19 task force to detect and control potential outbreaks of diseases of Public Health concern through Country-level Coordination, Planning and Monitoring, Risk Communication and Community Engagement, Surveillance, Rapid Response Teams, and effective case definition of suspected, line-listed, reported and verified cases through active surveillance and report at both community and facility level using the EWARS and IDSR Tools/sentinel sites. There will be inter-sectorial coordination between nutrition, protection, and WASH partners using state and County level platforms and referrals to complement synergies in the respective locations. The project, guided by the SOP, will establish effective COVID-19 triages, conduct screening ,referrals, establish hand washing stations, acquire PPEs for the health care staff, and support risk monitoring for community communication engagement (RCCE) activities. Field Supportive supervision: regular field visits by senior technical, medical cadres to conduct supportive management in the project areas, on-the-job training, and mentorship programs to field staff

3 - Project indicators

Monitoring: The work plans will align with the health cluster's sectoral log frame to ensure health programming consistency during humanitarian response. Therefore the below project indicators are directly adopted from the Health cluster sectoral plan; • Malaria incident rate • Percentage of children aged six months to 15 years who have received measles vaccination, on completion of a measles vaccination campaign• % of alerts, rumors and outbreaks investigated within 48 hours of notification • Percentage of deliveries conducted by skilled birth attendants• Number of consultations per person per year• Number of persons treated for mental conditions persons receiving MHPSS services• Number of health facilities providing SGBV health and MHPSS services• Coverage of PENTA3 in <One-year-old by administrative unit, Number of Primary healthcare centers with Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmONC)/500,000 population• Percentage of health facilities assessed using Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) scorecard Percentage of facilities with community feedback mechanism• Percentage of functioning health facilities provided with supportive supervision• Number of community health workers per 1,000 people, Number of SGBV survivors receiving CMR services• Number of persons with disability receiving rehabilitation services, Based on the above project indicators, HAA will adopt the standard MoH harmonized tools for monitoring both community level and facility base health indicators respectively. The tools that will be frequently used in monitoring the project activities and respective indicators are; Outpatient registers, pharmaceutical registers, maternal and child health registers, Health cluster, 5Ws spreadsheets, EWARS forms, IDSR forms, drug consumption reports, and the UNFPA PSDA spreadsheets last-mile service delivery tools. These tools will capture the required indicators stated in the Health cluster sectoral log frame. HAA will additionally use Qualitative tools, including Focus group discussions (FGDs) and Key informants Interviews (KIIs) to capture citizens’ perceptions of health services' quality in the respective locations. HAA staff will continue to participate in the coordinated assessments using relevant tools such as the Public Health situation analysis (PHSA), Inter-Agency Rapid Assessments (IRNA) tools, and other tools approved by the health cluster and MoH for gathering, analyzing, and reporting health information to guide health planning, programming and decision making. HAA shall adopt the community scorecard tools; ‘’Boda Boda talk talk’ from the community engagement communication working group, to facilitate dialogue in the communities during risk communications, infection prevention and control (IP&C), community mobilization for health, health education at the community level, handling complaints, redress, and feedback using local approaches in the communities. The Project will support the respective County departments in managing health information systems and ensure timely reporting and incorporation of data from the facilities into the DHMIS2. Health information will be coordinated and shared with the respective stakeholders; County Health Departments (CHDs), State Ministry of Health (SMoH), WHO, Health cluster, and other relevant stakeholders that are permitted to receive the various information the health system tiers.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Various literatures of assessment reports including SMART surveys and Humanitarian needs overviews conducted by different partners over the years sums that, he project areas have previous and current profile of violent armed conflict, sub national /inter-communal conflicts and revenge attacks since 2013. The recent reports are ; South Sudan HNO _OCHA Jan 2021; IRNA _Canal Pigi Jan 2021; SMART Survey _Health and Nutrition 2019_IMC Canal Pigi among others. Having these external uncertainties, HAA will use mobile services to reach the hard to reach Populations constraint by these shocks/hazards in their respective areas to ensure, vulnerable populations located in these areas access and utilize the health services while not exposed to harm or surrounding threats. HAA will also consider home base care for exceptional scenarios especially for people with special reported needs. There will be coordinated referrals and involvement of local authorities to ensure safety is guaranteed to the patients and their caregivers. The Health packages also includes integrated mental health services with psycho-social support activities, trauma management, effective case management of mass causalities/Injuries and referrals. The South Sudan Boma Health Initiative (MoH) module will be used for this purpose as recommended by MoHDuring the social mobilization for health, HAA will integrate peace massaging’s to all social groups to promote social co-existence and harmony. The project team will also participate in active protection threats monitoring and referrals to the protection care givers as well as referrals of suspected cases to peace committees, forums and local authorities as well as neutral partners operating in the counties

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

HAA will use both Mobile and Static health care Services across the Counties to increase access to both health and nutrition services, increase coverage and utilization of integrated health and nutrition services. This project package is tailored according to the tiers of health care system. In the mobile centers, HAA will conduct mass outreaches to the villages. Beside Basic case management of epidemic prone disease, vaccinations, Health Education and hygiene and Sanitation services, the project have integrated screening for malnutrition targeting children(6-59)Months, RDT for malaria, provision of preventative and curative services of patients with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and Vitamin A supplementation for under 5 every 6 months . There will be prompt referral of patients with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with Medical complications to the stabilization centers and Primary health care centers or county Hospitals for specialized care. There will also be up-down referral of MAM cases from static facilities to the Mobile centers for nutrition and growth monitoring. The project will also conduct Screening of pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition, Folic Acid distribution for pregnant women and distribution of treated bed nets for pregnant and lactating mothers. The project team will conduct integrate nutrition campaigns alongside the health continuum to raise awareness of service availability and increase nutritional behavior change communication in the counties. There will be active screening and treatment for Malaria, deworming services, and active case management among children under five years identified with acute malnutrition. Referrals for SAM with medical complications to the health and stabilization centers will be appropriately conducted and HAA will conduct effective case management in line with the standard treatment protocol UNICEF; MoH In all the sites, there will be active surveillance at both community and facility level to ensure early detection and treatment of girls and boys under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating mothers affected by acute malnutrition. There will be monthly supportive supervision visits to the project areas to monitor and track the project’s performance. HAA will also participate in planned Nutrition SMART surveys, support planning and roll-out of population-based surveys, and improve facility-based data collection, analysis, and reporting. The project will promote community engagement during design, implementation, and monitoring to advance community participation for responsive nutrition service delivery. There will be an integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM and mainstreaming of child protection and GBV risks mitigation at nutrition sites. HAA will use the new guidelines for service delivery in the COVID 19 context; the revised family MUAC guidelines will pivot this project to sustain services and expand community platforms for house-house nutrition service delivery. There will be refresher training on the family MUAC guideline with the revised nutrition guidelines for service delivery in the COVID 19 context. With this, the project will address the nutritional needs of children aged 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating mothers through treatment and empowerment services. The SAM cases will be managed in the stabilization centers
