Project Summary

DRC intervention will particularly be focused on providing access to basic, safe, and dignified Shelter solutions, and having access to sufficient and safe water for domestic use which will assist in enabling access of the most vulnerable population groups to safe and dignified shelter solutions which contributes to the 2023 Shelter HRP Objective 1: “Provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs.”
The conflict-affected population is assisted in compliance with the Sector and MoLAE standards. DRC seeks to engage with other DRC sectors to complement DRCs work this coordination and complementarity mechanism needs to be expressed accordingly between the shelter activities and achieving the principles of protection .DRC will achieve this through the outcome "Increased access to adequate gender- and disability-sensitive shelter, water, and hygiene infrastructure among conflict-affected persons in Dara'a, Homs, Hama, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, and Idleb".
DRC aims to provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support through addressing overburdened communities with a high ratio of returnees and, members of host communities and IDPs in the rehabilitation of partially damaged apartments, Provision of 600 Solar Systems in Rural Damascus as a response to severe shortage at common areas level. DRC is aiming for a multi-sectoral integrated intervention of Shelter, Education through schools rehabilitation activities, and WASH sectors to increase access to life-saving emergency and life-sustaining assistance while also transitioning to more resilience/early recovery-oriented activities in selected locations of intervention to maximize the impact of humanitarian actions, and enabling access of the conflict-affected population to safe water for drinking and domestic use and safe excreta. A total of 3,100 beneficiaries and 150 PWDs will be reached out in different locations with a 3 and above severity scale. DRC will rehabilitate 500 PDA benefiting 2500 individuals ( 50 in Aleppo, 50 in Idleb, 300 in Rural Damascus, 50 in Daraa, 25 in Homs, 25 in Hama).
DRC’s programme strategy is realized through two programme objectives which are responding to emergencies and addressing solutions to protracted displacement. When combined, these can provide a basis for a comprehensive response in all phases of the displacement cycle transitioning.
DRC will respond to emergencies where sudden population movements cause conflict-affected populations to be living in conditions posing immediate life-threatening risks.
IDPs assistance follows SHELTER Sector Standards will be provided in collective shelters, and private unfinished/demolished buildings in both, urban and rural areas. Newly displaced populations get prioritized as an emergency response including natural and/or conflict disasters, according to the Sector’s directive. The intervention will concentrate on areas where DRC has a current presence.
Selection of targeted beneficiaries will be done by DRC in close coordination with MoLA and SARC with verification of needs and beneficiaries by DRC. DRC will liaise with the Mukhtar, with any humanitarian actors present in the area, and with local residents to identify HHs, ensure coordination and avoid any overlap. DRC staff will have free access to move around the target areas scoping houses and approaching HHs directly to explain the scope of the intervention and offer to conduct an initial technical assessment. Initial technical assessments will provide an understanding of each family’s situation/vulnerability and determine the feasibility of works.

3 - Needs

The scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. A decade of conflict in Syria has with a total of 15.3 M individuals in need according to 2023 HNO.12.7M are threatened by accelerating economic deterioration, limited or unavailable basic services, and environmental shocks. Displaced populations living in inadequate shelters, such as partially damaged houses, are vulnerable to the unavailability of adequate and affordable shelter, the limited response for longer-term solutions, the new challenges for communal shelters, and the displacement trends in NES and NWS. The extent of the destruction in the areas of intervention are relatively large, distributed between total and partial destruction. Based on the needs assessments conducted by DRC in 2022, there is still a huge need and the rehabilitation of the damaged apartments is still an urgent goal for the people to secure the minimum living requirements. Most of the apartments are inhabited and devoid of doors and windows and need rehabilitated bathrooms and kitchens. Education has reached its lowest stage, as the schools infrastructure is almost destroyed. This lack of access to basic services including safe and adequate shelter increases the vulnerability of households and forces the adoption of harmful coping mechanisms such as selling assets or keeping children out of education, especially among female-headed households, households including youth, and those with functional difficulties.

4 - Implementation of activities

DRC will provide safe and dignified shelter solutions by repairing partially damaged houses/apartments for 3100 beneficiaries including shelter package comprising, electrical repairs, windows and doors, accessories for WASH facilities. DRC will prioritize apartments hosting IDP families alongside members of the host community and returnees for repair. DRC will target the old cities, Rural areas. Additionally, activities will include the installation of public lighting/solar system panels to promote the safety and access of beneficiaries in areas of intervention. DRC will be responsible for the procurement of these items, as well as monitoring the installation by the selected contractors. The skilled labor will be identified based on criteria set by both the Shelter team. DRC will provide HHs with Shelter packages including items for the installation of windows, doors, electrical wiring, accessories for WASH facilities etc. DRC shelter team will overlook the works to ensure that they are done with the required quality. Once works are finalized, they would be handed over to DoLAE/municipality and the beneficiaries families. The maintenance of the solar panels will be done by the technical team in the municipality in the future. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria, or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between the host community and IDPs, due to a lack of assistance from the previous. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria are uniform/agreed upon among sector members and with the government and are clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. Establish a complaint mechanism at the community level. 2.Selection criteria are influenced by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, the tension in the community, and the risk of the most vulnerable being left out. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community.3.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1.Partially damaged apartments may affect women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children, elderly, and people with disabilities: LH: high, Impact: high, mitigation measures: Ensuring agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consulting separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design implementation phases; Establish complaint & feedback mechanism; Monitor safe and equitable access and use of water distribution points.2. Low awareness of the existence of helplines and complaint mechanisms. Most beneficiaries fear retaliation or even losing their rights to a. Likelihood: low, Impact: Medium. Mitigation: DRC will Enhance FCM, Raise awareness among field staff and beneficiaries, and Ensure timely response to the complaints received.3. Insufficient/inadequate beneficiaries’ safety, security and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).Likelihood: Low, Impact: High, Mitigation: Increase Awareness on PSEA and the obligation to report SEA cases among staff, SARC volunteer and vendors, Volunteers and vendors should sign the organization COC, PSEA policy before entering into contractual agreement with DRC, Designate PSEA focal point and alternate focal point and ensure they are trained on how to handle reports of SEA, Enhance Complaint and feedback mechanism/hotlines and ensure confidentiality, transparency, accessibility.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Security situation as well as economic situation do not deteriorate further and remain stable.- Areas of intervention remain accessible to DRC staff, and contractors.- Items needed for rehabilitation are available in the local market.- Approval from MoLA and SARC is granted. - Liquidity is available in the country and DRC is able to pay contractors on time.Risks:- The areas of intervention are no longer accessible and are witnessing high tension thereby impacting DRC staff, beneficiaries, and contractors. mitigation: DRC will approach Donors to update them on the progress and suggest changing the area if possible.- High inflation impacts contractors' ability in securing items needed therefore impacting the quality of rehabilitation works; mitigation: DRC will reach out to other contractors and will relaunch the tenders if needed. - Exchange rate fluctuation and contractors do not respond to tenders, mitigation: DRC will try to relaunch the tenderers and try to encourage the contractors to respond to the published tenders
