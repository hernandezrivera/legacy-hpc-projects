Project Summary

UNHCR will ensure that Rohingya refugee children, adolescent, and youth from 3 to 24 years have access to equitable quality education opportunities through the continuation of existing programs by maintaining learning facilities, recruitment, training and deployment of teachers, procurement of teaching and learning materials, and expansion of learning facilities by constructing additional double-storied classrooms with sex-segregated latrines and WASH facilities. Looking at implementation beyond the Myanmar Curriculum (MC) Pilot, UNHCR will be developing a comprehensive scale-up concept note and implementation plan outlining the needs and specific actions to be undertaken. Furthermore, considering the COVID-19 pandemic and its implications, UNHCR and its partners will implement EdTech solutions along with caregiver led education and ensure community engagement in the overall project cycle ensuring Age Gender and Diversity (AGD) mainstreaming.
In addition, the transitioning of Learning Competency Framework and Approach (LCFA) level II students to the MC, in grades 1-3will be a critical undertaking for the second half of the year. As a step to ensure the provision of quality education and with the introduction of MC, language and teacher professional development (TPD) training is required to ensure educators and teachers have the required language competencies and expected teaching qualifications, hence TPD incorporating both in-service and ongoing teacher professional development activities, continuation and expansion of language acquisition program for Myanmar and English language as well as certified trainings for teachers and educators in collaboration with BARD college and BRAC University will be undertaken.
UNHCR will continue its host community support through maintaining and operationalizing the Community Technology Access (CTA) and the satellite teachers training facility, constructing or renovating classrooms and WASH facilities, and provide classroom furniture and supplies. UNHCR further will work with sector and partners to ensure a synchronized student data collection system using proGres database to avoid duplication and ensure reliable education data and analysis.

1 - Identified Needs

As per the JMSNA carried out in October 2020, about 41.5% of 3 to 5-year-olds, 28% of 6 to 14-year-olds, and an alarming 82% of 15 to 24-year-olds were not attending any form of education services before the closure of learning centres due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Retention rates are low, and high dropout rates will increase due to the impact of the prolonged closure of the learning centers, increasing the risks of children being subjected to child labor, exploitation, abuse, child marriage, etc. Insufficient classroom and scarcity of available space, inadequate WASH facilities, and lack of qualified and trained teachers remain major barriers in providing quality education. Multi-sectoral collaboration with Shelter, Site Planning, Education and Government authorities is needed to identify suitable space to ensure well planned establishment of learning facilities to curb shortage of space. While the implementation of the Myanmar Curriculum (MC) pilot and the transition of LCFA level II is a positive development, lack of clarity on certification and recognition of acquired documentation remains an issue of concern to the community as well as the education stakeholders. For the implementation of the MC based teaching and learning, the language barrier is a persistent challenge because the mother tongue of the Rohingya is a spoken language that without written scripts. While all the teaching and learning materials are in a non-native language, English and Burmese, teaching in a multilingual setting is already very challenging, and it is especially so for facilitators/teachers with no qualification, and professional certified training on pedagogy, language, and content matter. It is essential for the learners to also acquire skills in academia and life. In this connection, in addition to the short term teacher induction trainings and continued efforts for training leading to certification, teachers must also be trained on DRR, psycho-social support (PSS), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Sanitation and Hygiene promotion, and Child safeguarding principles. With the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, all the education facilities closed from March 2020 onwards and partners have had limited access to the camp settlement. Students are missing vital learning opportunities and partners are struggling to provide necessary assistance including psychosocial support to both students and educators. This also hinders teachers and educators to seek opportunities for professional development. Government restrictions coupled with poor internet connectivity and lack of knowledge among teachers/facilitators on use of technology further contributes to barriers in introducing EdTech intervention and continue providing education services. Besides, the poor enrollment rate for adolescents and youth signals a concern from the community which has been emerging as to the adequacy and appropriateness of the available education services for adolescent and youth. For instance, during Focus Group Discussions, parents have asked progression pathways and opportunities available and why their children were only allowed to study informal curriculum as well as adolescent and youth do not have access to appropriate skills training. Additionally, disengaged adolescents and youth could be exposed to protection risks including worst forms of child labor, forced marriages, sexual and gender-based violence, human trafficking, negative coping such as drug abuse among other risks. Moreover, there is a need for EdTech support and construction or renovation of classrooms for the host community education facilities which the District education officials seeks our support to address the needs on the ground.

2 - Activities or outputs

Lifelong learning for 17320 adolescent and youth: UNHCR will collaborate with various partners to promote skills development and self-reliance through the establishment of adolescent and youth clubs as well upgrading existing adolescent clubs for ages 15-24 with an opportunity to bridge the learning gap that currently exists for the age cohort.Retention and expand access to equitable quality education for 100,000 children, adolescent and youth (Refugee, 84,000 and Host community 16,000): UNHCR and its partners will seek to strengthen and expand equitable quality lifelong learning opportunities aligned with the education sector, Ministry of Primary and Mass Education (MoPME) as well as Ministry of Education (MoE) standards. Contribute to the System strengthening: UNHCR will continue extending technical support and contribute towards the overall improvement of education services for refugees and host community children, co-leading the Strategic Advocacy Group (SAG), actively participating in different education sector working groups, advocating to the government of Bangladesh and Myanmar for meaningful and certified education services including inclusion to the national education system in collaboration with UNICEF and SCI.Myanmar curriculum scaleup: Looking at implementation beyond the Myanmar Curriculum pilot, UNHCR will be developing a comprehensive scale up concept note outlining the specific actions to be undertaken in relation to identified technical areas and will revise and develop teacher professional development framework in line with Myanmar curriculum scaleup approach. Provision of Teachers and educator professional development (Teachers 3500 and master trainers 200) : To enhance teaching learning processes and facilitate quality education, 1,777 (more than 50% female) teachers and learning facilitators will undergo in-service and ongoing professional development actives while newly recruited 400 teachers will be inducted on basic teachers training course before deployment. Additionally, in collaboration with BRAD college UNHCR will continue to facilitate piloting of certified mentorship training for 25 mentors and training on pedagogy for 250 teachers. Learning continuation during and post COVID 19: At the national level, as a response to the covid-19 pandemic, learners access education through TV, radio, mobile phones and internet platform. These efforts offer a great opportunity to engage learners within the registered camps currently using the Bangladesh curriculum. Nonetheless, using available resources including the two well equipped CTA centers, the two language hubs and pre-distributed 350 tablets, digital contents will be uploaded and distributed to the Kutupalong and Nyapara refugee students. For the students residing in the rest of the camps (makeshift camps), the education sector guideline is being followed. Host community support: UNHCR will continue its host community support through constructing or renovating of 10 classrooms, constructing or renovating of WASH block and toilets in 10 schools and providing school supplies to 10 schools. Community engagement: The project will engage the community in overall project cycle through organizing training on MHPSS, DRR and SGBV and organizing joint monitoring visit, FGDs, KII and protection mainstreaming.Tech based education interventions: Acknowledging the prevailing challenges, and current policy position on education for refugees, UNHCR seeks to explore low cost and locally applicable technologies to enhance access and delivery of education services. Provision of quality education and assessment of learning outcome: UNHCR continue its technical support to its partners staffs through organizing meeting, FGDs, KII, workshop and training on principle of EiE, cross cutting issues, pedagogy, supportive supervision.
