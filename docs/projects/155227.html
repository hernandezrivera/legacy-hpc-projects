Needs

Sexual and Gender-based violence (SGBV) continues to affect Rohingya refugees and constitutes one of the main protection risks within the community, according to community assessments conducted by UNHCR and its partners 2017 and 2018.Fear of social stigma and rejection experienced by survivors severely hampers reporting of cases and reduces the request of service provision, with a negative impact on addressing the consequences of SGBV. Many women and girls have been exposed to widespread and severe forms of sexual violence before and during the flight. They continue to be at risk of SGBV in displacement. Child marriage for girls and polygamous relations are known in the camps. Rohingya women and girls are often not allowed to leave home without their husbands’ or family permission. Adolescent girls are prevented from attending school. Women and girls limit their movements within the settlements and have very limited opportunities and decision-making bodies. Economic stress increases risks of exploitation for women and. Reports link incidents of rape and trafficking to high risk informal work including domestic labour. Fear of abduction, harassment, and sexual violence severely restricts the mobility of women and girls, which results in lack of access to service information, social support networks, and safety alternatives for those in threat of harm at home. Despite the national legislation protecting women and girls, SGBV survivors, in particular refugees without registration, face severe challenges in accessing the justice system. Refugees wanting to lodge a legal complaint or initiate police investigation on a specific SGBV incident need a prior authorization from the Camp-in-Charge. Lack of knowledge of SGBV among judges, police and other authorities prevents to address impunity of perpetrators.The project aims at increasing accessibility and quality of SGBV services to Rohingya refugees living in Cox’s Bazar area, with a particular focus on working with key government authorities and increasing community engagement as preventative and response measures. UNHCR intends to provide SGBV survivors and individuals with specific risk of SGBV (UASC, single women, women heads of households, adolescent girls, persons with disabilities) with effective case management and referral to appropriate services, including medical, psychosocial support, legal and security, as needed and wished, according to the survivor’s centred approach. Interventions will address the need for building capacities, through training and mentoring of UNHCR staff, partner organizations, government authorities and persons of concern on SGBV prevention and response. The project strengthen the engagement of communities to address SGBV with an expected result in improved dissemination of information on services available and enhanced identification and referral of survivors to appropriate services. Prevention will be strengthened through sensitization and awareness-raising on SGBV and life-saving information on available services ensuring the involvement of men and boys. The office will also focus on mainstreaming SGBV through integration of prevention and response activities across all sectors. With most existing services focusing on women and girls, the project will also include the creation of avenues where male survivors can report and receive support in a confidential and dignified manner According to UNHCR participatory assessment conducted in 2017 and focus group discussions of 2018, Rohingya refugees especially women and girls, remain highly exposed to SGBV including but not limited to sexual and physical violence. The consequences and costs of SGBV have impacts at the individual level, as well as within the family, community and wider society.

Activities or outputs

SGBV activities in 2019 will aim at addressing above factors, with a particular focus on working with key government authorities and increasing community engagement as preventative and response measures. This will be key to disseminate information on services available and enhance identification and referral of survivors to appropriate services.Community members’ capacities will be built to take proactive steps to lead or participate in SGBV prevention, through community watch groups and SGBV committees, who will also participate to orient and accompany SGBV survivors to response services. UNHCR will increase quality and coverage of service provision, notably for individuals at risk of SGBV, while Survivors will be provided with effective case management and appropriate services, including legal assistance, medical treatment, psychosocial support, and security. Prevention of incidents will be strengthened through sensitization and awareness-raising on SGBV and life-saving information on available services. Mitigation measures will be integrated across different areas of UNHCR’s interventions (particularly health, shelter, WASH and site management), to reduce risks of SGBV.Specific activities will include:• Strengthening community-based mechanism and build new community structures engaged in prevention of and response to SGBV. UNHCR will explore SASA approach in order to enhance a community based involvement. • Promote women’s engagement in camp/community-leadership structures, in order to enhance their meaning full participation in decision making and provide support to the whole community. UNHCR will provide a continued mentorship of the women in these structures• The management of 13 Women Friendly Spaces (WFS) and establishment of an additional 6 WFS in partnership with UN Women. • Building capacity on SGBV prevention and response and PSEA.• Early identification of SGBV survivors.• Provision of quality services to SGBV survivors, including case management. • Support the coordination mechanism, notably the GBV sub-sector, by meaningful participation in order to strengthen SOPs and referral pathways, where existing, and support to the establishment of new ones for the other locations; Coordinate with GBV sub-sector and partners on the standardization of approaches, prevention activities and qualitative service provision;• Regular assessments, including safety audits, to identify and minimize risks of SGBV faced by women and girls as well as men and boys and inform recommendations for action plans and advocacy at camp level and for overall coordination with other stakeholders, particularly through the GBV sub-sector;• Enhanced strategy on awareness-raising on SGBV and information dissemination on life-saving as well as broader available services. SGBV prevention campaigns will target both refugee and host communities; • Promotion of men and boys engagement in order to enhance gender equality, challenge gender stereotypes and reduce the risk of SGBV;• SGBV information management and incidents data collection standardization, analysis and regular production of trend analysis reports; • Establishment of risk mitigation measures across different sectors, (especially health, shelter, WASH and site management), including through training, awareness raising and on the job advice and support; SGBV prevention will be integrated in WASH, through distribution of 100,000 hygiene kits to women of reproductive age, as part of UNHCR commitments to women. • Access to education, vocational and livelihood opportunities by linking with key state actors such as the Ministry of Women and Children’s Affairs.• Mainstreaming SGBV in all emergency preparedness and response plans.
