Project Summary

The crisis in Syria has increased humanitarian needs which has been worsened by the economic crisis and the earthquake. Humanitarian need has increased from 15.3 million people in 2023 to 16.7 million people in 2024 across Syria (HNO Summary, 2024). The increase in protection risks including malnutrition, disease outbreaks, and food insecurity are contributing to an increase in mortality rate. High inflation rate, currency depreciation and increase in commodity prices has led to increase in poverty, increasing humanitarian needs and people relying on humanitarian assistance to survive (HNO Summary, 2024). These needs have disproportionately impacted the most vulnerable including people with disabilities, pregnant and lactating mothers, the elderly and children, forcing households to employ exploitive mechanisms to enhance survival such as child labor and early child marriage.
ADRA’s presence in Syria has identified great humanitarian needs in Latakia and Homs which were affected by the crisis. There are 957,222 people in need in Homs and 753,005 people in need in Latakia in 2024 (HNO Summary, 2024). ADRA will implement activities in line with the Cash Working Group (CWG) targeting a total of 6,024 households in the targeted areas. The Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) will provide approximately 30,120 people in need of assistance to cover basic needs in Homs and Latakia including food and non-food items. ADRA will obtain the required approvals and sign MoU with Financial Service Providers (FSP), and provide the beneficiaries with cash assistance for 7 rounds to relieve their financial difficulties. ADRA will follow the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) and Cash Working Group (CWG) recommendations during implementation for the transfer value.
The selection criteria will focus on contributing to strategic objective 3 (increase resilience and access to services) and strategic objective 1 (Save lives) targeting 1.19million individuals, and aim to reach 50,000 people at risk. Cash assistance will be provided for two groups; Group 1 will receive one transfer for emergency MPC in response to the earthquake and group 2 will receive 6 rounds for regular MPC for people with disabilities. ADRA will conduct social assessments to identify areas that are most in need and ensure that the target beneficiaries include men, women, the elderly and people with disabilities. The beneficiaries will be informed through a phone call or text about the objective of the cash provided, the selection criteria of the beneficiaries, the value of the voucher, the implementation process of the intervention, the required documents required to receive the funds, the organization and the donor providing assistance. ADRA will provide a telephone number for any inquiries and an explanation on how to raise complaints and concern. MEAL team will conduct surveys before and after provision of the cash assistance by monitoring the progress of the project, the adequacy of the intervention and the impact of the intervention on the beneficiaries. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) survey will be done, which will assess topics related to the distribution, accessibility, knowledge about the project, satisfaction, selection process, protection measures, and the impact of the MPC transfers, if the assistance corresponded to their needs, safety and appropriateness of the intervention.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project poses risks to the beneficiaries including violence which could occur from the distribution point, gender-based violence at home, or at the community level. There is also a risk of spread of communicable diseases at distribution points as a result of overcrowding. To mitigate against the risks, ADRA will select the beneficiaries with consideration for protection mainstreaming ensuring “do no harm” principle is followed. The selection criteria will prioritize people with disabilities, female headed households, single headed households, households with the elderly, households with people with health concerns among other vulnerabilities. ADRA has an established protection mechanisms and procedures which includes a code of conduct which is signed by all the staff upon completion of training of the ADRA protection policy which outlines the responsibilities of the staff and appropriate behavior of the staff with beneficiaries. At the distribution points, ADRA will ensure presence of staff, both male and female to ensure there is order at the distribution point. ADRA will also ensure that there is adequate space at the distribution points to mitigate against the risk of spread of communicable diseases. ADRA will ensure that the beneficiaries will collect their cash according to their own convenience and safety, and select trustworthy financial service providers who will be required to submit detailed reports to verify with the beneficiaries during PDM to ensure no fraud. The risk of depreciation of Syrian pound currency reducing the purchasing power will be mitigated by providing a limited number of days when the cash vouchers can be spent to prevent inflation risks. ADRA Syria has a protection policy which will ensure protection of beneficiaries from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation and Gender-Based violence (SGBV). Any issues concerning SGBV will be addressed during the lifecycle of the project through ADRA Syria protection policy. The project will be guided by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) GBV gender guidelines and protection mainstreaming especially PSEA. All ADRA staff and volunteers undergo mandatory PSEA training before the beginning of assignments and refreshment training is offered annually. ADRA will offer additional PSEA and gender mainstreaming training and ensure that complaint and feedback mechanism is available and equipped to handle sensitive complaints. ADRA staff will be present at the cash distribution points to monitor the process and ensure the safety of the beneficiaries. By implementing these measures ADRA will ensure that the project does not pose risks of sexual exploitation to the beneficiaries.
