Project Summary

Strengthened self-reliance and peaceful coexistence between 14709 CAR refugees and 5846 host community members in Cameroon.
The project consolidates training for all 4,500 agriculture beneficiaries on post-harvest management, value chain addition and marketing and builds on this with trainings on transformation and processing of key crops. It provides material support to farmers groups for value addition, as per their needs.
100 training sessions will be organized on transformation/processing of specific agricultural product and the project will install 40 agricultural processing units, including Grinder and Corn Sheller.
In line with the global compact for refugees, UNHCR is collaborating with private sector to enable refugees become entrepreneurs and obtain economic independence. MaisCam, maize processing factory based in the Adamawa region, is one of the potential buyers contacted by UNHCR on behalf of local farmers, including refugees. The pilot phase, targets the provision of at least 80 tons of maize for the 2020-2021 agricultural season.
As part of the COVID-19 mitigation measures, 4500 supported farmers, will be provided with short-cycle food crops, including maize, beans, cowpeas, soybeans, peanuts and vegetable crops and tools (moto pump for irrigation, sprayers and accessories), in order to reduce the risk of food shortages in the project area.
IGA groups will be linked to national microfinance structures and exchange and learning visits will be organized for IGA groups. As part of the COVID-19 mitigation measures, the project will support 50 groups of entrepreneurial beneficiaries from Y1 and Y2, to implement profitable income-generating activities that do not require a lot of means. They include poultry and small ruminant production, buying and selling of foodstuffs, etc.
A refresher training of VLSA members on bookkeeping, basic accounting will be organized.
The project will hold refresher training for 70 existing Community Resource Persons, and 30 new ones selected high-performers from beneficiaries, from refugee and host communities to support implementation and provide close guidance and follow up of beneficiaries.
The project will support agricultural, entrepreneurial and vocational training beneficiaries to attend trade fairs to advertise and sell their products.
Finally, an end of project evaluation will be conducted to analyze the changes in the socio-economic profile of households after 3 years support.
Considering the many and varied types of conflicts, the project guides committees to focus on specific conflict sources in each year. This helps the community to explore conflict resolution strategies most appropriate to each type of conflict.
In this year, the project will address agropastoral, borehole, interreligious, intergeneration conflicts (youth opposed to elders) and conflicts in schools.
LWF will support existing peace forums members to coordinate meeting with neighbourhood watch committee.
In total, the project supplies 47 listening clubs for adults and four school listening clubs of 10 members each, with radios, USB sticks and battery packs to listen to peace messages. After 3 years, capacity and structures for social cohesion and peaceful cohabitation between diverse communities will have been strengthened. Peace monitors and CRPs participate in the listening sessions to ensure that the messages are well listened to and then give feedback. They will ensure that the beneficiaries are listening to peace messages, news and that batteries are properly used. There are also monitoring visits carried out by the program staff. The project will draft and sponsor 20 messages to be broadcasted twice a week.
Most CAR refugees are women and girls, as many men have been killed during the conflict, many women have entered Cameroon alone.
