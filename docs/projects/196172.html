Project Summary

DRC will aim to continue GBV prevention and response programming to reduce the underlying causes and drivers of gender violence and continue providing specialized GBV case management services aimed at providing immediate support to the acute needs of survivors of GBV, including non-specialized psychosocial support, dignity kit distributions and referrals to specialized services. DRC's Response programming will be complemented with interventions aimed at preventing GBV by changing mindsets and harmful attitudes and practices within the community that result in violence against gender. Wherever appropriate, DRC will seek to strengthen synergies with other protection areas (CP and GP) and between protection and non-protection sectors. This will include building capacities of GBV case workers on CP risks/case management principles, providing training to non-protection staff on GBV mainstreaming, identification and referral of GBV cases.
Under this project, DRC will address the activities in Camp 6, 8E and Palongkhali, Rajapalong unions from the host community. DRC is committed to ensuring its GBV programming is relevant, appropriate and does not cause harm. DRC will also participate in safety audits for CM conducted from the GBVSS to ensure the services are provided adhering to the GBV guiding principles. Accordingly, protection monitoring activities will be strengthened to ensure more consistent documentation of risks including GBV. DRC also intends to strengthen an AGD approach to data collection to ensure information on risks can be disaggregated by age, gender and diversity profiles including survivors with disabilities and at a later stage hijra and other sexual minorities. DRC GBV team will also extend lifesaving case management services for women and girls, male survivors, gender diverse population, and child survivors in the aforementioned camps and host community.
DRC will provide outreach sessions and psychosocial support for women and girls in Camp 6 and 2 unions (Rajapalong and Palongkhali union) from host community. Besides, DRC will continue to operate Hizra safe space in Palongkhali. Men and boy engagement activities will also be scaled up in selected areas. DRC will continue to involve the beneficiaries in each stage of planning and implementation for identifying the needs, vulnerabilities, priorities, and coping mechanisms. In all locations, outreach will be designed to make services more inclusive for persons with disabilities and the gender diverse population with specialized outreach and participation modules by focusing on protection mainstreaming in each stage.
DRC GBV technical expertise will be available to build the capacity of local GBV actors, including local authorities and local NGOs based on tailored needs assessments, and PSEA will also be shared through training. DRC has already an established PSEA mechanism which will be further strengthened by providing training to volunteers and raising awareness on the reporting mechanism. Also, DRC will continue to develop and map a partnership strategy by linking government, local NGOs, and private organizations and aligning the ISCG sector strategies for mainstreaming the activities. DRC will diversify the service accessibility fund in place for contingency needs. A hotline for case management support will be available throughout the year. The selection of locations is based on an analysis of GBV-SS mapping and complements DRC’s other sectoral program. DRC will set up CFRM in the host locations and direct to DRC CoC or CFRM as appropriate in refugee camps for AAP.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to ISCG Gender Profile for Rohingya Refugee Response, women and adolescent girls, who comprise 52% of the refugee population, are vulnerable to risks of abuse, exploitation, domestic violence and gender-based violence (GBV). The policy restrictions on Rohingya refugees' freedom of movement, opportunities for income generation, and means of subsistence are highlighted in an interagency report by UNHCR, CARE, and ActionAid titled "Intersectional Analysis of Gender Amongst Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh." These restrictions amplify risks for refugee women and girls, including survival sex, child marriage, trafficking, and limited access to aid. The paper asserts that in this setting, refugee men and boys face the risks of perpetrating IPV, sexual harassment, and polygamy. In many areas, hijras are still underrepresented and neglected. Inter-agency report titled “Intersectional Analysis of Gender Amongst Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh” produced by UNHCR, CARE and ActionAid in 2020 highlights the role of the policy restrictions on freedom of movement, income generation and livelihoods opportunities for Rohingya refugees leading to negative coping strategies and exacerbating risks to refugee women and girls including survival sex, child marriage, trafficking and reduced access to assistance items. According to the report, refugee men and boys are at risk of perpetrating IPV and sexual harassment and engaging in polygamy in this environment. Hijras and other gender diverse community remain underserved and unrepresented across sectors. These patterns entrench the consequences of systemic denial of rights faced by the refugees when in Myanmar, following experiences of acute conflict related sexual violence during flight. For displacement-affected host communities, where patriarchy and the needs of a development context already caused GBV risks, the influx threatened social cohesion, income generation and livelihood opportunities. The influx threatened social cohesion, income generation, and livelihood prospects for host communities who had already experienced relocation and were at risk for GBV due to patriarchal and development-related requirements. Currently the gender diverse population is receiving services from DRC safe space and receiving sessions on prevention, life-skill, referral services to access HIV tests. Also, GBV incidences are increasing, including IPV, polygamy and transphobic violence. While refugee respondents on the JSMNA 2020 rated SGBV services highly both prior to and after the pandemic, the analysis of health, safety, legal and PSS services and components of case management are not sufficiently available. IRC's report on "No time to Lose" in 2020 also suggested that without a pathway to knowledge and skills, refugee children living in the refugee camps have little opportunity to support themselves in the future, exposing them to a higher risk of trafficking, violence, child labor, and child marriage. For host communities, according to the JSMNA 2020, information itself is a gap. This is also indicated by DRC GBV’s case management supervision experience. Thus, the relevance of DRC’s full package of GBV response and prevention services for host and refugee communities remains for 2023. This includes engagement of men and boys, and dedicated programming for child survivors, GDP survivors and male survivors. DRC’s unique contribution in having run a safe space for hijras since 2019 anticipated gaps being flagged in the recent reports and is proposed for scale-up. DRC has been working towards making services more inclusive for ethnic minority groups. In Telkhola (Palong Khali union), DRC has established a case management desk for creating better access to the ethnic minority group Tanchangya , who previously has very limited access to protection services but the gap for required GBV service is still way lagged behind.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is aligned with the Joint Response Plan (JRP) plan and recognizes the importance of focusing on critical protection needs and targeted protection activities for people at high protection risks including GBV. In line with rationalization Principle #3 for promoting integrated approach, DRC will aim to fill the gaps to ensure access to GBV, general protection, community-based protection, protection monitoring, gender-based violence including services for women, men, adolescent girls/boys and persons with disabilities following integrated approach from 2 camps and 2 host community. DRC will also seek to improve the inter-sectoral camp-based coordination in a coordinated manner through CCCM and integrating appropriate GBV service delivery to refugees and host communities while maintaining a collaborative and complementary relations with the Government/CiCs and other sector agencies. This project will demonstrate prevention activities by arranging specific prevention session with age and gender segregated group. DRC has already developed module of prevention session which is adapted from IRC EMAP modules. Considering the Principle #4 for ensuring predictability and continuity, DRC will ensure its continuity and stability of the program together with other donor funded projects by DANIDA, ECHO, etc. In the interest of sustainability and continuity, DRC technical expertise is made available to partners and stakeholders in GBV through training, sensitization and mentoring and building capacity of local actors. Besides, DRC will regularly interact with the District Commissioner, RRRC, sector and other government agencies and host community to ensure alignment with govt. rules. DRC is currently conducting a comprehensive local partner mapping and selection exercise to extend valued services far beyond the project period

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

DRC protection project will be mainstreamed into other programs of DRC such as CCCM and Livelihoods. Also, Gender Based Violence with protection’s other components such as case management, community-based protection, protection monitoring and livelihoods. DRC protection team will ensure mainstreaming to all sectors mentioned above and at the camp level where DRC exists through inputting need assessments and providing training to protection and no-protection staff. Additionally, producing protection sector information management products and building the capacity of the camp level sectors by the protection focal receive ToT through trained protection focal. DRC is already an active member of the ISCG PSEA networking group.PSEA incidents are being reported to the sector coordinator and data regarding volunteers and staff’s training on PSEA and reported PSEA incidents tracker are being shared. At the same time,DRC will collaborate with PSEA networking Group for investigation of PSEA case

3.2 - Gender

The different needs of men and women, boys and girls and gender-diverse population has been considered while designing the project. Gender disaggregated data will be collected and used to monitor the progress against the indicator. DRC has Age, gender, and diversity mainstreaming policy in place, which provides an overall framework for mainstreaming gender elements in the projects. The AGDM policy will be put into practice by DRC Bangladesh while the project is being carried out. A general framework for mainstreaming gender components in projects is provided by the Age, Gender, and Diversity Mainstreaming Policy, which exists at DRC and will be utilized for the project. DRC safe spaces will have a corner for women and girls and this corner will store the sanitary napkins and panties for women and girls coming to safe spaces. DRC has also worked in collaboration with MSF in setting up active referral mechanism to ensure the safe access to HIV test for hijra (transgender) participants.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

DRC is communited to ensuring age including safeguarding approach and principle (best interest of children, confidentiality, do no harm) accorss its programming cycle. All DRC staff and volunteers have been trained on safeguarding issue. And the reporting mechanism is also in place. Community-centered approach will be followed to deliver the project. The contents of the sessions will be finalized by the affected people of the community through Focused Group Discussions( FGDs). Prior to finalizing the sessions, need assessment will be conducted through key informant interviews among FGDs. In case management, caseworkers will be trained to ensure the client is empowered to make decisions. While decorating the safe space, the women and girls including Persons with disabilities will be taking charge to lead the process. The community volunteers will be consulted regularly to understand the effectiveness of the services. DRC staff and volunteers are abided by the Code of Conduct.

3.4 - Diversity

DRC will Implement AGDM policy while implementing its projects.DRC will work towards making services more accessible for all age and gender groups. DRC is also working with gender diverse population and providing them NFI (condoms and lubricants). DRC is working towards making centers more accessible for people with disabilities by receiving technical support from HI. HI, is doing regular assessment in DRC centers and providing constructive feedback on which DRDC will take further action. Also, an action point has been developed on disability inclusion from DRC Bangladesh and which will be implemented from the next phase.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

DRC has established complaints, feedback, response mechanims (CFRM) to receive, refer and address complaints and feedbacks of the beneficiaries. This project will use CFRM mechanisms to receive and address complainst to the beneficiaries. DRC staffs and volunteers have been trained on PSEA.The PSEA policy in place and PSEA focal points have been nominated in each camp. For the locations where DRC SMS team is the concerns are being referred to SMS team. And in the locations where DRC SMS (CFRM) team is not present DRC refers to other organizations. Beside, Satisfaction surveys and IPA assessments will help in understanding the client's feedback on the case management services. Regular feedback sessions will be held to monitor the quality of the participants. These outputs will then direct the improvement of the services and retain the good practices.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, DRC community-based protection team received training on DRR and provide/will provide awareness sessions to the community groups so that they can continue to contribute for protection services including GBV at the camp level while any climate and hazards occur in the camps. During emergency situation, DRC also has a standard provision of critical services which will be provided to the people in need ( including GBV case management, counseling, referral services) etc. Caseworkers are also trained on remote GBV case management services in case of emergency as a part of contingency plan.
